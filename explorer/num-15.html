The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Numbers 15 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And the LORD spake unto Moses, saying,

2 Speak unto the children of Israel, and say unto them, When ye be come into the land of your habitations, which I give unto you,

3 And will make an offering by fire unto the LORD, a burnt offering, or a sacrifice in performing a vow, or in a freewill offering, or in your solemn feasts, to make a sweet savour unto the LORD, of the herd or of the flock:

4 Then shall he that offereth his offering unto the LORD bring a meat offering of a tenth deal of flour mingled with the fourth part of an hin of oil.

5 And the fourth part of an hin of wine for a drink offering shalt thou prepare with the burnt offering or sacrifice, for one lamb.

6 Or for a ram, thou shalt prepare for a meat offering two tenth deals of flour mingled with the third part of an hin of oil.

7 And for a drink offering thou shalt offer the third part of an hin of wine, for a sweet savour unto the LORD.

8 And when thou preparest a bullock for a burnt offering, or for a sacrifice in performing a vow, or peace offerings unto the LORD:

9 Then shall he bring with a bullock a meat offering of three tenth deals of flour mingled with half an hin of oil.

10 And thou shalt bring for a drink offering half an hin of wine, for an offering made by fire, of a sweet savour unto the LORD.

11 Thus shall it be done for one bullock, or for one ram, or for a lamb, or a kid.

12 According to the number that ye shall prepare, so shall ye do to every one according to their number.

13 All that are born of the country shall do these things after this manner, in offering an offering made by fire, of a sweet savour unto the LORD.

14 And if a stranger sojourn with you, or whosoever be among you in your generations, and will offer an offering made by fire, of a sweet savour unto the LORD; as ye do, so he shall do.

15 One ordinance shall be both for you of the congregation, and also for the stranger that sojourneth with you, an ordinance for ever in your generations: as ye are, so shall the stranger be before the LORD.

16 One law and one manner shall be for you, and for the stranger that sojourneth with you.

17 And the LORD spake unto Moses, saying,

18 Speak unto the children of Israel, and say unto them, When ye come into the land whither I bring you,

19 Then it shall be, that, when ye eat of the bread of the land, ye shall offer up an heave offering unto the LORD.

20 Ye shall offer up a cake of the first of your dough for an heave offering: as ye do the heave offering of the threshingfloor, so shall ye heave it.

21 Of the first of your dough ye shall give unto the LORD an heave offering in your generations.

22 And if ye have erred, and not observed all these commandments, which the LORD hath spoken unto Moses,

23 Even all that the LORD hath commanded you by the hand of Moses, from the day that the LORD commanded Moses, and henceforward among your generations;

24 Then it shall be, if ought be committed by ignorance without the knowledge of the congregation, that all the congregation shall offer one young bullock for a burnt offering, for a sweet savour unto the LORD, with his meat offering, and his drink offering, according to the manner, and one kid of the goats for a sin offering.

25 And the priest shall make an atonement for all the congregation of the children of Israel, and it shall be forgiven them; for it is ignorance: and they shall bring their offering, a sacrifice made by fire unto the LORD, and their sin offering before the LORD, for their ignorance:

26 And it shall be forgiven all the congregation of the children of Israel, and the stranger that sojourneth among them; seeing all the people were in ignorance.

27 And if any soul sin through ignorance, then he shall bring a she goat of the first year for a sin offering.

28 And the priest shall make an atonement for the soul that sinneth ignorantly, when he sinneth by ignorance before the LORD, to make an atonement for him; and it shall be forgiven him.

29 Ye shall have one law for him that sinneth through ignorance, both for him that is born among the children of Israel, and for the stranger that sojourneth among them.

30 But the soul that doeth ought presumptuously, whether he be born in the land, or a stranger, the same reproacheth the LORD; and that soul shall be cut off from among his people.

31 Because he hath despised the word of the LORD, and hath broken his commandment, that soul shall utterly be cut off; his iniquity shall be upon him.

32 And while the children of Israel were in the wilderness, they found a man that gathered sticks upon the sabbath day.

33 And they that found him gathering sticks brought him unto Moses and Aaron, and unto all the congregation.

34 And they put him in ward, because it was not declared what should be done to him.

35 And the LORD said unto Moses, The man shall be surely put to death: all the congregation shall stone him with stones without the camp.

36 And all the congregation brought him without the camp, and stoned him with stones, and he died; as the LORD commanded Moses.

37 And the LORD spake unto Moses, saying,

38 Speak unto the children of Israel, and bid them that they make them fringes in the borders of their garments throughout their generations, and that they put upon the fringe of the borders a ribband of blue:

39 And it shall be unto you for a fringe, that ye may look upon it, and remember all the commandments of the LORD, and do them; and that ye seek not after your own heart and your own eyes, after which ye use to go a whoring:

40 That ye may remember, and do all my commandments, and be holy unto your God.

41 I am the LORD your God, which brought you out of the land of Egypt, to be your God: I am the LORD your God.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

A claim does NOT become Documented merely because a commentary states it. Commentary views are attributed to their authors and labeled Interpreted unless a primary textual source directly supports the claim. Where evidence runs out, the page says Unresolved and stops — it does not fill the gap.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

This chapter looks forward to life in the promised land, opening with the encouraging promise that Israel will come into the land of their habitation. It sets the proportions of meat and drink offerings to accompany burnt and peace offerings, gives one law for native and stranger alike, and commands a cake of the first dough as a heave offering. It then distinguishes sins of ignorance, which have an offering, from presumptuous sin, which is cut off, records the stoning of a man found gathering sticks on the sabbath, and ends with the command to make fringes with a ribband of blue on the borders of garments, to remember and do all the commandments. Interpreted Editorial overview, grounded in the chapter text and its study notes

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 2. When you come into the land of your habitation which I give you, and will make an offering by fire to the Lord, the law of proportions applies. Henry notes the opening is very encouraging after chapter 14: it plainly intimates God would secure the promised land to their seed, despite the sentence on that generation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. The offering by fire might be a burnt offering, a sacrifice in performing a vow, a freewill offering, or in solemn feasts, to make a sweet savour to the Lord. Henry explains these sacrifices of acknowledgment were intended as the food of God's table, requiring a constant supply of bread, oil and wine whatever the flesh meat was. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. He that offers his offering shall bring a meat offering of a tenth deal of flour mingled with a fourth part of a hin of oil. The intent of the law, Henry says, is to direct the proportions: every flesh offering carried its measured bread and oil, so that God's table was furnished in full. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. The fourth part of a hin of wine for a drink offering was prepared with the burnt offering or sacrifice, for one lamb. The drink offering matched the meat offering in scale, a quarter hin for the smallest animal, rising with the size of the sacrifice. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. For a ram, the meat offering was two tenth deals of flour mingled with oil. The proportion doubled for the ram, keeping the bread in step with the flesh, neither stinted nor excessive. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. For a drink offering with the ram, the third part of a hin of wine was offered for a sweet savour. Wine, like flour and oil, rose by fixed steps: a fourth for a lamb, a third for a ram, a half for a bullock. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. When a bullock was prepared for a burnt offering, or a sacrifice in performing a vow, or peace offerings, the same law of proportions governed it. The largest animal carried the largest accompaniment, so that no offering came to God's table bare. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. With a bullock he brought a meat offering of three tenth deals of flour mingled with half a hin of oil. The three tenths and half hin complete the ascending scale, lamb, ram, bullock, each with its appointed measure. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. For a drink offering with the bullock, half a hin of wine was brought, for an offering made by fire of a sweet savour to the Lord. The half hin crowned the scale, and the sweet savour belonged to the whole offering, flesh, bread, oil and wine together. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. Thus it was done for one bullock, or one ram, or a lamb, or a kid. The rule was universal across the kinds: no animal was offered without its proportioned bread and wine. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. According to the number prepared, so it was done to every one according to their number. The proportions multiplied with the offerings: ten lambs meant ten measures, not one measure stretched over ten, so that God's table was furnished for every sacrifice. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. All that are born of the country shall do these things after this manner, in offering an offering made by fire of a sweet savour to the Lord. The native Israelite was bound by the same measures as everyone else: birth in the land gave no exemption from the law of the table. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. If a stranger sojourns with you, or whoever is among you in your generations, and will offer an offering made by fire of a sweet savour to the Lord, he shall do as you do. The stranger who worshipped Israel's God worshipped by Israel's measures, a happy forewarning, Henry says, of the Gentiles' calling and admission into the church. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. One ordinance shall be both for you of the congregation and for the stranger that sojourns with you, an ordinance for ever in your generations: as you are, so shall the stranger be before the Lord. The permanence matters: the equality was not a temporary concession but a standing ordinance. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. One law and one manner shall be for you, and for the stranger that sojourns with you. Henry draws the gospel conclusion: if the law made so little difference between Jew and Gentile, much less would the gospel, which broke down the partition wall and reconciled both to God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. Speak to Israel: when you come into the land whither I bring you, the law of the dough offering will apply. Like the offering law of verse 2, this law was given in the wilderness for a land not yet entered, a promise in itself that Israel would eat Canaan's bread. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 19. Then it shall be, that when you eat of the bread of the land, you shall offer up a heave offering to the Lord. The heave offering began at the eating itself: the first use of Canaan's harvest was God's, before Israel's own tables were spread. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 20. You shall offer up a cake of the first of your dough for a heave offering, as you do the heave offering of the threshing floor. The first cake, like the first sheaf, acknowledged that the whole harvest came from God, and the first portion sanctified the rest. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 21. Of the first of your dough you shall give to the Lord a heave offering in your generations. The gift was perpetual, generation after generation, so that every household's baking remembered the Giver. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 22. If you have erred and not observed all these commandments which the Lord has spoken to Moses, even all he commanded by Moses from the day he gave commandment onward, the law of ignorance applies. Henry notes ignorance will in a degree excuse, but will not justify those who might have known their Lord's will yet did it not, and David prayed to be cleansed from secret faults he was not aware of. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 23. Even all that the Lord commanded you by the hand of Moses, from the day the Lord gave commandment and onward throughout your generations, is covered by this law when missed through ignorance. The scope is total: no commandment was too small for atonement, and none too great to be missed unknowingly without remedy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 24. If anything was committed by ignorance without the knowledge of the congregation, the whole congregation offered one young bullock for a burnt offering with its meat and drink offering, and one kid of the goats for a sin offering. The congregation's ignorance was atoned corporately, with burnt offering for dedication and sin offering for guilt. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 25. The priest made an atonement for all the congregation of Israel, and it was forgiven them, for it was ignorance. Henry notes sins committed ignorantly are forgiven through Christ the great Sacrifice, who on the cross seemed to explain one part of his offering's intention in praying, Father, forgive them, for they know not what they do. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 26. It was forgiven to all the congregation and to the stranger that sojourned among them, seeing all the people were in ignorance. Henry observes it looked favourably upon the Gentiles that this law of atoning for sins of ignorance was expressly made to extend to strangers to Israel. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 27. If any soul sinned through ignorance, he brought a she goat of the first year for a sin offering. The individual's ignorance had its own offering, smaller than the congregation's, but of the same kind: a sin offering for a sin not intended. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 28. The priest made an atonement for the soul that sinned ignorantly, when he sinned by ignorance before the Lord, and it was forgiven him. Atonement was personal as well as corporate: the ignorant soul, not only the ignorant congregation, was forgiven. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 29. You shall have one law for him that sins through ignorance, both for him born among Israel and for the stranger that sojourns among them. The same one law that governed offerings governed ignorance: native and stranger stood equal before atonement as before the table. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 30. But the soul that does anything presumptuously, whether born in the land or a stranger, reproaches the Lord, and that soul shall be cut off from among his people. Henry defines the presumptuous sinner as one who sins designedly against God's will and glory, whose sins are exceedingly sinful because he reproaches the Lord himself. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 31. Because he has despised the word of the Lord and broken his commandment, that soul shall utterly be cut off, and his iniquity shall be upon him. Henry adds the presumptuous despise the word, thinking themselves too great, too good, and too wise to be ruled by it, so the guilt remains on them without atonement. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 32. While Israel was in the wilderness, they found a man gathering sticks on the Sabbath day. Henry gives the particular instance of presumption: gathering sticks to make a fire, whereas the people were to bake and seethe what they needed the day before, was done as an affront both to the law and to the Lawgiver. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 33. Those who found him gathering sticks brought him to Moses and Aaron and to all the congregation. The case was brought to the appointed judges rather than settled by the finders: God was the Judge, and before him they brought this cause. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 34. They put him in ward, because it was not declared what should be done to him. The delay was judicial, not negligent: the sentence for this new case had not yet been declared, so the man was held until God spoke. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 35. The Lord said to Moses, the man shall surely be put to death: all the congregation shall stone him with stones without the camp. Henry notes the sentence adjudged him a Sabbath-breaker according to the law's intent, and the most terrible death was appointed to show how great the crime was and how displeasing to God, that others might hear and fear. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 36. All the congregation brought him without the camp and stoned him with stones, and he died, as the Lord commanded Moses. Henry notes as many as could were employed in the execution, that those at least who threw a stone might be afraid of breaking the Sabbath, and God intended the exemplary punishment as a standing warning to make conscience of keeping the Sabbath holy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 38. Israel was bid to make fringes in the borders of their garments throughout their generations, and put upon the fringe a ribband of blue. Henry explains the Jews were distinguished from neighbours in dress as in diet, taught not to be conformed to heathen ways, proclaiming themselves Jews wherever they were, as not ashamed of God and his law, and the fringes were memorandums to prevent sin through forgetfulness. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 39. The fringe was to be looked upon, to remember all the Lord's commandments and do them, and not to seek after your own heart and eyes, after which you go a whoring. Henry notes the fringes were not for trimming or adorning clothes, but to stir up minds by remembrance, and if tempted to sin, the fringe would warn them not to break God's commandments. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 40. That you may remember and do all my commandments, and be holy unto your God. Henry's summary is that we should use every means of refreshing our memories with God's truths and precepts, to strengthen and quicken obedience and arm our minds against temptation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 41. I am the Lord your God, who brought you out of the land of Egypt, to be your God: I am the Lord your God. Henry notes this great reason for all the commandments is again and again repeated, and holiness means cleansed from sin and sincerely devoted to his service. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry

Henry notes the fringes were not for trimming or adorning clothes, but to stir up minds by remembrance, and if tempted to sin, the fringe would warn them not to break God's commandments. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 40. That you may remember and do all my commandments, and be holy unto your God. Henry's summary is that we should use every means of refreshing our memories with God's truths and precepts, to strengthen and quicken obedience and arm our minds against temptation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 41. I am the Lord your God, who brought you out of the land of Egypt, to be your God: I am the Lord your God. Henry notes this great reason for all the commandments is again and again repeated, and holiness means cleansed from sin and sincerely devoted to his service.
