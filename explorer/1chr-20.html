The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — 1 Chronicles 20 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And it came to pass, that after the year was expired, at the time that kings go out to battle, Joab led forth the power of the army, and wasted the country of the children of Ammon, and came and besieged Rabbah. But David tarried at Jerusalem. And Joab smote Rabbah, and destroyed it.

2 And David took the crown of their king from off his head, and found it to weigh a talent of gold, and there were precious stones in it; and it was set upon David’s head: and he brought also exceeding much spoil out of the city.

3 And he brought out the people that were in it, and cut them with saws, and with harrows of iron, and with axes. Even so dealt David with all the cities of the children of Ammon. And David and all the people returned to Jerusalem.

4 And it came to pass after this, that there arose war at Gezer with the Philistines; at which time Sibbechai the Hushathite slew Sippai, that was of the children of the giant: and they were subdued.

5 And there was war again with the Philistines; and Elhanan the son of Jair slew Lahmi the brother of Goliath the Gittite, whose spear staff was like a weaver’s beam.

6 And yet again there was war at Gath, where was a man of great stature, whose fingers and toes were four and twenty, six on each hand, and six on each foot and he also was the son of the giant.

7 But when he defied Israel, Jonathan the son of Shimea David’s brother slew him.

8 These were born unto the giant in Gath; and they fell by the hand of David, and by the hand of his servants.

How to read the labels

Every substantive historical claim in the editorial panels below carries one of three labels, plus a source reference:

  • Documented — directly supported by a cited source.
  • Interpreted — a reasoned explanation or proposed relationship.
  • Unresolved — evidence is insufficient to establish the claim.

A claim does NOT become Documented merely because a commentary states it. Commentary views are attributed to their authors and labeled Interpreted unless a primary textual source directly supports the claim. Where evidence runs out, the page says Unresolved and stops — it does not fill the gap.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

This chapter tells the siege and capture of Rabbah by Joab while David remained at Jerusalem, and the slaying of the giants of Gath by David's servants. Interpreted Editorial summary of this chapter’s text and study notes

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. After the year expired, at the time kings go out to battle, Joab led the army, wasted Ammon, besieged Rabbah, but David tarried at Jerusalem, and Joab smote Rabbah and destroyed it. Henry notes the parenthesis, David tarried, hints at the sin in Uriah's matter, which Chronicles deliberately does not repeat: we should not delight in repeating others' faults. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. David took their king's crown, weighing a talent of gold with precious stones, set it on his own head, and brought exceeding much spoil out of the city. Henry lists the crown and spoil among the fruits of Rabbah's destruction. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. He brought out the people and cut them with saws, harrows of iron, and axes, and so dealt with all Ammon's cities, then David and all the people returned to Jerusalem. Henry notes the great severity used toward Rabbah's people and all Ammon's cities. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. After this war arose at Gezer with the Philistines, where Sibbechai the Hushathite slew Sippai, of the giant's children, and they were subdued. Henry notes giants of Gath were last subdued, as Anak's sons were last in Joshua's conquest: some sins, like giants, keep ground long but judgment comes to victory at last. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. There was war again with the Philistines, and Elhanan son of Jair slew Lahmi brother of Goliath the Gittite, whose spear staff was like a weaver's beam. Henry notes David's servants, men of ordinary stature, were too hard for Gath's giants because they had God on their side. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. Yet again there was war at Gath, where was a man of great stature, with twenty-four fingers and toes, six on each hand and foot, also son of the giant. Henry asks what a finger more on each hand can do in contest with Omnipotence, and notes God's plants grow in usefulness, not bulk. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. When he defied Israel, Jonathan son of Shimea, David's brother, slew him. Henry notes giants who defied Israel paid for insolence: none are more visibly marked for ruin than those who reproach God and his Israel. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. These were born unto the giant in Gath, and fell by the hand of David and his servants. Henry closes the giant wars with gradual victory: as David's victories were gradual, so the Son of David's, until death itself, the last enemy, is triumphed over. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry

Connections to other passages

  • The study note for verse 4 connects this passage to the Joshua account of the conquest, in which Anak's sons were the last subdued. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
