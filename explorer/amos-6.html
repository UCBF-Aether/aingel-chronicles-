The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Amos 6 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Woe to them that are at ease in Zion, and trust in the mountain of Samaria, which are named chief of the nations, to whom the house of Israel came!

2 Pass ye unto Calneh, and see; and from thence go ye to Hamath the great: then go down to Gath of the Philistines: be they better than these kingdoms? or their border greater than your border?

3 Ye that put far away the evil day, and cause the seat of violence to come near;

4 That lie upon beds of ivory, and stretch themselves upon their couches, and eat the lambs out of the flock, and the calves out of the midst of the stall;

5 That chant to the sound of the viol, and invent to themselves instruments of musick, like David;

6 That drink wine in bowls, and anoint themselves with the chief ointments: but they are not grieved for the affliction of Joseph.

7 Therefore now shall they go captive with the first that go captive, and the banquet of them that stretched themselves shall be removed.

8 The Lord GOD hath sworn by himself, saith the LORD the God of hosts, I abhor the excellency of Jacob, and hate his palaces: therefore will I deliver up the city with all that is therein.

9 And it shall come to pass, if there remain ten men in one house, that they shall die.

10 And a man’s uncle shall take him up, and he that burneth him, to bring out the bones out of the house, and shall say unto him that is by the sides of the house, Is there yet any with thee? and he shall say, No. Then shall he say, Hold thy tongue: for we may not make mention of the name of the LORD.

11 For, behold, the LORD commandeth, and he will smite the great house with breaches, and the little house with clefts.

12 Shall horses run upon the rock? will one plow there with oxen? for ye have turned judgment into gall, and the fruit of righteousness into hemlock:

13 Ye which rejoice in a thing of nought, which say, Have we not taken to us horns by our own strength?

14 But, behold, I will raise up against you a nation, O house of Israel, saith the LORD the God of hosts; and they shall afflict you from the entering in of Hemath unto the river of the wilderness.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Woe to those at ease in Zion and trusting in the mountain of Samaria, chief of the nations, who are told to look at Calneh, Hamath, and Gath and ask whether those kingdoms were better. Their luxury is described in detail, beds of ivory, songs to the viol, bowls of wine, chief ointments, while they are not grieved for the affliction of Joseph, so they will go first into captivity. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Woe to those at ease in Zion and trusting in Samaria's mountain, named chief of the nations, to whom Israel came, ease and vain confidence in privilege being the greatest danger, especially for those at ease in Zion. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. Pass to Calneh and see, then to great Hamath, then down to Gath of the Philistines: are they better than these kingdoms, or their border greater, the examples of others' ruin forbidding security. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. You who put far away the evil day and cause the seat of violence to come near, by delaying judgment in thought while hastening oppression in deed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. You who lie on ivory beds, stretch on couches, and eat lambs from the flock and calves from the stall, luxury described as the mark of those doing well for their bodies but not for their souls. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. You who chant to the viol and invent instruments of music like David, turning worship's art into self-indulgence. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. You who drink wine in bowls and anoint with chief ointments, but are not grieved for Joseph's affliction, set upon pleasures and careless of others' troubles, a great offense to God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. Therefore now they shall go captive with the first captives, and the banquet of those stretched out shall be removed, pleasures they set their hearts on being taken away. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. The Lord God has sworn by himself, says the Lord God of hosts, that he abhors Jacob's excellency and hates his palaces, therefore he will deliver up the city with all therein, dreadful being the case of those whose ruin the Lord himself has sworn, since none can alter his purpose. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. And if ten men remain in one house, they shall die, death reaching even a remnant household. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. And a man's uncle shall take him up, and he who burns him, to bring bones out of the house, and shall ask one by the house's sides if any remain, and hearing no, shall say, hold thy tongue, for we may not mention the Lord's name, hearts so hardened they will not worship God even when sickness and death are in their families. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. For behold, the Lord commands, and he will smite the great house with breaches and the little house with clefts, great and small alike broken. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. Shall horses run upon rock, or will one plow there with oxen, for they have turned judgment into gall and righteousness' fruit into hemlock, services soured with sin making God's providences bitter in return. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. You who rejoice in a thing of nought, saying, have we not taken horns by our own strength, boasting in power that is nothing. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. But behold, God will raise up a nation against Israel, says the Lord God of hosts, and they shall afflict them from Hamath's entering to the wilderness river, pride being destroyed by the very nation God raises. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry

Unfamiliar terms

  • Samaria. the capital city of the northern kingdom of Israel, standing in the prophets for the ten tribes as Jerusalem stands for Judah. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry
