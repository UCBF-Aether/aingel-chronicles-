The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Psalms 10 (King James Version (1611) for Psalms 1–150; Charles Thomson, The Septuagint (1808) for Psalm 151)

1 Why standest thou afar off, O LORD? why hidest thou thyself in times of trouble?

2 The wicked in his pride doth persecute the poor: let them be taken in the devices that they have imagined.

3 For the wicked boasteth of his heart’s desire, and blesseth the covetous, whom the LORD abhorreth.

4 The wicked, through the pride of his countenance, will not seek after God: God is not in all his thoughts.

5 His ways are always grievous; thy judgments are far above out of his sight: as for all his enemies, he puffeth at them.

6 He hath said in his heart, I shall not be moved: for I shall never be in adversity.

7 His mouth is full of cursing and deceit and fraud: under his tongue is mischief and vanity.

8 He sitteth in the lurking places of the villages: in the secret places doth he murder the innocent: his eyes are privily set against the poor.

9 He lieth in wait secretly as a lion in his den: he lieth in wait to catch the poor: he doth catch the poor, when he draweth him into his net.

10 He croucheth, and humbleth himself, that the poor may fall by his strong ones.

11 He hath said in his heart, God hath forgotten: he hideth his face; he will never see it.

12 Arise, O LORD; O God, lift up thine hand: forget not the humble.

13 Wherefore doth the wicked contemn God? he hath said in his heart, Thou wilt not require it.

14 Thou hast seen it; for thou beholdest mischief and spite, to requite it with thy hand: the poor committeth himself unto thee; thou art the helper of the fatherless.

15 Break thou the arm of the wicked and the evil man: seek out his wickedness till thou find none.

16 The LORD is King for ever and ever: the heathen are perished out of his land.

17 LORD, thou hast heard the desire of the humble: thou wilt prepare their heart, thou wilt cause thine ear to hear:

18 To judge the fatherless and the oppressed, that the man of the earth may no more oppress.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

A hard question, why does the Lord seem far off while the proud wicked prey on the poor, boasting that God will never see, answered by prayer: God has seen, he is King for ever, and he hears the humble and will judge the fatherless and oppressed, so that human tyranny ends. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown; chapter text

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Why does the Lord stand afar off and hide himself in trouble? The psalm voices the believer's perplexity when God seems absent while the wicked prosper. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. The wicked in pride persecutes the poor; let him be taken in the devices he imagined, his own schemes becoming his snare. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. The wicked boasts of his heart's desire and blesses the covetous whom the Lord abhors: he praises the very greed God hates. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. Through pride of countenance he will not seek God; God is not in all his thoughts — practical atheism, living as if God did not see. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. His ways are always grievous, God's judgments far above out of his sight, and he puffs at all his enemies: prosperity hardens him into contempt. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. He says in his heart he shall not be moved and shall never be in adversity: security built on self-confidence is his delusion. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. His mouth is full of cursing, deceit, and fraud, with mischief and vanity under his tongue: speech betrays the heart that plots harm. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. He lurks in villages and secret places, murdering the innocent, his eyes set privily against the poor: oppression hides itself to strike safely. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 9. Like a lion in his den he lies in wait to catch the poor, drawing him into his net: the image is of patient, predatory cruelty. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. He crouches and humbles himself so the poor may fall by his strong ones: feigned lowliness is a hunting posture, not humility. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 11. He says in his heart God has forgotten, hides his face, and will never see: the wicked's theology is that God does not notice. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. Arise, O Lord, lift up your hand, and forget not the humble: the prayer answers verse 1 — the God who seems afar is called to act near. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 13. Why does the wicked contemn God, saying in his heart, You will not require it? Contempt rests on the false belief that sin goes unreckoned. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. But God has seen mischief and spite, to requite it with his hand; the poor commits himself to him, for he is the helper of the fatherless. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. Break the arm of the wicked and evil man, and seek out his wickedness till none is found: strip oppression of its power and expose it fully. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. The Lord is King for ever and ever, and the heathen perish out of his land: his reign outlasts every invader. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. The Lord has heard the desire of the humble, prepares their heart, and causes his ear to hear: God both forms the prayer and answers it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 18. He judges the fatherless and oppressed, so that the man of the earth may no more oppress: justice for the defenceless is the end of the psalm, and the limit of human tyranny. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
