The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — 1 Samuel 22 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 David therefore departed thence, and escaped to the cave Adullam: and when his brethren and all his father’s house heard it, they went down thither to him.

2 And every one that was in distress, and every one that was in debt, and every one that was discontented, gathered themselves unto him; and he became a captain over them: and there were with him about four hundred men.

3 And David went thence to Mizpeh of Moab: and he said unto the king of Moab, Let my father and my mother, I pray thee, come forth, and be with you, till I know what God will do for me.

4 And he brought them before the king of Moab: and they dwelt with him all the while that David was in the hold.

5 And the prophet Gad said unto David, Abide not in the hold; depart, and get thee into the land of Judah. Then David departed, and came into the forest of Hareth.

6 When Saul heard that David was discovered, and the men that were with him, (now Saul abode in Gibeah under a tree in Ramah, having his spear in his hand, and all his servants were standing about him;)

7 Then Saul said unto his servants that stood about him, Hear now, ye Benjamites; will the son of Jesse give every one of you fields and vineyards, and make you all captains of thousands, and captains of hundreds;

8 That all of you have conspired against me, and there is none that sheweth me that my son hath made a league with the son of Jesse, and there is none of you that is sorry for me, or sheweth unto me that my son hath stirred up my servant against me, to lie in wait, as at this day?

9 Then answered Doeg the Edomite, which was set over the servants of Saul, and said, I saw the son of Jesse coming to Nob, to Ahimelech the son of Ahitub.

10 And he enquired of the LORD for him, and gave him victuals, and gave him the sword of Goliath the Philistine.

11 Then the king sent to call Ahimelech the priest, the son of Ahitub, and all his father’s house, the priests that were in Nob: and they came all of them to the king.

12 And Saul said, Hear now, thou son of Ahitub. And he answered, Here I am, my lord.

13 And Saul said unto him, Why have ye conspired against me, thou and the son of Jesse, in that thou hast given him bread, and a sword, and hast enquired of God for him, that he should rise against me, to lie in wait, as at this day?

14 Then Ahimelech answered the king, and said, And who is so faithful among all thy servants as David, which is the king’s son in law, and goeth at thy bidding, and is honourable in thine house?

15 Did I then begin to enquire of God for him? be it far from me: let not the king impute any thing unto his servant, nor to all the house of my father: for thy servant knew nothing of all this, less or more.

16 And the king said, Thou shalt surely die, Ahimelech, thou, and all thy father’s house.

17 And the king said unto the footmen that stood about him, Turn, and slay the priests of the LORD: because their hand also is with David, and because they knew when he fled, and did not shew it to me. But the servants of the king would not put forth their hand to fall upon the priests of the LORD.

18 And the king said to Doeg, Turn thou, and fall upon the priests. And Doeg the Edomite turned, and he fell upon the priests, and slew on that day fourscore and five persons that did wear a linen ephod.

19 And Nob, the city of the priests, smote he with the edge of the sword, both men and women, children and sucklings, and oxen, and asses, and sheep, with the edge of the sword.

20 And one of the sons of Ahimelech the son of Ahitub, named Abiathar, escaped, and fled after David.

21 And Abiathar shewed David that Saul had slain the LORD’s priests.

22 And David said unto Abiathar, I knew it that day, when Doeg the Edomite was there, that he would surely tell Saul: I have occasioned the death of all the persons of thy father’s house.

23 Abide thou with me, fear not: for he that seeketh my life seeketh thy life: but with me thou shalt be in safeguard.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

David escapes to the cave of Adullam, and there gather to him every one in distress, in debt and discontented, about four hundred men, and he becomes their captain; he first secures his aged parents with the king of Moab, then, at the prophet Gad's word, leaves the hold for the forest of Hareth in Judah. Saul, sitting under a tree in Ramah with spear in hand, accuses his Benjamites of conspiracy, asking whether the son of Jesse will give them all fields, vineyards and captaincies, and Doeg the Edomite answers that he saw David come to Nob, where Ahimelech enquired of the LORD for him, gave him victuals and the sword of Goliath. Ahimelech is summoned with all his father's house and defends himself in conscious innocence, who is so faithful among thy servants as David, the king's son-in-law, but Saul pronounces death anyway; his footmen will not fall on the priests of the LORD, so Doeg turns and slays that day fourscore and five persons wearing a linen ephod, and then smites Nob itself, men, women, children, sucklings, oxen, asses and sheep. Only Abiathar escapes to David, who answers, I have occasioned the death of all the persons of thy father's house, and promises, abide thou with me, fear not, for he that seeketh my life seeketh thy life, but with me thou shalt be in safeguard. Interpreted Overview, grounded in the chapter text and its study notes

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. David departed and escaped to the cave Adullam, and when his brethren and all his father's house heard it, they went down to him there. See what weak instruments God sometimes uses to bring about his purposes, Henry notes; the Son of David is ready to receive distressed souls who will be commanded by him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 2. Every one in distress, in debt, and discontented gathered unto him, and he became captain over them, about four hundred men. He receives all who come to him, however vile and miserable, Henry says, changes them into a holy people, employs them in his service — and those who would reign with him must first be content to suffer with and for him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 3. David went to Mizpeh of Moab and said to the king of Moab, Let my father and mother come forth and be with you, till I know what God will do for me. Observe with what tender concern David provided for his aged parents, Henry notes: the first thing he does is find them a quiet habitation, whatever became of himself. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 4. He brought them before the king of Moab, and they dwelt with him all the while David was in the hold. Let children learn to honour their parents, Henry counsels, consulting their ease and satisfaction in everything, and not forgetting aged parents however highly preferred or employed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 5. The prophet Gad said to David, Abide not in the hold; depart, and get thee into the land of Judah, and David departed and came into the forest of Hareth. The steps of a good man are ordered by the Lord, Henry says, and the Lord will preserve his people for their appointed work, however hated and exposed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 6. When Saul heard that David was discovered, and the men with him — Saul abode in Gibeah under a tree in Ramah, spear in hand, all his servants standing about him. The king held court under a tree, spear in hand, a picture of armed suspicion. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 7. Saul said to his servants, Hear now, ye Benjamites; will the son of Jesse give every one of you fields and vineyards, and make you all captains of thousands and hundreds? See the nature of jealous malice and its pitiful arts, Henry says: Saul looked upon all about him as enemies because they did not just say as he said. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 8. That all of you have conspired against me, and none showeth me that my son hath made a league with the son of Jesse, and none is sorry for me, or showeth me that my son hath stirred up my servant against me, to lie in wait, as at this day? The king's self-pity accused his whole court of conspiracy. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 9. Then answered Doeg the Edomite, set over Saul's servants, I saw the son of Jesse coming to Nob, to Ahimelech son of Ahitub. The witness from the tabernacle stepped forward with his report. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 10. And he enquired of the LORD for him, and gave him victuals, and gave him the sword of Goliath the Philistine. Doeg's report, true in its facts, was framed as treason. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 11. Then the king sent to call Ahimelech son of Ahitub and all his father's house, the priests in Nob, and they came all to the king. The whole priestly house was summoned to answer one man's visit. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 12. Saul said, Hear now, thou son of Ahitub, and he answered, Here I am, my lord. The priest answered the summons with deference. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 13. Saul said, Why have ye conspired against me, thou and the son of Jesse, in giving him bread and a sword and enquiring of God for him, that he should rise against me, to lie in wait, as at this day? Saul alleged that which was utterly false and unproved, Henry notes. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 14. Ahimelech answered, Who is so faithful among all thy servants as David, the king's son-in-law, who goeth at thy bidding and is honourable in thine house? In Ahimelech's answer, Henry says, we have the language of conscious innocence. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 15. Did I then begin to enquire of God for him? Be it far from me; let not the king impute anything unto his servant, nor to all my father's house, for thy servant knew nothing of all this, less or more. The priest pleaded ignorance of any conspiracy, and his plea was true. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 16. The king said, Thou shalt surely die, Ahimelech, thou, and all thy father's house. Innocence answered, and death was pronounced anyway. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 17. The king said to the footmen about him, Turn, and slay the priests of the LORD, because their hand also is with David, and because they knew when he fled and did not show it to me. But the king's servants would not put forth their hand to fall upon the priests of the LORD. The footmen's refusal, Henry notes, showed their reverence for the priestly office and conviction of the priests' innocence. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 18. The king said to Doeg, Turn thou, and fall upon the priests, and Doeg the Edomite turned and fell upon the priests, and slew that day fourscore and five persons that did wear a linen ephod. The most bloody tyrants, Henry observes, have found instruments of cruelty as barbarous as themselves, and Doeg obeyed willingly. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 19. Nob, the city of the priests, smote he with the sword, men and women, children and sucklings, oxen, asses, and sheep. Doeg, having murdered the priests, went to Nob and put all to the sword; nothing so vile but those may do it who have provoked God to give them up to their hearts' lusts. Yet this was the accomplishment of the threatenings against Eli's house, Henry adds: though Saul was unrighteous in doing this, God was righteous in permitting it, and no word of God shall fall to the ground. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 20. One of Ahimelech's sons, named Abiathar, escaped and fled after David. A single priest survived to carry the news. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 21. Abiathar showed David that Saul had slain the LORD's priests. The tidings reached the fugitive whose visit had set the slaughter in motion. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 22. David said to Abiathar, I knew it that day, when Doeg the Edomite was there, that he would surely tell Saul: I have occasioned the death of all the persons of thy father's house. David greatly lamented the calamity, Henry notes; it is great trouble to a good man to find himself any way the cause of evil to others, and he must have been much pained that his falsehood was one cause of this fatal event. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
  • Verse 23. Abide thou with me, fear not, for he that seeketh my life seeketh thy life, but with me thou shalt be in safeguard. David spoke with assurance of his own safety and promised Abiathar his protection; with the Son of David, Henry says, all who are his may be sure they shall be in safeguard. In all his hurry and distraction, David found time for communion with God and comfort in it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry (Concise)
