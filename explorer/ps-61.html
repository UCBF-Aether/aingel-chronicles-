The Text — Psalms 61 (King James Version (1611) for Psalms 1–150; Charles Thomson, The Septuagint (1808) for Psalm 151)

1 Hear my cry, O God; attend unto my prayer.

2 From the end of the earth will I cry unto thee, when my heart is overwhelmed: lead me to the rock that is higher than I.

3 For thou hast been a shelter for me, and a strong tower from the enemy.

4 I will abide in thy tabernacle for ever: I will trust in the covert of thy wings. Selah.

5 For thou, O God, hast heard my vows: thou hast given me the heritage of those that fear thy name.

6 Thou wilt prolong the king’s life: and his years as many generations.

7 He shall abide before God for ever: O prepare mercy and truth, which may preserve him.

8 So will I sing praise unto thy name for ever, that I may daily perform my vows.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

From the end of the earth, heart overwhelmed: lead me to the rock higher than I. Past shelter and strong tower ground present prayer, abiding in God's tabernacle under wings' covert, vows heard, heritage given. The king's life is prolonged as many generations, preserved by mercy and truth, so praise is sung for ever and vows performed daily. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown; chapter text

Heading note: the text panel prints the verses only, without a heading. The king identified in verse 6 as David comes from the psalm's traditional heading as reported by the commentators, not from a verse printed above. Authorship and date remain Unresolved (see Trace). Interpreted Study notes for this chapter

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Hear my cry, O God; attend unto my prayer: hearing and attending are asked for cry and prayer together. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. From the end of the earth will I cry to you, when my heart is overwhelmed; lead me to the rock that is higher than I: from earth's end, with overwhelmed heart, he cries to be led to a rock higher than himself — refuge above his reach. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. For you have been a shelter for me, and a strong tower from the enemy: past shelter and strong tower are the grounds for present leading to the rock. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. I will abide in your tabernacle for ever; I will trust in the covert of your wings: abiding for ever in God's tabernacle, trusting in the covert of his wings — dwelling and shelter together. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. For you, O God, have heard my vows; you have given me the heritage of those who fear your name: heard vows and a heritage given — the heritage of the God-fearing is his by God's gift. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. You will prolong the king's life, and his years as many generations: the king's prolonged life, years as generations, is prayed for — the king here is David, and in the fuller sense his everlasting Son. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. He shall abide before God for ever; O prepare mercy and truth, which may preserve him: abiding before God for ever, preserved by mercy and truth prepared for him. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 8. So will I sing praise to your name for ever, that I may daily perform my vows: singing praise for ever and daily performing vows — the psalm ends in the vow-keeping it began by recalling. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
