The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Numbers 28 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 And the LORD spake unto Moses, saying,

2 Command the children of Israel, and say unto them, My offering, and my bread for my sacrifices made by fire, for a sweet savour unto me, shall ye observe to offer unto me in their due season.

3 And thou shalt say unto them, This is the offering made by fire which ye shall offer unto the LORD; two lambs of the first year without spot day by day, for a continual burnt offering.

4 The one lamb shalt thou offer in the morning, and the other lamb shalt thou offer at even;

5 And a tenth part of an ephah of flour for a meat offering, mingled with the fourth part of an hin of beaten oil.

6 It is a continual burnt offering, which was ordained in mount Sinai for a sweet savour, a sacrifice made by fire unto the LORD.

7 And the drink offering thereof shall be the fourth part of an hin for the one lamb: in the holy place shalt thou cause the strong wine to be poured unto the LORD for a drink offering.

8 And the other lamb shalt thou offer at even: as the meat offering of the morning, and as the drink offering thereof, thou shalt offer it, a sacrifice made by fire, of a sweet savour unto the LORD.

9 And on the sabbath day two lambs of the first year without spot, and two tenth deals of flour for a meat offering, mingled with oil, and the drink offering thereof:

10 This is the burnt offering of every sabbath, beside the continual burnt offering, and his drink offering.

11 And in the beginnings of your months ye shall offer a burnt offering unto the LORD; two young bullocks, and one ram, seven lambs of the first year without spot;

12 And three tenth deals of flour for a meat offering, mingled with oil, for one bullock; and two tenth deals of flour for a meat offering, mingled with oil, for one ram;

13 And a several tenth deal of flour mingled with oil for a meat offering unto one lamb; for a burnt offering of a sweet savour, a sacrifice made by fire unto the LORD.

14 And their drink offerings shall be half an hin of wine unto a bullock, and the third part of an hin unto a ram, and a fourth part of an hin unto a lamb: this is the burnt offering of every month throughout the months of the year.

15 And one kid of the goats for a sin offering unto the LORD shall be offered, beside the continual burnt offering, and his drink offering.

16 And in the fourteenth day of the first month is the passover of the LORD.

17 And in the fifteenth day of this month is the feast: seven days shall unleavened bread be eaten.

18 In the first day shall be an holy convocation; ye shall do no manner of servile work therein:

19 But ye shall offer a sacrifice made by fire for a burnt offering unto the LORD; two young bullocks, and one ram, and seven lambs of the first year: they shall be unto you without blemish:

20 And their meat offering shall be of flour mingled with oil: three tenth deals shall ye offer for a bullock, and two tenth deals for a ram;

21 A several tenth deal shalt thou offer for every lamb, throughout the seven lambs:

22 And one goat for a sin offering, to make an atonement for you.

23 Ye shall offer these beside the burnt offering in the morning, which is for a continual burnt offering.

24 After this manner ye shall offer daily, throughout the seven days, the meat of the sacrifice made by fire, of a sweet savour unto the LORD: it shall be offered beside the continual burnt offering, and his drink offering.

25 And on the seventh day ye shall have an holy convocation; ye shall do no servile work.

26 Also in the day of the firstfruits, when ye bring a new meat offering unto the LORD, after your weeks be out, ye shall have an holy convocation; ye shall do no servile work:

27 But ye shall offer the burnt offering for a sweet savour unto the LORD; two young bullocks, one ram, seven lambs of the first year;

28 And their meat offering of flour mingled with oil, three tenth deals unto one bullock, two tenth deals unto one ram,

29 A several tenth deal unto one lamb, throughout the seven lambs;

30 And one kid of the goats, to make an atonement for you.

31 Ye shall offer them beside the continual burnt offering, and his meat offering, (they shall be unto you without blemish) and their drink offerings.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

This chapter opens the calendar of regular offerings. It begins with the continual burnt offering, two lambs a day, one in the morning and one in the evening, with their meat and drink offerings, and doubles it on the sabbath. It then gives the monthly offering on the first day of each month, the passover and seven days of unleavened bread in the first month, and the offering of firstfruits at the feast of weeks, each with its burnt, sin, and meat offerings, all without blemish, beside the continual daily offering. Interpreted Editorial overview, grounded in the chapter text and its study notes

Unfamiliar terms

  • Continual burnt offering. The daily offering of two lambs, one in the morning and one in the evening, beside which all the calendar offerings in this chapter and the next are made. Interpreted Chapter text in the Scripture panel above

What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 2. Command Israel, and say to them, my offering, and my bread for my sacrifices made by fire, for a sweet savour to me, shall you observe to offer to me in their due season. JFB notes the repetition of several laws formerly enacted was seasonable and necessary, not only for their importance and frequent neglect, but because a new generation had sprung up since their first institution and Israel was about to settle in the land where the ordinances were to be observed, while my offering and my bread enforce regularity and care. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. And you shall say to them, this is the offering made by fire which you shall offer to the Lord: two lambs of the first year without spot day by day, for a continual burnt offering. The daily offering was the foundation of the whole calendar of sacrifices, two lambs every day without exception. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. The one lamb shall you offer in the morning, and the other lamb shall you offer at even. Henry notes the daily sacrifice is called a continual burnt offering, and when we are bid to pray always, at least every morning and evening we should offer up solemn prayers and praises to God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. And a tenth part of an ephah of flour for a meat offering, mingled with the fourth part of a hin of beaten oil. Each lamb carried its measure of flour and oil, so that the daily offering was not bare slaughter but bread and oil with it, the people's food offered back to God. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. It is a continual burnt offering, which was ordained in mount Sinai for a sweet savour, a sacrifice made by fire to the Lord. The daily offering reached back to Sinai, and its continuance, morning and evening without break, was the point: a sweet savour rising perpetually from the camp. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. And the drink offering thereof shall be the fourth part of a hin for the one lamb: in the holy place shall you cause the strong wine to be poured to the Lord for a drink offering. Henry notes nothing is added here but that the wine poured out is to be strong wine, to teach us to serve God with the best we have, and it was a figure of the blood of Christ, whose memorial is still left to the church in wine. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. And the other lamb shall you offer at even: as the meat offering of the morning, and as the drink offering thereof, you shall offer it, a sacrifice made by fire, of a sweet savour to the Lord. The evening lamb repeated the morning exactly, closing the day as the morning had opened it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. And on the sabbath day two lambs of the first year without spot, and two tenth deals of flour for a meat offering, mingled with oil, and the drink offering thereof. JFB notes there is no previous mention of a sabbath burnt offering, which was additional to the daily sacrifices, and Henry adds this teaches us to double our devotions on sabbath days, for so the duty of the day requires. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 10. This is the burnt offering of every sabbath, beside the continual burnt offering, and his drink offering. The sabbath offering stood beside, not instead of, the daily offering: the sabbath rest was to be observed in order to apply ourselves more closely to the sabbath work, which ought to fill up the sabbath time. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. And in the beginnings of your months you shall offer a burnt offering to the Lord: two young bullocks, and one ram, seven lambs of the first year without spot. JFB notes the new moons were held as sacred festivals, distinguished by trumpets blown over the sacrifices, suspension of labour, public worship and family feasts, the month's beginning being known by messengers watching the first visible new moon and announced by signal fires on the mountain tops. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 12. And three tenth deals of flour for a meat offering, mingled with oil, for one bullock, and two tenth deals of flour for a meat offering, mingled with oil, for one ram. The monthly offering scaled its measures to the animals: three tenth deals for a bullock, two for a ram, keeping proportion between great and small. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 14. And their drink offerings shall be half a hin of wine to a bullock, and the third part of a hin to a ram, and a fourth part of a hin to a lamb: this is the burnt offering of every month throughout the months of the year. The drink measures, like the flour measures, were fixed by the month's calendar, so that every new moon brought the same proportioned offering. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 15. And one kid of the goats for a sin offering to the Lord shall be offered, beside the continual burnt offering, and his drink offering. JFB notes the kid was offered to the Lord, not to the moon as the Egyptians and Syrians did, since the new-moon festivals, common among the heathen, were instituted in Israel to give the people's minds a better direction. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 16. And in the fourteenth day of the first month is the passover of the Lord. JFB notes the law for that great annual festival is given elsewhere, but some details are here introduced, as certain specified offerings are prescribed for each of the seven days of unleavened bread. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 17. And in the fifteenth day of this month is the feast: seven days shall unleavened bread be eaten. The passover day was followed by a seven-day feast of unleavened bread, binding remembrance of the exodus to a week of eating without leaven. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. In the first day shall be a holy convocation; you shall do no manner of servile work therein. The feast opened with a holy convocation and a halt to servile work, so that the first day belonged wholly to the Lord. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 19. But you shall offer a sacrifice made by fire for a burnt offering to the Lord: two young bullocks, and one ram, and seven lambs of the first year: they shall be to you without blemish. Each day of the feast carried the same great burnt offering, two bullocks, a ram and seven lambs, all without blemish. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 22. And one goat for a sin offering, to make an atonement for you. Beside the burnt offering each day, a goat for a sin offering made atonement, so that the feast of rejoicing never stood without repentance beside it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 23. You shall offer these beside the burnt offering in the morning, which is for a continual burnt offering. The feast offerings stood beside the morning continual offering, never replacing it, as every festival stood upon the daily foundation. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 24. After this manner you shall offer daily, throughout the seven days, the meat of the sacrifice made by fire, of a sweet savour to the Lord: it shall be offered beside the continual burnt offering, and his drink offering. The same manner daily for seven days: the feast's constancy matched the daily offering's, and Henry notes the sacrifices remind us of the continued power of Christ's sacrifice and our continual need to depend on it. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 25. And on the seventh day you shall have a holy convocation; you shall do no servile work. The feast closed as it opened, with a holy convocation and no servile work, bracketing the week in worship. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 26. Also in the day of the firstfruits, when you bring a new meat offering to the Lord, after your weeks be out, you shall have a holy convocation; you shall do no servile work. JFB notes a new sacrifice is here ordered for this festival, in addition to the other offering accompanying the firstfruits, marking the completion of the weeks after passover. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 27. But you shall offer the burnt offering for a sweet savour to the Lord: two young bullocks, one ram, seven lambs of the first year. The firstfruits burnt offering matched the passover's great offering, two bullocks, a ram and seven lambs, for the harvest's first yield. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 30. And one kid of the goats, to make an atonement for you. The firstfruits also carried its goat of atonement, so that thanksgiving for the harvest stood beside confession. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 31. You shall offer them beside the continual burnt offering, and his meat offering, (they shall be to you without blemish) and their drink offerings. The closing rule gathered the whole chapter: festival offerings beside the continual offering, all without blemish, and Henry closes that Christ is able to supply the wants of every day, every week, every month, every year, every ordinance, every case. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
