The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — Psalms 11 (King James Version (1611) for Psalms 1–150; Charles Thomson, The Septuagint (1808) for Psalm 151)

1 In the LORD put I my trust: how say ye to my soul, Flee as a bird to your mountain?

2 For, lo, the wicked bend their bow, they make ready their arrow upon the string, that they may privily shoot at the upright in heart.

3 If the foundations be destroyed, what can the righteous do?

4 The LORD is in his holy temple, the LORD’s throne is in heaven: his eyes behold, his eyelids try, the children of men.

5 The LORD trieth the righteous: but the wicked and him that loveth violence his soul hateth.

6 Upon the wicked he shall rain snares, fire and brimstone, and an horrible tempest: this shall be the portion of their cup.

7 For the righteous LORD loveth righteousness; his countenance doth behold the upright.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Refusing fearful flight: trust in the Lord holds when foundations shake, because his throne is in heaven and his eyes try all people. The righteous are tried, the violent are hated, and the upright shall see his face. Interpreted Study notes for this chapter, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown; chapter text

The name David in the verse notes (verse 1) comes from the psalm's traditional heading as reported by the commentators, not from a verse printed above. What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. In the Lord David puts his trust, and therefore he rejects the counsel to flee like a bird to the mountain: flight urged by fear would betray the trust he professes. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 2. The wicked bend the bow and ready the arrow to shoot privily at the upright in heart: persecution is secret as well as violent. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 3. If the foundations are destroyed, what can the righteous do? When law, order, and public justice seem overthrown, human help looks hopeless — which is why the next verse lifts the eye to heaven. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 4. The Lord is in his holy temple, his throne is in heaven, and his eyes behold and try the children of men: earthly foundations may shake, the heavenly throne does not. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 5. The Lord tries the righteous, but the wicked and the lover of violence his soul hates: trial of the godly is fatherly; hatred of the violent is settled. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 6. Upon the wicked he shall rain snares, fire and brimstone, and a horrible tempest — the portion of their cup: Sodom's fate is the measure of what rebellion drinks. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
  • Verse 7. For the righteous Lord loves righteousness, and his countenance beholds the upright: the godly are seen, approved, and kept by the God whose nature is just. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
