The Text — Sinodos 8 (Ge'ez text of G. Horner, The Statutes of the Apostles; or, Canones Ecclesiastici (London: Williams & Norgate, 1904), public domain)

1 The twenty-fifth canon: concerning them that have believed and are chastised[?] for the name of our Lord Jesus Christ. If he that believeth be in affliction, in the affliction of confession[?], for the name of Christ, let them not read the hand upon him for the laying on of hands[?], for this is the honour of the deacon.

2 But honour belongeth unto the priesthood; and this is the honour of the priesthood: for him that believeth, if he die, let one priest [?] read his hand upon him; but if he that believeth live …[breaks off].

3 …who cometh not before the rulers[?], and if he was not chastised in affliction [fragment].

4 And he was not shut up in the house of affliction, and entered not into tribulation, but was set apart[?] alone for the name of his Lord; and he was not chastised, neither with light chastisement[?]; and let all acknowledge[?] the work of the priesthood: as he willeth, let them read the hand upon him.

5 And let them make him a deacon, and let one priest present him[?], as we commanded before — we have spoken. And do according to that which we commanded before, as we have spoken, that he may be examined[?] openly, with observance[?], and let them present him[?] unto God, as they will. Let them pray [?]; and if there be one that is able, let him pray [?] … or the prayer of the great and the exalted[?]; and he is approved[?]. And if he hath prayed and said "Glory" [?] … as he prayeth; and he is right in faith.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Canon 25 concerns believers chastised, or confessing, for the name of Christ: the chapter rules when the hand is and is not read upon such a person for ordination, and honours the priesthood by providing that a believer who dies shall have one priest read his hand upon him. It distinguishes the confessor who was shut up and afflicted from one set apart alone for his Lord's name without chastisement, and provides that such a man may be made a deacon and presented to God after open examination. The chapter is short and breaks off mid-sentence in places. Interpreted Summary of this chapter's text, as printed above
