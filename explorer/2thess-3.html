The Text

Scripture — not editorial explanation

The Text — 2 Thessalonians 3 (King James Version (1611); public-domain text as received (Project Gutenberg no. 10))

1 Finally, brethren, pray for us, that the word of the Lord may have free course, and be glorified, even as it is with you:

2 And that we may be delivered from unreasonable and wicked men: for all men have not faith.

3 But the Lord is faithful, who shall stablish you, and keep you from evil.

4 And we have confidence in the Lord touching you, that ye both do and will do the things which we command you.

5 And the Lord direct your hearts into the love of God, and into the patient waiting for Christ.

6 Now we command you, brethren, in the name of our Lord Jesus Christ, that ye withdraw yourselves from every brother that walketh disorderly, and not after the tradition which he received of us.

7 For yourselves know how ye ought to follow us: for we behaved not ourselves disorderly among you;

8 Neither did we eat any man’s bread for nought; but wrought with labour and travail night and day, that we might not be chargeable to any of you:

9 Not because we have not power, but to make ourselves an ensample unto you to follow us.

10 For even when we were with you, this we commanded you, that if any would not work, neither should he eat.

11 For we hear that there are some which walk among you disorderly, working not at all, but are busybodies.

12 Now them that are such we command and exhort by our Lord Jesus Christ, that with quietness they work, and eat their own bread.

13 But ye, brethren, be not weary in well doing.

14 And if any man obey not our word by this epistle, note that man, and have no company with him, that he may be ashamed.

15 Yet count him not as an enemy, but admonish him as a brother.

16 Now the Lord of peace himself give you peace always by all means. The Lord be with you all.

17 The salutation of Paul with mine own hand, which is the token in every epistle: so I write.

18 The grace of our Lord Jesus Christ be with you all. Amen.

Understand (editorial explanation — not Scripture)

Overview

Paul asks prayer that the gospel will run freely and that he will be delivered from wicked and unreasonable people, confident the Lord will establish the readers. Then comes a firm command: withdraw from any brother walking idly, following Paul's own example of working night and day rather than eating bread for nothing, and if anyone disobeys, note him without treating him as an enemy, but warn him as a brother. What the chapter says, verse by verse

The notes below are the site’s study-note summaries of the public-domain commentators named. They are paraphrase summaries, not quotations from those commentators.

  • Verse 1. Paul asks their prayers that the word of the Lord may have free course and be glorified among others as it was among them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 2. He also asks deliverance from unreasonable and wicked men, for all men have not faith, and unbelief often turns restless and malicious. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 3. But the Lord is faithful, and will establish them and keep them from evil, so their safety rests on his faithfulness, not their own. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 4. Paul's confidence that they do and will do what he commands is founded on his confidence in God, for otherwise there is no confidence in man. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 5. He prays the Lord to direct their hearts into the love of God and into the patient waiting for Christ, the two affections that keep duty steady. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 6. In Christ's name he commands them to withdraw from every brother who walks disorderly and not after the tradition received from the apostles. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 7. They know how they ought to follow the apostles, who did not behave disorderly among them but lived by the pattern they preached. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 8. Paul ate no man's bread for nothing but worked night and day, so as not to be chargeable to any of them. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 9. This was not for lack of right to support, but to make himself an example for them to follow in honest labour. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 10. Even while with them he commanded that if any would not work, neither should he eat, since the gospel does not countenance sloth. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 11. He hears that some walk disorderly, working not at all but acting as busybodies, idleness breeding meddling in other people's affairs. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 12. Such persons he commands and exhorts by Christ to work quietly and eat their own bread, restoring industry to its proper dignity. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 13. The rest must not grow weary in well doing because others are disorderly, for the fault of a few does not cancel the duty of all. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 14. If any man obeys not the word of this epistle, they are to note him and have no company with him, that he may be ashamed. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 15. Yet he must not be counted as an enemy but admonished as a brother, since the aim of withdrawing is shame leading to reform, not hatred. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 16. The Lord of peace himself is asked to give them peace always by all means, and to be with them all, since peace is his own gift. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 17. Paul's own handwritten salutation is the token in every epistle, a guard against counterfeit letters, which he knew would be dangerous. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry
  • Verse 18. The epistle closes with grace, for it is through the grace of the Lord Jesus Christ that believers may hope for peace with God and his presence. Interpreted Study note, summarizing Matthew Henry

Unfamiliar terms

  • disorderly. idle and unruly, here walking without working and busy in others’ affairs rather than one’s own (verse 11) Interpreted Editorial gloss on the Text panel above
