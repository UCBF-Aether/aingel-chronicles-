The Prophets

Ezekiel and the Wheels of Light

From the Book of Ezekiel, chapters 1 and 10

Section: The Prophets

Ezekiel was a priest of God living far from home, in the land of Babylon, beside a wide, slow river called Chebar. God's people were captives there. The temple was far away, and many wondered: *Has God stayed behind in Jerusalem? Can He see us here?*

One day, by the river, the heavens opened — and Ezekiel saw the most astonishing sight in all the Bible.

First came a great storm cloud, flashing with fire, glowing like polished gold. And out of the cloud came four living creatures — the mighty cherubim who carry God's throne. Each one had four faces: the face of a man, the face of a lion, the face of an ox, and the face of an eagle. Each one had four wings, and beneath their wings were hands like ours. Their feet sparkled like polished brass, and they moved like lightning — flash! — faster than eyes could follow.

Beside each creature stood a wheel — and what wheels they were! A wheel inside a wheel, turning every way at once without ever stopping to turn around. The rims of the wheels were covered all over with eyes, and the wheels glowed like golden topaz stone. Wherever the creatures went, the wheels went too, for the spirit of the creatures was in the wheels.

Above the creatures stretched a shining expanse, clear as crystal, terrible and beautiful. And above the crystal — a throne, blue as sapphire. And on the throne, surrounded by brightness like a rainbow in the rain, sat the likeness of the glory of the LORD.

Ezekiel fell on his face. He could do nothing else.

But here is the wonder inside the wonder: this throne — God's own throne, flashing and flying and full of eyes — had come to Babylon. To a riverbank. To captives. God had not stayed behind in Jerusalem. He had come to be with His people in the far country, and His throne could go anywhere in the whole world, because the King of heaven needs no road.

Years later Ezekiel saw the cherubim again, and he understood: God's throne was still moving, still watching, still near.

There is nowhere you can go — no riverbank, no far country — where God's throne cannot come to you.
