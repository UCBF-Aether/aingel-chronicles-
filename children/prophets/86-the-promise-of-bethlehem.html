The Prophets

The Promise of Bethlehem

From the Book of Micah, chapter 5

Section: The Prophets

Long, long ago — hundreds of years before it happened — God gave Micah a promise about a little town.

The town was called Bethlehem Ephratah. It was tiny. Among all the towns of Judah, it barely counted at all — a few shepherds' houses, some sheep on the hills, an ordinary little place that nobody important ever visited.

But God chose it. God loves to choose the little places.

"But you, Bethlehem Ephratah," God said, "though you are little among the thousands of Judah — out of YOU will come the One who will rule My people Israel. His goings forth are from of old, from everlasting."

From that little town would come a King unlike any other king. He would not be a king who takes. He would be a King who feeds — like a shepherd. "He shall stand and shepherd His flock in the strength of the LORD. And His people shall live safely, for He shall be great to the ends of the earth. And this One — this One shall be our peace."

A Shepherd-King. From the littlest town. Whose kingdom would stretch to the ends of the earth, and whose gift would be peace.

Micah told the people, and the people held onto the promise the way you hold onto a lamp in a dark year. Empires rose and fell. Kings came and went. Bethlehem stayed small and quiet, with its sheep on the hills, for century after century after century.

But God never forgets a promise — not for a hundred years, not for five hundred. And one night, long afterwards, shepherds on those very hills would look up and see the sky explode with angels — and run down into that same little town to find the Promise, wrapped in cloths, lying in a manger.

But that part of the story comes later. First came the promise — spoken into the dark, about a little town, by a God who always, always keeps His word.

God keeps His promises — even when they take hundreds of years, and even when they begin in the littlest town.
