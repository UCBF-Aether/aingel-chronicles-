The Prophets

Zechariah's Night Pictures

From the Book of Zechariah, chapters 1–6

Section: The Prophets

When God's people came home to Jerusalem after their long years far away, the city was still broken and the Temple still in ruins. Then God gave a prophet named Zechariah a gift: in one night, He showed him eight pictures — like dreams, only true.

In the first picture, Zechariah saw a man on a red horse, standing among sweet-smelling myrtle trees in a valley, with red and speckled and white horses behind him. "These are the riders God sends to walk through all the earth," said the angel. The riders' report was good: the whole earth was quiet. Then the angel prayed for Jerusalem — and God answered with kind words: "I am returning to Jerusalem with mercy. My house will be built again, and my cities will overflow with good things."

Next came four horns — the powers that had scattered God's people — and four craftsmen coming to throw those horns down.

Next, a man with a measuring line, setting out to measure Jerusalem. "Don't bother," said the angel. "Jerusalem will grow so big it won't need walls — because I myself will be a wall of fire around her," said God, "and the glory in her middle."

Next, Zechariah saw Joshua the high priest standing in filthy clothes — and God ordered: "Take the dirty clothes away!" They dressed him in clean, rich clothes and set a shining mitre on his head. "See," said God, "I have taken your sin away." And God promised that one day He would send His Servant, the Branch, and take away the sin of the whole land in a single day.

Next came a lampstand of pure gold with seven lamps, fed with oil by two olive trees growing beside it. "What does it mean?" asked Zechariah. "This," said the angel: "Not by might, and not by power, but by my Spirit, says the LORD." Little by little, God's work would be finished — and no mountain could stand in its way.

Then a scroll came flying through the sky, carrying God's warning against stealing and lying. Then a basket with a heavy lead lid, carrying wickedness far away — two women with wings like a stork's flew it off!

Last came four chariots rolling out from between two mountains of bronze — red horses, black horses, white horses, dappled horses — God's spirits going out to every corner of the earth.

And the night ended with crowns of silver and gold, and a promise: a King is coming to build God's house and rule in peace.

God showed Zechariah the whole future like picture-pages — and every picture said the same thing: I have not forgotten you.
