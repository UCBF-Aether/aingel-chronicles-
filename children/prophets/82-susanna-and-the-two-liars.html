The Prophets

Susanna and the Two Liars

From the Book of Daniel, chapter 13

Section: The Prophets

In Babylon there lived a good and kind woman named Susanna. She loved God, she loved her family, and her house had a beautiful garden where she walked every day.

But two wicked old judges had begun telling lies about her — terrible lies, whispered to everyone. The whole town believed them, because the men were important and sounded so certain. Susanna was in deep trouble, and no one would listen to her side at all.

But one person knew the truth: God. And Susanna cried out to Him with a loud voice, weeping: "O everlasting God, You know all secrets. You know everything before it happens. You know these men have told lies about me!"

And God heard her. God always hears.

At that very moment, a young boy named Daniel — yes, Daniel, the same one, when he was just a youth — was stirred up by God's Spirit in the middle of the crowd. He shouted: "Stop! I will not be part of this. This woman has not been fairly heard!"

The people froze. "Young man," said the elders, "come and sit with us, and explain what you mean."

Daniel said, "Separate the two judges — far apart from each other — and I will question them one at a time."

He called the first old man. "You say you saw wrongdoing in the garden. Under what tree was it?"

"A mastick tree," said the first man quickly.

Then Daniel called the second old man — who had heard nothing of the first answer. "Under what tree was it?"

"A holm tree," said the second man.

Two trees. Two different stories. The lie fell apart like a rotten rope! The whole crowd saw it at once: the men had invented everything. Their own mouths had caught them.

The crowd shouted for joy. The two liars were punished exactly as liars deserve — with the very penalty they had plotted for an innocent woman — and Susanna was free, unharmed, her name clean, hugging her family.

From that day on, everyone respected young Daniel greatly. All because God heard a woman's prayer — and a boy was brave enough to ask one careful question.

God hears the truth even when no one else will listen — and one honest question can break a lie.
