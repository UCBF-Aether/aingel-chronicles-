The Prophets

Malachi and the Messenger Coming

From the Book of Malachi, chapters 3–4

Section: The Prophets

Malachi was the last prophet of the old part of the Bible. After him, God's people would wait a long, quiet time for the next page of the story. And Malachi's message was mostly a promise: **Someone is coming.**

"I will send my messenger," said God, "and he will prepare the way before me. Then the Lord you are looking for will suddenly come to His house."

When the Lord comes, said Malachi, He will be like a refiner's fire. A refiner is a silversmith who heats silver in the fire — not to destroy it, but to melt away everything that isn't silver, until the metal shines clean and bright and the smith can see his own face in it. That is what God's coming would do for His people: burn away the wrong, and leave the shining.

And in the meantime? "Bring the whole tithe into my storehouse," said God — bring Me your proper gifts — "and test me in this: see if I will not throw open the windows of heaven and pour out so much blessing on you that there won't be room to hold it!"

Then Malachi told them something tender. God listens. When the people who loved God talked together about Him, God heard every word — and He wrote their names in a special book, His book of remembrance.

"They will be mine," said God, "on the day when I make up my jewels. I will spare them, the way a father spares his own son who serves him."

And at the very end, Malachi pointed forward twice.

First: "For you who honour my name, the Sun of righteousness will rise, with healing in its wings — and you will go out leaping like calves let out of the stall!" Picture a calf that has been shut in a barn all winter, finally let out into the spring sunshine — kicking its legs, dancing on the grass. That is what God's sunrise will feel like.

Second: "I will send you Elijah the prophet, before the great day of the LORD comes — and he will turn the hearts of the fathers to their children, and the hearts of the children to their fathers."

Then Malachi's book ended. The old part of the Bible closed its last page — facing forward, watching the road, waiting for the messenger, and the Sunrise, and the King.

The very last promise of the old book is a sunrise: healing in its wings, and children dancing like calves in the spring.
