The Prophets

The Day God Sings

From the Book of Zephaniah, chapter 3

Section: The Prophets

Zephaniah's little book ends with the happiest surprise in all the prophets.

The city of Jerusalem had been in trouble — the people had stopped listening to God, and hard days had come. But God had a plan for the end of the story, and the plan was joy.

First, God promised to gather His scattered people from everywhere — even, He said, "from beyond the rivers of Ethiopia" His worshippers would come bringing their gifts. He would give all the peoples one pure language, so that everyone could call on His name together, shoulder to shoulder, serving Him with one heart.

The proud would be gone. In their place, God would leave a humble, gentle people — people who trust in His name, who do no wrong and tell no lies, who can rest and eat in peace with nobody making them afraid.

And then Zephaniah turned to the city and shouted the most wonderful command: "Sing, daughter of Zion! Shout, Israel! Be glad and rejoice with all your heart, daughter of Jerusalem! The LORD has taken away your punishment. He has turned back your enemy. The LORD, the King of Israel, is right here in your midst — you will never fear harm again. Do not be afraid, Zion. Do not let your hands hang limp. Get up — it's singing time!"

But why should the city sing? Zephaniah saved the best reason for last. Listen to this — it may be the most astonishing verse in the whole Bible:

"The LORD your God is in your midst, mighty to save. He will rejoice over you with gladness. He will quiet you with His love. He will sing over you — with shouts of joy!"

Did you hear it? Not just *we* sing to God. God sings over *us*. The mighty King of heaven looks at His people — rescued, gathered, home — and His joy gets so big that it bursts into song. A lullaby and a victory shout at the same time: "I will bring you home," He says, "and I will gather you, and your name will be praised in every land where you were once put to shame."

That is how Zephaniah's book ends — not with the sound of weeping, but with the sound of singing. Ours — and God's.

God sings over His people with joy. Let your heart sing back to Him.
