The Prophets

The Suffering Servant

From the Book of Isaiah, chapters 52–53

Section: The Prophets

Long before Jesus was born, God gave His prophet Isaiah a song about someone who was coming — a Servant of God, the most special servant of all.

The song says the Servant would grow up quietly, like a little green plant growing out of dry ground. He would not look like a king. There would be nothing grand about the way He looked, nothing that made people stop and stare. Many people would not want Him at all.

Isaiah sang that this Servant would be a man who knew sorrow. He would carry other people's griefs. He would carry other people's sadnesses, the way a strong friend carries a heavy load for someone whose arms are tired. He would be hurt because of the wrong things *we* had done — and by His hurting, we would be healed.

"All of us, like sheep, have wandered away," the song says. Sheep are like that. They nibble a little grass here, and a little grass there, and suddenly they look up and they are lost. Every one of us has wandered off and gone our own way. And God, said Isaiah, would lay all our wandering on His Servant instead of on us.

The song says the Servant would not shout or fight back. Like a lamb led along quietly, like a sheep that makes no sound at all, He would open not His mouth. There was never a lie in His mouth, and He never hurt anyone — and still He would be treated like a criminal, and His grave would be made with the wicked, and with a rich man too, just as the song says.

But the song does not end in the grave. God promised that after all the sorrow, His Servant would see life again, and be glad. He would see children and days stretching out before Him. And because He carried the wrongs of so many people, God would give Him a place among the great ones — the greatest place of all.

When Jesus came, God's people read Isaiah's old song again — and their hearts beat fast. The quiet Servant, the sorrow-carrier, the lamb who never opened His mouth — it was Jesus. The song had been about Him all along.

Thank You, God, for the Servant who carried our sorrows — so that we could be healed, and come home.
