The Prophets

Jeremiah's Friend and the Fig Basket

From the Book of the Rest of the Words of Baruch, chapters 1–7

Section: The Prophets

When trouble came to Jerusalem, Jeremiah the prophet had a faithful friend named Abimelech. Abimelech was from the land of Ethiopia, and he was brave and kind — once, when Jeremiah was stuck in a deep, muddy pit, it was Abimelech who came with ropes and old soft cloths and pulled him out gently, so the ropes would not hurt him.

Now the city was about to fall, and God did something tender. He spoke to Jeremiah: "Send Abimelech away on an errand, so that he will not have to see the city fall." So Jeremiah sent his friend by the mountain road to a vineyard, to gather figs for a man who was sick.

It was the middle of the day, and the sun was hot, and Abimelech was tired. He found a shady tree, set down his basket of fresh figs — figs so fresh that sweet drops were still on them — leaned his head upon the basket, and fell fast asleep.

And he slept.

And slept.

And slept — for sixty-six years!

Kings rose and fell. The city was broken and built again. Children grew up and had children. And all that time, Abimelech slept peacefully under the tree, kept safe by God, while his basket of figs stayed fresh beside him, as if he had picked them that very morning.

At last he woke up. He rubbed his eyes. "I slept sweetly," he said, "but I have a headache — I must have slept a little too long." He looked at the figs — still fresh, still dripping! "Strange," he said. "I filled this basket at midday, and it is midday still."

Then he met an old, old man in the fields, and the old man told him the astonishing truth: "You have slept for sixty-six years. The city fell long ago. And now God's people are coming home."

Abimelech hurried to find Baruch, who was waiting in a quiet tomb, keeping watch for God's promises. Baruch was overjoyed — and he wrote a letter to Jeremiah, far away, and tied the letter and fifteen of the fresh figs to a great eagle. The eagle spread its mighty wings and carried the figs — still fresh after sixty-six years! — across the sky, so that Jeremiah and all the people would know: God keeps His friends, and God keeps His word, and it is time to come home.

God can keep you safe through a sleep of sixty-six years — and wake you up right on time.
