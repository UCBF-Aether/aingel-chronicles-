The Prophets

Jonah and the Little Plant

From the Book of Jonah, chapter 4

Section: The Prophets

But Jonah was not glad that God had forgiven Nineveh. He was angry — very angry. He had wanted the city to be punished, and now God had let everyone go free.

"God," Jonah cried, "I knew it! I knew You are kind and slow to anger, and quick to forgive! That is why I ran away in the first place! I would rather die than watch this!"

But God only asked him gently, "Jonah, is it right for you to be angry about this?"

Jonah did not answer. He stomped out of the city and sat down on the east side, where he could look back at Nineveh. He built himself a little shelter of branches and sat under it in the shade, waiting to see what would happen to the city.

Then God did a kind thing for His angry prophet. God made a little plant grow up beside Jonah — up, up, up it climbed, until its wide leaves made a cool shadow over Jonah's head. Jonah was so glad for the plant! It was the best thing that had happened in days.

But when morning came, God sent a little worm. The worm nibbled and nibbled at the plant — and the plant withered and fell down.

Then the sun came up, hot and beating, and a dry east wind blew dust in Jonah's face. Jonah felt faint in the heat, with no shade at all. "I would rather die than bear this!" he cried.

And God asked him again, gently, "Jonah, is it right for you to be angry about the plant?"

"Yes!" said Jonah. "I am angry enough to die!"

Then God said, "Jonah, you felt sorry for one little plant. You did not plant it. You did not make it grow. It came up in one night, and it was gone the next morning — and still you loved it. So should I not feel sorry for Nineveh, this great city, where there are more than a hundred and twenty thousand people who do not yet know right from wrong — and also many cattle?"

Jonah had no answer to that.

Thank You, God, for loving even the people we find hardest to love.
