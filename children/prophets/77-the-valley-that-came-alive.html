The Valley That Came Alive

From the Book of Ezekiel, chapter 37

Section: The Prophets

God took Ezekiel, in a vision, and set him down in the middle of a wide valley. And the valley was full of bones — very many bones, and very dry. They covered the ground as far as he could see.

Then God asked him a question: "Son of man, can these bones live?"

Ezekiel looked at the valley. He looked at the bones — dry, white, lifeless. It seemed impossible. But he knew his God, so he answered the wisest answer in the world: "O Lord God — You know."

"Prophesy to the bones," said God. "Tell them: Dry bones, hear the word of the LORD! I will bring breath into you, and you shall live."

So Ezekiel spoke God's words over the valley. And suddenly — a noise! A rattling! Clickety-clack, clatter-clatter — the bones began to move! Bone rushed to bone, each one finding its own. Skeletons stood assembled like puzzle pictures snapped together. Then muscles grew on them, and flesh, and skin covered them — whole people, lying still across the valley. But there was no breath in them yet.

"Prophesy to the wind," said God. "Call the breath from the four winds: Come, Breath, and breathe on these, that they may live!"

So Ezekiel called — and the breath came! Chest after chest rose and fell. Eyes opened. And all at once, the whole valley stood up on its feet — a great, great army of living people, blinking in the sunlight.

Then God explained the picture. His people, far from home, were saying, "Our bones are dried up. Our hope is gone." They felt finished — like the valley. But God said: "I will open your graves and bring you up. I will put My Spirit inside you, and you shall live. And I will bring you home to your own land."

The dry valley was never about bones. It was about hope — God's promise that nothing He loves is ever finished while He is still speaking.

When everything feels dry and finished, remember: God can make a whole valley stand up and breathe.
