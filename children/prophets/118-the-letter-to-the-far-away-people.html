The Prophets

The Letter to the Far-Away People

From the Book of Jeremiah, chapter 29

Section: The Prophets

Far away in Babylon, God's people were living in a land that was not their home. An enemy king had carried them there, and their hearts ached for Jerusalem. How do you live in a far-away place when your heart is somewhere else?

Then a letter arrived — a long letter from the prophet Jeremiah, carried all the way from Jerusalem by two messengers. And the letter was not from Jeremiah only. It was from God.

"Build houses," said the letter, "and live in them. Plant gardens, and eat what grows. Let your sons and daughters marry and have children, and watch your families grow bigger, not smaller. And work for the good of the city where you are living, and pray to Me for it — because when the city does well, you will do well too."

Some people were telling the exiles not to bother — whispering that this would all be over in no time, that there was no need to plant or build or pray. "Do not listen to them," said the letter. "They are telling you dreams, not My words."

Then came the promise. "When seventy years have passed in Babylon, I will come to you, and I will keep My good promise, and I will bring you back to your own place."

And then the letter said the thing God's people have held onto ever since, in every far-away place on earth:

"I know the plans I have for you," says the LORD — "plans of peace, and not of trouble — plans to give you hope, and a future."

"And you will call on Me, and pray to Me, and I will listen to you. You will look for Me, and you will find Me — when you look for Me with all your heart. I will be found by you," says the LORD, "and I will bring you home."

The letter had to travel a long, long way to reach the homesick people it was written for. But it arrived. And God's people planted their gardens in Babylon, and prayed for their city, and waited for the God whose plans for them were peace — then, and always.

Thank You, God, that wherever Your people are, Your plans for them are peace — and You always bring them home.
