The Prophets

Hosea's Family, God's Heart

From the Book of Hosea, chapters 1–3 and 11

Section: The Prophets

God gave Hosea a family — and through Hosea's family, God showed His people the story of His own heart.

Hosea loved his wife with all his heart. But she ran away from home. She forgot the one who loved her, who fed her and cared for her and gave her every good thing. She went chasing after others, thinking they gave her bread and water and oil — when all along, every good gift had come from the husband who loved her.

Hosea's heart was broken. And God said: now you understand My heart — for My people have run away from Me in just the same way.

Their children were given sorrowful names, names like a message: one was called *No Mercy*. One was called *Not My People*. That is how far away the family had wandered.

But Hosea did not stop loving. He went after his wife. He paid silver and barley to bring her home, and said to her: "Stay with me now. Come home — and be mine, and I will be yours." And she came home. The door of hope opened in the middle of the valley of trouble, and she sang there, as she had sung when she was young and free.

And then God told Hosea the ending of the bigger story — of God and His people — the tenderest part of the whole book. God remembered His people when they were little:

"When Israel was a child, I loved him. I taught him to walk, holding him by the hands. I healed him. I drew him to Me with gentle cords, with bands of love. I bent down to him and fed him."

Yes, they had run away. Yes, they had forgotten. But God's heart turned over inside Him, and He cried out: "How can I give you up? How can I hand you over? My heart recoils — My compassion is kindled, all of it, like a fire. I will not let anger have the last word. For I am God, not a man."

And here is God's promise for the end of the story: the sad names will be changed! *No Mercy* will be overflowed with mercy. *Not My People* will hear the sweetest words: "You are My people." And God's children will come trembling home — like a bird fluttering back from faraway Egypt, like a dove coming home from a distant land — and God Himself will settle them safely in their houses.

That is God's heart. It bends down. It goes after. It brings home. And it never stops loving.

God's heart bends down to you and will never stop bringing you home.
