The Prophets

Daniel the Detective and the Dragon

From the Book of Daniel, chapter 14

Section: The Prophets

Daniel was the king's friend, and the wisest man in Babylon. But people there worshipped a statue called Bel — and daily they set out a feast for it: flour, forty sheep, and jars of wine. By morning, every crumb was gone.

"See?" said the king. "Bel eats it all. He must be a living god!"

Daniel smiled. "O king, don't be fooled. That statue is only clay and bronze. It has never eaten."

The priests of Bel were furious. "Tomorrow, if the food is gone, Daniel dies! If it isn't, we die!" They sealed the temple doors with the king's seal.

But Daniel had a detective's idea. Before the doors shut, his servants sprinkled ashes on the floor — where only the king could see, unknown to the priests.

That night, the priests came through their secret door under the table — with wives and children — and ate the whole feast, as they always had.

In the morning, the king opened the doors. The food was gone! "Great is Bel!" he cried.

But Daniel laughed and held him back. "Wait, O king. Look at the floor. Whose footprints are these?"

There in the ashes were footprints — big, small, men's, women's, children's — leading to a hidden door under the table. Mystery solved! The angry king gave Bel to Daniel, who broke it. A statue that cannot eat is no god.

Then the king pointed to a dragon people worshipped. "At least this one is alive. It eats and drinks!"

"I will only worship the living God," said Daniel. "But give me permission, king, and I'll show you the truth about this dragon — without a sword." He made cakes of pitch and fat and hair, and fed them to the dragon — and the greedy dragon gobbled them and burst. "See," said Daniel, "these are the gods you worshipped."

Some wicked men threw Daniel into a pit of seven lions — left there six days. But God had not forgotten him. Far away, a prophet, Habbacuc, carrying dinner to workers when an angel swept him up by his hair and flew him, WHOOSH, to Babylon — dinner and all — and set him at the lions' pit!

"Daniel! Daniel!" cried Habbacuc. "Take the dinner God sent you!"

Daniel ate, safe among the lions, and thanked God: "You remembered me, O God. You never leave the people who love You."

On the seventh day the king came to the pit — and there was Daniel, calm among the lions. "Great is the God of Daniel!" he shouted — and pulled Daniel out.

God remembers His friends — even in a pit of lions, you are never forgotten.
