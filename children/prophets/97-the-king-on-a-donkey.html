The Prophets

The King on a Donkey

From the Book of Zechariah, chapter 9

Section: The Prophets

Hundreds of years before Jesus was born, the prophet Zechariah sang a song about a King who was coming. And it was a very strange song — because this King would not come the way other kings come.

Other kings ride tall war-horses, with spears and chariots and armies marching behind them. They come to conquer. They come to make people afraid.

Not this King.

"Rejoice greatly, daughter of Zion!" sang Zechariah. "Shout, daughter of Jerusalem! Look — your King is coming to you!"

He is just, and gentle, and He brings salvation with Him. And how does He ride into His city?

On a donkey. On a young donkey — a colt, the foal of a donkey.

Zechariah sang more: this King's rule would stop the wars. God would cut off the war-chariots and the battle-bows, and the King would speak peace — peace to the nations, peace from sea to sea, peace to the very ends of the earth.

He would set prisoners free. He would save His people the way a shepherd saves his flock. His people would shine like jewels in a crown, lifted up where everyone could see them.

"How great is His goodness!" sang Zechariah. "How great is His beauty!"

So God's people waited — year after year, king after king, generation after generation — for the gentle King on the donkey. The little children who first heard the song grew old, and their children grew old, and their children's children grew old.

But the song did not grow old. The song waited too.

And one spring day, hundreds of years later, a man came riding down the Mount of Olives toward Jerusalem — on a young donkey — and the crowds began to shout. But that is another story in this book, and it is one of the best ones.

The King God promised did not come to frighten the world — He came riding gently, bringing peace.
