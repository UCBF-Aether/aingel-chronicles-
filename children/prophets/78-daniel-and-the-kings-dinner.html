Daniel and the King's Dinner

From the Book of Daniel, chapter 1

Section: The Prophets

When Daniel was a young man, he and his three best friends — Hananiah, Mishael, and Azariah — were carried far away to Babylon, to serve in the palace of the king.

The king had a plan. He would feed the young men from his own table — rich meats, fancy dishes, and wine — and teach them the ways of Babylon, until they thought and lived like Babylonians. He even gave them new Babylonian names: Belteshazzar, Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego.

But deep in his heart, Daniel made a brave, quiet decision. The king's food had been offered to idols, and Daniel would not eat it. He would obey God first — even at the king's table, even far from home.

He did not shout or stamp. He asked politely. "Please," he said to the king's steward, "do not make us eat what would defile us."

The steward was worried. "If the king sees you looking thin and pale, it will cost me my head!"

So Daniel proposed a test — a ten-day experiment. "Give us only vegetables to eat and water to drink, for ten days. Then look at us, and look at the young men who eat the king's food — and decide."

The steward agreed. For ten days, Daniel and his friends ate simple vegetables and drank clear water. And when the ten days were over — wonder of wonders — the four friends looked healthier and stronger than all the young men who had feasted on the king's rich dinners!

So the steward let them keep their simple food. And God gave the four friends something no dinner could give: knowledge, skill, and understanding. Daniel could even understand visions and dreams. When the king examined all the young men at the end of their training, he found no one like Daniel, Hananiah, Mishael, and Azariah. In everything he asked them, they were ten times wiser than all the king's wise men.

New names could not change who they were. A new country could not change whom they obeyed. Four young men, a plate of vegetables, and a heart that decided early — that was enough for God to work with.

Decide in your heart early, like Daniel — and God will stand with your decision.
