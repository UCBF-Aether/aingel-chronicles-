The Prophets

Grandfather Sirach's Best Sayings

From the Book of Sirach, chapters 1, 6, 24 and 44

Section: The Prophets

Sirach was a wise old grandfather in Jerusalem, and he wrote down his best sayings so grandchildren — including you — could read them forever. Listen to some of his treasures.

**All wisdom comes from the Lord.** "Who can count the sand of the sea?" asked Sirach. "Who can count the drops of rain? Nobody! And God's wisdom is bigger than all of it." God made Wisdom before everything, then poured her out on everything He had made. And wisdom's first step is to honour and trust the Lord with all your heart.

**A faithful friend is a treasure.** "A faithful friend is a strong shelter," said Sirach. "Whoever finds one has found a treasure. Nothing is worth as much as a faithful friend — he is the medicine of life." So Sirach warned: when you make a new friend, test him first, and don't trust too quickly — some friends only stay while the sun shines. But a true friend? Hold on to him forever.

**Hunt for Wisdom like a farmer, and like a guest.** Sirach said: go after Wisdom the way a farmer ploughs and sows his field — work at her a little, and soon eat her good fruit. At first she may feel heavy, like a stone you have to carry; but stay with her, and her chains will turn to a robe of glory, and she herself to a crown of joy. "Stand among the elders," said Grandfather Sirach. "Listen to every wise word. If you see a man of understanding, go to him early — wear out his doorsteps with your feet!"

**Wisdom speaks for herself.** In Sirach's book, Wisdom herself stands up and tells her story: "I came out of the mouth of the Most High. I searched everywhere for rest — and the Creator said to me: 'Make your home in Jacob. Settle down in Israel.' So I took root in God's people, and grew like a cedar of Lebanon, like a palm tree, like a rose in Jericho, like a beautiful olive in the field. Come to me, all who long for me, and fill yourselves with my fruits — for remembering me is sweeter than honey."

**And last of all — let us praise the famous men.** Sirach finished by parading the heroes of the story past his grandchildren: Enoch, who pleased God. Noah, who was righteous when the flood came. Abraham, the great father, found faithful when he was tested. "Their bodies are buried in peace," said Grandfather Sirach, "but their names live for ever."

Grandfather Sirach's whole book is one big sentence: wisdom is a treasure, friends are a treasure — and both begin with loving God.
