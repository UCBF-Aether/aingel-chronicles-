The Prophets

Josippon Tells What Happened Next

From the Book of Josippon, the history of the years between the prophets and the birth of Jesus

Section: The Prophets

After Malachi, the last prophet, something strange happened: for hundreds of years, God sent no new prophet to write a new book. Did the story stop?

Not at all. It was just quiet — the way winter is quiet before spring. And our canon keeps a book that tells those quiet years: the book of Josippon.

Josippon does something wonderful. He counts. He counts the years the way a grandfather counts grandchildren — Adam, and Seth, and on and on; the judges, one by one; the kings, one by one, down to Nebuchadnezzar, the great king of Babylon who carried God's people away. Josippon writes down every name, because every name is a person God carried through history.

Then he counts on past the prophets. After Babylon came Persia, and God's people went home. After Persia came Alexander the Great, young and swift, conquering the whole world before he was old enough to be a grandfather of anyone. After Alexander, his kingdom broke into pieces, and other kings rose and fell over little Jerusalem like waves breaking over a rock. Then came Rome, greatest and hardest of all, ruling the whole world with iron — and still Jerusalem stood, and still God's people kept the Law, kept the feasts, kept the promises, and waited.

Many of them thought the Promised King would come as a warrior, to throw Rome out and rule the land. That is not how God sent Him.

Because at the end of Josippon's long count of years, the road comes out — of all places — at Bethlehem. Josippon tells it himself: a star rose in the east. Wise men saw it and followed it a very long way, asking, "Where is He who has been born King of the Jews? We have seen His star, and we have come to worship Him." And the star brought them to Bethlehem, to the Child, and they bowed down and worshipped Him.

King Herod raged, because he wanted to be the only king. But God kept watch. An angel warned the family in a dream, and they travelled down to Egypt until it was safe — and then home to Nazareth, where the Child grew up, in every good work, without sin.

Do you see what Josippon is for? He is the bridge. One end of him stands with the prophets, and the other end stands at the manger. All those quiet years, all those names, all those empires rising and falling — the road was going somewhere the whole time.

Hundreds of quiet years, and God never lost count of a single one — the road was always heading for Bethlehem.
