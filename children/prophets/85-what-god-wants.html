The Prophets

What God Wants

From the Book of Micah, chapter 6

Section: The Prophets

One day the prophet Micah heard God calling His people to a great outdoor court — and God called the mountains and the hills to be the witnesses, for they had been standing there since the world was young.

"O My people," said God, and His voice was almost sad. "What have I done to you? How have I wearied you? Answer Me. I brought you up out of slavery in Egypt. I sent you Moses, and Aaron, and Miriam, to lead you. I rescued you again and again. O My people — remember!"

The people realized they had wandered from God. And they asked the question everyone asks when they want to come back: *What does God want from us? What can we bring Him?*

"Shall we bring calves — baby cows, a whole herd of them?" "Would God be pleased with thousands of rams?" "With ten thousand rivers of oil, pouring and pouring?" "Shall we give Him the most precious thing we have, to pay for what we've done?"

Bigger. And bigger. And bigger. The offers grew and grew — until Micah gave God's answer, and it was beautifully simple. No mountain of gifts. No rivers of oil. Just this:

"He has shown you, O man, what is good. And what does the LORD require of you? To do justly. To love mercy. And to walk humbly with your God."

Do what is fair. Love being kind. Walk close to God, step by step, without showing off.

That is what God wants. Not everything you own — just your fairness, your kindness, and your footsteps beside His, every day.

Do justly. Love mercy. Walk humbly with your God. That is what He asks — and it is enough.
