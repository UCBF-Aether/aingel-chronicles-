The Prophets

Jeremiah and the Potter's Clay

From the Book of Jeremiah, chapter 18

Section: The Prophets

"Jeremiah," said God one day, "get up and go down to the potter's house. There I will let you hear My words."

So Jeremiah went down to the potter's house. The potter was at work at his wheels, shaping a pot out of soft clay. Round and round the wheel spun, and up rose a tall, smooth vessel between the potter's wet hands.

But something went wrong. The pot slipped and spoiled in the potter's hands — lopsided, ruined.

Did the potter throw the clay away? He did not.

He squashed it down, and he made it again — a different vessel, a good one, just as he thought best. The clay was still in his hands, and the potter knew what to do with spoiled clay: he began again.

Then God spoke to Jeremiah, and Jeremiah understood. The potter's workshop had been a picture all along.

"My people," said God, "cannot I do with you as this potter does? Look: just as the clay is in the potter's hand, so are you in My hand."

And God explained. If God warns a nation that it is heading for trouble, and that nation turns away from its wrongdoing — God will hold back the trouble. He is glad to change His plans for people who change their ways. And if God promises to build a nation up and plant it like a tree, but it stops listening to Him — then He must think again about the good He planned.

"Nations are in My hand," says God. "And so are hearts."

So God sent Jeremiah back to the people with an invitation shaped like wet clay: "Turn back, every one of you, from your wrong way. Make your ways and your doings good."

It was really an invitation to be remade. The Potter's hands were still open. The wheel was still turning. Nothing spoiled had to stay spoiled — not a pot, not a nation, not a heart — as long as it stayed in the Potter's hands and let Him begin again.

Thank You, God, our Potter — when we are spoiled and lopsided, You do not throw us away; You make us again.
