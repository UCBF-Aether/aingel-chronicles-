The Prophets

The Little Book of Obadiah

From the Book of Obadiah

Section: The Prophets

Obadiah's book is the shortest book in the whole Old Testament — just one little chapter. But it carries one big lesson, and the lesson is about two brothers' families.

Remember Jacob and Esau, the twin brothers from long ago? Jacob's family became Israel, God's people. Esau's family became Edom — and Edom lived high up in the mountains, in cities carved into the cliffs, in the clefts of the rock where no enemy could reach them.

Edom grew proud. "Who can bring me down to the ground?" Edom boasted. "I live in the clefts of the rock. My home is high. I am untouchable!"

"You may soar like the eagle," said God. "You may set your nest among the very stars. But even from there, I can bring you down. Pride has deceived your heart."

But pride was not Edom's only sin. When Jerusalem fell and strangers carried Jacob's people away, what did brother Edom do? Edom stood on the other side and watched. Edom rejoiced over his brother's trouble. Edom looted what was left. Edom even blocked the roads and caught the people who were escaping. On the day of his brother's worst trouble, Edom gloated instead of helping.

"You should not have looked on your brother's day," said God. "You should not have rejoiced. You should not have spoken proudly. As you have done to others — so it shall be done to you."

That is Obadiah's whole short book: God sees how we treat our brothers and sisters, especially on their hardest days. Standing by, laughing, or taking advantage — God notices. Helping is what brothers are for.

And the book ends with hope for Jacob's family: "On Mount Zion there shall be deliverance, and there shall be holiness. The house of Jacob shall possess their possessions. And the kingdom — the kingdom shall be the LORD's."

Twenty-one verses. One chapter. And the last word belongs to God's kingdom.

Never laugh at someone else's hard day. Help — that's what brothers and sisters are for.
