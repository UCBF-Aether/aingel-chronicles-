The Prophets

Jonah and the Great Fish

From the Book of Jonah, chapters 1–3

Section: The Prophets

God spoke to a man named Jonah: "Go to the great city of Nineveh, and tell the people there to stop doing wrong."

But Jonah didn't want to go. So Jonah ran away — the wrong way! He ran down to the sea, paid for a ticket on a ship sailing the other way, and climbed aboard. As if anyone can run away from God.

God sent a mighty wind, and a storm came roaring over the sea. The waves rose like mountains. The ship groaned and nearly broke apart. The sailors cried out and threw their cargo into the water to make the ship lighter.

And where was Jonah? Down inside the ship — fast asleep!

The captain woke him up. "How can you sleep? Get up and pray!" The sailors drew lots to find out who was causing all this trouble, and the lot pointed straight at Jonah.

"It's my fault," Jonah said honestly. "I ran away from God. Pick me up and throw me into the sea, and the storm will stop."

The sailors did not want to. They rowed with all their might to reach the land — but the storm only grew wilder. So at last, asking God to forgive them, they lifted Jonah up and let him go into the sea.

And at once — the wind stopped. The waves lay down flat. The sea was calm, and the sailors stood amazed at the power of God.

But Jonah's story was not over! God had prepared a great fish, and the fish opened its enormous mouth and swallowed Jonah whole — splash! There he stayed, in the dark belly of the fish, for three days and three nights.

In the dark, Jonah prayed. "God, I was running away, and You came after me. I will do what You asked. Thank You for not letting me go."

And God spoke to the fish, and the fish carried Jonah to shore and let him out onto dry land.

Then God gave Jonah a second chance. "Go to Nineveh."

This time Jonah went! He walked through the enormous city calling out God's message: "Forty more days, and Nineveh will be overturned!" And the surprising thing happened: the people listened. From the greatest to the smallest, they said they were sorry. Even the king got down from his throne and sat in ashes to show his sorrow. Even the animals were covered, king's orders, while the city asked God for mercy.

And God saw they truly turned from doing wrong — and He forgave the whole great city.

God gives second chances — to runaways, and even to a whole city that says it is sorry.
