The Prophets

Jeremiah Weeps Over the City

From the Book of Lamentations, chapters 1–3

Section: The Prophets

Once, Jerusalem had been full of people — markets busy, streets laughing, the temple shining on the hill. Now the city sat all alone, like a widow with nobody to comfort her. The roads were empty. The gates were broken. The beautiful city was in ruins, and in the quiet streets there was weeping in the night.

And the prophet Jeremiah sat down among the broken stones — and he cried too.

"For these things I weep," he said. "My eyes run down with water." Jeremiah did not cry from far away. He sat right in the middle of the sadness, with his people, and his tears were prayers. He knew why the trouble had come: God's people had turned away from God, and the city had not listened, though God had warned them again and again. "The LORD is righteous," Jeremiah said honestly, "for we rebelled against His word."

But in the middle of the saddest book in the Bible, something shines.

Halfway through his tears, Jeremiah remembered something — and remembering it gave him hope:

"It is because of the LORD's mercies that we are not finished," he said. "His compassions never fail. They are new every morning. Great is Your faithfulness!"

New every morning. Like the sunrise. Like bread still warm. God's kindness does not run out — not in a ruined city, not in the worst week of your life, not ever.

"The LORD is my portion," said Jeremiah — my share, my treasure, mine — "so I will hope in Him. The LORD is good to everyone who waits for Him. He will not cast anyone off forever. Even when He lets grief come, He will have compassion, because His mercies are so many. He does not *want* to make His children sad."

And when Jeremiah called to God out of the deepest, darkest place, God drew near to him and said the words He loves to say:

"Fear not."

Jeremiah wept over the city — and right there among his tears he found the treasure buried in the middle of the book: a God whose mercies begin again with every single morning.

Great is Your faithfulness, God — Your mercies never end, and every morning of our lives, they are new.
