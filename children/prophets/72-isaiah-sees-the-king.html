The Prophets

Isaiah Sees the King

From the Book of Isaiah, chapter 6

Section: The Prophets

In the year that King Uzziah died, the prophet Isaiah saw something that no words can quite hold — so we will let Isaiah tell it the way he told it.

"I saw the LORD, sitting on a throne, high and lifted up — and the train of His robe filled the whole temple." All around the throne stood the seraphim, the flaming angels. Each one had **six wings**: with two he covered his face, with two he covered his feet, and with two he flew. And they cried out to one another, back and forth like the waves of a song that never stops:

**"Holy, holy, holy is the LORD of hosts! The whole earth is full of His glory!"**

The doorposts shook at the sound, and the house filled with shining smoke.

Isaiah fell to the ground, trembling. "Woe is me!" he cried. "I am a man of unclean lips, and I live among a people of unclean lips — and my eyes have seen the King!"

Then one of the seraphim flew to him, holding a glowing coal from the altar in a pair of tongs. He touched Isaiah's mouth with it, and said: "See — this has touched your lips. Your wrong is taken away; your sin is forgiven." The coal did not harm Isaiah. It healed him. God does not want His messengers to be afraid of Him — He cleanses them so they can stand up glad.

And then Isaiah heard the voice of the Lord, asking a question for all the ages: "Whom shall I send? And who will go for Us?"

And Isaiah — forgiven, unafraid, on his feet — answered the words every child of God can answer:

"**Here am I. Send me!**"

When God forgives you and calls you, there is one joyful answer: "Here am I — send me!"
