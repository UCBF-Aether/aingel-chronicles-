The Prophets

Nahum's Word Against Nineveh

From the Book of Nahum, chapter 1

Section: The Prophets

Do you remember Nineveh — the great city where Jonah once preached, and all the people repented? Well, many years passed, and Nineveh went back to its old cruel ways. It became a bully of a city, hurting nation after nation — and now it was threatening God's people in Judah. People in Judah were frightened.

Then God gave a message to a prophet named Nahum. And Nahum's message was not for Nineveh's comfort. It was for Judah's.

Nahum began by telling who God is. God is slow to anger — oh, so slow — and great in power. His way is in the whirlwind and the storm, and the clouds are the dust of His feet. He is mighty enough that no bully anywhere frightens Him at all.

And then Nahum said the verse that Judah needed most — the verse to hold in the dark:

"The LORD is good — a stronghold in the day of trouble. And He knows those who trust in Him."

A stronghold is a fortress — a strong, safe place with thick walls, where trouble cannot reach you. When trouble comes, God Himself is the fortress. And He *knows* His people — by name, one by one, everyone who trusts in Him.

God promised that Nineveh's cruelty would end. The heavy yoke on Judah's neck would be snapped, the chains burst. Never again would the wicked city march through their land.

And Nahum's chapter ends with the happiest picture of all — the picture of the messenger: "Look! On the mountains — the feet of the one who brings good news, who publishes peace!" A runner, racing over the ridges with wonderful tidings! And down in Judah, the people could keep their feasts again, sing their songs again, live their quiet lives again — in peace.

The bully's day always ends. God's stronghold never falls.

The LORD is good, a stronghold in the day of trouble — and He knows everyone who trusts in Him.
