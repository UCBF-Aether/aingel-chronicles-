The Prophets

The Lions' Den

From the Book of Daniel, chapter 6

Section: The Prophets

Daniel grew old serving kings — and he served them so well, so honestly, that King Darius planned to put him in charge of the whole kingdom.

That made the other officials jealous. They hunted for some fault in Daniel's work — some mistake, some dishonesty, anything. But they found nothing. Daniel was faithful in everything. At last they said, "We'll never find anything against Daniel — unless it has to do with his God."

So they made a trap out of prayer. They flattered the king into signing a law: "For thirty days, no one may pray to any god or man except you, O king. Whoever disobeys goes into the den of lions."

Daniel heard about the law. He knew exactly what it meant. And he went home, climbed the stairs to his upper room, opened his windows toward faraway Jerusalem — and knelt down and prayed, giving thanks to God, three times a day. Just as he had always done. He did not hide. He did not whisper. He did not skip a single day.

The officials rushed in, dragged Daniel before the king, and demanded the law be kept. The king was heartbroken — he laboured until sunset trying to save his friend — but the law could not be changed. "Daniel," he said, as they cast him into the den, "your God, whom you serve always — may He deliver you!" A stone was rolled over the mouth of the den and sealed.

That night the king could not eat. He could not sleep. He could not bear music. He tossed and turned until the sky lightened — and at the very first crack of dawn, he ran to the den and cried out in an anguished voice, "O Daniel, servant of the living God! Was your God able to deliver you from the lions?"

And from the darkness came a voice — Daniel's own! "O king, live forever! My God sent His angel and shut the lions' mouths. They have not hurt me at all — because I was innocent before God, and I never did you any wrong either, O king."

They lifted Daniel out. There was not a scratch on him, because he had trusted in his God.

Then the king wrote to every nation on earth: everyone must honour Daniel's God — "for He is the living God, who endures forever. He delivers and rescues. He works wonders in heaven and on earth. He has delivered Daniel from the power of the lions."

Pray like Daniel — openly, faithfully, every day — and trust the God who sends His angel.
