The Prophets

Baruch Reads the Book Aloud

From the Book of Baruch, chapters 1–3

Section: The Prophets

Far away from home, beside a river in Babylon, God's people sat down and cried. Their city was broken. Their temple was far behind them. Everything they loved seemed lost.

But they were not alone, and they had not been forgotten. With them was a man named Baruch. Baruch was the friend and helper of Jeremiah the prophet — when Jeremiah spoke God's words, Baruch wrote them down carefully in a book.

One day Baruch stood up beside the river, opened the book, and began to read aloud. He read to everyone — to the king's family, to the leaders, to the old people, and to the very smallest children. Nobody was left out. God's words were for them all.

As the people listened, they wept. They prayed. And they said something very honest and very brave: "We have done wrong. God has always done right. We did not listen to His voice, and that is why all this trouble came."

They did not make excuses. They did not blame anyone else. They told God the truth about themselves — and telling the truth is where coming home begins.

Then they did something beautiful. They collected money — silver, gathered from everyone's pockets, even in a strange land — and sent it home to Jerusalem with the book, so that Baruch's reading could be heard there too, on God's holy days, in God's own house. The book would travel where they could not — yet.

And in the book were these shining words: that God had searched for Wisdom like a treasure, and kings and giants and rich merchants had never found her. But God found her — and He gave her as a gift to His people, like a father handing his child something precious. And even the stars, the book said, shine happily in their places; when God calls them, they answer, "Here we are!" — and twinkle with joy.

So beside that faraway river, a homesick people dried their tears. They had confessed. They had prayed. They had sent their gift ahead of them, home.

They were still far away. But they had begun to turn around — and God was already listening.

When you tell God the truth, He hears you — even beside a faraway river.
