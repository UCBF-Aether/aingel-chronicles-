The Prophets

Habakkuk Watches and Sings

From the Book of Habakkuk, chapters 2–3

Section: The Prophets

Habakkuk had hard questions. All around him, the wicked seemed to win and the good people seemed to suffer, and Habakkuk did not understand. So he did something wonderful with his questions: he climbed up to his watchtower, stood at his post like a guard on a wall, and said, "I will stand here and watch, to see what God will say to me."

And God answered. "Write the vision down," God said. "Write it big and plain on tablets, so that a runner passing by can read it as he runs. The promise seems slow — but wait for it. It will surely come. It will not be late. In the meantime: the just shall live by his faith — by trusting Me all the way through the waiting."

God also looked ahead to a day when "the earth shall be filled with the knowledge of the glory of the LORD, the way the waters cover the sea" — glory everywhere, deep and wide as the ocean.

Then Habakkuk prayed — and his prayer turned into a song. He remembered how God had marched through history for His people, how mountains trembled and the deep lifted its hands, how God went forth for the salvation of His people. Habakkuk trembled too, hearing it all. But then he sang the bravest verse in his whole book:

"Even if the fig tree does not blossom — even if there is no fruit on the vines, even if the olive crop fails and the fields give no food, even if the sheep are gone from the fold and there are no cattle in the stalls — YET I will rejoice in the LORD. I will be joyful in the God of my salvation!"

Habakkuk decided to sing before he could see the rescue. His joy did not wait for full barns. It rested on God Himself.

"The LORD God is my strength," he sang. "He will make my feet like the feet of the deer — and set me walking on the high places!"

Questions brought honestly. Answers waited for patiently. Joy chosen bravely. That is Habakkuk's whole song.

You can rejoice in God even before the fig tree blossoms — He will make your feet like the deer's.
