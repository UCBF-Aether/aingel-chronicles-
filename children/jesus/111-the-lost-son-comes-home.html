The Stories of Jesus

The Lost Son Comes Home

From the Book of Luke, chapter 15

Section: The Stories of Jesus

A man had two sons. One day the younger son said: "Father, give me my share someday. Give it to me now."

Asking while his father lived was shocking — but the grieving, gentle father divided his wealth and gave him his share.

Later, he gathered everything, travelled far and wasted it all on wild living.

When the money ran out, famine came. No jobs. No food. The young man was starving. He begged work from a farmer, who sent him to feed pigs — the lowest job. Hungry, he would have eaten the pigs' husks. Nobody gave him anything.

Then, among the pigs, he came to his senses.

"My father's servants have bread to spare, he thought — yet I am dying! I will go to my father and say: Father, I have sinned against heaven and you. I am no longer worthy to be called your son. Treat me as a hired servant."

So he started home, rehearsing his speech.

But the story isn't about the son's walk — it's about the father's window.

Because his father was watching. While the son was far off, his father saw him — and did what no dignified father did.

He ran.

He gathered his robes, ran down the road, and embraced his filthy, pig-smelling son, kissing him.

He began: "Father, I have sinned against heaven and you. I am no longer worthy to be your son..." — but the father was already calling to the servants: "Quick! Best robe for him! Ring on his hand, shoes on his feet! Bring the fattened calf! Eat and celebrate — this son of mine was dead, and is alive again! He was lost, and is found!"

Feasting, music and dancing began.

The elder son was in the fields. Coming home, he heard music and dancing. A servant told him: "Your brother has come home, and your father is feasting — he has him back safe."

The elder son stood outside, angry, and would not go in. His father came out and pleaded. "Lo, all these years I've served you," the elder son said, "and you never gave me even a young goat to feast with my friends. But when this son of yours came home — who wasted everything — you killed the fattened calf for him!"

And the father answered, tenderly: "Son, you are always with me. Everything I have is yours. But we were right to celebrate — your brother was dead, and is alive again. He was lost — and is found."

God is the Father at the window — watching the road, ready to run. However far you've gone, the whole way home, He is looking for you.
