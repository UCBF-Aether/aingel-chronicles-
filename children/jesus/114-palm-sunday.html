The Stories of Jesus

Palm Sunday

From the Book of Matthew, chapter 21, and the Book of John, chapter 12

Section: The Stories of Jesus

The great feast of Passover was near, and Jerusalem was filling up with pilgrims from everywhere. Jesus and His friends were on their way to the city too. When they came to the village of Bethphage, near the Mount of Olives, Jesus sent two of His disciples ahead with a strange errand:

"Go into the village in front of you. Straight away you'll find a donkey tied up, and her colt with her. Untie them, and bring them to me. If anyone asks what you're doing, say: 'The Lord needs them' — and he'll let you go."

The disciples went — and found it exactly as Jesus had said.

Now, hundreds of years before, the prophet Zechariah had sung about this very day: "Fear not, daughter of Zion! Look — your King is coming to you, gentle, and riding on a donkey, on a colt, the foal of a donkey." (You read that song earlier in this book — everything God promises, God keeps.)

The disciples laid their cloaks on the young donkey, like a saddle, and Jesus sat on it. And then the road turned into a parade.

The crowds ahead of Him and behind Him spread their cloaks on the road, so the donkey's feet wouldn't even touch the dust. Others cut branches — palm branches, long and green and waving like flags — and spread them on the road, and carried them, shouting:

"Hosanna to the Son of David! Blessed is He who comes in the name of the Lord! Hosanna in the highest!"

Many in that crowd had been there when Jesus called Lazarus out of the tomb — they had seen it with their own eyes, and they told everyone. That's why the whole city poured out to meet Him. By the time Jesus rode through the gates, all Jerusalem was stirred up, asking: "Who is this?"

And the crowds answered: "This is Jesus — the prophet from Nazareth of Galilee!"

In the Temple courts, Jesus healed people who were blind and people who couldn't walk. And then something happened that made the day perfect: the children in the Temple began shouting too — "Hosanna to the Son of David!" — in their high, happy voices.

The chief priests and the teachers didn't like it at all. "Do You hear what these children are saying?" they demanded.

"Yes," said Jesus. "Haven't you ever read: 'Out of the mouths of little children, You have perfected praise'?"

The King had come into His city at last — not on a war-horse, not with an army, but on a borrowed donkey, with children shouting and palm branches waving in the spring air.

Zechariah's old song came true on a spring morning: the gentle King rode in, and the children did the shouting.
