The Stories of Jesus

Jesus Feeds the Hungry Crowd

From the Book of Matthew, chapter 14, and the Book of John, chapter 6

Section: The Stories of Jesus

Jesus had gone across the lake looking for a quiet place to rest. But the crowds heard where He was going, and they hurried around the shore on foot — thousands of them — and were waiting for Him when the boat landed.

When Jesus saw them all — the grandmothers, the children, the sick people carried on mats, everyone hungry for His words — His heart was moved with compassion. He welcomed them. He healed their sick. He taught them all day long.

Then evening came, and the disciples looked at the enormous crowd and did some worried maths. "This is a deserted place," they said to Jesus, "and it's getting late. Send the people away, so they can go into the villages and buy themselves food."

But Jesus said something that made their eyes pop: "They don't need to go away. You give them something to eat."

The disciples did more maths, faster. "Two hundred coins' worth of bread wouldn't give everyone even a bite!" said Philip.

Then Andrew, Simon Peter's brother, spoke up. He had found something — well, someone. "There is a boy here," he said, "who has five barley loaves and two small fish." A pause. "But what are they, among so many?"

Five small loaves — a child's packed lunch. Two little fish. And five thousand hungry men, besides all the women and children.

"Have the people sit down on the grass," said Jesus.

So everybody sat — row upon row upon row on the green hillside, thousands and thousands of them. Jesus took the boy's five loaves and two fish. He looked up to heaven. He gave thanks. He broke the loaves — and gave the pieces to the disciples, and the disciples gave them to the crowd.

And the bread kept coming. Basket after basket. Hand after hand. Nobody was skipped, nobody got a crumb-counted portion — everyone ate, and ate, until every single person had eaten their fill.

Then Jesus said, "Gather up the pieces that are left over, so that nothing is wasted."

The disciples went around with baskets — and filled twelve baskets full of leftover pieces. Twelve! From five little loaves.

The people looked at the baskets. Then they looked at Jesus. "This is truly the Prophet," they said, "the One who was to come into the world!"

And somewhere in the crowd was a boy who would dine out on this story for the rest of his life: the day his lunch fed five thousand people, because he let Jesus hold it.

Five loaves and two fish are almost nothing — until you put them in Jesus' hands; then there's lunch for thousands, and twelve baskets to spare.
