The Stories of Jesus

Jesus Walks on the Water

From the Book of Matthew, chapter 14, and the Book of John, chapter 6

Section: The Stories of Jesus

Straight after the great feast of loaves and fish, Jesus did something surprising. He told His disciples: "Get into the boat, and go on ahead of me to the other side." Then He sent the crowds home, and He went up the mountain alone, to pray.

Night fell.

Out on the lake, the disciples were rowing into a headwind. The wind was against them, the waves were high, and the boat — now miles from shore, right in the middle of the sea — was being beaten and tossed. Hour after hour they rowed, and the wind did not drop, and Jesus had not come.

Then, in the deep part of the night — the fourth watch, nearly morning — they saw something.

A figure. On the water. Walking on the sea, toward the boat.

The disciples were terrified. "It's a ghost!" they cried out, loud with fear.

Straight away, Jesus spoke to them, across the wind and the waves: "Take heart. It is I. Do not be afraid."

And Peter — brave, impulsive Peter — called back: "Lord, if it is You, tell me to come to You on the water!"

Jesus said one word: "Come."

So Peter climbed over the side of that boat, in the dark, in the wind — and he walked on the water toward Jesus! Step after step, doing the impossible, with his eyes on the Lord.

But then Peter looked around. He saw how boisterous the wind was, and how the waves rose and fell under his feet — and fear rushed in, and Peter began to sink.

"Lord, save me!" he cried.

Immediately — not after a pause, not after a lecture — immediately, Jesus stretched out His hand and caught him.

"O you of little faith," Jesus said, holding him fast, "why did you doubt?"

Together they climbed into the boat. And the wind stopped. Just like that — the howling stopped, the waves lay down, and John's Gospel tells us the boat was suddenly at the shore they were heading for.

The disciples worshipped Him, right there among the nets and the oars, with their clothes still dripping. "Truly," they said, "You are the Son of God."

Peter sank when he looked at the waves — and Jesus' hand was already there when he cried out. It always is.
