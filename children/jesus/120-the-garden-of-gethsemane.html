The Stories of Jesus

The Garden of Gethsemane

From the Gospel of Matthew, chapter 26, and the Gospel of Luke, chapter 22

Section: The Stories of Jesus

After the supper was over, Jesus took His friends out into the night, to a quiet place He loved — a garden called Gethsemane, full of old olive trees.

"Sit here," He said to most of them, "while I go and pray over there."

But Peter, James, and John He took a little further in. And as they walked, Jesus began to be very sad — sadder than they had ever seen Him.

"My soul is very, very sad," He told them — "sad even to death. Stay here, and watch with Me."

Then He went on a little way by Himself, about as far as you could throw a stone, and He knelt down among the olive trees and prayed.

"My Father," He prayed, "if it can be, please let this cup pass away from Me. But not what I want — what *You* want."

It was a brave prayer and a trusting prayer, prayed by someone who knew that something very hard was coming in the morning.

When Jesus went back to His three friends, He found them fast asleep. The sadness had made them heavy and tired, and their eyes would not stay open.

"Could you not watch with Me for one hour?" He asked them gently. "Watch and pray."

And He went away again, and prayed again: "My Father, if this cup cannot pass away — then Your will be done."

And God did not leave Him alone in the dark garden. An angel came from heaven, and strengthened Him.

The third time Jesus came back, the friends were sleeping again. He let them rest a moment longer. Then He woke them, and His voice was steady and brave.

"Rise," He said. "Let us be going."

He was not running away. He was not afraid anymore. Jesus stood up in the garden of olive trees, strengthened by His Father, ready for everything that was coming — because of how much He loved the world.

Thank You, Jesus, for praying in the dark garden — and for loving us enough to stay.
