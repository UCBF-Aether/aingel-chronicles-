The Stories of Jesus

The Cross

From the Gospel of Matthew, chapters 26–27, the Gospel of Luke, chapter 23, and the Gospel of John, chapter 19

Section: The Stories of Jesus

That night, after the garden, soldiers came and took Jesus away. In the morning, the leaders brought Him to the Roman governor, Pilate. Pilate asked Him many questions, and Pilate could find nothing wrong that He had done. But a great crowd was shouting, and in the end Pilate listened to the crowd instead of doing what was right.

So Jesus was led out of the city to a hill. On the way, the soldiers made a man named Simon, who came from a city called Cyrene, carry the cross behind Jesus.

Many people followed, and many women were crying. Jesus turned to them and said, "Daughters of Jerusalem, do not weep for Me" — even on that road, He was thinking of others.

On the hill, they lifted Jesus up on the cross. Two others were lifted up beside Him, one on His right and one on His left.

The very first thing Jesus said from the cross was a prayer — not for Himself, but for the people who had put Him there:

"Father, forgive them, for they do not know what they are doing."

One of the two men beside Him understood who Jesus was. "Lord," he said, "remember me when You come into Your kingdom." And Jesus answered him with a wonderful promise: "Truly I tell you — today you will be with Me in paradise."

Near the cross stood Jesus' mother, Mary, and His dear friend John. Jesus looked down at them, even then, and made sure His mother would be cared for. "Woman," He said to her, "here is your son." And to John He said, "Here is your mother." And from that hour, John took Mary into his own home.

Then, in the middle of the day, the sun stopped shining. Darkness covered the whole land — from noon until three o'clock — the way night covers the land, only it was not night.

Inside the temple, far away, the great curtain that hung in God's house tore in two, from the very top to the very bottom.

And Jesus cried out, "Father, into Your hands I give My spirit!" And He bowed His head, and died.

The Roman captain who stood watching — who had seen the darkness, and heard the prayer, and watched how Jesus died — said the truest words of that whole sad day:

"Truly, this was the Son of God."

That evening, a kind man named Joseph, who loved Jesus, laid His body gently in a new tomb, cut out of the rock — and a great stone was rolled across the door.

It was the saddest day the world had ever known. But it was not the end of the story.

Thank You, Jesus, for loving us all the way to the cross — and for praying, even then, "Father, forgive them."
