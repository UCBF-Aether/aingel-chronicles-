The Stories of Jesus

The Last Supper

From the Book of Matthew, chapter 26, the Book of Luke, chapter 22, and the Book of John, chapter 13

Section: The Stories of Jesus

It was the first day of the Passover feast — the great meal God's people ate every year to remember how He had brought them out of Egypt. Jesus sent Peter and John ahead into Jerusalem: "Go and get the Passover ready for us."

"Where?" they asked.

"You'll meet a man carrying a jar of water," said Jesus. "Follow him to his house, and ask for the guest room." (Carrying water was usually women's work, so a man with a water jar would be easy to spot.) And it happened exactly so: they followed the man, and found a large upstairs room, furnished and ready, and there they prepared the feast.

When evening came, Jesus sat down at the table with His twelve friends. "I have wanted so much to eat this Passover with you," He told them, "before what comes next."

Then Jesus did something nobody expected.

He got up from the table. He took off His outer robe. He wrapped a towel around His waist, the way a servant does. He poured water into a basin — and He began to wash His disciples' feet. Dusty, road-worn, sandal feet — the job of the lowest servant in the house — done by the Lord Himself, one friend at a time, drying each foot with the towel.

When He came to Peter, Peter was scandalised. "Lord — You, wash my feet? Never!"

Jesus answered, "What I am doing, you don't understand now. You will understand later." And when Peter still refused, He said, "If I don't wash you, you have no part with me."

Then Peter — whole-hearted as always — burst out: "Lord, then not my feet only — wash my hands and my head too!"

After He had washed all their feet, Jesus put His robe back on and sat down. "Do you understand what I have done for you?" He asked. "You call me Teacher, and Lord — and you are right, for so I am. If I, your Lord and Teacher, have washed your feet, then you also ought to wash one another's feet. I have given you an example: do as I have done to you."

Then, at the table, Jesus took bread. He gave thanks and broke it, and gave it to them, saying: "Take, eat. This is my body, which is given for you. Do this in remembrance of me."

And He took the cup, and gave thanks, and passed it to them: "Drink from it, all of you. This is my blood of the new covenant, which is poured out for many, for the forgiveness of sins. I will not drink of this fruit of the vine again until the day when I drink it new with you in my Father's kingdom."

There was one sad shadow over that table: Jesus told them that one of His own friends had agreed to hand Him over that very night — and the disciples looked at one another in sorrow, each asking, "Lord, is it I?" Judas slipped out into the night. The others did not understand yet.

When the meal was done, they sang a hymn together — all twelve voices (eleven, now) in the lamplit room — and went out into the dark, to the Mount of Olives.

Two thousand years later, God's people are still doing exactly what happened at that table: washing one another's feet in a thousand small ways, and breaking the bread, and remembering.

That night Jesus gave His friends two gifts to keep forever: a towel, to serve each other with — and a table, to remember Him by.
