Jesus Calms the Storm

From the Book of Matthew, chapter 8, and the Book of Mark, chapter 4

Section: The Stories of Jesus

It had been a long day of teaching by the Sea of Galilee, and evening was coming on. Jesus said to His friends, "Let's cross over to the other side of the lake."

So they climbed into a boat — Jesus too, just as He was — and pushed out onto the water. Jesus was tired. He lay down in the back of the boat, put His head on a cushion, and fell fast asleep.

Then the storm came.

The Sea of Galilee is famous for storms that arrive from nowhere, and this one was a monster. The wind screamed down off the hills. The waves reared up and crashed over the sides, again and again, until the boat was filling with water and riding lower and lower. Some of Jesus' friends were fishermen who had worked that lake all their lives, in all weathers — and even they were frightened now.

And through all of it, Jesus slept.

At last the friends shook Him awake. "Master!" they cried. "Don't You care that we're going to drown? Lord, save us! We're perishing!"

Jesus got up. He looked at the howling wind and the rolling sea, and He spoke to them the way you might speak to a frightened animal:

"Peace. Be still."

And the wind stopped.

The waves flattened. The water went smooth as glass, under a sky suddenly quiet. A great calm covered the sea, as if the storm had never been.

Then Jesus turned to His friends. "Why were you so afraid?" He said gently. "Do you still not trust me?"

And the friends looked at each other, half glad and half trembling, with the water still dripping off their clothes.

"What kind of man is this?" they whispered, "that even the wind and the sea obey Him?"

The storm did its very worst — and one word from Jesus made the whole sea lie down like a lamb.
