The Stories of Jesus

The Lost Sheep

From the Book of Matthew, chapter 18, and the Book of Luke, chapter 15

Section: The Stories of Jesus

Jesus told a story about a shepherd, and it begins like this: the shepherd had one hundred sheep.

Every evening he counted them as they came through the gate of the fold: ninety-six, ninety-seven, ninety-eight, ninety-nine... and then he stopped. One sheep was missing.

One sheep had wandered off — over the hills, away from the flock, away from the shepherd's voice, out where the dark was coming down and the wild places began. Lost.

Now, ninety-nine sheep were safe at home. Many shepherds would have said: "Ninety-nine out of a hundred — that's not bad. I'll stay with the flock."

Not this shepherd.

He left the ninety-nine in the open country, lit his lantern, and went out into the mountains after the one that was lost. He searched. He climbed. He called its name into the wind. He didn't stop when his feet got sore, and he didn't stop when the night got dark, and he didn't stop when it would have been so much easier to go home.

He searched until he found it.

And when he found it — oh, you should have seen his face! He didn't scold it. He didn't drag it home by a rope. He lifted it up, laid it across his shoulders, with its legs dangling either side of his neck, and he carried it home, rejoicing the whole way.

When he reached the village, he called his friends and neighbours together. "Rejoice with me!" he cried. "I have found my sheep that was lost!"

Then Jesus explained the point of the story, and the point is bigger than the hills. "I tell you," He said, "there is joy in heaven when even one person turns back to God — more joy than over ninety-nine who never wandered away."

And Matthew tells us Jesus said something else about it, something especially for the little ones: "It is not the will of your Father in heaven that even one of these little ones should be lost."

Not one. Not you. Not ever. If you were the one sheep who wandered off, heaven itself would go out into the dark with a lantern, calling your name.

Ninety-nine safe sheep couldn't keep the shepherd home — because to the Shepherd, the lost one still counts as His.
