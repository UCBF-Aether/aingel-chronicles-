The Stories of Jesus

Lazarus, Come Out!

From the Book of John, chapter 11

Section: The Stories of Jesus

In Bethany lived Jesus' dearest friends: Martha and Mary, and their brother Lazarus. Jesus loved this family, eating and resting at their house.

Then Lazarus fell sick, sicker and sicker. The sisters sent word to Jesus: "Lord, the one You love is sick."

Hearing it, Jesus said: "This sickness will not end in death. It is for the glory of God." Then He stayed two days.

At last He told them, "Lazarus has fallen asleep. I am going to wake him up." The disciples thought He meant sleep. So Jesus told them plainly: "Lazarus has died. Let us go."

When Jesus reached Bethany, Lazarus had been in the tomb four days. The house was full of mourners; the sisters were broken-hearted.

Martha heard Jesus was coming and ran to meet Him. "Lord," she said through tears, "if You had been here, my brother would not have died. But even now — I know whatever You ask of God, God will give You."

"Martha," said Jesus, "your brother will rise again."

"I know he will rise at the last day," she answered.

And Jesus said to her the great words: "I am the resurrection, and the life. Whoever believes in me — even if he dies — will live. Do you believe this?"

"Yes, Lord," said Martha. "I believe that You are the Christ, the Son of God."

Then Mary fell at Jesus' feet, weeping: "Lord, if You had been here, my brother would not have died." Mary wept, and her friends wept — and Jesus groaned, troubled to His heart.

"Where have you laid him?" He asked.

"Lord, come and see," they said.

And then — the shortest verse in the Bible: **Jesus wept.**

The people standing by watched tears run down His face. "See how He loved him!"

They came to the tomb: a cave, a great stone across the entrance.

"Take away the stone," said Jesus.

They rolled it away. Jesus looked to heaven. "Father, I thank You that You have heard me," He prayed aloud, so everyone would know Whose power was at work.

Then He cried out, in a voice ringing off the hillside:

"LAZARUS! COME OUT!"

And the dead man came out.

Out of the dark cave, into sunlight — wrapped in burial cloths, hands and feet bound, a cloth over his face, shuffling out as the crowd stood frozen.

"Untie him," said Jesus, "and let him go."

And Lazarus went home, alive — to Martha's cooking, Mary's laughter, his own house — and all who saw believed.

Before He called Lazarus out of the tomb, Jesus stood outside it and cried — because He loves us in our grief, not only after it.
