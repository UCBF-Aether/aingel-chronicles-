Jesus and the Children

From the Book of Matthew, chapter 19, and Mark, chapter 10

Section: The Stories of Jesus

Wherever Jesus went, crowds followed Him — crowds of grown-ups with big questions and heavy worries. But one day, some mothers and fathers brought someone else to Jesus: their little children.

They carried the babies in their arms. They led the toddlers by the hand. They brought them so that Jesus would touch them and pray for them and bless them — because everyone who had seen Jesus knew that His hands brought good things.

But Jesus' helpers, the disciples, stepped in front. "No, no," they said. "The Teacher is too busy for children. Take them away."

When Jesus saw that, He was not pleased at all. And He said words that have rung through the whole world ever since:

"Let the little children come to me. Do not stop them! For the kingdom of heaven belongs to people who are like these little ones."

Like the little ones! Not like the proud, or the pushy, or the people who think they know everything — like children, who come simply, and trust completely, and are not ashamed to be small in their Father's arms.

Then Jesus did the best thing of all. He took the children up into His arms — one by one, the babies and the toddlers and the shy ones hiding behind their mothers' skirts — and He laid His hands on their heads, and blessed them, every single one, with words of love and prayer for each of them, while His helpers watched and learned.

No child was too small. No child was turned away. No child was told to come back when they were bigger, or quieter, or more important.

And that has never changed. Jesus says the same words today, to every child who hears this book:

Come.

"Let the little children come to me" — and that means you, little reader. Jesus blesses you too.
