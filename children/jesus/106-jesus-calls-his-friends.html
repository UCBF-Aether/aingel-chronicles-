The Stories of Jesus

Jesus Calls His Friends

From the Book of Matthew, chapter 4, the Book of Mark, chapters 1–3, and the Book of Luke, chapter 5

Section: The Stories of Jesus

Jesus did not do His work alone. He began by choosing friends — and you'll never guess what kind of people He picked. Not kings. Not scholars. Ordinary working people, with rough hands.

One morning He was walking along the shore of the Sea of Galilee when He saw two brothers, Simon (who would be called Peter) and Andrew, throwing a fishing net into the water — fishing was their work.

But that night, the fishing had been terrible. Peter told Him so: "Master, we worked all night and caught nothing."

Jesus said, "Push out into deep water, and let down your nets."

Peter looked at the nets. He looked at Jesus. "We've caught nothing all night... but because You say so, I'll let down the net."

The net went down — and came up alive! Fish — great flapping silver multitudes — so many that the net began to break! Peter waved frantically to his partners in the other boat, James and John, and both boats were filled to the brim, piled so high with fish that they began to sink.

Peter fell at Jesus' knees. "Go away from me, Lord," he said, astonished, "for I am a sinful man!"

But Jesus said, "Don't be afraid. From now on, you will be catching men."

And Peter and Andrew left their nets on the shore — straight away — and followed Him. A little further along, Jesus called James and John, sitting in the boat mending nets with their father Zebedee — and they left the boat, and their father, and followed too.

Not long after, Jesus passed a tax collector's booth. Tax collectors were the most unpopular men in town — Matthew (called Levi) sat there counting other people's money. Jesus looked at him and said: "Follow me."

And Matthew left everything — the table, the money, all of it — stood up, and followed. Then he threw a great feast at his house so his friends could meet Jesus too.

At last, Jesus went up a mountain and spent time in prayer, and chose twelve followers to be His special friends and messengers — His apostles. Here are their names, the way Mark lists them: Simon Peter; James and John, the brothers Jesus nicknamed "Sons of Thunder"; Andrew; Philip; Bartholomew; Matthew the tax collector; Thomas; James the son of Alphaeus; Thaddaeus; Simon; and Judas Iscariot — who would later turn away from Him.

Fishermen, a tax man, ordinary men — these were the friends the King of heaven picked to change the world.

Jesus didn't pick the grandest people in the land — He picked ordinary friends, and said: come, and I will make you more.
