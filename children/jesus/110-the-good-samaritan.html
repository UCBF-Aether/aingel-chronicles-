The Stories of Jesus

The Good Samaritan

From the Book of Luke, chapter 10

Section: The Stories of Jesus

A teacher of God's law came to Jesus with a big question. "Master," he said, "what must I do to inherit eternal life?"

Jesus asked him, "What is written in the Law? What do you read there?"

The man knew the answer by heart: "You shall love the Lord your God with all your heart, and all your soul, and all your strength, and all your mind — and your neighbour as yourself."

"You've answered right," said Jesus. "Do this, and you will live."

But the man wanted to pin the word down. "And who is my neighbour?" he asked.

So Jesus told him a story.

"A man was travelling down the road from Jerusalem to Jericho — a steep, twisty road that runs through lonely rocks, where robbers love to hide. And sure enough: robbers jumped out! They took everything he had, hurt him badly, and ran off, leaving him lying in the dust, half dead.

"Now, down that same road came a priest. He saw the man lying there... and he passed by on the other side.

"Then came a Levite, one of the Temple helpers. He came, and looked at the man... and he passed by on the other side too.

"But then came a third traveller — a Samaritan. (The Samaritans and Jesus' people did not get along at all; each side thought the other side was wrong about everything.)

"The Samaritan saw the hurt man — and his heart filled up with compassion. He didn't pass by. He knelt down in the dust, right where the man lay. He poured oil and wine gently onto the wounds, to clean and soothe them, and bandaged them up. Then he lifted the man onto his own donkey, and led the donkey carefully, on foot himself, all the way to an inn — and there he looked after him all night.

"In the morning, the Samaritan had to travel on. So he took out two silver coins and gave them to the innkeeper. 'Take care of him,' he said, 'and whatever more you spend on him, I will pay you back when I come this way again.'"

Then Jesus looked at the teacher of the law. "Now — which of these three was a neighbour to the man who fell among the robbers?"

The teacher knew. "The one who showed him mercy."

And Jesus said, "Go — and do the same."

A neighbour isn't the person who lives next door — a neighbour is anyone who needs you, and mercy is stopping to help.
