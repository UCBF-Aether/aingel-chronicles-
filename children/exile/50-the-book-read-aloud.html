Exile, Return, and Brave Scrolls

The Book Is Found and Read Aloud

From the Book of Nehemiah, chapter 8

Section: Exile, Return, and Brave Scrolls

When the wall of Jerusalem was rebuilt, the people had one more request. All of them — as one person — gathered in the wide street before the Water Gate and said to Ezra the teacher: "Bring the Book! Bring out the Book of the Law of Moses, and read it to us."

So Ezra brought the great Book before the whole gathering — men and women and everyone old enough to understand — and he stood on a tall platform of wood that had been built specially for that day, with helpers standing on his right hand and his left.

And Ezra opened the Book, up where everyone could see — and as he opened it, *all the people stood up*.

Ezra blessed the LORD, the great God. And all the people cried, "Amen! Amen!" — lifting up their hands — and bowed their heads to the ground and worshipped.

Ezra read from early morning until the middle of the day. And the Levites moved among the people, group by group, reading the words distinctly and explaining what they meant, so that *everyone* understood — even the children. Think of it: a whole city, standing still for hours, hungry for words many of them had never heard read aloud in their whole lives.

And as they listened, they began to weep. The words were so beautiful — and they had been away from them so long.

But Nehemiah the governor, and Ezra, and the teachers said: "This day is holy to the LORD your God. Do not be sad, and do not weep! Go your way — eat good food, and drink sweet drinks, and send portions to everyone who has nothing prepared. For this day is holy to our Lord, and the joy of the LORD is your strength!"

So the people went away cheering, and ate, and drank, and carried dinner portions to their neighbours who had none — and made a great celebration, because they had understood the words that were read to them.

And the very next day they discovered, written in the Book, the Feast of Booths! So everyone went out to the hills and fetched branches — olive branches and pine branches, myrtle branches and palm branches — and built themselves little leafy huts: on rooftops and in courtyards, in the temple courts and in the streets. The whole city camped out under green branches — which God's people had not done like that since the days of Joshua, hundreds of years before. And there was very great gladness.

And day by day, for seven days, Ezra stood up and read in the Book of the Law of God. The feast ended the way it began: with the Book open, and everybody listening.

A whole city once stood still for hours just to hear God's Book — and then they feasted, because the joy of the LORD is strength.
