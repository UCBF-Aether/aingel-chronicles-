Going Home to Jerusalem

From the Book of Ezra, chapters 1–3

Section: Exile, Return, and Brave Scrolls

God's people had been far from home a very long time — carried away to strange Babylon, while their city and temple lay in ruins. Grandfathers told Jerusalem's stories to children who had never seen it.

Then God stirred the heart of Cyrus, the great king of Persia — as Jeremiah had promised long before. And the king made a proclamation that rang through his empire:

"The LORD God of heaven has given me all the kingdoms of the earth, and He has charged me to build Him a house at Jerusalem. Who is there among you of all His people? Let his God be with him — and let him go up to Jerusalem, and build the house of the LORD!"

And people God had stirred rose up to go! Their neighbours loaded them with gifts — silver and gold, goods, and animals — for the long journey home. And King Cyrus himself brought out a treasure locked away for years: the golden vessels of God's house, which wicked Nebuchadnezzar had stolen from the temple. Cyrus's treasurer counted them out, one by one — thirty chargers of gold, a thousand chargers of silver — five thousand and four hundred vessels of gold and silver, all going HOME.

The company that went up was enormous: forty-two thousand, three hundred and sixty people — and among them two hundred singing men and women, so the road home had music.

And what was the very first thing they did when they reached the ruined city? Before houses, before walls — they built God's altar on its old stones, right where it had always stood. Morning and evening offering smoke went up again over Jerusalem. And they kept the Feast of Booths, sleeping in branch-huts under the stars like their ancestors, though a little afraid of the peoples round about.

In the second year, the builders laid the temple's foundation. And when the great stones were set, priests in robes blew trumpets, and Levites clashed cymbals, and they sang to the LORD, answering one another: "For He is good — for His mercy endureth for ever toward Israel!"

And all the people shouted!

But some singers were very old men — grandfathers who, as boys, had seen the FIRST temple before it burned. And when they saw this little foundation, they wept out loud. Tears and shouting, mixed together — joy and weeping in one great noise, so from far away, nobody could tell the shout of joy from crying.

Both were true. They were home.

The road home had singing on it — and at the end of it, tears and shouting both meant the same thing: we are home.
