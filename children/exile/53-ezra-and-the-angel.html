Ezra and the Angel Who Explains Sad Things

From the Book of 2 Esdras, chapters 3–14

Section: Exile, Return, and Brave Scrolls

Many years after Jerusalem was ruined, Ezra lay awake on his bed in Babylon. His city lay desolate, and his heart ached with the question children still ask tonight: *God, why do sad things happen to Your people?*

So Ezra prayed the whole story back to God — how He made Adam and the garden; how He saved Noah; how He chose Abraham and promised never to forsake his children; how He gave the Law on the smoky mountain. "You have been SO faithful," Ezra prayed. "So why does the story hurt so much?"

And God sent him an angel — Uriel — to answer. But the angel's answers were riddles.

"Go," said Uriel, "and weigh me the weight of fire. Or measure me the blast of the wind. Or call back to me the day that has already gone past."

Ezra said: "What person on earth can do that?"

"No one," said the angel. "You cannot even weigh the fire and the wind — things you live with every day! How could your little heart hold ALL the ways of the Most High? His way is like the deep sea. But listen: a grain of evil seed was sown in the human heart long ago, and it has grown and grown. Yet God has weighed the world in a balance, and numbered the times — and when His measure is full, the harvest will come, and the good He sowed will stand, and the evil will pass away. He has not forgotten. He has numbered everything."

And then, one day as Ezra sat under an oak, a voice called him out of a bush — the way God had once called Moses! "Esdras! Esdras!" "Here am I, Lord," said Ezra, and stood up.

"Your law is burned, Lord," he said. "No one knows the things You have done. But if I have found favour with You — send Your Holy Spirit into me, and I will WRITE everything that has been done in the world since the beginning, so that people yet to be born may find Your path, and live."

"Bring five men who can write quickly," said God, "and I shall light a candle of understanding in your heart that will not go out."

So Ezra gathered five swift writers — Sarea, Dabria, Selemia, Ecanus, and Asiel. And a voice called: "Esdras! Open your mouth and drink what I give you." And a cup was held out — full, as if of water — but its colour was like fire.

Ezra drank. At once his heart poured out understanding, wisdom grew in his chest, his memory grew strong, and his mouth was opened and would not close. He spoke, and the five men wrote — all day, and all night he did not stop. In forty days they wrote **two hundred and four books**.

And God said: "The first books you have written — publish them openly, so that everyone may read them. But keep the last seventy, and give them only to the wise among the people — for in them is the spring of understanding, the fountain of wisdom, and the stream of knowledge."

So the book that had been burned came back — every word of it — because one sad man lay awake asking hard questions, and God answered him not with anger, but with an angel, a burning bush, and a pen.

God does not scold us for asking sad questions — He sends answers, and sometimes He sends them with a pen in His hand.
