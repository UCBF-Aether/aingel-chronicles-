Judith the Brave

From the Book of Judith, chapters 8–13

Section: Exile, Return, and Brave Scrolls

The little city of Bethulia sat on a hill, and all around it camped a gigantic army. Their captain, Holofernes, had cut off the city's water, and the people were thirsty and frightened. "In five days," said the city's governor sadly, "we must give up."

But in a small tent on her rooftop lived a widow named Judith. Judith loved God, and prayed to Him every day, and she was not ready to give up. She called the governor and the elders to her tent.

"You must not put God to a test," she told them, "and you must not give Him five days, as if He were small. God is not like a man who can be hurried. Let us wait for Him — and trust Him. And tonight," she said, "I am going out of the gate with my maid. God will save our city — and I believe He will do it by my hand."

Then Judith knelt and prayed a great prayer: "O God of my fathers, You are the God of the afflicted, the Helper of everyone who has no helper. Your power does not stand in big armies. Give strength into the hand of a widow, I pray You."

Then she did something surprising. She took off her plain widow's clothes, washed, braided her hair, and put on her most beautiful dress and all her jewels, until she shone like a queen. Her maid packed a bag with bread and figs and oil and wine — Judith would only eat her own clean food — and the two women walked out of the city gate, down the mountain, and straight into the enemy camp.

The soldiers stared. "Who are you?" they asked. "I have come to speak to your captain," said Judith bravely. So they led her to the tent of Holofernes, the most feared captain in the world — and Judith was not afraid, for she knew Who had sent her.

For three nights, Judith went out into the valley to pray beside the spring, and the guards let her pass. And on the fourth night, inside the captain's tent, Judith did a brave and dangerous thing — a thing so bold that the whole earth would hear of it. God gave her the strength, and the cruel captain would never march against her city again.

Early in the morning, two women walked up the mountain to the city gate. "Open! Open the gate!" called Judith. "God, even our God, is with us!"

The whole city ran to meet her, small and great together, and praised God with one voice. And when the great army below woke and found their captain was gone, they panicked and ran away — every one of them — and Bethulia was saved.

"Blessed are you, our daughter," said the governor, "above all the women on the earth — because you did not spare your own life, but walked a straight way before our God."

When everyone else is ready to give up, God can do a mighty thing through one brave person who prays.
