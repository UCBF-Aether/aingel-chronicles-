Three Young Men and the Strongest Thing

From the Book of 1 Esdras, chapters 3–4

Section: Exile, Return, and Brave Scrolls

King Darius feasted princes from a hundred and twenty-seven provinces, India to Ethiopia. That night, three bodyguards competed: "Let each name the STRONGEST thing in the world. Whoever's answer the king judges wisest shall wear purple, drink from gold, sit next to the king!" Each wrote his sentence on a scroll under the king's pillow.

The first wrote: **Wine is the strongest.** The second wrote: **The king is the strongest.** The third — Zorobabel, of God's people — wrote: **Women are the strongest: but above all things, Truth bears away the victory.**

In the morning the king read the scrolls. "Young men — defend your sentences!"

The first stood up. "How strong is wine! It makes king and poor orphan think alike, turns minds to jollity, makes men forget sorrows and debts. Drunk, men draw swords on friends — and next morning can't remember!"

The second stood up. "Men rule sea and land — but the king is mightier. He commands 'build,' and they build; 'plant,' and they plant. And he is only *one* man!"

Then Zorobabel stood up. "It is not the great king who rules men, nor wine. Women bore the king himself — and every soldier! Men give all their gold and silver for love of a wife. I have seen it: a court lady wore the king's crown — and the king just gazed, open-mouthed. If she laughed, he laughed. O men, how can women not be strong?"

But Zorobabel had not finished.

"And yet — great is earth! High is heaven! Swift is the sun, running his circle daily! Therefore great is TRUTH, stronger than all. Wine, kings, people are faulty — their works fade. But truth endures, always strong; she lives and conquers for evermore. With her, no favouritism or bribes — only what is just. Blessed be the God of truth!"

All the people shouted: "GREAT IS TRUTH — AND MIGHTY ABOVE ALL THINGS!"

The king turned to Zorobabel: "Ask more than the prize — whatever you want — you are found wisest."

Zorobabel asked for no gold. "Remember your vow, O king," he said, "to build Jerusalem again, and send back the holy vessels taken away. That is my prize."

King Darius stood and kissed him! He wrote to his governors: let this man and his people travel safely, with cedar from Lebanon. And he sent back the sacred vessels, as vowed.

Zorobabel looked to heaven: "From You comes victory and wisdom, and Yours is the glory — I am Your servant. Blessed are You, giver of wisdom, O Lord of our fathers."

Wine fades, kings fade, strength fades — but Truth endures, and is always strong.
