Esther the Queen Who Was Brave

From the Book of Esther, chapters 1–10 and the added chapters

Section: Exile, Return, and Brave Scrolls

In Shushan, the king chose Esther, an orphan raised by Mordecai. Nobody knew her secret: she was a Jew.

Once, at the king's gate, Mordecai heard two guards plotting against him. He warned Esther, Esther told the king, and he was saved — written in the king's book.

But Haman, the king's helper, loved bows — and one man would not bow. "I bow to God alone," said Mordecai. Haman's heart burned. Hating one man was not enough: he planned to hurt *all* God's people — tricking the king into sealing it with his ring.

Mordecai tore his clothes and sent Esther a message: "Go to the king! Beg for your people! Do not think palace life keeps you safe. Who knows — perhaps you were made queen **for such a time as this**."

Esther trembled. Nobody — not even the queen — could approach the king uninvited; without his golden sceptre held out, you died. "I have not been called for thirty days," she said.

Then came her bravest words: "Gather our people; pray and fast for me three days. Then I will go to the king, though it is against the law. **And if I perish, I perish.**"

Esther changed her clothes and prayed: "O my Lord, You alone are our King. Help me — my only helper."

On the third day, in robes, she stood at the doorway. The king looked up and held out the golden sceptre! She entered, touched its top, and was safe.

"What do you wish, Queen Esther?" said the king. "Even half my kingdom is yours."

Esther smiled. "Let the king — and Haman — come to my feast." At the feast the king asked, "What is your request?" And Esther spoke: "Let my life be given — and my people! A wicked man has planned to destroy us. The enemy is *this* man — Haman!"

The king was astonished, angry — at Haman, not Esther. Haman's plan turned back on himself; the king punished him, and he never troubled anyone again.

But the sealed letter could not be torn up — so the king gave his ring to Mordecai, and a new letter raced on horses: God's people could defend themselves! On the day enemies had hoped to harm them, God's people were safe and glad.

Mordecai went out in blue, white and gold, and the city shouted for joy. Since then, God's people keep **Purim** yearly — feasting, gifts to friends, presents for the poor — remembering the day sorrow turned to joy, because one brave queen spoke.

God can put you in exactly the right place — and give you the courage to speak when it matters most.
