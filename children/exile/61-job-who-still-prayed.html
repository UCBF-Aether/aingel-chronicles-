Job — The Man Who Lost Everything and Still Prayed

From the Book of Job, chapters 1–2, 38, and 42

Section: Exile, Return, and Brave Scrolls

In the land of Uz there lived a man named Job, and there was nobody like him on all the earth. He loved God and did what was right, always. God had blessed him greatly: seven sons and three daughters, thousands of sheep and camels and oxen, and a house full of joy. Every morning Job prayed for his children, just in case any of them had done wrong. That is the kind of father Job was.

But in heaven, God's enemy whispered, "Job only loves You because You give him good things. Take it all away, and see!" So God allowed Job to be tested — and in one terrible day, everything was lost. Raiders took the oxen and the camels; fire took the sheep; a great wind took even his dear family. Job tore his robe and shaved his head in sorrow — but listen to what he said: "The LORD gave, and the LORD has taken away. Blessed be the name of the LORD." He would not blame God.

Then Job became sick and sore all over. His wife said, "Give up!" His friends came and talked and talked, saying it must all be Job's fault somehow. But Job kept praying, and asking, and holding on, even when he did not understand at all.

And then — God answered! Out of the whirlwind, God took Job on the most wonderful tour. "Where were you," God asked, "when I laid the foundations of the earth, and the morning stars sang together? Can you count the clouds? Do you feed the baby ravens?" God showed him the funny ostrich, who laughs at horses; the mighty war horse, who paw-stamps and snorts and is afraid of nothing; and great Leviathan in the deep sea, too wonderful for anyone to tame. Job's mouth fell open. "I have heard of You," he said, "but now my eye sees You." That was enough. God was God — and God was near.

And the end of Job's story is better than the beginning. Job prayed for his friends — and God blessed him with **twice** as much as before: fourteen thousand sheep, six thousand camels, and a new family, seven sons and three daughters, the fairest in the land. Job lived to see his grandchildren's grandchildren, old and full of days.

Even when we don't understand, God is near — and He loves to give good endings.
