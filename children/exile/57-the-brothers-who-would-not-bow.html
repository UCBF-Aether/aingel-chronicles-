The Three Brothers Who Would Not Bow

From the First Book of Meqabyan, chapters 1–10

Section: Exile, Return, and Brave Scrolls

In a land far away there ruled a mighty king whose name was Tseerutsaydan. The king was strong and proud, and he bowed down to idols — statues of gold and silver, wood and stone, which men had made with their own hands. "These are my gods!" he said. "They made me king!" And he sent messengers through all his kingdom with a dreadful order: "Everyone must bow to my gods and bring them sacrifices — or be punished."

But there were three brothers — Abiya, Sila, and Fentos — who loved the true God, the Maker of heaven and earth. The king's men came to them and said, "Bow down, as the king commands!"

The brothers answered as with one voice: "We cannot. There is One to whom we bow — the God of our fathers, who made the sun and the moon and the stars. These idols of yours have no heart and no mind. They cannot smell the food set before them. They cannot see, and they cannot hear. We will neither sacrifice nor bow down to them."

The king was furious. He had the brothers beaten and thrown into a burning house, and for three days and three nights they stayed there, praying. Then he called them out and cried, "Now tell me — will you bow to my gods, or not?"

"We will not bow," they said, "and we will not sacrifice to unclean gods."

So the king sent wild beasts against them — leopards, and bears, and lions! But when the great beasts came to the brothers, a wonderful thing happened: the beasts **bowed down** before them, gentle as lambs, and would not hurt them at all. The beast-keepers trembled, and the king leaped from his throne in fear.

And a voice came from heaven to the brothers, saying: "Abiya, and Sila, and Fentos — as you have seen these who died and rose again, so shall you rise after your death, and your faces shall shine in the kingdom of heaven."

At last the king gave his cruel order, and the brothers gave their lives bravely, praying as they went — for they loved God more than their lives. And God received them. The angels themselves carried their souls home to rest with Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob, in the garden of delight.

And their younger brothers stood up and said: "We will stand where our brothers stood. If we must die, we will die — but we will not bow."

Some things are worth more than life itself — and God never forgets the ones who stand firm for Him.
