King Tseerutsaydan Repents

From the First Book of Meqabyan, chapters 13–20

Section: Exile, Return, and Brave Scrolls

After the three brothers died, King Tseerutsaydan could not rest. That night he lay awake in his palace — and suddenly, there they were! Abiya, Sila, and Fentos stood before his bed, shining bright, though he had thought them dead and gone.

The king's knees trembled. "What do you want of me?" he cried. "What shall I do for you? I did you evil — and I did not know what would come upon me!"

And the brothers answered him kindly — for the way of God's people is mercy. "We are alive," they said, "for God guarded us, because we trusted in Him. And we have come to tell you the truth, for your own sake. These gods of yours are gold and silver, wood and stone — the work of men's hands. They have no soul, no heart, no mind. They did not make you, O king. **You made them!** Leave this mistake behind, and worship God who created you."

"Now I know," whispered the king, "now I know truly that the dead shall rise!"

And from that day, the king turned away from his idols. The proud heart that once said, "My gods made me king!" bent low before the true King — the God who made heaven and earth, the sea, the sun, the moon, and the stars.

For God is the One who humbles the proud and lifts up the lowly. He gives strength to the weak and weakness to the strong. And here is the best news of all, the news the brothers' book sings out loud: **if even a proud king turns and comes back to God, God will love him.** He is not angry forever. He waits to forgive.

So the mighty king of Ethiopia learned the lesson his whole palace full of gold statues could never teach him: there is no other God in heaven above or on earth beneath — and no heart is too proud, and no person too far, for Him to welcome home.

It is never too late to turn around — God loves to forgive, even kings.
