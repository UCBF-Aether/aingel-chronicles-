Nehemiah Builds the Wall

From the Book of Nehemiah, chapters 1–6

Section: Exile, Return, and Brave Scrolls

Nehemiah worked in a faraway palace as the king's cupbearer — who carried the king's cup. But one day his brother Hanani came from Jerusalem. "The wall of Jerusalem is broken down," he said, "and its gates are burned."

Nehemiah wept, fasted and prayed: "O LORD God of heaven, we have sinned — but You promised to gather us home if we turned back. Please give Your servant favour with the king today."

And one day the king said to him: "Why is your face sad? You are not sick. This is sorrow of heart, isn't it?"

Nehemiah was afraid — sadness before a king was forbidden! But he answered bravely: "Let the king live for ever! How could my face not be sad, when my fathers' city lies in ruins?" And in his heart, he prayed to the God of heaven — then asked: "Send me to Jerusalem, so I may build it."

The king said yes! He gave Nehemiah letters for the governors and Asaph, keeper of the forest, for timber. And Nehemiah rode home.

That first night, he told no one. He slipped out at night and rode around Jerusalem — past rubble and burned gates, until ruins piled so deep his horse could not get through. He saw it all himself.

Then he told the people: "Jerusalem lies waste, her gates burned. Come: let us build the wall!"

And the people answered: "Let us build!"

So everybody built! Priests and goldsmiths, fathers and daughters, rulers and merchants — each family took its nearest stretch, and the wall climbed.

But wicked Sanballat and Tobiah laughed. "What are these feeble people doing? If a FOX climbed on that wall of theirs, it would break down!" Nehemiah prayed — and kept building.

When laughing failed, the mockers plotted. So the builders worked, trowel in hand and sword in hand, with a trumpeter by Nehemiah: "Wherever you hear the trumpet, hurry to us. Our God will fight for us!" They worked from dawn until stars.

Then the mockers tried tricks. Four times a messenger came: "Come down to the plain of Ono!" Four times Nehemiah answered: "I am doing a great work, so I cannot come down. Why should the work stop?" They sent a lying letter. Nehemiah answered: "No such things happened. You made them up." And he prayed: "O God — strengthen my hands!"

And the wall went up — until it was finished in **fifty-two days**! And when the enemies heard, even they were afraid — seeing, clear as daylight, God's help in this work.

When mockers laugh and work is big, remember Nehemiah's answer: "I am doing a great work — I cannot come down."
