The Church Begins and Grows

Treasure in Clay Pots

From the Book of 2 Corinthians, chapters 4–5

Section: The Church Begins and Grows

Paul had a picture in his mind, and he wrote it down for his friends in Corinth.

Think of a plain clay pot — the kind anyone might have in their kitchen. It is not made of gold. It is not fancy. It can crack, and it can break. But inside it, someone has hidden a treasure so bright that light pours out through the cracks.

"That is us," said Paul. "We have this treasure in earthen vessels — in plain clay pots — so that everyone can see the power belongs to God, and not to us."

The treasure was the good news of Jesus. God, who at the very beginning said "Let there be light," had shone His light into Paul's heart — and now the light shone out of Paul, the way light shines out of a cracked pot.

Paul's life was not easy, and he did not pretend it was. "We are pressed on every side," he said, "but we are not crushed. We are puzzled sometimes, but we never give up hope. People chase us and hurt us, but God never leaves us. We are knocked down — but we are never knocked out."

How could a clay pot keep going like that? Because of what Paul could see with the eyes of his heart. "Our troubles are small, and they will not last long," he said. "But they are winning for us a glory that will last forever. So we do not look only at the things we can see. The things we can see are passing away. The things we cannot see — God's things — last forever."

And then Paul told them the most comforting secret of all. Our bodies, he said, are like tents. A tent is a good home for a journey, but nobody lives in a tent forever. God has a house waiting for His children — a house not made with hands, in heaven, that will never wear out. "So we walk by faith, not by sight," said Paul. "We trust what God has promised, even before we can see it."

And anyone who belongs to Jesus — Paul saved the best for last — anyone at all: "he is a new creature. The old things have passed away. Look! All things have become new."

You are a clay pot with heaven's light inside — and even your cracks can let it shine.
