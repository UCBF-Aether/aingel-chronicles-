The Church Begins and Grows

Paul's Last Letter

From the Book of 2 Timothy, chapters 3–4

Section: The Church Begins and Grows

This is the last letter Paul ever wrote — and he knew it.

He was an old man now, in a cold prison in Rome, and he could feel the end coming the way you feel winter coming. Some of his friends had left him. Only Luke was still with him. So he took up his pen and wrote to Timothy — his dear son in the faith — one more time.

He reminded Timothy where his faith had started: at home, as a little boy. Timothy's grandmother Lois and his mother Eunice had loved God first, and they had taught him the Holy Scriptures from childhood — bedtime words, morning words, words around the table. "From a child you have known the holy scriptures," Paul wrote proudly, "which are able to make you wise."

And what are the Scriptures? Paul explains it better than anyone: "All scripture is given by inspiration of God" — breathed by God Himself — "and is profitable: for teaching, for correcting, for training in what is right, so that God's people may be complete, ready for every good work."

Then the old apostle passed the torch. "Preach the word," he charged Timothy. "Be ready in season and out of season. Teach it, encourage with it, be patient with people as you teach."

Why so urgent? Because Paul's own preaching was nearly done — and here he wrote the most famous farewell in the Bible:

"I have fought a good fight. I have finished my race. I have kept the faith. And now there is waiting for me a crown of righteousness, which the Lord — the righteous Judge — will give me on that Day. And not to me only, but to everyone who has loved His appearing."

Then, wonderfully, the great letter comes right down to earth, to small ordinary things — the things last letters are really made of. "Do your best to come to me soon," he wrote. "Come before winter. And when you come — bring the cloak I left at Troas. I am cold without it. And bring the books — especially the parchments."

An old man, cold, asking for his coat and his books. And not one bit afraid.

"The Lord stood with me," he wrote, "and strengthened me. And the Lord will deliver me, and preserve me for His heavenly kingdom. To Him be glory for ever and ever. Amen."

Finishing the race with Jesus beside you is worth more than any crown — and He gives the crown as well.
