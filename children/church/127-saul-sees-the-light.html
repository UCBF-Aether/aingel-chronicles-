The Church Begins and Grows

Saul Sees the Light

From the Acts of the Apostles, chapter 9

Section: The Church Begins and Grows

Saul was the fiercest enemy Jesus' friends had. He went house to house in Jerusalem, dragging believers to prison, breathing threats wherever he went. He got letters from the leaders, giving him permission to travel to Damascus and arrest Jesus' friends there too.

So Saul set out for Damascus, with some men.

But as they came near the city, something happened nobody on that road forgot.

Suddenly — a light from heaven flashed around Saul, brighter than the sun at noon! Saul fell to the ground and heard a voice:

"Saul, Saul, why are you hurting Me?"

"Who are You, Lord?" Saul asked.

"I am Jesus — the One you are hurting. Now get up, go into the city, and you will be told what to do."

The men with Saul stood speechless. They heard the voice, but could see nobody.

Saul got up and opened his eyes... and saw *nothing*. He was blind. His friends led him into Damascus. For three days he could not see, and he did not eat or drink anything. He just prayed.

Meanwhile, God spoke to a believer named Ananias in a vision. "Ananias, go to the street called Straight, to the house of Judas, and ask for Saul. He is praying. He has seen you coming to lay hands on him, so he can see again."

"Lord," said Ananias, worried, "I have heard about this man. He has hurt Your people terribly!"

"Go," said God. "Because I have chosen him. He will carry My name to the nations, to kings, and to Israel."

So Ananias went, found the house, went in, laid hands on his enemy — and called him *brother*.

"Brother Saul," he said, "the Lord Jesus, who appeared to you on the road, has sent me — so that you may see again, and be filled with the Holy Spirit."

At once, something like scales fell from Saul's eyes, and he could see! He got up, was baptised, ate, and grew strong again.

Then Saul did the most surprising thing. Straight away, he went to the meeting houses of Damascus and preached: "Jesus is the Son of God!"

Everyone who heard him was amazed. "Isn't this the man who hurt believers in Jerusalem?" they said. "Hasn't he come here to arrest them?" But Saul grew stronger, and nobody could answer him.

The church's fiercest enemy had become its bravest friend — all because, on the Damascus road, Saul saw the Light.

Thank You, Jesus, that no one is too far gone for You — You can turn even Your fiercest enemy into Your bravest friend.
