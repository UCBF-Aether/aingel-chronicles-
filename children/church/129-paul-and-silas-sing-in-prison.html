The Church Begins and Grows

Paul and Silas Sing in Prison

From the Acts of the Apostles, chapter 16

Section: The Church Begins and Grows

In the city of Philippi, Paul and Silas got into trouble for doing good. Paul had set a servant girl free from a spirit that troubled her — and her masters, who had made money from her trouble, were furious. They dragged Paul and Silas to the rulers, and a crowd stirred everything up, and though the two men had done nothing wrong, they were beaten and thrown into prison.

The jailer was told to guard them carefully, so he put them in the deepest, darkest part of the prison — the inner prison — and locked their feet into heavy wooden stocks, so they could not move.

Their backs were sore. Their feet were trapped. It was midnight — the middle of the darkest night.

And what do you think Paul and Silas did at midnight, in the inner prison, with their feet in the stocks?

They prayed — and they *sang*. They sang hymns to God, out loud, in the dark. And all the other prisoners lying around them in the night were listening.

Suddenly — there was a great earthquake! The ground shook and the prison shook on its foundations. And all at once, every door in the prison flew open — and everyone's chains came loose!

The jailer woke up with a crash. He saw the prison doors standing wide open, and his heart dropped into his stomach — he was sure all the prisoners had escaped, and he knew he would be punished terribly. He reached for his sword.

But Paul shouted out of the darkness, with all his strength: "Do not hurt yourself! We are all here!"

The jailer called for lights, and he ran in, trembling all over, and fell down before Paul and Silas. "Sirs," he cried, "what must I do to be saved?"

"Believe in the Lord Jesus Christ," they told him, "and you will be saved — you, and everyone in your house."

Right then, in the middle of the night, Paul and Silas told the jailer and his whole family the good news about Jesus. And that very hour — before the sun came up — the jailer gently washed the wounds on their backs, and he and his whole family were baptised.

Then he brought them up into his house and set food in front of them, and the whole house was full of joy — because now they believed in God.

The men who had spent the night singing in a prison went to sleep in a home, with full tummies, in a family that heaven had just gained.

Thank You, God, that Your people can sing at midnight — and that You can turn the darkest night into a house full of joy before breakfast.
