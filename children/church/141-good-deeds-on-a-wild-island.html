The Church Begins and Grows

Good Deeds on a Wild Island

From the Book of Titus, chapters 2–3

Section: The Church Begins and Grows

Far out in the blue sea sits an island called Crete — and in Paul's day, Crete had a reputation. Its people were rough: loud, quarrelsome, unkind, always getting into trouble. Even the islanders said it about themselves.

And the good news of Jesus came to Crete — to that island, to those people. Paul left his young friend Titus there to care for the brand-new churches, and this letter tells Titus what to teach the islanders who had started to follow Jesus.

The teaching was for everybody, one by one. The grandfathers: be steady and clear-headed, strong in faith and love and patience. The grandmothers: live in a way that honours God, and teach the younger women — to love their families and to make their homes places of kindness. The young people: be sensible and self-controlled. And Titus himself, right in the middle of it: "In all things, show yourself a pattern of good works."

Why would rough islanders change? Paul gives the reason in one of the prettiest sentences in the Bible: "For the grace of God that brings salvation has appeared to all men."

And later he says it again, even better: "When the kindness and love of God our Saviour appeared…" Kindness appeared — in a Person. Jesus came into the world like kindness walking on two feet.

And how were they saved? Not because they had done good enough things — nobody on Crete could say that, and neither can we. "Not by works of righteousness which we have done, but according to His mercy He saved us" — He washed us clean and made us new by His Holy Spirit, so that we could become God's heirs, with the hope of life that never ends.

So what does a washed-clean islander do all day? Paul's answer is practical enough to touch: be ready for every good work. Speak evil of no one. Do not be a brawler. Be gentle — showing all meekness to all people, even the rough ones, because "we ourselves were also once foolish and disobedient" too.

That is the whole secret of Crete's change: people who remembered how lost they had been, treating other lost people gently — and filling a wild island, deed by deed, with good works.

The kindness of God appeared in person once. Now it appears in persons — in Titus, in the grandmothers and grandfathers and children of Crete, and in anyone anywhere who will let it.

God's kindness appeared in a Person — and now it can appear in you, one good deed at a time.
