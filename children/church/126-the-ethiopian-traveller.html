The Church Begins and Grows

The Ethiopian Traveller

From the Acts of the Apostles, chapter 8

Section: The Church Begins and Grows

An angel of the Lord said to Philip: "Get up and go south, to the road that goes down from Jerusalem to Gaza — the desert road."

So Philip got up and went. And on that lonely desert road, he met a traveller.

The traveller was an important man from Ethiopia, far away in Africa. He was the keeper of all the treasure of Candace, the Queen of Ethiopia — and he had made the long, long journey to Jerusalem to worship God. Now he was riding home in his chariot, sitting up high, reading aloud from the book of the prophet Isaiah as he travelled.

The Holy Spirit said to Philip: "Go near, and run alongside that chariot."

So Philip *ran*. He ran beside the chariot in the dust of the desert road until he could hear the traveller reading. And the words he heard were from Isaiah's old song about God's suffering Servant: *"He was led like a sheep... He opened not His mouth."*

"Do you understand what you are reading?" Philip called up.

"How can I," said the traveller, "unless someone guides me?" And he invited Philip to climb up and sit with him in the chariot.

"Tell me," he asked, "who is the prophet talking about? Is it himself — or somebody else?"

And Philip — beginning right there, with that very song of Isaiah — told him the good news about Jesus.

As they travelled on together, they came to some water. And the traveller said: "Look! Here is water! What stops me from being baptised?"

"I believe that Jesus Christ is the Son of God," he said.

He commanded the chariot to stop. And Philip and the traveller both went down into the water together, and Philip baptised him there, beside the desert road.

When they came up out of the water, the Spirit of the Lord took Philip away — and the traveller saw him no more. But he did not mind. He got back into his chariot and went on his way *rejoicing* — all the way home to Ethiopia, carrying the good news in his heart.

And Philip? Philip was found far away, still telling everyone the good news, in every town he came to, all the way up the coast.

Thank You, God, that Your good news runs down every road — even the desert roads — to everybody who is looking for You.
