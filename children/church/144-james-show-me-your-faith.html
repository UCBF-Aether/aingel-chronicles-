The Church Begins and Grows

James Says: Show Me Your Faith

From the Book of James, chapters 1–2

Section: The Church Begins and Grows

James wrote the most practical letter in the Bible — a letter full of things to do. If you are ever unsure what faith looks like on an ordinary Tuesday, James will show you.

Are you in trouble? Count it joy, he says, because hard times grow patience the way rain grows wheat. Do you need wisdom — real wisdom, about what to do? Just ask God! "If any of you lacks wisdom, let him ask of God, who gives to everyone generously and never scolds you for asking." Every good gift and every perfect gift comes down from the Father of lights, who never changes like a shifting shadow.

Then James gets very practical indeed. "Be swift to hear," he says, "slow to speak, and slow to get angry." And: "Be doers of the word — not hearers only." A hearer who never does is like a man who looks at his face in a mirror, walks away, and instantly forgets what he looks like! The mirror told him the truth; he just never did anything about it.

And what does doing look like? James names the purest religion of all — the kind God the Father smiles at: "Visit the fatherless and the widows in their trouble, and keep yourself clean from the world's dirt." The loneliest people, the forgotten people — those are religion's first address.

Then James tells a little story, a scene in church. Two men walk in. One wears gold rings and shining clothes — and everyone hurries: "Sit here! Take the best seat!" The other is poor, in shabby clothes — and he hears: "You stand over there. Sit on the floor by my feet."

"My brothers and sisters," says James, "has not God chosen the poor of this world to be rich in faith? When you seat the rich man high and the poor man low, you are breaking the royal law: You shall love your neighbour as yourself."

And then James lands his famous point — the one this story is named for. Suppose a brother or a sister comes to you with no food and nothing warm to wear, and you say, "Goodbye! Be warm! Be filled!" — but you give them nothing. What good is that? "Faith, if it has no works, is dead — it is all alone, like a body with no breath in it."

"You say you have faith? I will show you my faith — by my works! Abraham believed God, yes — and Abraham acted: he got up, he saddled the donkey, he went. His faith and his deeds walked together, holding hands."

That is James's whole sermon in one sentence: real faith is not what you say with your mouth. It is what you do with your hands.

Faith you can see is faith that feeds, visits, shares, and loves — so let your works preach.
