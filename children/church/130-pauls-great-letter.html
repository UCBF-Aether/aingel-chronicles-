The Church Begins and Grows

Paul's Great Letter

From Paul's Letter to the Romans, chapters 1, 5, and 8

Section: The Church Begins and Grows

Paul was a tentmaker — he worked with his hands, sewing tough cloth into tents. But the most famous thing Paul ever made was not a tent. It was a letter.

Far away was the great city of Rome, full of believers Paul had never met, and Paul longed to visit them. So he wrote them a long letter — the biggest, fullest letter he ever wrote — to tell them the good news properly, from the beginning.

"I am not ashamed of the good news about Jesus," he wrote, "because it is God's own power to save everyone who believes."

The letter explained the problem first. Long ago, people knew God — but they stopped thanking Him, and turned away to worship things they had made themselves, and the whole world wandered off from God and got lost, the way all of us do.

"But God shows His love for us in this," Paul wrote: "while we were still sinners — while we were still lost — Christ died for us."

Because of Jesus, the letter said, we can have *peace* with God. God's love has been poured into our hearts like water from a full jug. And where wrong grew big in the world, God's grace grew even bigger — bigger than all the wrong there is.

Then the letter reached its happiest part. "There is no punishment left," Paul wrote, "for the people who belong to Jesus. God has made us His own children! When we pray, we can call Him *Abba* — Father — the way a little child calls for their daddy. And when we do not even know what to pray, God's own Spirit helps us, and prays for us."

"And we know," wrote Paul, "that God works everything together for good, for the people who love Him. If God is for us — who can be against us? God did not even spare His own Son, but gave Him up for us all!"

And then Paul's pen wrote the strongest sentences in the whole letter — sentences God's people have been holding onto ever since, in storms and prisons and dark nights:

"Who can separate us from the love of Christ? I am certain of this: not death, and not life; not angels, and not rulers; not things happening now, and not things that will happen later; not anything high, and not anything deep — nothing in the whole wide creation can separate us from the love of God!"

The letter was rolled up, and sealed, and sent on its way to Rome. It has been travelling ever since — and it has reached you.

Thank You, God, for Paul's great letter — and for the love that nothing in the whole wide creation can ever take away from us.
