The Church Begins and Grows

Stand Firm

From the Book of 2 Thessalonians, chapters 2–3

Section: The Church Begins and Grows

A frightening rumour had run through the church in Thessalonica. Someone was saying that the great Day of the Lord had already come — that they had somehow missed it, and been left behind!

The people were shaken and scared. So Paul wrote quickly, the way you would write to a friend who had a bad dream.

"Do not be quickly shaken," he told them. "Do not be troubled. That Day has not come — and when the Lord Jesus comes, He will come in brightness that destroys every wicked thing in a breath. No one will miss it, and no one who belongs to Him needs to be afraid of it."

Then he gave them two things to hold on to, like two strong hands.

The first hand: "Stand fast," said Paul. "Hold tight to everything you were taught — the true words you heard from us and read in our letters. Do not let go of the truth just because a rumour blows through town. The Lord Jesus Christ Himself, and God our Father, who loves us, will comfort your hearts and make you strong in every good word and every good work."

The second hand was about everyday life — because standing firm is mostly done on ordinary days. Paul reminded them how he had lived when he was with them: he had worked with his own hands, night and day, making tents, so that he would never be a burden to anyone. "And this was our rule," he said, "if anyone will not work, neither shall he eat. With quietness, work — and eat your own bread."

So no idling about, no stirring up trouble in other people's business — just honest, quiet work, done well.

And if your hands get tired of doing good? Paul had already thought of that too. "Brother, sister," he wrote, "do not grow weary in doing good."

And over the whole letter, like a blessing over a doorway, he put the words that made it all possible: "But the Lord is faithful. He will strengthen you, and He will keep you from evil. The Lord of peace Himself give you peace — always, and by all means."

So stand firm, little believer. Hold the truth with one hand and your work with the other. You are not holding on alone — the faithful Lord is holding on to you.

The Lord is faithful — He holds you fast, so you can stand firm.
