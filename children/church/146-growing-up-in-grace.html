The Church Begins and Grows

Growing Up in Grace

From the Book of 2 Peter, chapters 1 and 3

Section: The Church Begins and Grows

Old Peter wrote this letter near the end of his life — like a grandfather sitting his grandchildren down to tell them how to grow.

"God has given us everything we need," he begins — everything for life, everything for godliness, all through knowing Jesus. And with the gifts came promises — "exceedingly great and precious promises" — so God's children could share in His own nature and grow into His likeness.

And then Peter draws a picture of growing: a staircase. "Add to your faith," he says — one step, then the next:

Add goodness to your faith. Add knowledge to your goodness. Add self-control to your knowledge. Add patience to your self-control. Add godliness to your patience. Add brotherly kindness to your godliness. And at the very top, shining like the sun at the top of the stairs — add love.

"If these things are in you and growing," says Peter, "you will never be idle, and never be unfruitful." Growing is not standing still. Growing is climbing — grace by grace, step by step, kinder, braver, stronger every year.

Peter knew what he was talking about. He had stood on a mountain with Jesus — "we were eyewitnesses of His majesty" — and heard God the Father's voice shake the sky: "This is my beloved Son, in whom I am well pleased." He had seen it all with his own eyes, and wanted the children to know: this is not a clever story somebody invented. We were there. We saw. We heard.

And the Scriptures, he says, are like a lamp shining in a dark place — hold onto that lamp until the morning comes, and the Day Star — Jesus Himself — rises in your hearts.

At the end of the letter Peter looks ahead. Scoffers will come laughing, asking "Where is this coming He promised?" — because the world rolls on, just as it always has. But God is not slow, Peter answers. God is patient. "He is not willing that anyone should perish. He is waiting, because He wants everyone to come home and be saved." One day this world will pass away — and then God will make what He promised long ago: new heavens and a new earth, where righteousness lives — goodness at home at last, by every door, forever.

So how should the children live while they wait? Peter's last words say: "Grow in grace — and in knowing our Lord and Saviour Jesus Christ."

Up the stairs, grandchildren. One step at a time. The top of the stairs is love.

Growing in grace is climbing a staircase — and the top step is love.
