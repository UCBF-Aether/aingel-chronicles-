The Church Begins and Grows

The Little Letter About Onesimus

From the Letter to Philemon

Section: The Church Begins and Grows

The shortest letter Paul ever wrote is about one runaway — and it fits on a single page, the way the most important things sometimes do.

Philemon was a rich man in Colosse, a kind and generous Christian, and the church met in his house. Philemon had a servant named Onesimus — and Onesimus had done wrong, and run away. All the way to Rome he ran, across the sea, into the biggest city in the world, hoping to disappear.

But in Rome, of all the doors in all the streets, Onesimus found his way to Paul — Paul the prisoner, Paul his master's friend. And there, in the prisoner's room, the runaway heard about Jesus, and believed, and his whole heart changed. He stayed and helped Paul, who loved him and called him "my son Onesimus, whom I have begotten in my chains."

Now Paul had a problem. Onesimus belonged in Philemon's house — and by every law and custom, a returned runaway could expect terrible punishment. So Paul picked up his pen and wrote the little letter.

"I could command you," Paul wrote to Philemon. "I have every right. But for love's sake, I would rather ask."

"I am sending Onesimus back to you — and he is my very heart going with him. Receive him the way you would receive me. Not now as a servant, but better than a servant: a beloved brother."

"And if he has wronged you in any way, or owes you anything — put it all on my account. Charge it to me. I, Paul, write this with my own hand: I will repay it."

Then, just at the end, Paul added one more gentle request — the request of a man who fully believed the story was turning out well: "And one more thing, Philemon. Prepare a guest room for me. I hope that soon, through your prayers, I will be free — and I will come and stay with you."

So the runaway walked home — not sneaking, not in chains, but carrying a letter that called him brother, written by a prisoner who was paying his debts for him.

Forgiveness can fit on one page. Sometimes it can fit in one sentence: Receive him as you would receive me.

In God's family, nobody comes home as a servant — everybody comes home as a brother or a sister.
