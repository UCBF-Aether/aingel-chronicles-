The Church Begins and Grows

A Letter to Young Timothy

From the Book of 1 Timothy, chapters 4 and 6

Section: The Church Begins and Grows

Every young person in this book should know Timothy — because the most encouraging letter in the Bible was written to somebody young.

Timothy was a young man, and Paul had left him in charge of a whole church in the city of Ephesus. Grown men and women looked to him for teaching. Sometimes, perhaps, Timothy worried: "Who will listen to me? I am too young. I have nothing important to say yet."

So Paul — his old friend, his teacher, almost a second father — wrote him a sentence that has been cheering up young people ever since: "Let no one despise your youth. Instead, be an example to the believers — in your words, in the way you live, in love, in faith, in being pure."

Read that again, slowly. You do not have to wait until you are old to be an example. A child who speaks kindly is an example. A child who loves honestly is an example. A child whose faith is real is an example — to everybody, even to grown-ups.

Paul filled the letter with grandfatherly advice. "Until I come," he wrote, "give attention to reading." Sit with the Scriptures. Let the words soak in. "Do not neglect the gift that is in you." Whatever God has put in your hands — a song, a kindness, a quick mind, strong arms — do not let it sit in a drawer. Use it, practise it, grow it.

Then Paul taught Timothy the secret of a rich life that has nothing to do with money. "Godliness with contentment is great gain," he wrote. "We brought nothing into this world, and we can carry nothing out. So if we have food and clothing, let us be content with that." And he warned him, plainly and lovingly: beware of loving money — people who chase riches pierce themselves through with many sorrows.

Instead: "Flee those things, and follow after righteousness, godliness, faith, love, patience, gentleness. Fight the good fight of faith! Lay hold on eternal life!"

And what about people who are rich? Tell them, said Paul, not to trust their riches, but to trust God — "and to be rich in good works, generous, always ready to share."

The letter ends the way it began — with Timothy's name: "O Timothy! Keep safe what was trusted to you."

Young reader: that letter has your name in it too.

You are not too young to be an example — your kindness, your faith, and your words already teach.
