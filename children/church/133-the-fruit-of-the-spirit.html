The Church Begins and Grows

The Fruit of the Spirit

From the Book of Galatians, chapter 5

Section: The Church Begins and Grows

"Stand free!" Paul wrote to his friends in Galatia. "Christ has set us free — so do not let anyone tie you up again in the old heavy chains."

But free for what? Not free to be selfish. "You were called to be free," said Paul, "only do not use your freedom to please only yourselves. Use it to serve one another — in love. Because the whole law can be said in one sentence: You shall love your neighbour as yourself."

Then Paul showed them two ways to walk, the way a farmer might show you two paths through an orchard.

One path follows our own selfish wishes — and it grows a sorry crop: hatred and quarrelling, jealousy and hot tempers, selfishness and people split into sides that will not speak. Nothing good ripens there.

The other path follows God's Spirit. "Walk in the Spirit," said Paul. "Let Him lead you, step by step, the way you would follow a kind guide who knows the road."

And what grows on anyone who walks with the Spirit? Not a crop they have to squeeze out of themselves by trying very hard — fruit! Fruit grows quietly, all by itself, on a healthy tree that is rooted and watered. Listen to the fruit Paul counted on the tree: love, joy, peace, patience, kindness, goodness, faithfulness, gentleness, and self-control.

Nine fruits — and they all grow together on the one tree, the way the different fruits of one orchard share the same sun.

Love first — the biggest fruit of all. Then joy, bubbling up. Peace, like deep water. Patience, that can wait without grumbling. Kindness, that notices who is hurting. Goodness, that loves to do right. Faithfulness, that never lets go. Gentleness, strong and soft at once. And self-control, that can say no to itself — and mean it.

"Against such things," Paul laughed, "there is no law at all!" No one ever made a rule that says you may not be too kind.

So the picture is simple. Stay close to the Spirit. Walk where He walks. And one day people will look at your life the way they look at a tree in late summer — branches bending low, heavy with good fruit.

Stay close to God's Spirit, and good fruit will grow in you — quietly, sweetly, all by itself.
