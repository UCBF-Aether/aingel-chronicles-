The Church Begins and Grows

Paul's Joyful Letter from Prison

From the Book of Philippians, chapters 1 and 4

Section: The Church Begins and Grows

Of all Paul's letters, this is the happiest — and Paul wrote it from prison.

He was locked up, chained to guards, waiting to find out what would happen to him. And he sat down and wrote to his dear friends in Philippi, and the letter sings from the first line to the last.

"I thank my God every time I remember you," he wrote. "Every time I pray for you, I pray with joy. And I am sure of this: God, who began a good work in you, will keep on working in you until it is finished."

The prison could not stop the good news — it seemed to spread it! The guards heard about Jesus. The palace heard about Jesus. And Paul's friends, seeing him so brave in chains, grew brave themselves and spoke about Jesus without fear. "Christ is preached," Paul shrugged happily, "and because of that, I rejoice. Yes — and I will keep on rejoicing."

Then, near the end, Paul told them his open secret.

"Rejoice in the Lord always," he wrote. "Again I say: rejoice!"

"Do not worry about anything. Instead, pray about everything. Tell God what you need, and thank Him for all He has done — and the peace of God, which is bigger than anything we can understand, will guard your hearts and your minds."

And when worrying thoughts crowd in, crowd them out with better ones: "Whatever is true, whatever is honourable, whatever is just, whatever is pure, whatever is lovely — think about these things."

Had Paul learned this in easy times? No — in prison he explained it: "I have learned the secret of being content. I know how to have almost nothing, and I know how to have plenty. I have learned it in every place and every time: I can do all things through Christ, who strengthens me."

His friends had sent him a gift of money and food with their messenger Epaphroditus, and Paul's heart spilled over with thanks. "My God shall supply all your need," he promised them, "out of His riches in glory."

So the happiest letter in the Bible was written by a man in chains — which tells you joy was never about the room Paul was sitting in. It was about who was sitting with him in it.

Joy lives wherever Jesus is — even in a locked room, there is something to sing about.
