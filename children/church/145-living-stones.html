The Church Begins and Grows

Living Stones

From the Book of 1 Peter, chapters 2 and 5

Section: The Church Begins and Grows

Peter — Simon Peter, the fisherman who became an apostle — wrote this letter, and in it he told the Christians the most surprising thing about themselves. He did not call them bricks. He called them stones — living stones.

It starts with Jesus. "He is the Living Stone," says Peter. Men looked at Jesus and threw Him away, the way builders throw away a stone that seems no good. But God chose Him — God picked Him up and set Him as the chief Corner Stone of the whole building, the great first stone that every other stone is lined up against. "And anyone who believes on Him will never be put to shame."

"And you," says Peter, turning to the Christians, "you also — as living stones — are being built up into a spiritual house."

A house made of people! Every believer is a stone in God's house, fitted in next to the others, with Jesus as the Corner Stone holding the whole building true. And what a people this house is full of: "You are a chosen generation, a royal priesthood, a holy nation, God's own special people — so that you can tell everyone the praises of Him who called you out of darkness into His marvellous light."

Peter remembers what they were before Jesus: "You were like sheep going astray — wandering off, every one his own way. But now you have returned to the Shepherd and Guardian of your souls." The lost sheep, home again, under the eye of the kindest Shepherd there is.

And Peter shows them how the house is held together: by following Jesus' footsteps. When people hurt Jesus, He did not threaten back. He handed everything over to His Father, who judges fairly. The stones can do the same.

Then the letter turns, at the end, to the shepherds — the older Christians who care for the flock. "Feed the flock of God," Peter tells them, "not because you must, but gladly; not for money, but with a willing heart." And when the Chief Shepherd appears, He will give them a crown of glory that never fades away.

And for everyone else — the young, the old, the worried, the tired — Peter saves his tenderest words of all: "Humble yourselves under the mighty hand of God, and He will lift you up in time. Cast all your care upon Him — every worry, every fear, every heavy thing — for He cares for you."

Stay awake and watchful, he adds, because the devil prowls about like a roaring lion, looking for someone to frighten — but stand fast in your faith, held by the Shepherd's hand. And at the end, the great promise: "The God of all grace will Himself restore you, strengthen you, and settle you — firm as a stone in His house, forever."

You are not a brick in a pile — you are a living stone in God's house, and the Shepherd of your soul cares for you.
