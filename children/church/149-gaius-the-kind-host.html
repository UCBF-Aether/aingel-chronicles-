The Church Begins and Grows

Gaius the Kind Host

From the Third Letter of John

Section: The Church Begins and Grows

Old John wrote this tiny letter to his dear friend Gaius. Just saying his name made John happy.

"Dear Gaius," he wrote, "I love you in the truth. I pray that everything goes well with you, and that you are as healthy in your body as you are strong in your soul."

People kept arriving at John's door with stories about Gaius. They said: Gaius walks in the truth. Gaius is faithful. "Nothing makes me happier," John wrote, "than hearing that my children walk in the truth."

But the best story of all was about Gaius's open door.

Travelling teachers used to walk from town to town, telling everyone about Jesus. They carried no money-bags, and they took nothing from people who did not know God yet. Who would feed them? Who would give them a bed?

Gaius would.

When the travellers came to his town, Gaius welcomed them in — brothers, and even strangers he had never met before. He fed them, he rested them, and when they left, he sent them on their way with everything they needed, in a manner worthy of God.

"You do a faithful thing," John told him, "whatever you do for these travellers. We should welcome people like them — then we are working together with them for the truth."

John had to warn him about one man, too: Diotrephes, who loved to be the most important. Diotrephes would not welcome the travellers. He even tried to push welcoming people out of the church. "Don't copy what is wrong," John wrote. "Copy what is good. The one who does good is from God."

And he praised another friend, Demetrius — everyone spoke well of Demetrius, and the truth itself spoke well of him.

Then, like the end of 2 John, comes the smile: "I had so much to write to you — but I would rather not use pen and ink. I hope to see you very soon, and we will talk face to face."

"Peace be with you. Your friends here send their love. Greet our friends there, each one by name."

A door opened in love is a door opened for God Himself — be a Gaius, and welcome them in.
