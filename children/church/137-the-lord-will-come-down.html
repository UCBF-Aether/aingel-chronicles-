The Church Begins and Grows

The Lord Will Come Down

From the Book of 1 Thessalonians, chapter 4

Section: The Church Begins and Grows

Some of Paul's friends in Thessalonica were carrying a heavy sadness. People they loved had died — grandparents, friends, brothers and sisters in the faith — and they were asking a hard question: "When Jesus comes back, will we ever see them again? Or are they lost to us forever?"

So Paul wrote them one of the most comforting letters in the whole Bible.

"Brothers and sisters," he said, "I do not want you to be sad the way people are sad who have no hope. We do have hope — the biggest hope there is."

And this is the hope. Jesus died, and Jesus rose again — really rose, truly alive. So God will bring with Jesus all the people who have fallen asleep in Him. Falling asleep is what Paul calls it: not gone, not lost — asleep, the way you are asleep in your bed at night, safe, while the morning is on its way.

"Here is what will happen," Paul wrote, and you can almost hear him leaning closer.

"The Lord Himself will come down from heaven — with a shout! With the voice of the archangel calling out! With the trumpet of God sounding over the whole world!"

And first of all — before anyone else moves — the dead in Christ will rise. Grandmother will rise. Every sleeping friend of Jesus will wake up to the sound of that trumpet, alive and shining and never to die again.

"Then we who are still alive," said Paul, "will be caught up together with them in the clouds, to meet the Lord in the air."

Together. That was the word Paul wanted them to hold: together — the ones who slept and the ones who waited, all caught up in one great crowd, rising like larks at sunrise to meet the King coming down.

"And so," he finished, "we shall always be with the Lord. Always. Nobody will ever be lost again, and nobody will ever be left behind, and the together will never end."

Then Paul gave them their job — the job for anyone who knows this story: "So comfort one another with these words."

When someone you love is sad because death has visited their house, you know what to tell them now. Tell them about the trumpet. Tell them about the morning. Tell them about together — forever.

Jesus is coming down, the trumpet will sound, and God's family will be together — always.
