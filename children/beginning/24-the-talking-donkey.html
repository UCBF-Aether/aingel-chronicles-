The Beginning and the Fathers

The Talking Donkey

From the Book of Numbers, chapter 22

Section: The Beginning and the Fathers

Far away in the land of Moab, King Balak looked out and saw God's people camped on the plains — a nation as wide as the sea. The king was frightened, so he sent messengers with gifts to a man named Balaam, who was known for speaking with God.

“Come,” said the king's message, “and curse this people for me, for they are too mighty for me.”

Balaam asked God, and God said clearly: “You shall not go with them. You shall not curse the people — for they are blessed.” So Balaam sent the messengers home.

But King Balak sent grander princes, with promises of silver and gold and great honour. This time, God let Balaam go — with one strict rule: “Only the word that I speak to you — that you shall speak.”

Early in the morning Balaam saddled his little donkey and rode off down the road.

But on the road stood the angel of the LORD, with a shining sword drawn in his hand, right in Balaam's path.

Balaam could not see the angel. The donkey could!

She veered off the road into a field. Balaam, angry, struck her and pulled her back onto the road.

Further on, the path squeezed between two vineyard walls — and there stood the angel again. The donkey pressed herself against the wall to get past, and squashed Balaam's foot! Balaam struck her again.

Further still, the path became so narrow there was no turning right or left — and there stood the angel a third time. Nowhere to go! So the wise little donkey simply lay down in the road.

Balaam was furious. He raised his staff to strike her a third time —

— and God opened the donkey's mouth!

“What have I done to you,” said the donkey, “that you have hit me these three times?”

And Balaam — too angry to even be surprised that his donkey was talking — answered her, just as if donkeys talked every day! “Because you have made fun of me! If I had a sword in my hand, I would finish you!”

“Am I not your own donkey,” she said, “the one you have ridden all your life, up to this very day? Have I ever, ever done anything like this to you before?”

And Balaam had to admit it: “No. Never.”

At that moment, God opened Balaam's eyes — and Balaam saw the angel standing in the road with the sword, and he fell flat on his face in the dust. “I have sinned,” he cried. “I did not know you were standing against me. If my going displeases you, I will turn straight back!”

“Go on with the men,” said the angel. “But speak only the words I give you — nothing else.”

And when Balaam finally opened his mouth over God's people, out came — not curses, but blessings! Again and again, blessings. God's people are blessed, and not even a king's gold can change that.

God can speak through anyone — even a little donkey, who saw what a wise man couldn't.
