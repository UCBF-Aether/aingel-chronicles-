The Beginning and the Fathers

God's Holy Days

From the Book of Leviticus, chapter 23

Section: The Beginning and the Fathers

Did you know that God gave His people a calendar of special days? Not just ordinary days of work — but rests, feasts, and parties spread all through the year, so that His people would stop, remember, and be glad.

First, and most often, came the Sabbath. Every week, on the seventh day, all work stopped. No plowing, no sewing, no carrying, no cooking all day — nothing but rest. Fathers and mothers, children, servants, even the animals got a whole day off, to be quiet and be with God. Fifty-two holidays a year, built right into every week!

In the spring came the Passover — the night to remember! That was the night God had brought His people out of Egypt with a mighty hand. Families ate the special meal together and told the great story to their children, year after year after year, so nobody would ever forget.

Then, for seven days after Passover, came the Feast of Unleavened Bread — a whole week of flat, crackly bread without yeast, baked in a hurry just like their great-grandparents had baked it on the night they left Egypt in a hurry.

When the golden grain harvest came in, the people brought the very first sheaf of it to God's house, waving it before Him with thankful hearts — first fruits for God, because every field was His gift. Then they counted seven whole weeks, day by day, seven times seven — and on the fiftieth day came the Feast of Weeks, the great harvest party, with fresh loaves of new bread.

In the autumn, a trumpet blast opened the New Year — the Feast of Trumpets. Long horns sang out over the hills: a new year is beginning!

Soon after came one quiet, still day — the Day of Atonement. No feasting that day — it was the day of saying sorry for every wrong thing, so that everyone's heart could be washed clean and start the year new.

And then — the happiest feast of all — the Feast of Booths! For seven whole days, every family built a little shelter in the open air, out of leafy branches: palm branches and boughs of thick trees and willows from the brook. And they lived in their little branch-houses — eating there, sleeping there, singing there under the stars — rejoicing before the LORD for seven days. Why? “So that your children and grandchildren will know,” said God, “that I made the children of Israel live in booths when I brought them out of the land of Egypt.” Camping out with the whole family, to remember God's care — what a party!

Rest days and feast days, trumpet days and sorry days and camping days — God's whole year was full of remembering, because He wanted His people close, and thankful, and glad.

God's year was full of parties, rests, and remembering — He wanted His people happy, thankful, and close.
