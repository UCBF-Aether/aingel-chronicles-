The Beginning and the Fathers

Bread from Heaven

From the Book of Exodus, chapter 16

Section: The Beginning and the Fathers

God's people were out in the desert now, free from Egypt — but the desert is a great, dry, empty place, and after a while all the food they had carried was gone.

Hungry tummies rumbled all through the camp. The people began to grumble to Moses and Aaron: “In Egypt we sat by pots full of meat, and ate all the bread we wanted! Now we are hungry out here.”

Moses prayed, and God answered with a promise: “Behold, I will rain bread from heaven for you.”

That same evening, the sky filled with birds — quail, more quail than anyone could count — fluttering down all over the camp. That night there was meat for everyone.

And in the morning, the whole camp woke to a wonder. Dew lay wet all around the tents. When the dew dried away, there on the ground lay something small and round and white, fine and frosty, covering the desert like a sparkling blanket.

“What is it?” the people asked each other. (That is how it got its name — manna, which sounds like “What is it?” in their language.)

Moses said, “It is the bread which the LORD has given you to eat. Gather it up — everyone, as much as your family needs.”

So out they went with baskets and pots, grandmothers and fathers and children, gathering the gift off the ground. And when they measured it at home, something beautiful happened: the one who gathered lots had none left over, and the one who gathered a little had exactly enough. There was always enough.

And the taste? It was sweet and good, like thin wafers made with honey.

God gave one rule with the gift: “Take only today's bread today. Trust Me for tomorrow.” Some people did not listen and stuffed extra away for the morning — but by morning it had spoiled and gone bad. Trust would have tasted better!

On the sixth day, the gatherers found their baskets filled with twice as much. Moses explained: “Tomorrow is God's holy day of rest. Bake today and boil today, and keep the rest for tomorrow.” And the sixth day's bread stayed fresh and sweet — God's day-off present.

Moses had one pot of manna kept safe in a special place, so that children and grandchildren for years to come could see the bread God fed their families in the desert.

And God kept His promise morning after morning — for forty whole years, all the way to the promised land.

Every single morning for forty years, breakfast came down from the sky — because God had not forgotten His hungry people.
