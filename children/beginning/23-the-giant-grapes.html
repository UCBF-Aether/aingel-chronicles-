The Beginning and the Fathers

Twelve Spies and the Giant Grapes

From the Book of Numbers, chapters 13–14

Section: The Beginning and the Fathers

At last God's people stood at the edge of the promised land God had been leading them to.

Moses chose twelve men, one leader from each of the twelve tribes, and sent them in first as spies. “Go and see the land,” he told them. “See what it is like, and see the people who live there — whether they are strong or weak, few or many. And bring back some of the fruit of the land!”

Off they went, walking the land from end to end for forty days. They saw green valleys and strong cities. And in a valley called Eshcol, they found a grapevine with a cluster of grapes so enormous that they cut it down and carried it hanging from a long pole, one man at each end, staggering under its sweet, purple weight! They picked fat pomegranates and figs too.

After forty days the twelve came back, and the camp crowded around to hear. They set down the pole with the giant grapes, and reported:

“We went into the land, and truly it flows with milk and honey — look at its fruit!”

But then ten of the spies shook their heads. “The people who live there are strong. The cities have huge walls. And worse — we saw giants there, the children of Anak! Next to them, we felt like little grasshoppers!”

Fear spread through the camp like a cold wind. All that night the people cried, and wished they had never left Egypt.

But two of the spies — Caleb and Joshua — believed God. Caleb stood up and quieted the crowd: “Let us go up at once and take the land — we are well able!”

And Joshua cried out: “The land we walked through is an exceeding good land! If the LORD is pleased with us, He will bring us into it and give it to us. Only — do not turn away from the LORD, and do not be afraid!”

But the people would not listen. Their fear was bigger than their trust.

God's heart was grieved. Because the grown-ups had been too afraid to trust Him, they would wander in the desert for forty years, and not enter the good land. But God promised that their children would go in one day — and Caleb and Joshua, the two who believed, would go in too.

It was a sad lesson. Ten spies saw giants and forgot God. Two spies saw the same giants — and remembered that God was bigger.

Ten men saw giants, but two men saw God bigger — and one day the children would eat those grapes.
