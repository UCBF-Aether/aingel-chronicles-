The Beginning and the Fathers

Three Visitors and a Promise

From the Book of Genesis, chapter 18

Section: The Beginning and the Fathers

It was the hot middle of the day, and Abraham was sitting in the door of his tent, resting in the shade of the great oak trees of Mamre.

He looked up — and there, suddenly, stood three men nearby.

Abraham jumped up and ran to meet them, and bowed low to the ground. “My lord,” he said, “please do not pass by. Stay a while! Rest here under the tree. I will bring water to wash your dusty feet, and a little bread to make you strong for your journey.”

The visitors said, “Do as you have said.”

Abraham hurried into the tent. “Sarah! Quick — knead some fine flour and bake cakes!” Then he ran to the herd, chose a good young calf, and had it cooked. He set out curds and milk and the tender meat, and stood by under the tree while his guests ate their feast in the cool shade.

Then one of the visitors asked, “Where is Sarah, your wife?”

“Here, in the tent,” said Abraham.

And the visitor said the most surprising words: “I will come back to you at this time next year — and Sarah your wife shall have a son.”

Behind the tent door, Sarah was listening. Sarah was very old, and Abraham was very old too — nearly a hundred years! A baby? Sarah laughed inside herself. “After I am grown old, shall I have a baby?”

But the visitor was the LORD Himself, and He heard the laugh behind the tent door. He said to Abraham, “Why did Sarah laugh? Is any thing too hard for the LORD? At the time I have promised, I will return — and Sarah shall have a son.”

Sarah grew frightened. “I did not laugh,” she said.

And the LORD answered, gentle and sure: “No — but you did laugh.”

He knew. And He was not angry. The promise stood, as sure as the sunrise: within the year, old Sarah would be holding a baby boy in her arms — the very baby God had promised Abraham for so many years.

God heard Sarah's laugh behind the tent door — and He answered it with a baby.
