The Beginning and the Fathers

The Tower That Tried to Touch Heaven

From the Book of Genesis, chapter 11

Section: The Beginning and the Fathers

Long, long ago, every person in the whole world spoke the same language, and everyone could understand everyone else.

The people traveled together until they found a wide, flat plain called Shinar, and there they settled down. And the people said to one another, “Come on! Let us make bricks, and bake them hard in the fire.” They had no stones in that flat land, so bricks were their stones, and sticky clay was what held them together.

Then they had a bigger idea. “Come on!” they said. “Let us build ourselves a city — and a tower! A tower whose top may reach unto heaven! And let us make a name for ourselves, so that everyone will know how great we are!”

So the building began. Brick upon brick, story upon story, the tower climbed up and up toward the clouds. The builders carried the bricks higher and higher, and every day they looked up and felt very proud. Higher than the hills! Higher than anyone had ever built!

But God came down to look at the city and the tower that the people were building. God saw their proud hearts. They were not building toward Him — they were building toward themselves. And God knew that if they went on like this, nothing they dreamed up would be beyond them.

So God did something gentle and clever. He mixed up their language.

Suddenly, up on the tower, a builder called out for a brick — and his friend just stared at him. The words came out of his mouth all jumbled, in a language nobody had ever heard. Down below, a man asked for clay and got a puzzled shrug. Another man answered in sounds like music from a faraway country. Nobody could understand anybody!

The builders tried shouting. They tried pointing. But at last they had to climb down from the tower, because people who cannot talk to each other cannot build together. Family by family, the people wandered away in every direction across the wide earth — which was God's plan for them all along, that the whole earth should fill up with people.

The great tower was never finished. And the place was called Babel, because there God mixed up the language of all the earth.

The tower never did touch heaven — but God's whole wide earth filled up with people, just as He had planned.
