Moses and the Burning Bush

From the Book of Exodus, chapters 3–4 and 14

Section: The Beginning and the Fathers

God's people, the children of Israel, were slaves in Egypt, and their lives were very hard. But God saw them. God heard them crying. And God had a plan — and His plan started with a shepherd named Moses.

Moses was watching his sheep near a great mountain when he saw something strange. A bush was on fire — flames leaping and crackling! — but the bush was not burning up. The leaves stayed green inside the flames.

"That's amazing," Moses said. "I must go and look."

As he came close, a voice called out of the fire: "Moses! Moses!"

"Here I am," said Moses, trembling.

"Take off your shoes," said God, "for the ground where you stand is holy. I am the God of Abraham, of Isaac, and of Jacob. I have seen how hard my people's lives are in Egypt, and I have heard their crying. I am sending you to Pharaoh, the king, to bring my people out."

Moses was afraid. "Who am I, that I should go to Pharaoh?"

God said the words that matter most of all: "I will be with you."

God gave Moses a helper, his brother Aaron. And Moses went — an old shepherd with a staff in his hand, walking into the palace of the most powerful king on earth, carrying God's message: "Let my people go!"

Pharaoh said no, again and again, even when God sent trouble after trouble upon Egypt to show His power. But at last Pharaoh let the people go, and Israel marched out of Egypt free — fathers and mothers, children and lambs, a whole nation walking into the sunrise.

Then Pharaoh changed his mind. His army came thundering after them — horses and chariots and spears — and in front of the people lay the great wide sea. There was nowhere to run!

"Do not be afraid," Moses told the people. "Stand still, and see what God will do." Moses stretched out his hand over the sea — and God sent a strong wind, all night long, that pushed the water back and made a dry road right through the middle of the sea, with the water standing tall like walls on both sides.

The children of Israel walked through on dry ground, every single one of them. And when Pharaoh's army tried to follow, the sea came back together, and God's people stood safe on the far shore, singing a song of joy to God — free at last.

When there is no way out, God can make a way — right through the middle of the sea.
