The Beginning and the Fathers

The Garden and the First People

From the Book of Genesis, chapters 2–3

Section: The Beginning and the Fathers

God planted a garden in a place called Eden, and it was the most beautiful garden ever made. Four rivers ran through it. Trees grew there heavy with good fruit, and in the middle of the garden stood two special trees: the Tree of Life, and the Tree of the Knowledge of Good and Evil.

God put the man, Adam, in the garden to care for it. "You may eat from every tree," God said, "except one. Do not eat from the Tree of the Knowledge of Good and Evil."

God gave Adam a wonderful job: naming all the animals. The elephants and the eagles, the lions and the lambs — Adam named them all. But among them all there was no helper just right for him. So God made the woman, Eve, from Adam's side while he slept, and Adam woke up and was glad.

Adam and Eve lived in the garden with nothing to fear and nothing to hide. God Himself walked with them in the cool of the day.

But a serpent came to Eve, and the serpent lied. "Did God really say not to eat?" it whispered. "Eat, and you will be like God." Eve looked at the fruit. It looked so good. She ate, and she gave some to Adam, and he ate too.

Suddenly everything felt different. They were ashamed, and afraid, and when they heard God walking in the garden, they hid among the trees.

But God came looking for them. "Adam, where are you?" God called — because God always comes looking for the people He loves.

There were sad consequences, the way there always are when we don't listen. Life would be harder now. Adam and Eve had to leave the beautiful garden, and an angel with a shining sword guarded the way back to the Tree of Life.

But listen — before they left, God made them a promise. One day, a child would be born from Eve's own family who would crush the serpent and mend everything the serpent had spoiled. And God made clothes for Adam and Eve with His own hands, to cover them and keep them warm.

They walked out of the garden into a wide, hard world. But God went with them. And the promise went with them too.

God came looking for His children in the garden — and He has been coming to find us ever since.
