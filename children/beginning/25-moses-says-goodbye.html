The Beginning and the Fathers

Moses Says Goodbye

From the Book of Deuteronomy, chapters 31–34

Section: The Beginning and the Fathers

Moses was one hundred and twenty years old. For forty years he had led God's people — out of Egypt, through the sea, across the desert — and now, at last, they stood at the border of the promised land.

But God had told Moses that he would not cross the river Jordan into the land. So Moses called all the people together for his goodbye.

“I am a hundred and twenty years old today,” he told them. “I can no longer go out and come in with you, and the LORD has told me I shall not cross over Jordan. But do not be afraid! The LORD your God — He goes over before you. Be strong, and of good courage. Do not fear, and do not be troubled, for the LORD your God goes with you. He will not fail you, and He will never, ever leave you.”

Then Moses called Joshua, his helper for many years, and in front of everyone he put the leadership into Joshua's hands, and spoke the same brave words to him: “Be strong and of a good courage — for you shall bring the children of Israel into the land I promised them. And I will be with you, says the LORD.”

Moses wrote down all of God's teaching in a book, and gave it to the priests to keep safely beside the ark, so the people would always have God's words. And God gave Moses a song to teach the people — a song to carry in their hearts and sing down the years, so they would never forget.

Then God called His old friend up the mountain — Mount Nebo. Slowly Moses climbed, up and up, until he stood at the very top, all alone with God.

And God showed him the whole promised land. Hills and valleys. Green fields and the winding river. The city of palm trees, Jericho. Hills rolling away to the far-off sea. All the land God had promised to Abraham, to Isaac, to Jacob.

“I have let you see it with your eyes,” said God.

And there on the mountain, Moses died — as God had said — the servant of the LORD. God Himself buried him, gently, in the valley, and no one has ever known the place. Moses was a hundred and twenty years old, but his eyes were not dim, and his strength had not faded.

Down below, the people wept for Moses for thirty days. Then they dried their tears and stood behind Joshua — who was full of wisdom, for Moses had laid his hands on him — and followed him across the river into the land.

And Israel has never had another prophet quite like Moses — the man whom the LORD knew face to face.

Moses never stepped into the land — but he saw it, and he left his people God's brave words: “He will not fail you, and He will never leave you.”
