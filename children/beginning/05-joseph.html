The Beginning and the Fathers

Joseph and the Beautiful Coat

From the Book of Genesis, chapters 37 and 39–45

Section: The Beginning and the Fathers

Jacob had twelve sons, and Joseph was dearly loved. Jacob made Joseph a beautiful coat of many colours, and Joseph wore it everywhere.

But Joseph's brothers were not kind to him. They were jealous of the coat, and jealous of their father's love, and one day, out in the fields, they did a cruel thing: they took Joseph's beautiful coat and sold their own brother to travelling merchants, who carried him away to a far country called Egypt. Then they told their father a sad lie, and Jacob wept and wept.

In Egypt, Joseph worked hard and did everything well, and God was with him. But a lie was told about Joseph there too, and he was put in prison — even though he had done nothing wrong. Still God was with Joseph, even in prison. Joseph helped the other prisoners, and he explained their dreams for them, because God gave him understanding.

One night Pharaoh, the king of Egypt, had a strange dream: seven fat, healthy cows came up from the river — and then seven thin, hungry cows, and the thin cows ate up the fat ones! Nobody could explain it. But somebody remembered Joseph.

Joseph stood before the king and said, "God is showing you what is coming. There will be seven years of plenty, with food growing everywhere — and then seven years of hunger. Store up food now, while there is plenty."

Pharaoh saw that God's wisdom was in Joseph. He took the ring from his own finger and put it on Joseph's hand, and dressed him in fine linen with a gold chain, and made him ruler over all Egypt — second only to the king himself! Joseph filled storehouses with grain.

When the hungry years came, people travelled from every country to buy food in Egypt. And one day, ten hungry brothers came bowing down before the ruler of Egypt. They did not know him. But Joseph knew them.

Joseph's heart pounded. He waited until he could see that his brothers had changed — that they were sorry, and kinder now. And then, weeping with joy, he said the words they never expected to hear:

"I am Joseph — your brother! Do not be sad, and do not be angry with yourselves. God sent me here ahead of you, to keep everybody alive."

And he hugged them all, and forgave them completely, and sent wagons to bring his father Jacob down to Egypt, where there was food and safety and family — together again.

God can take even the unkind things and weave them into something good — He never stopped being with Joseph, and He never stops being with us.
