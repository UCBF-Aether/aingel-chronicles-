The Beginning and the Fathers

Abraham and the Stars

From the Book of Genesis, chapters 12, 15, 17 and 22

Section: The Beginning and the Fathers

There was a man named Abram who lived in a faraway city. One day God spoke to him: "Leave your country and your father's house, and go to a land I will show you. I will make you into a great nation, and I will bless you — and through you, every family on earth will be blessed."

Abram was seventy-five years old. But he packed up his tents, took his wife Sarai, and went — all that way, just trusting God's word.

One night God brought Abram outside and said, "Look up at the sky. Count the stars, if you can." Abram looked up. Stars and stars and stars, more than anyone could ever count. "That's how many children your family will have," God said. And Abram believed God — and God counted that believing as goodness.

Years went by. Abram became Abraham, and Sarai became Sarah. They grew very old — Abraham was a hundred, and Sarah was ninety — and still there was no baby. But God keeps His promises, even slow ones. At last, just as God had said, Sarah had a little boy. They named him Isaac, which means *laughter* — because God had made them laugh with joy.

Then came a hard morning. God asked Abraham to take Isaac up a mountain and give him back to God. Abraham's heart must have ached all the way up that mountain. But he trusted that God was good, even when he could not understand. When Isaac asked, "Father, where is the lamb?" Abraham answered, "God will provide the lamb, my son."

And God did. Just at the right moment, an angel called from heaven: "Abraham! Abraham! Do not hurt the boy. Now I know that you trust God with everything." And there, caught in a bush, was a ram — the lamb God had provided. Abraham and Isaac thanked God together on that mountain, and went home with glad hearts.

God said to Abraham, "Because you trusted me with your very best, I will bless your family beyond counting — like the stars in the sky, like the sand on the shore."

God always keeps His promises — and He provided the lamb Himself.
