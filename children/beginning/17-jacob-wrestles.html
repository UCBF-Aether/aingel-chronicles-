The Beginning and the Fathers

Jacob Wrestles Until Morning

From the Book of Genesis, chapter 32

Section: The Beginning and the Fathers

After many years in a far country, Jacob was coming home at last — with his wives, his children, and great flocks of sheep and goats and camels.

But there was one thing Jacob feared. Somewhere ahead was his brother Esau. Long ago, Jacob had tricked Esau and taken what should have been his, and Esau had been angry. Now messengers came running back with news: “Your brother Esau is coming to meet you — with four hundred men!”

Jacob's heart sank. He divided his family into two groups, so that if one was caught, the other might escape. He sent rich gifts of animals ahead for Esau, drove after drove. And he prayed: “O God of my fathers, You told me to come home, and You promised to do me good. Save me, I pray, from my brother's hand.”

At night, Jacob helped his family cross the sparkling brook called Jabbok — and then he stayed behind, all alone in the dark.

And in the night, a Man came to Jacob — and wrestled with him.

All night long they wrestled by the river, twisting and gripping under the stars. Jacob would not give up. When the Man saw that Jacob would not stop, He touched the hollow of Jacob's thigh, and Jacob's hip slipped out of its place with a jolt of pain. Still Jacob held on, limping and clinging, holding on with everything he had.

As the sky began to pale, the Man said, “Let me go, for the day is breaking.”

But Jacob gasped, “I will not let You go — not unless You bless me!”

“What is your name?” asked the Man.

“Jacob.”

“Your name shall be Jacob no more,” He said. “From now on your name is Israel — because you have wrestled with God and with men, and you have held on.”

Jacob asked, “Please, tell me Your name.”

But the Man only answered, “Why do you ask?” — and He blessed Jacob there.

When it was over, Jacob stood alone in the early light, amazed that he was alive. He called the place Peniel, which means “the face of God,” and he said, “I have seen God face to face — and my life is safe.”

The sun rose on Jacob as he walked on to meet his brother — limping on his hurt leg, blessed with a brand-new name, and no longer afraid.

Jacob limped into the sunrise with a new name — Israel, the man who would not let go of God.
