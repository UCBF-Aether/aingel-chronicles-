The Beginning and the Fathers

Jacob's Ladder

From the Book of Genesis, chapter 28

Section: The Beginning and the Fathers

Jacob was on a long journey, all alone, walking to a faraway country to find his mother's family. He had no house, no tent, and no traveling friends — just his staff in his hand and the clothes he wore.

As the sun went down, Jacob came to a quiet place in the open country. It was time to stop for the night. So he took one of the stones of that place and put it under his head for a pillow, and lay down on the bare ground to sleep, with the stars for his blanket.

And Jacob dreamed.

He dreamed of a great ladder — a stairway — set down on the earth, with its top reaching all the way up to heaven. And on it, God's angels were going up and coming down, up and down, in a river of light.

And above the ladder stood the LORD. And God spoke to Jacob in the dream:

“I am the LORD, the God of Abraham your grandfather, and the God of Isaac. The land where you are lying — I will give it to you, and to your children after you. Your family will grow and spread out over the whole earth, and through you, every family on earth will be blessed. And remember this: I am with you. I will watch over you everywhere you go, and I will bring you back to this land. I will not leave you.”

Jacob woke up out of his sleep, and his heart was thumping. He looked around at the same stones and bushes, but now everything felt different.

“Surely the LORD is in this place,” he said, “and I did not know it! How wonderful and holy this place is! This is God's own house — and this is the gate of heaven!”

Early in the morning, Jacob took the stone that had been his pillow and set it standing upright as a marker. He poured oil over the top of it, the way you honor something precious, and he called the place Bethel — which means “the house of God.”

Then Jacob made a promise back to God: “If God will be with me and keep me on this journey, and give me bread to eat and clothes to wear, and bring me home again in peace — then the LORD shall be my God.”

And Jacob walked on down the road — alone no longer.

Jacob slept with a stone for a pillow — and woke up knowing he was not alone.
