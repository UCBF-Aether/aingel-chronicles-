The Beginning and the Fathers

God Makes Everything

From the Book of Genesis, chapter 1

Section: The Beginning and the Fathers

In the very beginning, there was God — and nothing else at all.

No sun. No moon. No ground under anyone's feet. Just darkness and deep water, and God's Spirit moving gently over it, the way a mother bird hovers over her nest.

Then God spoke. "Let there be light."

And there was light! Bright and warm and good. God called the light Day, and the darkness He called Night. That was the first day.

On the second day, God made the wide blue sky above the waters.

On the third day, God gathered the waters together and said, "Let the dry land appear." Up came the ground — hills and valleys and shores. And God said, "Let the earth grow green things," and up sprang grass and flowers and trees with fruit, with seeds inside the fruit so more trees could grow.

On the fourth day, God set lights in the sky: the great golden sun to rule the day, the silver moon to rule the night, and stars — more stars than anyone can count — twinkling all across the dark.

On the fifth day, the waters came alive. Fish darted and whales splashed, and the sky filled with birds, singing and soaring on wide wings.

On the sixth day, God made the animals: cows and lions, elephants and tiny ants, everything that walks and creeps on the earth. And then God made the best thing of all. He made people — a man and a woman — in His own image, to look after the whole beautiful world He had made, the way He looks after it.

God looked at everything He had made. And it was very good.

On the seventh day, God rested. He blessed that day and made it holy, a day of quiet and gladness, to remember the Maker of it all.

Everything in the whole wide world began with God's good words — and He called all of it very good.
