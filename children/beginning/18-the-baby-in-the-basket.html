The Beginning and the Fathers

The Baby in the Basket

From the Book of Exodus, chapters 1–2

Section: The Beginning and the Fathers

Long after Joseph's time, God's people had grown into a great big family in the land of Egypt — more and more children, more and more tents, year after year.

But a wicked king did not want God's people to grow. He gave a cruel order that put Hebrew baby boys in danger, and he made the people's lives hard and heavy with work.

In those hard days, a mother from the family of Levi had a baby boy. When she saw what a fine, beautiful child he was, she hid him in her house for three whole months, keeping him safe and quiet.

But babies grow, and babies cry louder. At last she could hide him no longer. So she made a plan.

She took a basket woven from river reeds — a little ark — and sealed it all over with sticky pitch so no water could creep in. She laid her baby gently inside, carried him down to the great River Nile, and set the little basket afloat among the tall reeds at the water's edge.

And the baby's big sister stood a little way off, watching and watching — to see what would happen to her baby brother.

Soon someone came down to the river: the king's own daughter, Pharaoh's princess, come to wash in the water with her maids walking along the bank.

She saw the basket among the reeds. “Bring it to me,” she said.

She opened the lid — and the baby cried. The princess looked down at the tiny, crying face, and her heart melted with pity. “This is one of the Hebrew babies,” she said softly.

Just then, the brave big sister stepped out of hiding. “Shall I go and find a Hebrew mother to nurse the baby for you?”

“Yes — go!” said the princess.

And the girl ran — and brought the baby's own mother!

The princess said to her, “Take this child and nurse him for me, and I will pay you.” So the mother took her baby home and nursed him with joy, and no one could take him from her.

When the boy was grown, his mother brought him to the princess, and he became her son, and lived like a prince in the palace of Egypt. The princess named him Moses — she said, “Because I drew him out of the water.”

The river was supposed to be a danger — but it carried one small basket straight into God's plan.
