The Land and the Kings

David the King and the Little Man at the Door

From the Book of 2 Samuel, chapters 6–9

Section: The Land and the Kings

When David was king of all Israel, one treasure was missing. God's ark — the holy chest that travelled with God's people through the desert — was still far away, resting in somebody else's house.

So David gathered the people to bring the ark home. They set it on a cart, and everyone made music — harps, timbrels, cymbals. It was a parade for God!

But on the road something sad happened. The oxen stumbled, and Uzzah reached out to steady the ark — though God had commanded no one should ever touch it. And Uzzah died there beside the ark.

David's heart sank in fear, and the ark stayed for three months in the house of Obed-edom — and how God blessed that house and everyone in it! When David heard this, he went again — and this time priests carried the ark on their shoulders, as God had commanded.

What a day! Trumpets blew, the people shouted, and King David danced before the LORD with all his might. And when the ark was set in its tent, David gave every person in the crowd a loaf of bread, meat, and a flagon of wine.

That night David sat in his cedar palace, thinking: "I live in a house of cedar, but God's ark lives in a tent! I will build God a house." But God sent word: "Your son will build My house. And *I* will build your house, David — your family will be kings for ever." And David whispered: "Who am I, O Lord GOD, that You have brought me this far?"

Then David remembered Jonathan, who was gone, and the promise they had made. He called servant Ziba and asked: "Is there anyone left of Saul's family, that I may show kindness for Jonathan's sake?"

"There is one," said Ziba. "Jonathan's son, lame in both his feet."

His name was Mephibosheth. He lived in Lo-debar, poor and forgotten — a prince who could not walk, out of the way of a king he feared. When the king's messengers brought him through the palace door, trembling, David said first: "Mephibosheth — fear not! For your father's sake I will show you kindness. All your grandfather's land is yours again — and you shall eat at my table every day, like one of the king's own sons."

And so it was. From that day Mephibosheth lived in Jerusalem, and every evening a place was set for him at the king's table — the man who could not walk, seated with princes — because a king remembered his promise.

Kindness remembers its promises — and there is always room at the King's table for one more.
