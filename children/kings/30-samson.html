The Land and the Kings

Samson the Strong

From the Book of Judges, chapters 13–16

Section: The Land and the Kings

Before Samson was born, an angel came to his mother with wonderful news: “You shall have a son. And he will belong to God in a special way from the very beginning — no razor shall ever touch his head — and he will begin to rescue Israel from their enemies, the Philistines.”

The baby was born, and God blessed him as he grew — and Samson grew up stronger than any man alive.

Once, a young lion came roaring at him in a vineyard — and Samson tore it apart with his bare hands. Later he found that bees had made honey inside it, and scooped out the sweetness as he walked. That gave him his famous riddle:

“Out of the eater came something to eat — and out of the strong came something sweet!”

Once, a whole city lay in wait for him at night. Samson simply got up at midnight, lifted the city's great gates — doors, posts, bar and all — onto his shoulders, and carried them to the top of a hill!

But Samson had a weakness: he could not keep a secret when he was pressed.

In the valley of Sorek lived a woman named Delilah, and the lords of the Philistines promised her piles of silver to discover where Samson's great strength lay. Three times she begged him to tell her, and three times Samson fooled her with made-up answers — fancy ropes, new ropes, weaving his hair into a loom — and every time, he broke free as strong as ever.

But Delilah asked and asked, every single day, until at last Samson told her the whole truth: “No razor has ever come on my head, for I have belonged to God from my birth. If my hair is shaved off, my strength will leave me, and I will become weak like any other man.”

While Samson slept, Delilah had his seven long locks shaved off. He woke and shook himself — “I will go out as before!” — but his strength was gone. The Philistines seized him, hurt his eyes so he could not see, and set him to grinding grain in prison.

But in the prison, quietly, Samson's hair began to grow again.

At a great feast in the house of their god, the Philistines brought Samson out to laugh at him — thousands of people, even on the roof. Samson asked the boy who led him, “Let me feel the pillars that hold up this house.” He stood between the two great middle pillars, one hand on each.

And then Samson prayed — truly prayed, maybe for the first time in his life: “O Lord GOD — remember me, I pray You, and strengthen me, I pray You, only this once.”

He bowed himself against the pillars with all his might, and the whole house came down upon his enemies — and upon Samson too.

So Samson died — but his last words were not boasting. His last words were a prayer. And God heard him.

Samson's strength came and went — but when he finally prayed like a child, God was listening.
