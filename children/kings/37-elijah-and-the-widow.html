The Land and the Kings

Elijah and the Widow's Jar

From the Book of 1 Kings, chapter 17

Section: The Land and the Kings

There had been no rain for a long time, and the land was dry and dusty. Elijah the prophet was hiding beside a brook called Cherith — and morning and evening, God sent him breakfast and supper by the strangest delivery: ravens! Black birds came carrying bread and meat, just for him. And Elijah drank from the brook.

But at last the brook dried up. So God said: "Get up and go to Zarephath. I have told a widow there to feed you."

At the town gate, Elijah found a woman gathering sticks. "Please," he called, "bring me water to drink." As she went, he called: "And a morsel of bread, if you can!"

Then she told him the truth. "I have no bread," she said. "Only a handful of flour in a barrel, and a drop of oil in a jar. I am gathering sticks to make a fire, to bake one last cake for me and my son — and after that, we have nothing."

"Fear not," said Elijah. "Go and bake the cake — but make me a little one first, and then some for you and your son. For this is what the LORD God of Israel says: The barrel of flour shall not run out, and the jar of oil shall not run dry, until the day the LORD sends rain on the earth."

So she did it. She baked Elijah's cake first, out of her almost-empty barrel. And then she went back for her own cake — the flour was still there. The next morning — still there! The oil jar? Still not empty. Day after day, the widow, her son, and Elijah ate from a barrel and a jar that never ran out, while the dry days lasted.

But one day the widow's boy became sick — so sick that he stopped breathing. His mother carried him to Elijah with tears: "Man of God, what have I done?"

Elijah took the boy to the upstairs room where he slept, and laid him on the bed, and cried out to God: "O LORD my God, let this child's life come back into him!" Three times he stretched over the boy, praying with all his heart.

And the LORD heard Elijah. The child's life came back into him, and he breathed again!

Elijah carried him downstairs and put him in his mother's arms. "See," he said. "Your son lives."

And the widow held her boy and said: "Now I know that you are a man of God — and that the word of the LORD in your mouth is truth."

One handful of flour, given away first — and God's jar never ran empty.
