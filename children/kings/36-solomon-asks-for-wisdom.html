The Land and the Kings

Solomon Asks for Wisdom

From the Book of 1 Kings, chapter 3

Section: The Land and the Kings

Solomon was still young when he became king after his father David. He had a whole kingdom to rule — more people than anyone could count — and king-size problems waiting every morning.

One night, at Gibeon, God came to him in a dream and said the most wonderful words a king ever heard: "Ask what I shall give you. Ask for anything."

Solomon could have asked for gold treasure, a long, easy life, or for victory over all his enemies. But this is what he asked:

"O LORD my God, You have made me king — and I am but a little child. I do not know how to go out or come in. Your people are a great people, too many to count. So give Your servant an understanding heart, to judge Your people and to know good from bad. For who is able to rule this great people — without Your wisdom?"

And God was pleased. "Because you have asked for THIS," God said, "and not for long life, and not for riches, and not for the defeat of your enemies — I have given you a wise and understanding heart, such as no one before you has ever had, and no one after you ever will. And I give you also what you did not ask: riches and honour. And if you walk in My ways, as David walked, I will lengthen your days."

Solomon woke up — and it had been a dream! But the wisdom was real.

Soon everyone saw it. Two mothers who lived in one house came to the king, quarrelling. Each had a baby boy — but one had died in the night, and now both women claimed the living child. "He is mine!" said one. "No — the living one is MY son!" cried the other. There were no witnesses. Who could know?

"Bring me a sword," said Solomon. "Divide the living child in two, and give half to the one and half to the other."

Then the true mother cried out — her heart ached for her son — "O my lord! Give HER the living child, and whatever you do, do not hurt him!"

But the other woman said, "Let him be neither mine nor yours. Divide him."

Then Solomon pointed to the first woman. "Give HER the living child, and do not hurt him," he said. "She is his mother."

And all Israel heard about the judgment, and their mouths fell open — because they could see the wisdom of God was inside this young king, helping him to judge what was right.

If you could ask God for one thing, ask for a heart that understands.
