The Land and the Kings

Elisha and the Widow's Oil

From the Book of 2 Kings, chapter 4

Section: The Land and the Kings

A poor widow came crying to Elisha the prophet. Her husband had been one of God's servants, but he had died, and now the man they owed money to was coming to take her two sons away to work for him until the debt was paid.

"What can I do for you?" said Elisha. "Tell me — what do you have in the house?"

She shook her head. "Your servant has nothing in the house at all — nothing, except one pot of oil."

"Then go," said Elisha, "and borrow empty jars from all your neighbours. Borrow lots of them — don't borrow just a few! Then go inside with your sons, and shut the door, and pour the oil from your pot into every jar. Set each full jar aside, and keep pouring."

So she sent her boys running from house to house: "May we borrow your empty jars?" — and the neighbours brought jars, big ones and little ones, until the room was crowded with them. Then she shut the door, picked up her one small pot, and began to pour.

The oil flowed into the first jar... and filled it! Into the second... filled! A third, a fourth — her sons kept carrying empty jars to her, and she kept pouring, and the little pot never went dry. Jar after jar after jar was set aside, brimming and shining, until at last she said: "Bring me another jar."

And her son answered: "There isn't another one, Mother. We've used them all."

And at that moment — the oil stopped.

She ran and told the man of God. And Elisha said: "Go and sell the oil, and pay what you owe. And you and your sons shall live on what is left over."

And that is exactly what she did. The debt was paid, her boys stayed home where they belonged — and it had all come out of one little pot of oil, poured out behind a shut door, to a God who sees what nobody else can see.

Bring God what you have — even if it's only a little — and watch what He can pour.
