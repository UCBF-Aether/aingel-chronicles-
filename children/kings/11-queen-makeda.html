The Land and the Kings

The Queen Who Came from Far Away

From the Book of 1 Kings, chapter 10, and the Kebra Nagast

Section: The Land and the Kings

Far away in the rich, sunny land of Ethiopia, there reigned a queen named Makeda. She was wise, and her kingdom was famous for gold, spices, and fine things. Her merchant Tamrin sailed the seas and loaded five hundred camels with treasures to trade.

One day Tamrin came home from Jerusalem with a story. "My Queen," he said, "there is a king in Israel named Solomon, and there has never been a king like him. God has given him wisdom like the sand on the shore. He answers every question. His words are sweeter than honey, and like bread to the hungry."

Morning after morning, Tamrin told her more, and her heart began to burn with a wish. She already had gold beyond counting — but wisdom cannot be bought, and she wanted it more than anything.

"Wisdom is better than treasure of gold and silver," she told her nobles. "Wisdom is sweeter than honey, and shines brighter than the sun. I will go and seek it."

So the Queen made ready for her longest journey. Seven hundred and ninety-seven camels were loaded with gifts — gold, spices, precious stones — and the caravan set out across deserts and mountains to Jerusalem.

King Solomon welcomed her with honour, and the Queen asked him all the questions in her heart — every hard question she had saved on the long road. There was not one thing he could not answer.

She saw his palace and his table, and how kindly he ruled. She heard him say, of a poor workman carrying a heavy stone, "That man is my brother. God made us both." Her breath was taken away with wonder.

"It was all true," she told the King, "and more than true — the half was not told me! Blessed be your God, who made you king." And she said the most important words of her journey: "From this moment, I will not worship the sun. I will worship the God who made the sun — the Creator, the God of Israel."

She gave Solomon her gifts — a hundred and twenty talents of gold, and more spices than had ever come into that land. And Solomon gave her gifts in return, everything she asked, carried home on six thousand camels.

Queen Makeda carried home more than gold. She carried the knowledge of the true God — and she taught her people, and ruled them with the wisdom she had crossed the world to find. And in time she had a son, and Ethiopia's story with God had begun.

The Queen crossed the whole world for wisdom — and found that the best treasure of all is knowing God.
