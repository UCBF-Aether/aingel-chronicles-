The Land and the Kings

Ruth Stays With Naomi

From the Book of Ruth, chapters 1–4

Section: The Land and the Kings

In the days of the judges, a famine came to Bethlehem — the barns were empty, the bread gone. So Naomi traveled with her husband and two sons to live in Moab.

There, Naomi's husband died, and in time both sons died too, after marrying two Moabite women, Orpah and Ruth. Naomi was left a widow, far from home.

Then Naomi heard the LORD had given His people bread back home, so she started for Bethlehem. Orpah and Ruth walked with her.

On the road, Naomi stopped and said gently, “Go back to your mothers' houses. May the LORD deal kindly with you, as you have dealt with me. May you find rest in a new home.”

Orpah wept, kissed Naomi, and turned back home.

But Ruth clung to her, and said the words:

“Do not ask me to leave you! Where you go, I will go. Where you stay, I will stay. Your people will be my people, and your God will be my God.”

So the two widows walked to Bethlehem — arriving as the barley harvest was beginning.

Naomi's heart was heavy. “Don't call me Naomi anymore,” she told her friends. “My life has turned bitter.”

Something had to be done for food. In Israel, God's law said reapers must leave grain in the fields for the poor — and Ruth said, “Let me go to the fields and glean.” She worked, gathering barley behind the reapers in Boaz's field — a wealthy relative of Naomi's husband.

Boaz came greeting his workers: “The LORD be with you!” When he heard who she was — the foreign girl who would not leave Naomi — he was kind: “Stay in my field. Drink from the water jars. You are safe here.” He told his men to drop extra handfuls of barley for her, and at the meal he shared his grain.

Ruth came home with a measure of barley, and Naomi's eyes lit up: “Blessed be the man! He is a near relative — a kinsman-redeemer!”

And God finished the story. Boaz, as near kinsman, made everything right at the city gate before the elders — in those days, a bargain was sealed by passing over a shoe! — and he married Ruth.

A baby boy was born, and the women crowded around Naomi: “Blessed be the LORD! Your daughter-in-law who loves you is better to you than seven sons!” They named the baby Obed.

Naomi hugged her grandson, her heart full.

And Obed grew up and became the father of Jesse, and Jesse became the father of David, the shepherd-king!

Ruth never left Naomi's side — and her faithfulness runs all the way down to King David himself.
