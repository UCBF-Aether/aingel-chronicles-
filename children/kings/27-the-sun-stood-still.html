The Land and the Kings

The Day the Sun Stood Still

From the Book of Joshua, chapter 10

Section: The Land and the Kings

The city of Gibeon had made peace with God's people — and five kings did not like that at all. They joined their armies together and marched against Gibeon.

Gibeon sent a fast message to Joshua: “Do not let go of us! Come up quickly, and save us, and help us!”

Joshua and his men marched all night long, uphill, in the dark, to the rescue. And as they marched, God spoke to Joshua: “Do not fear them — for I have given them into your hand.”

By morning, Joshua's army came down on the five kings like a surprise out of the hills, and the enemy armies broke and ran. They fled down the road — and as they fled, God fought from the sky, sending great stones of hail crashing down among them, until the kings' armies were scattered far and wide.

But the day was rushing on, and there was still work to do. The sun was climbing toward the middle of the sky. Soon it would sink, and the dark would hide the fleeing enemy.

So Joshua prayed the boldest prayer anyone has ever prayed — he spoke out loud, in front of all Israel:

“Sun — stand still over Gibeon!”

And the sun stood still.

It stopped in the middle of the sky, and did not hurry down — not for an hour, not for two — for about a whole day, the sun hung there shining, while Joshua's men finished rescuing Gibeon and chased the five kings away for good.

The longest day that has ever been!

There has never been a day like it, before or since — a day when the LORD listened to the voice of a man and stopped the sun in the sky. For the LORD was fighting for Israel.

God once stopped the sun in the sky for His people — He was in no hurry at all to stop helping them.
