The Land and the Kings

Elijah on the Mountain

From the Book of 1 Kings, chapters 18–19

Section: The Land and the Kings

In the third year of drought, Elijah told King Ahab: "Gather all Israel to Mount Carmel, with Baal's four hundred and fifty prophets. Today everyone will see who the true God is."

Baal's prophets built their altar, laid a bull on the wood, and called on Baal all morning: "O Baal, hear us!" They shouted and jumped, but no voice answered. "Cry louder," said Elijah. "Perhaps he is asleep!" They cried until evening. Nothing.

Then Elijah rebuilt the LORD's altar with twelve stones, dug a trench, and laid wood and offering. Then he did something unexpected: "Fill four barrels and pour water on the offering and wood." Three times, until water filled the trench.

Then Elijah prayed: "LORD God of Abraham, Isaac, and Israel — let it be known today You are God in Israel, and I am Your servant. Hear me, O LORD, so this people may know You turned their hearts back."

And the fire of the LORD fell! It burned offering, wood, stones and dust, and licked up the water in the trench. And the people fell and cried: "The LORD — He is God! The LORD — He is God!" And Baal's prophets slunk home.

Then Elijah climbed Carmel and prayed for rain. Seven times his servant looked to the sea. Six times: nothing. The seventh time — "A cloud, as small as a hand!" And soon the sky was black, and rain poured.

But Jezebel sent Elijah a frightening message, and he fled into the wilderness. Under a juniper tree he sat, sad. "It is enough," he prayed. "I am all alone." He fell asleep, then an angel touched his shoulder. "Arise and eat, because the journey is too great for you." Beside him were a cake and a jar of water. Twice the angel fed him, and that food strengthened him for forty days and nights to Horeb.

In a cave, God asked: "What are you doing here, Elijah?" "I am the only one left, LORD," he said.

"Go out and stand on the mountain," said God, "for the LORD is passing by." Wind tore mountains and broke rocks, but the LORD was not in the wind; then an earthquake, but not in it; then a fire, but not in it; after the fire, a still, small voice.

And the whisper told him: you are NOT alone. "I have kept seven thousand in Israel who have never bowed to Baal." And God sent him back down with a new friend: Elisha, a farmer, who would walk beside him.

God is not always in the wind and the fire — sometimes He comes in a whisper, to tell a tired heart: you are not alone.
