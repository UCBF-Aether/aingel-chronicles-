The Land and the Kings

The Temple Is Built

From the Book of 2 Chronicles, chapters 2–7

Section: The Land and the Kings

David had dreamed of building God a house, but God had said: your *son* will build it. And now Solomon — David's son, the wisest king — began to build on Mount Moriah, on the very hill where God had once appeared to his father.

What a building it was! The walls were fir wood covered over with fine gold, carved with palm trees and chains. Inside the most holy place stood two great cherubim — angel statues with their long wings spread wide, touching wall to wall, covered in gold. Before the house stood two tall pillars with a hundred bronze pomegranates apiece strung on chains — the pillar on the right was called Jachin, and the one on the left Boaz. And a beautiful curtain of blue, and purple, and crimson hung in the doorway, with cherubim woven into it.

When everything was finished, the priests carried the ark of God into the most holy place, and set it carefully under the cherubim's wings. Inside the ark there was nothing but the two stone tablets Moses had put there long ago at Mount Horeb.

Then the singers took their places — all of them in white linen, with cymbals and harps, and one hundred and twenty priests with trumpets. And when the trumpeters and the singers lifted their voices together, as one sound, singing, "For He is good — for His mercy endureth for ever!" —

— a cloud filled the house of the LORD! The glory of God came down like a shining cloud and filled the whole temple, so full that the priests could not even stand up to do their work.

Then Solomon climbed onto a bronze platform, and knelt down before all the people, and spread out his hands to heaven, and prayed: "O LORD God of Israel, there is no God like You! Will God truly live with men on the earth? Even the heaven of heavens cannot hold You! But You promised to put Your name in this house. So let Your eyes be open toward it day and night, and hear the prayers Your people pray here."

And when Solomon finished praying — fire came down from heaven and lit the altar all at once, and the glory of the LORD filled the house again! And all the people bowed with their faces to the ground and sang: "For He is good — for His mercy endureth for ever!"

And that night God spoke to Solomon: "I have heard your prayer, and I have chosen this place for Myself. If My people — who are called by My name — will humble themselves, and pray, and seek My face, then I will hear from heaven, and I will forgive them, and heal their land. My eyes and My heart will be in this house for ever."

God filled His new house with glory — and He promised to keep His eyes and His heart there, and to hear every prayer.
