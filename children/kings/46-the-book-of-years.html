The Land and the Kings

The Book of Years

From the Book of Jubilees, chapters 4 and 23

Section: The Land and the Kings

God counts the years the way a shepherd counts sheep — carefully, and by name — and the Book of Jubilees is where His people learned how He does it.

The years, God showed Moses, are gathered like wheat. Seven years make a *week of years*. Seven weeks of years — and then comes a **jubilee**: nearly fifty years all together, a year of freedom and rest, like a birthday party for the whole earth, when the land lies quiet and everyone begins again. Even the forefathers' long lives were counted this way. Abraham — who lived a hundred and seventy-five years — lived "three jubilees and four weeks of years," says the book. Every one of his days was on God's calendar.

And do you know who wrote the calendar down first? Enoch! The angel told Moses: "Enoch was the first among men born on earth who learned writing, and knowledge, and wisdom. And he wrote down the signs of heaven — the sun and the moon in their order, month by month — in a book, so that people could know the seasons of the years. He counted out the weeks of the jubilees, and made known the days of the years, and set the months in their places."

Before Enoch, nobody could write anything down. After Enoch, there were books.

The generations after Abraham, the angel said, would find the years growing harder and shorter. People would forget God's commands, and their days would be full of trouble — and they would look back and say, "The fathers lived nearly a thousand years, and their days were good. Ours are only seventy or eighty, and they are hard!" And the book tells the truth about that sadness. God does not pretend the sad years were not sad.

But the angel also told Moses how the story of the years turns out. "In those days," God said, "the children will begin to study the laws again, and to seek the commandments, and to return to the path of righteousness. And the days will grow long again — longer and longer, until they draw near to a thousand years. And all their days they shall complete in peace and in joy, and there will be no enemy and no evil destroyer: all their days will be days of blessing and healing."

"And do thou, Moses," said the angel, "write down these words — for they are written on the heavenly tables, as a testimony for the generations for ever."

So Moses wrote. And that is why, tonight, you can hear the years of the world counted out loud — because heaven keeps the records, and God once let a man copy them into a book.

God writes the years down on heavenly tables — not one of His children's days is lost or forgotten.
