The Land and the Kings

Gideon's Tiny Army

From the Book of Judges, chapters 6–7

Section: The Land and the Kings

The Midianites kept swooping down on Israel — stealing crops, taking animals, spoiling harvests — until God's people were hiding in caves, crying out to God.

In one village lived a young man named Gideon. One day he threshed wheat in secret by the winepress, so the Midianites would not see — when the angel of the LORD appeared under an oak tree.

“The LORD is with you, you mighty man of courage!” said the angel.

Gideon stared. “If the LORD is with us,” he said, “why has all this trouble come on us?”

“Go in this strength of yours and save Israel. Have I not sent you?” said the angel.

Gideon brought food and set it on a rock. The angel touched it with his staff — and fire rose up out of the rock and took the food! Then the angel was gone. Gideon built an altar there and named it “The LORD Is Peace.”

Still, Gideon wanted to be sure God was calling him. At night he laid a fleece of sheep's wool on the threshing floor, and prayed: “If in the morning the dew is on the fleece only, and the ground around is dry, then I will know.” In the morning, the fleece was soaking wet — Gideon wrung a bowlful of water from it — and the ground was dry! Then he asked once more, the other way round: fleece dry, dew everywhere else. And God did that too.

Gideon gathered an army — thirty-two thousand men! But God said, “Too many. If you win with so many, Israel might say, ‘Our own hands saved us.’ Send home everyone who is afraid.” Twenty-two thousand went home. Ten thousand stayed.

“Still too many,” said God. At the stream, God chose the men who stayed alert, scooping water to drink — only three hundred.

That night, God sent Gideon's army to creep around the enemy camp. Every man carried a trumpet and a torch hidden in an empty clay pitcher.

At Gideon's signal, at once: trumpets blasted! Pitchers smashed! Three hundred torches flared in a ring of fire, and the men shouted, “The sword of the LORD, and of Gideon!”

The enemy camp woke in panic — running, shouting, stumbling into each other — and fled into the night as fast as they could. Gideon's three hundred had not lifted a sword.

Three hundred men, some clay pots, and firelight — that was the army. Everyone who heard the story knew: God had done this, so no one could say they had saved themselves.

God does not need a big army — He once won a battle with three hundred men, some clay pots, and firelight.
