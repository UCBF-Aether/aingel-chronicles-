The Land and the Kings

Enoch Sees the Storehouses of the Stars

From the Book of 1 Enoch, chapters 18, 33 and 41

Section: The Land and the Kings

After Enoch had seen the angels who broke God's rules, the holy angels took him on a journey farther than any person had ever travelled — to the very ends of the earth, where the sky comes down and rests.

"I saw the storehouses of all the winds," Enoch said. Imagine it: great chambers in heaven where God keeps the winds the way a farmer keeps grain in a barn! There are storehouses for the hail, and storehouses for the mist, and chambers for the clouds — and the clouds have been hovering over the earth from the very beginning of the world, waiting for God's word to let the rain out.

Enoch saw the corner-stone of the earth, and the four mighty winds that hold up the sky like pillars. He saw the winds that carry the clouds across the heavens, and the winds that turn the great wheel of the sun and stars and bring them around to their setting.

And he saw mountains at the edge of the world: seven mountains of shining, coloured stone — one of pearl, one of red stone, one sparkling like jewels — and the mountain in the middle rose all the way to heaven, like the very throne of God, made of pale alabaster with a crown of deep-blue sapphire.

Then Enoch travelled to the far east, past great beasts of every kind and birds of every colour, to the place where heaven opens its doors. "And I saw how the stars of heaven come forth," he said. Every star has its own door — its own portal — out of which it steps each night! The holy angel Uriel, who walked with Enoch, showed him every one: "and I counted the portals out of which the stars come, and I wrote down all their goings-out, every single star by itself — its number, its name, its path, its place, its time and its month."

Nothing in the sky is lost or wandering. Every star knows its door.

Enoch saw the chambers of the sun and the moon, where they come out from and where they return to. The sun and moon never leave their paths; they add nothing to their journey and take nothing away. They keep faith with each other, like two friends who have promised. And all of them — sun, moon, and stars — give thanks and praise to God, and never rest from thanking Him. Even the lightning and the thunder have their secrets and their storehouses, and the dew knows exactly where it comes from when it falls on the dusty earth.

And Enoch, the first man who ever learned to write, wrote it all down — so that thousands of years later, a child going to bed under the stars could know: every single one of them is counted, named, and kept.

God has a storehouse for the winds and a door for every star — and He has written all their names down.
