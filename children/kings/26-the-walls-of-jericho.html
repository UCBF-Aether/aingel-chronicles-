The Land and the Kings

The Walls of Jericho

From the Book of Joshua, chapters 5–6

Section: The Land and the Kings

God's people were in the promised land at last! They celebrated the Passover there, and ate the food of the new land — and the manna stopped, because now God's garden-land would feed them.

Near Jericho, Joshua looked up and saw a Man standing before him, sword drawn.

Joshua went to Him. “Are You for us, or for our enemies?”

“Neither,” He answered. “I am the Captain of the LORD's army, and I have come now.”

Joshua fell on his face to the ground. “What does my Lord say to His servant?”

And the Captain said, “Take your shoe off — for the place where you stand is holy.” And Joshua did.

Now Jericho's gates were shut and barred, because the people inside were afraid. No one went out, and no one came in. But God said to Joshua: “See — I have given Jericho into your hand.”

And God gave them the strangest battle plan ever heard.

“Walk around the city once — all the men, in procession. Seven priests shall carry trumpets of rams' horns and blow them before the ark of God. Do this for six days. On the seventh day, walk around the city seven times. Then the priests shall blow one long blast — and when you hear it, all the people shall shout with a great shout. And the wall shall fall down flat.”

So they did it. Day one: feet marching, trumpets blowing, the ark carried high — and nobody said a word, for Joshua had ordered, “You shall not shout, nor let your voice be heard, until the day I tell you.” Around the walls they went, and back to camp. Day two. Day three. Day four, day five, day six — marching, trumpets, silence, while Jericho watched from its high walls and wondered.

On the seventh day, they rose at dawn and marched around the city seven times. On the seventh time around, the priests blew the long blast —

— and Joshua cried, “SHOUT! For the LORD has given you the city!”

The people shouted with a great shout — thousands of voices, like thunder —

And the wall fell down flat! The great stones crashed and tumbled into dust and ruin, and the way into the city lay wide open before them, just as God had said.

And one household in Jericho was kept safe through it all: Rahab, the brave woman who had hidden God's messengers, with everyone in her house — brought out safely, exactly as she had been promised.

No ladders, no battering rams — just marching feet, trumpet music, and one great shout. God had knocked the walls down Himself.
