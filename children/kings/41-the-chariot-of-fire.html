The Land and the Kings

The Chariot of Fire

From the Book of 2 Kings, chapter 2

Section: The Land and the Kings

The day had come when God was going to take Elijah up into heaven — and Elisha, his faithful helper, would not leave his side. Not for anything.

"Stay here," said Elijah, "for the LORD has sent me to Bethel."

"As the LORD lives," said Elisha, "I will not leave you."

So they went down to Bethel together. The prophets there whispered to Elisha: "Do you know that today the LORD will take your master away?" "Yes, I know it," said Elisha. "Hold your peace."

At Jericho it was the same. "Stay here," said Elijah. "I will not leave you," said Elisha. And on they walked together, all the way to the river Jordan — fifty prophets following at a distance to watch.

At the riverbank, Elijah took off his cloak, rolled it up, and struck the water with it. And the Jordan split in two! — the waters standing aside to the left and to the right, so that the two of them crossed over on dry ground, just as Israel had crossed long ago.

On the far side, Elijah turned to his friend. "Ask what I shall do for you, before I am taken away."

And Elisha said: "Let a double portion of your spirit be upon me. Let me carry on your work — twice as much of it."

"You have asked a hard thing," said Elijah. "But if you see me when I am taken from you, it shall be yours."

And as they walked on, still talking together — suddenly! — there appeared a chariot of fire, and horses of fire, flashing between them! And Elijah went up, up, up — into heaven in a whirlwind!

Elisha saw it, and cried out: "My father! My father! The chariot of Israel and its horsemen!" He tore his clothes, because his heart was both soaring and breaking at once — and then Elijah was gone.

But there on the ground lay something: Elijah's cloak, fallen from him as he rose. Elisha picked it up, and walked back to the river, and stood at the bank of the Jordan. He struck the water with the cloak and cried: "Where is the LORD God of Elijah?"

And the water split in two — and Elisha crossed over on dry ground!

The prophets watching from Jericho saw it and bowed low. "The spirit of Elijah rests on Elisha," they said.

The master had gone home to heaven in fire and glory. And his work on earth had already begun again — in the hands of the friend who would not leave him.

Some goodbyes are really God saying: just wait, and watch what I do next.
