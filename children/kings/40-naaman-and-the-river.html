The Land and the Kings

Naaman and the River

From the Book of 2 Kings, chapter 5

Section: The Land and the Kings

Naaman was captain of Syria's army — a great, mighty hero of battles. Kings honoured him. But Naaman had leprosy, which made his skin sore, and no doctor could cure him.

Now, in Naaman's house worked a servant girl carried away from Israel. One day she said to her mistress: "If only my master were with the prophet in Samaria! He would cure him of his leprosy."

Such a small voice, but it reached the king of Syria! The king sent Naaman to Israel with a letter, ten talents of silver, six thousand pieces of gold, and ten changes of fine clothing.

But when the king of Israel read the letter, he tore his clothes in fright. "Am I God, that I can cure a man of leprosy? This king of Syria is looking for a fight!"

Then Elisha sent word: "Why have you torn your clothes? Send Naaman to me, and he shall know there is a prophet in Israel."

So Naaman came thundering to Elisha's house with horses, chariot, and treasure, and stood at the door. And Elisha did not come out! He just sent a messenger: "Go and wash in the river Jordan seven times, and your flesh shall be clean again, like new."

Naaman was furious. "I THOUGHT he would surely come out, stand, and call on his God's name, and wave his hand over the place! Are not my country's rivers better than all Israel's waters? Couldn't I wash in THEM?" And he stormed away.

But his servants ran after him, speaking gently. "Father," they said, "if the prophet had asked some GREAT thing, wouldn't you have done it? How much easier when he says: Wash — and be clean?"

Naaman stopped, thought. And the proud captain climbed from his chariot, down the Jordan's muddy bank, and dipped under the water. Once. Twice. Three times... seven times.

And when he came up the seventh time, his skin was clean and new, like a little child's!

Naaman hurried back to Elisha, eyes shining. "Now I KNOW," he said, "that there is no God in all the earth except in Israel!" He begged Elisha to take his gold and silver, but Elisha would take nothing. So Naaman loaded two mules with Israelite earth to carry home, so in Syria he could kneel on God's ground and worship the LORD there.

"Go in peace," said Elisha. And the captain went home clean — healed by a river, a servant girl, and seven dips.

Pride wanted a great show — but healing came in a muddy river, the moment Naaman humbled his heart.
