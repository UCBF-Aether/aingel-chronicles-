The Land and the Kings

David and Jonathan, Best Friends

From the Book of 1 Samuel, chapters 18–20

Section: The Land and the Kings

After David defeated Goliath, Saul kept him at the palace — and Saul's son Jonathan became David's friend.

From the first day, Jonathan loved David as his soul — their hearts were knit like brothers. Jonathan gave David his robe — and his sword, bow, belt. And the two made a covenant: a promise of friendship before God.

David went wherever the king sent him, and prospered, for the LORD was with him. And when the armies came home, the women danced, singing, “Saul has slain his thousands — and David his ten thousands!”

That song made Saul jealous. Twice, as David played, Saul's spear flashed toward him — and twice David slipped aside in time. David was no longer safe, and fled.

He came to Jonathan. “What have I done?” he asked. “What is my wrong, that your father seeks my life? There is a step between me and death.”

“God forbid — you shall not die!” said Jonathan. “My father hides nothing from me. Whatever you ask, David, I will do it.”

So they made a plan. At the feast in three days, David's seat would be empty — he would hide by a stone called Ezel. Jonathan would learn his father's heart, then come with a servant boy, shoot three arrows, and call the signal:

If he called, “The arrows are on this side of you — bring them!” all was well, and David could come home. But if he called, “The arrows are beyond you!” David must run for his life.

At the feast, David's seat stood empty — one day, then the second. The king raged at Jonathan: as long as David lived, Jonathan would never be king? “Why shall he be slain? What has he done?” cried Jonathan. His father would not answer, and Jonathan left the table in anger, eating nothing.

In the morning Jonathan went to the field with the lad. He shot an arrow past the boy, and called out:

“Is not the arrow beyond you? Hurry! Do not stay!”

The boy gathered the arrows, knowing nothing, and carried them to the city.

When the lad was gone, David rose, and the friends fell into each other's arms and wept. And Jonathan said:

“Go in peace — for we have promised each other in the name of the LORD, saying: the LORD shall be between me and you, and between your children and my children, for ever.”

David went one way, Jonathan to the city. But their friendship did not end at that field — it lasted for ever.

A prince gave his friend his robe, his sword, and his heart — and kept him safe at the cost of everything.
