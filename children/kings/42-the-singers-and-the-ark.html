The Land and the Kings

The Singers and the Ark

From the Book of 1 Chronicles, chapters 15–16

Section: The Land and the Kings

When the ark of God was brought home to Jerusalem, King David made sure of one thing: this time, there would be singing.

First, the ark was carried properly — on the shoulders of the Levites, with the carrying poles, just as God had commanded. And then David gathered the musicians, and he gave them the most joyful job in the whole kingdom.

Asaph was the chief singer, and he crashed the cymbals of brass. Heman and Ethan sang with him. There were harps and psalteries, and priests blowing trumpets before the ark — and a man named Chenaniah was put in charge of the music, because he was skilful and understood songs better than anyone. David himself walked with the procession, dressed in a robe of fine linen, while all Israel brought the ark up with shouting, and the sound of the horn, and trumpets, and cymbals, and harps.

And when the ark was set in the middle of the tent, David fed the whole crowd — every man and woman got a loaf of bread, a good piece of meat, and a flagon of wine — and then he handed Asaph a brand new song, written for that very day. It was the first psalm the choir ever sang:

"Give thanks unto the LORD! Call upon His name! Sing to Him, sing psalms to Him, and talk about all His wonderful works! Let the heavens be glad, and let the earth rejoice! Let the sea roar, and the fields be joyful, and let the trees of the wood sing out! O give thanks unto the LORD, for He is good — for His mercy endureth for ever!"

And the singing did not stop when the party ended. David stationed Asaph and the singers before the ark *continually* — morning and evening, day after day, with trumpets and cymbals and harps — the first choir that ever was, singing in God's house every single day.

God's house has had singing in it ever since — all because a king decided that joy needs a choir.
