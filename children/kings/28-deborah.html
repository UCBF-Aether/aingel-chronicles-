The Land and the Kings

Deborah the Judge

From the Book of Judges, chapters 4–5

Section: The Land and the Kings

In those days, God's people were in trouble again. A king named Jabin ruled over them from far away, and his captain, Sisera, had nine hundred chariots of iron — nine hundred roaring war-wagons, with iron wheels and iron sides. For twenty years Sisera pressed hard on the people, and they cried out to the LORD.

The leader God had given Israel then was not a soldier. She was a wise woman named Deborah.

Deborah was a prophetess — she listened to God — and she was the judge of Israel. Every day she sat under a palm tree between Ramah and Bethel, the palm tree of Deborah, and the people came up to her from all around with their quarrels and questions, and she listened to God and decided what was right and fair.

One day Deborah sent for a man named Barak. She had a message from God for him: “Hasn't the LORD, the God of Israel, commanded you? Take ten thousand men and go to Mount Tabor. And I will draw Sisera and his chariots and his army to the river Kishon — and I will give him into your hand.”

But Barak answered, “If you will go with me, then I will go. But if you will not go with me, then I will not go.”

Deborah did not hesitate. “I will surely go with you,” she said.

So Deborah went with Barak, and the army gathered at the mountain. Deborah said, “Up! For this is the day the LORD has given Sisera into your hand. Hasn't the LORD gone out before you?”

Down the mountain charged Barak and his men — and God threw Sisera's whole army into confusion at the river. The nine hundred mighty chariots of iron stuck fast and were scattered, and Sisera himself jumped down and ran away on foot, away from the battle, until he came to the tent of a woman named Jael — and there, in Jael's tent, the proud captain's story came quietly to its end in his sleep, just as Deborah had said: the great victory would be finished by a woman's hand.

When it was over and the land was free, Deborah and Barak sang a great, ringing song of thanks to God — a song the people sang for years afterward, telling how God had saved them.

And the land had rest for forty years.

Under a palm tree sat the wisest leader in Israel — and every one of her decisions began with listening to God.
