The Land and the Kings

David and the Giant

From the Book of 1 Samuel, chapter 17

Section: The Land and the Kings

The army of Israel was camped on one hill, and the army of the Philistines on another — and down in the valley between them stood a giant.

His name was Goliath, and he was taller than any man alive: a walking tower, covered in shining bronze armour, with a spear as thick as a tree trunk. Every morning and every evening for forty days, Goliath stomped out and roared at Israel's army:

"Send out a man to fight me! If he wins, we will serve you. If I win — you serve us!"

And every morning and every evening, all the soldiers of Israel — even King Saul — were afraid.

Then along came David. David was the youngest of eight brothers, just a shepherd boy, sent by his father to carry bread and cheese to his big brothers at the camp. He heard the giant's roaring. He saw all the soldiers hiding.

"Who is this man," David asked, "that he should shout insults at the army of the living God?"

David ran to King Saul. "I'll go and fight him," he said.

"You're only a boy!" said Saul.

But David said, "When a lion or a bear came to steal my father's sheep, God helped me drive it away. The same God will help me now."

Saul tried to give David his armour — helmet, coat of mail, a great sword — but it was all too heavy, and David took it off. Instead he went down to the stream and picked out five smooth stones, and put them in his shepherd's bag. He took his sling and his staff, and he walked out into the valley to meet the giant.

Goliath laughed. "Am I a dog, that you come at me with sticks?" he boomed. "Come here, little boy!"

But David called back, clear and brave: "You come to me with a sword and a spear. But I come to you in the name of the LORD of heaven's armies — and He will help me win today, so that everybody will know there is a God in Israel."

David ran toward the giant. Running! He put one smooth stone in his sling, swung it around — once, twice — and let it fly.

The stone struck Goliath right on the forehead, and the giant came crashing down like a falling tree.

Israel's army let out a shout that shook the hills, and charged, and the Philistines ran away. The battle was won — by a shepherd boy, five smooth stones, and a God who was with him.

You don't have to be big to be brave — you just have to remember Who is with you.
