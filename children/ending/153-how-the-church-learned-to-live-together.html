The End and the Beginning Again

How the Church Learned to Live Together

From the Sinodos, the apostles' book of the Church's order (a selection)

Section: The End and the Beginning Again

After Jesus went back to heaven, His friends — the apostles — carried His good news everywhere. And in every town, a new family was born: the Church.

But a family needs to know how to live together. Who will lead? Who will serve? Who makes sure nobody is forgotten? So the apostles left God's people a book of house rules. In Ethiopia it is called the Sinodos, and the apostles' own voices speak in it, one after another: "And Peter said — and John said — and James said —"

Here is how the Church learned to live together.

First, they chose shepherds. A bishop — the family's chief shepherd — must be chosen carefully, prayed over by everyone, a man of clean life who can teach. Hands were laid on his head, and the whole church said Amen.

Then they chose helpers: deacons, whose special job was to serve — to carry the bread, watch the doors, and make sure the gifts people brought reached the people who needed them.

And who must never, ever be forgotten? The widows — the grandmothers whose husbands had died. The book says it again and again: the widows have their own honoured place. Bread reaches them first. Nobody eats while a widow goes hungry.

Everyone brought gifts, too: the first and best of the harvest — the firstfruits — grain and figs and oil, carried to the church so there would always be enough to share.

The book set the family's prayer times, so the whole Church prayed together like one voice. And it taught small, tender things: wash your hands before you pray — clean hands for a clean heart! Rest on the Lord's Day, for God rested, and His day is a feast, not a burden.

And if hard days came, when God's people could not gather in the church at all? Then keep the family ways at home, said the book — pray where you are, and do not let go of each other.

That is the whole secret of the Sinodos. Page after page of rules — and every single rule is really one rule, said a hundred ways: *take care of each other.*

In God's family, little one, there is a rule with your name on it too — love, and take care of each other.
