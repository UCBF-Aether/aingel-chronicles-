The End and the Beginning Again

The Teachers of the Church

From the Didascalia, the Teaching of the Apostles (a selection)

Section: The End and the Beginning Again

When the apostles grew old, they wrote down their teaching for the shepherds who would come after them — the bishops, whose job is to teach and care for God's household. In Ethiopia's Bible this book is called the Didascalia, which means *The Teaching* — and in the grown-ups' Bible, its words are quite literally the last words of the whole canon.

What do the apostles teach the teachers?

A bishop, they say, must be a father and a shepherd, not a master with a stick. When people whisper tales about someone — "Did you hear what *he* did?" — the bishop must not swallow a false accusation. He must listen to proper witnesses and examine everything fairly, in righteousness and uprightness.

And when someone truly has done wrong, and comes back sorry? Then the bishop must do what the father did for his lost son: run to him. Receive the one who repents in love and meekness, the apostles say. Do not drive him away — God does not drive away the sorry-hearted.

Christians must be peacemakers, too. If you and your neighbour quarrel, forgive him — do not let revenge sit in your heart until the sun goes down. Bishops must be men of mercy, making peace wherever they go, pardoning people instead of piling on blame.

And in God's household, everyone has a place. The widows are prayed for and provided for, so they can give thanks with full hearts. The deacons serve. The fathers teach their own children God's ways at home, correcting them with love, the way a gardener trains a young tree.

And here is one of the tenderest rules in the whole book: if a child is left with no father and no mother, then a Christian family without children should take that child in — educate him, raise him, love him as their very own. An orphan in God's household does not stay an orphan. He gets a family.

At the end of the book, the apostles even wrote down the names of the first bishops they themselves ordained — name after name, so the family would always remember where it came from.

That is the teaching of the teachers: one big household of God, where the shepherds are kind, the sorry are welcomed home, and nobody is left outside.

There is a place for you in God's household, little one — a place at the table, with your name on it.
