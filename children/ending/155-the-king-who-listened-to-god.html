The End and the Beginning Again

The King Who Listened to God

From the First Book of the Covenant (a selection)

Section: The End and the Beginning Again

After Jesus rose from the dead, He did not leave His friends without a King. *He* was their King — the risen Jesus. And before He went back to heaven, He gave His apostles a covenant: His own kingly rules for His people.

That is the First Book of the Covenant. It is the rule-book of a King who listens — because Jesus had listened to His Father first.

And what kind of kingdom do His rules make? Listen.

In Jesus' kingdom, a shepherd is never chosen by pushing. When the Church needs a bishop, the apostles teach, he must be chosen by *all* the people together — someone everyone knows and trusts, chosen with everyone's consent, and prayed over by all. No grabbing. No sneaking. The whole family agrees, and God blesses it.

In Jesus' kingdom, the shepherd stays close to God. The bishop must be constant at the altar and keep praying day and night, says the King — for a shepherd who stops praying soon stops hearing the Shepherd's voice.

In Jesus' kingdom, the day begins before the sun does. Everyone gathers at dawn, while the sky is still grey, to praise God together — because the King rose from the dead at dawn, and His people love to meet Him early.

In Jesus' kingdom, the deacon is called "the eye of the Church." His eyes must stay open for people in trouble. If a woman has been hurt by someone and has no one to defend her, the deacon must not look away — he must find out the whole truth, carefully and exactly, and see that she is protected. In this kingdom, no one's tears go unwatched.

And in Jesus' kingdom, the widows are chosen, honoured and cared for — and they answer with songs, singing hymns to God. Every day in this kingdom ends the same way: with a hymn of praise on everybody's lips.

Those are the rules of the King who listened to God. And His kingdom is still standing — wherever people choose their shepherds honestly, pray before the sun, watch out for the hurt, and end the day with a song.

A kingdom is kind when its King is kind — and our King is Jesus, who hears every whisper and sees every tear.
