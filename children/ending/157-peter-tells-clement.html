The End and the Beginning Again

Peter Tells Clement the Story of Everything

From the Qalementos, the words of Peter to Clement (a selection)

Section: The End and the Beginning Again

Peter was an old man now. His beard was white, and he had seen everything — the nets and the boats, Jesus walking on the water, the empty tomb, the tongues of fire. And sitting beside him was Clement, a young man he loved like a son, hungry to be taught.

"My son Clement," Peter would say — and the book of Qalementos is full of his voice, an old fisherman-apostle pouring a whole life of wisdom into one young heart. Here is some of what Peter told him.

"Do not rush the holy things, Clement. Don't pour out baptism or lay hands on a priest in a hurry. Holy things are like seeds — they need their season. Wait, prepare hearts, and let God do the growing."

"If someone wrongs you, forgive him — and if he wrongs you again, forgive again, even seven times. And if a brother has done wrong and is sorry, do not push him down. Lift him up. Strengthen the one who stumbles; that is what hands are for."

"Teach the children of the dead, Clement, and look after them. Guard the children who have no father — strengthen them, and never leave them standing alone."

"Never drive a poor man away from your gate, and never laugh at him. Welcome him. The poor man at your door is God's own guest."

"Honour the old ones. Listen to their words, and do not make fun of grey hair or trembling hands — old age is a crown, and one day, God willing, you will wear it too."

"Pray in the morning. Pray at the third hour. Pray in the evening. Keep the feast days gladly, and remember the ones who have gone before us on their days, with love. Read David's psalms in the day, and sing God's praises in the night."

And at the very end, Peter told him where it all came from: "Everything I have commanded you, my son, came with the commandment of God. It was given on the Mount of Olives" — from Jesus Himself, the Master they both loved.

The story of everything, told the way grandfathers tell it: not all the facts in a row, but all the things that matter, hand to hand, heart to heart.

Find an old, wise heart and sit close, little one — the story of everything is passed on just like that.
