The End and the Beginning Again

The Singing Book

From the Gessew, the Church's book of worship and song (a selection)

Section: The End and the Beginning Again

In Ethiopia, the Church does not only pray — she sings. She sings when the sun comes up. She sings through the hours of the day. She sings when the stars come out. Morning, noon and night, somebody in Ethiopia is singing to God.

And so the Church keeps a book to guard the singing, so it is done beautifully, together, the way the apostles taught. It is called the Gessew.

The Gessew says: let there be readers, who read God's words aloud clearly, so everyone can hear. And let there be psalm-singers, whose one special job is to sing David's songs — for King David left the Church a whole song-book of his own, the Psalms, and Ethiopia sings them all.

Singers must sing with their whole hearts, not just their mouths. And the book even keeps the great list of all the Bible's books — Genesis to Revelation, every one named in order — so that God's library is never lost and every book can be read aloud in its turn.

And Ethiopia tells a story about how her singing first began — a story the grandfathers and grandmothers hand down with love, about a boy named Yared.

Yared loved God's songs, but learning was hard for him, and his lessons would not stay in his head. Then one day he heard a little bird singing outside — the same sweet tune, over and over, patient as can be. Phrase by phrase, Yared learned the bird's song, until he could sing the whole of it back. And the Church says: that is how Yared learned to sing to God — and he grew up to give Ethiopia hundreds of hymns, songs the Church still sings today, more than a thousand years later.

That is the story Ethiopia tells, and she tells it with love.

The drums still beat. The golden sistra still shimmer. The boys in white still stand beside the old men and sing David's psalms at sunrise — just as the Singing Book says.

When you sing to God, little singer, you join a song that has been rising with the sun for a thousand years.
