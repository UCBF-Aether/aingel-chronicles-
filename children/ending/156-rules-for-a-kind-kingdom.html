The End and the Beginning Again

Rules for a Kind Kingdom

From the Second Book of the Covenant (a selection)

Section: The End and the Beginning Again

The Second Book of the Covenant is full of small doors into everyday life. It does not talk about thrones and crowns. It talks about tables, and sick-beds, and first fruits, and children — because a kind kingdom is built out of kind choices, one ordinary day at a time.

Here are some of its choices.

When someone new wants to join God's family, the book says: teach them patiently. Let them learn God's ways for a good long time before they are baptized — like a tree given seasons to grow strong roots before the fruit comes.

When there is a supper of love — the feast the book calls the Agape — everyone eats together, rich and poor at one table, and no one is sent away empty.

When the harvest comes in, bring the first fruits — the first and best of what grew — not the leftovers nobody wanted. First for God, because He gave the whole field.

If a father dies, the book says, whatever he left for his children must be kept safe for those children. An orphan's share is not anyone else's to spend. Guard it like a treasure chest with the child's name on the lid.

If someone in the town is sick, don't keep it to yourself — tell the bishop, says the book, so the Church knows, and goes to sit beside the sick one's bed.

When the psalms are sung, the virgins and the boys answer the singers back and forth, like two choirs tossing a golden ball of song between them.

And if the poorest man in the town dies, with no family left in the world? Then the Church is his family. The book says: his brothers in Christ provide clean burial clothes and lay him to rest with honour. In a kind kingdom, nobody — nobody — is forgotten at the end.

Rise early, says the book. Wash. Pray. Teach one another, and encourage one another, day after day.

Small choices. Kind choices. That is how a kingdom becomes kind.

You don't need a crown to build the kind kingdom, little one — just today's small kind choice.
