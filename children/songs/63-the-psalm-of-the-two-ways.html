The Psalm of the Two Ways

From the Psalms of David, Psalm 1

Section: Songs and Wisdom

The very first psalm in the songbook is about two roads — and a tree.

Happy, says the psalm — truly happy — is the person who does not walk with the wicked, does not stand around with people doing wrong, and does not sit down to join in their mocking. Instead, this person **loves** God's teaching. He thinks about it in the morning. He thinks about it at night, the way you turn a favourite story over and over in your mind before you sleep.

And what is that person like? Like a tree! A tree planted right beside a river of water, where its roots can always drink. In its season it grows good fruit. Its leaves stay green and never wither. Everything it does grows well — because it is planted by the water.

But the wicked are not like the tree at all. They are like chaff — the light, dry husks left over when the good grain is gathered. The wind comes... and blows them away, here and there and gone.

So there are two ways to walk in this world: the way of the tree by the river, and the way of the chaff in the wind. God knows the way of the tree — He watches over it and cares for it. And the psalm wants you to choose, every day, to be planted close to Him, where your roots can drink deep.

Plant yourself close to God, like the tree by the river — and you will grow strong and green and full of good fruit.
