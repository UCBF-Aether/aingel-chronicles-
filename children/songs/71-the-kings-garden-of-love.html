The King's Garden of Love

From the Song of Songs (a selection)

Section: Songs and Wisdom

The Song of Songs is a garden full of singing. It is the love song of a King for his beloved — and God's people have always heard something even deeper in it: it is a picture of how greatly God loves **us**, His cherished people, and how we may love Him back.

Listen to the garden waking up in spring. "The winter is past," sings the King. "The rain is over and gone. The flowers appear on the earth, the time of singing has come, and the voice of the turtledove is heard in our land. Rise up, my love, my fair one, and come away!"

The beloved answers, "I am like a rose of Sharon, a lily of the valleys" — just a wild flower of the fields. But the King treasures her above everyone: "Like a lily among thorns, that is how my love is among all the daughters." And she answers with the happiest sentence in the book: "**My Beloved is mine, and I am His.**" She sits in His shade like sitting under an apple tree in summer, and His fruit is sweet, and His banner over her is love.

In the song there are little foxes to keep out of the vineyard, so the tender grapes are not spoiled — even small wrong things must be caught early, before they ruin something sweet. There is a garden that belongs to the King alone, with fountains and streams and every spice — and the King comes into His garden and is glad.

And at the end, the song says the strongest thing that has ever been said about love: "Set me as a seal upon your heart... for **love is strong as death. Many waters cannot quench love, and floods cannot drown it.**"

That is God's love for you: a garden in springtime, a feast under the apple tree — a love that no waters can ever wash away.

God's love for you is strong as death and sweet as spring — and no flood can ever drown it.
