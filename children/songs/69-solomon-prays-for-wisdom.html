Songs and Wisdom

Solomon Prays for Wisdom

From the Wisdom of Solomon, chapters 7–9

Section: Songs and Wisdom

You have heard how King Solomon, when he was young, asked God for wisdom instead of riches. This story is Solomon himself, grown older and wiser, telling us why — and praying the prayer again for all of us.

"I myself am only a man, like everyone else," he says. "When I was born, I cried, just as every baby cries. So I prayed — and understanding was given to me. I called — and the spirit of wisdom came to me. I valued her more than thrones and sceptres. I loved her more than gold and silver and precious stones, more even than health and beauty — because the light that comes from her never, ever goes out."

And then Solomon tries to tell us what Wisdom is like, and his words shine like morning. She is "the breath of the power of God," he says, "flowing pure from His glory. She is the brightness of the everlasting light, the spotless mirror of God's power, and the picture of His goodness." She can do all things. She orders everything kindly, from one end of the world to the other. Nothing unclean can touch her. And she is a friend — she enters into holy souls, in every generation, and makes them friends of God.

Then the king bows his head and prays the great prayer: "O God of my fathers, Lord of mercy, You made all things by Your word. **Give me Wisdom, who sits by Your throne.** Do not push me away from Your children! For I am Your servant, and I am weak, and my life is short, and I am far too small to understand everything. Send her out from Your holy heavens, so that she may be with me and work with me — and then I will know what pleases You."

That is a prayer any of us can pray — a child, a king, anyone at all. Wisdom is not hiding. Ask, and God delights to give.

Wisdom sits by God's throne — and the King of heaven loves to share her with everyone who asks.
