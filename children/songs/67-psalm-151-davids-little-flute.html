Psalm 151 — David and the Little Flute

From the Psalms of David, Psalm 151

Section: Songs and Wisdom

At the very end of the Ethiopian songbook there is one more psalm — a short one David wrote about himself when he was just a boy. It is not in every Bible, but it is in ours, and it is a treasure.

"I was little among my brothers," David wrote, "and the youngest in my father's house. I fed my father's flocks."

Out in the fields with the sheep, the boy was not idle. "My hands made a flute," he wrote, "and my fingers tuned a harp." All day long he played under the sky, and who heard his music? "**My Lord Himself hears,**" David said. God was his audience, out there in the quiet hills, and that was enough.

Then one day everything changed. God sent His messenger, the prophet Samuel, to the house of Jesse. David's brothers were brought out one by one — big, tall, strong, handsome young men. "But the Lord did not delight in them," David wrote. Instead, God chose the littlest one, the shepherd boy out with the sheep — and Samuel took the horn of oil and anointed him, right there. From shepherd boy to chosen king, all in one morning!

And years later, when a giant mocked God's people, it was that same little shepherd who went out to meet him, trusting the God who had always heard his songs. And the giant fell, and the shame was taken away from Israel.

That is David's own little psalm: the smallest brother, a homemade flute, and a God who hears.

God hears every song you sing — even the little ones, even all by yourself.
