David's Sorry Psalm

From the Psalms of David, Psalm 51

Section: Songs and Wisdom

King David had done a wrong and hurtful thing, and his heart was heavy with it. He did not hide it, and he did not make excuses. He sat down and wrote a psalm — and the psalm is a prayer that anyone can pray when they are truly sorry.

"Have mercy on me, O God," he prayed, "because Your love never runs out. Wash me clean from my sin. I know what I did was wrong — it is always in front of me. Create in me a **clean heart**, O God, and give me a right spirit again."

"Purge me, and I shall be clean. Wash me, and I shall be whiter than snow. Do not send me away from You. Give me back the joy of being Yours."

David knew something wonderful about God: God does not want big presents from a sorry heart. "The sacrifices of God," David wrote, "are a broken spirit. A broken and sorry heart, O God — You will never turn it away."

He never will. When you do wrong, you do not have to hide from God like Adam hid in the garden. You can run to Him, like David ran, and say the very same words: *Have mercy on me. Wash me clean. Create in me a clean heart.* And the God who heard David will hear you — and His face will shine on you again, and joy will come back like morning.

God never turns away a truly sorry heart — He washes it whiter than snow.
