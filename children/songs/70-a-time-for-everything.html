A Time for Everything

From the Book of Ecclesiastes, chapter 3

Section: Songs and Wisdom

King Solomon, who had seen everything under the sun, wrote a poem about time — and it may be the truest poem ever written. It begins like this:

**To everything there is a season, and a time to every purpose under heaven.**

A time to be born, and a time to die. A time to plant, and a time to gather up what was planted. A time to break down, and a time to build up. A time to weep, and a time to laugh. A time to be sad, and a time to dance! A time to throw stones away, and a time to gather stones together. A time to hug, and a time to stop hugging. A time to get, and a time to lose. A time to keep, and a time to throw away. A time to tear, and a time to sew. A time to be quiet, and a time to speak. A time to love, and a time for peace.

Do you see? Life is not all one season. There are spring days and winter days, laughing days and crying days — and God has made **everything beautiful in its time**. That is why you do not need to be afraid of the crying days, and why you should enjoy the laughing days with all your heart while they are here.

And Solomon found the secret of being happy in every season: rejoice, do good, eat your dinner with thanks, and enjoy the work of your hands — "it is the gift of God." Whatever God does lasts forever; nothing can be added to it and nothing taken away. So whatever time you are in right now — planting or gathering, laughing or crying — it is a time God knows, and God is in it with you.

Every season belongs to God — and He makes everything beautiful in its time.
