Ethiopia's Own Books

Menelik and the Journey Home

From the Kebra Nagast, the Glory of Kings, chapters 29–63 (a selection)

Section: Ethiopia's Own Books

When Queen Makeda came home to Ethiopia from Solomon's city, expecting a baby, she had a son nine months later: Menelik.

He grew bright. At twelve he asked the boys, "Who is my father?" — "Solomon the King!" He kept asking his mother until she told him. At twenty-two, strong, skilled, he said: "I will see my father's face — and come back, by God's will."

Makeda prepared Tamrin to guide him, soldiers, servants, gifts, provisions. She gave him Solomon's ring, so Solomon would know his son. Everywhere they stopped, people stared: "He has Solomon's face!" Messengers ran to Jerusalem; Solomon's heart was troubled and glad.

In Jerusalem, father and son met; Solomon knew him. There he was anointed king with holy oil. Horns, flutes, harps, drums filled the air with joy. He held the altar's horns, and they gave him a king's name: David. He rode his father's mule around the city; Solomon blessed him and told Zadok to teach him God's judgments.

Then came the parting. Israel's nobles' firstborn sons must travel home with the king to live in Ethiopia. Their hearts broke leaving Zion — the Tabernacle of God's Law in the Temple. Azaryas, the priest's son, said: "Let us take our Lady Zion with us." They swore secrecy and built a frame her size.

While Azaryas slept, the Lord's Angel came in a dream: Israel had angered God, and Zion wished to go. Sacrifices were offered. On the last night, the angel appeared as a pillar of fire, filling it with light. The sanctuary stood open. Azaryas and his companions carried Zion in a twinkling to the hiding place, setting the frame in her place under her coverings. For seven days she rested, with lamps and incense.

"Bless me, father," said Menelik. Solomon raised, embraced, and blessed him — with seed like the stars of heaven, and a heart righteous, merciful. "And my Lady Zion," said Solomon, "shall guide you always."

They travelled one day thirteen days' march — men and animals neither hungry nor thirsty nor tired — until they reached Ethiopia's border river. The nobles told him. "King," they said, "we must tell a secret. Zion is with you. It was not ours, but God's will — she wished to go."

Menelik stood before Zion, bowed, and praised God with all his heart. And Ethiopia's people took flutes, blew horns, beat drums, and sang — for Zion was coming home with their king.

And in Jerusalem, King Solomon grieved, learning Zion was gone. But the truth stood; his nobles agreed: this had happened by God's will.

Some treasures travel, little one — because God Himself is carrying them to the home He chose.
