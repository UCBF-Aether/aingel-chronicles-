The King's Prayer of Sorry

From the Prayer of Manasseh, the whole prayer

Section: Ethiopia's Own Books

Manasseh was a king of Judah — and for a long time, a bad king. He did wrong after wrong, and would not listen to God. In the end he was carried away in chains, a prisoner far away.

And there, at the very bottom, the king did the best thing of his whole life. He said sorry — the truest sorry in the whole Bible. This is his prayer, short enough to learn by heart, and strong enough to carry anyone home.

"O Lord, Almighty God of our fathers — God of Abraham, and Isaac, and Jacob, and of their righteous children!

"You made the heaven and the earth, and everything in them. You shut the sea and held it in with Your word. You closed the deep and sealed it with Your wonderful name. Everyone trembles before Your power — and yet, Your mercy is bigger than anyone can measure. You are the Lord most high. You are kind. You are patient. You are full of mercy. And You are sorry when people suffer for their sins.

"You, Lord, in Your great goodness, promised forgiveness to everyone who is sorry and turns back. You appointed repentance — a way home — for sinners, so that they may be saved.

"Lord, my sins are more than the sands of the sea. I am bowed down with many iron bands; I cannot lift up my head. I have no rest, because I angered You and did the wrong that You hate.

"But now I bow the knee of my heart, and I beg for Your goodness. I have sinned, Lord. I have sinned — and I know my wrongs; I am not hiding them. I ask You: forgive me, Lord, forgive me! Do not destroy me with my sins. Do not stay angry with me forever. Do not save up punishment for me, and do not hand me over to the depths of the earth.

"For You, Lord, are the God of everyone who says sorry. In me, You will show all Your goodness — for You will save even me, though I am not worthy, according to Your great mercy.

"And so I will praise You always, all the days of my life. All the powers of heaven sing to You — and Yours is the glory, forever and ever. Amen."

That is the king's prayer of sorry. A bad king. Iron bands. The bottom of everything. And a God with mercy bigger than the sea's sand — waiting to hear it.

No one is too far for God's mercy, little one — not even a king in chains. Just say it true: I am sorry. He hears.
