Ethiopia's Own Books

Ethiopia Stretches Out Her Hands

From the Kebra Nagast, the Glory of Kings, chapters 64–117 (a selection)

Section: Ethiopia's Own Books

The King of Ethiopia came home with joy, marching with songs, pipes and wagons like an army of angels, arriving from Jerusalem in one day. Messengers sailed ahead with the news: "Your son has become king — and he has brought the heavenly Zion!"

Queen Makeda spread the news through the country. "Come and meet your king," cried the heralds, "and meet Zion, the Tabernacle of the God of Israel!" Horns blew before her, and all Ethiopia rejoiced — least to greatest, men, women and children.

On the third day, Makeda did a queenly thing. She handed her son everything his by law: horses and mules, gold and silver, robes of honour — and her whole throne. "You are the king now," said the Queen. And Ethiopia's nobles and governors swore their oath to him.

Then Menelik sat with his mother and told the story — how Jerusalem had made him king, how his father had blessed him, how Zion had come. And Makeda spoke kindly to Israel's children who had travelled so far: you are not strangers here. This is your home too.

Azaryas the priest taught the people God's ways — what to eat and what to leave, how to live clean and kindly — and called for jubilee trumpets over the land, as God commanded in Moses' days.

And through this book, like gold thread through cloth, run the prophets' promises: that a Child would be born who is Christ the Lord; that He would suffer, die, and rise again on the third day; that He would go up into heaven in glory; and that one day He will come again, and every knee will bow. Moses believed it. The prophets believed it. Ethiopia believed it — and still does.

And Zion? The book promises: the Tabernacle of God's Law shall remain in Ethiopia until our Lord comes in glory. She is still there, guarded and honoured, in Aksum.

Far ahead, the book sees the King of Rome and the King of Ethiopia meeting in Jerusalem — not with spears, but with gifts. They made offerings together, established the faith in love, and shared the kiss of peace, and shared one royal name like brothers, sons of David and Solomon.

That is how the Glory of Kings ends: not with a sword, but with outstretched hands. A whole country, hands lifted to heaven. It is just what the psalm said long ago:

"Princes shall come out of Egypt; Ethiopia shall soon stretch out her hands unto God."

And she did. And she still does.

Stretch out your hands too, little one — God loves to see them coming, from every country under the sky.
