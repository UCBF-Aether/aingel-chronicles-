AINGEL CHRONICLES  ·  THE MUSEUM

The Hidden Shelf

Extra hidden treasures, kept for history.

If you've read the whole Bible and want more — this shelf is for you. These are extra books, related to the faith but not part of it: old writings, lost writings, and strange writings, kept here so they aren't forgotten. NOT CANON. NOT SCRIPTURE. Read them for extra thoughts and for historical value — and weigh everything against the Book itself.

Claimants

Books wearing lost names. These are NOT the originals — each card says so plainly, and the line never blurs.

Sefer ha-Yashar (1840 English "Book of Jasher")

STATUS: CLAIMANT — NOT THE LOST BOOK OF JASHER · CHAPTERS 1–91 COMPLETE

A medieval work first printed in Venice in 1625, published in English in 1840 under the name "Book of Jasher." A book of midrashic legends — interesting in its own right, but not the original cited in Joshua and Samuel.

Read the text →

Epistle to the Laodiceans (Latin)

STATUS: CLAIMANT — NOT THE LOST ORIGINAL · READABLE TEXT

A short Latin letter, found in the Codex Fuldensis (c. 546) and medieval Latin Bibles, stitched together from Philippians and Galatians. Long called a clumsy forgery by scholars — kept here as a historical curiosity, not as Paul's lost letter.

Read the text →

3 Corinthians

STATUS: CLAIMANT — ANCIENT, BUT NOT PAUL'S LOST EARLIER LETTER · READABLE TEXT

An ancient correspondence between Corinth and Paul, preserved in Armenian and Syriac and tied to the Acts of Paul (c. 200 AD). Ancient and real — but not the lost letter of 1 Corinthians 5:9.

Read the text →

Pseudo-Jasher (Ilive, 1751)

STATUS: CLAIMANT — FORGERY, NOT THE ORIGINAL · CARD ONLY

Jacob Ilive's 1751 "Book of Jasher," a forgery for which he was imprisoned. It wears the lost book's name and is nothing of the kind — the card records the fraud honestly.
