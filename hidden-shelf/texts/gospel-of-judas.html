The Gospel of Judas

A talk between Jesus and Judas, in the days before the Passover.

NOT CANON. NOT SCRIPTURE.
Kept here for extra thoughts and for historical value — and weighed against the Book itself. This writing is not part of the Bible, was not received as Scripture by the early church, and is presented here only as an ancient document, not as teaching to follow.

Translated anew for Aingel from the Coptic text.

Translator's Note

Source manuscript and edition. The only surviving copy is in Codex Tchacos, a papyrus book found in Egypt in the 1970s, radiocarbon-dated to about 220–340 CE. This text occupies pages 33–58 of that codex; its closing title, in Coptic, stands at the end of the work, not at the beginning. The work itself was most likely first written in Greek in the second century, and survives here only in a Coptic rendering, in a Sahidic dialect with Middle Egyptian colouring.

Base text used here. The preliminary Coptic transcription established by Rodolphe Kasser and Gregor Wurst, dated February 2006 and issued by the National Geographic Society in April 2006 as the basis for its first English edition. That transcription was made from a manuscript already badly harmed by decades of poor keeping, breaking, and sale in pieces. A fuller critical edition followed in 2007, and further fragments recovered later (the so-called Ohio fragments, reported in 2010) fill some of the 2006 holes. This translation deliberately stays with the 2006 base text and does not silently fill its holes from later fragments: where the 2006 text is broken, the break is shown.

Original language. Coptic. This is a fresh, literal rendering made for Aingel from that Coptic transcription, in the translator's own wording throughout.

Method. The Coptic was rendered as literally as English allows, keeping its order of thought where possible, and keeping its names, numbers, and images intact. Published scholarly English renderings (notably Kasser, Meyer, and Wurst, 2006) were used only as a sense check — to test whether a difficult Coptic passage had been understood — and no wording has been copied from them. Where the Coptic is broken, restored only by conjecture, or readable in more than one way, that fact is marked in the text rather than smoothed away.

Damaged state of the manuscript. The damage is severe and uneven. Whole stretches are gone: the lower part of one page, about fifteen lines in one place, about seventeen lines in another, about nine lines and about six and a half lines in others, plus many single lines and half-lines. Some pages survive only in poor photographs. In this translation every such loss is marked [gap in manuscript], [about … lines missing], or [uncertain]. Square-bracket restorations in the scholarly editions are treated here as uncertain unless the sense is plain, and are flagged as such. Nothing has been invented to join the surviving pieces into a smooth story.

What is missing, in plain terms. The beginning survives, the middle is pitted with holes, and the ending breaks off in fragments: after Judas hands Jesus over, only the closing title survives intact. The codex's other contents — the Letter of Peter to Philip, James, and Allogenes — are not part of this page. The conclusion of the whole codex, beyond the Judas text, is not preserved in the material used here.

A note on disputed readings is given at the foot of this page. Read those notes before drawing any conclusion from a single striking line: in this damaged text, a single word can turn the sense.

Opening — the hidden telling

A hidden telling of the disclosure that Jesus spoke when he talked with Judas Iscariot, in the week — three days — before he kept the Passover. [The exact sense of the time phrase is uncertain; see Notes on disputed readings.]

Jesus on earth

When Jesus showed himself on earth, he did wonders and great works for the rescue of humankind. And because some walked in the road of right living while others walked in their crossings-over, the twelve learners were called.

He began to talk with them about the hidden things outside the world, and about what would happen at the end. Often he did not show himself to his learners as himself; instead he was found among them in the form of a child.

Scene 1 — Jesus laughs at the thanksgiving prayer

One day he was with his learners in Judea, and he found them gathered and seated in devout practice. When he [came near] to his learners, gathered and seated and offering a prayer of thanks over the bread, he laughed.

The learners said to him, “Teacher, why are you laughing at our prayer of thanks? We have done what is right.”

He answered and said to them, “I am not laughing at you. You are not doing this by your own will; rather, it is through this that your god [will be] praised.”

They said, “Teacher, you are [gap in manuscript] the son of our god.”

Jesus said to them, “How do you know me? Truly I tell you, no generation of the people who are among you will know me.”

The learners grow angry

When his learners heard this, they began to grow angry and enraged, and to speak ill of him in their hearts.

When Jesus saw their lack of [understanding], he said to them, “Why has this stirring led you into anger? Your god who is inside you, and [gap in manuscript], have stirred you to anger inside your souls. Let any one of you who is strong enough among human beings bring forward the complete human and stand before my face.”

They all said, “We have the strength.”

But their spirits did not dare to stand before him — except for Judas Iscariot. He was able to stand before him, but he could not look him in the eyes, and he turned his face away.

Judas said to him, “I know who you are, and where you have come from. You are from the deathless realm of Barbelo. And I am not worthy to speak the name of the one who sent you.”

Jesus speaks to Judas alone

Knowing that Judas was turning over something lofty in his mind, Jesus said to him, “Come away from the others, and I will tell you the hidden things of the kingdom. You can reach it, but you will grieve greatly. For another will take your place, so that the twelve learners may once more be complete with their god.”

Judas said to him, “When will you tell me these things, and when will the great day of light dawn for the generation?”

But as he said this, Jesus left him.

Scene 2 — The disciples' vision of the temple

The next morning, after this happened, Jesus [showed himself] to his learners again.

They said to him, “Teacher, where did you go, and what did you do when you left us?”

Jesus said to them, “I went to another great and holy generation.”

His learners said to him, “Lord, what is the great generation that is above us and holier than us, that is not now in these realms?”

When Jesus heard this, he laughed and said to them, “Why are you thinking in your hearts about the strong and holy generation? Truly I tell you, no one born of this aeon will see that generation, and no army of star-angels will rule over that generation, and no person of mortal birth can keep company with it, because that generation does not come from [gap in manuscript] which has become [gap in manuscript]. The generation of people among you is from the generation of humankind, [gap in manuscript] power, which [gap in manuscript] the other powers, by which you rule.”

When his learners heard this, each was troubled in spirit. They could not speak a word.

Another day Jesus came to them. They said to him, “Teacher, we have seen you in a vision, for we have had great dreams [gap in manuscript] in the night [gap in manuscript].”

He said, “Why have you [gap in manuscript] when you have gone into hiding?” [The wording of this reply is broken and uncertain.]

The vision of the temple

They said, “We have seen a great house with a large altar in it, and twelve men — they are the priests, we would say — and a name; and a crowd of people is waiting at that altar, until the priests [gap in manuscript] and receive the offerings. But we kept waiting.”

Jesus said, “What are the priests like?”

They said, “Some [gap in manuscript] two weeks; some offer up their own children, others their wives, in praise and lowliness toward one another; some lie with men; some are caught up in killing; some commit a host of sins and lawless deeds. And the men who stand before the altar call on your name, and in all the deeds of their lack, the sacrifices are brought to an end [gap in manuscript].”

After they said this, they fell quiet, for they were troubled.

Jesus explains the temple vision

Again I tell you, my name has been written on this [gap in manuscript] of the generations of the stars, through the human generations. The cattle you have seen brought for sacrifice are the many people you lead astray before that altar. [gap in manuscript] will stand and use my name in this way, and generations of the devout will stay loyal to him. After him, another man will stand there from the fornicators, and another will stand there from the killers of children, and another from those who lie with men, and those who hold back, and the rest of the people of defilement and lawlessness and error, and those who say, ‘We are like angels’; they are the stars that bring everything to its end. For to the human generations it has been said, ‘Look, God has taken your sacrifice from the hands of a priest’ — that is, a servant of error.

But it is the Lord, the Lord of all, who commands, ‘On the last day they will be put to shame.’”

Jesus said to them, “Stop offering sacrifices [gap in manuscript] which you have [gap in manuscript] over the altar, since they are over your stars and your angels and have already reached their end there. So let them be caught before you, and let them go [about 15 lines missing] generations [gap in manuscript]. A baker cannot feed all creation under heaven. And [gap in manuscript] to them [gap in manuscript] and [gap in manuscript] to us and [gap in manuscript].”

Scene 3 — Judas tells his own vision

Judas asks about that generation

Jesus said, “The souls of every human generation will die. When these people, however, have finished the time of the kingdom and the spirit leaves them, their bodies will die but their souls will be alive, and they will be taken up.”

Judas said, “And what will the rest of the human generations do?”

Jesus said, “It is not possible to sow seed on rock and gather its fruit. This is also the way [gap in manuscript] the defiled generation [gap in manuscript] and corruptible Sophia [gap in manuscript] the hand that made mortal people, so that their souls go up to the realms above that last forever. Truly I tell you, [gap in manuscript] angel [gap in manuscript] power will be able to see that [gap in manuscript] these to whom [gap in manuscript] holy generations [gap in manuscript].”

After Jesus said this, he went away.

Judas recounts a vision

Judas said, “Teacher, as you have listened to all of them, now also listen to me. For I have seen a great vision.”

When Jesus heard this, he laughed and said to him, “You thirteenth demon — why do you strain so hard? But speak, and I will put up with you.” [On “demon” versus “spirit” here, see Notes on disputed readings.]

Judas said to him, “In the vision I saw myself as the twelve learners were stoning me and persecuting me harshly. And I also came to the place where [gap in manuscript] after you. I saw a house [gap in manuscript], and my eyes could not take in its size. Great people were around it, and that house had a roof of green leaves, and in the middle of the house was a crowd [about two lines missing], saying, ‘Teacher, take me in along with these people.’”

Jesus answered and said, “Judas, your star has led you astray.” He went on, “No person of mortal birth is worthy to enter the house you have seen, for that place is set aside for the holy. Neither the sun nor the moon will rule there, nor the day, but the holy will dwell there always, in the realm that lasts forever with the holy angels. Look, I have laid out for you the hidden things of the kingdom, and I have taught you about the error of the stars; and [gap in manuscript] send it [gap in manuscript] over the twelve aeons.”

Judas asks about his own fate

Jesus answered and said to him, “Come, that I [about two lines missing], but that you will grieve much when you see the kingdom and all its generation.”

When he heard this, Judas said to him, “What good is it that I have received it? For you have set me apart for that generation.”

Jesus answered and said, “You will become the thirteenth, and you will be cursed by the other generations — and you will come to rule over them. In the last days they will curse your rising to the holy generation.”

The teaching about the Spirit and the Self-Generated

Jesus said, “Come, that I may teach you about hidden things no person has ever seen. For there is a great and boundless realm, whose breadth no generation of angels has seen, in which there is a great unseen Spirit, which no eye of an angel has ever seen, no thought of the heart has ever grasped, and which was never called by any name.”

“And a shining cloud appeared there. He said, ‘Let an angel come into being as my attendant.’”

“A great angel, the enlightened divine Self-Generated, came out of the cloud. Because of him, four other angels came into being from another cloud, and they became attendants for the angelic Self-Generated. The Self-Generated said, ‘Let [gap in manuscript] come into being [gap in manuscript],’ and it came into being [gap in manuscript]. And he made the first luminary to rule over him. He said, ‘Let angels come into being to serve him,’ and tens of thousands without number came into being. He said, ‘Let an enlightened aeon come into being,’ and it came into being. He made the second luminary to rule over him, together with tens of thousands of angels without number, to offer service. That is how he made the rest of the enlightened aeons. He set them to rule over them, and he made for them tens of thousands of angels without number, to help them.”

THE GOSPEL OF JUDAS

The closing title as it stands at the end of the Coptic text. No further text of this work survives in the base edition used here.

Notes on disputed readings

These notes record disagreements rather than settling them silently. They are part of the translation, not an afterthought.

1. “Thirteenth demon” or “thirteenth spirit”? In the vision scene, the Coptic word is daimōn (borrowed from Greek). The 2006 first edition rendered the address to Judas in a softer way; later scholars, notably April DeConick, argue the Coptic must be read as “demon,” which would make Judas a thirteenth, ruling demon-figure rather than a honoured thirteenth spirit. This translation keeps the Coptic word's plain sense — “demon” — and flags the dispute, because the choice changes whether the passage praises Judas or condemns him.

2. “Set apart for” or “cut off from” that generation? When Judas says Jesus has set him apart for a generation, the Coptic verb can be heard in more than one direction, and scholars divide over whether Judas is being singled out for the holy generation or separated from it. The rendering here follows the 2006 sense (“set apart for”), with this flag.

3. “You will sacrifice the man who clothes me.” This is the most quoted line in the book. All editors agree on the Coptic image — a human body as clothing worn by Jesus — but they disagree sharply on what it means for Judas: a commanded, honoured act that frees Jesus, or a wicked act for which Judas is cursed. The text itself gives both notes side by side: Judas “will go beyond all” and also “will be cursed by the other generations.” This translation does not resolve that tension.

4. The opening time phrase. The incipit places the talks “during a week, three days before” the Passover. The Coptic phrasing is compressed and its exact count is debated. The rendering here keeps the words in their order and marks the phrase uncertain, rather than forcing it into a modern calendar.

5. Gaps filled by later fragments. The Ohio fragments reported in 2010 are said to raise the readable share of the text to roughly nine-tenths. They are not used to fill gaps here, because this page's stated base is the 2006 preliminary transcription. A future revision that adopts the critical edition and the later fragments should list, line by line, every gap it closes.

6. Names. Barbelo, Adamas, Nebro/Yaldabaoth, Saklas, Seth, Harmathoth, Galila, Yobel, and Adonaios are kept as they stand in the Coptic, including the double name Nebro, “which means ‘rebel,’” whom others call Yaldabaoth. “Aeon,” “luminary,” and “firmament” are kept as technical terms of this text's cosmology, not modernised away.
