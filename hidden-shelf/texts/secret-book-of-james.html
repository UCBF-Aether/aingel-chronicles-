The Secret Book of James

The Apocryphon of James

NOT CANON. NOT SCRIPTURE. Kept here for extra thoughts and for historical value.
This is an ancient writing outside the Bible, kept on this shelf so it is not forgotten — and weighed, like everything here, against the Book itself.

TRANSLATOR'S NOTE — READ FIRST

Source manuscript. Nag Hammadi Codex I, tractate 2 (NHC I,2), pages 1–16, a fourth-century Coptic copy now in the Coptic Museum, Cairo. The manuscript itself carries no title; the name “Secret Book of James” comes from what the letter calls itself — an apocryphon, a secret book. The Coptic is a translation of a lost Greek original, most likely composed in the second century.

A correction to the brief for this page. The commissioning brief described this book as “Nag Hammadi Codex V,3” with a second copy in Codex Tchacos. That description belongs to a different James book — the First Apocalypse of James (NHC V,3; Codex Tchacos, tractate 2). The Secret Book of James survives in one copy only, NHC I,2. This page translates that book, and does not borrow from the Apocalypse to fill it out. To have followed the brief's shelfmark literally would have produced the wrong text.

Original language and method. Original language: Coptic. This is a literal, fresh translation made in my own words from the Coptic text, following the public-domain Coptic transcription of NHC I,2 (Mark M. Mattison, based on the Williams/Attridge edition) and the standard printed Coptic edition. Existing scholarly English renderings (notably Meyer/Barnstone and Mattison) were used only as a sense check — to test whether a difficult passage had been understood — and none of their wording has been copied.

Uncertainties — not smoothed over. The manuscript is damaged, above all in its first pages. Every place where the papyrus is broken, a line is missing, or the sense is genuinely doubtful is marked inline as [gap in manuscript], [uncertain], or with a note. Nothing has been invented to complete a broken sentence. The addressee's name is lost except for its ending, “…thos”; the old guess “Cerinthus” is [uncertain] and is not adopted as fact. Where scholars disagree on a reading, the disagreement is flagged rather than settled silently. The letter claims to be written “in the Hebrew alphabet”; the surviving text is Coptic from Greek, so that claim is the letter's own framing, not a description of the manuscript we have.

What this book is. A letter-frame around a dialogue: after the risen Saviour appears to the twelve, he draws James and Peter aside and speaks to them alone — about being filled and lacking, about the cross and death, about prophecy and parables, about the word, and about knowing oneself — before he departs and James sends the other disciples away and goes up to Jerusalem. It is presented here complete as far as the surviving manuscript allows; the gaps are part of the text, not defects to be hidden.

1. Salutation

JAMES WRITES to […]thos [gap in manuscript — the addressee's name is lost except for its ending; “Cerinthus” is an uncertain guess, not a fact]:

Peace to you from peace, love from love, grace from grace, faith from faith, life from holy life!

2. The Letter: Two Secret Books

You asked me to send you a secret book — one revealed to Peter and to me by the Lord — and I could neither refuse you nor speak with you face to face, so I have written it in the Hebrew alphabet and sent it to you, and to you alone. But since you serve the well-being of the saints, take care: do not spread this book abroad to many people. It is a book the Saviour did not wish to disclose even to all twelve of us, his disciples. Yet blessed are those who will be made whole through faith in this discourse.

Ten months ago I sent you another secret book, one the Saviour revealed to me. Count that one as revealed to me, James. But this one […] [gap in manuscript]

Here the letter-frame breaks off. A damaged stretch (page 2, lines 1–7) interrupts the text; when it resumes, the scene has moved to the twelve disciples together.

3. The Saviour Appears

Now the twelve disciples were all sitting together, recalling what the Saviour had told each one of them, whether in private or in public, and setting it down in books. I was writing down what was in my book, when — look! — the Saviour appeared, after he had left us while we were watching for him.

It was five hundred and fifty days after he had risen from the dead when we said to him, “Have you gone away and left us?”

JESUS SAID: “No. I shall go back to the place from which I came. If you wish to come with me, come.”

They all answered, “If you command us, we will come.”

HE SAID: “Truly I tell you, no one will ever enter the kingdom of heaven merely because I commanded it, but because you yourselves are full. Leave James and Peter to me, so that I may fill them.”

Having called those two, he drew them aside and told the rest to keep at what they were doing.

4. Being Filled, and Lacking

THE SAVIOUR SAID: “You have received mercy […] [gap in manuscript — several lines missing here] they have not understood. Do you not want to be filled? Your hearts are drunk. Do you not, then, want to be sober? Be ashamed!

From now on, waking or sleeping, remember that you have seen the Son of Man, and spoken with him face to face, and heard him face to face.

Woe to those who have seen the Son of Man! Blessed are those who have not seen him, nor kept company with him, nor spoken with him, nor heard anything from him. Yours is life!

Know this: he healed you when you were sick, so that you might reign. Woe to those who have found relief from their sickness, for they will fall sick again. Blessed are those who have not been sick, and who found relief before falling sick. The kingdom of God is yours!

So I tell you: be filled, and leave no empty space inside you, or the one who is coming will be able to mock you.”

THEN PETER ANSWERED: “Look — three times you have told us, ‘Be filled,’ but we are filled.”

THE SAVIOUR ANSWERED: “That is why I told you, ‘Be filled’ — so that you would not lack. Those who lack will not be made whole. To be filled is good, and to lack is bad. Yet just as it is good for you to lack and bad for you to be filled, so whoever is filled also lacks. One who lacks is not filled in the same way as another who lacks is filled; but whoever is filled is brought to completion.

So you should lack while you can still be filled, and be filled while you can still lack, so that you may fill yourselves all the more. Be filled with spirit, but lack in reasoning — for reasoning belongs to the soul. Indeed, it is soul.”

5. The Cross and Death

I ANSWERED AND SAID TO HIM: “Master, we can obey you if you wish, for we have left behind our fathers and our mothers and our villages, and followed you. Grant us a way not to be tempted by the evil devil.”

THE MASTER ANSWERED: “What good does it do you if you do the Father's will, yet when you are tempted by Satan you are not given your share of his bounty? But if you are oppressed by Satan and persecuted, and still do the Father's will, I tell you: he will love you, he will make you my equal, and he will count you beloved — through his foreknowledge, and by your own choosing.

Will you not stop loving the flesh and fearing suffering? Do you not know that you have not yet been mistreated, falsely accused, thrown into prison, condemned unlawfully, crucified [uncertain — “for no reason” is an editorial filling; without it the Coptic could read “crucified with fine words”], or buried in the sand, as I myself was by the evil one? Do you dare to spare the flesh — you, for whom the spirit is a wall all around you?

If you weigh how long the world existed before you, and how long it will exist after you, you will find your life is a single day and your sufferings a single hour. The good will not come into the world. So despise death, and give your thought to life. Remember my cross and my death, and you will live.”

I ANSWERED: “Master, do not speak to us of the cross and death, for they are far from you.”

THE MASTER ANSWERED: “Truly I tell you, no one will be made whole unless they believe in my cross, for the kingdom of God belongs to those who have believed in my cross. Become seekers of death, then, like the dead who seek life — for what they seek is shown to them. And what have they to worry about?

As for you: when you search into death, it will teach you about being chosen. Truly I tell you, no one who fears death will be made whole, for the kingdom of death belongs to those who are put to death. Become better than I am. Be like the child of the Holy Spirit.”

6. Prophecy, Parables, and Being Eager

THEN I ASKED HIM: “Master, can we prophesy to those who ask us to prophesy to them? There are many who make the request of us and look to us to hear a pronouncement.”

THE MASTER ANSWERED: “Do you not know that the head of prophecy was cut off with John?”

I SAID: “Master, is it possible for the head of prophecy to be removed?”

THE MASTER SAID TO ME: “When you grasp what ‘head’ means, and that prophecy comes from the head, then you will understand what ‘its head was removed’ means.

At first I spoke to you in parables, and you did not understand. Now I speak to you openly, and you still do not perceive it. Yet to me you were a parable among parables, and a revelation among things revealed.

Be eager to be made whole without being urged. Be ready on your own, and if possible get there ahead of me — for in that way the Father will love you.

Come to hate hypocrisy and evil intent, for intent gives birth to hypocrisy, and hypocrisy stands far from truth.

Do not let the kingdom of heaven wither. It is like a shoot of a date palm whose fruit has fallen down around it. It put out leaves, and after they sprouted, its fruitfulness dried up. So it was also with the fruit that came from this single root: when it was picked, many obtained fruit. Would it not truly be good if you could bring forth the new plants now? You would find it.

Since I was glorified in this way once before, why do you hold me back when I am eager to go? After the labour, you have kept me with you another eighteen days, because of the parables. For some people it was enough to hear the teaching and to understand ‘The Shepherds,’ ‘The Seed,’ ‘The Building,’ ‘The Lamps of the Young Women,’ ‘The Wage of the Workers,’ and ‘The Silver Coins and the Woman.’

7. Eager for the Word

Be eager for the word. The first part of the word is faith, the second is love, the third is works — and from these comes life.

The word is like a grain of wheat. When someone sowed it, he had faith in it; when it sprouted, he loved it, because he saw many grains in place of one. And after he worked, he was made whole, because he prepared it as food and still kept some aside to sow.

This is how you yourselves can receive the kingdom of heaven. Unless you receive it through knowledge, you will not be able to find it.”

8. The Great Light, and Plain Speech

“So I tell you: be sober. Do not go astray. Many times I have said to you all together — and to you alone, James, I have said it — ‘Be made whole!’ I have commanded you to follow me, and I have taught you what to say before the rulers.

See: I have come down, I have spoken, I have laboured hard, and I have taken my crown, when I saved you. I came down to dwell with you, so that you might dwell with me. And when I found your houses had no roofs, I lived in houses that could receive me when I came down.

Trust me, my brothers, in this: understand what the great light is. The Father does not need me — a father does not need a son; it is the son who needs the father. I am going to him, for the Father of the Son does not need you.

Listen to the word, understand knowledge, love life — and no one will persecute you, and no one will oppress you, except you yourselves.”

9. Woe and Blessing

“You wretches! You poor devils! You pretenders to truth! You falsifiers of knowledge! You sinners against the Spirit! Do you still dare to listen, when from the beginning you should have been speaking? Do you still dare to sleep, when from the beginning you should have been awake, so that the kingdom of heaven might receive you?

Truly I tell you, it is easier for a holy person to sink into defilement, and for an enlightened person to sink into darkness, than for you to reign — or not to reign.

I remember your tears, your mourning, and your grief. They are far from us. You who stand outside the Father's inheritance: weep when you must, mourn, and proclaim what is good, as is fitting, while the Son is ascending.

Truly I tell you, if I had been sent only to those who would listen to me, and had spoken to them alone, I would never have come down to earth. Now be ashamed.

Look, I shall leave you and go away. I do not want to remain with you any longer — just as you yourselves have not wanted this. Follow me quickly. I tell you, it is for you that I came down. You are loved ones. You will bring life to many people. Call upon the Father, pray to God often, and he will be generous with you.

Blessed is the one who has seen you with him when he is proclaimed among the angels and glorified among the saints. Yours is life. Rejoice and be glad as children of God. Keep his will, that you may be made whole. Accept correction from me, and save yourselves. I am pleading for you with the Father, and he will forgive you many things.”

10. Few Have Found the Kingdom

When we heard this, we were glad. We had grown gloomy because of what we had said earlier. But when he saw us happy, he said:

“Woe to you who need an advocate! Woe to you who stand in need of grace! Blessed will be those who have spoken out and gained grace for themselves!

Compare yourselves to foreigners. How are they regarded in your city? Why are you anxious to cast yourselves out on your own, and cut yourselves off from your city? Why abandon your dwelling on your own, and make it open to those who wish to live in it? O you exiles and runaways — woe to you. You will be seized.

Or do you think the Father is a lover of people, or that he is won over by prayers [uncertain — the Coptic can also be read as “not won over by prayers”], or that he shows grace to one person for another's sake, or that he tolerates whoever seeks?

He knows desire, and what the flesh needs. The flesh does not desire the soul: without the soul the body does not sin, just as without the spirit the soul is not made whole. But if the soul is made whole apart from evil, and the spirit is made whole too, then the body becomes sinless. The spirit gives life to the soul, but the body kills it — that is, the soul kills itself.

Truly I tell you, he will never forgive the sin of the soul, nor the guilt of the flesh, for none of those who have worn the flesh will be made whole. Do you think many have found the kingdom of heaven? Blessed is the one who has seen oneself as a fourth one in heaven [uncertain — “fourth” may count faith, love, and works as the first three; the text does not explain it].”

11. Know Yourselves

When we heard this, we became sad. But when he saw that we were sad, he said: “I say this to you so that you may know yourselves.

The kingdom of heaven is like a head of grain that sprouted in a field. When it ripened, it scattered its seed, and again it filled the field with heads of grain for another year. So with you: be eager to harvest for yourselves a head of the grain of life, that you may be filled with the kingdom.

And while I am with you, pay attention to me and trust in me; but when I am far from you, remember me. Remember me because I was with you and you did not know me.

Blessed will be those who have known me. Woe to those who have heard and have not believed. Blessed will be those who have not seen, yet have [gap in manuscript — the verb is lost; “believed” is the sense the context suggests, but it is not in the surviving text].

And once again I appeal to you: I am revealed to you as one building a house that will be useful to you when you find shelter in it — and it will hold up your neighbour's house when theirs threatens to fall. Truly I tell you, woe to those for whom I was sent down here. Blessed will be those who are going up to the Father.

Again I warn you, you who are: be like those who are not, that you may be with those who are not.

Do not let the kingdom of heaven become a desert within you. Do not be proud because of the light that enlightens. Instead, act toward yourselves as I myself have acted toward you. I have put myself under a curse for you, to save you.”

12. The Last Word

PETER RESPONDED TO THESE WORDS AND SAID: “Sometimes you urge us toward the kingdom of heaven, and at other times you turn us away, Master. Sometimes you encourage us and draw us toward faith and promise us life, and at other times you drive us away from the kingdom of heaven.”

THE MASTER ANSWERED US AND SAID: “I have offered you faith many times — and I have revealed myself to you, James — and you have not known me. Now I see you rejoicing often. And while you are delighted about the promise of life, you are sad and downcast when you are taught about the kingdom.

Nevertheless, through faith and knowledge you have received life. So disregard rejection when you hear it, but when you hear the promise, rejoice all the more. Truly I tell you, whoever receives life and believes in the kingdom will never leave it — not even if the Father wished to banish them.

This is all I shall tell you for now. Now I shall go up to the place from which I came. When I was eager to go, you cast me out, and instead of accompanying me, you chased me away. But be attentive to the glory that awaits me, and when you have opened your hearts, listen to the hymns that await me up in heaven — for today I must sit at the right hand of the Father.

Now I have spoken my last word to you, and I shall leave you, for a chariot of spirit has taken me up, and from now on I shall strip myself off, that I may be clothed.

Pay attention: blessed are those who proclaimed the Son before he came down, so that when I came, I might go up. Blessed three times over are those who were proclaimed by the Son before they came into being, so that you may have a share with them.”

13. The Ascent

When he had said this, he left. Peter and I knelt down, gave thanks, and sent our hearts up to heaven. We heard with our ears and saw with our eyes the noise of wars, a trumpet blast, and great turmoil.

When we passed beyond that place, we sent our minds up further. We saw with our eyes and heard with our ears hymns, angelic praises, and angelic rejoicing. Heavenly majesties were singing hymns, and we rejoiced too.

Again after this we wished to send our spirits up to the majesty. When we ascended, we were not allowed to see or hear anything. The other disciples called to us and asked, “What did you hear from the teacher? What did he tell you? Where did he go?”

We answered them, “He ascended. He gave us his right hand, and promised all of us life. He showed us children who will come after us, having commanded us to love them, since we are to be made whole for their sakes.”

14. Conclusion: Jerusalem

When they heard this, they believed the revelation, but they were angry about those who would be born. Not wishing to give them cause for offence, I sent each of them to a different place. I myself went up to Jerusalem, praying that I might gain a share with the loved ones who are to come.

I pray that the beginning may come from you. This is how I can be made whole. They will be enlightened through me, by my faith — and through another's faith, better than mine. I wish mine to be the lesser.

Do your best to be like them, and pray that you may gain a share with them. Beyond what I have said, the Saviour did not disclose any revelation to us on their behalf. We proclaim a share with those for whom the message was proclaimed — those whom the Lord has made his children.
