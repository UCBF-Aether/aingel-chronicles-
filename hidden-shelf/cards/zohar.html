TRADITION Jewish, Kabbalah  ·  ERA 13th-century Spain  ·  STATUS TOO LARGE TO TRANSLATE WHOLE

The Zohar, the Book of Splendor, is the central text of Kabbalah: a vast mystical commentary on the Torah, written mostly in Aramaic, moving through the divine emanations, the soul, and the hidden life behind the letter of Scripture.

Tradition attributes it to Shimon bar Yochai, a sage of the 2nd century; scholarship attributes its composition to Moses de León's circle in 13th-century Spain. Either way, it shaped Jewish mysticism for centuries and still does.

A full fresh translation from Aramaic is a multi-volume, multi-year project — far too large a translation job for this shelf. This card therefore links outward rather than pretending the whole book could be translated here.

READ ELSEWHERE: A public-domain English excerpt (Bereshith to Lekh Lekha, translated by Nurho de Manhar) can be read at the Internet Sacred Text Archive: archive.sacred-texts.com — The Zohar. That is a partial, early translation, not the complete Zohar. No fresh translation is included on this card.
