A forgery wearing a lost book's name.

TRADITION English, 18th century  ·  ERA 1751  ·  STATUS CLAIMANT (forgery)

In 1751 Jacob Ilive, a Bristol printer, published a book he claimed was a translation of a lost Hebrew manuscript of the Book of Jasher, supposedly discovered in Persia. It was presented as the very book the Bible cites in Joshua 10:13 and 2 Samuel 1:18.

Scholars recognized it as a fabrication almost at once: its contents betray anachronisms and modern philosophical leanings no ancient Hebrew book could carry. Ilive was imprisoned for the fraud.

This card exists as a warning label. The lost Book of Jasher is still lost, and this is not it. No text from Ilive's book is hosted here, and none should be mistaken for the original.
