TRADITION Arabic, Andalusi  ·  ERA 10th–11th century  ·  STATUS DESCRIBED, NOT HOSTED

The Picatrix is the Latin name for Ghayat al-Hakim, The Aim of the Sage, an Arabic work on astrology and magic composed in 10th–11th-century al-Andalus. It gathers astral lore, the powers attributed to planets and stars, talismanic theory, and the philosophical idea that the heavens move the lower world.

It matters historically far beyond its own pages: translated into Latin in the 13th century, it carried Arabic astral thought into medieval Europe, where scholars, courts, and later occult writers read, quoted, and argued over it for centuries.

This is a description card only. No part of the text is hosted here, and no ritual instructions, recipes, or operative procedures from it are reproduced — its history is the point, not its practice.
