Sabbath Teaching · Week 47

By Faith — and the Small Letters

Sabbath, September 11, 2027 · Readings: Ephesians 6; Philippians 1–4; Colossians 1–4; 1 Thessalonians 1–5; 2 Thessalonians 1–3; 1 Timothy 1–6; 2 Timothy 1–4; Titus 1–3; Philemon 1; Hebrews 1–13; James 1–5; 1 Peter 1

Who are all these people, gathered at the end of the apostolic shelf? This is the week's wonder: after the armour of Ephesians 6 and letter after letter — joyful, urgent, tender, brief — you arrive at Hebrews 11, and suddenly the whole year stands in review. "Now faith is the substance of things hoped for, the evidence of things not seen."

Read that chapter as the roll call it is. Abel offers. Enoch walks with God — the same Enoch whose book you read whole back in the winter of this year, when other canons' readers could only meet him in Jude's quotation. Noah builds. Abraham goes out, not knowing whither he went, and offers Isaac and receives him back in figure. Moses refuses Egypt's treasures. The walls of Jericho fall. Rahab is named among the faithful. Then the names come too fast to list — judges, kings, prophets — and the writer breaks off: the world was not worthy of them. You have met them all. That is the gift of reading Hebrews here, near the year's end, in this canon: chapter 11 is not a list of strangers from a Sunday-school wall. It is a summary of the road you yourself have walked since Genesis 1, face by familiar face.

Around that summit, the week is crowded with smaller lights, and the small letters deserve their own honour. Philippians sings from a prison cell: rejoice in the Lord alway. Colossians lifts Christ above every power and then dresses the new life in compassion, kindness, and forgiveness, garment by garment. The Thessalonian letters steady a young church about the Lord's return and about honest work while waiting. Timothy and Titus receive a mentor's charge — guard the deposit, appoint elders of proven character, preach the word in season and out — the church-order seed from which this canon's closing books will grow.

And Philemon: one chapter, the quietest work of the week. Paul could command; instead he appeals in love, asking a master to receive back a runaway slave, Onesimus, "not now as a servant, but above a servant, a brother beloved" — and offering to put any debt on his own account. No law is thundered. A household is quietly rearranged by grace.

James then insists that faith prove itself alive in works — the tongue governed, the widow and orphan visited, no favour shown to the rich over the poor — and 1 Peter opens with living hope for scattered sufferers. Keep Hebrews 11 in the middle of it all, where the week placed it: the great cloud of witnesses is not behind you. After a year in their company, they are around you.

You have walked with them all year — now hear their names called, and take your place.
