Sabbath Teaching · Week 47

By Faith — and the Small Letters

Sabbath, September 11, 2027 · Readings: Ephesians 6; Philippians 1–4; Colossians 1–4; 1 Thessalonians 1–5; 2 Thessalonians 1–3; 1 Timothy 1–6; 2 Timothy 1–4; Titus 1–3; Philemon 1; Hebrews 1–13; James 1–5; 1 Peter 1

Who are all these people, suddenly, standing in one crowded week? After the armour of Ephesians 6 and letter after letter — joyful, urgent, tender, brief — the week sets Hebrews 11 in front of you like a roll call at the end of a long journey: "Now faith is the substance of things hoped for, the evidence of things not seen." Read that definition as the chapter performs it. Faith is not a feeling about the future; it is substance — the thing hoped for already having weight in the hand. And then the writer proves his definition the only way it can be proved, by lives.

Move down the roll and notice what the year has done to you. Abel offers, and by faith, being dead, yet speaks. Enoch walks with God — this Enoch, whose whole book you read in February, when most Bible readers in the world can only meet him in a quotation. Noah builds an ark for a flood he cannot see. Abraham goes out not knowing where he is going, and offers Isaac and receives him back in a figure. Moses refuses Egypt's treasures. Jericho's walls fall. Rahab the harlot is named in the honoured list, without embarrassment. Then the names come too fast to list and the writer admits it — time would fail him — and sums up a crowd who conquered kingdoms and a crowd who were tortured and wandered in deserts, and says of both the same astonishing sentence: of whom the world was not worthy.

Spurgeon's exposition of this chapter noticed where the chapter itself points: these men and women are, in the apostle's next breath, the "cloud of witnesses" surrounding the runners who come after — not spectators in the vague sense, but the men and women whose names are inscribed, as Spurgeon put it in another sermon, on the triumphal arch of Hebrews 11, watching how the race they began is run. For you, this year, that image is not a metaphor borrowed from a commentary. You have walked with every one of those witnesses in their own books, at full length, in order. Hebrews 11 read in week 47 is a summary of your own road. That is a privilege no shortcut reading plan can give, and it is worth pausing to receive it as such.

Around that summit the small letters do their quiet work, and this teaching honours them deliberately. Philippians sings from prison. Colossians dresses the new life garment by garment. Timothy and Titus carry a mentor's charge — guard the deposit, appoint leaders of proven character — the seed of the church-order books you will read in the year's last weeks. James will not let faith stay theoretical: "Pure religion and undefiled before God and the Father is this, To visit the fatherless and widows in their affliction, and to keep himself unspotted from the world." And Philemon — one chapter, no thunder — may be the week's bravest text. Paul could command Philemon; instead he appeals, asking a master to receive a runaway back "Not now as a servant, but above a servant, a brother beloved" — and offering to charge any debt to his own account. No law is passed. A household is rearranged by grace from the inside.

Doctrine, plainly: faith is trust that acts — it offers, builds, goes out, refuses, receives — and dead faith, faith with no works, is not a quieter kind of faith; James says it is no living faith at all. Practice this week in Philemon's pattern: find one relationship where you hold the stronger position — creditor, employer, elder, parent — and deliberately use that strength the way Paul used his apostleship, by appeal and by absorbing a cost yourself, to turn a transaction into a brotherhood.

You have walked with them all year — now hear their names called, and take your place in the roll.
