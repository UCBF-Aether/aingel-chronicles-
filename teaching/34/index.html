Sabbath Teaching · Week 34

Can These Bones Live?

Sabbath, June 12, 2027 · Readings: Ezekiel 19–44

"Son of man, can these bones live?" Begin where Ezekiel begins: not with the answer, but with the honesty of the man asked. He is set down in a valley full of bones, very many and very dry, and he is made to pass by them round about — he is not allowed a glance; he is given a tour. The bones are then explained to him in the chapter's own interpretation: they are the whole house of Israel, saying, our bones are dried, our hope is lost, we are cut off. And to God's question the prophet gives the only answer that is both truthful and faithful: "And he said unto me, Son of man, can these bones live? And I answered, O Lord GOD, thou knowest." Not yes, flippantly. Not no, despairingly. Thou knowest — the valley handed back to the only one who knows what can live in it.

Then watch the raising, because God does it in two distinct acts, and the distinction is the doctrine. First Ezekiel prophesies to the bones: "So I prophesied as I was commanded: and as I prophesied, there was a noise, and behold a shaking, and the bones came together, bone to his bone." "And when I beheld, lo, the sinews and the flesh came up upon them, and the skin covered them above: but there was no breath in them." Form without life. A nation reassembled, a church reorganised, a person reformed on the outside — and still dead. So a second prophesying is commanded, this time not to the bones but to the breath: "Then said he unto me, Prophesy unto the wind, prophesy, son of man, and say to the wind, Thus saith the Lord GOD; Come from the four winds, O breath, and breathe upon these slain, that they may live." "So I prophesied as he commanded me, and the breath came into them, and they lived, and stood up upon their feet, an exceeding great army." And the promise seals it with God's own agency: "And shall put my spirit in you, and ye shall live, and I shall place you in your own land: then shall ye know that I the LORD have spoken it, and performed it, saith the LORD."

Spurgeon preached this text in 1864 in The Restoration and Conversion of the Jews, and returned to it in 1890 in Come From the Four Winds, O Breath! — in the later sermon opening from verse 9, the second prophesying, and insisting the vision pictures more than one kind of resurrection at once. Take his structure seriously: the word alone assembles; the Spirit alone enlivens; and the prophet's part in both is obedience with a voice. Ezekiel contributes no breath of his own. He speaks what he is given, twice, and God does everything that looks like a miracle.

The surrounding chapters teach you to believe the valley. In chapter 33 God swears his own heart toward the wicked: "Say unto them, As I live, saith the Lord GOD, I have no pleasure in the death of the wicked; but that the wicked turn from his way and live: turn ye, turn ye from your evil ways; for why will ye die, O house of Israel?" In chapter 34 the shepherds who fed themselves are dismissed, and God takes the flock into his own hands — Spurgeon's sermon A Time of Finding for Lost Sheep is preached on exactly this chapter's promise: "For thus saith the Lord GOD; Behold, I, even I, will both search my sheep, and seek them out." "I will seek that which was lost, and bring again that which was driven away, and will bind up that which was broken, and will strengthen that which was sick: but I will destroy the fat and the strong; I will feed them with judgment." And from chapter 40 a temple is measured, until: "And, behold, the glory of the God of Israel came from the way of the east: and his voice was like a noise of many waters: and the earth shined with his glory." "And the glory of the LORD came into the house by the way of the gate whose prospect is toward the east."

Live it this week. Name your valley honestly to God — one person, one hope, one part of your own life that looks very dry — and answer as Ezekiel answered: thou knowest. Then do the prophet's part and no more: speak the word you are given over it, and pray specifically for breath, for the Spirit, not merely for rearrangement. Form is not life, and God is not finished with what he has only assembled.

Answer the valley "thou knowest," speak the word given you — and pray for breath, not just form.
