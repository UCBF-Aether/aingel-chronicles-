Sabbath Teaching · Week 34

Can These Bones Live?

Sabbath, June 12, 2027 · Readings: Ezekiel 19–44

"Son of man, can these bones live?" It may be the most honest question in the prophets, because Ezekiel does not pretend to know its answer. This week the priest-prophet who began by the river Chebar — carried into exile, shown God's glory departing the temple — is set down in a valley full of bones, very many, and very dry. The bones are explained to him plainly: they are the whole house of Israel, saying, our hope is lost, we are cut off. Before you rush to the famous ending, sit with Ezekiel's reply, because it is the right prayer for every valley you will ever stand in: "O Lord GOD, thou knowest."

Watch how the raising happens, because God does it in stages, and the stages preach. First the word is prophesied to the bones, and there is a noise, and a rattling, and bone comes to bone; sinews and flesh and skin cover them — but there is no breath in them. Form without life. A people can be reassembled, reorganized, even impressive, and still be dead. So Ezekiel must prophesy a second time, to the wind, to the breath itself — and "the breath came into them, and they lived, and stood up upon their feet, an exceeding great army." The same passage promises graves opened — "I will open your graves, and cause you to come up out of your graves" — teaching you that restoration is God's work from first to last. Ezekiel contributes only obedience and a voice. That is all God asks of him, and it is enough.

The chapters around the valley prepare you to believe it. In chapter 33 Ezekiel is set again as a watchman to the house of Israel, hearing the word at God's mouth and warning the people — and the chapter turns tender, as God swears he has no pleasure in the death of the wicked, but that the wicked turn and live. In chapter 34 the false shepherds who fed themselves instead of the flock are dismissed, and God takes the flock into his own hands: "Behold, I, even I, will both search my sheep, and seek them out." One shepherd of David's line is promised, and a covenant of peace, and showers in their season. And from chapter 40 the prophet is carried, in the visions of God, to a temple measured reed by reed — the glory that departed early on returning through the east gate in chapter 43, to dwell among the children of Israel for ever.

Dry bones, sought sheep, a returning glory: this week's Ezekiel is one long argument that God finishes what exile interrupts. Bring him your own valley. Answer as Ezekiel answered — thou knowest — and then speak what you are given to speak, and watch for the breath.

Answer the valley as Ezekiel answered — "O Lord GOD, thou knowest" — and watch for the breath.
