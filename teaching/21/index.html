Sabbath Teaching · Week 21

Ezra's Questions

Sabbath, March 13, 2027 · Readings: Nehemiah 4–13; 1 Esdras 1–9; 2 Esdras 1–16

Why do the righteous suffer, while the wicked seem to flourish? Of all the questions in this week's wide reading, that is the one 2 Esdras will not put down. The book speaks in the name of Ezra — Salathiel, who is also Esdras — a generation after Jerusalem's fall, and it gives its Ezra no easy comfort. In chapters 3–7 he argues with the angel Uriel, honestly and at length: Adam sinned, and we are his children; Israel was chosen, and Israel lies crushed; the world is full of people, he says, and very few of them seem to be saved. Why?

Uriel's answers are humbling rather than satisfying, and that is the book's honesty. “Go thy way,” the angel says, “weigh me the weight of the fire, or measure me the blast of the wind, or call me again the day that is past.” Ezra cannot — and that is the point. A vessel made of the world, Uriel tells him, cannot comprehend the way of the Highest by measuring it. What Ezra is given instead is a promise: this age of sorrow is real but not final, a harvest is coming, and the end will make sense of the seed-time. Generations of readers in times of loss have kept this book close precisely because it asks the forbidden question out loud, in prayer, and stays in the room for the answer.

The week's other books ask their questions in deeds. In Nehemiah the wall rises under threat, and the builders work as chapter 4 describes them — “every one with one of his hands wrought in the work, and with the other hand held a weapon.” Prayer and a trowel, watchfulness and work, in the same pair of hands: Nehemiah's answer to danger is to pray and to keep building.

And in 1 Esdras comes the week's famous contest. Three young guardsmen debate what is strongest in the world — wine, the king, women — and the young Zorobabel takes the prize by adding a fourth: truth. “As for the truth, it endureth, and is always strong; it liveth and conquereth for evermore,” and the people shout the verdict: “Great is Truth, and mighty above all things” (1 Esdras 4:38, 41). His reward is permission to rebuild Jerusalem. Questions, work, and truth — this week's Ezra, in his several voices, holds all three together.

Bring God the hardest question honestly — and keep building while you listen for the answer.
