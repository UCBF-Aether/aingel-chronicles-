Sabbath Teaching · Week 28

The Souls of the Righteous

Sabbath, May 1, 2027 · Readings: Proverbs 10–31; Wisdom of Solomon 1–19; Ecclesiastes 1

What does this canon say, plainly and without flinching, about death? You finished Proverbs this week, and Proverbs is mostly about life — work, speech, money, family. But the book that follows it on this wider shelf, the Wisdom of Solomon, turns and looks death in the face, and its third chapter holds one of the most beloved sentences in all the wider canon. If you read only one passage again this Sabbath, read that one.

"But the souls of the righteous are in the hand of God, and there shall no torment touch them." So Wisdom begins its answer. In the sight of the unwise, it goes on, the righteous seemed to die, and their departure was taken for misery — but they are in peace. The book does not pretend that the righteous escape suffering or early graves. It says something quieter and stronger: that what death looks like from outside is not what death is. A hand holds them. Read the whole chapter and watch how it refuses both easy comfort and despair. It will not call death nothing, and it will not call death the end.

That hope lands differently because of the company it kept this week. Proverbs 10–31 is wisdom worked out at ground level, one short saying at a time. A soft answer turneth away wrath. The diligent hand makes rich, while the slothful hand makes poor. A prudent wife, a faithful friend, a controlled tongue, an honest scale — this is holiness with its sleeves rolled up, wisdom for kitchens and marketplaces and doorsteps. And the book closes with its portrait of the woman of valour: "Who can find a virtuous woman? for her price is far above rubies." Her works, the last verse says, praise her in the gates. Wisdom, in Proverbs, is not an idea. It is a life you can point to.

Hold the two together and you have this week's whole teaching. Proverbs teaches you how to live the days; Wisdom teaches you how to trust God with the end of them. Neither book rushes you. The sayings can be read one a day, a mouthful at a time, and the great promise of chapter 3 can be read the same way — one sentence a day, until it is yours by heart. Then, at the week's edge, a new voice muttered its first word: the Preacher, and "vanity of vanities." Next week you will hear him out. For today, rest in the older, steadier sentence: the souls of the righteous are in the hand of God.

Live the days diligently, as Proverbs teaches — and trust the end of them to the hand that holds the righteous.
