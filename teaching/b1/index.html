Bonus Teaching · From the Appendix

The Teaching of the Mysteries

Readings: The Teaching of the Mysteries 1–5

At the very back of this edition, past the eighty-one books of the canon, ride two beloved classics of Ethiopian Christian literature, clearly marked as what they are: a bonus appendix, not part of the count. The smaller of them is a book of five short chapters and twenty-seven verses, and it calls itself a hidden teaching. Its Ge'ez name is Temherta Hebu'at — the Teaching of the Mysteries — and small as it is, its very name promises a teaching kept hidden.

It opens with a handing-down: “Concerning the hidden teaching, which was spoken of old by Peter Fora[?], unto the hidden believers.” What follows is not a story but a creed — a confession of Christ spoken in paradoxes, one after another, as if ordinary grammar kept breaking under the weight of it. He who was before, and is. He who suffered, who cannot suffer. He who was born in the flesh, who cannot be born. Dead, living. Contained, who cannot be contained. The book does not explain these opposites away. It sets them side by side and worships between them.

Then, in the fourth chapter, the questions begin — “Who is this who, by the power of the flame of fire, enters into struggle with death? Who is this who has vanquished the darkness?” Read them as the cry of the powers who thought the tomb was theirs: a stranger has entered death's own country, and death is asking his name. The fifth chapter answers with the ending: “The dead arose; Sheol he trampled; by his death, death he destroyed.” And then, simply, he glorified the Father — glory and power for ever and ever. Amen.

How did this little book reach us? It was translated anew, directly from the Ge'ez, for this edition — its middle chapters from the printed Ge'ez of Lifchitz and Grébaut (1940), its first and last chapters from the project's Ge'ez corpus. Its documented standing deserves to be stated exactly: its 1940 edition prints the Ge'ez with a French translation only, and no earlier published English translation is known. The French of that edition was consulted as a scholarly reference, and never republished. What you read here stands on the Ge'ez itself.

Watch for the paradox pattern as you read — it is the book's whole method. Again and again a thing is said and its opposite is said in the same breath, and both are meant. The Teaching of the Mysteries is not hiding its truth from you. It is teaching you that some truths can only be carried in two hands at once.

Read this one aloud and slowly — a creed five chapters long, spoken of old to hidden believers, is meant to be heard, not hurried.
