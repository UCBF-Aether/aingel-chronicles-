Sabbath Teaching · Week 8

From Jericho's Walls to Shechem's Stones

Sabbath, December 12, 2026 · Readings: Joshua 6–24; Judges 1–7

What does a conquered wall teach, and what does a farewell stone require? This week ran from Jericho to Shechem — almost the whole book of Joshua — and into the opening of Judges. At Jericho, Israel does nothing that looks like warfare: priests, trumpets, a silent march, day after day, and on the seventh day a shout. The walls fall flat, and the city is taken. The lesson is set at the very gate of the land: the first victory is God's gift, won by obedience to a command that must have looked foolish to everyone watching. Rahab, whom you met last week in Joshua 2, is brought out safely with her household, as promised.

The campaigns that follow are harder and messier. There is defeat at Ai because of hidden sin in the camp, covenant made hastily with Gibeon, long days of battle in south and north, and then, slowly, land divided by lot among the tribes, cities of refuge appointed, and the Levites given their towns. Joshua grows old with the work still unfinished. The book refuses to pretend that one shout at Jericho settled everything.

That honesty makes Joshua's farewell, in chapters 23–24, land with such weight. At Shechem — a place already heavy with memory — the old leader retells the whole story once more, from Abraham's father serving other gods beyond the flood, through Egypt and the sea and the wilderness, to the land they now hold, with its cities they did not build and vineyards they did not plant. Then he puts the choice plainly: “Choose you this day whom ye will serve… but as for me and my house, we will serve the LORD.” The people answer that they will serve the Lord, Joshua warns them what they are promising, and a great stone is set up under an oak as a witness against them if they lie to their God.

Then Judges opens, and the ground shifts. Tribe by tribe the conquest stalls, a new generation arises that knew not the Lord, and the long cycle of the book begins: forsaking, oppression, crying out, deliverance. By its end this week you have met Gideon — a fearful man threshing wheat in hiding, called a mighty man of valour, given a fleece for a sign, and sent out with an army deliberately whittled down to three hundred, so no one could say Israel's own hand had saved it.

Watch for stones and choices this week: stones at Shechem to remember, and a choice that must be made again by every generation after Joshua, because a gift received is not yet a faith kept.

Choose again, deliberately, whom you will serve — and set up something that helps you remember you chose.
