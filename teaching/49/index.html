Sabbath Teaching · Week 49

The Sinodos — the Church's Rulebook

Sabbath, September 25, 2027 · Readings: Sirate Tsion (Sinodos 1) 7–10; Tizaz (Sinodos 2) 11–19; Gitsew (Sinodos 3) 20–28; Abtilis (Sinodos 4) 29–37; Gessew 1–57; 1 Book of the Covenant 1–10

What happens after the apostles — who decides how a church actually lives? This week you hold the answer this canon kept: the Sinodos whole, thirty-seven chapters of church order, read straight through alongside its companion the Gessew. If the Gospels are the story and the letters are the counsel, these books are the working rulebook of a living community.

The Sinodos comes in four traditional parts, and this week you finish the first and read the other three entire. Sirate Tsion, the Order of Zion, chapters 1–10, opens the collection — you began it last week. Tizaz, the Commandments, chapters 11–19, follows. Gitsew, chapters 20–28, is the third part. Abtilis, chapters 29–37, stands last, closing the book. Together they carry rulings on the things real congregations actually face: bishops, priests, and deacons chosen and held to account; widows cared for; prayer and fasting ordered; disputes settled inside the household of faith rather than dragged before the world; the poor named as the church's own responsibility. Watch for how concrete it stays. Holiness here is not a mood. It has names, times, duties, and a table where people in need are fed.

For this edition the Sinodos was translated anew, directly from the Ge'ez, from the text printed by G. Horner in The Statutes of the Apostles in 1904. Horner's own English was used only as a check on difficult passages — the rendering here stands on the Ge'ez itself. That matters for how you receive it: not a summary of an old book, but the book itself, carried over for this canon's readers.

Beside it stands the Gessew, all fifty-seven chapters: the fifty-six apostolic canons and a closing epilogue counted as canon 57. Where the Sinodos ranges broadly, the Gessew speaks in short, direct rules — how officers should be chosen and should conduct themselves, and how the community handles worship, discipline, marriage, and correction. It was translated anew from the Ge'ez of W. Fell's edition, Canones apostolorum Aethiopice, printed in 1871. And here a naming thread ties the week together: the Gessew is traditionally the Sinodos' third part, under the spelling Gitsew — the same name you just read as the Sinodos' chapters 20–28. One thing it is not, and the record is plain about it: the Gessew is not the "Book of Adam and Eve." Do not let that label attach to it.

At the week's edge, a new voice opens: the first ten chapters of the 1 Book of the Covenant, where the risen Lord himself is presented as ordering his church. Next week we give those books their full hearing.

A church that loves its story must also order its life — these pages show how one church did.
