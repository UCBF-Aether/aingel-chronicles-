Sabbath Teaching · Week 31

The Prophet of the New Covenant

Sabbath, May 22, 2027 · Readings: Jeremiah 2–31

What do you do when the old arrangement has failed — not once, but for centuries, in plain sight? That is Jeremiah's question, and this week's reading climbs, chapter by chapter, toward the most hopeful answer any prophet ever gave. You have walked with Jeremiah through sermons and tears and a potter's workshop. This Sabbath, gather the week around its summit: chapter 31, and the words "new covenant."

First, feel the weight the promise has to lift. In chapter 7 Jeremiah stood in the gate of the LORD's house and preached the sermon that nearly cost him his life: do not trust in lying words, saying, "The temple of the LORD, the temple of the LORD." Amend your ways, he cried — execute judgment, oppress not the stranger, the fatherless, and the widow — and stop supposing that stone walls make a corrupt people safe. In chapter 18 he watched a potter rework a marred vessel on the wheels, and heard God say: "Behold, as the clay is in the potter's hand, so are ye in mine hand, O house of Israel." The nation is clay that keeps spoiling in the shaping. Something deeper than repair is needed.

Chapter 29 shows Jeremiah's pastoral heart in the meantime. To exiles already carried to Babylon, longing for a quick rescue, he wrote a letter of almost shocking calm: build houses, plant gardens, marry, have children — and "seek the peace of the city whither I have caused you to be carried away captives, and pray unto the LORD for it: for in the peace thereof shall ye have peace." Settle, pray, bless the place of your punishment. God's plans for them, the letter promises, are plans of peace, to give them a future and a hope — but on God's timetable, not theirs.

Then chapter 31 arrives, set inside a little book of consolation where Rachel's weeping is answered and Ephraim's bowing turns to mercy. "Behold, the days come, saith the LORD, that I will make a new covenant with the house of Israel, and with the house of Judah." Not like the covenant broken at the Exodus, though God had been a husband to them. This one will be written inside: "I will put my law in their inward parts, and write it in their hearts; and will be their God, and they shall be my people." No one will need to teach his neighbour to know the LORD, for all will know him, from the least to the greatest — "for I will forgive their iniquity, and I will remember their sin no more." The failed arrangement is not patched. It is replaced by forgiveness and an inward law. Sit with that this week. Everything Jeremiah wept over is answered here.

A covenant written on the heart, sealed with forgiveness — let his law move inward in you this week.
