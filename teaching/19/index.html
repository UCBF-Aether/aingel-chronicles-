Sabbath Teaching · Week 19

The Son of Man Enoch Saw

Sabbath, February 27, 2027 · Readings: 1 Enoch 15–84

Who is the figure standing beside the throne? That is the question this week's long reading keeps asking. You have come through Enoch's journeys and judgements, and now, in the section called the Parables — chapters 37–71, “the second vision which he saw, the vision of wisdom” — the seer's eyes are lifted to the court of heaven itself, and someone is standing there.

Enoch describes him carefully, in the words Charles's translation gives us. He sees One whose head is days, whose head is “white like wool” — the Head of Days — “and with Him was another being whose countenance had the appearance of a man, and his face was full of graciousness, like one of the holy angels.” He asks who this is, and the answer is the beating heart of the Parables: “This is the Son of Man who hath righteousness, with whom dwelleth righteousness, and who revealeth all the treasures of that which is hidden, because the Lord of Spirits hath chosen him.” This Son of Man will raise up kings and the mighty from their seats and break the teeth of sinners, because they did not exalt the Lord of Spirits nor acknowledge whence their kingdom came.

Chapter 48 presses the wonder further back. “At that hour that Son of Man was named in the presence of the Lord of Spirits, and his name before the Head of Days. Yea, before the sun and the signs were created, before the stars of the heaven were made, his name was named before the Lord of Spirits.” Named before creation. Chosen and hidden before the world was. And his purpose is mercy as much as judgement: “He shall be a staff to the righteous whereon to stay themselves and not fall, and he shall be the light of the Gentiles, and the hope of those who are troubled of heart.” Keep it, this Sabbath, exactly where the text keeps it: this is what Enoch's pages say, in their own words — an Elect One, a Son of Man, enthroned with the Head of Days, before whom the kings of the earth will one day be downcast, and on whom the righteous lean like a staff.

Then the week closes in a different register entirely: the book of the luminaries, chapters 72–82, where the angel Uriel shows Enoch the laws of the sun and moon — the portals of heaven in the east and the west, the luminaries following “each other in accurately corresponding order.” After thrones and judgement, the ordered heavens: the same God who sets the Elect One on the throne of glory sets the sun in its gates, morning by morning.

Lean on the Elect One like a staff — he was named before the stars were made.
