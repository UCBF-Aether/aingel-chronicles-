Sabbath Teaching · Week 19

The Son of Man Enoch Saw

Sabbath, February 27, 2027 · Readings: 1 Enoch 15–84

Who is standing beside the throne? That is the question this week's long reading refuses to let go. You have come through Enoch's rebukes of the Watchers, his journeys, his visions of judgement — and then, in the section called the Parables, chapters 37–71, the seer is lifted into the court of heaven itself, and someone is standing there.

Open chapter 46 and read as slowly as Enoch saw it. “And there I saw One, who had a head of days, And His head was white like wool, And with Him was another being whose countenance had the appearance of a man, And his face was full of graciousness, like one of the holy angels.” Two figures. One is the Head of Days — God in the imagery Daniel will later use. The other has a man's face, full of graciousness. Enoch does what any reader does: he asks. Who is this, and why does he stand with the Head of Days? The angel's answer is the beating heart of the Parables: “This is the Son of Man who hath righteousness, With whom dwelleth righteousness, And who revealeth all the treasures of that which is hidden, Because the Lord of Spirits hath chosen him.” Notice the titles piling up across these chapters — Son of Man, Elect One, Righteous One, Anointed — and notice that Charles, in his scholarly introduction to the book, observed that several of these messianic titles appear here, in this form, for the first time. Enoch is not describing a second god or a rival throne. He is describing the one through whom the Lord of Spirits will judge the kings of the earth and shelter the righteous.

Chapter 48 presses the wonder further back in time than any reader expects: “before the sun and the signs were created, Before the stars of the heaven were made, His name was named before the Lord of Spirits.” Named before creation. Chosen and hidden before the world was. And then the text tells you what this pre-existent one is for — and it is not terror first: “He shall be a staff to the righteous whereon to stay themselves and not fall, And he shall be the light of the Gentiles, And the hope of those who are troubled of heart.” A staff. Light. Hope. The judge of kings is introduced to the troubled of heart as the thing they lean on. Keep the teaching exactly there, where Enoch keeps it: this is what these pages say, in their own words, centuries of readers before you have heard in them the shape of the Messiah's coming — a human face beside the Ancient of Days, righteousness dwelling with him, the mighty raised down from their seats and the righteous raised up.

Then the week closes in a completely different register: the Book of the Luminaries, chapters 72–82, where Uriel shows Enoch the laws of the sun and moon, the portals of the east and west, until “the year is exactly as to its days three hundred and sixty-four.” After thrones and judgement, the ordered heavens. The same Lord who sets the Elect One beside his throne sets the sun in its gates, morning by morning, and never once is late.

Scholarly preaching on the Parables — there is no historic sermon tradition here, and this record will not invent one — centres on the Daniel 7 connection: Enoch's Son of Man expands Daniel's “one like the Son of man,” giving him a name before creation, a throne of glory, and a congregation of the righteous leaning on him. That is genuine, and it is enough.

The doctrine: the one who will judge the world is not a stranger appointed at the last minute — he was named before the stars, and his first description to the troubled is a staff to lean on. The practice this week: lean, deliberately. Take one trouble of heart — name it specifically, not generally — and pray it toward the Elect One as staff, not as slogan: Lord, be to this what you promised to be. And stop leaning on one false staff — the approval, the substance, the distraction you actually reach for first. Enoch's righteous are not the untroubled. They are the ones who know where their weight rests.

Lean on the Elect One like a staff — he was named before the stars were made.
