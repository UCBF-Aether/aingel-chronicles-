Sabbath Teaching · Week 45

Paul's Roads, Paul's Letter

Sabbath, August 28, 2027 · Readings: Acts 11–28; Romans 1–12

What carries a message across an empire — roads, or words? This week gives you both. Acts closes with Paul travelling sea and courtroom until he preaches in Rome itself, and Romans opens: the one great letter Paul wrote to a church he had not yet visited, setting out the gospel he carried on all those roads.

Follow the roads first, because Acts wants you to feel their length. The gospel reaches Antioch, where the followers are first called Christians. Paul and Barnabas are sent out; councils sit in Jerusalem to settle what the Gentiles must bear; cities follow in a rhythm you can almost chant — synagogue, preaching, some believe, trouble rises, the road again. Philippi, Thessalonica, Athens with its altar to the unknown god, Corinth, Ephesus. Then arrest, trials before governors and a king, a storm and shipwreck on the way to Italy, and finally Rome, where the book ends not with a verdict but with Paul in his own hired house, preaching the kingdom of God to all who come, no man forbidding him. Acts refuses to tie its ending in a bow. The witness reaches the capital and keeps speaking.

Then turn to Romans, and notice the shift. Acts is the gospel in motion; Romans is the gospel explained. Paul writes ahead of himself to a mixed church of Jews and Gentiles in that same capital, and he argues like a man laying foundations stone by stone. "For I am not ashamed of the gospel of Christ: for it is the power of God unto salvation to every one that believeth; to the Jew first, and also to the Greek." All have sinned — the verdict is universal before the mercy is. Then Abraham takes the stand: "Abraham believed God, and it was counted unto him for righteousness." Justification by faith is not Paul's invention of a slogan; it is, he insists, the oldest story Israel owns, read rightly.

By chapter 8 the argument has become a song. "There is therefore now no condemnation to them which are in Christ Jesus." Nothing in creation — not death, not life, not powers, not things present or things to come — can separate the believer from the love of God. And chapters 9–11, far from abandoning Israel, grieve and hope over her: God's promises have not failed, and the root still bears the branches. Read those chapters slowly; Paul weeps inside his own theology.

So the week holds a traveller and a letter-writer who are the same man. The roads prove the message can travel anywhere. The letter proves it can bear the weight of the hardest questions. Next week the letters multiply, and the traveller becomes a pastor.

The gospel Paul carried on his feet, he also carried in his heart onto the page.
