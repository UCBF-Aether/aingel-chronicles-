Sabbath Teaching · Week 9

Loyalty in a Lawless Time

Sabbath, December 19, 2026 · Readings: Judges 8–21; Ruth 1–4; 1 Samuel 1–11

What does faithfulness look like when no one around you is being faithful? This week you read Judges to its bleak end, and its last line explains the whole book: “In those days there was no king in Israel: every man did that which was right in his own eyes.” The chapters before it show what that means in practice — Gideon's victory soured by a golden ephod, Abimelech murdering his way to a crown, Jephthah's rash vow, Samson's strength wasted on his own appetites, and at the last, idolatry, violence, and civil war inside Israel itself. These are hard chapters, and the Bible does not soften them.

Set right after them, like a lamp lit in a dark house, is Ruth — and Ruth's own book tells you exactly when it happened: “in the days when the judges ruled.” In those same lawless days, a famine drives Naomi's family to Moab, death takes her husband and both her sons, and a young Moabite widow refuses to leave her. “Intreat me not to leave thee,” Ruth says, “for whither thou goest, I will go; and where thou lodgest, I will lodge: thy people shall be my people, and thy God my God.”

That loyalty is the week's whole light, and it works itself out in ordinary places: a field in Bethlehem, gleanings left behind by reapers, a kinsman named Boaz who notices kindness and answers it with kindness, elders sitting in the gate. No trumpets, no armies. And the child born at the end of Ruth's little book, Obed, is the grandfather of David. In a time when everyone did what was right in his own eyes, God carried the royal line forward through a foreign widow who simply would not let go of love.

Then 1 Samuel opens, and loyalty takes another humble form: a childless woman, Hannah, praying in tears at Shiloh, pouring out her soul, and then keeping her vow by giving her long-awaited son back to God's service. Little Samuel grows up in that same house, hears God's voice in the night while Eli sleeps, and learns to answer, “Speak; for thy servant heareth.” Around him, a corrupt priesthood falls, the ark is lost and returned, and at last an ageing Samuel anoints Saul — tall, handsome, hiding among the stuff — as Israel's first king.

Watch for where God is working in a lawless time. Not, this week, mainly in armies and judges, but in a widow's vow, a mother's prayer, and a boy listening in the dark. Loyalty, prayed and kept in small rooms, is how this story survives its worst chapters.

Stay loyal in a lawless time — go where love calls you, and let God carry the line forward.
