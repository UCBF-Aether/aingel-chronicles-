Sabbath Teaching · Week 22

The Books of the Hidden Years

Sabbath, March 20, 2027 · Readings: Tobit 1–14; Judith 1–16; Esther 1–17; 1 Meqabyan 1–11

What does faithfulness look like when God's people have no king, no army, and no home of their own? This week's reading widens suddenly — story after story from the hidden years under foreign rule — and each book answers that question with a different face.

Tobit gives us the road. An old, blinded exile who kept burying the dead when it was dangerous to do so sends his son Tobias on a journey, and God sends a travelling companion: Raphael, the angel unawares, walking chapters 5–12 beside the young man as guide, protector, and, in the end, revealer. When Raphael finally names himself, his parting teaching is as plain as it is deep: “Prayer is good with fasting and alms and righteousness” (Tobit 12:8), and, just before it, “Do that which is good, and no evil shall touch you.” Everyday righteousness — alms, honest work, care for the dead — is the road this book blesses.

Judith gives us courage. Bethulia is besieged, its water cut off, its leaders ready to set God a deadline. Judith — a widow who has been fasting and praying — rebukes the faint-heartedness first, in chapter 8, and then prays at length, in chapter 9, before she acts at all. Prayer before action, and action without flinching: she goes out to the enemy camp trusting God, and the siege breaks. Her faith is not a substitute for courage; it is its source.

Esther gives us the queen — and here in her fuller, seventeen-chapter form. The familiar ten chapters stand first, and after them, as chapters 11–17, the Greek additions this canon keeps: Mordecai's dream, which opens the added chapters and is explained at their close, the royal letters in full, and the prayers of Mordecai and Esther restored to the story. The hidden hand of God in Esther is well known; in this fuller telling, the praying that undergirds it stands in the open too.

And then a new voice opens: 1 Meqabyan, whose first eleven chapters you finished this week — Ethiopia's own book of confessors under persecution, of which much more next Sabbath. Keep each thread short this week, as the reading itself moves swiftly. The wonder is the company they keep: an old man, a traveller, a widow, a queen, and confessors, all keeping faith in somebody else's empire — and heaven, in every story, keeping faith with them.

Faithfulness has many faces — pray, give, act bravely, and trust the hand you cannot see.
