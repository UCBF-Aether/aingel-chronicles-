Sabbath Teaching · Week 37

Sirach's School of Life

Sabbath, July 3, 2027 · Readings: Sirach 1–49

Who taught you how to live — not how to believe, but how to live: how to speak, lend, work, eat, keep a friend, and hold your temper? This week you entered a classroom as wide as life itself. Sirach, in the forty-nine chapters you have read so far, is a teacher gathering a lifetime of counsel for his students and his community, and it begins, fittingly, by telling you where wisdom lives: "All wisdom cometh from the Lord, and is with him for ever."

But before the lessons, notice the book's own doorway, because Sirach is the rare ancient book that tells you how it reached you. Its prologue is the translator's own account. The writer's grandfather, Jesus the son of Sirach, composed the book in Hebrew, a labour of a lifetime spent in the law, the prophets, and the other books of the fathers. The grandson came into Egypt, found the work, and translated it into Greek for readers abroad — and the prologue asks those readers' pardon in advance where the rendering falls short, since words spoken in Hebrew do not keep the same force when carried into another tongue. Sit with that honesty a moment. A translator, two thousand years before this edition, confessing at his own threshold that translation is hard and asking to be read with indulgence where he has fallen short. This edition knows that posture well; it is built on the same rule: render faithfully, and say plainly where the rendering is uncertain. Sirach's grandson would have understood.

Inside, the school covers the whole of life, and nothing is too small for it. Friendship — how to win a friend and test him before you trust him. Speech — its power to build and to ruin, and the wisdom of being quick to hear and slow to answer. Money — lending, suretyship, honest gains, and the danger of loving riches. Family — honouring father and mother, training children, choosing a wife. Work, humility, prayer, almsgiving, the care of the poor: Sirach moves through the household and the marketplace like a master teacher who believes God cares about Tuesdays. Watch for how often reverence and practicality arrive in the same verse. The fear of the Lord, for Sirach, is not one subject among many. It is the root the whole tree grows from.

Then, near the week's end, the teacher turns historian. "Let us now praise famous men, and our fathers that begat us" — and chapters 44 to 49 walk the whole story you have been reading all year, Enoch and Noah, Abraham and Moses, Aaron and David and Solomon, the prophets one by one, as a gallery of examples. You have met them all. This week Sirach teaches you to remember them on purpose.

Learn wisdom for Tuesdays — speech, money, friendship, family — rooted in the fear of the Lord.
