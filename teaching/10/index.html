Sabbath Teaching · Week 10

Kings Answerable

Sabbath, December 26, 2026 · Readings: 1 Samuel 12–31; 2 Samuel 1–9

What is a king for, if he is not answerable to anyone above him? This week Saul falls and David rises, and the Bible tells both stories without flinching. Saul begins with every gift a people could ask for, and loses the kingdom not in one great crime but in a pattern of small, anxious disobediences. In chapter 13, pressed by a scattering army, he offers the sacrifice himself rather than wait for Samuel. In chapter 15, sent against Amalek, he spares what God told him to destroy and brings back the best of the spoil, calling it worship.

Samuel's answer gives this week its cutting edge: “Hath the LORD as great delight in burnt offerings and sacrifices, as in obeying the voice of the LORD? Behold, to obey is better than sacrifice, and to hearken than the fat of rams.” Saul confesses with his mouth and keeps excusing himself in the same breath. The kingdom is torn from him, Samuel mourns for him, and the rest of 1 Samuel watches a rejected king hunt the very man who will succeed him — growing harder, lonelier, and more afraid until he dies on Mount Gilboa, far from home, with his sons beside him.

Against Saul stands David, and watch how David rises: not by seizing, but by waiting and refusing. As a boy in chapter 17 he faces Goliath with a sling and a conviction — “the battle is the LORD's” — trusting that God, not weaponry, decides. As a fugitive he twice has Saul in his power, in a cave and in a sleeping camp, and twice refuses to lift his hand against the Lord's anointed. The crown will come to David in God's time, or not at all. He will not steal what God has promised to give.

When David finally reigns in 2 Samuel, brings up the ark to Jerusalem, and plans to build God a house, the answer he receives reverses everything: God will build David a house instead. “Thine house and thy kingdom shall be established for ever before thee: thy throne shall be established for ever.” A promise that large could make a king careless — unless he has learned Saul's lesson first.

That is the thread to hold this week. In Israel, a king is not above the word that made him king. Saul was answerable, and fell by refusing to be. David, at his best, was answerable too — and the difference between them is not talent, but hearkening.

Obey is better than sacrifice — accept being answerable, before power teaches you the harder way.
