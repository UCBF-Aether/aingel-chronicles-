Sabbath Teaching · Week 20

The Return Begins

Sabbath, March 6, 2027 · Readings: 1 Enoch 85–108; Ezra 1–10; Nehemiah 1–3

What does a beginning sound like, when it comes after long ruin? This week it sounds like two noises at once. In Ezra 3 the returned exiles rebuild the altar, keep the feast, and lay the foundation of the house of God — and when the foundation is laid, the priests sing, the people shout with a great shout, and the old men who had seen the first house weep with a loud voice. “So that the people could not discern the noise of the shout of joy from the noise of the weeping of the people: for the people shouted with a loud shout, and the noise was heard afar off” (Ezra 3:13). Joy and grief in the same sound, and the text does not ask you to separate them. That is what a return begins like.

Cyrus's decree — the very decree you heard at the close of Chronicles, weeks ago — opens Ezra's book and sets the whole return in motion: the king stirs, the vessels of God's house are brought out and counted, the families are named, and a people goes up. Later in the book Ezra himself comes, a scribe skilled in the law, and leads the community through confession and hard renewal. But the foundation scene is the week's heart: before there are walls, before there is a finished house, there is an altar, a feast kept, and a foundation praised by people who knew exactly how much had been lost.

Beside it, this week, stands another ending. The closing chapters of 1 Enoch — the dream-visions of chapters 85–90, retelling the whole history of the world in the figures of beasts and shepherds, and then the seer's final words — give Enoch his farewell: a blessing and a charge left for his son Methuselah “and for those who will come after him,” with the promise that those who have done good shall wait for the days when an end is made of those who work evil. The old seer's book closes looking forward. The exiles' book opens looking home. Read together, in one week, they rhyme: God's people have always lived by a word about what comes next.

And then Nehemiah hears. A cupbearer in a foreign palace asks after Jerusalem, and the report breaks him: “the wall of Jerusalem also is broken down, and the gates thereof are burned with fire.” He sits down and weeps, fasts and prays — and then goes to the king, and goes to the city, and rides around the broken walls by night. The return is begun. The rebuilding is next.

Shout and weep together if you must — a foundation laid is still a beginning.
