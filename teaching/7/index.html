Sabbath Teaching · Week 7

The Torch Passes

Sabbath, December 5, 2026 · Readings: Deuteronomy 9–34; Joshua 1–5

How does a people go on when the leader who carried it for forty years is gone? This week you read to the end of Deuteronomy and the opening of Joshua — the saddest and bravest handover in the Bible so far. Moses climbs Mount Nebo, sees the land stretched out before him — Gilead, Dan, the sea, the south, the plain of Jericho — and dies there, in sight of a promise he will not enter. “So Moses the servant of the LORD died there in the land of Moab, according to the word of the LORD.” No man knows his grave to this day.

Before that climb, Moses spends his last strength blessing and warning. In chapter 32 he teaches Israel a song — a witness they are to carry in their mouths when he can no longer speak to them, rehearsing God's faithfulness and their own forgetfulness in one long, searching poem. He blesses the tribes one by one. He lays his hands on Joshua. The torch is not dropped in a panic at Moses' death. It is passed, deliberately, while Moses still lives.

And Joshua receives it with a charge repeated until it rings: “Be strong and of a good courage.” God says it to him in Joshua 1, Moses has already said it, the people will say it back to him. Courage here is not a feeling Joshua must manufacture. It rests on a promise spoken in the same breath: “for the LORD thy God is with thee whithersoever thou goest.” The book of the law is not to depart from his mouth; he is to meditate in it day and night. Strength, in this telling, grows out of staying close to the words Moses leaves behind.

The opening chapters of Joshua then move quietly and deliberately. Spies go out, the Jordan is cut off and crossed on dry ground, twelve stones are taken up from the riverbed for a memorial, a new generation is circumcised at Gilgal — and there, in chapter 5, Israel keeps the Passover in the land for the first time, eats of its old corn, and the manna ceases. The wilderness food stops the morning after the land's food is tasted.

Watch for how unhurried this passing of the torch is. A song, a blessing, a charge, a river, a feast. God does not rush a grieving people past Moses' grave. He gives them words, stones, and a table — and then, step by step, he leads them in.

Take up what faithful hands pass to you, and be strong — the same presence goes with you.
