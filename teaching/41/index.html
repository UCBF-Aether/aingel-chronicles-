Sabbath Teaching · Week 41

The King and His Kingdom

Sabbath, July 31, 2027 · Readings: Matthew 4–28

What kind of king announces his kingdom by blessing the poor in spirit? This week you read Matthew whole — from the temptation in the wilderness to the empty tomb — and Matthew's answer never changes: this King rules by teaching, by mercy, and at last by a cross his enemies meant as an ending.

The Gospel opens its public story at the Jordan, then in the wilderness, where Jesus is tempted and answers every offer with Scripture. Then comes the mountain. The Sermon on the Mount, chapters 5–7, is the kingdom's charter: "Blessed are the poor in spirit: for theirs is the kingdom of heaven." Blessed, too, the mourning, the meek, the merciful, the peacemakers. Here the King teaches his people to be salt and light, to pray in secret, to forgive as they have been forgiven, and to build on rock rather than sand. Watch for how practical it all is. The kingdom is not first a place on a map; it is a people being remade from the inside.

From the mountain Matthew moves to the road: lepers cleansed, storms stilled, the dead raised, disciples sent out. Then the parables of chapter 13 — the sower and the soils, the mustard seed, the treasure hidden in a field, the pearl of great price. Each one asks the same quiet question: what is this kingdom worth to you? Matthew gathers Jesus's teaching into five great blocks, like a teacher who wants the lessons remembered in order, and between them the story keeps narrowing toward Jerusalem. The crowds cheer a king riding on a donkey; the leaders plot; the King washes no feet in this Gospel, but he does something just as startling — he shares a final Passover and speaks of his blood shed for many.

The cross in Matthew is loud with signs: darkness at noon, the veil of the temple torn, the earth shaking, tombs opening. It looks like the kingdom collapsing. Then comes the third day, the stone rolled away, the angel's word that he is not here, for he is risen, and the women running with fear and great joy. The King death could not hold meets his disciples on a mountain in Galilee and sends them outward: "Go ye therefore, and teach all nations, baptizing them in the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Ghost."

Hold the shape of it this week: announced on a mountain, taught on the roads, crucified outside the city, vindicated at an empty tomb, and still sending. Matthew's last word is a promise to carry with you — lo, I am with you alway, even unto the end of the world.

The kingdom begins wherever this King's words are heard — and obeyed.
