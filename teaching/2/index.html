Sabbath Teaching · Week 2

The Long Road of Joseph

Sabbath, October 31, 2026 · Readings: Genesis 32–50; Exodus 1–10

What do you do when the road between a promise and its fulfilment runs through a pit and a prison? This week began with Jacob, alone at night, wrestling until morning and coming away limping, with a new name: Israel. It ends with that same family — seventy souls, then a multitude — groaning under a new Pharaoh in Egypt who “knew not Joseph.” Between those two nights lies the long road of Joseph, and it is the road this week's reading asks you to walk slowly.

Joseph is seventeen when his brothers see his coat and his dreams and hate him for both. He is thrown into a pit, sold, carried to Egypt, falsely accused, and forgotten in prison by a man he helped. Scripture does not hurry past those years, and it does not explain them while they are happening. What it repeats, quietly, is a simpler sentence: the Lord was with Joseph. In Potiphar's house, in prison, and at last in Pharaoh's court, that presence — not Joseph's cleverness alone — is what the story keeps pointing to.

When famine finally brings his brothers down to Egypt, Joseph holds their lives in his hand. He could repay the pit with a prison. Instead, after testing them and weeping over them, he makes himself known, and at the end of Genesis he says the sentence the whole road was travelling toward: “But as for you, ye thought evil against me; but God meant it unto good, to bring to pass, as it is this day, to save much people alive.” Forgiveness here is not forgetting. Joseph names the evil plainly — ye thought evil — and still refuses to let it have the last word.

Hold that sentence beside the opening of Exodus. A Pharaoh rises who treats Joseph's family as a threat, sets taskmasters over them, and orders their sons destroyed. It looks, for a long moment, as if the good God meant has been buried in Egypt. Yet already a child has been hidden in a basket, drawn out of the water, and called to a burning bush. The long road does not end in the pit, and it does not end in slavery either.

If your own road feels longer than the promise, this week is for you. Jacob limped away blessed. Joseph forgave from the far side of years he would never get back. Watch for how God works in this story: rarely by shortening the road, often by staying on it with his people until the road itself becomes the place where much people are saved alive.

Walk the long road without letting the pit name you — God can mean good even there.
