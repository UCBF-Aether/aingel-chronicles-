Sabbath Teaching · Week 1

The Seventh Day

Sabbath, October 24, 2026 · Readings: Genesis 1–31

Why does the story of everything begin with a day when nothing was made? This week you read Genesis 1–31: light spoken into darkness, land and sea set in their places, living things filling the earth, and at last man and woman made in God's image. Then, before any work is given to them, comes the seventh day. “And on the seventh day God ended his work which he had made; and he rested on the seventh day from all his work which he had made. And God blessed the seventh day, and sanctified it.”

Notice that the rest comes first in the Bible, before the fall, before any law, before Israel exists. Rest is not a reward handed out after the story goes wrong. It belongs to the world as God first made it, very good. God did not rest because he was tired. He blessed the day and made it holy — set it apart — so that his creatures would learn, every week, that the world is held together by his gift, not by our striving.

That lesson matters because the rest of this week's reading shows how quickly we forget it. In Eden, the man and woman reach for what was not given, and rest gives way to hiding, blame, and toil. Outside the garden the story widens: Cain kills Abel, wickedness fills the earth, and the flood washes the world back to something like a new beginning with Noah. Even then, people at Babel try to make a name for themselves, as if heaven could be reached by building harder.

Against that striving, God begins again — not with a crowd, but with one man. Abraham is called to leave his country and trust a promise he cannot make happen himself: a land, a family, a blessing for all families of the earth. He believes, he waits, he stumbles, he waits again. A son comes in old age. The promise passes, slowly, to Isaac and then to Jacob. Nothing in these chapters moves at the speed of human impatience.

So the seventh day stands over the whole sweep like a doorway you pass through at the very start. Creation, fall, flood, and call — all of it rests, whether we acknowledge it or not, on a God who finishes his work and then invites us to stop and receive it. That is why this teaching gathers on Saturday, the seventh day itself. Each Sabbath, we are not escaping the week's reading. We are returning to its first gift.

Watch for the pattern as you go on reading this year: God gives, people grasp, God gives again. The rest was there before the grasping began, and it will still be there when you finish the next chapter.

Rest first, because God finished first — receive this seventh day as the gift it was made to be.
