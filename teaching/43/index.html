Sabbath Teaching · Week 43

The Word Became Flesh

Sabbath, August 14, 2027 · Readings: Luke 8–24; John 1–6

What if the story does not begin at the manger, or even at Abraham — what if it begins before time itself? This week you reach John's first chapter, the deepest doorway in the Gospels: "In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God." You have been preparing to hear that sentence all year, whether you knew it or not.

Think back over the road behind you. In Enoch you watched a seer carried through heavens of thrones and glory, and you met the Son of Man set over judgment and mercy. In the Wisdom of Solomon you met Wisdom pictured radiant and pure, with God from the beginning, guiding honest lives. Those books trained the ear of this canon's readers. Now John speaks, and the preparation pays off — but stay with John's own words, because his claim goes further than any picture before it: "And the Word was made flesh, and dwelt among us, (and we beheld his glory, the glory as of the only begotten of the Father,) full of grace and truth." Not a vision of glory from a distance. Glory with a face, eating, weeping, touchable.

John's first six chapters unfold that claim in signs and conversations. Water becomes wine at a wedding in Cana. Nicodemus comes by night and hears of being born again. A Samaritan woman at a well — an outsider twice over — hears living water promised and becomes the first herald of her town. A man sick for thirty-eight years rises and walks. Then, in chapter 6, Jesus feeds a multitude in the wilderness and calls himself the bread of life, and many who followed for the loaves turn back. Watch for the pattern: each sign is given, and then Jesus explains himself in long, searching speech. John wants you not only to see what Jesus did, but to sit and listen to who he says he is.

This week Luke also closes, and his ending belongs beside John's beginning. Luke 23–24 gives you the cross in this Gospel's tender register — a word of forgiveness, a promise to a dying thief, a loud cry and a yielded spirit. Then, on the road to Emmaus, the risen Jesus walks unrecognized beside two grieving disciples, "and beginning at Moses and all the prophets, he expounded unto them in all the scriptures the things concerning himself." Their hearts burn; the bread is broken; their eyes open. Then the ascension, and disciples returning to Jerusalem with great joy.

Hold the two endings and the one beginning together. Luke says: the whole Scripture was always pointing here. John says: the One it pointed to was there before the Scripture's first page. Next week, John's Gospel finishes — and a new book begins.

The Word who made all things has come near enough to be heard, seen, and touched.
