Day 6: Pray in Secret

Day 1: Follow and Obey · Day 2: Love Your Enemies · Day 3: Forgive · Day 4: Serve · Day 5: Give · Day 6 · Day 7: Rest

Prayer is not a performance. Jesus sends His followers behind a shut door, to a Father who sees in secret. One prayer today with no audience at all is worth more than a week of prayers said to be heard.

The Word

Matthew 6:6 — Standard text:

6 But thou, when thou prayest, enter into thy closet, and when thou hast shut thy door, pray to thy Father which is in secret; and thy Father which seeth in secret shall reward thee openly.

The Teaching

The whole passage is about audience. “Take heed that ye do not your alms before men, to be seen of them.” Alms given to be seen have their reward already — the seeing was the reward. Then prayer: “when thou prayest, thou shalt not be as the hypocrites are: for they love to pray standing in the synagogues and in the corners of the streets, that they may be seen of men.” And then the turn: “But thou, when thou prayest, enter into thy closet, and when thou hast shut thy door, pray to thy Father which is in secret.”

The Study Notes are careful about what exactly is condemned here, and the care matters: it is not simple publicity that is condemned — prayer may be offered openly if not prompted by ostentation — but the retiring character of true prayer is what is taught. The question is never “did anyone see?” It is “who was it for?” A prayer performed for an audience has already been paid in full, in applause. A prayer prayed in secret is still owed its hearing — by the Father who sees in secret, and rewards openly.

And the next verses remove the other false motive: “when ye pray, use not vain repetitions, as the heathen do: for they think that they shall be heard for their much speaking. Be not ye therefore like unto them: for your Father knoweth what things ye have need of, before ye ask him.” Secret prayer is not informing God of something He missed. It is being with the Father who already knows — which is why it can be brief, plain, and unadorned, and still be complete.

Thomas à Kempis gives the physical form of it: “enter into thy chamber and shut out the tumults of the world.” And Brother Lawrence gives the all-day form of it: “I made this my business, as much all the day long as at the appointed times of prayer.” The closet is where the practice starts; the presence is where it spreads. Lawrence is also the honest witness about difficulty: “I found no small pain in this exercise, and yet I continued it, notwithstanding all the difficulties that occurred, without troubling or disquieting myself when my mind had wandered involuntarily.” A wandering mind is not a failed prayer. It is the ordinary condition of prayer, and the return — without scolding yourself — is the prayer itself.

How It Looks

What follows is not a set of steps to complete. It shows the manner of the teaching — what it looks like in an ordinary day, what it looks like when it gets hard, and what it looks like when you fail at it and begin again — so you can carry a way of acting into whatever your own day brings. The particulars stay open to you. This is guidance drawn from the sources named below, not Scripture.

The closet, lived, is any shut door: a quiet corner, a closed door, a parked car, a walk alone. The manner of it is brevity and plainness — one thing, prayed in plain words, with no length requirement and no beautiful phrasing, because the Father knows what you need before you ask. You are there to be with Him, not to impress Him, and that changes the whole posture of the prayer. It stops being a performance with an audience of one admiring yourself, and becomes a conversation.

When the mind wanders — and it does, within a minute — Lawrence’s rule is the whole method: return without troubling or disquieting yourself. No scolding, no quitting, no grading the prayer. The return is the exercise. And when a prayer today was prayed to be seen — mentioned, posted, performed — the repair is one more prayer in secret, told to no one. The secret one is the real one, and it repairs the other without any announcement.

The Day’s Rhythm

The practices on this page are guidance drawn from the sources named below, not Scripture. Only the words under “The Word” are Scripture, quoted word for word from this site’s Standard text.

Morning: one word to carry

Find your closet: a quiet corner, a closed door, a parked car, a walk alone. Decide when you will go there.

Midday: one act to live

Go there and pray briefly, for one thing only, with no audience and no performance. The Father who sees in secret is enough.

Night: one honest look back

Did you pray to be seen today, or to be heard by the Father? Secret prayer is where the public life is built.
