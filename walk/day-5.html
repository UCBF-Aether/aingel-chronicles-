Day 5: Give

Day 1: Follow and Obey · Day 2: Love Your Enemies · Day 3: Forgive · Day 4: Serve · Day 5 · Day 6: Pray in Secret · Day 7: Rest

Give, and it shall be given unto you — good measure, pressed down, shaken together, running over. Giving is not a transaction; it is a way of trusting that the Father’s storehouse does not run dry. Today, give one thing and let it go.

The Word

Luke 6:38 — Standard text:

38 Give, and it shall be given unto you; good measure, pressed down, and shaken together, and running over, shall men give into your bosom. For with the same measure that ye mete withal it shall be measured to you again.

The Teaching

The image is a merchant’s, and Jesus chooses it on purpose. Grain in a market basket: good measure, then pressed down with the hand, then shaken together so it settles and holds more, then heaped until it runs over the rim — and that overflowing basket is what men give into your bosom. Your lap becomes the basket. The measure you use becomes the measure used on you: “For with the same measure that ye mete withal it shall be measured to you again.”

The passage around it keeps the same current running. “Give to every man that asketh of thee”; “as ye would that men should do to you, do ye also to them likewise”; “love ye your enemies, and do good, and lend, hoping for nothing again.” Giving sits in a chain with mercy, forgiveness, and not judging — one life, not four separate virtues. The Study Notes on this verse record Henry’s caution, and it matters: Providence does what should encourage us in doing good, but the full returns are made in another world, not this one. That sentence kills the one false motive for giving. If you give in order to be repaid, you have changed the act into a loan.

So why give at all? Because giving is how a person stops believing the lie that everything depends on keeping. The closed hand says: what I hold is what I have. The open hand says: what I give is what I trust. À Kempis puts the fruit of it simply: “He doth much who loveth much. He doth much who doth well.” And the charity passage again: “Without charity no work profiteth, but whatsoever is done in charity, however small and of no reputation it be, bringeth forth good fruit.” The size of the gift is not the measure. The charity in it is.

One more thing the verse quietly teaches: giving is not only money. Time, food, attention, a seat, a skill, an hour nobody will repay — all of it is the same act when it is given freely. The basket is your bosom, your life; whatever fills it is what you have been willing to let pass through your hands.

How It Looks

What follows is not a set of steps to complete. It shows the manner of the teaching — what it looks like in an ordinary day, what it looks like when it gets hard, and what it looks like when you fail at it and begin again — so you can carry a way of acting into whatever your own day brings. The particulars stay open to you. This is guidance drawn from the sources named below, not Scripture.

A giving day often starts with a decision made before the day spends you — one thing named in the morning: money, food, an hour, full attention to someone who rarely gets it. Left unnamed, the day usually decides for you, and decides nothing. The gift, given with no announcement and no receipt, is simply let go; if thanks come, they are received simply, not collected. That is the open hand, and it feels different from the closed one the moment you try it.

Score-keeping is the thing to watch for, because it arrives quietly: the thought that says, I gave, now they owe. That is the old measure creeping back, and noticing it is the practice, not a reason for shame. And when a gift comes out grudgingly, or is regretted the moment it leaves your hand, the way back is one more small thing given freely before the day ends — a compliment you mean, a minute of real listening. Generosity is a muscle: it strengthens by use, and a grudging gift is still a beginning, not a verdict.

The Day’s Rhythm

The practices on this page are guidance drawn from the sources named below, not Scripture. Only the words under “The Word” are Scripture, quoted word for word from this site’s Standard text.

Morning: one word to carry

Decide one thing to give today: time, food, money, attention. Pick it now, before the day spends you.

Midday: one act to live

Give it, and keep it quiet. No announcement, no receipt, no story about it later.

Night: one honest look back

Did you give freely, or keep score? Generosity that counts its cost is still a beginning — give again tomorrow.
