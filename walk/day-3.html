Day 3: Forgive

Day 1: Follow and Obey · Day 2: Love Your Enemies · Day 3 · Day 4: Serve · Day 5: Give · Day 6: Pray in Secret · Day 7: Rest

Forgiveness is not pretending nothing happened. It is refusing to carry the debt any longer. Jesus ties the two directions together: forgive, and ye shall be forgiven. Today, one debt goes down.

The Word

Luke 6:37 — Standard text:

37 Judge not, and ye shall not be judged: condemn not, and ye shall not be condemned: forgive, and ye shall be forgiven:

The Teaching

The verse is built as three pairs, and the pairing is the teaching. Judge not, and ye shall not be judged. Condemn not, and ye shall not be condemned. Forgive, and ye shall be forgiven. In each pair, the thing you hand out is the thing that comes back to you. Jesus is not describing a bargain here; He is describing a world that runs on measure — the very next verse says so outright: “with the same measure that ye mete withal it shall be measured to you again.” The measure you use on other people becomes the measure used on you.

The Study Notes on this verse record Henry’s counsel: take care when blaming others, because we need allowance ourselves, and a giving, forgiving spirit reaps its own benefit. That is the sober version of the same point: the person doing the judging is never the person without fault. Thomas à Kempis says it sharper: “Look well unto thyself, and beware that thou judge not the doings of others. In judging others a man laboureth in vain; he often erreth, and easily falleth into sin; but in judging and examining himself he always laboureth to good purpose.” Judging others is wasted labour; examining yourself is labour that pays.

What forgiveness is, in this verse, needs saying plainly, because the word gets stretched in both directions. Forgiveness here is releasing the debt — the claim that someone owes you repayment, apology, or suffering before you can be free. It is not pretending nothing happened, and it is not a command to trust again or to call evil good. The verse says forgive, not forget, not approve, not return. The debt goes down. That is the whole act, and it is enough.

And notice the direction of the verse: it speaks to the forgiver, not the offender. Nothing here waits on the other person’s repentance, apology, or even knowledge. The release happens on your side, in your hands, today. That is why it belongs in a week about becoming like Jesus rather than feeling like Him: forgiveness is something you do before you feel it, and the feeling often follows the release rather than causing it.

How It Looks

What follows is not a set of steps to complete. It shows the manner of the teaching — what it looks like in an ordinary day, what it looks like when it gets hard, and what it looks like when you fail at it and begin again — so you can carry a way of acting into whatever your own day brings. The particulars stay open to you. This is guidance drawn from the sources named below, not Scripture.

Forgiveness, lived, is quieter than people expect. It looks like a person naming the debt inwardly — the apology never given, the money, the years — and then releasing it, by name, in prayer, without waiting for the other person to repent, apologise, or even know. Nothing dramatic happens on the outside. What changes is that the claim is no longer being carried. The debt stops being the thing you check every morning to see if it has been paid yet.

When the memory returns, as it will, the release is repeated, briefly, and the day goes on. That repetition is not failure; it is how a release becomes a settled state rather than a single feeling. And prudence keeps its own place beside it: releasing a debt does not mean walking back into harm’s way, any more than forgiving a debt means calling it good. The verse commands the release. It does not forbid wisdom. If the grievance gets rehearsed for the tenth time, à Kempis points the examination inward first: look to your own ledger before re-opening theirs.

The Day’s Rhythm

The practices on this page are guidance drawn from the sources named below, not Scripture. Only the words under “The Word” are Scripture, quoted word for word from this site’s Standard text.

Morning: one word to carry

Name one debt you are holding against someone — something they owe you that they may never repay.

Midday: one act to live

Release it in prayer. Say the person’s name, and let the debt go. You do not need their permission to forgive.

Night: one honest look back

Is there anything you still need to forgive? Is there anything you need to ask forgiveness for? Both doors open from the inside.
