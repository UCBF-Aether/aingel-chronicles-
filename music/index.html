The Music Room

Everything in this room is old, real, and free. These are complete recordings — nothing trimmed, nothing looped — kept here with the names of the people who made and shared them, because a gift should carry its giver’s name.

Press play, and read with music in the room. One practical word: open this page in its own tab, and the music will keep playing while you read elsewhere in the library.

The Oldest Song in the World

The Epitaph of Seikilos

The oldest complete song in the world that survives with its music — a Greek song carved on a tombstone near Tralles some nineteen centuries ago, set there, its inscription says, as “a long-lasting sign of deathless remembrance.” It is sung here in its own Koine Greek.

Sung and uploaded by Marcus Harvey · Wikimedia Commons · CC BY-SA 2.5 — shared here under the same license.

Veni Sancte Spiritus

The great sequence of Pentecost — “Come, Holy Spirit” — sung in Gregorian chant by a single voice.

Rorate Caeli

The introit of Advent: “Drop down, ye heavens, from above” — the church’s cry for the coming of Christ, sung in Gregorian chant.

Samaii Hijaz Kar Kurdi

A traditional Lebanese sama‘i in the Hijaz mode, played on the oud by Chahadé Saadé — an Odeon 78 rpm pressing from about 1926, with the gentle surface sound of shellac in its voice.

Bells of the World

And the room has bells. Three towers whose ringers gave their ringing to the world freely — each kept with the church it hangs in, the face of its tower, and a little of its story.

Evangelische Kirche · Mülheim an der Mosel, Germany

The Bells of Mülheim

The Protestant church of Mülheim, a wine village on the Moselle in Rhineland-Palatinate, is a baroque church built in 1669–1675 around an older Romanesque tower, in one of the oldest Protestant parishes of the Rhineland. You hear its second and third bells. And these are this site’s own Sabbath bells: the first three strikes of this very recording are the peal that rings the weekly teaching in — its ringer, Helge Klaus Rieder, also took the photograph.

Bells recorded by Helge Klaus Rieder · Wikimedia Commons · CC BY 3.0
Photograph by Helge Klaus Rieder · Wikimedia Commons · CC BY-SA 3.0 — shared here under the same license.

Pfarrkirche St. Jodok · Schruns, Austria

The Bells of St. Jodok

The parish church of St. Jodok — the Schrunser Münster — stands in the centre of Schruns, a market town in Austria’s Montafon valley, 690 metres up in the Vorarlberg Alps. A chapel of St. Jodok here is first named in a register of 1433, and the church is still the town’s heart, a protected monument. Its bells ring out over the town, recorded by Helge Klaus Rieder and given to everyone, freely, under CC0.

Bells recorded by Helge Klaus Rieder · Wikimedia Commons · CC0 — given to the public domain
Photograph by Schlaier · Wikimedia Commons · CC BY-SA 3.0 — shared here under the same license.

Benediktinerabtei St. Matthias · Trier, Germany

The Bells of St. Matthias

The abbey church of St. Matthias in Trier is the Romanesque basilica of a Benedictine abbey whose monks have lived on this ground since late antiquity. Since the twelfth century it has guarded the tomb of the Apostle Matthias — the only apostle’s grave in Germany, and north of the Alps — and pilgrims still seek it out. Here its bells ring on for three and a half minutes, recorded by Helge Klaus Rieder and given freely under CC0.
