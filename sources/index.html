How This Edition Was Made

This book contains the Bible of the Ethiopian Orthodox Tewahedo Church: eighty-one books, the largest canon in Christendom. Where most traditions settled on sixty-six books, the Ethiopian Church kept also 1 Enoch and Jubilees, the three books of Meqabyan, the Sinodos and the Didascalia, the Books of the Covenant, the Qalementos, the history called Josippon, and more besides. This edition follows the eighty-one-book enumeration of the Ethiopian Orthodox Bible Project. Because the Church's count groups some paired works that are easier to read apart — Samuel, Kings, and Chronicles are two books each, for example — the eighty-one books appear here as eighty-eight reading units, followed by a bonus appendix of two classics of Ethiopian Christian literature that are not part of the canon and are marked as such.

No single English Bible contains all of this, so the edition was built from three kinds of sources. First, where a sound English text already exists in the public domain, it is printed as received: the King James Version for the books of the common canon, and the classic scholarly translations where they exist — R. H. Charles for 1 Enoch and Jubilees, J. M. Harden for the Didascalia, Cooper and Maclean for the Books of the Covenant, E. A. Wallis Budge for the Kebra Nagast, and Charles Thomson's Septuagint for the 151st Psalm. Second, where the Ethiopian canon includes passages that stand in the King James Apocrypha — the Prayer of Azariah and the Song of the Three Children within Daniel, Susanna and Bel and the Dragon as Daniel's final chapters, and the Greek additions to Esther — they were restored from that text. Third, where no usable English existed, the book was translated fresh, directly from the Ge'ez, the ancient liturgical tongue of Ethiopia: the three books of Meqabyan, the Qalementos, the Sinodos, the Gessew, 4 Baruch, the Teaching of the Mysteries, and Josippon.

Every text, however it arrived, passed through the same pipeline. A transcription was verified against its source — a printed edition checked page by page, or, for Josippon, a manuscript read from photographs folio by folio. Translations were then audited independently: a second reader compared the English against the Ge'ez and reported every divergence, and the reports were worked through one by one. Where a received text proved damaged — as the Jubilees text was, its scan scarred by machine-reading errors — it was repaired against a named edition, word by word, with every repair logged; the repair reports are part of the project's permanent record. Nothing was smoothed over silently.

One rule governed all of it: nothing is invented. Where a word could not be read with confidence, the working text marks it; where a passage is physically gone — a torn leaf, a destroyed foot of page — the loss is recorded, not bridged. In the files from which this book was set, those marks stand by the thousand, each one a place where we refused to guess. The printed reader's edition reads clean, as a Bible should; its translator's note says only what can honestly be said: We did our best with every word. What you see is what the manuscript gives.

Josippon deserves its story told straight. The text first set for this book under that name was found, on verification, not to be a translation at all: it was a patchwork of keyword fragments and working notes that had been mistaken for the book. It was withdrawn entirely, and the Josippon printed here was rebuilt from the beginning — a fresh transcription of the manuscript BL EAP704/2/3, a Ge'ez codex copied around 1600 carrying the church's Christian redaction of the work, verified chapter by chapter; a new translation of all 104 chapters; and independent audits of the whole. Three passages the manuscript itself has lost — chapter 7, verses 12–14; chapter 84, verses 21–35; chapter 104, verses 14–19 — were hunted through every other witness we could reach, from Strasbourg to the British Library to the printed catalogues of five thousand Ethiopian manuscripts, and were not found. They stand in the working text as honest blanks. What the parchment gives, we give; what it does not, we do not invent.

The Sounds in the Music Room

The Music Room keeps a small shelf of historical recordings beside the books — chant sung by single voices, an oud pressed onto shellac in 1926, a song carved on a Greek tombstone. Each is public domain or freely licensed, is played complete and unaltered apart from conversion to a web format, and is shelved with its performer and license named on its card in the Music Room. The rule is the edition’s rule: what we did not make, we credit; what we cannot verify, we do not claim.

The Art in the Gallery

The Gallery hangs historic sacred art beside the books — Ethiopian Gospel illuminations, the earliest church painting, illuminated Psalters, and the great Bible engravers. Every piece is public domain or CC0, fetched from its holding institution or its Wikimedia Commons file page, and hangs in the Gallery with its brief history and its keeper named. The rule is the edition’s rule again: what we did not make, we credit; what we cannot verify, we do not hang.

Sinodos

Read it: Standard Edition · Modern American Edition

Source text / edition: Ge'ez text of G. Horner, The Statutes of the Apostles; or, Canones Ecclesiastici (London: Williams & Norgate, 1904), public domain

How the English was made: Translated anew from Ge'ez

Notes: 37 chapters. The project's corpus held no Ge'ez for this book, so Horner's printed Ge'ez was ingested (machine-read, then proofread) and translated fresh; Horner's own English was used only as a sense-check on corrupt passages. The chapter division follows the project's documented mapping of Horner's statutes (translations/sinodos/CHAPTER_GUIDE.md; ingestion record translations/sinodos/INGESTION.md).

Manuscripts — Josippon (Zena Ayhud) and the search for its lost passages

EAP704/2/3

Repository / shelfmark: British Library, Endangered Archives Programme — Ge'ez codex of the Zena Ayhud, Christian redaction, copied 1595–1605

Base witness. Verified transcription of all 104 chapters made from its photographs; the English translation was made from that transcription.

Éthiop. 5 (= Ms. 4366; Kamil's siglum S)

Repository / shelfmark: Bibliothèque nationale et universitaire, Strasbourg; digitized on Gallica (ark:/12148/btv1b101086039)

Collation witness. Read against obscured passages of the base (supplied readings marked [···] in the working files); swept codex-wide for the two losses — does not carry the discourse appendix: it transmits the chronicle plus an appended computus treatise, neither the Sawiros doxology (ch. 84) nor the Marian ending (ch. 104).

Manuscripts — the Treatise of Sawiros of Asmunayn (the discourse lost at Josippon ch. 84)

Or. 691

Repository / shelfmark: British Library — 13th-century Ḥǝdār homiliary; the treatise is item 8 (ff. 106r–155v)

Read in full at §4's close (f. 155v): the treatise's own ending is a doxology truncated mid-word, and it lacks the base redaction's own Marian bridge (Josippon ch. 84 vv. 15–20). Under the edition's fidelity rule it could not be merged into the text; the loss stands. Report: translations/josippon/SAWIROS_READ.md.

Éthiopien d'Abbadie 92

Repository / shelfmark: Bibliothèque nationale de France, Paris — 16th c., "Book of Ḥǝdār"; the treatise at ff. 71–117v, §4 from f. 96; digitized on Gallica (ark:/12148/btv1b531387571)

Read in full at §4's close (f. 117v): the treatise's ending survives complete here — a doxology closing "unto peace. Amen.", followed by the copyist's own colophon ("upon its scribe be mercy, by the prayer of Michael") and a divider ornament. Not imported into Josippon ch. 84: Paris's close is that codex's scribe's own colophon, Or. 691 closes differently again (an authorial epilogue, itself truncated mid-word), and the base redaction's doxology followed its own Marian bridge (ch. 84 vv. 15–20), which no treatise witness carries. Paris certifies the discourse's identity and that the archetype ended complete; the redaction's own ending remains lost, and stands as found. Report: translations/josippon/SAWIROS_READ.md §§2b–2c.

Éthiopien d'Abbadie 155; EMML 1833, 1399, 3459, 788(6)

Repository / shelfmark: BnF, Paris; EMML (vHMML)

Further codices carrying the Sawiros treatise, identified from the catalogues in the passage hunt; catalogued, not read.

The project Ge'ez corpus (~/workspace/geez-study/)

Verse- and chapter-level Ge'ez texts underlying the fresh translations of 1–3 Meqabyan, Qalementos, 4 Baruch, the Teaching of the Mysteries chs 1 and 5, and Kebra Nagast ch. 119. The corpus is held under licence and is not republished; only the translations made from it appear here.
