Study Notes — Numbers

Chapter 34

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

2 COMMENTARY: Command Israel, and say to them, when you come into the land of Canaan, this is the land that shall fall to you for an inheritance, even the land of Canaan with the coasts thereof. JFB notes these details mark the general boundary of Israel's inheritance west of Jordan, which Israel never actually possessed in full, even when most extended under David and Solomon, while Henry notes Canaan as bounded here was but about one hundred and sixty miles long and fifty broad, yet this was the country promised to the father of the faithful, the vineyard of the Lord, the garden enclosed, whose narrowness was made up by the fruitfulness of its soil. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: Then your south quarter shall be from the wilderness of Zin along by the coast of Edom, and your south border shall be the outmost coast of the salt sea eastward. JFB notes the southern line is the most difficult to trace, beginning where the wilderness of Zin touches Edom's border, so that the boundary extends eastward from the Dead Sea's extremity. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: And your border shall turn from the south to the ascent of Akrabbim, and pass on to Zin, and the going forth thereof shall be from the south to Kadesh-barnea, and shall go on to Hazar-addar, and pass on to Azmon. JFB notes the line winds around the precipitous ridge of Akrabbim, meaning scorpions, thought to be the high and difficult Pass of Safeh, crossing the great valley of the Arabah that reaches from the Dead Sea to the Red Sea. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: And the border shall fetch a compass from Azmon to the river of Egypt, and the goings out of it shall be at the sea. JFB identifies the river of Egypt as the ancient brook Sihor, the Rhinocolura of the Greeks, a little south of El-Arish, where the wady gently descends toward the Mediterranean. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: And as for the western border, you shall even have the great sea for a border: this shall be your west border. JFB notes there is no uncertainty about this boundary, universally allowed to be the Mediterranean, called the great sea in comparison with the small inland seas or lakes known to the Hebrews. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: And this shall be your north border: from the great sea you shall point out for you mount Hor. JFB notes the difficulty arises from what translators called mount Hor, since the Hebrew Hor-ha-Hor properly signifies the mountain of the mountain, or the high double mountain, which from its situation can mean nothing else than mount Amana, a member of the great Lebanon range. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: From mount Hor you shall point out your border to the entrance of Hamath, and the goings forth of the border shall be to Zedad. JFB notes the entrance of Hamath is the northern plain between the mountain ranges, now the valley of Balbeck, and Zedad is identified as the present Sudud. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: And the border shall go on to Ziphron, and the goings out of it shall be at Hazar-enan: this shall be your north border. JFB notes Ziphron means sweet odour and Hazar-enan village of fountains, though the places are unknown, and the frontier meant is an imaginary line from mount Cassius on the coast along Lebanon's northern base to the entering of the Bekaa valley at Kamosa Hermel. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: And you shall point out your east border from Hazar-enan to Shepham. The eastern border began at Hazar-enan and ran to Shepham, the first leg of a clearly defined eastern line. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: And the coast shall go down from Shepham to Riblah, on the east side of Ain, and the border shall descend, and shall reach to the side of the sea of Chinnereth eastward. JFB notes Shepham and Riblah lay in the valley of Lebanon, the boundary commencing a little higher than the Jordan's sources, Ain being supposed the source of that river, whence the eastern boundary extended along the Jordan and the sea of Chinnereth. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: And the border shall go down to Jordan, and the goings out of it shall be at the salt sea: this shall be your land with the coasts thereof round about. JFB notes the line, drawn on the east of the river and the seas, included those waters within the western tribes' territory, and the border ended at the salt sea, closing the circuit of the land. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: Moses commanded Israel, saying, this is the land which you shall inherit by lot, which the Lord commanded to give to the nine tribes, and to the half tribe. The land was to be inherited by lot, as the Lord commanded, for the nine tribes and the half tribe west of Jordan. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: For the tribe of Reuben according to the house of their fathers, and the tribe of Gad according to the house of their fathers, have received their inheritance, and half the tribe of Manasseh have received their inheritance. JFB notes the conquered territories of Sihon and Og between Arnon and Hermon were allotted to them, Reuben in the most southerly part, Gad north of it, and half Manasseh in the northernmost portion. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: The two tribes and the half tribe have received their inheritance on this side Jordan near Jericho eastward, toward the sunrising. The two tribes and the half tribe already held their inheritance east of Jordan near Jericho, toward the sunrise, and were therefore excluded from the western lot. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: These are the names of the men which shall divide the land to you: Eleazar the priest, and Joshua son of Nun. JFB notes this appointment before the Jordan tended both to animate Israel's faith in the certainty of the conquest and to prevent subsequent dispute and discontent, dangerous in the natives' presence, and the dividers stood subordinate to the great military and ecclesiastical chiefs, Joshua and Eleazar. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

18 COMMENTARY: And you shall take one prince of every tribe, to divide the land by inheritance. One prince from every western tribe would divide the land, so that each tribe's inheritance was assigned by its own prince under Joshua and Eleazar. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

19 COMMENTARY: And the names of the men are these: of the tribe of Judah, Caleb son of Jephunneh. JFB notes the princes' names are mentioned in the exact order in which the tribes obtained possession of the land, and according to brotherly connection, beginning with Judah's Caleb, the faithful spy who had wholly followed the Lord. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

20 COMMENTARY: And of the tribe of Simeon, Shemuel son of Ammihud. Simeon's prince was Shemuel son of Ammihud, second in the order of division. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

21 COMMENTARY: Of the tribe of Benjamin, Elidad son of Chislon. Benjamin's prince was Elidad son of Chislon, dividing for the tribe that would hold Jerusalem's hills. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

22 COMMENTARY: And the prince of the tribe of Dan, Bukki son of Jogli. Dan's prince was Bukki son of Jogli, for the northern and southern Danite portions alike. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

23 COMMENTARY: The prince of Joseph, for the tribe of Manasseh, Hanniel son of Ephod. Joseph's western prince for Manasseh was Hanniel son of Ephod, representing the half tribe that crossed Jordan. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

24 COMMENTARY: And the prince of the tribe of Ephraim, Kemuel son of Shiphtan. Ephraim's prince was Kemuel son of Shiphtan, for the tribe of Joshua himself. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

25 COMMENTARY: And the prince of the tribe of Zebulun, Elizaphan son of Parnach. Zebulun's prince was Elizaphan son of Parnach. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

26 COMMENTARY: And the prince of the tribe of Issachar, Paltiel son of Azzan. Issachar's prince was Paltiel son of Azzan. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

27 COMMENTARY: And the prince of the tribe of Asher, Ahihud son of Shelomi. Asher's prince was Ahihud son of Shelomi, for the northern coastal tribe. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

28 COMMENTARY: And the prince of the tribe of Naphtali, Pedahel son of Ammihud. Naphtali's prince was Pedahel son of Ammihud, closing the ten princes for the nine and a half western tribes. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

29 COMMENTARY: These are they whom the Lord commanded to divide the inheritance to Israel in the land of Canaan. The ten princes, with Eleazar and Joshua, were those the Lord commanded to divide the inheritance in Canaan, so that the division was settled by divine appointment before a single lot was cast. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

No note for verse(s) 1, 16 — the public-domain sources consulted give no distinct explanation for these verses beyond the list itself, so nothing was invented for them.
