Study Notes — Numbers

Chapter 18

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: The Lord said to Aaron: you, your sons and your father's house with you shall bear the iniquity of the sanctuary, and you and your sons shall bear the iniquity of your priesthood. Henry notes Aaron would see reason not to be proud of his preferment when he considered the great care and charge upon him: be not high-minded, but fear, for the greater the trust committed, the greater the danger of betraying it. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: Your brethren of Levi, the tribe of your father, bring with you, that they may be joined to you and minister to you, while you and your sons minister before the tabernacle of witness. The Levites were joined to Aaron's house as helpers, not rivals: their ministry supported the priesthood it did not replace. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: They shall keep your charge and the charge of all the tabernacle, only they shall not come nigh the vessels of the sanctuary and the altar, that neither they nor you die. The boundary protected both sides: Levites who overstepped it endangered priests as well as themselves. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: They shall be joined to you, and keep the charge of the tabernacle of the congregation, and no stranger shall come nigh to you. The joining was permanent and exclusive: the tabernacle's charge belonged to Levi alone, and strangers were kept from it by death. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: You shall keep the charge of the sanctuary and the charge of the altar, that there be no wrath any more upon the children of Israel. The priests' charge was itself Israel's protection: faithful keeping at sanctuary and altar kept wrath from the whole people. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: I, behold, I have taken your brethren the Levites from among Israel, to you they are given as a gift for the Lord, to do the service of the tabernacle. The Levites were God's gift twice over: taken by God, then given to Aaron, so that their service was grace, not a tribal claim. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: Therefore you and your sons with you shall keep your priest's office for everything of the altar and within the veil, and you shall serve: I have given your priest's office to you as a service of gift, and the stranger that comes nigh shall be put to death. Henry calls the priest's service a warfare, and asks who goes a warfare at his own charges: the office was a gift, and its boundary was death, because God's institutions must not be invaded. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: Behold, I also have given you the charge of my heave offerings, of all the hallowed things of Israel: to you have I given them by reason of the anointing, and to your sons, by an ordinance for ever. Henry notes the provision was given by an ordinance for ever, that the lamp might not go out for want of oil, and so a gospel ministry should continue till Christ comes. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: This shall be yours of the most holy things, reserved from the fire: every oblation of theirs, every meat offering, every sin offering, and every trespass offering, which they render to me, shall be most holy for you and for your sons. The priests lived on the most holy portion itself: what was reserved from the altar fire became their food, because they served at the altar. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: In the most holy place shall you eat it; every male shall eat it: it shall be holy to you. The eating was itself holy and restricted: only males of the priestly house, and only in the most holy place, so that provision never became common use. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: And this is yours: the heave offering of their gift, with all the wave offerings of Israel, I have given to you, and to your sons and daughters with you, by a statute for ever, and everyone clean in your house shall eat of it. The heave and wave offerings, unlike the most holy things, fed the whole priestly household, sons and daughters, if clean. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: All the best of the oil, and all the best of the wine, and of the wheat, the firstfruits of them which they offer to the Lord, them have I given you. Henry stresses the best should be offered for firstfruits: those who think to save by putting God off with the refuse deceive themselves, for God is not mocked. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: Whatsoever is first ripe in the land, which they bring to the Lord, shall be yours, and everyone clean in your house shall eat of it. The first ripe fruit, like the best oil and wine, went to the priests, so that God's portion was first in time as well as in quality. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: Everything devoted in Israel shall be yours. Devoted things, given wholly to the Lord without redemption, passed to his priests, the human hands of the divine ownership. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: Everything that opens the matrix in all flesh, which they bring to the Lord, whether of men or beasts, shall be yours: nevertheless the firstborn of man you shall surely redeem, and the firstling of unclean beasts you shall redeem. The firstborn belonged to God by the exodus claim, but man and unclean beast were redeemed rather than sacrificed, while clean firstlings were offered. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: Those to be redeemed from a month old you shall redeem, according to your estimation, for five shekels, after the shekel of the sanctuary, which is twenty gerahs. The redemption price was fixed and early: a month old, five sanctuary shekels, so that no priest could bargain over the firstborn's ransom. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: But the firstling of a cow, or a sheep, or a goat, you shall not redeem: they are holy: you shall sprinkle their blood on the altar, and burn their fat for an offering made by fire, for a sweet savour to the Lord. Clean firstlings were not redeemed because they were holy in themselves, fit for altar blood and fire, not for ransom money. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: And the flesh of them shall be yours, as the wave breast and as the right shoulder are yours. After blood and fat went to the altar, the flesh fed the priests, on the same pattern as the peace offering's breast and shoulder. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: All the heave offerings of the holy things, which Israel offers to the Lord, have I given to you and your sons and daughters with you, by a statute for ever: it is a covenant of salt for ever before the Lord to you and your seed with you. The covenant of salt marks permanence: as salt preserves, so this provision was unchanging, for the priesthood and its maintenance alike. — Matthew Henry

20 COMMENTARY: You shall have no inheritance in their land, neither shall you have any part among them: I am your part and your inheritance among the children of Israel. Henry notes those who have God for their inheritance and portion for ever ought to look with holy contempt and indifference upon worldly possessions, and the priests were to be wholly devoted to ministry, not diverted by worldly care or business. — Matthew Henry

21 COMMENTARY: Behold, I have given the children of Levi all the tenth in Israel for an inheritance, for their service which they serve, even the service of the tabernacle. Henry notes Levi, the smallest tribe, yet received a tenth of the yearly profits without ploughing and sowing, a national acknowledgment of their public service, owning them as God's agents and receivers of the heave offering lifted heavenward. — Matthew Henry

22 COMMENTARY: Neither must the children of Israel henceforth come nigh the tabernacle of the congregation, lest they bear sin and die. Henry draws the sphere lesson: if ministers expect people to keep in their sphere and not intermeddle with sacred offices, ministers must keep in theirs and not entangle themselves in secular affairs. — Matthew Henry

23 COMMENTARY: But the Levites shall do the service of the tabernacle, and they shall bear their iniquity: it shall be a statute for ever throughout your generations, that among Israel they have no inheritance. The Levites bore the sanctuary's iniquity as its servants, and the statute was perpetual: service, not land, was their inheritance in every generation. — Matthew Henry

24 COMMENTARY: But the tithes of Israel, which they offer as a heave offering to the Lord, I have given to the Levites to inherit: therefore I have said to them, among Israel they shall have no inheritance. The tithe was the inheritance in place of land: God, whose the whole is, gave a tenth of all the land's produce to the tribe that owned no land. — Matthew Henry

26 COMMENTARY: Thus speak to the Levites: when you take of Israel the tithes which I have given you for your inheritance, then you shall offer up a heave offering of it for the Lord, even a tenth part of the tithe. Henry notes the order was given to the Levites to offer it cheerfully, rather than to the priests to levy it, and God's tenants paid rent: they were not exempted by office, and ministers must be charitable out of what they receive. — Matthew Henry

27 COMMENTARY: And this your heave offering shall be reckoned to you, as though it were the corn of the threshing floor, and as the fulness of the winepress. The Levites' tenth of the tithe counted as if it were their own harvest, though they had sown nothing: God reckoned their service as husbandry. — Matthew Henry

28 COMMENTARY: Thus you also shall offer a heave offering to the Lord of all your tithes which you receive from Israel, and you shall give the Lord's heave offering from it to Aaron the priest. The chain of giving ran from people to Levites to priests, each link heaving upward to the Lord, and Aaron received the priests' portion as the people's gift passed through Levi. — Matthew Henry

29 COMMENTARY: Out of all your gifts you shall offer every heave offering of the Lord, of all the best thereof, even the hallowed part thereof out of it. The best rule applied again at the second level: the Levites' heave offering, like the people's firstfruits, came from the best, not the refuse. — Matthew Henry

30 COMMENTARY: Therefore you shall say to them, when you have heaved the best thereof from it, then the rest shall be counted to the Levites as the increase of the threshing floor and as the increase of the winepress. Once the best was heaved, the rest was cleanly theirs, counted as ordinary harvest, free for household use. — Matthew Henry

31 COMMENTARY: And you shall eat it in every place, you and your households, for it is your reward for your service in the tabernacle of the congregation. Unlike the most holy things, the tithe after heaving could be eaten anywhere: it was wages for service, not sanctuary food, and the household shared the reward. — Matthew Henry

32 COMMENTARY: And you shall bear no sin by reason of it, when you have heaved from it the best of it, neither shall you pollute the holy things of Israel, lest you die. Henry gives the way to have comfort in worldly possessions without sin: be sure what we have is got honestly and in God's service, and be sure God has his dues out of it, for we have comfort in our substance when we have honoured the Lord with it. — Matthew Henry

No note for verse(s) 25 — the public-domain sources consulted give no distinct explanation for these verses beyond the list itself, so nothing was invented for them.
