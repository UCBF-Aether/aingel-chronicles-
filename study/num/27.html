Study Notes — Numbers

Chapter 27

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Then came the daughters of Zelophehad, son of Hepher, son of Gilead, son of Machir, son of Manasseh, of the families of Manasseh son of Joseph, and these are the names of his daughters: Mahlah, Noah, Hoglah, Milcah, and Tirzah. Henry notes the five daughters considered themselves left destitute, having neither father nor brother to inherit any land, yet came in believing expectation that the Lord's word would be performed in due season. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: They stood before Moses, and before Eleazar the priest, and before the princes and all the congregation, by the door of the tabernacle of the congregation. JFB notes they made their grievance known to Moses and the authorities conjoined with him in administering justice, in a case whose peculiarity would be no infrequent occurrence. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: Our father died in the wilderness, and he was not in the company of them that gathered themselves together against the Lord in the company of Korah, but died in his own sin, and had no sons. JFB explains the declaration was necessary because his death might have occurred about the time of that rebellion, and since conspirators' children were involved in the punishment, their plea was the more forcible that their father did not die for any cause dooming his family to lose lives or inheritance, while died in his own sin means by the common law of mortality to which men through sin are subject. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: Why should the name of our father be done away from among his family, because he has no son? Give to us therefore a possession among the brethren of our father. JFB notes the young women perceived only males had been registered in the census, so their household was omitted, and their case mattered because the law of inheritance, under divine authority, would be extended not only to similar cases but to others, preserving a patrimony from alienation to another tribe. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: Moses brought their cause before the Lord. Henry notes God himself gives judgment here: he takes notice of the affairs not only of nations but of private families, and orders them according to his will, and Moses, rather than deciding a case without precedent, laid it before God. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: And the Lord spoke to Moses, saying, the daughters of Zelophehad speak right: you shall surely give them a possession of an inheritance among their father's brethren, and you shall cause the inheritance of their father to pass to them. The petition was granted: Henry notes those who seek an inheritance in the land of promise shall have what they seek, and other things shall be added to them. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: And you shall speak to the children of Israel, saying, if a man die, and have no son, then you shall cause his inheritance to pass to his daughter. A single family's petition became a standing law, extending inheritance to daughters where there was no son. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: And if he have no daughter, then you shall give his inheritance to his brethren. Where no daughter survived, the inheritance passed to the dead man's brothers, the next in the ordered succession. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: And if he have no brethren, then you shall give his inheritance to his father's brethren. Failing brothers, the inheritance rose to the father's brothers, keeping the patrimony within the wider family. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: And if his father have no brethren, then you shall give his inheritance to his kinsman that is next to him of his family, and he shall possess it, and it shall be to the children of Israel a statute of judgment, as the Lord commanded Moses. The order closed with the nearest kinsman, and the whole became a statute of judgment for Israel, a legal provision for preserving patrimony in comparatively rare cases. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: The Lord said to Moses, get up into this mount Abarim, and see the land which I have given to the children of Israel. JFB notes that although Israel stood on the confines of the promised land, Moses was not privileged to cross the Jordan, but died on one of the Moabitic mountains called Abarim, while Henry adds this sight of Canaan signified his believing prospect of the better country, the heavenly. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: And when you have seen it, you also shall be gathered to your people, as Aaron your brother was gathered. Henry notes Moses must die, but death does not cut him off; it only brings him to rest with the holy patriarchs, to die as they died, having lived as they lived, with peace as their end. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: For you rebelled against my commandment in the desert of Zin, in the strife of the congregation, to sanctify me at the water before their eyes. That is the water of Meribah in Kadesh in the wilderness of Zin. JFB notes the privation of the great honour of entering the land was owing to Moses' unhappy conduct in striking the rock at Meribah, and the pious leader submitted with meek acquiescence to the divine decree. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: And Moses spoke to the Lord, saying, let the Lord, God of the spirits of all flesh, set a man over the congregation. Henry notes envious spirits do not love their successors, but Moses was not one of these, and we should concern ourselves, in prayers and endeavours, for the rising generation, that religion may be maintained when we are in our graves. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: Let the Lord, God of the spirits of all flesh, set a man over the congregation. JFB notes the request was most suitably made to God in this character, as Author of all the intellectual gifts and moral graces with which men are endowed, who can raise up qualified persons for the most arduous duties and difficult situations. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: Which may go out before them, and which may go in before them, and which may lead them out, and which may bring them in, that the congregation of the Lord be not as sheep which have no shepherd. JFB notes Moses, in genuine patriotism, prayed fervently for a worthy and competent successor, and the shepherd image defined the office: a leader who goes before the people in war and in peace alike. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

18 COMMENTARY: The Lord said to Moses, take Joshua son of Nun, a man in whom is the spirit, and lay your hand upon him. JFB notes a strong testimony is borne here to the personality of the divine Spirit, and the imposition of hands was an ancient ceremony, while Henry adds Joshua had signalized himself by courage in fighting Amalek, humility in ministering to Moses, and faith and sincerity in witnessing against the evil spies' report. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

19 COMMENTARY: And set him before Eleazar the priest, and before all the congregation, and give him a charge in their sight. The succession was made public before priest and congregation alike, so that no one could doubt whose charge Joshua carried. — Matthew Henry

20 COMMENTARY: And you shall put some of your honour upon him, that all the congregation of Israel may be obedient. JFB notes no prophet or ruler in all respects like Moses arose until the Messiah, whose glory eclipsed all, but Joshua was honoured and qualified in an eminent degree, through the special service of the high priest asking counsel for him after the judgment of Urim. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

21 COMMENTARY: And he shall stand before Eleazar the priest, who shall ask counsel for him after the judgment of Urim before the Lord: at his word shall they go out, and at his word they shall come in, both he, and all the children of Israel with him, even all the congregation. Joshua's leadership was to be guided, not autonomous: the high priest would ask counsel by Urim, and the whole congregation would move at that word. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

22 COMMENTARY: Moses did as the Lord commanded him, and he took Joshua, and set him before Eleazar the priest, and before all the congregation. Moses obeyed exactly, performing the public appointment in the order God had given it. — Matthew Henry

23 COMMENTARY: And he laid his hands upon him, and gave him a charge, as the Lord commanded by the hand of Moses. Henry closes the chapter with the type: the law was given by Moses, who by reason of transgression could not bring us to heaven, but grace and truth came by Jesus Christ, for the salvation of every believer. — Matthew Henry

No note for verse(s) 6 — the public-domain sources consulted give no distinct explanation for these verses beyond the list itself, so nothing was invented for them.
