Study Notes — Numbers

Chapter 4

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

2 COMMENTARY: The sons of Kohath were summed by families and fathers' houses for the work of the tabernacle. The census here is not for war but for service: Levi's host is counted for the warfare of worship. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: Only men from thirty years old to fifty, entering into the host to do the work, were counted for service. Henry's rule is that the service of God requires the best of our strength and the prime portion of our time, and should be done when we are most lively and active, not put off to old age. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: This was the service of the Kohathites: the most holy things. Theirs was the highest charge among the Levites, carrying what no other family might carry, and therefore hedged with the strictest reverence. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: When the camp set forward, Aaron and his sons took down the covering veil and covered the ark of testimony with it. The priests covered first; the Kohathites never saw the holy things uncovered, which marked both reverence for holy things and the mystery of the types under a dark dispensation. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: The table of shewbread was spread with blue cloth and furnished with its dishes, spoons, bowls and covers, with the continual bread left thereon. Even in transit the table kept its character: provision before the Lord was never interrupted by the march. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: The candlestick of the light was covered with blue cloth, with its lamps and vessels. The light of the sanctuary travelled veiled, seen by none on the road, a sign that the full light of the gospel was not yet unveiled. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: The golden altar was spread with blue cloth and covered with badgers' skins. Altar, table and lampstand all travelled in the same guarded way: holy things were carried, not displayed, and handled only after priestly covering. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: The ashes were taken away from the altar and a purple cloth spread on it. The altar was thus prepared for travel as a clean, covered thing, its service paused in order, not abandoned in haste. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: All the altar's vessels, wherewith they ministered about it, were put upon it under the covering. The completeness matters: nothing belonging to the altar was left to travel loose or unguarded. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: When Aaron and his sons finished covering the sanctuary, the Kohathites came to bear it, but they might not touch any holy thing, lest they die. Henry notes the case of Uzza, struck dead for breaching this law: even those who bore the vessels of the Lord were kept in fear under that dispensation of terror. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: To Eleazar, now Aaron's eldest son, pertained the oil for the light, the sweet incense, the daily meat offering and the anointing oil, with oversight of all the tabernacle. Henry observes that as a priest he had more honour than the Levites, but also more care, a heavier burden on his heart than all the burdens on their shoulders. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: The Lord warned: cut not off the tribe of the Kohathite families from among the Levites. Henry's reading is that those who do not what they can to keep others from sin do what they can to cut them off, so the warning is itself a guard for their lives. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: Aaron and his sons were to act so that the Kohathites might live and not die when approaching the most holy things, assigning every man his service and burden. The rule protected life by assigning order: each man knew exactly what he might touch and what he might not. — Matthew Henry

20 COMMENTARY: The Kohathites might not go in to see when the holy things were covered, lest they die. Henry notes they saw not what they bore, so much were even those who expounded the law in the dark concerning the gospel; now, through Christ, believers are encouraged to come boldly to the throne of grace. — Matthew Henry

22 COMMENTARY: The sons of Gershon were also summed by their fathers' houses, for the same service age, thirty to fifty. The second family is counted with the same care as the first: no family of Levi was left unordered. — Matthew Henry

23 COMMENTARY: Gershon's men, from thirty to fifty, entered the host to do the work in the tabernacle. The age bounds applied to every Levitical family alike, since the work required full strength, whether the burden was heavy or light. — Matthew Henry

24 COMMENTARY: This was the service of the Gershonites, to serve and for burdens. Their charge, though less honourable than the Kohathites', was necessary and to be done regularly, and Henry notes all things were delivered to them by name, intimating God's care for his church and every member of it. — Matthew Henry

25 COMMENTARY: The Gershonites bore the curtains of the tabernacle, the tabernacle of the congregation, its covering and the badgers' skins above it. Their burden was the soft fabric of the dwelling, the shelter under which the holy things rested. — Matthew Henry

27 COMMENTARY: All the service of the Gershonites was at the appointment of Aaron and his sons, and their burden was in their charge. Even a lesser charge was not self-directed: the priests assigned it, and the Levites bore what was assigned. — Matthew Henry

28 COMMENTARY: This was the service of the Gershonite families under Ithamar, son of Aaron. Each family had its priestly overseer, so that the work of carrying the tabernacle was supervised as carefully as the work within it. — Matthew Henry

29 COMMENTARY: The sons of Merari were numbered after their families and fathers' houses. The third family completes the pattern: every son of Levi had his appointed place in the moving sanctuary. — Matthew Henry

30 COMMENTARY: Merari's men were numbered from thirty to fifty, every one entering the host to do the service. The same prime of life was required for the heaviest burden as for the lightest, since all of it was the Lord's work. — Matthew Henry

31 COMMENTARY: Merari's charge was the boards of the tabernacle, its bars, pillars and sockets. Their burden was the solid framework, the heaviest and least showy part, without which curtains and coverings had nothing to stand on. — Matthew Henry

32 COMMENTARY: The pillars of the court, their sockets, pins and cords were also Merari's charge. Court as well as tabernacle travelled in their hands: the whole enclosure of worship, not only its inner rooms, was carried in order. — Matthew Henry

33 COMMENTARY: This was the service of the Merarite families, under the hand of Ithamar, son of Aaron. Like Gershon, Merari worked under Aaron's younger son, a division of oversight that kept the whole camp's labour accountable. — Matthew Henry

34 COMMENTARY: Moses, Aaron and the chief of the congregation numbered the Kohathites. The counting was a public, shared act, witnessed by the congregation's chiefs, not a private reckoning of Moses alone. — Matthew Henry

35 COMMENTARY: The Kohathites numbered were those from thirty to fifty entering the service of the tabernacle. The age rule held at the count as at the command: only men in their prime strength were enrolled for the host of the sanctuary. — Matthew Henry

36 COMMENTARY: The Kohathites numbered by their families were two thousand seven hundred and fifty. Henry notes they were eight thousand six hundred in all from a month old, so fewer than a third were serviceable men, a reminder that many add to the numbers of the church while comparatively few contribute to its service. — Matthew Henry

38 COMMENTARY: The Gershonites were numbered throughout their families and fathers' houses. Their enrolment followed the same pattern as Kohath's, family by family, so that every burden-bearer was known and assigned. — Matthew Henry

40 COMMENTARY: The Gershonites numbered were two thousand six hundred and thirty, from seven thousand five hundred in all, little more than a third serviceable. The proportion matches Kohath's: the working strength of the tribe was always a minority of its numbers. — Matthew Henry

42 COMMENTARY: The Merarites were numbered after their families, completing the enrolment of the three Levitical houses. Each family's serviceable men were counted separately, so that every burden of the tabernacle had its own assigned bearers. — Matthew Henry

44 COMMENTARY: The Merarites numbered after their families were three thousand and two hundred. Henry marvels at God's ordering: though Merari was the fewest in all, only six thousand two hundred, yet more than half were serviceable men, because the greatest burden lay on them. — Matthew Henry

46 COMMENTARY: All the Levites numbered by Moses, Aaron and the chiefs of Israel were eight thousand five hundred and eighty. Henry contrasts this with the other tribes: the least of them had almost four times as many able men, since those engaged in the world's service are many more than those devoted to God's. — Matthew Henry

48 COMMENTARY: The total of the serviceable Levites was eight thousand five hundred and eighty, every man from thirty to fifty coming to do the service of the tabernacle and the service of burden. The sum closes the chapter as a complete host, small beside Israel's army, yet sufficient for the sanctuary. — Matthew Henry

49 COMMENTARY: They were numbered by the hand of Moses according to the Lord's commandment, every one according to his service and burden. Henry's closing lesson is that whatever service God calls men to, he will furnish them for it and give strength in proportion to the work. — Matthew Henry

No note for verse(s) 1, 6, 8, 10, 12, 17, 21, 26, 37, 39, 41, 43, 45, 47 — the public-domain sources consulted give no distinct explanation for these verses beyond the list itself, so nothing was invented for them.
