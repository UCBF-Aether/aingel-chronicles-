Study Notes — Numbers

Chapter 28

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

2 COMMENTARY: Command Israel, and say to them, my offering, and my bread for my sacrifices made by fire, for a sweet savour to me, shall you observe to offer to me in their due season. JFB notes the repetition of several laws formerly enacted was seasonable and necessary, not only for their importance and frequent neglect, but because a new generation had sprung up since their first institution and Israel was about to settle in the land where the ordinances were to be observed, while my offering and my bread enforce regularity and care. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: And you shall say to them, this is the offering made by fire which you shall offer to the Lord: two lambs of the first year without spot day by day, for a continual burnt offering. The daily offering was the foundation of the whole calendar of sacrifices, two lambs every day without exception. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: The one lamb shall you offer in the morning, and the other lamb shall you offer at even. Henry notes the daily sacrifice is called a continual burnt offering, and when we are bid to pray always, at least every morning and evening we should offer up solemn prayers and praises to God. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: And a tenth part of an ephah of flour for a meat offering, mingled with the fourth part of a hin of beaten oil. Each lamb carried its measure of flour and oil, so that the daily offering was not bare slaughter but bread and oil with it, the people's food offered back to God. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: It is a continual burnt offering, which was ordained in mount Sinai for a sweet savour, a sacrifice made by fire to the Lord. The daily offering reached back to Sinai, and its continuance, morning and evening without break, was the point: a sweet savour rising perpetually from the camp. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: And the drink offering thereof shall be the fourth part of a hin for the one lamb: in the holy place shall you cause the strong wine to be poured to the Lord for a drink offering. Henry notes nothing is added here but that the wine poured out is to be strong wine, to teach us to serve God with the best we have, and it was a figure of the blood of Christ, whose memorial is still left to the church in wine. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: And the other lamb shall you offer at even: as the meat offering of the morning, and as the drink offering thereof, you shall offer it, a sacrifice made by fire, of a sweet savour to the Lord. The evening lamb repeated the morning exactly, closing the day as the morning had opened it. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: And on the sabbath day two lambs of the first year without spot, and two tenth deals of flour for a meat offering, mingled with oil, and the drink offering thereof. JFB notes there is no previous mention of a sabbath burnt offering, which was additional to the daily sacrifices, and Henry adds this teaches us to double our devotions on sabbath days, for so the duty of the day requires. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: This is the burnt offering of every sabbath, beside the continual burnt offering, and his drink offering. The sabbath offering stood beside, not instead of, the daily offering: the sabbath rest was to be observed in order to apply ourselves more closely to the sabbath work, which ought to fill up the sabbath time. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: And in the beginnings of your months you shall offer a burnt offering to the Lord: two young bullocks, and one ram, seven lambs of the first year without spot. JFB notes the new moons were held as sacred festivals, distinguished by trumpets blown over the sacrifices, suspension of labour, public worship and family feasts, the month's beginning being known by messengers watching the first visible new moon and announced by signal fires on the mountain tops. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: And three tenth deals of flour for a meat offering, mingled with oil, for one bullock, and two tenth deals of flour for a meat offering, mingled with oil, for one ram. The monthly offering scaled its measures to the animals: three tenth deals for a bullock, two for a ram, keeping proportion between great and small. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: And their drink offerings shall be half a hin of wine to a bullock, and the third part of a hin to a ram, and a fourth part of a hin to a lamb: this is the burnt offering of every month throughout the months of the year. The drink measures, like the flour measures, were fixed by the month's calendar, so that every new moon brought the same proportioned offering. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: And one kid of the goats for a sin offering to the Lord shall be offered, beside the continual burnt offering, and his drink offering. JFB notes the kid was offered to the Lord, not to the moon as the Egyptians and Syrians did, since the new-moon festivals, common among the heathen, were instituted in Israel to give the people's minds a better direction. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: And in the fourteenth day of the first month is the passover of the Lord. JFB notes the law for that great annual festival is given elsewhere, but some details are here introduced, as certain specified offerings are prescribed for each of the seven days of unleavened bread. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: And in the fifteenth day of this month is the feast: seven days shall unleavened bread be eaten. The passover day was followed by a seven-day feast of unleavened bread, binding remembrance of the exodus to a week of eating without leaven. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: In the first day shall be a holy convocation; you shall do no manner of servile work therein. The feast opened with a holy convocation and a halt to servile work, so that the first day belonged wholly to the Lord. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: But you shall offer a sacrifice made by fire for a burnt offering to the Lord: two young bullocks, and one ram, and seven lambs of the first year: they shall be to you without blemish. Each day of the feast carried the same great burnt offering, two bullocks, a ram and seven lambs, all without blemish. — Matthew Henry

22 COMMENTARY: And one goat for a sin offering, to make an atonement for you. Beside the burnt offering each day, a goat for a sin offering made atonement, so that the feast of rejoicing never stood without repentance beside it. — Matthew Henry

23 COMMENTARY: You shall offer these beside the burnt offering in the morning, which is for a continual burnt offering. The feast offerings stood beside the morning continual offering, never replacing it, as every festival stood upon the daily foundation. — Matthew Henry

24 COMMENTARY: After this manner you shall offer daily, throughout the seven days, the meat of the sacrifice made by fire, of a sweet savour to the Lord: it shall be offered beside the continual burnt offering, and his drink offering. The same manner daily for seven days: the feast's constancy matched the daily offering's, and Henry notes the sacrifices remind us of the continued power of Christ's sacrifice and our continual need to depend on it. — Matthew Henry

25 COMMENTARY: And on the seventh day you shall have a holy convocation; you shall do no servile work. The feast closed as it opened, with a holy convocation and no servile work, bracketing the week in worship. — Matthew Henry

26 COMMENTARY: Also in the day of the firstfruits, when you bring a new meat offering to the Lord, after your weeks be out, you shall have a holy convocation; you shall do no servile work. JFB notes a new sacrifice is here ordered for this festival, in addition to the other offering accompanying the firstfruits, marking the completion of the weeks after passover. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

27 COMMENTARY: But you shall offer the burnt offering for a sweet savour to the Lord: two young bullocks, one ram, seven lambs of the first year. The firstfruits burnt offering matched the passover's great offering, two bullocks, a ram and seven lambs, for the harvest's first yield. — Matthew Henry

30 COMMENTARY: And one kid of the goats, to make an atonement for you. The firstfruits also carried its goat of atonement, so that thanksgiving for the harvest stood beside confession. — Matthew Henry

31 COMMENTARY: You shall offer them beside the continual burnt offering, and his meat offering, (they shall be to you without blemish) and their drink offerings. The closing rule gathered the whole chapter: festival offerings beside the continual offering, all without blemish, and Henry closes that Christ is able to supply the wants of every day, every week, every month, every year, every ordinance, every case. — Matthew Henry

No note for verse(s) 1, 13, 20, 21, 28, 29 — the public-domain sources consulted give no distinct explanation for these verses beyond the list itself, so nothing was invented for them.
