Study Notes — Numbers

Chapter 3

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: These are the generations of Aaron and Moses from the day the Lord spoke with Moses on Sinai. JFB explains why Aaron is named before Moses here, contrary to usual style: the priesthood is the subject, and that dignity belonged exclusively to Aaron's posterity, while Moses' children stood in the private ranks of the Levites. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: Aaron's sons were four, all consecrated to the priest's office. JFB notes the two oldest enjoyed only a brief term of office, while Eleazar and Ithamar served dutifully during their father's lifetime as his assistants, under his superintendence. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: These sons were the anointed priests, consecrated to minister. The anointing set them apart for the work alone: priesthood here is not inherited honour in general, but a specific consecration to a specific office. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: Nadab and Abihu died before the Lord when they offered strange fire in the wilderness of Sinai, leaving no children. Their brief term stands as the warning that consecration does not license carelessness about God's appointed worship. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: The Lord spoke to Moses about bringing Levi near, and JFB notes the Hebrew word for bringing near is a sacrificial term: presenting an offering to God. The Levites were thus themselves devoted as an offering to the sanctuary, no longer to be employed in common offices. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: Levi was presented before Aaron to minister to him, subordinate to the priests who alone entered the holy place. Their humbler duties were nevertheless of great utility and importance to the religion and morals of the people. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: The Levites kept Aaron's charge and the charge of the whole congregation before the tabernacle. Henry's remark frames the whole chapter: those whom God finds work for, he will find help for, and Aaron, left with only two sons for much priestly work, was given the whole tribe as helpers. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: The Levites kept all the instruments of the tabernacle and the charge of Israel. Their service was practical and constant: the vessels and furniture of worship were in their hands, even though the priestly acts themselves were not. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: The Levites were wholly given to Aaron and his sons out of Israel. JFB puts it sharply: the priests hold the place of God, and the Levites are the servants of God in the obedience they render to the priests. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: Aaron and his sons waited on the priest's office, and the stranger who came near would die. The severity guards the honour of holy things: none might see or touch them but those called of God to the service, as Henry had already stressed in chapter 1. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: The Lord spoke again, and JFB stresses that Levi's consecration did not originate in Moses' legislative wisdom but in God's special appointment. The tribe was chosen partly as a tribute to Moses and Aaron, partly for its zeal in the golden calf affair, and partly because, being the smallest tribe, it could ill find other employment and support. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: God took the Levites instead of all the firstborn among Israel. The exchange recalled the last judgment on Egypt, from which Israelite households were miraculously exempt: all firstborn had been consecrated to God, and now one whole tribe stood in their place. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: All the firstborn are God's because on the day he smote Egypt's firstborn he hallowed Israel's. Henry draws the redemption lesson: when he who made us also saves us, as the firstborn were saved, we are under further obligation to serve him faithfully. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: Levi was numbered in the wilderness of Sinai, but on a different principle from the other tribes. JFB explains: the other tribes were counted from twenty years upward for war, from which Levites were totally exempt, while Levites were counted from a month old upward, because they entered a work for which they were trained from childhood. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: Levi was numbered after fathers' houses and families, every male from a month old upward. The lower age reflects the nature of the service: Levi's work was not the battlefield but the sanctuary, entered as soon as a boy was capable of instruction. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: Moses numbered them according to the word of the Lord, as he was commanded. The census of Levi, like that of Israel, was an act of obedience, not administration for its own sake. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: Levi's sons were Gershon, Kohath and Merari, the three ancestral heads of the tribe's subdivisions. JFB notes their stations: Gershonites on the west, Kohathites on the south, Merarites on the north, with Moses and Aaron on the honoured east side. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

23 COMMENTARY: The Gershonite families pitched behind the tabernacle westward. Their western station, second in honour, matched Gershon's place as the eldest son, while the heavier drudgery fell to Merari. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

25 COMMENTARY: The charge of Gershon's sons was the tabernacle itself: its coverings, hangings and cords. Their work was the fabric of the dwelling, the outer shelter of the holy place, carried and kept by their families. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

29 COMMENTARY: The Kohathite families pitched on the south side of the tabernacle. They held the principal place about the sanctuary, JFB notes, probably because the Aaronic family belonged to this division. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

31 COMMENTARY: The Kohathites' charge was the ark, the table, the candlestick, the altars and the vessels of the sanctuary. They carried the most precious and sacred things, the very furniture of God's presence, while the priests themselves covered them first. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

32 COMMENTARY: Eleazar, son of Aaron, was chief over the chief of the Levites, overseeing all who kept the charge of the sanctuary. JFB notes he was therefore called the second priest, and performed the high priest's duties in his absence from illness or necessity. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

36 COMMENTARY: The Merarites' charge was the boards, bars, pillars, sockets, pins and cords of the tabernacle and court. Theirs was the heavy framework, the drudgery of the structure, without which the coverings Gershon carried had nothing to hang on. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

38 COMMENTARY: Moses, Aaron and his sons encamped before the tabernacle eastward, on the entrance side. JFB notes this was the post of honour, reserved to Moses and the priestly family, while the sons of Moses himself had no station here. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

39 COMMENTARY: The Levites numbered twenty-two thousand males from a month old upward. JFB notes the separate family numbers add to twenty-two thousand three hundred, and the missing three hundred is variously explained, most probably by a copyist's confusion of similar Hebrew letters used as figures. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

40 COMMENTARY: The firstborn males of Israel were now numbered on the same principle, from a month old upward, so a comparison could be made with Levi. The two numbers came near each other, and Henry remarks that known unto God are all his works beforehand: there is an exact proportion between them. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

41 COMMENTARY: The Levites were taken for the Lord instead of all the firstborn, and their cattle instead of the firstlings of Israel's cattle. The exchange covered beast as well as man, so that firstlings were not then brought to the altar as they afterwards were. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

43 COMMENTARY: The firstborn males numbered twenty-two thousand two hundred and seventy-three, leaving an excess of two hundred and seventy-three over the Levites. JFB notes the excess is so small that it is best explained by counting only firstborn still in their parents' households, or only those born since the departure from Egypt, when God claimed all the firstborn. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

45 COMMENTARY: The Lord spoke again, confirming the exchange: Levites for firstborn, Levite cattle for firstlings. The repetition seals the substitution as God's own decree: JFB renders the divine I am the Lord here as, I decree it to be so, and expect full obedience. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

46 COMMENTARY: The two hundred and seventy-three firstborn over the number of the Levites were to be redeemed. Since there were not Levites enough for an equal exchange, some parents had to incur the expense, and Jewish writers say the matter was settled by lot, with slips marked for a son of Levi or for five shekels. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

47 COMMENTARY: The redemption price was five shekels apiece by the poll, after the shekel of the sanctuary. JFB reckons the shekel at half a crown, making each ransom twelve shillings and sixpence, appropriated to the use of the sanctuary. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

48 COMMENTARY: The redemption money was given to Aaron and his sons. The payment went to the priesthood because the firstborn, had they not been exchanged, would have served the sanctuary themselves. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

50 COMMENTARY: Moses took the redemption money of the firstborn, a thousand three hundred and threescore and five shekels. The exact sum, five shekels times two hundred and seventy-three, shows the redemption was reckoned man by man, not waved in round numbers. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

51 COMMENTARY: Moses gave the money to Aaron and his sons according to the word of the Lord. Henry closes the chapter with the church lesson: the church is called the church of the firstborn, redeemed not with silver and gold but with the precious blood of the Son of God. — Matthew Henry

No note for verse(s) 18, 19, 20, 21, 22, 24, 26, 27, 28, 30, 33, 34, 35, 37, 42, 44, 49 — the public-domain sources consulted give no distinct explanation for these verses beyond the list itself, so nothing was invented for them.
