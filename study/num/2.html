Study Notes — Numbers

Chapter 2

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

2 COMMENTARY: Every man pitched by his own standard, with the ensign of his father's house, at a reverential distance from the tabernacle. JFB explains the standards as visible rallying signs, in three kinds: great tribal standards, standards of subdivided portions, and family ensigns, probably like Egyptian banners stripped of idolatrous symbols. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: Judah's camp, with Issachar and Zebulun under its standard, pitched on the east toward the sunrise and led the march, which ran mostly eastward. JFB notes the twelve census superintendents were also made captains of their tribes, a dignity owed to their hereditary office as tribal heads. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: Issachar pitched next to Judah, on one side. Henry's general lesson for the whole chapter is contentment with one's allotted place: Israel took their posts without murmuring or disputing, and that order was both their safety and their beauty. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: Zebulun pitched on Judah's other side, completing a strong and imposing van, since Judah was the most numerous tribe and Issachar and Zebulun were also very numerous. The leading camp was thus the strongest, fit to break the way. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: The whole camp of Judah numbered a hundred and fourscore and six thousand and four hundred. The van's great strength, with the tabernacle at the centre, shows a host arranged for both protection and advance, not a crowd wandering at random. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: Reuben's camp held the south side, Ephraim's the west and Dan's the north, with Judah on the east, forming a great square around the tabernacle, computed at more than twelve square miles for the fighting men alone, besides families, tents and baggage. JFB adds the march order: Judah first, then Reuben's division, then the Levites bearing the tabernacle, then Ephraim's division, with Dan's bringing up the rear. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: The camp of Reuben numbered a hundred and fifty-one thousand four hundred and fifty. JFB notes the wisdom of the placement: the strongest camps, Judah and Dan, formed van and rear, while the smallest and weakest, Reuben's and Ephraim's, marched in the centre, guarded on both sides. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: The tabernacle of the congregation set forward in the midst of the camps, with the Levites around it. As the chief's tent stands at the centre of a nomad encampment, so the tent of Israel's Divine King stood at the centre of Israel, with his own tribe guarding it. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

18 COMMENTARY: Ephraim's camp held the west side, forming the third great division on the march, after the Levites. Its position kept the sacred centre protected by a full division on either side of it. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

24 COMMENTARY: The camp of Ephraim numbered a hundred and eight thousand and a hundred. Ephraim's division, with Manasseh and Benjamin, was the smallest of the four camps, and its central place on the march matched its lesser strength. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

25 COMMENTARY: Dan's camp held the north side and brought up the rear of the march. JFB notes Dan was next in force to Judah, so the rearguard, like the van, was a strong camp, guarding the host from behind. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

31 COMMENTARY: The camp of Dan numbered a hundred and fifty-seven thousand and six hundred, the largest camp after Judah's. The rear was therefore no weak tail but a second great strength, closing the host as Judah opened it. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

32 COMMENTARY: These are those numbered of Israel by their fathers' houses, six hundred and three thousand five hundred and fifty in all. The four camps together repeat the census total of chapter 1, now arranged into a marching and encamping order. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

33 COMMENTARY: The Levites were not numbered among Israel, as the Lord commanded Moses, because their service was the tabernacle, not war. Their separate place, camped around the sanctuary itself, is the exception that proves the order of the whole chapter. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

34 COMMENTARY: Israel did according to all the Lord commanded: they pitched by their standards and journeyed by their families. Henry draws the church lesson: the gospel church ought to be compact after this model, everyone knowing and keeping his place, so that beholders rejoice at its order. — Matthew Henry

No note for verse(s) 1, 4, 6, 8, 11, 12, 13, 14, 15, 19, 20, 21, 22, 23, 26, 27, 28, 29, 30 — the public-domain sources consulted give no distinct explanation for these verses beyond the list itself, so nothing was invented for them.
