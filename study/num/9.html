Study Notes — Numbers

Chapter 9

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: In the first month of the second year after the exodus, the Lord spoke to Moses in the wilderness of Sinai. JFB notes the date places this command shortly after the tabernacle's erection, a month before the numbering of chapter 1, though it is narrated here to introduce the case that follows. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: Israel was to keep the passover at his appointed season. JFB notes this was the first observance since the exodus, kept on the exact anniversary of the departure, with the lamb and unleavened bread, though the hurried circumstances of girded loins, sandals and staff were not repeated, being incidental to the flight itself. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: On the fourteenth day of the first month at evening they were to keep it, according to all its rites and ceremonies. The exactness of the season mattered: the memorial was tied to the very day of deliverance, not left to convenience. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: Moses spoke to Israel that they should keep the passover. The command was delivered through Moses as law for the whole congregation, not as an optional remembrance for the willing. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: They kept the passover on the fourteenth day of the first month at evening in the wilderness of Sinai, according to all the Lord commanded Moses. JFB adds it was probably the only observance of the feast during the forty years, since circumcision, required for eating it, was not practised in the wilderness. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: Certain men were defiled by the dead body of a man and could not keep the passover on that day. JFB explains the dilemma: discharging the last offices to deceased relatives was imperative, yet funeral attendance brought seven days' exclusion, and Jewish writers say these men had carried out the bodies of Nadab and Abihu. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: The defiled men came before Moses and Aaron asking why they were kept back from offering to the Lord at its appointed season. Henry observes with what trouble and concern they complained: it should be a trouble to us when by any occasion we are kept back from the solemnities of a sabbath or a sacrament. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: Moses said, stand still, and I will hear what the Lord will command concerning you. Henry praises Moses' deliberation: ministers must ask counsel of God's mouth, not determine by their own fancy, and time taken to spread a difficult case before God by prayer will be directed in the good and right way. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: If a man is unclean by reason of a dead body, or on a journey afar off, he shall keep the passover on the fourteenth day of the second month at evening. JFB calls this a special license by divine authority: the disqualified or distant kept it by themselves a month later, under all the same solemn formalities. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: They ate it with unleavened bread and bitter herbs. The second passover was no shortened rite: the same bread and herbs marked the same deliverance, only a month later. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: They left none of it until the morning, nor broke any bone of it, according to all the ordinances of the passover. The unbroken bones and the nothing-left rule kept the second observance identical to the first in every particular. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: But the man who is clean and not on a journey, yet forbears to keep the passover, shall be cut off from his people, because he brought not the Lord's offering in its season, and shall bear his sin. Henry contrasts the two absences: those forced to absent themselves may expect grace under affliction, but those who of choice absent themselves may expect God's wrath, for God is not mocked. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: If a stranger sojourns among you and will keep the passover, he shall do so according to its ordinance, one ordinance for stranger and homeborn alike. JFB notes Gentile converts were admitted if circumcised, to the same privileges as native Israelites, and liable to excommunication if they neglected it. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: On the day the tabernacle was reared, the cloud covered it, the tent of the testimony, and at evening it appeared as fire until morning. JFB describes the cloud as a visible token of God's special presence and guardian care, resting by day as a dark column and by night as fire over the ark's part of the sanctuary. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: So it was always: the cloud covered it by day, and the appearance of fire by night. The sign never left them, teaching, as Henry says, that God is always near us, both night and day. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: When the cloud was taken up from the tabernacle, then Israel journeyed. JFB explains the cloud rose higher so as to be conspicuous at the remotest extremities of the camp, and its rising was properly called the commandment of the Lord, since from it as a glorious throne he gave the order. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

18 COMMENTARY: At the commandment of the Lord Israel journeyed, and at his commandment they pitched, as long as the cloud abode on the tabernacle. The cloud's motion regulated the commencement and termination of every journey, so that marching and resting were alike acts of obedience. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

19 COMMENTARY: When the cloud tarried long on the tabernacle, Israel kept the charge of the Lord and journeyed not. JFB notes the Israelites had imbibed a nomad love of change and longed to hasten to Canaan, yet whatever irksomeness they felt in long encampment, they stayed while the cloud stayed. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

20 COMMENTARY: Sometimes the cloud was but a few days on the tabernacle, and then according to the Lord's command they abode, and according to his command they journeyed. Short stays and long stays alike were governed: there was no time lost while waiting God's time, as Henry puts it. — Matthew Henry

21 COMMENTARY: Sometimes the cloud abode from evening to morning, and when it was taken up in the morning they journeyed, or by day or by night when it was taken up. The rule covered every pattern of stay, so that no hour of departure was left to human guessing. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

22 COMMENTARY: Whether the cloud tarried two days, or a month, or a year, Israel abode in their tents and journeyed not, and when it was taken up they journeyed. The length of the stay made no difference to the duty: a year of waiting was obedience, just as a day's march was. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

23 COMMENTARY: At the commandment of the Lord they rested in their tents, and at his commandment they journeyed, keeping the charge of the Lord by the hand of Moses. Henry closes with the heart's response: at the Lord's command our hearts should always move and rest, saying, Father, thy will be done, dispose of me and mine as thou pleasest. — Matthew Henry

No note for verse(s) 9 — the public-domain sources consulted give no distinct explanation for these verses beyond the list itself, so nothing was invented for them.
