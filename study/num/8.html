Study Notes — Numbers

Chapter 8

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

2 COMMENTARY: When Aaron lit the lamps, the seven lamps gave light over against the candlestick. JFB describes the candlestick as one solid mass of pure gold, six lamps on branches with a seventh on the central shaft, and Aaron first lighting the middle lamp from the altar fire, then the others from each other, a symbol that all heavenly light derives from Christ and is diffused by his ministers. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: Aaron did so, lighting the lamps over against the candlestick as the Lord commanded Moses. Henry notes Aaron himself lit them, representing his Divine Master, and the work of ministers is to light these lamps by expounding and applying the word of God. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: The work of the candlestick was beaten gold, from shaft to flowers, made after the pattern shown to Moses. The single beaten piece, completed exactly to the pattern, shows the light of the sanctuary was not a human contrivance but God's own appointment. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: The Levites were taken from among Israel and cleansed. JFB stresses hereditary descent alone did not qualify them: they needed a special ceremony, simpler than the priests', with no washing, anointing or robes of office, yet still a real setting apart. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: To cleanse them, Moses sprinkled water of purifying on them, shaved all their flesh, and they washed their clothes. JFB explains the combination: sprinkling with water mixed with red heifer ashes, shaving all over, and washing clothes reminded them of mortifying carnal desires and maintaining the purity fitting God's servants. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: Then they took a young bullock with its meat offering of fine flour mingled with oil, and another young bullock for a sin offering. The sacrifices belonged to the consecration itself: cleansing by water was joined to atonement by blood before any Levite touched the sanctuary's work. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: The whole assembly of Israel was brought before the tabernacle. JFB notes the whole multitude could not literally do this, so a select portion is meant, supposed by some to be the firstborn transferring their privilege to Levi, and by others the princes blessing the tribe. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: The children of Israel laid their hands on the Levites. The imposition of hands was the ancient ceremony for consecrating persons to holy office, perpetuated in the Christian church from the example of the Lord and his apostles. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: Aaron offered the Levites before the Lord as an offering of the children of Israel, that they might execute the service of the Lord. JFB renders the Hebrew as a wave offering: the high priest likely brought them one by one to the altar with movements like those of a wave offering, first devoting them to God and then receiving them back for the priests' service. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: The Levites laid their hands on the heads of the bullocks, one offered for a sin offering and one for a burnt offering, to make atonement for them. By laying hands on the sacrifices, the Levites owned the atonement as their own before they were offered to God themselves. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: The Levites were set before Aaron and his sons and offered as an offering to the Lord. The setting before the priests marked their subordination: they were given to Aaron's house, not set up as a rival ministry. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: Thus the Levites were separated from among Israel, and the Levites shall be mine. JFB explains the separation: exempt from military duty and secular work, free from pecuniary imposition, wholly devoted to the custody and service of the sanctuary. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: After that the Levites went in to do the service of the tabernacle, once Aaron had made atonement for them. JFB notes their service meant entering the court to assist the priests, and at removals standing at the tabernacle door to receive the covered furniture. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: The Levites are wholly given to God from among Israel, instead of all the firstborn that open the matrix. The exchange is stated as a gift in both directions: Israel gave Levi, and God gave Levi back to the priests for the people's sake. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: All the firstborn of Israel are God's, man and beast, since on the day he smote Egypt's firstborn he sanctified Israel's to himself. The consecration of Levi reaches back to the exodus night, when the firstborn were spared and thereby claimed. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

18 COMMENTARY: God took the Levites for all the firstborn of Israel. The substitution was total, tribe for firstborn, so that no family kept a private claim to priestly service once Levi had been taken in its place. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

19 COMMENTARY: God gave the Levites as a gift to Aaron and his sons, to do the service of Israel in the tabernacle and make atonement for them, so that no plague would come on Israel when they came near the sanctuary. JFB renders the purpose as redemption: a sanctified body guarded the sanctuary so the people would not presumptuously meddle with holy things and expose themselves to judgment. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

20 COMMENTARY: Moses, Aaron and all the congregation did to the Levites according to all the Lord commanded Moses concerning them. Henry's point for the whole consecration is that all Israel must know the Levites took not this honour to themselves, but were called of God to it. — Matthew Henry

21 COMMENTARY: The Levites were purified, washed their clothes, and Aaron offered them as an offering and made atonement for them. The cleansing was completed by the priest's act, not by their own washing alone: God declared his acceptance of them through Aaron. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

22 COMMENTARY: After that the Levites went in to do their service in the tabernacle before Aaron and his sons, as the Lord had commanded Moses concerning them. Henry's closing rule is that all who expect to share in the tabernacle's privileges must resolve to do the tabernacle's service: none of God's servants are merely honorary, doing nothing. — Matthew Henry

24 COMMENTARY: This belongs to the Levites: from twenty-five years old and upward they waited on the service of the tabernacle. JFB reconciles this with chapter 4: they entered at twenty-five as pupils and probationers under their senior brethren, and at thirty were admitted to the full discharge of office. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

25 COMMENTARY: From fifty years old they ceased waiting upon the service of the tabernacle. JFB explains this as release from the laborious and exhausting parts of the work, not from all service, since age that can no longer carry may still direct. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

26 COMMENTARY: But they ministered with their brethren in the tabernacle, to keep the charge, and did no service. JFB describes the easier and higher duties of age: instructing and directing the young, or superintending important trusts, in Milton's words, they also serve who only wait. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

No note for verse(s) 1, 5, 23 — the public-domain sources consulted give no distinct explanation for these verses beyond the list itself, so nothing was invented for them.
