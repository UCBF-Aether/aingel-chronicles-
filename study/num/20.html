Study Notes — Numbers

Chapter 20

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Then Israel, the whole congregation, came into the desert of Zin in the first month, and the people abode in Kadesh, and Miriam died there and was buried there. Henry notes that after thirty-eight years' tedious abode in the wilderness, the armies of Israel advanced toward Canaan again, and the generation's turning point opened with Miriam's death at Kadesh. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: There was no water for the congregation, and they gathered themselves together against Moses and against Aaron. Henry notes we live in a wanting world, and wherever we are must expect something to put us out, and plenty of water is a great mercy whose worth we should own more when we feel its want. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: The people chode with Moses, saying, would God that we had died when our brethren died before the Lord! Henry notes they spoke the same absurd and brutish language their fathers had done, and their crime was worse because they had smarted so long for their fathers' discontent and distrust, yet ventured in the same steps. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: And why have you brought up the congregation of the Lord into this wilderness, that we and our cattle should die there? The complaint charged the leaders with a murderous purpose, as if the wilderness journey were designed to kill the congregation rather than to bring it to Canaan. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: And wherefore have you made us come up out of Egypt, to bring us into this evil place, a place of no seed, figs, vines or pomegranates, neither is there any water to drink? The catalogue of absences measured Canaan's promised plenty against the desert's bareness, and turned a present want into a verdict on the whole exodus. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: Moses and Aaron went from the presence of the assembly to the door of the tabernacle, fell upon their faces, and the glory of the Lord appeared to them. As in every crisis, the leaders turned from the assembly's face to God's presence, and glory answered their prostration. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: Take the rod, gather the assembly together, you and Aaron your brother, and speak to the rock before their eyes, and it shall give forth his water, and you shall bring forth water for them out of the rock, for the congregation and their cattle to drink. Henry notes God is as able as ever to supply his people with what is needful, and the command was to speak to the rock, not to smite it. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: Moses took the rod from before the Lord, as he commanded him. The rod, the same instrument of former wonders, was taken from before the Lord's presence, so that the wonder would be visibly God's, not Moses'. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: Moses and Aaron gathered the congregation before the rock, and he said to them, hear now, you rebels, must we fetch you water out of this rock? Henry's charge is exact: they took much of the glory of the wonder to themselves, as if it were done by some power or worthiness of their own, asking, must we fetch water? — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: Moses lifted up his hand, and with his rod he smote the rock twice, and the water came out abundantly, and the congregation drank, and their beasts also. The water came despite the disobedience, abundantly and for cattle as well as people, so that God's mercy was not measured by his servants' failure. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: The Lord spoke to Moses and Aaron, because you believed me not, to sanctify me in the eyes of Israel, therefore you shall not bring this congregation into the land which I have given them. Henry explains they did not sanctify God, that is, they did not give to him alone the glory of this miracle due to his name, and, provoked by the people, Moses spoke unadvisedly with his lips. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: This is the water of Meribah, because the children of Israel strove with the Lord, and he was sanctified in them. The name fixed the place as strife, yet God was sanctified in it: even through his servants' failure, the wonder declared his holiness to Israel. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: Moses sent messengers from Kadesh to the king of Edom, saying, thus says your brother Israel, you know all the travail that has befallen us. Henry notes the nearest way to Canaan from Israel's encampment was through Edom, so the embassy was a reasonable request for the nearest road, addressed brother to brother. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: How our fathers went down into Egypt, and we have dwelt in Egypt a long time, and the Egyptians vexed us and our fathers. The embassy rehearsed the shared history first: Israel's long sojourn and affliction were the ground for asking a brother's kindness on the road. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: And when we cried to the Lord, he heard our voice, and sent an angel, and has brought us forth out of Egypt, and behold, we are in Kadesh, a city in the uttermost of your border. The deliverance was God's work by his angel, and Kadesh on Edom's border was the proof that the journey had reached its neighbour's door. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: Let us pass, I pray you, through your country: we will not pass through fields or vineyards, neither will we drink of the water of the wells: we will go by the king's high way, not turning to the right hand or the left, until we have passed your borders. The terms removed every ground of fear: no fields, no vineyards, no wells, only the highway, straight through. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: And Edom said to him, you shall not pass by me, lest I come out against you with the sword. Henry notes the Edomites feared damage from Israel, and under any other discipline than God's own there might have been cause for jealousy, but the refusal was still a brother's door shut in Israel's face. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: The children of Israel said to him, we will go by the high way, and if I and my cattle drink of your water, then I will pay for it: I will only, without doing anything else, go through on my feet. Israel answered the sword with a second, humbler offer: foot passage only, and payment for any water drunk. — Matthew Henry

20 COMMENTARY: And he said, you shall not go through, and Edom came out against him with much people and with a strong hand. The second refusal came with an army on the border, turning a denied request into an armed confrontation. — Matthew Henry

21 COMMENTARY: Thus Edom refused to give Israel passage through his border, wherefore Israel turned away from him. Henry reads the old hatred revived: Esau hated Jacob because of the blessing, and now, when the blessing was about to be inherited, the hatred revived, and we must not think it strange if reasonable requests are denied by unreasonable men. — Matthew Henry

22 COMMENTARY: The children of Israel, the whole congregation, journeyed from Kadesh and came to mount Hor. The turn away from Edom brought the whole congregation to the mountain where the priesthood itself would change hands. — Matthew Henry

24 COMMENTARY: Aaron shall be gathered to his people, for he shall not enter into the land which I have given to Israel, because you rebelled against my word at the water of Meribah. Henry finds displeasure, mercy and significance together: Aaron died for his transgression at Meribah, yet died with ease and honour, and his exclusion showed the Levitical priesthood could make nothing perfect, which must be done by bringing in a better hope. — Matthew Henry

25 COMMENTARY: Take Aaron and Eleazar his son, and bring them up to mount Hor. The succession was arranged before the death, in the open sight of the congregation, so that the priesthood passed without a gap. — Matthew Henry

26 COMMENTARY: Strip Aaron of his garments, and put them upon Eleazar his son, and Aaron shall be gathered to his people and shall die there. Henry notes it was a great satisfaction to Aaron to see his dear son preferred and his office preserved and secured, and to see in this a figure of Christ's everlasting priesthood. — Matthew Henry

27 COMMENTARY: Moses did as the Lord commanded, and they went up into mount Hor in the sight of all the congregation. The ascent was public: the whole congregation watched the old priest climb to die and the new priest climb to succeed him. — Matthew Henry

28 COMMENTARY: Moses stripped Aaron of his garments, put them upon Eleazar his son, and Aaron died there in the top of the mount, and Moses and Eleazar came down from the mount. Henry notes Aaron submitted and died in the method and manner appointed, for all that appears with as much cheerfulness as if he had been going to bed, and a good man would not desire to outlive his usefulness. — Matthew Henry

29 COMMENTARY: When all the congregation saw that Aaron was dead, they mourned for Aaron thirty days, even all the house of Israel. The thirty days' mourning, by the whole house, honoured the priest who had borne their iniquities for forty years, even though he died outside the land. — Matthew Henry

No note for verse(s) 7, 23 — the public-domain sources consulted give no distinct explanation for these verses beyond the list itself, so nothing was invented for them.
