Study Notes — Numbers

Chapter 26

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: It came to pass after the plague, that the Lord spoke to Moses and to Eleazar son of Aaron the priest, saying, take the sum of the congregation. Henry notes Moses did not number the people except when God commanded him, and the new numbering came after the plague, for a new generation that would enter the land. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: Take the sum of all the congregation of Israel, from twenty years old and upward, throughout their fathers' houses, all that are able to go forth to war in Israel. The rule was unchanged from Sinai: fighting men, twenty and upward, counted by fathers' houses, but the men counted were almost entirely new. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: Moses and Eleazar the priest spoke with them in the plains of Moab by Jordan near Jericho. Eleazar now stood where Aaron had stood at the first census, and the plains of Moab, within sight of the land, were the fitting place for counting its future inheritors. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: Take the sum of the people, from twenty years old and upward, as the Lord commanded Moses and the children of Israel, which went forth out of the land of Egypt. The command repeated the exodus generation's census for the generation that would actually possess Canaan. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: These are the families of the Reubenites, and they that were numbered of them were forty and three thousand and seven hundred and thirty. Reuben, the eldest son, now counted fewer than at Sinai, and Henry notes the total of the whole census was nearly the same as at Sinai, though the tribes within it had shifted greatly. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: And the sons of Eliab were Nemuel, and Dathan, and Abiram. This is that Dathan and Abiram, which were famous in the congregation, who strove against Moses and against Aaron in the company of Korah, when they strove against the Lord. The register pauses on the rebels' names, so that the census itself remembers the rebellion that had cut off the old generation. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: And the earth opened her mouth, and swallowed them up together with Korah, when that company died, what time the fire devoured two hundred and fifty men, and they became a sign. The census record turns the rebellion into a sign: the earth and the fire together marked the fate of those who strove against the Lord. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: Notwithstanding the children of Korah died not. Henry takes special notice of this: Korah's children died not as Dathan's and Abiram's did, and seem not to have joined even their own father in rebellion, so that if we partake not of sinners' sins, we shall not partake of their plagues. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: These are the families of the Simeonites, twenty and two thousand and two hundred. Simeon had fallen most sharply of all, from nearly sixty thousand at Sinai to twenty-two thousand two hundred, a loss the census records without explaining, in the shadow of Baal-peor and the plague. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: These are the families of the children of Gad according to those numbered of them, forty thousand and five hundred. Gad's families were registered as well as the tribe, Henry notes: the census preserved families, not only tribal totals, because the land would be divided by families. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: The sons of Judah were Er and Onan, and Er and Onan died in the land of Canaan. The register remembers Judah's first two sons only by their death in Canaan, before the exodus, so that the tribe's present families descend through Shelah and Pharez alone. — Matthew Henry

22 COMMENTARY: These are the families of Judah according to those numbered of them, threescore and sixteen thousand and five hundred. Judah remained the largest tribe, now grown beyond its Sinai number, keeping the pre-eminence Jacob had assigned it. — Matthew Henry

25 COMMENTARY: These are the families of Issachar according to those numbered of them, threescore and four thousand and three hundred. Issachar, like Judah, had grown since Sinai, and its families were counted for the inheritance that would match its numbers. — Matthew Henry

27 COMMENTARY: These are the families of the Zebulunites according to those numbered of them, threescore thousand and five hundred. Zebulun's count, like the others, was taken family by family, so that the division of the land could follow the register exactly. — Matthew Henry

33 COMMENTARY: And Zelophehad son of Hepher had no sons, but daughters, and the names of the daughters of Zelophehad were Mahlah, Noah, Hoglah, Milcah, and Tirzah. The register pauses on five daughters with no brother, whose names would soon raise the question of inheritance answered in the next chapter. — Matthew Henry

34 COMMENTARY: These are the families of Manasseh, and those numbered of them, fifty and two thousand and seven hundred. Manasseh, through Machir and Gilead, had grown into a tribe whose families would soon hold land on both sides of the Jordan. — Matthew Henry

37 COMMENTARY: These are the families of the sons of Ephraim according to those numbered of them, thirty and two thousand and five hundred. Ephraim, once the larger half of Joseph, now counted fewer than Manasseh, a reversal the census records without comment. — Matthew Henry

41 COMMENTARY: These are the sons of Benjamin after their families, and they that were numbered of them were forty and five thousand and six hundred. Benjamin's families were counted in full, a tribe that had grown by ten thousand since Sinai despite the wilderness years. — Matthew Henry

43 COMMENTARY: All the families of the Shuhamites, according to those numbered of them, were threescore and four thousand and four hundred. Dan, through the single family of Shuham, counted the second largest tribe, a host from one house. — Matthew Henry

47 COMMENTARY: These are the families of the sons of Asher according to those numbered of them, fifty and three thousand and four hundred. Asher's families, through Jimna, Jesui and Beriah, were registered for the northern inheritance they would receive. — Matthew Henry

50 COMMENTARY: These are the families of Naphtali according to their families, and they that were numbered of them were forty and five thousand and four hundred. Naphtali closed the tribal register, its families counted like the rest for the coming division. — Matthew Henry

51 COMMENTARY: These were the numbered of the children of Israel, six hundred thousand and a thousand seven hundred and thirty. Henry notes the total was nearly the same as at Sinai, six hundred and three thousand five hundred and fifty then, six hundred and one thousand seven hundred and thirty now, a new generation of almost exactly the old strength. — Matthew Henry

53 COMMENTARY: Unto these the land shall be divided for an inheritance according to the number of names. The census was taken for division: the land belonged to the numbered names, so that counting the people was counting the inheritors. — Matthew Henry

54 COMMENTARY: To many you shall give the more inheritance, and to few you shall give the less inheritance: to everyone shall his inheritance be given according to those that were numbered of him. Henry states the general rule of equity: many received more, fewer received less, in proportion to the census numbers. — Matthew Henry

55 COMMENTARY: Notwithstanding, the land shall be divided by lot, according to the names of the tribes of their fathers they shall inherit. Though the portions were proportioned by numbers, the particular lands were settled by lot, so that at last the matter must be settled by God's providence, with which all must be satisfied. — Matthew Henry

56 COMMENTARY: According to the lot shall the possession thereof be divided between many and few. The lot overruled human prudence in the final assignment: equity fixed the size, providence fixed the place, and both together were God's division. — Matthew Henry

57 COMMENTARY: And these are they that were numbered of the Levites after their families: of Gershon, the family of the Gershonites, of Kohath, the family of the Kohathites, of Merari, the family of the Merarites. Henry notes Levi was God's tribe, therefore it was not numbered with the rest, but alone, and it came not under the sentence that none should enter Canaan except Caleb and Joshua. — Matthew Henry

59 COMMENTARY: And the name of Amram's wife was Jochebed, daughter of Levi, whom her mother bore to Levi in Egypt, and she bore to Amram Aaron, Moses, and Miriam. The Levite register reaches back to Egypt for its most famous family: Levi's own daughter, born in Egypt, mother of the three leaders of the exodus. — Matthew Henry

60 COMMENTARY: And to Aaron was born Nadab, and Abihu, Eleazar, and Ithamar. Aaron's four sons are named in the register, two of whom would die and two of whom would carry the priesthood forward into the land. — Matthew Henry

61 COMMENTARY: And Nadab and Abihu died, when they offered strange fire before the Lord. The register remembers the strange fire as it remembered Korah: the priesthood's history included its own judgments, recorded beside its numbers. — Matthew Henry

62 COMMENTARY: And those numbered of them were twenty and three thousand, all males from a month old and upward, for they were not numbered among Israel, because there was no inheritance given them among Israel. The Levites numbered twenty-three thousand, counted from a month old as before, and still without land inheritance, since their inheritance was the Lord's service and its tithes. — Matthew Henry

63 COMMENTARY: These are they that were numbered by Moses and Eleazar the priest, who numbered Israel in the plains of Moab by Jordan near Jericho. The second census, like the first, was taken by named men in a named place, and its authority rested on Moses and Eleazar together. — Matthew Henry

64 COMMENTARY: But among these there was not a man of them whom Moses and Aaron numbered, when they numbered Israel in the wilderness of Sinai. Henry notes the execution of the sentence on the murmurers is observable here: not one man now numbered had been numbered then, except Caleb and Joshua. — Matthew Henry

65 COMMENTARY: For the Lord had said of them, they shall surely die in the wilderness, and there was not left a man of them, save Caleb son of Jephunneh, and Joshua son of Nun. Henry closes with God's righteousness in his threatenings and his truth in his promises: death made awful havoc, yet all was appointed in perfect wisdom, justice and truth, and Caleb and Joshua stood as living proof that God's promise outlived his sentence. — Matthew Henry
