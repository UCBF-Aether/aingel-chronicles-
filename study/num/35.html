Study Notes — Numbers

Chapter 35

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

2 COMMENTARY: Command Israel, that they give to the Levites of the inheritance of their possession cities to dwell in, and you shall give also to the Levites suburbs for the cities round about them. Henry notes the cities of the priests and Levites were not only to accommodate them, but to place them as religious teachers in several parts of the land, for though the tabernacle's typical service was in one place only, the preaching of God's word and prayer and praise were not thus confined, and each tribe thus made a grateful acknowledgement to God while gaining Levites dwelling among them to teach the knowledge of the Lord, so that no parts of the country were left to sit in darkness. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: And the cities shall they have to dwell in, and the suburbs of them shall be for their cattle, and for their goods, and for all their beasts. The suburbs gave the Levites their livelihood beside their dwellings, pasture and room for cattle, goods and beasts, so that God's ministers were freed from distracting cares and left at leisure for their station's duties. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: And the suburbs of the cities, which you shall give to the Levites, shall reach from the wall of the city and outward a thousand cubits round about. The suburbs were measured from the city wall outward, a thousand cubits round about, a fixed belt of pasture around every Levite city. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: And you shall measure from without the city on the east side two thousand cubits, and on the south side two thousand cubits, and on the west side two thousand cubits, and on the north side two thousand cubits, and the city shall be in the midst: this shall be to them the suburbs of the cities. The fuller measure, two thousand cubits on each side with the city in the midst, marked the suburbs as a square of pasture centred on the city, not a vague common. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: And among the cities which you shall give to the Levites there shall be six cities for refuge, which you shall appoint for the manslayer, that he may flee thither, and to them you shall add forty and two cities. Six of the Levite cities were set apart as cities of refuge for the manslayer, with forty-two more cities added to them, so that refuge and teaching went together across the land. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: So all the cities which you shall give to the Levites shall be forty and eight cities: them shall you give with their suburbs. The Levite cities totalled forty-eight with their suburbs, a network of teaching cities spread through every tribe's inheritance. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: And the cities which you shall give shall be of the possession of Israel: from them that have many you shall give many, but from them that have few you shall give few: every one shall give of his cities to the Levites according to his inheritance which he inherits. The cities were given in proportion: many from tribes that had many, few from tribes that had few, each according to the inheritance it possessed, as the gospel provides that he who is taught in the word should communicate to him that teaches in all good things. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: Speak to Israel, and say to them, when you be come over Jordan into the land of Canaan. The refuge law was given for the land across Jordan, to take effect once Israel dwelt in Canaan. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: Then you shall appoint you cities to be cities of refuge for you, that the slayer may flee thither, which kills any person unawares. Henry notes there were six cities of refuge, one or other reachable in less than a day's journey from any part of the land, to which manslayers might flee and be safe until they had a fair trial. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: And they shall be to you cities for refuge from the avenger, that the manslayer die not, until he stand before the congregation in judgment. Henry notes that to show plainly the abhorrence of murder and provide for the murderer's punishment, the nearest relation of the deceased, under the title of avenger or redeemer of blood, might in notorious cases pursue and execute vengeance, but the city of refuge held the slayer safe until he stood before the congregation in judgment. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: And of these cities which you shall give six cities shall you have for refuge. The number was fixed at six, enough that refuge lay within a day's journey anywhere in the land. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: You shall give three cities on this side Jordan, and three cities shall you give in the land of Canaan, which shall be cities of refuge. Three cities stood east of Jordan and three in Canaan itself, so that both sides of the river had refuge within reach. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: These six cities shall be a refuge, both for Israel, and for the stranger, and for the sojourner among them, that every one that kills any person unawares may flee thither. The refuge was for all: Israelite, stranger and sojourner alike, and Henry adds the city was a refuge for all, as in the gospel there is no respect of persons. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: And if he smite him with an instrument of iron, so that he die, he is a murderer: the murderer shall surely be put to death. Henry notes the distinction is not between sudden anger and malice aforethought, both of which are murder, but between intentionally striking a man with any weapon likely to cause death and an unintentional blow, and an iron instrument deliberately used was murder. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: And if he smite him with throwing a stone, wherewith he may die, and he die, he is a murderer: the murderer shall surely be put to death. A thrown stone, wherewith a man may die, deliberately cast to kill, was murder like the iron weapon. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: Or if he smite him with a hand weapon of wood, wherewith he may die, and he die, he is a murderer: the murderer shall surely be put to death. A hand weapon of wood, wherewith a man may die, likewise marked the striker as a murderer when death followed. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: The revenger of blood himself shall slay the murderer: when he meets him, he shall slay him. The avenger of blood himself executed the murderer when he met him, the nearest relation acting as the law's hand in notorious cases. — Matthew Henry

20 COMMENTARY: But if he thrust him of hatred, or hurl at him by laying of wait, that he die. A thrust of hatred or a hurled missile by laying in wait was murder, the intention proven by hatred and ambush. — Matthew Henry

21 COMMENTARY: Or in enmity smite him with his hand, that he die: he that smote him shall surely be put to death, for he is a murderer: the revenger of blood shall slay the murderer, when he meets him. A blow in enmity, struck by hand unto death, was murder, and the avenger might slay the murderer on meeting him. — Matthew Henry

22 COMMENTARY: But if he thrust him suddenly without enmity, or have cast upon him any thing without laying of wait. The opposite case opened the refuge: a sudden thrust without enmity, or a cast without laying in wait, was not murder but manslaughter. — Matthew Henry

23 COMMENTARY: Or with any stone, wherewith a man may die, seeing him not, and cast it upon him, that he die, and was not his enemy, neither sought his harm. A stone cast without seeing the victim, by one who was not his enemy and sought no harm, was the unintentional blow to which alone the city of refuge afforded protection. — Matthew Henry

24 COMMENTARY: Then the congregation shall judge between the slayer and the revenger of blood according to these judgments. The congregation judged between slayer and avenger according to these judgments, so that neither private vengeance nor private flight decided the case, but a fair trial. — Matthew Henry

25 COMMENTARY: And the congregation shall deliver the slayer out of the hand of the revenger of blood, and the congregation shall restore him to the city of his refuge, whither he was fled, and he shall abide in it to the death of the high priest, which was anointed with the holy oil. Henry notes that if acquitted, the slayer was protected from the avenger, yet must continue within the city's bounds until the high priest's death, and thus we are reminded that the death of the great High Priest is the only means whereby sins are pardoned and sinners set at liberty. — Matthew Henry

26 COMMENTARY: But if the slayer shall at any time come without the border of the city of his refuge, whither he was fled. Leaving the city's border at any time forfeited the protection, since the refuge's safety ended at its bounds. — Matthew Henry

27 COMMENTARY: And the revenger of blood find him without the borders of the city of his refuge, and the revenger of blood kill the slayer, he shall not be guilty of blood. An avenger who found and killed the slayer outside the refuge's borders was not guilty of blood, since the slayer had left the protection himself. — Matthew Henry

28 COMMENTARY: Because he should have remained in the city of his refuge until the death of the high priest: but after the death of the high priest the slayer shall return into the land of his possession. The high priest's death released the slayer to return to his own possession, the limit of his exile fixed by another man's death, as Henry's type points to the great High Priest's death setting sinners at liberty. — Matthew Henry

29 COMMENTARY: So these things shall be for a statute of judgment to you throughout your generations in all your dwellings. The refuge law stood as a statute of judgment throughout generations and in all dwellings, not a temporary camp rule. — Matthew Henry

30 COMMENTARY: Whoso kills any person, the murderer shall be put to death by the mouth of witnesses: but one witness shall not testify against any person to cause him to die. A murderer died by the mouth of witnesses, but one witness alone could not testify to cause death, guarding the death sentence against a single voice. — Matthew Henry

31 COMMENTARY: Moreover you shall take no satisfaction for the life of a murderer, which is guilty of death: but he shall be surely put to death. No ransom could be taken for a murderer's life: guilt of death meant death, and money could not buy it off. — Matthew Henry

32 COMMENTARY: And you shall take no satisfaction for him that is fled to the city of his refuge, that he should come again to dwell in the land, until the death of the priest. No ransom could buy an early return from refuge either: the manslayer stayed until the priest's death, and no payment shortened the term. — Matthew Henry

33 COMMENTARY: So you shall not pollute the land wherein you are: for blood it defiles the land: and the land cannot be cleansed of the blood that is shed therein, but by the blood of him that shed it. Henry notes murder in all its forms and under all disguises pollutes a land, and blood defiles the land so that only the shedder's blood can cleanse it. — Matthew Henry

34 COMMENTARY: Defile not therefore the land which you shall inhabit, wherein I dwell: for I the Lord dwell among the children of Israel. The final reason was God's own dwelling: the Lord dwelt among Israel, and the land he inhabited must not be defiled. — Matthew Henry

No note for verse(s) 1, 9 — the public-domain sources consulted give no distinct explanation for these verses beyond the list itself, so nothing was invented for them.
