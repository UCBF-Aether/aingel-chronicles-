Study Notes — Malachi

Chapter 1

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: The burden of the word of the LORD to Israel by Malachi, for Malachi was the last of the prophets, reproving priests and people for their evil practices and inviting them to repentance, with promises of blessings at the coming of the Messiah. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: I have loved you, saith the LORD, yet ye say, Wherein hast thou loved us, for all advantages, outward or spiritual, come from the free love of God, yet we are all prone to undervalue his mercies and excuse our own offences. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: Was not Esau Jacob's brother, saith the LORD, yet I loved Jacob, and I hated Esau, and laid his mountains and his heritage waste for the dragons of the wilderness, for God chose his people that they might be holy, and if we love him, it is because he has first loved us. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: Whereas Edom saith, We are impoverished, but we will return and build the desolate places, thus saith the LORD of hosts, They shall build, but I will throw down, and they shall call them, The border of wickedness, and, The people against whom the LORD hath indignation for ever, for all the evils sinners feel and fear are the just recompense of their crimes, while all their hopes and comforts are from unmerited mercy. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: And your eyes shall see, and ye shall say, The LORD will be magnified from the border of Israel, for God's judgments on Edom and mercies to Israel would both display his greatness. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: A son honoureth his father, and a servant his master, he said; if then I be a father, where is mine honour, and if I be a master, where is my fear, saith the LORD of hosts unto you, O priests, that despise my name, and ye say, Wherein have we despised thy name, for our relation to God as Father and Master strongly obliges us to fear and honour him, and we may each charge upon ourselves what is here charged upon the priests. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: Ye offer polluted bread upon mine altar, he said, and ye say, Wherein have we polluted thee, in that ye say, The table of the LORD is contemptible, for those who live in careless neglect of holy ordinances, attending without reverence and going away unconcerned, in effect say the table of the Lord is contemptible. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: And if ye offer the blind for sacrifice, is it not evil, he asked, and if ye offer the lame and sick, is it not evil, for if we worship ignorantly we bring the blind, if carelessly and coldly we bring the sick, if resting in bodily exercise without heart-work we bring the lame, and if we suffer vain thoughts to lodge within us we bring the torn, and is not this evil, a great affront to God and injury to our own souls. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: And now, I pray you, beseech God that he will be gracious unto us, he said in irony; this hath been by your means, shall he regard your persons, saith the LORD of hosts, for sinners ruin themselves by trying to baffle their convictions, and a worship offered in contempt cannot expect acceptance. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: Who is there even among you that would shut the doors for nought, he asked, neither do ye kindle fire on mine altar for nought; I have no pleasure in you, saith the LORD of hosts, neither will I accept an offering at your hand, for they grudged the expense, thinking all thrown away which did not turn to their profit, and understood not the sacrifices as shadowing forth the unblemished Lamb of God. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: For from the rising of the sun even unto the going down of the same my name shall be great among the Gentiles, he said, and in every place incense shall be offered unto my name, and a pure offering, for a spiritual worship shall be established, incense signifying prayer and praise, and when true worshippers worshipped the Father in spirit and in truth, then this pure offering was offered. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: But ye have profaned it, he said, in that ye say, The table of the LORD is polluted, and the fruit thereof, even his meat, is contemptible, for they despised God's name in what they did. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: Ye said also, Behold, what a weariness is it, he said, and ye have snuffed at it, saith the LORD of hosts, and ye brought that which was torn, and the lame, and the sick, thus ye brought an offering; should I accept this of your hand, saith the LORD, for in order to acceptance, it is not enough to do what is good in matter, but we must do it from a right principle, in a right manner, and for a right end. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: But cursed be the deceiver, he said, which hath in his flock a male, and voweth, and sacrificeth unto the Lord a corrupt thing, for the LORD is a great King, and my name is dreadful among the heathen, for if there be a willing mind it will be accepted though defective, but if any be a deceiver, devoting his best to Satan and his lusts, he is under a curse, and our constant mercies make worse our slothfulness and niggardliness in returns of duty. — Matthew Henry
