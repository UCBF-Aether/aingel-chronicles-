Study Notes — Wisdom of Solomon

Chapter 1

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Love righteousness, you judges of the earth, and think of the Lord with a good heart, seeking him in simplicity. Haydock notes the book's opening truth, pressed through the first five chapters, is that God must be approached with just sentiments and without duplicity. — George Leo Haydock

2 COMMENTARY: He will be found by those who do not tempt him, and shows himself to those who do not distrust him. Haydock's reading is that a sincere, undoubting heart is the condition on which God reveals himself. — George Leo Haydock

3 COMMENTARY: Froward thoughts separate from God, and his power, when tried, reproves the unwise. Haydock observes that such thoughts are not mere folly; tested power exposes them. — George Leo Haydock

4 COMMENTARY: Wisdom will not enter a malicious soul, nor dwell in a body subject to sin. Haydock notes soul and body are so joined that the defilement of one banishes wisdom from the other. — George Leo Haydock

5 COMMENTARY: The holy spirit of discipline flees deceit, removes from unwise thoughts, and is put to confusion at approaching iniquity. Haydock calls this spirit the instructive light of the Holy Ghost, which abandons those who deny God or live wickedly. — George Leo Haydock

6 COMMENTARY: Wisdom's spirit is loving to man, yet will not acquit a blasphemer, for God witnesses his reins and heart and hears his tongue. Haydock stresses that the same spirit which instructs will not acquit the guilty. — George Leo Haydock

7 COMMENTARY: The Spirit of the Lord fills the world, and what contains all things knows every voice. Haydock notes that this Spirit, having made and filled all things, must be perfectly acquainted with every action. — George Leo Haydock

8 COMMENTARY: Therefore he who speaks unrighteous things cannot be hidden, nor shall vengeance pass him by. Haydock's point is that the all-containing Spirit makes secret sin an impossibility. — George Leo Haydock

9 COMMENTARY: Inquisition shall be made into the ungodly man's counsels, and his words shall come to God for correction of his iniquities. Haydock reads this as the judgment in which words themselves are brought forward as evidence. — George Leo Haydock

10 COMMENTARY: God's ear of jealousy hears all things, and no muttering escapes it. Haydock notes that God, strong and jealous, examines even the smallest deviation from rectitude. — George Leo Haydock

11 COMMENTARY: Beware then of murmuring, which is unprofitable, and refrain the tongue from backbiting, for no secret speech goes unpunished. Haydock's warning is that murmuring, though whispered, is heard and judged. — George Leo Haydock

12 COMMENTARY: Do not seek death by the error of your life, nor draw destruction by your own hands. Haydock warns against eagerly seeking one's own ruin by an evil life, and against blaming death as if sin were necessity; God made man to be immortal. — George Leo Haydock

13 COMMENTARY: God did not make death, nor does he delight in the destruction of the living. Haydock's point is that death entered by sin, not by God's making. — George Leo Haydock

14 COMMENTARY: God created all things that they might have their being, and the generations of the world are healthful, with no poison of destruction in them. Haydock notes creation was pronounced good, and that poison and wild beasts harm man only after sin, while Christ is the antidote. — George Leo Haydock

15 COMMENTARY: Righteousness is immortal. Haydock treats this as the chapter's sum: the righteous life partakes of the immortality God intended. — George Leo Haydock

16 COMMENTARY: The ungodly, with hands and words, call death to themselves, deem it a friend, and make a covenant with it. Haydock notes they strive to draw upon themselves the second death, refusing repentance. — George Leo Haydock
