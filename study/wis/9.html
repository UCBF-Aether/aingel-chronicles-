Study Notes — Wisdom of Solomon

Chapter 9

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: O God of my fathers, and Lord of mercy, who made all things with your word. Haydock's prayer begins from creation by the word, the ground of asking wisdom. — George Leo Haydock

2 COMMENTARY: And ordained man through your wisdom, that he should have dominion over the creatures you made. Haydock notes man's dominion is ordained through wisdom, not by mere strength. — George Leo Haydock

3 COMMENTARY: And order the world according to equity and righteousness, and execute judgment with an upright heart. Haydock observes that ordering the world aright requires equity, righteousness, and an upright heart. — George Leo Haydock

4 COMMENTARY: Give me wisdom, who sits by your throne, and reject me not among your children. Haydock's petition is to sit, as it were, beside God's throne, and not to be rejected from sonship. — George Leo Haydock

5 COMMENTARY: For I am your servant, and son of your handmaid, a weak man and of a short time, and too young to understand judgment and laws. Haydock notes the petitioner pleads weakness, shortness of life, and youth as reasons for the gift. — George Leo Haydock

6 COMMENTARY: For though a man is never so perfect among the children of men, yet if your wisdom is not with him, he shall be nothing regarded. Haydock's point is that perfection without wisdom counts for nothing. — George Leo Haydock

7 COMMENTARY: You have chosen me to be king of your people, and a judge of your sons and daughters. Haydock observes the office itself creates the need: a king and judge must have wisdom. — George Leo Haydock

8 COMMENTARY: You commanded me to build a temple upon your holy mount, and an altar in the city of your dwelling, a resemblance of the holy tabernacle you prepared from the beginning. Haydock notes the temple is built after a heavenly pattern. — George Leo Haydock

9 COMMENTARY: With you is wisdom, who knows your works and was present when you made the world, and knew what was acceptable in your sight and right in your commandments. Haydock stresses wisdom's presence at creation and her knowledge of God's will. — George Leo Haydock

10 COMMENTARY: Send her out of your holy heavens, and from the throne of your glory, that being present she may labour with me, that I may know what is pleasing to you. Haydock's petition is for wisdom to be sent down to labour alongside the king. — George Leo Haydock

11 COMMENTARY: She knows and understands all things, and shall lead me soberly in my doings, and preserve me in her power. Haydock notes wisdom both guides soberly and preserves by her power. — George Leo Haydock

12 COMMENTARY: So shall my works be acceptable, and I shall judge your people righteously, and be worthy to sit in my father's seat. Haydock observes that acceptable works and righteous judgment follow wisdom's guidance. — George Leo Haydock

13 COMMENTARY: For what man is he that can know God's counsel, or who can think what the Lord's will is? Haydock's question is rhetorical: no man by himself knows God's counsel. — George Leo Haydock

14 COMMENTARY: The thoughts of mortal men are miserable, and our devices are but uncertain. Haydock notes human thought is miserable and devices uncertain without wisdom. — George Leo Haydock

15 COMMENTARY: The corruptible body presses down the soul, and the earthly tabernacle weighs down the mind that muses upon many things. Haydock's image is weight: body and tabernacle press down the musing mind. — George Leo Haydock

16 COMMENTARY: We can hardly guess at things on earth, and with labour find things before us; but things in heaven, who has searched out? Haydock observes that even earthly things are hard, and heavenly things beyond searching without God. — George Leo Haydock

17 COMMENTARY: Your counsel, who has known, except you give wisdom and send your Holy Spirit from above? Haydock's point is that counsel is known only by the gift of wisdom and the Holy Spirit. — George Leo Haydock

18 COMMENTARY: So the ways of those on earth were reformed, and men were taught the things pleasing to you, and were saved through wisdom. Haydock closes the prayer: reformation, teaching, and salvation all come through wisdom. — George Leo Haydock
