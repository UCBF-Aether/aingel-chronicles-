Study Notes — Wisdom of Solomon

Chapter 6

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Hear therefore, O kings, and understand; learn, you judges of the ends of the earth. Haydock notes the address turns to rulers: wisdom is first a lesson for those who judge. — George Leo Haydock

2 COMMENTARY: Give ear, you who rule the people and glory in the multitude of nations. Haydock observes that glorying in multitudes is no substitute for understanding. — George Leo Haydock

3 COMMENTARY: Power is given you of the Lord, and sovereignty from the Highest, who shall try your works and search out your counsels. Haydock stresses that royal power is derived, and will itself be tried. — George Leo Haydock

4 COMMENTARY: Because, being ministers of his kingdom, you have not judged aright, nor kept the law, nor walked after God's counsel, therefore he shall come upon you terribly and swiftly. Haydock's warning is that ministers of a kingdom are judged by the kingdom's law. — George Leo Haydock

5 COMMENTARY: A sharp judgment shall be to those in high places, for mercy will soon pardon the meanest, but the mighty shall be mightily tormented. Haydock notes the proportion: high place brings sharper judgment. — George Leo Haydock

6 COMMENTARY: God, Lord over all, shall not fear any man's greatness, nor respect any person's greatness, for he made small and great alike and cares for all alike. Haydock stresses God's impartiality: he made both, and regards both. — George Leo Haydock

7 COMMENTARY: The mighty are mightily examined. Haydock's point is that examination matches station. — George Leo Haydock

8 COMMENTARY: To you therefore, O kings, are my words, that you may learn wisdom and not fall away. Haydock reads the address as instruction, not flattery: learn, lest you fall. — George Leo Haydock

9 COMMENTARY: They who keep holy things holily shall be judged holy, and they who learn them shall find a defence. Haydock observes that keeping and learning holy things is itself a defence at judgment. — George Leo Haydock

10 COMMENTARY: Desire therefore my words, long for them, and you shall be instructed. Haydock notes desire is the beginning of instruction. — George Leo Haydock

11 COMMENTARY: Wisdom is glorious and never fades away, and is easily seen by those who love her, and found by those who seek her. Haydock's point is that wisdom, unlike earthly glory, does not fade. — George Leo Haydock

12 COMMENTARY: She prevents those who desire her, making herself first known to them. Haydock observes that wisdom meets the seeker halfway, coming before he finds her. — George Leo Haydock

13 COMMENTARY: Whoever seeks her early shall have no great travail, for he shall find her sitting at his doors. Haydock notes the nearness: wisdom waits at the door, not at the ends of the earth. — George Leo Haydock

14 COMMENTARY: To think upon her is perfection of wisdom, and whoever watches for her shall quickly be without care. Haydock stresses that meditation on wisdom is itself wisdom's perfection. — George Leo Haydock

15 COMMENTARY: She goes about seeking those worthy of her, shows herself favourably to them in the ways, and meets them in every thought. Haydock pictures wisdom as the seeker, finding the worthy in their daily ways. — George Leo Haydock

16 COMMENTARY: The very true beginning of her is the desire of discipline, and care of discipline is love. Haydock notes the chain: desire leads to discipline, discipline to love. — George Leo Haydock

17 COMMENTARY: Love is the keeping of her laws, and giving heed to her laws is the assurance of incorruption. Haydock observes that love proves itself by keeping laws, and keeping them assures incorruption. — George Leo Haydock

18 COMMENTARY: Incorruption makes us near to God. Haydock's point is that the incorruptible life draws near to the incorruptible God. — George Leo Haydock

19 COMMENTARY: Therefore the desire of wisdom brings to a kingdom. Haydock notes the end of the chain: desire of wisdom ends in a kingdom. — George Leo Haydock

20 COMMENTARY: If your delight is in thrones and sceptres, O kings of the people, honour wisdom, that you may reign for evermore. Haydock's counsel to kings is that lasting reign comes by honouring wisdom, not by holding sceptres. — George Leo Haydock

21 COMMENTARY: I will tell you what wisdom is, and from whence she came; I will not hide mysteries from you, but will seek her out from the beginning of her birth. Haydock observes the author's promise: wisdom's origin will be told openly, not hidden. — George Leo Haydock

22 COMMENTARY: I will not go with consuming envy, for such a man shall have no fellowship with wisdom. Haydock notes envy and wisdom cannot dwell together. — George Leo Haydock

23 COMMENTARY: A multitude of wise men is the welfare of the world, and a wise king is the upholding of the people. Haydock's political maxim is that wise counsel is a people's welfare. — George Leo Haydock

24 COMMENTARY: Receive therefore instruction through my words, and it shall do you good. Haydock closes the address with the practical end: instruction received does good. — George Leo Haydock
