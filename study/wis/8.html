Study Notes — Wisdom of Solomon

Chapter 8

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Wisdom reaches from one end to another mightily, and sweetly orders all things. Haydock notes wisdom's two qualities together: might in reach, sweetness in ordering. — George Leo Haydock

2 COMMENTARY: I loved her and sought her from my youth, desired to make her my spouse, and was a lover of her beauty. Haydock reads this as Solomon's lifelong courtship of wisdom. — George Leo Haydock

3 COMMENTARY: In that she is conversant with God, she magnifies her nobility; the Lord of all things himself loved her. Haydock observes that converse with God is wisdom's nobility. — George Leo Haydock

4 COMMENTARY: She is privy to God's mysteries and a chooser of his works. Haydock notes wisdom knows God's secrets and chooses among his works. — George Leo Haydock

5 COMMENTARY: If riches are a possession to be desired in this life, what is richer than wisdom, who works all things? Haydock's argument is that wisdom, worker of all, is richer than any possession. — George Leo Haydock

6 COMMENTARY: If prudence works, who of all that are is a more cunning workman than she? Haydock observes that prudence itself is wisdom's workmanship. — George Leo Haydock

7 COMMENTARY: If a man loves righteousness, her labours are virtues, for she teaches temperance and prudence, justice and fortitude, than which men can have nothing more profitable in life. Haydock notes the four virtues are wisdom's labours. — George Leo Haydock

8 COMMENTARY: If a man desires much experience, she knows things of old and conjectures aright what is to come, understands subtilties of speeches and solutions of dark sentences, foresees signs and wonders and events of seasons and times. Haydock observes that wisdom spans past, future, and the riddles of speech. — George Leo Haydock

9 COMMENTARY: Therefore I purposed to take her to me to live with me, knowing she would be a counsellor of good things and a comfort in cares and grief. Haydock reads this as a marriage purpose: wisdom as counsellor and comforter. — George Leo Haydock

10 COMMENTARY: For her sake I shall have estimation among the multitude, and honour with the elders, though I am young. Haydock notes wisdom gives a young man standing among elders. — George Leo Haydock

11 COMMENTARY: I shall be found of a quick conceit in judgment, and shall be admired in the sight of great men. Haydock observes that wisdom quickens judgment and wins admiration. — George Leo Haydock

12 COMMENTARY: When I hold my tongue, they shall bide my leisure, and when I speak, they shall give good ear to me; if I talk much, they shall lay their hands upon their mouth. Haydock pictures the authority wisdom gives in council: silence awaited, speech heeded. — George Leo Haydock

13 COMMENTARY: Moreover, by means of her I shall obtain immortality, and leave an everlasting memorial to those who come after me. Haydock's point is that wisdom's memorial outlasts the king. — George Leo Haydock

14 COMMENTARY: I shall set the people in order, and nations shall be subject to me. Haydock notes ordering a people is wisdom's political fruit. — George Leo Haydock

15 COMMENTARY: Horrible tyrants shall be afraid when they hear of me; I shall be found good among the multitude, and valiant in war. Haydock observes that wisdom makes a king both good in peace and valiant in war. — George Leo Haydock

16 COMMENTARY: After I return into my house, I will repose myself with her, for her conversation has no bitterness, and to live with her has no sorrow, but mirth and joy. Haydock's domestic image is rest with wisdom, whose company is without bitterness. — George Leo Haydock

17 COMMENTARY: Now when I considered these things in myself, and pondered them in my heart, that to be allied to wisdom is immortality. Haydock notes the pondering itself leads to the conclusion: alliance with wisdom is immortality. — George Leo Haydock

18 COMMENTARY: Great pleasure it is to have her friendship, and in the works of her hands are infinite riches, and in the exercise of conference with her, prudence, and in talking with her, a good report; I went about seeking how to take her to me. Haydock lists friendship's gains: pleasure, riches, prudence, good report, and the seeking that pursues them. — George Leo Haydock

19 COMMENTARY: I was a witty child and had a good spirit. Haydock notes natural gifts are acknowledged, yet they are not enough without seeking. — George Leo Haydock

20 COMMENTARY: Yea rather, being good, I came into a body undefiled. Haydock's point is that goodness preceded, not followed, his bodily life, on the author's view. — George Leo Haydock

21 COMMENTARY: Nevertheless, when I perceived that I could not otherwise obtain her, except God gave her me, and that it was a point of wisdom also to know whose gift she was, I prayed to the Lord and besought him with my whole heart. Haydock's turn is decisive: wisdom is God's gift, and knowing whose gift she is leads to prayer, which opens chapter 9. — George Leo Haydock
