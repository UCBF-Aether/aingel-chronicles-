Study Notes — Wisdom of Solomon

Chapter 18

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Nevertheless your saints had a very great light, whose voice they hearing and not seeing their shape, because they also had not suffered the same things, they counted them happy. Haydock notes Israel had great light, and hearing Egypt's voice without seeing shape, counted them happy for not suffering the same. — George Leo Haydock

2 COMMENTARY: For that they did not hurt them now, of whom they had been wronged before, they thanked them and besought pardon for that they had been enemies. Haydock observes Israel thanked Egypt for not hurting them now, and sought pardon for former enmity. — George Leo Haydock

3 COMMENTARY: Instead thereof you gave them a burning pillar of fire, to be a guide of their unknown journey and a harmless sun to entertain them honourably. Haydock notes the pillar: guide for an unknown journey, harmless sun for honourable entertainment. — George Leo Haydock

4 COMMENTARY: They were worthy to be deprived of light and imprisoned in darkness, who had kept your sons shut up, by whom the incorruptible light of the law was to be given to the world. Haydock's justice is exact: those who shut up the law's bearers are shut up in darkness. — George Leo Haydock

5 COMMENTARY: When they had determined to slay the babes of the saints, one child being cast forth and saved, to reprove them you took away the multitude of their children, and destroyed them altogether in a mighty water. Haydock notes the reproof: one child saved, a multitude of theirs taken by water. — George Leo Haydock

6 COMMENTARY: That night was known beforehand to our fathers, that assuredly knowing what oaths they had believed, they might be of good cheer. Haydock observes the night was foreknown to the fathers, so that known oaths gave them cheer. — George Leo Haydock

7 COMMENTARY: So your people received the salvation of the righteous and destruction of the enemies. Haydock notes one night brings both: salvation and destruction. — George Leo Haydock

8 COMMENTARY: As you punished the adversaries, so you glorified us whom you called. Haydock's correspondence is that punishment and glorification are one act's two sides. — George Leo Haydock

9 COMMENTARY: The good children of good men offered sacrifices secretly, and with one consent made a holy law, that the saints should be like partakers of the same good and evil, the fathers now singing out the songs of praise. Haydock notes the secret Passover: one law, shared good and evil, fathers singing praise. — George Leo Haydock

10 COMMENTARY: The enemies, on the other side, made a cry, and the sound of lamentation was heard, and the wailing for dead children was heard on every side. Haydock contrasts praise with lamentation for dead children. — George Leo Haydock

11 COMMENTARY: The same punishment befell servant and master, and the king suffered the same as the common person. Haydock stresses equality in punishment: servant, master, and king alike. — George Leo Haydock

12 COMMENTARY: All died together, and the number of deaths could not be counted, for the living were not sufficient to bury them. Haydock notes the dead outnumbered the living buriers. — George Leo Haydock

13 COMMENTARY: For all that they would not believe before by their enchantments, upon the destruction of the firstborn they confessed this people to be God's son. Haydock observes enchantments failed to persuade, but the firstborn's destruction forced confession. — George Leo Haydock

14 COMMENTARY: While all things were in quiet silence, and the night in the midst of her swift course. Haydock's stillness precedes the stroke: quiet silence, night at mid-course. — George Leo Haydock

15 COMMENTARY: Your Almighty word leaped down from heaven out of your royal throne, as a fierce man of war into the midst of a land of destruction. Haydock pictures the Almighty word leaping as a warrior into a doomed land. — George Leo Haydock

16 COMMENTARY: It brought an unfeigned commandment as a sharp sword, and standing up filled all things with death, touching heaven yet walking on earth. Haydock notes the sword touches heaven and walks earth, filling all with death. — George Leo Haydock

17 COMMENTARY: Then suddenly visions of horrible dreams troubled them sore, and fears came upon them unlooked for. Haydock observes dreams and unlooked-for fears precede the stroke. — George Leo Haydock

18 COMMENTARY: Half dead with fear, they confessed the cause of their death. Haydock notes the half-dead confess the cause. — George Leo Haydock

19 COMMENTARY: The dreams that troubled them foreshowed this, lest they should perish without knowing why they were afflicted. Haydock's point is that foreknowledge by dreams leaves no excuse of ignorance. — George Leo Haydock

20 COMMENTARY: The tasting of death touched the righteous also, and there was a disturbance in the wilderness, but the wrath endured not long. Haydock notes death touched the righteous too, but wrath did not endure long. — George Leo Haydock

21 COMMENTARY: For the blameless man made haste and stood forth to defend them, bringing the shield of his proper ministry, prayer and propitiation of incense, and set himself against the wrath, putting an end to the calamity, showing he was your servant. Haydock reads Aaron's intercession: prayer and incense as shield, ending calamity. — George Leo Haydock

22 COMMENTARY: He overcame the destroyer, not with strength of body nor force of arms, but by the word subdued him who punished, alleging the oaths and covenants made with the fathers. Haydock stresses the victory was by word and covenant, not by arms. — George Leo Haydock

23 COMMENTARY: When the dead were already fallen in heaps one upon another, he stood in the midst and cut off the wrath, parting the way to the living. Haydock pictures Aaron standing between dead and living, cutting off wrath. — George Leo Haydock

24 COMMENTARY: In the long garment was the whole world, and in the four rows of stones was the glory of the fathers, and your Majesty upon the diadem of his head. Haydock notes the high priest's garment bears the world, the fathers' glory, and God's Majesty. — George Leo Haydock

25 COMMENTARY: To these the destroyer gave place, and was afraid of them, for the trial of wrath sufficed. Haydock closes: the destroyer gives place, for wrath's trial sufficed. — George Leo Haydock
