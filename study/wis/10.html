Study Notes — Wisdom of Solomon

Chapter 10

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: She preserved the first formed father of the world, created alone, and brought him out of his fall. Haydock notes wisdom's first work: preserving Adam and raising him from his fall. — George Leo Haydock

2 COMMENTARY: And gave him power to rule all things. Haydock observes that dominion, lost by the fall, is restored in part through wisdom. — George Leo Haydock

3 COMMENTARY: But when the unrighteous went away from her in anger, he perished in the fury with which he murdered his brother. Haydock's contrast is Cain, who left wisdom and perished in fratricide's fury. — George Leo Haydock

4 COMMENTARY: When the earth was drowned because of him, wisdom again preserved it, and directed the course of the righteous in a piece of wood of small value. Haydock notes the ark: a small piece of wood, directed by wisdom, preserves the world. — George Leo Haydock

5 COMMENTARY: Moreover, when nations were confounded in wicked consent, she knew the righteous and preserved him blameless to God, and kept him strong against his tender compassion toward his son. Haydock reads this as Abraham, kept blameless amid confounded nations and strong in the trial of his son. — George Leo Haydock

6 COMMENTARY: She delivered the righteous man who fled from fire descending on the five cities, whose wickedness is witnessed by smoking land, plants bearing fruit that never ripens, and a standing pillar of salt as a monument of an unbelieving soul. Haydock notes Lot's deliverance and the smoking land as witness. — George Leo Haydock

7 COMMENTARY: The five cities' wickedness left a smoking land, unripe fruit, and a salt pillar for an unbelieving soul. Haydock observes the monuments remain as warnings. — George Leo Haydock

8 COMMENTARY: For regarding not wisdom, they got not only this hurt, that they knew not good things, but also left a memorial of their foolishness to the world, so their faults could not pass unnoticed. Haydock's point is double hurt: ignorance of good, and a lasting memorial of folly. — George Leo Haydock

9 COMMENTARY: Wisdom delivers from pain those who attend to her. Haydock's summary is that attendance on wisdom is deliverance. — George Leo Haydock

10 COMMENTARY: When the righteous fled from his brother's wrath, she guided him in right paths, showed him God's kingdom, and gave him knowledge of holy things, making him rich in labours and fruitful. Haydock reads this as Jacob, guided in flight and enriched in labour. — George Leo Haydock

11 COMMENTARY: In the covetousness of those who oppressed him, she stood by him and made him rich. Haydock notes wisdom stood by Jacob against oppressors and made him rich. — George Leo Haydock

12 COMMENTARY: She defended him from enemies and kept him safe from those who lay in wait, and in a sore conflict gave him victory, that he might know godliness is stronger than all. Haydock observes the victory's lesson: godliness is stronger than all. — George Leo Haydock

13 COMMENTARY: When the righteous was sold, she forsook him not, but delivered him from sin; she went down with him into the pit, and left him not in bonds, till she brought him the sceptre of the kingdom and power against those who oppressed him. Haydock reads this as Joseph, accompanied into pit and prison until the sceptre. — George Leo Haydock

14 COMMENTARY: She delivered him from those who devised against him and made liars of his accusers. Haydock notes wisdom's vindication: accusers proved liars. — George Leo Haydock

15 COMMENTARY: She delivered the holy people and blameless seed from a nation that oppressed them. Haydock's turn is to Israel in Egypt, delivered as a holy people. — George Leo Haydock

16 COMMENTARY: She entered into the soul of the Lord's servant, and withstood dreadful kings with wonders and signs. Haydock notes wisdom entered Moses and withstood Pharaoh with wonders. — George Leo Haydock

17 COMMENTARY: She rendered to the righteous the reward of their labours, guided them in a marvellous way, and was a cover by day and a light of stars in the night. Haydock pictures the Exodus guidance: cover by day, starlight by night. — George Leo Haydock

18 COMMENTARY: She brought them through the Red Sea and led them through much water. Haydock's point is the sea passage itself, led by wisdom. — George Leo Haydock

19 COMMENTARY: But she drowned their enemies, and cast them up out of the sea's bottom. Haydock notes the reversal: the same waters that bore Israel drowned Egypt. — George Leo Haydock

20 COMMENTARY: Therefore the righteous spoiled the ungodly, and praised your holy name, O Lord, and magnified with one accord your victorious hand. Haydock observes that spoil turns to praise of God's victorious hand. — George Leo Haydock

21 COMMENTARY: Wisdom opened the mouth of the dumb, and made the tongues of children eloquent. Haydock closes the chapter: wisdom gives speech to the dumb and eloquence to children. — George Leo Haydock
