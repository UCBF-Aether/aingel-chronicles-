Study Notes — Wisdom of Solomon

Chapter 4

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Better it is to have no children and to have virtue, for virtue's memorial is immortal, known with God and men. Haydock observes that a virtuous memory outlives any lineage. — George Leo Haydock

2 COMMENTARY: When virtue is present, men take example at it, and when gone they desire it; it wears a crown and triumphs for ever, winning undefiled rewards. Haydock notes virtue's crown is not of this world only. — George Leo Haydock

3 COMMENTARY: The multiplying brood of the ungodly shall not thrive, nor take deep root from bastard slips, nor lay any fast foundation. Haydock's point is that a line rooted in sin cannot root deeply. — George Leo Haydock

4 COMMENTARY: Though branches flourish for a time, being not firm they shall be shaken by wind, and plucked up by violent winds. Haydock observes that apparent flourishing without firmness ends in uprooting. — George Leo Haydock

5 COMMENTARY: The imperfect branches shall be broken, their fruit unprofitable, sour to eat, and fit for nothing. Haydock notes fruit that never ripens is the image of such a brood. — George Leo Haydock

6 COMMENTARY: Children begotten of unlawful beds are witnesses of wickedness against their parents in their trial. Haydock reads the children themselves as standing evidence at the parents' judgment. — George Leo Haydock

7 COMMENTARY: The righteous, though he die early, shall be in rest. Haydock stresses that rest, not length of days, is the righteous man's portion. — George Leo Haydock

8 COMMENTARY: Honourable age is not length of time nor number of years. Haydock's point is that age is measured by wisdom, not by years. — George Leo Haydock

9 COMMENTARY: A man's understanding is grey hairs, and an unspotted life is old age. Haydock observes that wisdom and an undefiled life are the true marks of age. — George Leo Haydock

10 COMMENTARY: The righteous pleased God and was loved, and living among sinners was taken away. Haydock notes that pleasing God, not pleasing men, is what counts, and removal may itself be mercy. — George Leo Haydock

11 COMMENTARY: He was taken away speedily, lest wickedness alter his understanding or deceit beguile his soul. Haydock's reading is that early death can be protection, not punishment. — George Leo Haydock

12 COMMENTARY: The bewitching of naughtiness obscures things that are honest, and the wandering of concupiscence undermines the simple mind. Haydock warns that fascination with evil darkens honest judgment. — George Leo Haydock

13 COMMENTARY: He, being made perfect in a short time, fulfilled a long time. Haydock observes that perfection is not measured by duration. — George Leo Haydock

14 COMMENTARY: His soul pleased the Lord, therefore he hastened him out of the midst of wickedness, though peoples saw and understood it not. Haydock notes the peoples' failure to understand is itself part of the mystery. — George Leo Haydock

15 COMMENTARY: God's grace and mercy are with his saints, and he has respect to his chosen. Haydock stresses that the chosen are not forgotten in their removal. — George Leo Haydock

16 COMMENTARY: The righteous who dies condemns the ungodly who live, and youth soon perfected condemns the ungodly man's many years. Haydock's point is that a short righteous life is a standing judgment on long unrighteous ones. — George Leo Haydock

17 COMMENTARY: The ungodly shall see the wise man's end and not understand what God decreed for him, nor why the Lord kept him safe. Haydock notes they see the end but miss its meaning. — George Leo Haydock

18 COMMENTARY: They shall see him and despise him, but the Lord shall laugh them to scorn. Haydock observes that contempt for the righteous returns upon the despisers. — George Leo Haydock

19 COMMENTARY: They shall be a fall, dishonoured and disgraced for ever among the dead, for God shall break them and cast them headlong speechless, shaking them from the foundations. Haydock's image is total overthrow, from the foundations up. — George Leo Haydock

20 COMMENTARY: At the reckoning of sins they shall come with fear, and their iniquities shall convince them to their face. Haydock notes that at reckoning, their own deeds stand as accusers. — George Leo Haydock
