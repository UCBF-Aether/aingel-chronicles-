Study Notes — Wisdom of Solomon

Chapter 7

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: I myself am also a mortal man, like all others, and the offspring of him first made of the earth. Haydock notes Solomon, whose sayings are recorded here, acquired wisdom, and all others may do the same, for he starts from common mortality. — George Leo Haydock

2 COMMENTARY: In my mother's womb I was fashioned to be flesh in ten months, compacted in blood, of the seed of man and the pleasure that came with sleep. Haydock observes that the king's beginnings are as lowly as any man's. — George Leo Haydock

3 COMMENTARY: When I was born, I drew in the common air, fell upon the earth of like nature, and my first voice was crying, as all others do. Haydock's point is that birth levels king and commoner. — George Leo Haydock

4 COMMENTARY: I was nursed in swaddling clothes and with cares. Haydock notes the same nursing and cares attend every infant, royal or not. — George Leo Haydock

5 COMMENTARY: No king had any other beginning of birth. Haydock stresses the universality: kings begin exactly as others do. — George Leo Haydock

6 COMMENTARY: There is one entrance to life for all, and a like going out. Haydock quotes the dying Augustus asking whether he had acted his part well, to show life is a stage before and after which all are equal. — George Leo Haydock

7 COMMENTARY: Wherefore I prayed, and understanding was given me; I called upon God, and wisdom's spirit came to me. Haydock notes that, having no natural advantage, Solomon prayed for the wisdom his office required. — George Leo Haydock

8 COMMENTARY: I preferred her before sceptres and thrones, and esteemed riches nothing in comparison of her. Haydock observes he did not ask for dominions, and that some things said of wisdom refer to uncreated wisdom, others to created. — George Leo Haydock

9 COMMENTARY: Neither compared I to her any precious stone, because all gold in respect of her is as a little sand, and silver shall be counted as clay before her. Haydock's comparison makes wealth mere sand and clay beside wisdom. — George Leo Haydock

10 COMMENTARY: I loved her above health and beauty, and chose to have her instead of light, for the light that comes from her never goes out. Haydock notes wisdom's light, unlike the sun's, never sets. — George Leo Haydock

11 COMMENTARY: All good things together came to me with her, and innumerable riches in her hands. Haydock observes that goods follow wisdom, rather than wisdom following goods. — George Leo Haydock

12 COMMENTARY: I rejoiced in them all, because wisdom goes before them, and I knew not that she was their mother. Haydock's point is that wisdom is the mother of the goods she brings. — George Leo Haydock

13 COMMENTARY: I learned diligently, and communicate her liberally; I do not hide her riches. Haydock notes such disinterestedness deserves imitation by all the learned, who should give what they received. — George Leo Haydock

14 COMMENTARY: For she is a treasure to men that never fails, and those who use it become God's friends, commended by the gifts that come from learning. Haydock observes the wise do not seek glory, but it follows them, and use of the treasure makes friends of God. — George Leo Haydock

15 COMMENTARY: God has granted me to speak as I would, and to conceive what is worthy of his gifts, because he himself guides wisdom and directs the wise. Haydock notes speech must be sincere and sententious, and presumption is far from true wisdom. — George Leo Haydock

16 COMMENTARY: In God's hand are both we and our words, all wisdom also and knowledge of workmanship. Haydock's point is that speaker, speech, and skill are all in God's hand. — George Leo Haydock

17 COMMENTARY: He has given me certain knowledge of the things that are: to know the constitution of the world and the operation of the elements. Haydock notes this knowledge covers things past, present, and to come in their seasons and vicissitudes. — George Leo Haydock

18 COMMENTARY: The beginning, ending, and midst of the times, the alterations of the turning of the sun and change of seasons. Haydock explains the times as the seasons and their changes of heat and cold. — George Leo Haydock

19 COMMENTARY: The circuits of years and positions of stars. Haydock observes that astronomy itself is part of wisdom's gift. — George Leo Haydock

20 COMMENTARY: The natures of living creatures and the furies of wild beasts, the violence of winds and reasonings of men. Haydock notes that knowledge of creatures and of human reasonings alike come from wisdom. — George Leo Haydock

21 COMMENTARY: The diversities of plants and the virtues of roots. Haydock's point is that even plants and roots are within wisdom's teaching. — George Leo Haydock

22 COMMENTARY: All things that are secret or manifest I have learned, for wisdom, the worker of all things, taught me. Haydock notes the epithets of wisdom's spirit are proper to the Holy Ghost, and also applicable to wisdom dwelling in man: one in essence, manifold in operations, subtle, knowing all. — George Leo Haydock

23 COMMENTARY: In her is an understanding spirit, holy, one only, manifold, subtil, lively, clear, undefiled, plain, not subject to hurt, loving the thing that is good, quick, which cannot be letted, ready to do good. Haydock's list describes a spirit active, eloquent to persuade, and open. — George Leo Haydock

24 COMMENTARY: For wisdom is more moving than any motion; she passes and goes through all things by reason of her pureness. Haydock notes wisdom is the same as the Spirit and the Son of God, passing through all things. — George Leo Haydock

25 COMMENTARY: She is the breath of God's power, a pure influence flowing from the Almighty's glory, therefore no defiled thing can fall into her. Haydock stresses wisdom's origin: breath and pure influence from God's glory itself. — George Leo Haydock

26 COMMENTARY: She is the brightness of everlasting light, the unspotted mirror of God's power, and the image of his goodness. Haydock's images are brightness, mirror, and image: wisdom shows God without spot. — George Leo Haydock

27 COMMENTARY: Being but one, she can do all things, and remaining in herself, she makes all things new, and in all ages entering into holy souls, she makes them friends of God and prophets. Haydock notes one wisdom makes all new and makes prophets of holy souls. — George Leo Haydock

28 COMMENTARY: God loves none but him who dwells with wisdom. Haydock's point is exclusive: God's love rests on wisdom's companions. — George Leo Haydock

29 COMMENTARY: She is more beautiful than the sun, and above all the order of stars; compared with light, she is found before it. Haydock observes wisdom outshines sun and stars, and precedes light itself. — George Leo Haydock

30 COMMENTARY: Night succeeds light, but against wisdom no evil can prevail. Haydock's close is that night follows day, but no evil overcomes wisdom. — George Leo Haydock
