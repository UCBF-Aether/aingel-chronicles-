Study Notes — Wisdom of Solomon

Chapter 3

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: The souls of the righteous are in God's hand, and no torment shall touch them. Haydock's reading is that death cannot reach what God himself holds. — George Leo Haydock

2 COMMENTARY: In the sight of the unwise they seemed to die, and their departure was taken for misery. Haydock notes the unwise judge by sight alone, mistaking departure for destruction. — George Leo Haydock

3 COMMENTARY: Their going from us was taken for utter destruction, but they are in peace. Haydock stresses the correction: what looks like ruin is peace. — George Leo Haydock

4 COMMENTARY: Though in men's sight they were punished, their hope is full of immortality. Haydock observes that apparent punishment and real hope stand together. — George Leo Haydock

5 COMMENTARY: Having been a little chastised, they shall be greatly rewarded, for God proved them and found them worthy of himself. Haydock reads chastisement here as proving, not rejection. — George Leo Haydock

6 COMMENTARY: As gold in the furnace he tried them, and received them as a burnt offering. Haydock notes the furnace image: trial purifies rather than consumes. — George Leo Haydock

7 COMMENTARY: In the time of their visitation they shall shine and run to and fro like sparks among stubble. Haydock's point is that visitation, dreaded by the ungodly, becomes the righteous' shining. — George Leo Haydock

8 COMMENTARY: They shall judge nations and have dominion over peoples, and their Lord shall reign for ever. Haydock observes the reversal: the judged become judges. — George Leo Haydock

9 COMMENTARY: Those who put trust in God shall understand truth, and the faithful in love shall abide with him, for grace and mercy belong to his saints. Haydock stresses that abiding with God is the portion of trust and love. — George Leo Haydock

10 COMMENTARY: The ungodly shall be punished according to their imaginations, for they neglected the righteous and forsook the Lord. Haydock notes punishment fits the imagination that devised evil. — George Leo Haydock

11 COMMENTARY: Whoever despises wisdom and nurture is miserable, and their hope is vain, their labours unfruitful, their works unprofitable. Haydock's point is that contempt for wisdom empties every enterprise. — George Leo Haydock

12 COMMENTARY: Their wives are foolish and their children wicked. Haydock reads this as the household fruit of a wisdom despised. — George Leo Haydock

13 COMMENTARY: Their offspring is cursed. Haydock notes the curse follows the parents' rejection of God into the next generation. — George Leo Haydock

14 COMMENTARY: Blessed is the barren that is undefiled, who has not known the sinful bed; she shall have fruit in the visitation of souls. Haydock observes that fruitfulness is counted by faithfulness, not by children. — George Leo Haydock

15 COMMENTARY: The fruit of good labours is glorious, and the root of understanding cannot fail. Haydock stresses that understanding, once rooted, does not wither. — George Leo Haydock

16 COMMENTARY: As for the children of adulterers, they shall not come to perfection, and the seed of an unlawful bed shall be rooted out. Haydock reads this as the cutting off of a line begun in sin. — George Leo Haydock

17 COMMENTARY: Though they live long, they shall be nothing regarded, and their last age shall be without honour. Haydock notes length of life without honour is no blessing. — George Leo Haydock

18 COMMENTARY: If they die quickly, they have no hope, nor comfort in the day of trial. Haydock's point is that a short life leaves the same emptiness when hope is absent. — George Leo Haydock

19 COMMENTARY: The end of the unrighteous generation is dreadful. Haydock sums the chapter: a generation that forsakes wisdom ends in dread, while the righteous, who seemed to die, are in peace. — George Leo Haydock
