Study Notes — Wisdom of Solomon

Chapter 5

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Then the righteous man shall stand in great boldness before those who afflicted him and made no account of his labours. Haydock's scene is the reversal: the afflicted stands, the afflicters answer. — George Leo Haydock

2 COMMENTARY: When they see it, they shall be troubled with terrible fear and amazed at the strangeness of his salvation, so far beyond what they looked for. Haydock notes their amazement: salvation comes in a form they never expected. — George Leo Haydock

3 COMMENTARY: Repenting and groaning for anguish of spirit, they shall say within themselves: this was he whom we had sometimes in derision and a proverb of reproach. Haydock observes that derision turns to recognition too late. — George Leo Haydock

4 COMMENTARY: We fools esteemed his life madness and his end without honour. Haydock's point is that the world's verdict on the righteous is confessed as folly by the world itself. — George Leo Haydock

5 COMMENTARY: How is he numbered among God's children, and his lot among the saints? Haydock notes the question that undoes their former contempt: the despised man is counted a son. — George Leo Haydock

6 COMMENTARY: Therefore we have erred from the way of truth, and the light of righteousness has not shined on us, nor the sun of understanding risen upon us. Haydock reads this as their own confession of the darkness they chose. — George Leo Haydock

7 COMMENTARY: We wearied ourselves in the way of wickedness and destruction, and walked through hard deserts, but the Lord's way we have not known. Haydock observes that the path of wickedness wearies, while the Lord's way was never even sought. — George Leo Haydock

8 COMMENTARY: What has pride profited us, or what good has riches with our vaunting brought us? Haydock notes the profit-and-loss account of pride: nothing remains. — George Leo Haydock

9 COMMENTARY: All those things are passed away like a shadow, and as a post that hastens by. Haydock's image is speed: pride's goods pass like a runner. — George Leo Haydock

10 COMMENTARY: As a ship passing through waves leaves no trace of its way, nor any path in the waves. Haydock notes the ship's wake vanishes, as does the ungodly life. — George Leo Haydock

11 COMMENTARY: As a bird flying through air leaves no token of its way, but the air, beaten by wings and parted by force, is passed through with no sign after. Haydock's point is that even the swiftest, proudest passage leaves nothing behind. — George Leo Haydock

12 COMMENTARY: As an arrow shot at a mark parts the air, which immediately closes, so that its passage is unknown. Haydock observes the arrow's path closes behind it, as the ungodly life closes without trace. — George Leo Haydock

13 COMMENTARY: So we, as soon as we were born, began to draw to our end, and had no sign of virtue to show. Haydock notes the sum: a life that leaves no sign of virtue. — George Leo Haydock

14 COMMENTARY: The hope of the ungodly is like dust blown away with wind, like thin froth driven by a storm, like smoke scattered by wind, and as the remembrance of a guest who tarried but a day. Haydock's four images all say the same: such hope does not endure. — George Leo Haydock

15 COMMENTARY: But the righteous live for evermore; their reward is with the Lord, and the Most High cares for them. Haydock's contrast is the chapter's hinge: the perishing hope against the living righteous. — George Leo Haydock

16 COMMENTARY: Therefore they shall receive a glorious kingdom and a beautiful crown from the Lord's hand, for with his right hand he shall cover them, and with his arm protect them. Haydock notes the crown and covering are God's own gift and defence. — George Leo Haydock

17 COMMENTARY: He shall take his zeal as complete armour and arm the creature for revenge on his enemies. Haydock pictures God arming creation itself as his instrument. — George Leo Haydock

18 COMMENTARY: He shall put on righteousness as a breastplate, and true judgment instead of a helmet. Haydock observes that God's armour is moral: righteousness and judgment. — George Leo Haydock

19 COMMENTARY: He shall take holiness for an invincible shield. Haydock notes holiness as defence that cannot be pierced. — George Leo Haydock

20 COMMENTARY: His severe wrath shall he sharpen for a sword, and the world shall fight with him against the unwise. Haydock's point is that creation joins its Maker against the unwise. — George Leo Haydock

21 COMMENTARY: Then shall rightly aimed thunderbolts go abroad, and from the clouds, as from a well-drawn bow, they shall fly to the mark. Haydock notes judgment's aim is unerring. — George Leo Haydock

22 COMMENTARY: Hailstones full of wrath shall be cast as out of a stone bow, the sea shall rage against them, and floods shall cruelly drown them. Haydock pictures sea and flood as fellow-combatants in judgment. — George Leo Haydock

23 COMMENTARY: A mighty wind shall stand up against them, and like a storm shall carry them away; thus iniquity shall lay waste the whole earth, and ill-dealing shall overthrow the thrones of the mighty. Haydock's close is that iniquity wastes the earth it ruled. — George Leo Haydock
