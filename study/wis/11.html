Study Notes — Wisdom of Solomon

Chapter 11

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: She prospered their works in the hand of the holy prophet. Haydock notes wisdom prospered Israel's works through Moses, the holy prophet. — George Leo Haydock

2 COMMENTARY: They went through a wilderness not inhabited, and pitched tents where there lay no way. Haydock observes the wilderness had no road, yet they were led through it. — George Leo Haydock

3 COMMENTARY: They stood against their enemies and were avenged of their adversaries. Haydock's point is that wisdom gave them standing against enemies, not mere flight. — George Leo Haydock

4 COMMENTARY: When they were thirsty, they called upon you, and water was given from the flinty rock, and thirst quenched out of the hard stone. Haydock notes the rock's water answers thirst with a miracle of provision. — George Leo Haydock

5 COMMENTARY: For by the things by which their enemies were punished, by the same they were benefited in their need. Haydock's key to chapters 11-19 is this correspondence: the same instrument punishes Egypt and benefits Israel. — George Leo Haydock

6 COMMENTARY: Instead of a perpetual running river troubled with foul blood, their enemies were punished, yet Israel was given water in abundance. Haydock contrasts the Nile turned to blood with Israel's abundant water. — George Leo Haydock

7 COMMENTARY: For a manifest reproof of the command to kill infants, you gave them abundant water unlooked for. Haydock reads the water as reproof: Egypt, which killed infants, is judged by water, while Israel is refreshed by it. — George Leo Haydock

8 COMMENTARY: Declaring by thirst at that time how you had punished their adversaries. Haydock notes thirst itself declared the punishment: Egypt's need exposed its judgment. — George Leo Haydock

9 COMMENTARY: When they were tried, though chastened in mercy, they understood how the ungodly were judged in wrath and tormented. Haydock observes Israel's trial was merciful chastening, teaching them the difference from wrathful judgment. — George Leo Haydock

10 COMMENTARY: You tried the one as a father admonishing, and the other as a severe king condemning. Haydock's contrast is father against king: admonition against condemnation. — George Leo Haydock

11 COMMENTARY: Whether absent or present, they were vexed alike. Haydock notes Egypt's torment followed them whether Israel was near or far. — George Leo Haydock

12 COMMENTARY: For a double grief came upon them, and groaning for remembrance of things past. Haydock's point is double grief: present pain plus remembrance of what they had done. — George Leo Haydock

13 COMMENTARY: When they heard by their own punishments that others were benefited, they perceived the Lord. Haydock observes that seeing Israel benefited by their punishments taught Egypt to perceive the Lord. — George Leo Haydock

14 COMMENTARY: For whom they had before in derision, and rejected with scorn, him in the end they admired, after suffering thirst unlike the righteous'. Haydock notes the reversal: the derided Moses is admired in the end. — George Leo Haydock

15 COMMENTARY: For the foolish devices of their wickedness, by which they were deceived to worship brute beasts and creeping things, you sent a multitude of brute beasts to punish them. Haydock's correspondence is exact: beast-worshippers are punished by beasts. — George Leo Haydock

16 COMMENTARY: That they might know that by the things by which a man sins, by the same also he shall be punished. Haydock states the book's rule of judgment: the instrument of sin becomes the instrument of punishment. — George Leo Haydock

17 COMMENTARY: Your Almighty hand, which made the world from formless matter, did not lack means to send a multitude of bears or fierce lions, or unknown wild beasts full of rage, or breathing fire, or sending smoke, or shooting terrible sparks from their eyes. Haydock stresses God's hand lacks no means, and lists the beasts he could have sent. — George Leo Haydock

18 COMMENTARY: Even without these they might have fallen with one blast, being persecuted by your judgment and scattered by your spirit's breath. Haydock notes God needed no army; one blast of judgment would suffice. — George Leo Haydock

19 COMMENTARY: But you ordered all things in measure, number, and weight. Haydock's famous line is that even judgment is ordered, measured, numbered, and weighed. — George Leo Haydock

20 COMMENTARY: You can always show great strength when you will; who can withstand your arm's power? Haydock observes that power is always at hand, yet exercised in measure. — George Leo Haydock

21 COMMENTARY: The whole world before you is as a little grain of the balance, as a drop of morning dew falling on earth. Haydock's image is littleness: the world weighs as a grain and falls as a dew drop. — George Leo Haydock

22 COMMENTARY: You have mercy on all, for you can do all things, and wink at men's sins, that they should amend. Haydock notes mercy is grounded in power: because God can do all, he can afford to wait for amendment. — George Leo Haydock

23 COMMENTARY: You love all things that are, and abhor nothing you made, for you would never have made anything if you had hated it. Haydock stresses that existence itself is a sign of love. — George Leo Haydock

24 COMMENTARY: How could anything have endured if it had not been your will, or been preserved if not called by you? Haydock's argument is that continuance depends on God's will alone. — George Leo Haydock

25 COMMENTARY: You spare all, for they are yours, O Lord, lover of souls. Haydock closes with the name: Lord, lover of souls, who spares what is his. — George Leo Haydock

26 COMMENTARY: You spare all, for they are yours, O Lord, lover of souls. Haydock's close to the chapter is the name itself: the Lord who spares is the lover of souls, and what is his he does not destroy lightly. — George Leo Haydock
