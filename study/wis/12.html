Study Notes — Wisdom of Solomon

Chapter 12

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Your incorruptible Spirit is in all things. Haydock notes the Spirit's presence in all things is the ground of God's patient dealings. — George Leo Haydock

2 COMMENTARY: Therefore you chasten offenders by little and little, warning them by reminding them wherein they offended, that leaving wickedness they may believe in you, O Lord. Haydock stresses gradual chastening aims at belief, not destruction. — George Leo Haydock

3 COMMENTARY: It was your will to destroy by our fathers' hands the old inhabitants of your holy land. Haydock observes that the Canaanites' destruction was God's will executed through Israel. — George Leo Haydock

4 COMMENTARY: Whom you hated for doing most odious works of witchcraft and wicked sacrifices. Haydock notes the cause: witchcraft and wicked sacrifices, not mere possession of land. — George Leo Haydock

5 COMMENTARY: And also those merciless murderers of children, devourers of man's flesh, and feasts of blood. Haydock lists the abominations: child murder, cannibal feasts, blood rites. — George Leo Haydock

6 COMMENTARY: With the priests out of the midst of their idolatrous crew, and the parents who killed with their own hands souls destitute of help. Haydock observes priests and parents alike shared the guilt. — George Leo Haydock

7 COMMENTARY: You determined to destroy them by our fathers' hands, that the land dearest to you might receive a worthy colony of God's children. Haydock's purpose is a worthy colony in a land dear to God. — George Leo Haydock

8 COMMENTARY: Nevertheless, even those you spared as men, sending wasps as forerunners of your host to destroy them by little and little. Haydock notes even the doomed were spared as men for a time, and wasps went before as gradual destroyers. — George Leo Haydock

9 COMMENTARY: Not that you were unable to bring the ungodly under the righteous in battle, or to destroy them at once with cruel beasts or one rough word. Haydock stresses God lacked no swifter means; gradualness was choice, not weakness. — George Leo Haydock

10 COMMENTARY: But judging them by little and little, you gave them place of repentance, not being ignorant that they were a naughty generation, and that their malice was bred in them, and their cogitation would never be changed. Haydock observes that place for repentance was given even to a generation known to be incorrigible. — George Leo Haydock

11 COMMENTARY: For it was a cursed seed from the beginning, neither did you pardon them for sins out of fear of any man. Haydock notes the seed was cursed from the beginning, and God feared no man in judging it. — George Leo Haydock

12 COMMENTARY: Who shall say, what have you done? Or who shall withstand your judgment? Or who shall accuse you for the nations that perish, which you made? Or who shall come to stand against you, to be revenged for the unrighteous men? Haydock's questions are unanswerable: no one can arraign God's judgment. — George Leo Haydock

13 COMMENTARY: There is no God but you, who care for all, that you might show you have not judged unjustly. Haydock stresses that care for all is the proof that judgment is just. — George Leo Haydock

14 COMMENTARY: Neither shall king nor tyrant be able to set his face against you for any whom you have punished. Haydock observes that no royal power can confront God's punishment. — George Leo Haydock

15 COMMENTARY: As you are just, you order all things justly, and condemn him who does not deserve punishment as unworthy of your power. Haydock's point is that justice restrains power: God will not condemn the undeserving. — George Leo Haydock

16 COMMENTARY: Your power is the beginning of righteousness, and because you are Lord of all, you make yourself gracious to all. Haydock notes power is righteousness's beginning, and lordship over all issues in graciousness to all. — George Leo Haydock

17 COMMENTARY: You show your power when men will not believe you to be of perfect power, and you rebuke the boldness of those who know it. Haydock observes power is shown both to unbelievers and to the bold who know it. — George Leo Haydock

18 COMMENTARY: But you, mastering your power, judge with equity, and order us with great favour, for you may use power when you will. Haydock stresses mastery of power: equity and favour, not mere force. — George Leo Haydock

19 COMMENTARY: By such works you taught your people that the just man should be merciful, and made your children to be of good hope that you give repentance for sins. Haydock's lesson is that God's own works teach mercy and hope of repentance. — George Leo Haydock

20 COMMENTARY: If you punished your people's enemies with such care and indulgence, giving them time and place to deliver themselves from wickedness, with how great circumspection did you judge your sons, to whose fathers you gave oaths and covenants of good promises? Haydock argues from the less to the greater: if enemies got time, how much more sons under covenant? — George Leo Haydock

21 COMMENTARY: Therefore, whereas you chasten us, you scourge our enemies a thousand times more, to the intent that when we judge, we should carefully think of your goodness, and when we ourselves are judged, we should look for mercy. Haydock notes the proportion teaches both careful judgment of others and hope of mercy for ourselves. — George Leo Haydock

22 COMMENTARY: Wherefore, whereas men have lived dissolutely and unrighteously, you tormented them with their own abominations. Haydock's rule returns: men are tormented by their own abominations. — George Leo Haydock

23 COMMENTARY: For they went astray very far in the ways of error, and held them for gods who among beasts are most esteemed, living like children without reason. Haydock observes the depth of error: beasts esteemed as gods, and men living unreasonably. — George Leo Haydock

24 COMMENTARY: They went astray very far in the ways of error, and held for gods those beasts which even their enemies despised, deceived as children without understanding. Haydock notes the depth of error: gods chosen from beasts their own enemies despised. — George Leo Haydock

25 COMMENTARY: Therefore you sent a judgment to mock them, as to children without the use of reason. Haydock notes judgment mocks them as children, fitting their childish error. — George Leo Haydock

26 COMMENTARY: For they who in their punishment did not consider that they were punished by the very things they worshipped, shall see and be condemned. Haydock's point is that punishment by their own gods should have taught them, and failure to learn condemns. — George Leo Haydock

27 COMMENTARY: For what they grudged when punished, that is, the things they thought to be gods, being now punished in them, they saw and acknowledged him to be the true God whom before they denied, and therefore extreme damnation came upon them. Haydock closes: the worshipped thing becomes the punishment, and the denied God is known at last. — George Leo Haydock
