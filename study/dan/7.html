Study Notes — Daniel

Chapter 7

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: In the first year of Belshazzar king of Babylon Daniel had a dream and visions of his head upon his bed; then he wrote the dream, and told the sum of the matters. This vision contains the same prophetic representations as Nebuchadnezzar's dream, Henry says, now shown under new figures. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

2 COMMENTARY: Daniel spoke: I saw in my vision by night, and behold, the four winds of the heaven strove upon the great sea. The great sea agitated by the winds, Henry explains, represented the earth and its dwellers troubled by ambitious princes and conquerors. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

3 COMMENTARY: And four great beasts came up from the sea, diverse one from another. The four beasts signified the same four empires as the four parts of Nebuchadnezzar's image, Henry says. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

4 COMMENTARY: The first was like a lion, and had eagle's wings; I beheld till the wings thereof were plucked, and it was lifted up from the earth, and made stand upon the feet as a man, and a man's heart was given to it. Mighty conquerors are but instruments of God's vengeance on a guilty world, Henry notes, and the savage beast represents the hateful features of their characters. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

5 COMMENTARY: And behold another beast, a second, like to a bear, and it raised up itself on one side, and it had three ribs in the mouth of it between the teeth of it, and they said thus unto it: Arise, devour much flesh. The second beast is the second empire in the series, Henry's reading shows, diverse from the first. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

6 COMMENTARY: After this I beheld, and lo another, like a leopard, which had upon the back of it four wings of a fowl; the beast had also four heads, and dominion was given to it. The dominion given to each has a limit, Henry stresses: their wrath shall be made to praise the Lord, and the remainder of it he will restrain. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

7 COMMENTARY: After this I saw in the night visions, and behold a fourth beast, dreadful and terrible, and strong exceedingly, and it had great iron teeth; it devoured and broke in pieces, and stamped the residue with the feet of it, and it was diverse from all the beasts that were before it, and it had ten horns. The fourth beast, Henry's outline shows, is the dreadful kingdom whose interpretation Daniel most desired. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

8 COMMENTARY: I considered the horns, and behold, there came up among them another little horn, before whom there were three of the first horns plucked up by the roots, and behold, in this horn were eyes like the eyes of man, and a mouth speaking great things. He especially desired to know respecting the little horn, Henry notes, which made war with the saints and prevailed against them. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

9 COMMENTARY: I beheld till the thrones were cast down, and the Ancient of days did sit, whose garment was white as snow, and the hair of his head like the pure wool; his throne was like the fiery flame, and his wheels as burning fire. These verses are for the comfort and support of God's people, Henry says, in reference to the persecutions that would come upon them. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

10 COMMENTARY: A fiery stream issued and came forth from before him; thousand thousands ministered unto him, and ten thousand times ten thousand stood before him; the judgment was set, and the books were opened. Many New Testament predictions of the judgment to come, Henry notes, have plain allusion to this vision, especially Revelation 20:11-12. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

11 COMMENTARY: I beheld then because of the voice of the great words which the horn spoke; I beheld even till the beast was slain, and his body destroyed, and given to the burning flame. The great event foretold, Henry says, is Christ's glorious coming, to destroy every antichristian power. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

12 COMMENTARY: As concerning the rest of the beasts, they had their dominion taken away, yet their lives were prolonged for a season and time. Each dominion had its limit, Henry stresses, and was taken away in its season. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

13 COMMENTARY: I saw in the night visions, and behold, one like the Son of man came with the clouds of heaven, and came to the Ancient of days, and they brought him near before him. The Messiah is here called the Son of man, Henry says: he was made in the likeness of sinful flesh and found in fashion as a man, but he is the Son of God. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

14 COMMENTARY: And there was given him dominion, and glory, and a kingdom, that all people, nations, and languages, should serve him; his dominion is an everlasting dominion, which shall not pass away, and his kingdom that which shall not be destroyed. Before the end shall come, Henry says, the Father will openly give to his incarnate Son, our Mediator and Judge, the inheritance of the nations as his willing subjects. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

15 COMMENTARY: I Daniel was grieved in my spirit in the midst of my body, and the visions of my head troubled me. It is desirable to obtain the right and full sense of what we see and hear from God, Henry says, and those that would know must ask by faithful and fervent prayer. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

16 COMMENTARY: I came near unto one of them that stood by, and asked him the truth of all this. So he told me, and made me know the interpretation of the things. The angel told Daniel plainly, Henry notes, when he asked in earnest. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

17 COMMENTARY: These great beasts, which are four, are four kings, which shall arise out of the earth. The angel's plain answer, Henry says, fixes the beasts as four kings arising out of the earth. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

18 COMMENTARY: But the saints of the most High shall take the kingdom, and possess the kingdom for ever, even for ever and ever. The saints of the Most High shall possess the kingdom for ever, Henry stresses, and Daniel had a joyful prospect of the prevalence of God's kingdom among men. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

19 COMMENTARY: Then I would know the truth of the fourth beast, which was diverse from all the others, exceeding dreadful, whose teeth were of iron, and his nails of brass, which devoured, broke in pieces, and stamped the residue with his feet. Daniel especially desired to know the fourth beast, Henry notes, diverse and dreadful above the rest. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

20 COMMENTARY: And of the ten horns that were in his head, and of the other which came up, and before whom three fell, even of that horn that had eyes, and a mouth that spoke very great things, whose look was more stout than his fellows. Here is foretold, Henry says, the rage of papal Rome against true Christians, and St. John in his visions has plain reference to these visions. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

21 COMMENTARY: I beheld, and the same horn made war with the saints, and prevailed against them. The little horn's war against the saints, Henry notes, is the persecution Daniel's vision chiefly comforts God's people under. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

22 COMMENTARY: Until the Ancient of days came, and judgment was given to the saints of the most High, and the time came that the saints possessed the kingdom. This refers to the second coming of our blessed Lord, Henry says, when the saints shall triumph in the complete fall of Satan's kingdom. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

23 COMMENTARY: Thus he said: The fourth beast shall be the fourth kingdom upon earth, which shall be diverse from all kingdoms, and shall devour the whole earth, and shall tread it down, and break it in pieces. The fourth kingdom's diversity and destructiveness, Henry's reading shows, mark it out from all the rest. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

24 COMMENTARY: And the ten horns out of this kingdom are ten kings that shall arise, and another shall rise after them, and he shall be diverse from the first, and he shall subdue three kings. The ten horns are ten kings, Henry notes, with another rising after them to subdue three. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

25 COMMENTARY: And he shall speak great words against the most High, and shall wear out the saints of the most High, and think to change times and laws, and they shall be given into his hand until a time and times and the dividing of time. The horn's war is limited, Henry stresses: a time, times, and dividing of time, and no longer. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

26 COMMENTARY: But the judgment shall sit, and they shall take away his dominion, to consume and to destroy it unto the end. The judgment shall sit, Henry says, and the horn's dominion shall be consumed and destroyed unto the end. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

27 COMMENTARY: And the kingdom and dominion, and the greatness of the kingdom under the whole heaven, shall be given to the people of the saints of the most High, whose kingdom is an everlasting kingdom, and all dominions shall serve and obey him. It promises that the gospel kingdom shall be set up, Henry says: a kingdom of light, holiness, and love, whose privileges are the earnest of the kingdom of glory. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

28 COMMENTARY: Hitherto is the end of the matter. As for me Daniel, my cogitations much troubled me, and my countenance changed in me, but I kept the matter in my heart. Far be it from us to infer from hence that dominion is founded on grace, Henry cautions; the full accomplishment will be in the everlasting happiness of the saints, the kingdom that cannot be moved. — Matthew Henry (Concise)
