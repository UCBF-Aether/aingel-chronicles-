Study Notes — Daniel

Chapter 6

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: It pleased Darius to set over the kingdom a hundred and twenty princes, which should be over the whole kingdom. We notice to the glory of God, Henry says, that though Daniel was now very old, yet he was able for business, and had continued faithful to his religion. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

2 COMMENTARY: And over these three presidents, of whom Daniel was first, that the princes might give accounts unto them, and the king should have no damage. Daniel's high place, Henry's account shows, was earned by faithful service under successive kings. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

3 COMMENTARY: Then this Daniel was preferred above the presidents and princes, because an excellent spirit was in him, and the king thought to set him over the whole realm. It is for the glory of God, Henry says, when those who profess religion conduct themselves so that their most watchful enemies may find no occasion for blaming them. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

4 COMMENTARY: Then the presidents and princes sought to find occasion against Daniel concerning the kingdom, but they could find none occasion nor fault, forasmuch as he was faithful, neither was there any error or fault found in him. Their most watchful enemies found no occasion, Henry notes, save only in the matters of his God, in which he walked according to his conscience. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

5 COMMENTARY: Then said these men: We shall not find any occasion against this Daniel, except we find it against him concerning the law of his God. The enemies' own confession, Henry's reading shows, was a tribute to Daniel's blameless life. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

6 COMMENTARY: Then these presidents and princes assembled together to the king, and said: King Darius, live for ever. Persecuting laws are always made on false pretences, Henry warns, but it does not become Christians to make bitter complaints or indulge in revilings. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

7 COMMENTARY: All the presidents, governors, princes, counsellors, and captains have consulted together to establish a royal statute, and to make a firm decree, that whosoever shall ask a petition of any god or man for thirty days, save of thee, O king, he shall be cast into the den of lions. To forbid prayer for thirty days, Henry says, is for so long to rob God of all the tribute he has from man, and to rob man of all the comfort he has in God. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

8 COMMENTARY: Now, O king, establish the decree, and sign the writing, that it be not changed, according to the law of the Medes and Persians, which alters not. The decree's unchangeableness, Henry's account shows, was the trap's hinge. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

9 COMMENTARY: Wherefore king Darius signed the writing and the decree. Through want of due thought, Henry notes, we often do that which afterwards, like Darius, we see cause a thousand times to wish undone again. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

10 COMMENTARY: Now when Daniel knew that the writing was signed, he went into his house, and his windows being open in his chamber toward Jerusalem, he kneeled upon his knees three times a day, and prayed, and gave thanks before his God, as he did aforetime. Daniel prayed openly and avowedly, Henry says, and though a man of vast business, he did not think that would excuse him from daily exercises of devotion. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

11 COMMENTARY: Then these men assembled, and found Daniel praying and making supplication before his God. It is good to have hours for prayer, Henry says, and Daniel kept his hours even under the decree. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

12 COMMENTARY: Then they came near, and spoke before the king concerning the king's decree: Hast thou not signed a decree, that every man that shall ask a petition of any god or man within thirty days, save of thee, O king, shall be cast into the den of lions? The king answered: The thing is true, according to the law of the Medes and Persians, which alters not. It is no new thing, Henry notes, for what is done faithfully in conscience toward God to be misrepresented as done obstinately and in contempt of the civil powers. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

13 COMMENTARY: Then answered they: That Daniel, which is of the children of the captivity of Judah, regards not thee, O king, nor the decree that thou hast signed, but makes his petition three times a day. In trying times, Henry warns, we must take heed lest, under pretence of discretion, we are guilty of cowardice in the cause of God. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

14 COMMENTARY: Then the king, when he heard these words, was sore displeased with himself, and set his heart on Daniel to deliver him, and he laboured till the going down of the sun to deliver him. The king saw cause a thousand times to wish the decree undone, Henry says, but could find no lawful way out. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

15 COMMENTARY: Then these men assembled unto the king, and said: Know, O king, that the law of the Medes and Persians is, that no decree nor statute which the king establishes may be changed. The enemies pressed the unchangeable law, Henry's account shows, to close the king's last escape. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

16 COMMENTARY: Then the king commanded, and they brought Daniel, and cast him into the den of lions. Now the king spoke unto Daniel: Thy God whom thou servest continually, he will deliver thee. Daniel, that venerable man, Henry says, is brought as the vilest of malefactors, and thrown into the den of lions, only for worshipping his God. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

17 COMMENTARY: And a stone was brought, and laid upon the mouth of the den, and the king sealed it with his own signet, and with the signet of his lords, that the purpose might not be changed concerning Daniel. The placing of the stone was ordered by providence, Henry notes, that the miracle of Daniel's deliverance might appear more plain. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

18 COMMENTARY: Then the king went to his palace, and passed the night fasting, neither were instruments of music brought before him, and his sleep went from him. The best way to have a good night, Henry says, is to keep a good conscience. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

19 COMMENTARY: Then the king arose very early in the morning, and went in haste unto the den of lions. The king's haste at dawn, Henry's account shows, betrayed both his doubt and his hope. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

20 COMMENTARY: And when he came to the den, he cried with a lamentable voice unto Daniel: O Daniel, servant of the living God, is thy God, whom thou servest continually, able to deliver thee from the lions? We are sure of what the king doubted, Henry says, that the servants of the living God have a Master well able to protect them. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

21 COMMENTARY: Then said Daniel unto the king: O king, live for ever. Daniel's calm greeting from the den, Henry implies, already answered the king's question. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

22 COMMENTARY: My God has sent his angel, and has shut the lions' mouths, that they have not hurt me, forasmuch as before him innocency was found in me, and also before thee, O king, have I done no hurt. See the power of God over the fiercest creatures, Henry says, and believe his power to restrain the roaring lion that goes about continually seeking to devour. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

23 COMMENTARY: Then was the king exceedingly glad for him, and commanded that they should take Daniel up out of the den. So Daniel was taken up, and no manner of hurt was found upon him, because he believed in his God. Daniel was kept perfectly safe because he believed in his God, Henry says, and those who boldly and cheerfully trust in God in the way of duty shall always find him a present help. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

24 COMMENTARY: And the king commanded, and they brought those men which had accused Daniel, and they cast them into the den of lions, them, their children, and their wives, and the lions had the mastery of them, and broke all their bones in pieces or ever they came at the bottom of the den. Thus the righteous is delivered out of trouble, Henry says, and the wicked comes in his stead; the short triumph of the wicked will end in their ruin. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

25 COMMENTARY: Then king Darius wrote unto all people, nations, and languages, that dwell in all the earth: Peace be multiplied unto you. If we live in the fear of God and walk according to that rule, Henry says, peace shall be upon us. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

26 COMMENTARY: I make a decree, that in every dominion of my kingdom men tremble and fear before the God of Daniel, for he is the living God, and stedfast for ever, and his kingdom that which shall not be destroyed, and his dominion shall be even unto the end. The kingdom, the power, and the glory, for ever, are the Lord's, Henry says. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

27 COMMENTARY: He delivers and rescues, and he works signs and wonders in heaven and in earth, who has delivered Daniel from the power of the lions. Many are employed in making known his wonderful works to others, Henry warns, who themselves remain strangers to his saving grace. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

28 COMMENTARY: So this Daniel prospered in the reign of Darius, and in the reign of Cyrus the Persian. May we be doers as well as believers of his word, Henry closes, lest at the last we should be found to have deceived ourselves. — Matthew Henry (Concise)
