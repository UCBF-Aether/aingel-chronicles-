Study Notes — Daniel

Chapter 1

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: In the third year of Jehoiakim king of Judah came Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon unto Jerusalem and besieged it. Nebuchadnezzar, in the first year of his reign, took Jerusalem and carried whom and what he pleased away, Henry notes, and from this first captivity most think the seventy years are to be dated. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

2 COMMENTARY: The Lord gave Jehoiakim into Nebuchadnezzar's hand, with part of the vessels of God's house, which he carried into the land of Shinar to the treasure house of his god. The captivity itself, Henry's introduction stresses, sets the scene of a book partly historical and chiefly prophetical, foretelling the four great empires, the Messiah, and the restoration of the Jews. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

3 COMMENTARY: The king spoke to Ashpenaz, master of his eunuchs, to bring certain of the children of Israel, of the king's seed and of the princes. It is the interest of princes to employ wise men, Henry says, and their wisdom to find out and train up such. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

4 COMMENTARY: The youths were to be without blemish, well favoured, skilful in all wisdom, cunning in knowledge, understanding science, and able to stand in the king's palace, to be taught the learning and tongue of the Chaldeans. Nebuchadnezzar ordered that these chosen youths should be taught, Henry notes. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

5 COMMENTARY: The king appointed them a daily provision of his meat and wine, to be nourished three years, that at the end they might stand before the king. Their training was thorough and royal, Henry's account implies, yet it carried the danger of corrupting their principles. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

6 COMMENTARY: Now among these were Daniel, Hananiah, Mishael, and Azariah, of the children of Judah. Daniel was of noble birth, Henry notes, carried captive to Babylon as a youth in B.C. 606. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

7 COMMENTARY: Unto them the prince of the eunuchs gave names: to Daniel, Belteshazzar; to Hananiah, Shadrach; to Mishael, Meshach; and to Azariah, Abednego. All their Hebrew names had something of God in them, Henry says, but to make them forget the God of their fathers, the heathen gave them names that savoured of idolatry. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

8 COMMENTARY: Daniel purposed in his heart that he would not defile himself with the portion of the king's meat, nor with the wine which he drank, and requested of the prince of the eunuchs that he might not defile himself. Daniel was still firm to his religion, Henry says: whatever they called him, he still held fast the spirit of an Israelite. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

9 COMMENTARY: Now God had brought Daniel into favour and tender love with the prince of the eunuchs. The interest we think we make for ourselves, Henry notes, we must acknowledge to be God's gift. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

10 COMMENTARY: The prince of the eunuchs said he feared the king, lest their countenances be worse than the other children and he endanger his head. Daniel avoided defiling himself with sin, Henry says, and we should more fear that than any outward trouble; it is easier to keep temptation at a distance than to resist it when near. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

11 COMMENTARY: Then said Daniel to Melzar, whom the prince had set over Daniel, Hananiah, Mishael, and Azariah: prove thy servants ten days. We cannot better improve our interest with any with whom we have found favour, Henry says, than to use it to keep us from sin. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

12 COMMENTARY: Prove thy servants, I beseech thee, ten days; and let them give us pulse to eat, and water to drink. These youths scrupled concerning the meat lest it should be sinful, Henry notes, and when God's people are in Babylon they need special care that they partake not of her sins. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

13 COMMENTARY: Then let our countenances be looked upon before thee, and the countenance of the children that eat of the king's meat, and as thou seest, deal with thy servants. It is much to the praise of young people, Henry says, not to covet or seek the delights of sense. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

14 COMMENTARY: So he consented to them in this matter, and proved them ten days. Those who would excel in wisdom and piety, Henry says, must learn betimes to keep the body under. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

15 COMMENTARY: At the end of ten days their countenances appeared fairer and fatter in flesh than all the children which did eat the portion of the king's meat. People will not believe the benefit of avoiding excess and of a spare diet, Henry notes, nor how much they contribute to health, unless they try; conscientious temperance will always do more, even for this life's comfort, than sinful indulgence. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

16 COMMENTARY: Thus Melzar took away the portion of their meat and the wine that they should drink, and gave them pulse. The trial proved the point, Henry's account implies, and the youths' course was vindicated without defiance. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

17 COMMENTARY: As for these four children, God gave them knowledge and skill in all learning and wisdom, and Daniel had understanding in all visions and dreams. Daniel and his fellows kept to their religion, Henry says, and God rewarded them with eminence in learning. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

18 COMMENTARY: At the end of the days the king had said he should bring them in, the prince of the eunuchs brought them in before Nebuchadnezzar. Their training ended in presentation to the king, Henry notes, as those fitted for service. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

19 COMMENTARY: The king communed with them, and among them all was found none like Daniel, Hananiah, Mishael, and Azariah; therefore stood they before the king. Pious young persons should endeavour to do better than their fellows in useful things, Henry says, not for the praise of man, but for the honour of the gospel and that they may be qualified for usefulness. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

20 COMMENTARY: In all matters of wisdom and understanding that the king enquired of them, he found them ten times better than all the magicians and astrologers in all his realm. It is well for a country and for a prince's honour, Henry notes, when he can judge who are best fitted to serve him and prefers them on that account. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

21 COMMENTARY: And Daniel continued even unto the first year of king Cyrus. Let young men steadily attend to this chapter, Henry closes, and let all remember that God will honour those who honour him, but those who despise him shall be lightly esteemed. — Matthew Henry (Concise)
