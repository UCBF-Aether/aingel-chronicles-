Study Notes — Daniel

Chapter 8

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: In the third year of the reign of king Belshazzar a vision appeared unto me, even unto me Daniel, after that which appeared unto me at the first. God gives Daniel a foresight of the destruction of other kingdoms, Henry says, which in their day were as powerful as that of Babylon. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

2 COMMENTARY: And I saw in a vision, and it came to pass, when I saw, that I was at Shushan in the palace, which is in the province of Elam, and I saw in a vision, and I was by the river of Ulai. The vision's setting at Shushan, Henry's account implies, placed Daniel at the heart of the coming Persian empire. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

3 COMMENTARY: Then I lifted up mine eyes, and saw, and behold, there stood before the river a ram which had two horns, and the two horns were high, but one was higher than the other, and the higher came up last. The ram with two horns was the second empire, Henry explains, that of Media and Persia. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

4 COMMENTARY: I saw the ram pushing westward, and northward, and southward, so that no beasts might stand before him, neither was there any that could deliver out of his hand, but he did according to his will, and became great. Could we foresee the changes that shall be when we are gone, Henry says, we should be less affected with changes in our own day. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

5 COMMENTARY: And as I was considering, behold, a he-goat came from the west on the face of the whole earth, and touched not the ground, and the goat had a notable horn between his eyes. He saw this ram overcome by a he-goat, Henry says, and this was Alexander the Great. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

6 COMMENTARY: And he came to the ram that had two horns, which I had seen standing before the river, and ran unto him in the fury of his power. The goat's swift fury, Henry's reading shows, pictures Alexander's unstoppable conquest. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

7 COMMENTARY: And I saw him come close unto the ram, and he was moved with choler against him, and smote the ram, and broke his two horns, and there was no power in the ram to stand before him, but he cast him down to the ground, and stamped upon him, and there was none that could deliver the ram out of his hand. The ram's horns broken, Henry notes, mark Persia's fall to Greece. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

8 COMMENTARY: Therefore the he-goat waxed very great, and when he was strong, the great horn was broken, and for it came up four notable ones toward the four winds of heaven. Alexander, when about thirty-three and in his full strength, died, Henry notes, and showed the vanity of worldly pomp and power, which cannot make a man happy. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

9 COMMENTARY: And out of one of them came forth a little horn, which waxed exceeding great, toward the south, and toward the east, and toward the pleasant land. Instead of that one great horn, Henry explains, there came up four notable ones, Alexander's four chief captains, and out of one a little horn became a great persecutor of the church and people of God. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

10 COMMENTARY: And it waxed great, even to the host of heaven, and it cast down some of the host and of the stars to the ground, and stamped upon them. The little horn's growth to the host of heaven, Henry's reading shows, pictures persecution reaching God's own people. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

11 COMMENTARY: Yea, he magnified himself even to the prince of the host, and by him the daily sacrifice was taken away, and the place of his sanctuary was cast down. It is just with God, Henry says, to deprive those of the privileges of his house who despise and profane them, and to make those know the worth of ordinances by the want of them who would not know it by the enjoyment. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

12 COMMENTARY: And a host was given him against the daily sacrifice by reason of transgression, and it cast down the truth to the ground, and it practised, and prospered. The horn prospered, Henry notes, and at one time nearly destroyed the holy religion God's right hand had planted. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

13 COMMENTARY: Then I heard one saint speaking, and another saint said unto that certain saint which spoke: How long shall be the vision concerning the daily sacrifice, and the transgression of desolation, to give both the sanctuary and the host to be trodden under foot? Daniel heard the time of this calamity limited and determined, Henry says, but not the time when it should come. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

14 COMMENTARY: And he said unto me: Unto two thousand and three hundred days, then shall the sanctuary be cleansed. There is much difficulty as to the precise time here stated, Henry admits, but the end of it cannot be very distant, and God will, for his own glory, see to the cleansing of the church in due time. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

15 COMMENTARY: And it came to pass, when I, even I Daniel, had seen the vision, and sought for the meaning, then behold, there stood before me as the appearance of a man. If we would know the mind of God, Henry says, we must apply to Christ, in whom are hid all the treasures of wisdom and knowledge, not hid from us, but hid for us. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

16 COMMENTARY: And I heard a man's voice between the banks of Ulai, which called, and said: Gabriel, make this man to understand the vision. The eternal Son of God stood before the prophet in the appearance of a man, Henry says, and directed the angel Gabriel to explain the vision. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

17 COMMENTARY: So he came near where I stood, and when he came, I was afraid, and fell upon my face, but he said unto me: Understand, O son of man, for at the time of the end shall be the vision. Daniel's fainting and astonishment, Henry notes, at the prospect of evils coming on his people and the church, confirm that long-continued calamities were foretold. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

18 COMMENTARY: Now as he was speaking with me, I was in a deep sleep on my face toward the ground, but he touched me, and set me upright. The angel's touch raising Daniel, Henry's account shows, prepared him to hear the interpretation. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

19 COMMENTARY: And he said: Behold, I will make thee know what shall be in the last end of the indignation, for at the time appointed the end shall be. The vision concerns the last end of the indignation, Henry notes, at the time appointed. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

20 COMMENTARY: The ram which thou sawest having two horns are the kings of Media and Persia. The angel's plain identification, Henry says, fixes the ram as Media and Persia. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

21 COMMENTARY: And the rough goat is the king of Grecia, and the great horn that is between his eyes is the first king. The rough goat is the king of Grecia, Henry explains, and the great horn the first king, Alexander. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

22 COMMENTARY: Now that being broken, whereas four stood up for it, four kingdoms shall stand up out of the nation, but not in his power. While men dispute respecting the death of some prosperous warrior, Henry says, it is plain that the great First Cause had no more of his plan for him to execute, and therefore cut him off. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

23 COMMENTARY: And in the latter time of their kingdom, when the transgressors are come to the full, a king of fierce countenance, and understanding dark sentences, shall stand up. The latter-time king, Henry's reading shows, arises when transgressors are come to the full. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

24 COMMENTARY: And his power shall be mighty, but not by his own power, and he shall destroy wonderfully, and shall prosper, and practise, and shall destroy the mighty and the holy people. The horn's power, Henry notes, is mighty yet not by his own power, and its destruction falls on the holy people. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

25 COMMENTARY: And through his policy also he shall cause craft to prosper in his hand, and he shall magnify himself in his heart, and by peace shall destroy many; he shall also stand up against the Prince of princes, but he shall be broken without hand. Standing against the Prince of princes, Henry's reading shows, ends in being broken without hand. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

26 COMMENTARY: And the vision of the evening and the morning which was told is true; wherefore shut thou up the vision, for it shall be for many days. The vision being ended, Henry notes, a charge was given to Daniel to keep it private for the present. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

27 COMMENTARY: And I Daniel fainted, and was sick certain days; afterward I rose up, and did the king's business, and I was astonished at the vision, but none understood it. He kept it to himself and went on to do the duty of his place, Henry says; as long as we live in this world we must have something to do in it, and even those whom God has most honoured must not think themselves above their business. — Matthew Henry (Concise)
