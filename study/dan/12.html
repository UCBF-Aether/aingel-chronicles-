Study Notes — Daniel

Chapter 12

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: And at that time shall Michael stand up, the great prince which stands for the children of thy people, and there shall be a time of trouble, such as never was since there was a nation even to that same time, and at that time thy people shall be delivered, every one that shall be found written in the book. Michael signifies "Who is like God," Henry says, and his name, with the title of the great Prince, points out the Divine Saviour, who stood for the children of our people in their stead as a sacrifice, and stands for them in pleading at the throne of grace. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

2 COMMENTARY: And many of them that sleep in the dust of the earth shall awake, some to everlasting life, and some to shame and everlasting contempt. When God's gospel is preached, Henry says, many who sleep in the dust, both Jews and Gentiles, shall be awakened out of their heathenism or Judaism, and in the end the multitude that sleep in the dust shall awake, many to life and many to shame. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

3 COMMENTARY: And they that be wise shall shine as the brightness of the firmament, and they that turn many to righteousness as the stars for ever and ever. There is glory reserved for all the saints in the future state, Henry says, for all that are wise, wise for their souls and eternity; and those who turn many to righteousness will share in the glory of those they have helped to heaven. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

4 COMMENTARY: But thou, O Daniel, shut up the words, and seal the book, even to the time of the end; many shall run to and fro, and knowledge shall be increased. The book is sealed to the time of the end, Henry's outline shows, when the prophecy's varied events, extending through so many successive ages even to the general resurrection, shall be understood. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

5 COMMENTARY: Then I Daniel looked, and behold, there stood other two, the one on this side of the bank of the river, and the other on that side of the bank of the river. Two others stand on either bank, Henry's account shows, as the final questioning begins. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

6 COMMENTARY: And one said to the man clothed in linen, which was upon the waters of the river: How long shall it be to the end of these wonders? One of the angels asks how long it should be to the end of these wonders, Henry notes, and a solemn reply is made. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

7 COMMENTARY: And I heard the man clothed in linen, which was upon the waters of the river, when he held up his right hand and his left hand unto heaven, and swore by him that lives for ever that it shall be for a time, times, and a half, and when he shall have accomplished to scatter the power of the holy people, all these things shall be finished. The period is a time, times, and a half, Henry explains, the period mentioned in chapter 7:25 and in Revelation, signifying 1260 prophetic days or years, beginning from the time when the power of the holy people should be scattered. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

8 COMMENTARY: And I heard, but I understood not; then said I: O my Lord, what shall be the end of these things? Daniel heard but did not understand, Henry's account shows, and asked what the end of these things shall be. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

9 COMMENTARY: And he said: Go thy way, Daniel, for the words are closed up and sealed till the time of the end. The words are closed up and sealed till the time of the end, Henry repeats, and Daniel is told to go his way. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

10 COMMENTARY: Many shall be purified, and made white, and tried, but the wicked shall do wickedly, and none of the wicked shall understand, but the wise shall understand. All will end well at last, Henry says: all opposing rule, principality, and power shall be put down, and holiness and love will triumph and be in honour to eternity. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

11 COMMENTARY: And from the time that the daily sacrifice shall be taken away, and the abomination that makes desolate set up, there shall be a thousand two hundred and ninety days. The days are numbered from the taking away of the daily sacrifice, Henry's outline shows, within the same sealed reckoning. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

12 COMMENTARY: Blessed is he that waits, and comes to the thousand three hundred and five and thirty days. Blessed is he that waits and comes to the further number, Henry's reading shows, for the end, this end, shall come. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

13 COMMENTARY: But go thou thy way till the end be, for thou shalt rest, and stand in thy lot at the end of the days. Daniel must comfort himself with the pleasing prospect of his own happiness in death, in judgment, and to eternity, Henry says; it was a comfort to Daniel, and is a comfort to all the saints, that whatever their lot is in the days of their lives, they shall have a happy lot in the end of the days. — Matthew Henry (Concise)
