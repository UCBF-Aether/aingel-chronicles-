Study Notes — Daniel

Chapter 2

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: In the second year of Nebuchadnezzar, he dreamed dreams, wherewith his spirit was troubled and his sleep broke from him. The greatest men are most open to cares and troubles of mind which disturb their repose in the night, Henry notes, while the sleep of the labouring man is sweet and sound. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

2 COMMENTARY: The king commanded to call the magicians, astrologers, sorcerers, and Chaldeans, to show him his dreams, and they came and stood before the king. We know not the uneasiness of many who live in great pomp, Henry says, and, as others vainly think, in pleasure also. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

3 COMMENTARY: The king said, I have dreamed a dream, and my spirit was troubled to know the dream. Men are more eager to ask as to future events, Henry observes, than to learn the way of salvation or the path of duty, yet foreknowledge of future events increases anxiety and trouble. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

4 COMMENTARY: The Chaldeans spoke to the king in Syriack: O king, live for ever; tell thy servants the dream, and we will show the interpretation. They asked for the dream itself to be told, Henry's account implies, since without it their pretended art had nothing to work on. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

5 COMMENTARY: The king answered, the thing is gone from me; if ye will not make known unto me the dream, with the interpretation thereof, ye shall be cut in pieces, and your houses made a dunghill. The king said his learned men must tell him the dream itself, Henry notes, or they should all be put to death as deceivers. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

6 COMMENTARY: But if ye show the dream and the interpretation, ye shall receive gifts and rewards and great honour. The king set life and death against reward, Henry's account shows, to expose whether his wise men truly knew what they claimed. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

7 COMMENTARY: They answered again, let the king tell his servants the dream, and we will show the interpretation. Their second plea only repeated the first, Henry implies, and could not meet a test designed to prove their art false. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

8 COMMENTARY: The king answered, I know of certainty that ye would gain the time, because ye see the thing is gone from me. Those who deceived by pretending to do what they could not do were sentenced to death, Henry notes, for not being able to do what they did not pretend to. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

9 COMMENTARY: But if ye will not make known unto me the dream, there is but one decree for you, for ye have prepared lying and corrupt words to speak before me till the time be changed. The king saw their delay as a device to gain time, Henry's reading shows. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

10 COMMENTARY: The Chaldeans answered, there is not a man upon the earth that can show the king's matter, and no king, lord, nor ruler has asked such things at any magician, astrologer, or Chaldean. Their confession exposed the limits of their art, Henry implies, even as they pleaded the demand was unprecedented. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

11 COMMENTARY: It is a rare thing that the king requires, and there is none other that can show it before the king, except the gods, whose dwelling is not with flesh. Their own words pointed beyond themselves, Henry's reading suggests, to the God who alone reveals secrets. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

12 COMMENTARY: For this cause the king was angry and very furious, and commanded to destroy all the wise men of Babylon. The king's fury fell on all the wise men, Henry notes, including those who had made no such pretence. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

13 COMMENTARY: And the decree went forth that the wise men should be slain, and they sought Daniel and his fellows to be slain. The decree swept up Daniel and his companions, Henry's account shows, though they had not been before the king. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

14 COMMENTARY: Then Daniel answered with counsel and wisdom to Arioch, captain of the king's guard, who was gone forth to slay the wise men of Babylon. Daniel humbly sought God rather than raging against the decree, Henry's account shows, answering with counsel and wisdom. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

15 COMMENTARY: Daniel asked Arioch, why is the decree so hasty from the king? Then Arioch made the thing known to Daniel. Daniel's calm inquiry, Henry implies, opened the way to the mercy that followed. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

16 COMMENTARY: Then Daniel went in, and desired of the king that he would give him time, and that he would show the king the interpretation. Praying friends are valuable friends, Henry says, and it well becomes the greatest and best men to desire the prayers of others. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

17 COMMENTARY: Then Daniel went to his house, and made the thing known to Hananiah, Mishael, and Azariah, his companions. Let us show that we value our friends and their prayers, Henry urges, as Daniel shared the peril with his companions. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

18 COMMENTARY: That they would desire mercies of the God of heaven concerning this secret, that Daniel and his fellows should not perish with the rest of the wise men of Babylon. They were particular in prayer, Henry notes, and their plea with God was the peril they were in. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

19 COMMENTARY: Then was the secret revealed unto Daniel in a night vision. Then Daniel blessed the God of heaven. The mercy Daniel and his fellows prayed for was bestowed, Henry says, and the fervent prayers of righteous men avail much. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

20 COMMENTARY: Daniel answered, blessed be the name of God for ever and ever, for wisdom and might are his. Daniel was thankful to God for making known that to him which saved the lives of himself and his fellows, Henry notes. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

21 COMMENTARY: He changes the times and the seasons, removes kings and sets up kings, gives wisdom unto the wise and knowledge to them that know understanding. Whatever we pray for, Henry says, we can expect nothing but as the gift of God's mercies. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

22 COMMENTARY: He reveals the deep and secret things, knows what is in the darkness, and the light dwells with him. How much more should we be thankful, Henry asks, for God making known the great salvation of the soul to those who are not among the worldly wise and prudent! — Matthew Henry (Concise)

23 COMMENTARY: I thank thee and praise thee, O thou God of my fathers, who hast given me wisdom and might, and hast made known unto me now what we desired of thee, for thou hast now made known unto us the king's matter. Daniel's thanksgiving, Henry notes, names God as the giver of the wisdom that saved them. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

24 COMMENTARY: Therefore Daniel went in unto Arioch, whom the king had ordained to destroy the wise men, and said, bring me in before the king, and I will show unto the king the interpretation. Daniel takes away the king's opinion of his magicians and soothsayers, Henry says, directing attention to the God who reveals. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

25 COMMENTARY: Then Arioch brought in Daniel before the king in haste, and said, I have found a man of the captives of Judah, that will make known unto the king the interpretation. The insufficiency of creatures should drive us to the all-sufficiency of the Creator, Henry says. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

26 COMMENTARY: The king answered Daniel, whose name was Belteshazzar, art thou able to make known unto me the dream which I have seen, and the interpretation thereof? Daniel confirmed the king in his opinion that the dream was of great consequence, Henry notes, relating to the affairs and changes of this lower world. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

27 COMMENTARY: Daniel answered, the secret which the king has demanded cannot the wise men, the astrologers, the magicians, the soothsayers, show unto the king. There is One who can do that for us, Henry says, which none on earth can, particularly the work of redemption and the secret designs of God's love therein. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

28 COMMENTARY: But there is a God in heaven that reveals secrets, and makes known to the king Nebuchadnezzar what shall be in the latter days. Let those whom God has highly favoured and honoured lay aside all opinion of their own wisdom and worthiness, Henry urges, that the Lord alone may be praised. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

29 COMMENTARY: As for thee, O king, thy thoughts came into thy mind upon thy bed, what should come to pass hereafter, and he that reveals secrets makes known to thee what shall come to pass. Daniel confirmed that the dream concerned what should come to pass hereafter, Henry notes. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

30 COMMENTARY: But as for me, this secret is not revealed to me for any wisdom that I have more than any living, but for their sakes that shall make known the interpretation to the king, and that thou mightest know the thoughts of thy heart. Daniel disclaimed personal wisdom, Henry's reading stresses, so that the Lord alone might be praised. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

31 COMMENTARY: Thou, O king, sawest, and behold a great image, whose brightness was excellent, stood before thee, and the form thereof was terrible. This image represented the kingdoms of the earth, Henry says, that should successively rule the nations and influence the affairs of the Jewish church. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

32 COMMENTARY: This image's head was of fine gold, his breast and his arms of silver, his belly and his thighs of brass. The head of gold signified the Chaldean empire, then in being, Henry explains, and the breast and arms of silver the empire of the Medes and Persians. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

33 COMMENTARY: His legs of iron, his feet part of iron and part of clay. The belly and thighs of brass signified the Grecian empire founded by Alexander, Henry says, and the legs and feet of iron the Roman empire. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

34 COMMENTARY: Thou sawest till that a stone was cut out without hands, which smote the image upon his feet that were of iron and clay, and broke them to pieces. The stone cut out without hands represented the kingdom of our Lord Jesus Christ, Henry says, set up in the kingdoms of the world upon the ruins of Satan's kingdom in them. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

35 COMMENTARY: Then was the iron, the clay, the brass, the silver, and the gold, broken to pieces together, and became like the chaff of the summer threshingfloors, and the wind carried them away, and no place was found for them, and the stone that smote the image became a great mountain and filled the whole earth. Of the increase of Christ's government and peace there shall be no end, Henry notes. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

36 COMMENTARY: This is the dream, and we will tell the interpretation thereof before the king. Daniel now turns from the dream to its interpretation, Henry's outline shows, having proved the revelation genuine by telling the dream itself. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

37 COMMENTARY: Thou, O king, art a king of kings, for the God of heaven has given thee a kingdom, power, strength, and glory. The head of gold is the Chaldean empire then in being, Henry says, and Nebuchadnezzar himself its head. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

38 COMMENTARY: And wheresoever the children of men dwell, the beasts of the field and the fowls of the heaven has he given into thine hand, and has made thee ruler over them all. Thou art this head of gold. The image's successive kingdoms, Henry explains, should rule the nations in turn. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

39 COMMENTARY: And after thee shall arise another kingdom inferior to thee, and another third kingdom of brass, which shall bear rule over all the earth. The second kingdom of silver is the Medes and Persians, Henry says, and the third of brass the Grecian empire. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

40 COMMENTARY: And the fourth kingdom shall be strong as iron, forasmuch as iron breaks in pieces and subdues all things. The legs and feet of iron signified the Roman empire, Henry explains, which branched into ten kingdoms, as the toes of these feet. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

41 COMMENTARY: And whereas thou sawest the feet and toes, part of potters' clay and part of iron, the kingdom shall be divided, but there shall be in it of the strength of the iron. Some of the ten kingdoms were weak as clay, Henry notes, others strong as iron. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

42 COMMENTARY: And as the toes of the feet were part of iron and part of clay, so the kingdom shall be partly strong and partly broken. Endeavours have often been used to unite them for strengthening the empire, Henry says, but in vain. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

43 COMMENTARY: And whereas thou sawest iron mixed with miry clay, they shall mingle themselves with the seed of men, but they shall not cleave one to another, even as iron is not mixed with clay. The mingling without cleaving, Henry's reading shows, marks the divided kingdom's incurable weakness. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

44 COMMENTARY: And in the days of these kings shall the God of heaven set up a kingdom, which shall never be destroyed, and the kingdom shall not be left to other people, but it shall break in pieces and consume all these kingdoms, and it shall stand for ever. This was the Stone which the builders refused, Henry says, because it was not cut out by their hands, but it is become the head stone of the corner. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

45 COMMENTARY: Forasmuch as thou sawest that the stone was cut out of the mountain without hands, and that it broke in pieces the iron, the brass, the clay, the silver, and the gold, the great God has made known to the king what shall come to pass hereafter, and the dream is certain, and the interpretation thereof sure. As far as events have gone, Henry says, the fulfilling of this prophetic vision has been most exact and undeniable. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

46 COMMENTARY: Then the king Nebuchadnezzar fell upon his face, and worshipped Daniel, and commanded that they should offer an oblation and sweet odours unto him. It is our business to direct attention to the Lord as the Author and Giver of every good gift, Henry says, and many have thoughts of the divine power who do not think of serving God themselves. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

47 COMMENTARY: The king answered Daniel, of a truth it is, that your God is a God of gods, and a Lord of kings, and a revealer of secrets, seeing thou couldest reveal this secret. The king confessed the God of heaven, Henry's account shows, though confession alone is not service. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

48 COMMENTARY: Then the king made Daniel a great man, and gave him many great gifts, and made him ruler over the whole province of Babylon, and chief of the governors over all the wise men of Babylon. All should strive, Henry says, that God may be glorified and the best interests of mankind furthered. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

49 COMMENTARY: Then Daniel requested of the king, and he set Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego, over the affairs of the province of Babylon, but Daniel sat in the gate of the king. Daniel used his honour for his friends' good, Henry's account implies, and remained himself at the king's gate. — Matthew Henry (Concise)
