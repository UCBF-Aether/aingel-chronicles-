Study Notes — Daniel

Chapter 3

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Nebuchadnezzar made an image of gold, sixty cubits high and six broad, and set it up in the plain of Dura. In the height, about thirty yards, probably a pedestal is included, Henry notes, and most likely it was only covered with plates of gold, not a solid mass. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

2 COMMENTARY: The king sent to gather the princes, governors, captains, judges, treasurers, counsellors, sheriffs, and rulers of the provinces to the dedication of the image. Pride and bigotry cause men to require their subjects to follow their religion, Henry says, whether right or wrong. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

3 COMMENTARY: Then all the officers were gathered unto the dedication of the image. When worldly interest allures and punishment overawes, Henry notes, few refuse. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

4 COMMENTARY: Then a herald cried aloud: to you it is commanded, O people, nations, and languages. The command was universal, Henry's account shows, leaving no subject outside its demand. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

5 COMMENTARY: At the sound of cornet, flute, harp, sackbut, psaltery, dulcimer, and all kinds of music, ye shall fall down and worship the golden image. There is nothing so bad which the careless world will not be drawn to by a concert of music, Henry says, or driven to by a fiery furnace. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

6 COMMENTARY: Whoso falls not down and worships shall the same hour be cast into the midst of a burning fiery furnace. By such methods, Henry notes, false worship has been set up and maintained. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

7 COMMENTARY: Therefore at that time, when all the people heard the sound of the music, all the people, nations, and languages fell down and worshipped the golden image. This is easy to the careless, the sensual, and the infidel, Henry says, who are the greatest number, and most will go their ways. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

8 COMMENTARY: Wherefore at that time certain Chaldeans came near and accused the Jews. True devotion calms the spirit, Henry says, but superstition and devotion to false gods inflame men's passions. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

9 COMMENTARY: They spoke and said to the king Nebuchadnezzar, O king, live for ever. The accusers opened with flattery, Henry's reading implies, before pressing the charge. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

10 COMMENTARY: Thou, O king, hast made a decree, that every man that shall hear the sound of the music shall fall down and worship the golden image. The accusers recited the king's own decree, Henry's account shows, to make refusal a capital offence. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

11 COMMENTARY: And whoso falls not down and worships, that he should be cast into the midst of a burning fiery furnace. The matter is put into a little compass, Henry says: Turn, or burn. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

12 COMMENTARY: There are certain Jews whom thou hast set over the affairs of Babylon, Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego; these men, O king, have not regarded thee, they serve not thy gods, nor worship the golden image. Proud men are still ready to say, as Nebuchadnezzar, Henry notes, Who is the Lord, that I should fear his power? — Matthew Henry (Concise)

13 COMMENTARY: Then Nebuchadnezzar in his rage and fury commanded to bring Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego, and they were brought before the king. The king's rage, Henry's account shows, was the inflamed passion of devotion to a false god. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

14 COMMENTARY: Nebuchadnezzar spoke: Is it true, O Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego, do not ye serve my gods, nor worship the golden image which I have set up? The king gave them a hearing, Henry implies, but on his own terms of compliance or death. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

15 COMMENTARY: Now if ye be ready that at the sound of the music ye fall down and worship the image, well; but if ye worship not, ye shall be cast the same hour into the midst of a burning fiery furnace, and who is that God that shall deliver you out of my hands? Those that would avoid sin must not parley with temptation, Henry says, when that to which we are allured or affrighted is manifestly evil. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

16 COMMENTARY: Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego answered, O Nebuchadnezzar, we are not careful to answer thee in this matter. They did not hesitate whether they should comply or not, Henry says, and they did not contrive an evasive answer when a direct answer was expected. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

17 COMMENTARY: If it be so, our God whom we serve is able to deliver us from the burning fiery furnace, and he will deliver us out of thine hand, O king. The faithful servants of God find him able to control and overrule all the powers armed against them, Henry says: Lord, if thou wilt, thou canst. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

18 COMMENTARY: But if not, be it known unto thee, O king, that we will not serve thy gods, nor worship the golden image which thou hast set up. They must obey God rather than man, Henry says; they must rather suffer than sin, and must not do evil that good may come; God will deliver us, either from death or in death. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

19 COMMENTARY: Then was Nebuchadnezzar full of fury, and the form of his visage was changed against Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego, and he spoke and commanded that they should heat the furnace one seven times more than it was wont to be heated. Let Nebuchadnezzar heat his furnace as hot as he can, Henry says; a few minutes will finish the torment of those cast into it, but hell-fire tortures and yet does not kill. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

20 COMMENTARY: And he commanded the most mighty men in his army to bind Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego, and to cast them into the burning fiery furnace. The saving them from sinful compliance was as great a miracle in the kingdom of grace, Henry says, as the saving them out of the furnace was in the kingdom of nature. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

21 COMMENTARY: Then these men were bound in their coats, their hosen, and their hats, and their other garments, and were cast into the midst of the burning fiery furnace. Fear of man and love of the world, especially want of faith, make men yield to temptation, Henry notes, while a firm persuasion of the truth delivers them from denying Christ. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

22 COMMENTARY: Therefore because the king's commandment was urgent, and the furnace exceeding hot, the flame of the fire slew those men that took up Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego. The fire's first victims were the executioners, Henry's account implies, showing the furnace's heat exceeded man's control. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

23 COMMENTARY: And these three men fell down bound into the midst of the burning fiery furnace. We are to be meek in our replies, Henry says, but decided that we will obey God rather than man, as these three were. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

92 COMMENTARY: Then Nebuchadnezzar was astonied, and rose up in haste, and spoke to his counsellors: Did not we cast three men bound into the midst of the fire? They answered, True, O king. Now was fulfilled in the letter that great promise, Henry says: When thou walkest through the fire, thou shalt not be burned. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

93 COMMENTARY: He answered: Lo, I see four men loose, walking in the midst of the fire, and they have no hurt, and the form of the fourth is like the Son of God. Leaving it to that God who preserved them in the fire to bring them out, Henry says, they walked up and down in the midst, supported and encouraged by the presence of the Son of God. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

94 COMMENTARY: Then Nebuchadnezzar came near to the mouth of the burning fiery furnace, and spoke: Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego, ye servants of the most high God, come forth, and come hither. Then they came forth out of the midst of the fire. Nebuchadnezzar owns them for servants of the most high God, Henry notes, a God able to deliver them out of his hand. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

95 COMMENTARY: And the princes, governors, captains, and the king's counsellors, being gathered together, saw these men, upon whose bodies the fire had no power, nor was a hair of their head singed, neither were their coats changed, nor the smell of fire had passed on them. Those who suffer for Christ have his presence in their sufferings, Henry says, even in the fiery furnace. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

96 COMMENTARY: Then Nebuchadnezzar spoke: Blessed be the God of Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego, who has sent his angel, and delivered his servants that trusted in him, and have changed the king's word, and yielded their bodies, that they might not serve nor worship any god, except their own God. What God did for these servants would help to keep the Jews to their religion while in captivity, Henry says, and to cure them of idolatry. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

97 COMMENTARY: Therefore I make a decree, that every people, nation, and language, which speak any thing amiss against the God of Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego, shall be cut in pieces, and their houses made a dunghill, because there is no other God that can deliver after this sort. The miracle brought deep convictions on Nebuchadnezzar, Henry notes, but no abiding change then took place in his conduct. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

98 COMMENTARY: Then the king promoted Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego, in the province of Babylon. He who preserved these pious Jews in the fiery furnace, Henry closes, is able to uphold us in the hour of temptation, and to keep us from falling into sin. — Matthew Henry (Concise)
