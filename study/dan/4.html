Study Notes — Daniel

Chapter 4

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Nebuchadnezzar the king, unto all people, nations, and languages, that dwell in all the earth: Peace be multiplied unto you. After he was recovered from his madness, Henry notes, he told to distant places and wrote down for future ages how God had justly humbled and graciously restored him. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

2 COMMENTARY: I thought it good to show the signs and wonders that the high God has wrought toward me. When a sinner comes to himself, Henry says, he will promote the welfare of others by making known the wondrous mercy of God. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

3 COMMENTARY: How great are his signs! How mighty are his wonders! His kingdom is an everlasting kingdom, and his dominion is from generation to generation. The beginning and end of this chapter lead us to hope, Henry says, that Nebuchadnezzar was a monument of divine grace and mercy. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

4 COMMENTARY: I Nebuchadnezzar was at rest in mine house, and flourishing in my palace. The king's rest and flourishing set the stage, Henry's account shows, for the warning that pride would soon undo. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

5 COMMENTARY: I saw a dream which made me afraid, and the thoughts upon my bed and the visions of my head troubled me. Nebuchadnezzar, before he related the divine judgments upon him for his pride, told the warnings he had in a dream, Henry notes. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

6 COMMENTARY: Therefore made I a decree to bring in all the wise men of Babylon before me, that they might make known unto me the interpretation of the dream. The meaning was explained to him only after the wise men failed, Henry's account shows. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

7 COMMENTARY: Then came in the magicians, astrologers, Chaldeans, and soothsayers, and I told the dream before them, but they did not make known unto me the interpretation thereof. The court wise men again proved insufficient, Henry implies, as in chapter 2. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

8 COMMENTARY: But at the last Daniel came in before me, whose name was Belteshazzar, according to the name of my god, and in whom is the spirit of the holy gods, and before him I told the dream. Daniel, last resorted to, was the one in whom the spirit of the holy gods was, Henry's account stresses. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

9 COMMENTARY: O Belteshazzar, master of the magicians, because I know that the spirit of the holy gods is in thee, and no secret troubles thee, tell me the visions of my dream that I have seen, and the interpretation thereof. The king confessed Daniel's gift, Henry notes, even while still trusting his own court. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

10 COMMENTARY: Thus were the visions of mine head in my bed: I saw, and behold a tree in the midst of the earth, and the height thereof was great. The tree vision, Henry explains, signified the king himself, to be put down from honour. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

11 COMMENTARY: The tree grew, and was strong, and the height thereof reached unto heaven, and the sight thereof to the end of all the earth. The tree's greatness pictures the king's worldwide dominion, Henry's reading shows. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

12 COMMENTARY: The leaves thereof were fair, and the fruit thereof much, and in it was meat for all; under it the beasts of the field dwelt, and upon its branches the fowls of the heaven had their habitation, and all flesh was fed of it. The tree sheltered and fed all, Henry implies, as the king's empire sheltered the nations. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

13 COMMENTARY: I saw in the visions of my head upon my bed, and behold, a watcher and an holy one came down from heaven. The watcher from heaven, Henry says, brought the solemn sentence, applauded by the angels, though God needs no counsel or concurrence of angels. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

14 COMMENTARY: He cried aloud: Hew down the tree, and cut off his branches, shake off his leaves, and scatter his fruit; let the beasts get away from under it, and the fowls from his branches. The person signified was to be put down from honour, Henry explains. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

15 COMMENTARY: Nevertheless leave the stump of his roots in the earth, even with a band of iron and brass, in the tender grass of the field, and let it be wet with the dew of heaven, and let his portion be with the beasts in the grass of the earth. The stump left bound, Henry's reading shows, signified that the kingdom would be kept for him until restoration. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

16 COMMENTARY: Let his heart be changed from man's, and let a beast's heart be given unto him, and let seven times pass over him. The person signified was to be deprived of the use of his reason seven years, Henry says, surely the sorest of all temporal judgments. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

17 COMMENTARY: This matter is by the decree of the watchers, and the demand by the word of the holy ones, to the intent that the living may know that the most High rules in the kingdom of men, and gives it to whomsoever he will, and sets up over it the basest of men. God has determined it as a righteous Judge, Henry says, and the demand is by the word of the holy ones, God's suffering people: when the oppressed cry to God, he will hear. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

18 COMMENTARY: This dream I king Nebuchadnezzar have seen. Now thou, O Belteshazzar, declare the interpretation thereof, forasmuch as all the wise men of my kingdom are not able to make known unto me the interpretation, but thou art able, for the spirit of the holy gods is in thee. Let us diligently seek blessings which can never be taken from us, Henry urges, and especially beware of pride and forgetfulness of God. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

19 COMMENTARY: Then Daniel, whose name was Belteshazzar, was astonied for one hour, and his thoughts troubled him. The king spoke: Belteshazzar, let not the dream, or the interpretation thereof, trouble thee. Daniel answered: My lord, the dream be to them that hate thee, and the interpretation thereof to thine enemies. Daniel was struck with amazement and terror at so heavy a judgment coming upon so great a prince, Henry says, and gives advice with tenderness and respect. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

20 COMMENTARY: The tree that thou sawest, which grew, and was strong, whose height reached unto heaven, and the sight thereof to all the earth. Daniel begins the interpretation by naming the tree, Henry's outline shows, before applying it to the king. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

21 COMMENTARY: Whose leaves were fair, and the fruit thereof much, and in it was meat for all, under which the beasts of the field dwelt, and upon whose branches the fowls of the heaven had their habitation. The tree's fruitfulness pictures the king's empire feeding and sheltering all, Henry's reading implies. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

22 COMMENTARY: It is thou, O king, that art grown and become strong, for thy greatness is grown, and reaches unto heaven, and thy dominion to the end of the earth. Pride and self-conceit are sins that beset great men, Henry warns, apt to take to themselves the glory due to God only. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

23 COMMENTARY: And whereas the king saw a watcher and an holy one coming down from heaven, and saying, Hew the tree down, and destroy it, yet leave the stump of the roots thereof in the earth, even with a band of iron and brass, in the tender grass of the field, and let it be wet with the dew of heaven, and let his portion be with the beasts of the field, till seven times pass over him. The sentence, Henry says, was determined by God as a righteous Judge. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

24 COMMENTARY: This is the interpretation, O king, and this is the decree of the most High, which is come upon my lord the king. Daniel speaks the decree plainly, Henry notes, with tenderness and respect but without softening it. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

25 COMMENTARY: That they shall drive thee from men, and thy dwelling shall be with the beasts of the field, and they shall make thee to eat grass as oxen, and they shall wet thee with the dew of heaven, and seven times shall pass over thee, till thou know that the most High rules in the kingdom of men, and gives it to whomsoever he will. Whatever outward affliction God lays upon us, Henry says, we have cause to bear it patiently and be thankful he continues the use of our reason. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

26 COMMENTARY: And whereas they commanded to leave the stump of the tree roots, thy kingdom shall be sure unto thee, after that thou shalt have known that the heavens do rule. The stump preserved, Henry's reading shows, meant restoration after acknowledgment that heaven rules. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

27 COMMENTARY: Wherefore, O king, let my counsel be acceptable unto thee, and break off thy sins by righteousness, and thine iniquities by showing mercy to the poor, if it may be a lengthening of thy tranquillity. It is necessary in repentance, Henry says, not only to cease to do evil, but to learn to do good; though it might not wholly prevent the judgment, the trouble may be longer before it comes or shorter when it does. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

28 COMMENTARY: All this came upon the king Nebuchadnezzar. The warning was not heeded in time, Henry's account shows, and the sentence fell as spoken. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

29 COMMENTARY: At the end of twelve months he walked in the palace of the kingdom of Babylon. A full year of tranquillity passed, Henry implies, before the proud word was spoken. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

30 COMMENTARY: The king spoke and said: Is not this great Babylon, that I have built for the house of the kingdom by the might of my power, and for the honour of my majesty? While the proud word was in the king's mouth, Henry says, the powerful word came from God. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

31 COMMENTARY: While the word was in the king's mouth, there fell a voice from heaven, saying: O king Nebuchadnezzar, to thee it is spoken; The kingdom is departed from thee. His understanding and memory were gone, Henry says, and all the powers of the rational soul were broken. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

32 COMMENTARY: And they shall drive thee from men, and thy dwelling shall be with the beasts of the field; they shall make thee to eat grass as oxen, and seven times shall pass over thee, until thou know that the most High rules in the kingdom of men, and gives it to whomsoever he will. Nebuchadnezzar would be more than a man, Henry says, but God justly makes him less than a man. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

33 COMMENTARY: The same hour was the thing fulfilled upon Nebuchadnezzar, and he was driven from men, and did eat grass as oxen, and his body was wet with the dew of heaven, till his hairs were grown like eagles' feathers, and his nails like birds' claws. How careful we ought to be, Henry warns, not to do anything which may provoke God to put us out of our senses! — Matthew Henry (Concise)

34 COMMENTARY: And at the end of the days I Nebuchadnezzar lifted up mine eyes unto heaven, and mine understanding returned unto me, and I blessed the most High, and I praised and honoured him that lives for ever, whose dominion is an everlasting dominion, and his kingdom is from generation to generation. We may learn to believe concerning God, Henry says, that the most high God lives for ever, and his kingdom is like himself, everlasting and universal. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

35 COMMENTARY: And all the inhabitants of the earth are reputed as nothing, and he does according to his will in the army of heaven, and among the inhabitants of the earth, and none can stay his hand, or say unto him, What doest thou? His power cannot be resisted, Henry says. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

36 COMMENTARY: At the same time my reason returned unto me, and for the glory of my kingdom, mine honour and brightness returned unto me, and my counsellors and my lords sought unto me, and I was established in my kingdom, and excellent majesty was added unto me. When men are brought to honour God by confession of sin and acknowledging his sovereignty, Henry says, then, and not till then, they may expect God will honour them. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

37 COMMENTARY: Now I Nebuchadnezzar praise and extol and honour the King of heaven, all whose works are truth, and his ways judgment, and those that walk in pride he is able to abase. There can be no reasonable doubt, Henry closes, that Nebuchadnezzar was a true penitent and an accepted believer; the Lord knows how to abase those that walk in pride, but gives grace to the humble, broken-hearted sinner who calls upon him. — Matthew Henry (Concise)
