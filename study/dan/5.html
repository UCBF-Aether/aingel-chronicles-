Study Notes — Daniel

Chapter 5

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Belshazzar the king made a great feast to a thousand of his lords, and drank wine before the thousand. Belshazzar bade defiance to the judgments of God, Henry says; most historians consider that Cyrus then besieged Babylon, and security and sensuality are sad proofs of approaching ruin. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

2 COMMENTARY: Belshazzar, while he tasted the wine, commanded to bring the gold and silver vessels which his father Nebuchadnezzar had taken out of the temple in Jerusalem, that the king, his princes, wives, and concubines might drink therein. That mirth is sinful indeed, Henry says, which profanes sacred things. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

3 COMMENTARY: Then they brought the golden vessels taken out of the temple of God's house in Jerusalem, and the king, his princes, wives, and concubines drank in them. The feast's profanation was deliberate, Henry's account shows, turning holy vessels to drunken use. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

4 COMMENTARY: They drank wine, and praised the gods of gold, and of silver, of brass, of iron, of wood, and of stone. What are many of the songs used at modern feasts, Henry asks, better than the praises sung by the heathens to their gods! — Matthew Henry (Concise)

5 COMMENTARY: In the same hour came forth fingers of a man's hand, and wrote over against the candlestick upon the plaster of the wall of the king's palace, and the king saw the part of the hand that wrote. See how God struck terror upon Belshazzar and his lords, Henry says; God's written word is enough to put the proudest, boldest sinner in a fright. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

6 COMMENTARY: Then the king's countenance was changed, and his thoughts troubled him, so that the joints of his loins were loosed, and his knees smote one against another. What we see of God, Henry says, the part of the hand that writes in the book of creatures and Scriptures, should fill us with awful thoughts concerning that part which we do not see. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

7 COMMENTARY: The king cried aloud to bring in the astrologers, Chaldeans, and soothsayers, and said, whosoever shall read this writing and show me the interpretation shall be clothed with scarlet, have a chain of gold about his neck, and shall be the third ruler in the kingdom. The king's guilty conscience told him, Henry notes, that he had no reason to expect any good news from heaven. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

8 COMMENTARY: Then came in all the king's wise men, but they could not read the writing, nor make known to the king the interpretation thereof. The ignorance and uncertainty concerning the Holy Scriptures shown by many who call themselves wise, Henry says, only tend to drive sinners to despair, as the ignorance of these wise men did. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

9 COMMENTARY: Then was king Belshazzar greatly troubled, and his countenance was changed in him, and his lords were astonied. God can in a moment make the heart of the stoutest sinner tremble, Henry says, and there needs no more than to let loose his own thoughts upon him. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

10 COMMENTARY: Now the queen, by reason of the words of the king and his lords, came into the banquet house, and spoke: O king, live for ever; let not thy thoughts trouble thee, nor let thy countenance be changed. The queen's counsel, Henry's account shows, turned the king from his failing wise men to the forgotten Daniel. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

11 COMMENTARY: There is a man in thy kingdom, in whom is the spirit of the holy gods, and in the days of thy father light and understanding and wisdom, like the wisdom of the gods, was found in him, whom the king Nebuchadnezzar thy father made master of the magicians, astrologers, Chaldeans, and soothsayers. Daniel was forgotten at court, Henry notes; he lived privately, and was then ninety years of age. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

12 COMMENTARY: Forasmuch as an excellent spirit, and knowledge, and understanding, interpreting of dreams, and showing of hard sentences, and dissolving of doubts, were found in the same Daniel, whom the king named Belteshazzar, now let Daniel be called, and he will show the interpretation. Many consult servants of God on curious questions, Henry says, but without asking the way of salvation or the path of duty. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

13 COMMENTARY: Then was Daniel brought in before the king, and the king spoke: Art thou that Daniel, which art of the children of the captivity of Judah, whom the king my father brought out of Jewry? Daniel, long forgotten, was brought in, Henry's account shows, to speak to a condemned king. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

14 COMMENTARY: I have even heard of thee, that the spirit of the gods is in thee, and that light and understanding and excellent wisdom is found in thee. The king's report of Daniel, Henry implies, confessed a gift his own wise men lacked. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

15 COMMENTARY: And now the wise men, the astrologers, have been brought in before me, that they should read this writing and make known unto me the interpretation, but they could not show the interpretation of the thing. The wise men's failure, Henry notes, repeated the pattern of chapter 2. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

16 COMMENTARY: And I have heard of thee, that thou canst make interpretations, and dissolve doubts; now if thou canst read the writing, and make known unto me the interpretation thereof, thou shalt be clothed with scarlet, and have a chain of gold about thy neck, and shalt be the third ruler in the kingdom. The king again offered rank and reward, Henry's account shows, for what only God could give. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

17 COMMENTARY: Then Daniel answered: Let thy gifts be to thyself, and give thy rewards to another; yet I will read the writing unto the king, and make known to him the interpretation. Daniel slighted the offer of reward, Henry says, and spoke to Belshazzar as to a condemned criminal. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

18 COMMENTARY: O thou king, the most high God gave Nebuchadnezzar thy father a kingdom, and majesty, and glory, and honour. Daniel reads Belshazzar's doom, Henry says, beginning with the greatness God had given his father. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

19 COMMENTARY: And for the majesty that he gave him, all people, nations, and languages, trembled and feared before him; whom he would he slew, and whom he would he kept alive, and whom he would he set up, and whom he would he put down. The father's absolute power, Henry's reading shows, was itself God's gift. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

20 COMMENTARY: But when his heart was lifted up, and his mind hardened in pride, he was deposed from his kingly throne, and they took his glory from him. He had not taken warning by the judgments upon Nebuchadnezzar, Henry says, and now his own pride is set against that example. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

21 COMMENTARY: And he was driven from the sons of men, and his heart was made like the beasts, and his dwelling was with the wild asses; they fed him with grass like oxen, and his body was wet with the dew of heaven, till he knew that the most high God ruled in the kingdom of men, and that he appoints over it whomsoever he will. The father's humbling, Henry notes, was the warning Belshazzar ignored. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

22 COMMENTARY: And thou his son, O Belshazzar, hast not humbled thine heart, though thou knewest all this. Daniel's charge turns on knowledge, Henry says: Belshazzar sinned against light, not ignorance. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

23 COMMENTARY: But hast lifted up thyself against the Lord of heaven, and they have brought the vessels of his house before thee, and thou, and thy lords, thy wives, and thy concubines, have drunk wine in them, and thou hast praised the gods of silver, and gold, of brass, iron, wood, and stone, which see not, nor hear, nor know, and the God in whose hand thy breath is, and whose are all thy ways, hast thou not glorified. Sinners are pleased with gods that neither see, nor hear, nor know, Henry says, but they will be judged by One to whom all things are open. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

24 COMMENTARY: Then was the part of the hand sent from him, and this writing was written. The hand was sent from God, Henry's account shows, because the king had not glorified him. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

25 COMMENTARY: And this is the writing that was written: MENE, MENE, TEKEL, UPHARSIN. Daniel reads the sentence written on the wall, Henry says, word by word. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

26 COMMENTARY: This is the interpretation of the thing: MENE; God has numbered thy kingdom, and finished it. At death, Henry applies, the sinner's days are numbered and finished. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

27 COMMENTARY: TEKEL; Thou art weighed in the balances, and art found wanting. After death is the judgment, Henry applies, when he will be weighed in the balance and found wanting. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

28 COMMENTARY: PERES; Thy kingdom is divided, and given to the Medes and Persians. After judgment, Henry applies, the sinner will be cut asunder and given as a prey to the devil and his angels. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

29 COMMENTARY: Then commanded Belshazzar, and they clothed Daniel with scarlet, and put a chain of gold about his neck, and made a proclamation concerning him, that he should be the third ruler in the kingdom. We should despise all the gifts and rewards this world can give, Henry says, did we see, as we may by faith, its end hastening on; but let us do our duty in the world, and do it all the real service we can. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

30 COMMENTARY: In that night was Belshazzar the king of the Chaldeans slain. While these things were passing in the palace, Henry notes, it is considered that the army of Cyrus entered the city, and when Belshazzar was slain, a general submission followed. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

31 COMMENTARY: And Darius the Median took the kingdom, being about threescore and two years old. Soon will every impenitent sinner find the writing of God's word brought to pass upon him, Henry closes, whether weighed in the balance of the law as a self-righteous Pharisee, or in that of the gospel as a painted hypocrite. — Matthew Henry (Concise)
