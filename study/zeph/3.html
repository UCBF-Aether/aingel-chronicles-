Study Notes — Zephaniah

Chapter 3

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: This chapter resumes the denunciation of Jerusalem, as being unreformed by the punishment of other nations. She is filthy and polluted, her inward moral filth standing in glaring contrast to the holiness of the worship on Moriah, the hill of the temple, whose name her own name plays upon; and she is the oppressing city, wronging the poor, the weak, widows, orphans, and strangers. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: She obeyed not the voice, received not correction, trusted not in the Lord, and drew not near to her God. Jerusalem is incurable, obstinately rejecting salutary admonition, and her distrust in the Lord, as if he were insufficient, is the parent of all superstition and wickedness; though God was specially near to her as her God, she gratuitously estranged herself from him. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: Her princes within her are roaring lions, roaring for prey, and her judges are evening wolves, most ravenous at evening after being foodless all day, devouring all at once, bones and flesh, so ragingly ravenous are they. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: Her prophets are light and treacherous persons, in whose life and teaching there is no truth, gravity, or steadiness, false to Jehovah whose prophets they profess to be. Her priests have polluted the sanctuary by their profane deeds. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: The just Lord is in the midst of her, and he will not do iniquity; every morning does he bring his judgment to light, publicly by the teaching of his prophets and by samples of his judgments on the guilty. The Jews regard not God's justice manifested among them, and the unjust know no shame, and are not shamed by his justice into repentance. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: God had cut off the nations and made their towers desolate, and had hoped that his people, seeing his judgments on other nations, would be led to amendment. But they are not, so blinded by sin are they, and the judgment on the ten tribes and the great incursion of the Scythians under Josiah's long and peaceful reign passed without reforming them. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: God said, surely thou wilt fear me, surely thou wilt receive instruction, for he had set before them life and death in his word and in his providence, and had chastened them that, being chastened, they might not be condemned. Yet they rose early and corrupted all their doings, setting themselves with the greatest earnestness to corruption, more resolute in wickedness than ever. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: Therefore wait ye upon me, saith the Lord, until the day that I rise up to the prey: here Jehovah turns to the pious Jews, bidding them look forward to the glorious time of restoration to be ushered in by his outpouring of wrath on all nations. He will gather the nations against Jerusalem to pour out his indignation upon them there, rising like a warrior leading Israel to certain victory. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: For then will I turn to the people a pure language, that they may all call upon the name of the Lord, to serve him with one consent. The impure lips that called on idols shall be changed and given a pure language again, reversing the confusion at Babel, an earnest of which was the gift of tongues at Pentecost; and to serve with one consent is literally to serve with one shoulder, like two bearers of a yoke helping one another with conjoint effort. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: From beyond the rivers of Ethiopia my suppliants, even the daughter of my dispersed, shall bring mine offering. The suppliants are the dispersed Israelites, pictured as worshippers bringing incense, here made the representatives of all Israel which is to be restored, and the peoples brought to fear God by his judgments shall bring them as his offering. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: In that day shalt thou not be ashamed for all thy doings wherein thou hast transgressed against me, for then will I take away out of the midst of thee them that rejoice in thy pride. Those priding themselves on the temple, on election as God's people, and on other grounds of boasting shall be removed, and the converted remnant shall be of a humble spirit. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: I will also leave in the midst of thee an afflicted and poor people, and they shall trust in the name of the Lord. This is the blessed effect of sanctified affliction on the remnant, for entire trust in the Lord cannot be except where all cause for boasting is taken away. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: The remnant of Israel shall not do iniquity, nor speak lies, neither shall a deceitful tongue be found in their mouth. Worshipping God in truth and loving without dissimulation, they shall feed and lie down, and none shall make them afraid, neither foreign foe nor unjust prince, prophet, or priest. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: Sing, O daughter of Zion, shout, O Israel, be glad and rejoice with all the heart, O daughter of Jerusalem: the prophet in mental vision sees the joyful day of Zion present, and bids her rejoice at it. After the promises of taking away sin follow promises of taking away trouble, for when the cause is removed, the effect will cease. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: The Lord hath taken away thy judgments, he hath cast out thine enemy: the King of Israel, even the Lord, is in the midst of thee, and thou shalt not see evil any more. After the taking away of sin follows the taking away of trouble, and though he seemed to desert her for a time, he is now present as her safeguard, so that she shall not experience evil. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: In that day it shall be said to Jerusalem, Fear thou not, and to Zion, Let not thine hands be slack. Do not faint in the work of the Lord, for the day of deliverance has come. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: The Lord thy God in the midst of thee is mighty; he will save, he will rejoice over thee with joy; he will rest in his love, he will joy over thee with singing. To rest in his love is the calm, silent joy of contentment with his love as his supreme delight, too great for words, like God's silent satisfaction in his work after the six days of creation; the singing, by contrast, is joy uttered aloud. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

18 COMMENTARY: I will gather them that are sorrowful for the solemn assembly, who are of thee, to whom the reproach of it was a burden. They are pining after the solemn assembly which they cannot celebrate in exile, and they are God's true citizens, whom he will therefore restore, taking away the reproach and captivity that burdened them. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

19 COMMENTARY: Behold, at that time I will undo all that afflict thee, and I will save her that halteth, and gather her that was driven out. To undo is to deal with the afflicters as they deserve, and the destruction of Israel's foes precedes Israel's restoration; her that halteth is all that are helpless, whose weakness will be no barrier to restoration, and God will get them praise and fame in every land where they have been put to shame. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

20 COMMENTARY: At that time will I bring you again, even in the time that I gather you, for I will make you a name and a praise among all people of the earth. The Hebrew speaks of captivities in the plural, the captivities of different ages and places, and this gathering shall happen before your eyes, so that your own eyes with delight shall see what you would scarcely believe for joy. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
