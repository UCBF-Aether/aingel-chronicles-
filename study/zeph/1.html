Study Notes — Zephaniah

Chapter 1

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: This is the title-page of the book, and its authority is from heaven, not from men: it is the word of the Lord. Zephaniah's pedigree is traced back four generations to Hizkiah, likely Hezekiah king of Judah, so that a prophet of royal descent could with the better grace reprove the folly of the king's children, and he prophesied in the days of Josiah, who reigned well and carried on a work of reformation. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: Ruin is coming, utter ruin, and the expressions are figurative, speaking desolation everywhere. The Hebrew word for utterly consume means to sweep or scrape off utterly, and the land to be swept is Judah, left without inhabitants. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: This verse spells out in detail the all things of verse 2: man and beast, birds and fish, are all to be consumed. The stumblingblocks are the idols which cause Judah to stumble, and the idols and their worshippers shall be involved in a common destruction. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: God will stretch out his hand upon Judah, including Benjamin, and upon Jerusalem, the fountainhead of the evil, for God begins with his sanctuary. The remnant of Baal is the remains of Baal worship which Josiah had not yet been able utterly to eradicate in remote places, and the Chemarims, the idol priests, shall have their very name forgotten, along with the Aaronic priests who secretly abetted idolatry instead of eradicating it. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: Those to be cut off include those who worship the host of heaven upon the flat housetops, and those who swear to the Lord and yet swear by Malcham, that is, their king, the same as Molech. Those who think to divide their affections between God and idols will come short of acceptance with God, for if the Lord has but half, he will have none. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: This verse describes more comprehensively those guilty of defection from the Lord in any way: those turned back from following him, and those who have not sought him nor inquired for him. Neglect of God shows impiety and contempt. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: Hold thy peace at the presence of the Lord: let the earth be silent at his approach, and let murmurers cease their self-justifications and repent in time. The day of the Lord is at hand, and the sacrifice the Lord has prepared is the slaughter of the guilty Jews, with the Chaldeans bidden as guests to execute it. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: In the day of the Lord's sacrifice he will punish the princes, who ought to have been an example of good but were ringleaders in evil, and the king's children, a word fulfilled on Zedekiah's children and earlier on Jehoahaz and Eliakim. Those clothed with strange apparel are the courtiers in costly foreign garments, adopted partly for luxury and partly to ingratiate themselves with foreign nations, whose costume as well as their idolatries they imitated, whereas God would have his people separate from the nations. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: Those that leap on the threshold are invaders of their neighbours' rights, seizing their possessions, and they fill their masters' houses with goods obtained by violence and deceit. God's day is at hand, and such presumptuous sinners are reckoned with for their pride and vanity. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: This verse describes Jerusalem when besieged by Nebuchadnezzar, through the fish gate, named for the fish market near it, that the foe first entered. The cry is heard first at the fish gate, then in the second or lower city, and lastly with a great crashing from the hills within the walls, Zion, Moriah, and Ophel, as the foe advances. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: Maktesh, the mortar, is the valley where the merchants dwelt, and Jerusalem itself, lying among hills, is doomed to be the scene of its people being destroyed as corn is pounded in a mortar. The merchant people are called Canaanites in irony, for they are Canaanites in greed for gain and in idolatries, and those laden with silver carry what will prove only a burden. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: At that time God will search Jerusalem with lamps, so that no dark corner is left wherein sin can escape punishment, the Chaldeans being his instruments. The men settled on their lees are hardened like the crust on wine long left undisturbed, made stupidly secure by wealthy, undisturbed ease, and they deny that God regards human affairs or renders good to the good and evil to the evil. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: Therefore their goods shall become a booty and their houses a desolation, fulfilling the prophecy in Deuteronomy that they should build houses but not inhabit them and plant vineyards but not drink the wine. The trading people and the rich merchants are called to account for their secure and careless presumption. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: The great day of the Lord is near and hastens quickly, and it is a day of God's wrath to the utmost, in which he will show himself by taking vengeance. The mighty men shall cry bitterly in hopeless despair, for the might on which Jerusalem prides itself shall then fail utterly. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: That day is a day of wrath, trouble, and distress, devastation and desolation, darkness and gloominess, clouds and thick darkness. The Hebrew terms, similar in sound, express the dreary monotony of desolation. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: A day of the trumpet and alarm against the fortified cities and high towers: the trumpet is that of the besieging enemy, and the alarm is the war shout. The towers stood at the angles of the walls, built so that besiegers advancing might be assailed from the sides as well as in front. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: God will bring distress upon men, and they shall walk like blind men, unable to see where to turn to find escape from existing evils, because they have sinned against the Lord. Their blood shall be poured out like dust, and their flesh like refuse. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

18 COMMENTARY: Neither their silver nor their gold shall be able to deliver them in the day of the Lord's wrath, for riches profit not in the day of wrath. The whole land shall be devoured by the fire of his jealousy, his wrath jealous for his honour consuming the guilty, and he will make a complete and sudden end of all who dwell in the land. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
