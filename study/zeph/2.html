Study Notes — Zephaniah

Chapter 2

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: The prophet's design in the terrible description of the foregoing chapter was not to drive the people to despair, but to drive them to God and to their duty, and so he summons them to a national assembly. Gather yourselves together, that in a body you may humble yourselves before God, fast, pray, and seek his face, and enquire into yourselves to find the sin that provoked his displeasure; the nation is called not desired, either not desiring God or not desirable in itself, yet God says, gather together to me. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: They must act in earnest and with all speed, before the decree bring forth and before the day pass as the chaff. As the embryo lies hidden in the womb and then emerges in its due time, so God for a time hides his vengeance, yet brings it forth at the proper season, and then it will hurry the impenitent away into captivity as chaff before the wind. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: Seek the Lord, all you meek of the earth who have wrought his judgment, that is, who have obeyed his laws and made conscience of duty to him. The meek are modest, humble, and quiet in the land, yet even they must seek righteousness and seek meekness still, for those who excel in a grace must seek to excel yet more in it; it may be you shall be hid in the day of the Lord's anger, taken under special protection, whether by deliverance in this life or by being taken to heaven. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: Here begins the denunciation of the judgments of God against the neighbouring nations, first the Philistines, in verses 4 to 7. Gaza shall be forsaken, Ashkelon a desolation, Ashdod driven out at noonday, and Ekron rooted up, as the Chaldean inundation sweeps over those parts of the world. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: Woe unto the inhabitants of the sea coast, the nation of the Cherethites: the word of the Lord is against you, O Canaan, the land of the Philistines, and he will destroy it so that there shall be no inhabitant. The neighbouring nations, which assisted or rejoiced in Israel's calamity, shall drink of the same cup of trembling. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: The sea coast, emptied of its people, shall become dwellings and cottages for shepherds, and folds for flocks. What was once a populous coast of cities is reduced to pasture land. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: The coast shall be for the remnant of the house of Judah, who shall feed thereupon and lie down in the evening in the houses of Ashkelon. The Lord their God shall visit them and turn away their captivity, so that the judgment on their neighbours comforts the Jews with the thought that their case, though sad, is not singular. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: Next come the Moabites and Ammonites, in verses 8 to 11: God has heard the reproach of Moab and the revilings of the children of Ammon, whereby they reproached his people and magnified themselves against their border. The neighbouring nations are judged for the part they took against God's people. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: Therefore, as the Lord of hosts lives, Moab shall be as Sodom and the children of Ammon as Gomorrah, a place overrun with nettles and saltpits, a perpetual desolation. The residue and remnant of God's people shall spoil them and possess their lands, in retribution for their having occupied Judah's land. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: This shall they have for their pride, because they have reproached and magnified themselves against the people of the Lord of hosts. Their sin was not only against Judah, but against the Lord whose people Judah were. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: The Lord will be terrible unto them, for he will famish all the gods of the earth, and men shall worship him, every one from his place, even all the isles of the heathen. The judgment on the nations thus issues in the worship of the Lord far beyond Judah's borders. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: You Ethiopians also, you shall be slain by my sword: the third group in this denunciation, the Ethiopians and Assyrians in verses 12 to 15, is judged in turn. No distant nation is outside the reach of the Lord's sword. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: He will stretch out his hand against the north and destroy Assyria, and make Nineveh a desolation, as dry as the wilderness. The great imperial city that once threatened Judah is itself to be laid waste. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: Herds shall lie down in the midst of Nineveh, and every beast of the nation, with the cormorant and bittern lodging on the capitals of its pillars and singing in its windows. Such a heap of ruins shall this once pompous city be that wild beasts take up their abode in its houses, its fine cedar-work lies exposed, and songs of mirth are turned into hideous noises. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: This is the rejoicing city that dwelt carelessly, that said in her heart, I am, and there is none beside me: now she is become a desolation, a place for beasts to lie down in. Every one that passes by shall hiss and wag his hand in derision, for the adversities of those who were insolent and haughty in prosperity are unpitied and unlamented. — Matthew Henry
