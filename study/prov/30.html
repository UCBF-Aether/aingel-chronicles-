Study Notes — Proverbs

Chapter 30

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: The words of Agur the son of Jakeh, even the prophecy, the man spoke unto Ithiel, even unto Ithiel and Ucal, for Agur speaks of himself as wanting righteousness and having done very foolishly, and it becomes us all to have low thoughts of ourselves. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

2 COMMENTARY: Surely I am more brutish than any man, and have not the understanding of a man, for Agur speaks of himself as wanting righteousness and having done very foolishly, and it becomes us all to have low thoughts of ourselves. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

3 COMMENTARY: I neither learned wisdom, nor have the knowledge of the holy, for Agur speaks of himself as wanting revelation to guide him in truth and wisdom, and the more enlightened people are, the more they lament their ignorance and pray for clearer discoveries of God and his rich grace in Christ. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

4 COMMENTARY: Who has ascended up into heaven, or descended, who has gathered the wind in his fists, who has bound the waters in a garment, who has established all the ends of the earth, what is his name, and what is his son's name, if thou canst tell, for in this verse there is a prophetic notice of him who came down from heaven to be our Instructor and Saviour, and then ascended to be our Advocate, the Messiah distinct from the Father, his name as yet secret, and the great Redeemer in providence and grace cannot be found out to perfection. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

5 COMMENTARY: Every word of God is pure, he is a shield unto them that put their trust in him, for had it not been for Christ, the foundations of the earth had sunk under the load of the curse upon the ground for man's sin, and who, and what is the mighty One who does all this. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

6 COMMENTARY: Add thou not unto his words, lest he reprove thee, and thou be found a liar, for there is not the least ground to suspect anything wanting in God's word, and adding to his words opens the way to errors and corruptions. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

7 COMMENTARY: Two things have I required of thee, deny me them not before I die, for Agur wisely prayed for a middle state, that he might be kept at a distance from temptations, asking daily bread suited to his station, family, and real good. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

8 COMMENTARY: Remove far from me vanity and lies, give me neither poverty nor riches, feed me with food convenient for me, for Agur wisely prayed for a middle state, kept from temptations, asking daily bread suited to his station, and his prayer resembles several clauses of the Lord's prayer. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

9 COMMENTARY: Lest I be full, and deny thee, and say, Who is the LORD, or lest I be poor, and steal, and take the name of my God in vain, for when we consider how those with abundance are prone to abuse the gift, and what it is to suffer want, Agur's prayer will ever be found a wise one, though seldom offered, and our heavenly Father will supply all our need and not suffer us to want anything good. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

10 COMMENTARY: Accuse not a servant unto his master, lest he curse thee, and thou be found guilty, for slander not a servant to his master, accuse him not in small matters to make mischief. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

11 COMMENTARY: There is a generation that curses their father, and does not bless their mother, for in every age there are monsters of ingratitude who ill-treat their parents. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

12 COMMENTARY: There is a generation pure in their own eyes, and yet not washed from their filthiness, for many persuade themselves they are holy persons, whose hearts are full of sin, and who practise secret wickedness. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

13 COMMENTARY: There is a generation, O how lofty are their eyes, and their eyelids are lifted up, for there are others whose lofty pride is manifest. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

14 COMMENTARY: There is a generation, whose teeth are as swords, and their jaw teeth as knives, to devour the poor from off the earth, and the needy from among men, for there have also been cruel monsters in every age. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

15 COMMENTARY: The horseleech has two daughters, crying, Give, give, there are three things never satisfied, yea, four things say not, It is enough, for cruelty and covetousness are two daughters of the horseleech, still crying Give, give, and continually uneasy to themselves. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

16 COMMENTARY: The grave, and the barren womb, the earth not filled with water, and the fire that says not, It is enough, for four things never are satisfied, to which these devourers are compared, and those are never rich who are always coveting. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

17 COMMENTARY: The eye that mocks at his father, and despises to obey his mother, the ravens of the valley shall pick it out, and the young eagles shall eat it, for many who have come to a bad end have owned that their wicked courses began by despising their parents' authority. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

18 COMMENTARY: There be three things too wonderful for me, yea, four which I know not, for four things cannot be fully known, and the kingdom of nature is full of marvels. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

19 COMMENTARY: The way of an eagle in the air, the way of a serpent upon a rock, the way of a ship in the midst of the sea, and the way of a man with a maid, for four things cannot be fully known, and the kingdom of nature is full of marvels. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

20 COMMENTARY: Such is the way of an adulterous woman, she eats, and wipes her mouth, and says, I have done no wickedness, for the fourth is a mystery of iniquity: the cursed arts by which a vile seducer gains a female's affections, and the arts which a vile woman uses to conceal her wickedness. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

21 COMMENTARY: For three things the earth is disquieted, and for four which it cannot bear, for four sorts of persons are very troublesome. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

22 COMMENTARY: For a servant when he reigns, and a fool when he is filled with meat, for four sorts of persons are very troublesome: men of low origin and base spirit, who, getting authority, become tyrants, and foolish and violent men indulging in excesses. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

23 COMMENTARY: For an odious woman when she is married, and a handmaid heir to her mistress, for four sorts of persons are very troublesome: a woman of contentious spirit and vicious habits, and a servant who has obtained undue influence, and those advanced from low beginnings must carefully watch against the sin most easily besetting them. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

24 COMMENTARY: There be four things little upon the earth, but they are exceeding wise, for four things that are little are yet to be admired: those poor in the world and of small account, yet wise for their souls and another world. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

25 COMMENTARY: The ants are a people not strong, yet they prepare their meat in the summer, for four things little are yet to be admired: those poor in the world, yet wise for their souls and another world. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

26 COMMENTARY: The conies are but a feeble folk, yet make they their houses in the rocks, for four things little are yet to be admired: those poor in the world, yet wise for their souls and another world. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

27 COMMENTARY: The locusts have no king, yet go they forth all of them by bands, for four things little are yet to be admired: those poor in the world, yet wise for their souls and another world. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

28 COMMENTARY: The spider takes hold with her hands, and is in kings' palaces, for four things little are yet to be admired: those poor in the world, yet wise for their souls and another world. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

29 COMMENTARY: There be three things which go well, yea, four are comely in going, for we may learn from animals to go well, and also to keep our temper under all provocations. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

30 COMMENTARY: A lion strongest among beasts, and turning not away for any, for we may learn from animals to go well, and also to keep our temper under all provocations. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

31 COMMENTARY: A greyhound, an he goat also, and a king, against whom there is no rising up, for we may learn from animals to go well, and also to keep our temper under all provocations. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

32 COMMENTARY: If thou hast done foolishly in lifting up thyself, or if thou hast thought evil, lay thine hand upon thy mouth, for we must keep the evil thought in our minds from breaking out into evil speeches, and we must not stir up others' passions. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

33 COMMENTARY: Surely the churning of milk brings forth butter, and the wringing of the nose brings forth blood, so the forcing of wrath brings forth strife, for let nothing be said or done with violence, but everything with softness and calmness, and alas, how often have we done foolishly in rising up against the Lord our King, so let us humble ourselves before him, and having found peace with him, follow peace with all men. — Matthew Henry (Concise)
