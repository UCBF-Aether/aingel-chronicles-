Study Notes — Proverbs

Chapter 26

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: As snow in summer, and rain in harvest, so honour is not seemly for a fool, for honour is out of season to those unworthy and unfit for it. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

2 COMMENTARY: As the bird by wandering, as the swallow by flying, so the curse causeless shall not come, for he cursed without cause suffers no more harm than the bird flying over his head. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

3 COMMENTARY: A whip for the horse, a bridle for the ass, and a rod for the fool's back, for every creature must be dealt with according to its nature, but careless and profligate sinners will never be ruled by reason and persuasion, though some, by God's grace, are changed. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

4 COMMENTARY: Answer not a fool according to his folly, lest thou also be like unto him, for we are to fit our remarks to the man and address them to his conscience, so as may best end the debate. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

5 COMMENTARY: Answer a fool according to his folly, lest he be wise in his own conceit, for we are to fit our remarks to the man and address them to his conscience, so as may best end the debate. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

6 COMMENTARY: He who sends a message by a fool's hand cuts off the feet and drinks damage, for fools are not fit to be trusted, nor to have any honour, and wise sayings in a fool's delivery lose their usefulness. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

7 COMMENTARY: The legs of the lame are not equal, so is a parable in the mouth of fools, for fools are not fit to be trusted, nor to have honour, and wise sayings in a fool's delivery lose their usefulness. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

8 COMMENTARY: As he who binds a stone in a sling, so is he who gives honour to a fool, for fools are not fit to be trusted, nor to have honour, and wise sayings in a fool's delivery lose their usefulness. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

9 COMMENTARY: As a thorn goes up into a drunkard's hand, so is a parable in the mouths of fools, for fools are not fit to be trusted, nor to have honour, and wise sayings in a fool's delivery lose their usefulness. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

10 COMMENTARY: The great God who formed all things both rewards the fool and rewards transgressors, for this verse may declare how the Lord, Creator of all, will deal with sinners according to guilt, or how the powerful should disgrace and punish the wicked. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

11 COMMENTARY: As a dog returns to his vomit, so a fool returns to his folly, for the dog is a loathsome emblem of sinners who return to their vices. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

12 COMMENTARY: Seest thou a man wise in his own conceit, there is more hope of a fool than of him, for many have some little sense but are proud of it, describing those who think their spiritual state good when really it is very bad. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

13 COMMENTARY: The slothful man says, There is a lion in the way, a lion in the streets, for the slothful man hates everything requiring care and labour, but it is foolish to frighten ourselves from real duties by fancied difficulties, applicable to a man slothful in religion's duties. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

14 COMMENTARY: As the door turns upon its hinges, so does the slothful upon his bed, for having seen the slothful man in fear of his work, here we find him in love with his ease, and bodily ease is the sad occasion of many spiritual diseases. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

15 COMMENTARY: The slothful hides his hand in his bosom, it grieves him to bring it again to his mouth, for the sluggard is now out of bed, but might have lain there for anything he is likely to bring to pass in his work, and men who will not do their duty pretend they cannot. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

16 COMMENTARY: The sluggard is wiser in his own conceit than seven men who can render a reason, for he who takes pains in religion knows he works for a good Master, and his labour shall not be in vain. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

17 COMMENTARY: He who passes by and meddles with strife not his own is like one who takes a dog by the ears, for to make ourselves busy in other men's matters is to thrust ourselves into temptation. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

18 COMMENTARY: As a mad man who casts firebrands, arrows, and death, so is the man who deceives his neighbour and says, Am not I in sport, for he who sins in jest must repent in earnest, or his sin will be his ruin. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

19 COMMENTARY: So is the man who deceives his neighbour and says, Am not I in sport, for he who sins in jest must repent in earnest, or his sin will be his ruin. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

20 COMMENTARY: Where no wood is, there the fire goes out, so where there is no talebearer, strife ceases, for contention heats the spirit and puts families and societies into a flame, commonly kindled and kept burning by whisperers and backbiters. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

21 COMMENTARY: As coals are to burning coals, and wood to fire, so is a contentious man to kindle strife, for contention heats the spirit and puts families and societies into a flame, commonly kindled and kept burning by whisperers and backbiters. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

22 COMMENTARY: The words of a talebearer are as wounds, and they go down into the innermost parts of the belly, for contention heats the spirit and puts families into a flame, commonly kindled and kept burning by whisperers and backbiters. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

23 COMMENTARY: Burning lips and a wicked heart are like a potsherd covered with silver dross, for a wicked heart disguising itself is like a potsherd covered with the dross of silver. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

24 COMMENTARY: He who hates dissembles with his lips and lays up deceit within him, for always distrust when a man speaks fair unless you know him well, since Satan in temptation speaks fair, as he did to Eve, but it is madness to give him credit. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

25 COMMENTARY: When he speaks fair, believe him not, for there are seven abominations in his heart, for always distrust when a man speaks fair unless you know him well, since Satan speaks fair in temptation. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

26 COMMENTARY: Whose hatred is covered by deceit, his wickedness shall be shown before the whole congregation, for always distrust when a man speaks fair unless you know him well. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

27 COMMENTARY: Whoso digs a pit shall fall therein, and he who rolls a stone, it will return upon him, for what pains men take to do mischief to others, yet it is digging a pit and rolling a stone, hard work, and they prepare mischief to themselves. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

28 COMMENTARY: A lying tongue hates those afflicted by it, and a flattering mouth works ruin, for there are two sorts of lies equally detestable: a slandering lie, whose mischief everybody sees, and a flattering lie, which secretly works ruin, and a wise man will be more afraid of a flatterer than of a slanderer. — Matthew Henry (Concise)
