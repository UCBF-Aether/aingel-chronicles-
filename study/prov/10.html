Study Notes — Proverbs

Chapter 10

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: A wise son makes a glad father while a foolish son is his mother's heaviness, for the comfort of parents much depends on their children, suggesting motives to duty for both. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

2 COMMENTARY: Treasures of wickedness profit nothing, but righteousness delivers from death, for though the righteous may be poor, the Lord will not suffer him to want what is needful for spiritual life. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

3 COMMENTARY: The LORD will not suffer the righteous soul to famish, but casts away the substance of the wicked, for the poor righteous man is supplied with what spiritual life needs. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

4 COMMENTARY: A slack hand makes poor, but the diligent hand makes rich, for those fervent in spirit, serving the Lord, are likely to be rich in faith and in good works. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

5 COMMENTARY: Gathering in summer marks the wise son, while sleeping in harvest shames, for here is just blame of those who trifle away opportunities both for here and hereafter. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

6 COMMENTARY: Blessings are upon the head of the just, while violence covers the wicked mouth, for abundance of real blessings shall abide on good men. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

7 COMMENTARY: The memory of the just is blessed, but the name of the wicked shall rot, for both just and wicked must die, yet between their souls there is a vast difference. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

8 COMMENTARY: The wise in heart receives commandments, while a prating fool shall fall, for the wise in heart puts his knowledge into practice. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

9 COMMENTARY: He who walks uprightly walks surely, but the perverter of his ways shall be known, for dissemblers, after all their shuffling, will be exposed. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

10 COMMENTARY: Winking with the eye causes sorrow, while a prating fool shall fall, for trick and artifice will be no excuse for iniquity. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

11 COMMENTARY: The righteous mouth is a well of life, while violence covers the wicked mouth, for the good man's mouth is always open to teach, comfort, and correct others. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

12 COMMENTARY: Hatred stirs up strifes, but love covers all sins, for where hatred reigns everything stirs strife, while bearing with each other preserves peace and harmony. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

13 COMMENTARY: Wisdom is found in the lips of the understanding, but a rod is for the back of him void of understanding, for those who foolishly go on in wicked ways prepare rods for themselves. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

14 COMMENTARY: Wise men lay up knowledge, but the foolish mouth is near destruction, for useful knowledge must be laid up so it is not to seek when wanted, gained by reading, hearing, meditation, prayer, and faith in Christ. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

15 COMMENTARY: The rich man's wealth is his strong city, while the poor's destruction is their poverty, reflecting common mistakes about outward condition: wealth exposes to dangers, while a content poor man with a good conscience lives comfortably by faith. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

16 COMMENTARY: The labour of the righteous tends to life, while the fruit of the wicked is sin, for even a righteous man with no more than hard work earns finds that labour tending to life. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

17 COMMENTARY: He who keeps instruction is in the way of life, but he who refuses reproof errs, like a traveller who has missed his way and cannot bear to be told and shown the right way. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

18 COMMENTARY: Hiding hatred with lying lips and uttering slander mark a fool, for he is especially a fool who thinks to hide anything from God, and malice is no better. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

19 COMMENTARY: In the multitude of words sin is not lacking, but he who refrains his lips is wise, for those who speak much speak much amiss, and he who checks himself consults his own peace. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

20 COMMENTARY: The tongue of the just is choice silver, while the wicked heart is little worth, for the just tongue is sincere, freed from guile and evil design. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

21 COMMENTARY: The lips of the righteous feed many, but fools die for want of wisdom, for pious discourse is spiritual food to the needy, and fools die for want of a heart, that is, for want of thought. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

22 COMMENTARY: The blessing of the LORD makes rich and adds no sorrow, for truly desirable wealth has no vexation in enjoyment, no grief in loss, no guilt in abuse, since what comes from God's love has God's grace as companion. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

23 COMMENTARY: It is sport to a fool to do mischief, but a man of understanding has wisdom, for only foolish and wicked men divert themselves with harming others or tempting to sin. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

24 COMMENTARY: The fear of the wicked shall come upon him, but the desire of the righteous shall be granted, for the largest desire of eternal blessings the righteous can form will be granted. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

25 COMMENTARY: As the whirlwind passes, so the wicked is no more, but the righteous is an everlasting foundation, for the course of prosperous sinners soon spends itself and is gone. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

26 COMMENTARY: As vinegar to teeth and smoke to eyes, so is the sluggard to those who send him, vexing his employer as vinegar sets teeth on edge and smoke makes eyes smart. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

27 COMMENTARY: The fear of the LORD prolongs days, but the years of the wicked shall be shortened, for he who loves life should fear God, securing life enough here and eternal life hereafter. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

28 COMMENTARY: The hope of the righteous shall be gladness, but the expectation of the wicked shall perish, for fearing God secures life in this world and eternal life in the other. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

29 COMMENTARY: The way of the LORD is strength to the upright, but destruction to workers of iniquity, for the believer grows stronger in faith and obeys with increased delight. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

30 COMMENTARY: The righteous shall never be removed, but the wicked shall not inhabit the earth, for the wicked would gladly make earth their home for ever, but they must die and leave their idols behind. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

31 COMMENTARY: The mouth of the just brings forth wisdom, but the froward tongue shall be cut out, for a good man discourses wisely for others' benefit, while the wicked speaks what displeases God. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

32 COMMENTARY: The lips of the righteous know what is acceptable, but the wicked mouth speaks frowardness, and the righteous is kept by God's power, inseparable from his love in Christ Jesus. — Matthew Henry (Concise)
