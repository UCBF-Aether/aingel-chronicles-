Study Notes — Proverbs

Chapter 9

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Wisdom building her house and hewing seven pillars is the stately palace Henry pictures: heaven as Wisdom's house, and the church supported by God's power and promise as by seven pillars. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

2 COMMENTARY: Killing beasts, mingling wine, and furnishing the table is the sumptuous sacred feast, for Christ offered himself a sacrifice, and his flesh and blood are meat and drink indeed. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

3 COMMENTARY: Sending forth maidens who cry on the highest places of the city is the gospel ministry commissioned to call all they meet, even in highways and hedges, to Wisdom's feast. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

4 COMMENTARY: Whoso is simple, let him turn in hither, and him wanting understanding she addresses, for Wisdom invites precisely those who most need what she gives, excluding none who do not exclude themselves. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

5 COMMENTARY: Come, eat of my bread and drink of the wine mingled, is the invitation to taste true pleasures in the knowledge and fear of God, feeding by faith on the gospel promises. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

6 COMMENTARY: Forsaking the foolish and living, going in the way of understanding, requires breaking from bad company and joining those who walk in wisdom, not merely leaving vice behind. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

7 COMMENTARY: Reproving a scorner gets shame, and rebuking a wicked man gets a blot, for those who scorn means already given have forfeited further means, and reproof only exasperates them. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

8 COMMENTARY: Reprove not a scorner lest he hate thee, but rebuke a wise man and he will love thee, for the wise reckon faithful dealers friends and thank them for plain dealing. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

9 COMMENTARY: Giving instruction to a wise man makes him yet wiser, and teaching a just man increases his learning, for none must think themselves too wise to learn or too good to be better. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

10 COMMENTARY: The fear of the LORD is the beginning of wisdom, and knowledge of the holy is understanding, for reverence of God's majesty is the first step of true religion, and knowledge of holy things its best understanding. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

11 COMMENTARY: By Wisdom days are multiplied and years of life increased, contributing to bodily health here and to endless days in heaven, for there is no true life but in the end of religion's way. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

12 COMMENTARY: If thou be wise, thou shalt be wise for thyself, but if thou scornest, thou alone shalt bear it, for man cannot profit God, and the scorner bears both blame and loss himself. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

13 COMMENTARY: The foolish woman, clamorous, simple, knowing nothing, is Folly herself, especially carnal sensual pleasure, which defiles the mind and stupefies conscience more than anything else. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

14 COMMENTARY: Sitting at her house door, on a seat in the high places of the city, Folly affects authority and fashion, watching for prey not to do good but to entice. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

15 COMMENTARY: Calling to passers-by who go right on their ways shows the tempter pursuing even the well-educated who set out hopefully, calling after them when they will not look toward her. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

16 COMMENTARY: Whoso is simple, let him turn in hither, is Folly retorting wisdom's charge, calling the virtuous simple to cure them of religion's restraints, though the day will declare who are the fools. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

17 COMMENTARY: Stolen waters are sweet, and bread eaten in secret is pleasant, is the bait of prohibited lusts and dishonest gain, prized precisely because forbidden, in defiance of God's law. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

18 COMMENTARY: He knows not that the dead are there, and her guests are in the depths of hell, for those living in pleasure are dead in trespasses, already under sin's power and conscience's terrors. — Matthew Henry (Complete)
