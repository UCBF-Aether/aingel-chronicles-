Study Notes — Proverbs

Chapter 14

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Every wise woman builds her house, but the foolish plucks it down with her hands, for a woman without fear of God, wilful and wasteful, indulging ease, will as certainly ruin her family as if she pulled the house down. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

2 COMMENTARY: He who walks uprightly fears the LORD, but the perverse in his ways despises him, for here are grace and sin in their true colours: those despising God's precepts and promises despise God and all his power and mercy. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

3 COMMENTARY: In the mouth of the foolish is a rod of pride, but the lips of the wise preserve them, for pride grows from bitterness in the heart and the root must be plucked up, while prudent words of wise men get them out of difficulties. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

4 COMMENTARY: Where no oxen are, the crib is clean, but much increase is by the strength of the ox, for there can be no advantage without something, though of little moment, that will affright the indolent. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

5 COMMENTARY: A faithful witness will not lie, but a false witness will utter lies, for a conscientious witness will not dare represent anything otherwise than according to his knowledge. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

6 COMMENTARY: A scorner seeks wisdom and finds it not, but knowledge is easy to him who understands, for a scorner treats Divine things with contempt, while he who feels his ignorance and unworthiness searches the Scriptures humbly. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

7 COMMENTARY: Go from the presence of a foolish man when thou perceivest not lips of knowledge, for we discover a wicked man if there is no savour of piety in his discourse. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

8 COMMENTARY: The wisdom of the prudent is to understand his way, but the folly of fools is deceit, for we are travellers whose concern is to get to journey's end, understanding the rules and ends we walk toward, while the bad man cheats himself and goes on in his mistake. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

9 COMMENTARY: Fools make a mock at sin, but among the righteous there is favour, for foolish and profane men consider sin a mere trifle to be made light of, but those who make light of sin make light of Christ. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

10 COMMENTARY: The heart knows his own bitterness, and a stranger does not intermeddle with his joy, for we do not know what stings of conscience torment the prosperous sinner, nor does the world know the peace of a serious Christian even in poverty and sickness. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

11 COMMENTARY: The house of the wicked shall be overthrown, but the tabernacle of the upright shall flourish, for sin ruins many great families, while righteousness often raises and strengthens even mean families. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

12 COMMENTARY: There is a way seeming right to a man, but its end is the ways of death, for the ways of carelessness, worldliness, and sensuality seem right to those walking in them, but self-deceivers prove self-destroyers. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

13 COMMENTARY: Even in laughter the heart is sorrowful, and the end of mirth is heaviness, showing the vanity of carnal mirth, which Henry joins to the self-deceiving ways that seem right but end in death. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

14 COMMENTARY: The backslider in heart shall be filled with his own ways, and a good man satisfied from himself, for of all sinners backsliders will have the most terror when they reflect on their own ways. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

15 COMMENTARY: The simple believes every word, but the prudent looks well to his going, for eager readiness to believe what others say has ever proved mischievous, while the spiritually wise depends on the Saviour alone and takes heed to God's word. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

16 COMMENTARY: A wise man fears and departs from evil, but the fool rages and is confident, for holy fear guards against everything unholy. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

17 COMMENTARY: He soon angry deals foolishly, and a man of wicked devices is hated, for an angry man is to be pitied as well as blamed, but the revengeful is more hateful. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

18 COMMENTARY: The simple inherit folly, but the prudent are crowned with knowledge, for sin is the shame of sinners, but wisdom is the honour of the wise. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

19 COMMENTARY: The evil bow before the good, and the wicked at the gates of the righteous, for even bad men acknowledge the excellency of God's people. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

20 COMMENTARY: The poor is hated even of his own neighbour, but the rich has many friends, for friendship in the world is governed by self-interest, while it is good to have God our Friend, who will not desert us. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

21 COMMENTARY: He who despises his neighbour sins, but he who has mercy on the poor, happy is he, for to despise a man for his employment or appearance is a sin. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

22 COMMENTARY: Do they not err who devise evil, while mercy and truth shall be to those devising good, for how wisely do those consult their own interest who not only do good but devise it. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

23 COMMENTARY: In all labour there is profit, but the talk of the lips tends only to penury, for labour of head or hand will turn to some good account, while religion running all out in talk and noise comes to nothing. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

24 COMMENTARY: The crown of the wise is their riches, but the foolishness of fools is folly, for the riches of men of wisdom and piety enlarge their usefulness. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

25 COMMENTARY: A true witness delivers souls, but a deceitful witness speaks lies, for an upright man will venture the displeasure of the greatest to bring truth to light. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

26 COMMENTARY: In the fear of the LORD is strong confidence, and his children shall have a place of refuge, for those who fear the Lord so as to obey and serve him have strong ground of confidence and will be preserved. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

27 COMMENTARY: The fear of the LORD is a fountain of life, to depart from the snares of death, for those fearing the Lord shall be preserved, and we must seek this Fountain of life to escape death's snares. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

28 COMMENTARY: In the multitude of people is the king's honour, but in the want of people is the prince's destruction, for all who wish well to Christ's kingdom should do what they can that many may be added to his church. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

29 COMMENTARY: He slow to wrath is of great understanding, but he hasty of spirit exalts folly, for a mild, patient man learns of Christ, who is Wisdom itself, while unbridled passion is folly made known. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

30 COMMENTARY: A sound heart is the life of the flesh, but envy the rottenness of the bones, for an upright, contented, and benevolent mind tends to health. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

31 COMMENTARY: He who oppresses the poor reproaches his Maker, but he who honours him has mercy on the poor, for to oppress the poor is to reproach our Creator. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

32 COMMENTARY: The wicked is driven away in his wickedness, but the righteous has hope in his death, for the wicked man's soul is forced from him and he dies in his sins, while godly men have the blessed hope God, who cannot lie, has given them. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

33 COMMENTARY: Wisdom rests in the heart of him who has understanding, but that in the midst of fools is made known, for wisdom possesses the heart and thus regulates affections and tempers. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

34 COMMENTARY: Righteousness exalts a nation, but sin is a reproach to any people, for piety and holiness always promote industry, sobriety, and honesty. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

35 COMMENTARY: The king's favour is toward a wise servant, but his wrath is against him who causes shame, for the great King will reward faithful servants who honour his gospel by discharging their stations' duties, despising not the lowest services. — Matthew Henry (Concise)
