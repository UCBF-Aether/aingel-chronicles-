Study Notes — Proverbs

Chapter 13

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: A wise son hears his father's instruction, but a scorner hears not rebuke, for there is great hope of those who reverence their parents, and little hope of any who will not hear faithful dealers. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

2 COMMENTARY: A man shall eat good by the fruit of his mouth, but the soul of transgressors shall eat violence, for by our words we must be justified or condemned. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

3 COMMENTARY: He who keeps his mouth keeps his life, but he who opens wide his lips shall have destruction, for thinking before speaking and suppressing evil thought keeps the soul from much guilt and grief, while many are ruined by an ungoverned tongue. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

4 COMMENTARY: The soul of the sluggard desires and has nothing, but the soul of the diligent shall be made fat, for the slothful desire the diligent's gains but hate the diligent's pains, and this is especially true of the soul. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

5 COMMENTARY: A righteous man hates lying, but a wicked man is loathsome and comes to shame, for where sin reigns the man is loathsome, and an awake conscience would abhor itself and repent in dust and ashes. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

6 COMMENTARY: Righteousness keeps him upright in the way, but wickedness overthrows the sinner, for an honest desire to do right preserves from fatal mistakes better than a thousand fine-drawn distinctions. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

7 COMMENTARY: There is that makes himself rich yet has nothing, and that makes himself poor yet has great riches, for some really poor trade as if rich, which is sin and shame, while some really rich would be thought poor, and fearing Christians spiritually rich yet thinking themselves poor make themselves poor by doubts and griefs. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

8 COMMENTARY: The ransom of a man's life are his riches, but the poor hears not rebuke, for great riches often tempt to violence against their possessors, while the poor are free from such perils. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

9 COMMENTARY: The light of the righteous rejoices, but the lamp of the wicked shall be put out, for the righteous light is as the sun, eclipsed and clouded yet continuing, while the wicked light is a lamp of their own kindling, easily put out. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

10 COMMENTARY: Only by pride comes contention, but with the well advised is wisdom, for all contentions, between persons, families, churches, or nations, are begun and carried forward by pride. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

11 COMMENTARY: Wealth gotten by vanity shall be diminished, but he who gathers by labour shall increase, for wealth gotten by dishonesty or vice has a secret curse that speedily wastes it. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

12 COMMENTARY: Hope deferred makes the heart sick, but when desire comes it is a tree of life, for delay of anxiously hoped things is painful, obtaining them pleasant, and spiritual blessings are chiefly intended. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

13 COMMENTARY: Whoso despises the word shall be destroyed, but he who fears the commandment shall be rewarded, for he who stands in awe of God and reverences his word escapes destruction and is rewarded for godly fear. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

14 COMMENTARY: The law of the wise is a fountain of life, to depart from the snares of death, for the rule by which the wise regulate conduct is a fountain yielding life and happiness. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

15 COMMENTARY: Good understanding gives favour, but the way of transgressors is hard, for the way of sinners is hard upon others and hard to the sinner himself, the service of sin being slavery. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

16 COMMENTARY: Every prudent man deals with knowledge, but a fool lays open his folly, for it is folly to talk of things of which we know nothing and to undertake what we are no way fit for. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

17 COMMENTARY: A wicked messenger falls into mischief, but a faithful ambassador is health, for those wicked and false to Christ and to souls do mischief and fall into it, while the faithful find sound words healing to others and themselves. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

18 COMMENTARY: Poverty and shame shall be to him who refuses instruction, but he who regards reproof shall be honoured, for he who scorns to be taught will certainly be brought down. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

19 COMMENTARY: The desire accomplished is sweet to the soul, but it is abomination to fools to depart from evil, for men have strong desires after happiness, yet none who will not leave their sins can expect anything truly sweet to their souls. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

20 COMMENTARY: He who walks with wise men shall be wise, but a companion of fools shall be destroyed, for multitudes are brought to ruin by bad company, and all who make themselves wicked will be destroyed. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

21 COMMENTARY: Evil pursues sinners, but to the righteous good shall be repaid, for when God pursues sinners he is sure to overtake them, and he will reward the righteous. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

22 COMMENTARY: A good man leaves an inheritance to his children's children, and the wealth of the sinner is laid up for the just, for the servant of God not anxious about riches takes the best method of providing for his children. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

23 COMMENTARY: Much food is in the tillage of the poor, but there is that is destroyed for want of judgment, for the poor yet industrious thrive though homely, while the greatly rich are often brought to poverty for want of judgment. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

24 COMMENTARY: He who spares his rod hates his son, but he who loves him chastens him betimes, for false indulgence permitting sinful habits to gather strength acts as if hating the child, bringing sorrow here and misery hereafter. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

25 COMMENTARY: The righteous eats to the satisfying of his soul, but the belly of the wicked shall want, for the wicked's sensual appetites always crave, while the righteous feeds on the word and ordinances, satisfied with gospel promises and Christ, the Bread of life. — Matthew Henry (Concise)
