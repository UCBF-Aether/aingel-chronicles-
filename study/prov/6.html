Study Notes — Proverbs

Chapter 6

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Being surety for a friend, striking hands with a stranger, is the rash engagement Henry warns against, since it brings poverty and ruin into families. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

2 COMMENTARY: The surety is snared with the words of his mouth and taken with them, for a bond is soon sealed with a word yet not easily got clear of. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

3 COMMENTARY: Having come into a friend's hand, the counsel is to go, humble oneself, and make sure the friend, for threatening only provokes, while humble entreaty may discharge the bond. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

4 COMMENTARY: Giving no sleep to the eyes nor slumber to the eyelids pictures the urgency of escaping the snare, since delays are dangerous and feeble efforts will not serve. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

5 COMMENTARY: Deliverance as a roe from the hunter and a bird from the fowler is the speed Henry urges, sparing no pains and sticking at no submission to get affairs into a good posture. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

6 COMMENTARY: The sluggard is sent to school to the ant, to consider her ways and be wise, for slothfulness is as sure a way to poverty as rash suretiship, though not so short. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

7 COMMENTARY: The ant has no guide, overseer, or ruler, yet works of herself, which aggravates man's sloth, since he has reason, conscience, parents, and ministers to quicken him. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

8 COMMENTARY: Providing meat in summer and gathering food in harvest teaches prudent foresight: in gathering time treasure up for a spending time, and in life provide for death and eternity. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

9 COMMENTARY: How long wilt thou sleep, and when wilt thou arise, is the rousing question to the sluggard, applicable both to sloth in duty and to security in sin and danger. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

10 COMMENTARY: Yet a little sleep, a little slumber, a little folding of the hands exposes the sluggard's self-deceit, for the more a slothful temper is indulged the more it prevails. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

11 COMMENTARY: Poverty comes as one that travels and want as an armed man, silently and step by step, then irresistibly, leaving the slothful stripped as by a highwayman. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

12 COMMENTARY: The naughty person, a man of Belial, walks with a froward mouth, making a trade of evil, especially with his tongue, in lying and perverseness. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

13 COMMENTARY: Winking with eyes, speaking with feet, teaching with fingers shows the mischief-maker's artful design, carrying plots by signals so as not to be suspected. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

14 COMMENTARY: Frowardness in the heart, devising mischief continually and sowing discord, is downright malice for mischief's sake, resembling the devil in subtlety and ill nature. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

15 COMMENTARY: His calamity comes suddenly and he is broken without remedy, for ruin without warning and without relief punishes the arts used to surprise others into snares. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

16 COMMENTARY: Six things the LORD hates, yea seven are an abomination, are sins injurious to neighbours, showing God's good will to mankind in hating what harms human society. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

17 COMMENTARY: A proud look, a lying tongue, and hands shedding innocent blood are the first three of the hated seven: pride at the bottom of much sin, lying abhorred by God and good men, and bloodshed bearing the devil's image. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

18 COMMENTARY: A heart devising wicked imaginations and feet swift to run to mischief are hated for their craft and eagerness in sin, which may shame the cold diligence of the good. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

19 COMMENTARY: A false witness speaking lies and one sowing discord among brethren complete the seven, for false testimony injures every interest of a neighbour, and the God of love and peace hates the sower of discord. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

20 COMMENTARY: Keeping the father's commandment and not forsaking the mother's law is the general exhortation to adhere faithfully to the word of God as guide in all actions. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

21 COMMENTARY: Binding them continually on the heart and tying them about the neck means never laying God's word by, but keeping it ready as guard and ornament. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

22 COMMENTARY: When thou goest it shall lead thee, when thou sleepest it shall keep thee, when thou awakest it shall talk with thee: the word is guide, guard, and companion to one who binds it on the heart. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

23 COMMENTARY: The commandment is a lamp and the law is light, and reproofs of instruction are the way of life, for reproofs that show faults and teach better are the road to eternal life. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

24 COMMENTARY: The word keeps the son from the evil woman and the flattery of a strange woman's tongue, for that flattery pretends love but intends ruin. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

25 COMMENTARY: Lusting not after her beauty in the heart nor being taken with her eyelids is the distance Henry urges, since her looks are snares that wound and kill. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

26 COMMENTARY: By a whorish woman a man is brought to a piece of bread, and the adulteress hunts for the precious life, for the sin impoverishes and threatens death itself. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

27 COMMENTARY: Taking fire into the bosom without burning clothes is impossible, and so is venturing on this temptation without burning, for the way of the sin is downhill. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

28 COMMENTARY: Going upon hot coals without burning feet is impossible, and likewise he who plays with this fire cannot preserve his innocence. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

29 COMMENTARY: He who goes in to his neighbour's wife and touches her shall not be innocent, for God will not hold him guiltless, however he presumes he may escape punishment. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

30 COMMENTARY: Men do not despise a thief who steals to satisfy his soul when hungry, for hunger extenuates the crime, though it does not excuse it from guilt. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

31 COMMENTARY: If found, the thief restores sevenfold and gives all the substance of his house, yet his blood is not attainted, for theft's penalty is pecuniary, not death. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

32 COMMENTARY: Whoso commits adultery lacks understanding and destroys his own soul, for the adulterer cannot plead want as the thief can, only wantonness breaking God's law. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

33 COMMENTARY: A wound and dishonour he shall get, and his reproach shall not be wiped away, for though repentance may remove guilt, the infamy sticks to memory. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

34 COMMENTARY: Jealousy is the rage of a man who will not spare in the day of vengeance, for the injured husband will be as severe an avenger of his honour as of a brother's blood. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

35 COMMENTARY: He will not regard any ransom nor rest content though many gifts are given, for no bribe atones for adultery as substance might atone for theft. — Matthew Henry (Complete)
