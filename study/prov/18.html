Study Notes — Proverbs

Chapter 18

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Through desire a man, having separated himself, seeks and intermeddles with all wisdom, for if we would get knowledge and grace, we must try all methods of improving ourselves. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

2 COMMENTARY: A fool has no delight in understanding, but that his heart may discover itself, for those who make nothing to purpose of learning or religion have only the design of making a show. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

3 COMMENTARY: When the wicked comes, then comes also contempt, and with ignominy reproach, for as soon as sin entered, shame followed. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

4 COMMENTARY: The words of a man's mouth are as deep waters, and the wellspring of wisdom as a flowing brook, for the wellspring of wisdom in a believer's heart continually supplies words of wisdom. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

5 COMMENTARY: It is not good to accept the person of the wicked, to overthrow the righteous in judgment, for the merits of a cause must be looked to, not the person. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

6 COMMENTARY: A fool's lips enter into contention, and his mouth calls for strokes, for what mischief bad men do to themselves by their ungoverned tongues. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

7 COMMENTARY: A fool's mouth is his destruction, and his lips are the snare of his soul, for what mischief bad men do to themselves by their ungoverned tongues. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

8 COMMENTARY: The words of a talebearer are as wounds, going down into the innermost parts of the belly, for how base are those who sow contention, and what fatal effects may be expected from small beginnings of jealousy. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

9 COMMENTARY: He slothful in his work is brother to him who is a great waster, for omissions of duty, and in duty, are fatal to the soul, as well as commissions of sin. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

10 COMMENTARY: The name of the LORD is a strong tower, and the righteous run into it and are safe, for Divine power made known in and through our Lord Jesus Christ forms a strong tower for the believer who relies on the Lord. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

11 COMMENTARY: The rich man's wealth is his strong city, and as a high wall in his own conceit, yet how deceitful that defence is: it will fail when most in need, exposing him to the wrath of the Judge he despised as Saviour. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

12 COMMENTARY: Before destruction the heart of man is haughty, and before honour is humility, for after the heart is lifted up with pride a fall comes, but honour shall be humility's reward. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

13 COMMENTARY: He who answers a matter before he hears it, it is folly and shame to him, for eagerness with self-conceit will expose to shame. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

14 COMMENTARY: The spirit of a man will sustain his infirmity, but a wounded spirit who can bear, for firmness of mind supports under many pains, but when conscience is tortured with remorse no human fortitude can bear the misery. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

15 COMMENTARY: The heart of the prudent gets knowledge, and the ear of the wise seeks knowledge, for we must get knowledge not only into our heads but into our hearts. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

16 COMMENTARY: A man's gift makes room for him and brings him before great men, for blessed be the Lord who makes us welcome to his throne without money and without price, and may his gifts make room for him in our souls. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

17 COMMENTARY: He first in his own cause seems just, but his neighbour comes and searches him, for it is well to listen to our enemies, that we may form a better judgment of ourselves. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

18 COMMENTARY: The lot causes contentions to cease and parts between the mighty, for it was customary sometimes to refer matters to God by casting lots with solemn prayer. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

19 COMMENTARY: A brother offended is harder to be won than a strong city, and their contentions are like bars of a castle, for great care must be taken to prevent quarrels among relations and those under obligations to each other. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

20 COMMENTARY: A man's belly shall be satisfied with the fruit of his mouth, and with the increase of his lips shall he be filled, for the belly is here put for the heart, and what it is filled with, our satisfaction and inward peace will be accordingly. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

21 COMMENTARY: Death and life are in the power of the tongue, and they who love it shall eat its fruit, for many a one has caused his own death, or others', by a false or injurious tongue. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

22 COMMENTARY: Whoso finds a wife finds a good thing and obtains favour of the LORD, for a good wife is a great blessing to a man and a token of Divine favour. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

23 COMMENTARY: The poor uses entreaties, but the rich answers roughly, for poverty tells men they must not order or demand, and at God's throne of grace we are all poor and must use entreaties. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

24 COMMENTARY: A man who has friends must show himself friendly, and there is a friend who sticks closer than a brother, for Christ Jesus never will forsake those who trust in and love him. — Matthew Henry (Concise)
