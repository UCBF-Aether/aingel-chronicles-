Study Notes — Proverbs

Chapter 11

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: A false balance is abomination to the LORD, but a just weight is his delight, for however men make light of short weight or measure, and however common the crime, God abhors it. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

2 COMMENTARY: When pride comes, shame comes, but with the lowly is wisdom, for considering how safe, quiet, and easy the humble are, we see wisdom dwells with the lowly. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

3 COMMENTARY: The integrity of the upright guides them, while perverseness destroys transgressors, for an honest man's fixed principles make his way plain. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

4 COMMENTARY: Riches profit not in the day of wrath, but righteousness delivers from death, for riches stand men in no stead in the day of death. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

5 COMMENTARY: The righteousness of the perfect directs his way, but the wicked falls by his own wickedness, for the ways of wickedness are dangerous and sin is its own punishment. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

6 COMMENTARY: The righteousness of the upright delivers them, but transgressors are taken in their own naughtiness, for the ways of wickedness are dangerous and sin punishes itself. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

7 COMMENTARY: When a wicked man dies, his expectation perishes and the hope of unjust men perishes, for when a godly man dies his fears vanish, but when a wicked man dies his hopes vanish. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

8 COMMENTARY: The righteous is delivered out of trouble and the wicked comes in his stead, for the righteous are often wonderfully kept from dangerous situations, and the ungodly go in their stead. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

9 COMMENTARY: A hypocrite with his mouth destroys his neighbour, but through knowledge the just are delivered, for hypocrites delude men into error and sin by artful objections against God's word. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

10 COMMENTARY: When it goes well with the righteous the city rejoices, and when the wicked perish there is shouting, for nations prosper when wicked men are cast down. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

11 COMMENTARY: By the blessing of the upright the city is exalted, but it is overthrown by the mouth of the wicked, for nations prosper when wicked men are cast down. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

12 COMMENTARY: He void of wisdom despises his neighbour, but a man of understanding holds his peace, for a man of understanding does not judge others by their success. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

13 COMMENTARY: A talebearer reveals secrets, but a faithful spirit conceals the matter, for a faithful man will not disclose what he is trusted with unless God's honour and society's real good require it. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

14 COMMENTARY: Where no counsel is, the people fall, but in the multitude of counsellors there is safety, for we shall often find it to our advantage to advise with others. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

15 COMMENTARY: He who is surety for a stranger shall smart for it, while he who hates suretiship is sure, for family welfare, peace, and ability to pay just debts must not be endangered; yet Christ became Surety even for enemies. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

16 COMMENTARY: A gracious woman retains honour, as strong men retain riches, for a pious and discreet woman keeps esteem and respect as strong men keep possession of wealth. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

17 COMMENTARY: The merciful man does good to his own soul, but the cruel troubles his own flesh, for a cruel, froward, ill-natured man vexes those who should be as his own flesh, and punishes himself. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

18 COMMENTARY: The wicked works a deceitful work, but to him who sows righteousness shall be a sure reward, for he who makes it his business to do good shall have a reward as sure as eternal truth can make it. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

19 COMMENTARY: As righteousness tends to life, so he who pursues evil pursues it to his own death, for true holiness is true happiness, and the more violent in sinful pursuits, the more a man hastens his own destruction. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

20 COMMENTARY: They of froward heart are abomination to the LORD, but the upright in their way are his delight, for nothing is more hateful to God than hypocrisy and double dealing, while he delights in upright aim and action. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

21 COMMENTARY: Though hand join in hand, the wicked shall not be unpunished, but the seed of the righteous shall be delivered, for joining together in sin shall not protect sinners. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

22 COMMENTARY: As a jewel of gold in a swine's snout, so is a fair woman without discretion, for beauty is abused by those without discretion or modesty, as are all bodily endowments. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

23 COMMENTARY: The desire of the righteous is only good, but the expectation of the wicked is wrath, for the wicked desire mischief to others, but it shall return upon themselves. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

24 COMMENTARY: There is that scatters and yet increases, and one who withholds more than is meet tends to poverty, for a man may grow poor by not paying just debts, not relieving the poor, and not allowing needful expenses. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

25 COMMENTARY: The liberal soul shall be made fat, and he who waters shall be watered himself, for in temporal and spiritual things God commonly deals with his people according to the measure they deal with their brethren. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

26 COMMENTARY: He who withholds corn, the people curse him, but blessing is on him who sells it, for we must not hoard God's bounty merely for our own advantage. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

27 COMMENTARY: He who diligently seeks good procures favour, but he who seeks mischief, it shall come to him, for seeking mischief is set against seeking good: those not doing good are doing hurt, even to themselves. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

28 COMMENTARY: He who trusts in riches shall fall, but the righteous shall flourish as a branch, for the true believer is a branch of the living Vine, fruitful when world-rooted men wither. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

29 COMMENTARY: He who troubles his own house shall inherit the wind, and the fool shall be servant to the wise of heart, for one who brings trouble on himself and his family by carelessness or wickedness cannot keep what he gets. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

30 COMMENTARY: The fruit of the righteous is a tree of life, and he who wins souls is wise, for the righteous are as trees of life, whose influence supports and nourishes spiritual life in many. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

31 COMMENTARY: Behold, the righteous shall be recompensed in the earth, much more the wicked and the sinner, for even the righteous when they offend meet sharp corrections, much more the wicked their due reward. — Matthew Henry (Concise)
