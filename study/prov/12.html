Study Notes — Proverbs

Chapter 12

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Whoso loves instruction loves knowledge, but he who hates reproof is brutish, for those with grace delight in instructions given, while those who stifle convictions are like brutes. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

2 COMMENTARY: A good man obtains favour of the LORD, but a man of wicked devices will he condemn, for one who covers selfish and vicious designs under religion or friendship will be condemned. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

3 COMMENTARY: A man shall not be established by wickedness, but the root of the righteous shall not be moved, for sinful arts may advance men but cannot settle them, while those rooted in Christ by faith are firmly fixed. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

4 COMMENTARY: A virtuous woman is a crown to her husband, but she who makes ashamed is rottenness in his bones, for a pious, prudent wife who minds her household and bears crosses is her husband's honour and comfort, while her reverse preys on and consumes him. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

5 COMMENTARY: The thoughts of the righteous are right, but the counsels of the wicked are deceit, for thoughts are not free: they are under Divine knowledge and therefore under Divine command, and acting with trick and design is a man's shame. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

6 COMMENTARY: The words of the wicked lie in wait for blood, but the mouth of the upright delivers them, for wicked people speak mischief to neighbours, while one good word may sometimes do a good work. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

7 COMMENTARY: The wicked are overthrown and are not, but the house of the righteous shall stand, for God's blessing is often continued to godly families while the wicked are overthrown. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

8 COMMENTARY: A man shall be commended according to his wisdom, but he of perverse heart shall be despised, for the apostles showed wisdom by glorying in shame for Christ's name. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

9 COMMENTARY: He despised yet having a servant is better than he who honours himself and lacks bread, for a humble man who gets bread by his own labour is happier than one glorying in birth or attire while wanting necessaries. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

10 COMMENTARY: A righteous man regards the life of his beast, but the tender mercies of the wicked are cruel, for a godly man would not put even an animal to needless pain. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

11 COMMENTARY: He who tills his land shall be satisfied with bread, but he who follows vain persons is void of understanding, for it is wisdom to mind business and follow an honest calling, and folly to neglect it. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

12 COMMENTARY: The wicked desires the net of evil men, but the root of the righteous yields fruit, for when the ungodly see others prosper by sin they wish to act likewise, while the root of grace in the righteous produces other desires. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

13 COMMENTARY: The wicked is snared by the transgression of his lips, but the just shall come out of trouble, for many a man has paid dear in this world for the transgression of his lips. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

14 COMMENTARY: A man shall be satisfied with good by the fruit of his mouth, and the recompense of his hands rendered to him, for when men use their tongues aright, to teach and comfort others, they enjoy acceptance through Christ Jesus. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

15 COMMENTARY: The way of a fool is right in his own eyes, but he who hearkens to counsel is wise, for a fool in Scripture means a wicked man acting contrary to wisdom from above, whose rule is to do what is right in his own eyes. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

16 COMMENTARY: A fool's wrath is presently known, but a prudent man covers shame, for a foolish man is soon angry and hasty in expressing it, while it is kindness to ourselves to make light of injuries instead of making the worst of them. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

17 COMMENTARY: He who speaks truth shows forth righteousness, but a false witness deceit, for it is good for all to dread and detest lying and to be governed by honesty. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

18 COMMENTARY: There is that speaks like piercings of a sword, but the tongue of the wise is health, for whisperings and evil surmises separate dear friends, while the wise tongue makes all whole. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

19 COMMENTARY: The lip of truth shall be established for ever, but a lying tongue is but for a moment, for if truth be spoken it will hold good and keep its ground whoever is disobliged. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

20 COMMENTARY: Deceit is in the heart of those imagining evil, but to counsellors of peace is joy, for deceit brings terrors and perplexities, while those consulting others' peace have joy in their own minds. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

21 COMMENTARY: There shall no evil happen to the just, but the wicked shall be filled with mischief, for if men are sincerely righteous, the righteous God has engaged that no evil shall happen to them. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

22 COMMENTARY: Lying lips are abomination to the LORD, but they who deal truly are his delight, for we must make conscience of truth not only in words but in actions. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

23 COMMENTARY: A prudent man conceals knowledge, but the heart of fools proclaims foolishness, for foolish men proclaim to all the folly and emptiness of their minds. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

24 COMMENTARY: The hand of the diligent shall bear rule, but the slothful shall be under tribute, for those who will not take pains in an honest calling, living by tricks and dishonesty, are paltry and beggarly. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

25 COMMENTARY: Heaviness in the heart makes it stoop, but a good word makes it glad, for care, fear, and sorrow deprive men of vigour and courage, while a good word from God applied by faith makes the heart glad. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

26 COMMENTARY: The righteous is more excellent than his neighbour, but the way of the wicked seduces them, for the righteous is abundant in the graces and comforts of the Spirit, the true riches, while evil men vainly flatter themselves their ways are not wrong. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

27 COMMENTARY: The slothful man roasts not what he took in hunting, but the substance of a diligent man is precious, for the slothful makes no good use of Providence's advantages, while the diligent man's substance, though not great, does good to him and his family. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

28 COMMENTARY: In the way of righteousness is life, and in its pathway there is no death, for religion's way is straight and plain, with not only life at the end but life in the way. — Matthew Henry (Concise)
