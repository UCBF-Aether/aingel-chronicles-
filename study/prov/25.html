Study Notes — Proverbs

Chapter 25

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: These are also proverbs of Solomon, which Hezekiah's men copied out, for God needs no search into anything, nothing being hid from him, while it is rulers' honour to search out matters and bring hidden darkness to light. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

2 COMMENTARY: It is the glory of God to conceal a thing, but the honour of kings to search out a matter, for God needs no search, nothing being hid from him, while rulers' honour is to search out matters. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

3 COMMENTARY: The heaven for height, and the earth for depth, and the heart of kings is unsearchable, for it is rulers' honour to search out matters, bringing hidden works of darkness to light. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

4 COMMENTARY: Take away the dross from the silver, and there shall come forth a vessel for the finer, for a prince suppressing vice and reforming his people best supports his government. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

5 COMMENTARY: Take away the wicked from before the king, and his throne shall be established in righteousness, for a prince suppressing vice and reforming his people best supports his government. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

6 COMMENTARY: Put not forth thyself in the presence of the king, and stand not in the place of great men, for religion teaches humility and self-denial, and he who has seen the Lord's glory in Christ will feel his own unworthiness. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

7 COMMENTARY: For better it is that it be said unto thee, Come up hither, than that thou shouldest be put lower in the prince's presence, for religion teaches humility and self-denial before the Lord's glory in Christ. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

8 COMMENTARY: Go not forth hastily to strive, lest thou know not what to do in the end, when thy neighbour has put thee to shame, for hasty strife brings difficulties, and war must at length end and might better be prevented. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

9 COMMENTARY: Debate thy cause with thy neighbour himself, and discover not a secret to another, for hasty strife brings difficulties, and in private quarrels we should do all we can to settle the matter. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

10 COMMENTARY: Lest he who hears it put thee to shame, and thine infamy turn not away, for hasty strife brings difficulties, and private quarrels should be settled rather than spread. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

11 COMMENTARY: A word fitly spoken is like apples of gold in pictures of silver, for a word of counsel or reproof rightly spoken is especially beautiful, as fine fruit is still more beautiful in silver baskets. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

12 COMMENTARY: As an earring of gold, and an ornament of fine gold, so is a wise reprover upon an obedient ear, for a word of counsel or reproof rightly spoken is especially beautiful. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

13 COMMENTARY: As the cold of snow in harvest time, so is a faithful messenger to them that send him, for he refreshes his masters' souls, and faithfulness is the aim of one trusted with business, as a faithful minister, Christ's messenger, should be acceptable to us. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

14 COMMENTARY: Whoso boasts himself of a false gift is like clouds and wind without rain, for he who pretends to have received or given what he never had is like a morning cloud disappointing those who look for rain. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

15 COMMENTARY: By long forbearing is a prince persuaded, and a soft tongue breaks the bone, for be patient to bear a present hurt and mild to speak without passion, since persuasive language most effectually prevails over the hardened mind. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

16 COMMENTARY: Hast thou found honey, eat so much as is sufficient, lest thou be filled and vomit it, for God has given leave to use grateful things, but we are cautioned against excess. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

17 COMMENTARY: Withdraw thy foot from thy neighbour's house, lest he be weary of thee and hate thee, for we cannot be on good terms with neighbours without discretion as well as sincerity, while God, a far better Friend, welcomes us the oftener we come. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

18 COMMENTARY: A man bearing false witness against his neighbour is a maul, a sword, and a sharp arrow, for a false testimony is dangerous in everything. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

19 COMMENTARY: Confidence in an unfaithful man in time of trouble is like a broken tooth and a foot out of joint, for such confidence is painful and vexatious: he not only fails but makes us feel for it. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

20 COMMENTARY: As he who takes away a garment in cold weather, and as vinegar upon nitre, so is he who sings songs to a heavy heart, for we take a wrong course if we think to relieve sorrow by endeavouring to make the sorrowful merry. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

21 COMMENTARY: If thine enemy be hungry, give him bread to eat, and if thirsty, give him water to drink, for the precept to love even enemies is an Old Testament commandment, shown by our Saviour loving us when we were enemies. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

22 COMMENTARY: For thou shalt heap coals of fire upon his head, and the LORD shall reward thee, for loving enemies is an Old Testament commandment, exemplified by Christ loving us when enemies. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

23 COMMENTARY: The north wind drives away rain, so does an angry countenance a backbiting tongue, for slanders would not be so readily spoken if they were not readily heard, and sin, if checked, becomes cowardly. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

24 COMMENTARY: It is better to dwell in the corner of the housetop than with a brawling woman in a wide house, for it is better to be alone than joined to one who hinders life's comfort. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

25 COMMENTARY: As cold waters to a thirsty soul, so is good news from a far country, for heaven is a country afar off, and how refreshing is good news from thence in the everlasting gospel and the Spirit's witness that we are God's children. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

26 COMMENTARY: A righteous man falling down before the wicked is as a troubled fountain and a corrupt spring, for when the righteous are led into sin, it is as hurtful as if public fountains were poisoned. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

27 COMMENTARY: It is not good to eat much honey, so for men to search their own glory is not glory, for we must be, through grace, dead to the pleasures of sense and also to the praises of men. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

28 COMMENTARY: He who has no rule over his own spirit is like a city broken down and without walls, for the man with no command over his anger is easily robbed of peace, and we must give ourselves to the Lord, praying his Spirit within us to cause us to walk in his statutes. — Matthew Henry (Concise)
