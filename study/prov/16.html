Study Notes — Proverbs

Chapter 16

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: The preparations of the heart in man, and the answer of the tongue, is from the LORD, for renewing grace alone prepares the heart for every good work, and we are not sufficient of ourselves to think or speak anything wise and good. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

2 COMMENTARY: All the ways of a man are clean in his own eyes, but the LORD weighs the spirits, for ignorance, pride, and self-flattery render us partial judges of our own conduct. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

3 COMMENTARY: Commit thy works unto the LORD, and thy thoughts shall be established, for we must roll the burden of care upon God and leave it with him by faith and dependence. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

4 COMMENTARY: The LORD has made all things for himself, yea, even the wicked for the day of evil, for God makes use of the wicked to execute righteous vengeance on each other, and will be glorified by their destruction at last. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

5 COMMENTARY: Every one proud in heart is abomination to the LORD, and though hand join in hand he shall not be unpunished, for though sinners strengthen themselves and one another, they shall not escape God's judgments. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

6 COMMENTARY: By mercy and truth iniquity is purged, and by the fear of the LORD men depart from evil, for by God's mercy and truth in Christ Jesus believers' sins are taken away and sin's power broken. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

7 COMMENTARY: When a man's ways please the LORD, he makes even his enemies to be at peace with him, for he who has all hearts in his hand can make enemies at peace with a man. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

8 COMMENTARY: Better is a little with righteousness than great revenues without right, for a small estate honestly come by will turn to better account than a great estate ill-gotten. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

9 COMMENTARY: A man's heart devises his way, but the LORD directs his steps, for if men make God's glory their end and his will their rule, he will direct their steps by his Spirit and grace. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

10 COMMENTARY: A divine sentence is in the lips of the king, and his mouth transgresses not in judgment, for kings and judges of the earth should be just and rule in the fear of God. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

11 COMMENTARY: A just weight and balance are the LORD's, and all the weights of the bag are his work, for observing justice in dealings between man and man is God's appointment. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

12 COMMENTARY: It is abomination to kings to commit wickedness, for the throne is established by righteousness, and the ruler who uses power aright will find that his best security. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

13 COMMENTARY: Righteous lips are the delight of kings, and they love him who speaks right, for rulers should put in power those who know how to speak to the purpose. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

14 COMMENTARY: The wrath of a king is as messengers of death, but a wise man will pacify it, for those are fools who, to obtain an earthly prince's favour, throw themselves out of God's favour. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

15 COMMENTARY: In the light of the king's countenance is life, and his favour as a cloud of latter rain, yet those are fools who, to obtain an earthly prince's favour, throw themselves out of God's favour. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

16 COMMENTARY: How much better to get wisdom than gold, and understanding rather to be chosen than silver, for there is joy and satisfaction of spirit only in getting wisdom. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

17 COMMENTARY: The highway of the upright is to depart from evil, and he who keeps his way preserves his soul, for a sincerely religious man keeps at a distance from every appearance of evil, walking in Christ and led by his Spirit. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

18 COMMENTARY: Pride goes before destruction, and a haughty spirit before a fall, for when men defy God's judgments and think themselves far from them, it is a sign judgments are at the door. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

19 COMMENTARY: Better to be of humble spirit with the lowly than to divide spoil with the proud, for humility, though exposing to contempt in the world, is much better than high-spiritedness, which makes God an enemy. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

20 COMMENTARY: He who handles a matter wisely shall find good, and whoso trusts in the LORD, happy is he, for he who understands God's word shall find good. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

21 COMMENTARY: The wise in heart shall be called prudent, and the sweetness of the lips increases learning, for the man whose wisdom dwells in his heart will be found more truly prudent than many with shining talents. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

22 COMMENTARY: Understanding is a wellspring of life to him who has it, but the instruction of fools is folly, for as waters to a thirsty land, so is a wise man to his friends and neighbours. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

23 COMMENTARY: The heart of the wise teaches his mouth, and adds learning to his lips, for the wise man's self-knowledge always suggests something proper to be spoken to others. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

24 COMMENTARY: Pleasant words are as a honeycomb, sweet to the soul and health to the bones, for the word of God cures the diseases that weaken our souls. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

25 COMMENTARY: There is a way seeming right to a man, but its end is the ways of death, a caution to all to take heed of deceiving themselves as to their souls. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

26 COMMENTARY: He who labours labours for himself, for his mouth craves it of him, for we must labour for the meat enduring to everlasting life, or we must perish. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

27 COMMENTARY: An ungodly man digs up evil, and in his lips there is as a burning fire, for ungodly men bestow more pains to do mischief than would be needful to do good. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

28 COMMENTARY: A froward man sows strife, and a whisperer separates chief friends, for the whisperer separates friends: what a hateful, but how common a character. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

29 COMMENTARY: A violent man entices his neighbour and leads him into the way that is not good, for some do all the mischief they can by force and violence, and are blind to the result. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

30 COMMENTARY: He shuts his eyes to devise froward things, moving his lips he brings evil to pass, for some do all the mischief they can by force and violence, blind to the result. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

31 COMMENTARY: The hoary head is a crown of glory, if found in the way of righteousness, for old people especially should be found in the way of religion and godliness. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

32 COMMENTARY: He slow to anger is better than the mighty, and he who rules his spirit than he who takes a city, for overcoming our own passions requires more steady management than victory over an enemy. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

33 COMMENTARY: The lot is cast into the lap, but the whole disposing thereof is of the LORD, for all disposals of Providence concerning our affairs must be looked upon as determining what we referred to God, and we must be reconciled to them. — Matthew Henry (Concise)
