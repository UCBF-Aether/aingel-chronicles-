Study Notes — Proverbs

Chapter 19

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Better the poor walking in integrity than he perverse in lips and a fool, for a poor man fearing God is more honourable and happy than a man without wisdom and grace, however rich or advanced. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

2 COMMENTARY: That the soul be without knowledge is not good, and he who hastens with his feet sins, for what good can the soul do without knowledge, and he sins who will not ponder the path of his feet. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

3 COMMENTARY: The foolishness of man perverts his way, and his heart frets against the LORD, for men run into troubles by their own folly and then fret at God's appointments. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

4 COMMENTARY: Wealth makes many friends, but the poor is separated from his neighbour, showing how strong is men's love of money. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

5 COMMENTARY: A false witness shall not be unpunished, and he who speaks lies shall not escape, for those telling lies in discourse are in a fair way to bear false witness. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

6 COMMENTARY: Many will entreat the favour of the prince, and every man is a friend to him who gives gifts, for we are without excuse if we do not love God with all our hearts, since his gifts are past number and men's gifts are fruits of his bounty. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

7 COMMENTARY: All the brethren of the poor hate him, and his friends go far from him, yet he pursues them with words, for Christ was left by all his disciples, yet the Father was with him, encouraging faith by his large experience of poverty's sorrows. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

8 COMMENTARY: He who gets wisdom loves his own soul, and he who keeps understanding shall find good, for only those who get true wisdom love their souls aright. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

9 COMMENTARY: A false witness shall not be unpunished, and he who speaks lies shall perish, for lying is a damning, destroying sin. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

10 COMMENTARY: Delight is not seemly for a fool, much less for a servant to rule over princes, for a man without wisdom and grace has no right to true joy, and it is unseemly for a servant to sin to oppress God's free-men. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

11 COMMENTARY: The discretion of a man defers his anger, and it is his glory to pass over a transgression, for he attains the most true glory who most steadily overcomes evil with good. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

12 COMMENTARY: The king's wrath is as the roaring of a lion, but his favour as dew upon the grass, for Christ is a King whose wrath against enemies is as a lion's roar and whose favour to his people is as refreshing dew. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

13 COMMENTARY: A foolish son is the calamity of his father, and the contentions of a wife are a continual dropping, showing the vanity of the world, that we are liable to the greatest griefs where we promise ourselves the greatest comfort. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

14 COMMENTARY: House and riches are the inheritance of fathers, and a prudent wife is from the LORD, for a discreet and virtuous wife is more valuable than house and riches. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

15 COMMENTARY: Slothfulness casts into a deep sleep, and an idle soul shall suffer hunger, for a sluggish, slothful disposition makes men poor and brings them to want, in this life and the next. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

16 COMMENTARY: He who keeps the commandment keeps his own soul, but he who despises his ways shall die, for if we keep God's word, God's word will keep us from everything really hurtful, and free grace does not remove the necessity of obedience. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

17 COMMENTARY: He who has pity on the poor lends unto the LORD, and what he has given he will pay again, for God has chosen the poor of this world to be rich in faith and heirs of his kingdom. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

18 COMMENTARY: Chasten thy son while there is hope, and let not thy soul spare for his crying, for when parents keep under foolish tenderness, they do their best to render children a comfort and happy in themselves. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

19 COMMENTARY: A man of great wrath shall suffer punishment, for if thou deliver him, yet thou must do it again, for the spared and spoiled child is likely to become a man of great wrath. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

20 COMMENTARY: Hear counsel and receive instruction, that thou mayest be wise in thy latter end, for those who would be wise in their latter end must be taught and ruled when young. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

21 COMMENTARY: There are many devices in a man's heart, nevertheless the counsel of the LORD shall stand, for what should we desire but that all our purposes may agree with God's holy will. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

22 COMMENTARY: The desire of a man is his kindness, and a poor man is better than a liar, for it is far better to have a heart to do good and want ability, than ability and want a heart. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

23 COMMENTARY: The fear of the LORD tends to life, and he who has it shall abide satisfied and not be visited with evil, for those living in God's fear shall get safety, satisfaction, and true and complete happiness. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

24 COMMENTARY: A slothful man hides his hand in his bosom and will not bring it to his mouth again, for indolence indulged so grows upon people that they have no heart for the most needful things for themselves. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

25 COMMENTARY: Smite a scorner, and the simple will beware, and reprove one who has understanding, and he will understand knowledge, for a gentle rebuke goes farthest with a man of understanding. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

26 COMMENTARY: He who wastes his father and chases away his mother is a son causing shame and bringing reproach, for the young man who wastes his father's substance or makes his aged mother destitute is hateful and will come to disgrace. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

27 COMMENTARY: Cease, my son, to hear the instruction that causes to err from the words of knowledge, for it is the wisdom of young men to dread hearing talk that puts loose and evil principles into the mind. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

28 COMMENTARY: An ungodly witness scorns judgment, and the mouth of the wicked devours iniquity, for those are the worst of sinners who are glad of an opportunity to sin. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

29 COMMENTARY: Judgments are prepared for scorners, and stripes for the back of fools, for man's unbelief shall not make God's threatenings of no effect, and Christ himself, bearing sins not his own, was not spared. — Matthew Henry (Concise)
