Study Notes — Proverbs

Chapter 4

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Hear, ye children, the instruction of a father, and attend to know understanding, welcoming instruction even when it carries reproof and correction. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

2 COMMENTARY: The doctrine given is good, so forsake not the law, for wise and godly men in every age agree that true wisdom consists in obedience united to happiness. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

3 COMMENTARY: Solomon was his father's son, tender and only beloved, and his parents loved him and therefore taught him. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

4 COMMENTARY: His father taught him to let his heart retain words and keep commandments and live, taking pains for wisdom above the wealth of this world. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

5 COMMENTARY: Get wisdom, then hold it: the section makes wisdom the one thing needful, to be taken pains for above the wealth of this world, neither forgotten nor declined from. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

6 COMMENTARY: Forsake her not and she shall preserve thee; love her and she shall keep thee, for an interest in Christ's salvation is necessary. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

7 COMMENTARY: Wisdom is the principal thing, therefore get wisdom, and with all thy getting get understanding, taking more pains for it than for worldly wealth. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

8 COMMENTARY: Exalt wisdom and she repays the honour: promotion and honour come to the pupil who embraces her, as the section's rule that esteem for wisdom is answered with standing. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

9 COMMENTARY: Henry's section crowns wisdom's pupil with grace now and glory at last: the ornament and the crown are one wisdom's two gifts, not two masters' wages. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

10 COMMENTARY: Hear and receive these sayings and the years of thy life shall be many, for a soul without true wisdom and grace is a dead soul. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

11 COMMENTARY: The father has taught in the way of wisdom and led in right paths, and by taking and keeping fast hold of instruction our path will be plain before us. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

12 COMMENTARY: Taking and keeping fast hold of instruction is what keeps the path plain: by that hold the walker avoids being straitened or stumbling. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

13 COMMENTARY: Holding instruction fast is the exhortation of vv 1-13: get wisdom and take pains for it above the wealth of the world, for a soul without true wisdom and grace is a dead soul, and keeping hold keeps the path plain and free from stumbling. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

14 COMMENTARY: Enter not into the path of the wicked nor go in the way of evil men, for though it may seem pleasant and the nearest way, it is evil and will end ill. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

15 COMMENTARY: Avoid it, pass not by it, turn from it and pass away: keep not at a due distance but at a great distance, never thinking you can get far enough from it. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

16 COMMENTARY: The wicked way ends ill because it is pursued without rest: mischief must be done and someone made to fall before its travellers can sleep, sin as a nightly task. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

17 COMMENTARY: Their way is darkness, so they feed on darkness: bread of wickedness and wine of violence are the daily fare of a path that ends ill, however pleasant it seems. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

18 COMMENTARY: The righteous way is light that grows: Christ is their Way and he is the Light, and the saints shine brighter until they reach heaven and shine as the sun. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

19 COMMENTARY: The way of the wicked is as darkness: they know not at what they stumble, falling into sin without knowing how to avoid it and into trouble without seeking why God contends with them. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

20 COMMENTARY: Attend to the words and incline the ear to the sayings, for attentive hearing of God's word is a sign of grace begun and a means of carrying it on. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

21 COMMENTARY: Let them not depart from thine eyes but keep them in the midst of thine heart, for in God's word there is a remedy for all diseases of the soul. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

22 COMMENTARY: The sayings are not information but medicine: in God's word lies a proper remedy for all diseases of the soul, and that remedy is life and health to the whole man. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

23 COMMENTARY: Keep thy heart with all diligence, setting a strict guard upon the soul to keep it from doing hurt and getting hurt, for out of it are the issues of life. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

24 COMMENTARY: Put away a froward mouth and perverse lips far from thee, seeking from the Lord Jesus the living water of the sanctifying Spirit. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

25 COMMENTARY: Let thine eyes look right on and thine eyelids straight before thee, turned from beholding vanity and walking by the rule of God's word. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

26 COMMENTARY: Ponder the path of thy feet and let all thy ways be established, treading in the steps of the Lord and Master. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

27 COMMENTARY: Turn not to the right hand nor to the left, and remove thy foot from evil, following Christ more closely for the time to come. — Matthew Henry (Concise)
