Study Notes — Proverbs

Chapter 7

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Keeping words and laying up commandments is the safe laying-up Henry urges, for the word kept close guards against the fatal effects of passion and Satan's snares. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

2 COMMENTARY: Keeping commandments and living, with the law as the apple of the eye, means treasuring them as one cannot live without them, since the law in the heart is the eye of the soul. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

3 COMMENTARY: Binding them on the fingers and writing them on the table of the heart is letting the word dwell in us where it will always be at hand to be read. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

4 COMMENTARY: Calling wisdom sister and understanding kinswoman is the close kinship with God's word that confirms dread of sin and resolution against it. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

5 COMMENTARY: Wisdom and understanding keep the son from the strange woman who flatters with her words, for the word dwelling in us is the guard Henry sets against her snares. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

6 COMMENTARY: Looking through the casement at the window opens Henry's affecting example of youthful lusts, a history or parable meant to show the danger of even going near temptation. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

7 COMMENTARY: The youth void of understanding among the simple ones is the unwary figure of the example, whose disregard of God's commands is where self-ruined misery begins. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

8 COMMENTARY: Passing near her corner and going the way to her house is the first step Henry warns against, for we must ever avoid the neighbourhood of vice. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

9 COMMENTARY: Walking in twilight, evening, and black dark night shows the secrecy of the path, yet Henry's example is set plainly before the eyes so none may dare venture on it. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

10 COMMENTARY: The woman in harlot's attire, subtil of heart, is the seducer whose arts the chapter displays, that the reader may see the snare before it is spread for him. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

11 COMMENTARY: Loud and stubborn, her feet not abiding in her house, she embodies the restless vice whose neighbourhood Henry bids the reader ever avoid. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

12 COMMENTARY: Now without, now in the streets, lying in wait at every corner, she spreads snares for souls, and prayer to be kept from running into temptation is the defence Henry urges. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

13 COMMENTARY: Catching him and kissing him with an impudent face shows the boldness of the pleasant sin, which most easily gains the heart and closes it against repentance. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

14 COMMENTARY: Her claim of peace offerings and vows paid is the pious colour given to sin, yet Henry's rule stands: do nothing till thou hast well considered the end of it. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

15 COMMENTARY: Coming forth diligently to seek his face shows the tempter seeking the victim, and the reader who has seen another fall headlong should not dance on the same rock's edge. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

16 COMMENTARY: The bed decked with tapestry, carved works, and fine linen of Egypt is the delight sin offers, yet one hour of following anguish would outweigh a lifetime of such delights. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

17 COMMENTARY: Perfuming the bed with myrrh, aloes, and cinnamon is the pleasantness Henry warns makes the sin more dangerous, because it gains the heart before reason can weigh the end. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

18 COMMENTARY: Taking fill of love until morning and solacing in loves is the promised delight whose end Henry bids the reader consider before venturing. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

19 COMMENTARY: The goodman not at home, gone a long journey, is the false security of secrecy, yet the example is told precisely so the reader will not trust such cover. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

20 COMMENTARY: The bag of money taken and return at the day appointed prolongs the false security, but considering the end exposes it as a snare, not safety. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

21 COMMENTARY: With much fair speech she caused him to yield, and flattering lips forced him, showing how pleasant sins gain the heart and shut it against repentance. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

22 COMMENTARY: Going after her straightway, as an ox to slaughter or a fool to the stocks, is the headlong fall Henry pictures for one who ventures on temptation he has been warned against. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

23 COMMENTARY: Till a dart strikes through his liver, as a bird hastens to the snare not knowing it is for his life, the end of the pleasant sin is ruin the victim never weighed. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

24 COMMENTARY: Hearkening now and attending to the words of the mouth is the application of the example, turning from the seducer's voice to the father's counsel. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

25 COMMENTARY: Letting not the heart decline to her ways nor going astray in her paths is the daily prayer Henry urges: to be kept from running into temptation rather than inviting the enemy's snares. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

26 COMMENTARY: She has cast down many wounded, and many strong men have been slain by her, proving the danger is not for the weak only, and none should dare the edge. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

27 COMMENTARY: Her house is the way to hell, going down to the chambers of death, the end Henry bids every reader consider before a single step toward it. — Matthew Henry (Concise)
