Study Notes — Proverbs

Chapter 5

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: The son is urged to attend to wisdom and bow his ear to understanding, for Henry's opening caution to young men rests on heeding instruction before temptation speaks. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

2 COMMENTARY: Discretion is to be regarded and knowledge kept on the lips, since wisdom guarded in speech is the defence Henry sets against fleshly lusts. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

3 COMMENTARY: The strange woman's lips drop as a honeycomb and her mouth is smoother than oil, the flattering charm Henry warns must not be listened to, lest its sweetness hide its ruin. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

4 COMMENTARY: Her end is bitter as wormwood and sharp as a two-edged sword, for Henry stresses that however luscious the sin seems, take it any way and it wounds. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

5 COMMENTARY: Her feet go down to death and her steps take hold on hell, the direct tendency of this sin being, in Henry's words, the destruction of body and soul. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

6 COMMENTARY: Her ways are movable so the path of life is not pondered, for the tempter's shifting disguises keep the victim from soberly weighing where the road ends. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

7 COMMENTARY: The children are called to hear and not depart from the father's words, since Henry's caution is addressed to all young men as his children, to be treasured before assault comes. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

8 COMMENTARY: The way is to be removed far from her and her door not approached, for those who would be kept from harm must keep out of harm's way and not thrust themselves into temptation. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

9 COMMENTARY: Honour given to others and years to the cruel are the price Henry names for this sin, which blasts reputation and wastes time and estate. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

10 COMMENTARY: Strangers filled with wealth and labours housed by a stranger show the same ruin, for the sin wastes the estate and hands its fruits to others. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

11 COMMENTARY: Mourning at the last, when flesh and body are consumed, is the sorrow Henry says follows merriment sooner or later, since the sin is destructive to health. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

12 COMMENTARY: The sinner reproaches himself for hating instruction and despising reproof, making no excuse for his folly once the rooted habit has done its work. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

13 COMMENTARY: Not obeying teachers nor inclining the ear to instructors is the refusal of the very counsel Henry's caution offers, leaving the sinner without defence. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

14 COMMENTARY: Being almost in all evil amid congregation and assembly shows how far the sin carries a man, for by frequent acts its habits become rooted and confirmed. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

15 COMMENTARY: Drinking waters from one's own cistern and well pictures lawful marriage, which Henry calls a means God appointed to keep from these destructive vices. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

16 COMMENTARY: Fountains dispersed abroad and rivers in the streets warn against scattering what belongs to the marriage bond, for the remedy is faithfulness kept within God's appointed means. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

17 COMMENTARY: Letting the waters be only one's own and not strangers' with thee guards the marriage against the stranger, since Henry ties proper union to attending God's word with affection. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

18 COMMENTARY: The fountain blessed and rejoicing with the wife of youth is the affectionate union Henry commends, sought under God's direction and blessing. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

19 COMMENTARY: Being ravished always with her love, as with the loving hind and pleasant roe, is the delight Henry sets within lawful marriage against the strange woman's lure. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

20 COMMENTARY: To be ravished with a strange woman and embrace a stranger's bosom is the folly Henry asks why any son would choose, when the blessed fountain stands open at home. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

21 COMMENTARY: A man's ways are before the eyes of the LORD, who ponders all his goings, so secret sins that escape fellow creatures never escape God, who sees and weighs them. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

22 COMMENTARY: His own iniquities take the wicked and hold him with the cords of his sins, for those foolish enough to choose sin's way are justly left of God to themselves. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

23 COMMENTARY: Dying without instruction and going astray in great folly is the miserable end of the wicked, left to go on in the way to destruction. — Matthew Henry (Concise)
