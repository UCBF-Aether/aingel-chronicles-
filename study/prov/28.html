Study Notes — Proverbs

Chapter 28

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: The wicked flee when no man pursues, but the righteous are bold as a lion, for sin makes men cowards, while whatever difficulties the righteous meet in duty's way, they are not daunted. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

2 COMMENTARY: For the transgression of a land many are its princes, but by a man of understanding and knowledge the state shall be prolonged, for national sins disturb the public repose. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

3 COMMENTARY: A poor man who oppresses the poor is like a sweeping rain leaving no food, for if needy persons get opportunities of oppressing, their extortion will be more severe than that of the more wealthy. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

4 COMMENTARY: They who forsake the law praise the wicked, but such as keep the law contend with them, for wicked people strengthen one another in wicked ways. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

5 COMMENTARY: Evil men understand not judgment, but they who seek the LORD understand all things, for if a man seeks the Lord, it is a good sign he understands much, and a good means of understanding more. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

6 COMMENTARY: Better is the poor who walks in his uprightness than he who is perverse in his ways, though he be rich, for an honest, godly, poor man is better than a wicked, ungodly, rich man, having more comfort in himself and being a greater blessing to the world. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

7 COMMENTARY: Whoso keeps the law is a wise son, but he who is a companion of riotous men shames his father, for companions of riotous men not only grieve their parents, but shame them. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

8 COMMENTARY: He who by usury and unjust gain increases his substance shall gather it for him who will pity the poor, for that which is ill got, though it may increase much, will not last long, so the poor are repaid and God is glorified. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

9 COMMENTARY: He who turns away his ear from hearing the law, even his prayer shall be abomination, for the sinner at whose prayers God is angry is one who obstinately refuses to obey God's commands. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

10 COMMENTARY: Whoso causes the righteous to go astray in an evil way shall fall himself into his own pit, but the upright shall have good things in possession, for the success of ungodly men is their own misery. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

11 COMMENTARY: The rich man is wise in his own conceit, but the poor who has understanding searches him out, for rich men are so flattered that they think themselves superior to others. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

12 COMMENTARY: When righteous men do rejoice, there is great glory, but when the wicked rise, a man is hidden, for there is glory in the land when the righteous have liberty. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

13 COMMENTARY: He who covers his sins shall not prosper, but whoso confesses and forsakes them shall have mercy, for it is folly to indulge sin and excuse it, since he who covers his sins shall have no true peace, but he who humbly confesses with repentance and faith shall find mercy from God, the Son being our great atonement. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

14 COMMENTARY: Happy is the man who fears always, but he who hardens his heart shall fall into mischief, for there is a fear which causes happiness: faith and love deliver from fear of eternal misery, but we should always fear offending God and sinning against him. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

15 COMMENTARY: As a roaring lion, and a ranging bear, so is a wicked ruler over the poor people, for a wicked ruler, whatever we may call him, this scripture calls a roaring lion and a ranging bear. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

16 COMMENTARY: The prince who wants understanding is also a great oppressor, but he who hates covetousness shall prolong his days, for oppressors want understanding and do not consult their own honour, ease, and safety. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

17 COMMENTARY: A man who does violence to the blood of any person shall flee to the pit, let no man stay him, for the murderer shall be haunted with terrors, and none shall desire to save him from deserved punishment, nor pity him. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

18 COMMENTARY: Whoso walks uprightly shall be saved, but he who is perverse in his ways shall fall at once, for uprightness will give men holy security in the worst times, but the false and dishonest are never safe. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

19 COMMENTARY: He who tills his land shall have plenty of bread, but he who follows after vain persons shall have poverty enough, for those who are diligent take the way to live comfortably. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

20 COMMENTARY: A faithful man shall abound with blessings, but he who makes haste to be rich shall not be innocent, for the true way to be happy is to be holy and honest, not to raise an estate suddenly without regard to right or wrong. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

21 COMMENTARY: To have respect of persons is not good, for for a piece of bread that man will transgress, for judgment is perverted when anything but pure right is considered. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

22 COMMENTARY: He who hastens to be rich has an evil eye, and considers not that poverty shall come upon him, for he who hastens to be rich never seriously thinks how quickly God may take his wealth and leave him in poverty. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

23 COMMENTARY: He who rebukes a man afterwards shall find more favour than he who flatters with the tongue, for upon reflection, most will have a better opinion of a faithful reprover than of a soothing flatterer. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

24 COMMENTARY: Whoso robs his father or mother, and says, It is no transgression, is the companion of a destroyer, for here is the wickedness of those who think it no sin to rob their parents, by wheedling or threatening them, or by wasting what they have and running into debt. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

25 COMMENTARY: He who is of a proud heart stirs up strife, but he who puts his trust in the LORD shall be made fat, for those who make themselves always easy live in continual dependence upon God and his grace, and live by faith. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

26 COMMENTARY: He who trusts in his own heart is a fool, but whoso walks wisely shall be delivered, for a fool trusts to his own strength, merit, and righteousness, and to his own heart, which is deceitful above all things and has often deceived him. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

27 COMMENTARY: He who gives unto the poor shall not lack, but he who hides his eyes shall have many a curse, for a selfish man not only will not look out for objects of compassion, but will look off from those calling for his attention. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

28 COMMENTARY: When the wicked rise, men hide themselves, but when they perish, the righteous increase, for when power is put into the wicked's hands, wise men decline public business, and yet where at first there seems least of Christ, diligent reading will still find what leads to him. — Matthew Henry (Concise)
