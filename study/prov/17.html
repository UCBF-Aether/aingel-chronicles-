Study Notes — Proverbs

Chapter 17

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Better a dry morsel with quietness than a house full of sacrifices with strife, for these words recommend family love and peace as needful for the comfort of human life. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

2 COMMENTARY: A wise servant shall have rule over a son causing shame, and part of the inheritance among brethren, for the wise servant is more deserving and more likely to appear one of the family than a profligate son. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

3 COMMENTARY: The fining pot is for silver and the furnace for gold, but the LORD tries the hearts, for God tries the heart by affliction, often showing the sin remaining in the believer's heart. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

4 COMMENTARY: A wicked doer gives heed to false lips, and a liar gives ear to a naughty tongue, for flatterers, especially false teachers, are welcome to those living in sin. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

5 COMMENTARY: Whoso mocks the poor reproaches his Maker, and he glad at calamities shall not be unpunished, for those laughing at poverty treat God's providence and precepts with contempt. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

6 COMMENTARY: Children's children are the crown of old men, and the glory of children are their fathers, for it is an honour to children to have wise and godly parents continued to them even after they are grown and settled. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

7 COMMENTARY: Excellent speech becomes not a fool, much less lying lips a prince, for a fool in Solomon's Proverbs signifies a wicked man, whom excellent speech does not become because his conversation contradicts it. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

8 COMMENTARY: A gift is as a precious stone in the eyes of him who has it, whithersoever it turns it prospers, for those setting their hearts on money will do anything for it, and we should consider what influence God's gifts should have on our hearts. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

9 COMMENTARY: He who covers a transgression seeks love, but he who repeats a matter separates very friends, for the way to preserve peace is to make the best of everything and not notice what has been said or done against ourselves. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

10 COMMENTARY: A reproof enters more into a wise man than a hundred stripes into a fool, for a gentle reproof enters not only the head but the heart of a wise man. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

11 COMMENTARY: An evil man seeks only rebellion, therefore a cruel messenger shall be sent against him, for Satan and Satan's messengers shall be let loose upon an evil man. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

12 COMMENTARY: Let a bear robbed of her whelps meet a man, rather than a fool in his folly, for we must watch over our own passions and avoid the company of furious men. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

13 COMMENTARY: Whoso rewards evil for good, evil shall not depart from his house, for rendering evil for good is devilish, and he who does so brings a curse upon his family. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

14 COMMENTARY: The beginning of strife is as when one lets out water, therefore leave off contention before it is meddled with, for what danger there is in the beginning of strife: resist its earliest display, and leave it off, if possible, before you begin. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

15 COMMENTARY: He who justifies the wicked and he who condemns the just are both abomination to the LORD, for it is an offence to God to acquit the guilty or condemn those not guilty. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

16 COMMENTARY: Wherefore is there a price in the hand of a fool to get wisdom, seeing he has no heart to it, for man's neglect of God's favour and his own interest is very absurd. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

17 COMMENTARY: A friend loves at all times, and a brother is born for adversity, for no change of outward circumstances should abate affection for friends or relatives, though no friend except Christ deserves unlimited confidence. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

18 COMMENTARY: A man void of understanding strikes hands and becomes surety in his friend's presence, for let none wrong their families, yet Christ becoming Surety for men was a glorious display of Divine wisdom, since he could discharge the bond. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

19 COMMENTARY: He loves transgression who loves strife, and he who exalts his gate seeks destruction, for to keep a clear conscience and quiet mind we must shun all excitements to anger, and a man living above his means goes the way to ruin. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

20 COMMENTARY: He who has a froward heart finds no good, and he with a perverse tongue falls into mischief, for there is nothing got by ill designs, and many have paid dear for an unbridled tongue. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

21 COMMENTARY: He who begets a fool does it to his sorrow, and the father of a fool has no joy, speaking plainly what many wise and good men feel strongly: how grievous it is to have a foolish, wicked child. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

22 COMMENTARY: A merry heart does good like a medicine, but a broken spirit dries the bones, for it is great mercy that God gives us leave to be cheerful and cause to be cheerful, if by his grace he gives hearts to be cheerful. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

23 COMMENTARY: A wicked man takes a gift out of the bosom to pervert the ways of judgment, for the wicked are ready to part with money, though loved, that they may not suffer for their crimes. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

24 COMMENTARY: Wisdom is before him who has understanding, but the eyes of a fool are in the ends of the earth, for the prudent man keeps God's word continually in view, while the foolish man cannot fix his thoughts nor pursue any purpose steadily. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

25 COMMENTARY: A foolish son is a grief to his father and bitterness to her who bore him, for wicked children despise the authority of their father and the tenderness of their mother. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

26 COMMENTARY: Also to punish the just is not good, nor to strike princes for equity, for it is very wrong to find fault for doing what is duty. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

27 COMMENTARY: He who has knowledge spares his words, and a man of understanding is of an excellent spirit, for a man may show himself wise by good temper of mind and good government of his tongue. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

28 COMMENTARY: Even a fool, when he holds his peace, is counted wise, and he who shuts his lips is esteemed a man of understanding, for a wise man is careful when he speaks to speak to the purpose, and God knows his heart and cannot be deceived as men may be. — Matthew Henry (Concise)
