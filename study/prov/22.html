Study Notes — Proverbs

Chapter 22

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: A good name is rather to be chosen than great riches, and loving favour rather than silver and gold, for we should be more careful to get and keep a good name than to raise a great estate. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

2 COMMENTARY: The rich and poor meet together, and the LORD is the maker of them all, for Providence has ordered some rich and others poor, yet all are guilty before God, and at grace's throne the poor are as welcome as the rich. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

3 COMMENTARY: A prudent man foresees the evil and hides himself, but the simple pass on and are punished, for faith foresees evil coming upon sinners and looks to Jesus Christ as the sure refuge from the storm. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

4 COMMENTARY: By humility and the fear of the LORD are riches, honour, and life, for where God's fear is, there will be humility, and much is to be enjoyed by it: spiritual riches and eternal life at last. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

5 COMMENTARY: Thorns and snares are in the way of the froward, but he who keeps his soul shall be far from them, for sin's way is vexatious and dangerous, while duty's way is safe and easy. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

6 COMMENTARY: Train up a child in the way he should go, and when old he will not depart from it, for children must be trained not in the way they would go, of corrupt hearts, but in the way they should go, and as soon as possible led to knowledge of the Saviour. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

7 COMMENTARY: The rich rule over the poor, and the borrower is servant to the lender, showing how important it is to keep out of debt, though the poor should remember it is the Lord who made the difference. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

8 COMMENTARY: He who sows iniquity shall reap vanity, and the rod of his anger shall fail, for the power which many abuse will soon fail them. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

9 COMMENTARY: He who has a bountiful eye shall be blessed, for he gives of his bread to the poor, for he who seeks to relieve others' wants and miseries shall be blessed. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

10 COMMENTARY: Cast out the scorner, and contention shall go out, and strife and reproach shall cease, for profane scoffers and revilers disturb the peace. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

11 COMMENTARY: He who loves pureness of heart, for the grace of his lips the king shall be his friend, for God will be the Friend of a man in whose spirit there is no guile, an honour all saints have. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

12 COMMENTARY: The eyes of the LORD preserve knowledge, and he overthrows the words of the transgressor, for God turns treacherous men's counsels and designs to their own confusion. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

13 COMMENTARY: The slothful man says, There is a lion without, I shall be slain in the streets, for the slothful talks of a lion without but considers not his real danger from the devil, that roaring lion within, and from his own slothfulness, which kills him. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

14 COMMENTARY: The mouth of strange women is a deep pit, and he abhorred of the LORD shall fall therein, for the vile sin of licentiousness commonly besots the mind beyond recovery. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

15 COMMENTARY: Foolishness is bound in the heart of a child, but the rod of correction shall drive it far from him, for sin is foolishness in the heart, an inward inclination children bring into the world, cleaving close to the soul, and we all need correction by our heavenly Father. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

16 COMMENTARY: He who oppresses the poor to increase his riches, and he who gives to the rich, shall surely come to want, for we are but stewards and must distribute what God entrusts according to his will. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

17 COMMENTARY: Bow down thine ear and hear the words of the wise, and apply thine heart unto my knowledge, for to these words and this knowledge the ear must be bowed down and the heart applied by faith and love. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

18 COMMENTARY: For it is a pleasant thing if thou keep them within thee, and they shall be fitted in thy lips, for to live a life of delight in God and dependence on him is the foundation of all practical religion. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

19 COMMENTARY: That thy trust may be in the LORD, I have made known to thee this day, even to thee, for to live in delight in God and dependence on him is the foundation of practical religion. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

20 COMMENTARY: Have not I written to thee excellent things in counsels and knowledge, for the way to know the certainty of the word of truth is to make conscience of our duty. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

21 COMMENTARY: That I might make thee know the certainty of the words of truth, that thou mightest answer truth to them that send unto thee, for the way to know the certainty of truth is to make conscience of duty. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

22 COMMENTARY: Rob not the poor because he is poor, neither oppress the afflicted in the gate, for he who robs and oppresses the poor does so at his peril, and if men will not appear for them, God will. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

23 COMMENTARY: For the LORD will plead their cause, and spoil the soul of those who spoiled them, for he who robs the poor does so at his peril, and God will appear for them. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

24 COMMENTARY: Make no friendship with an angry man, and with a furious man thou shalt not go, for our corrupt hearts have so much tinder that it is dangerous to deal with those throwing about passion's sparks. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

25 COMMENTARY: Lest thou learn his ways, and get a snare to thy soul, for corrupt hearts have so much tinder that dealing with those throwing passion's sparks is dangerous. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

26 COMMENTARY: Be not thou one of them that strike hands, or of them that are sureties for debts, for every man ought to be just to himself and his family, and those are not who waste what they have by folly or carelessness. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

27 COMMENTARY: If thou hast nothing to pay, why should he take away thy bed from under thee, for every man ought to be just to himself and his family, not wasting what he has by folly or carelessness. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

28 COMMENTARY: Remove not the ancient landmark which thy fathers have set, for we are taught not to trespass on another man's right. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

29 COMMENTARY: Seest thou a man diligent in his business, he shall stand before kings and not before mean men, for it is hard to find a truly industrious man, and such a man will rise, especially one diligent in religion's business. — Matthew Henry (Concise)
