Study Notes — Proverbs

Chapter 3

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Keep the law in the heart, for in believing obedience to God's commandments health and peace may commonly be enjoyed. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

2 COMMENTARY: Length of days, long life and peace are added to the obedient, and though our days on earth may not be long, we shall live for ever in heaven. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

3 COMMENTARY: Let not mercy and truth forsake thee: God's mercy in promising and his truth in performing are to be lived up to, kept hold of, and taken comfort in. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

4 COMMENTARY: So favour and good understanding are found in the sight of God and man by one who lives by mercy and truth. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

5 COMMENTARY: Trust in the LORD with all the heart, believing he is able and wise to do what is best, and lean not on an understanding that is a broken reed. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

6 COMMENTARY: In all thy ways acknowledge him, in pleasant ways with thankfulness and in hedged-up ways with submission, and he shall direct thy paths safely and happily at last. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

7 COMMENTARY: Be not wise in thine own eyes, for self-conceit is the greatest enemy to the fear of the LORD in the heart; fear the LORD and depart from evil. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

8 COMMENTARY: Such God-fearing prudence and sobriety tend to the health of the soul and also of the body. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

9 COMMENTARY: Honour the LORD with thy substance and firstfruits, for worldly wealth is but poor substance, yet we must honour God with it. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

10 COMMENTARY: Then barns shall be filled with plenty, for those who do good with what they have shall have more to do more good with. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

11 COMMENTARY: Despise not the chastening of the LORD nor be weary of his correction, and do not faint under affliction however heavy, nor seek wrong means of relief. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

12 COMMENTARY: Whom the LORD loves he corrects, as a father the son in whom he delights, because he loves him and desires that he may be wise and good. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

13 COMMENTARY: Happy is the man that finds wisdom, for no precious jewels or earthly treasures are worthy to be compared with it, whether for time or eternity. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

14 COMMENTARY: The merchandise of wisdom is better than silver and its gain than fine gold, so we must make wisdom our business and venture all in it. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

15 COMMENTARY: No precious jewels or earthly treasures are worthy to be compared with true wisdom: rubies are named only to be outweighed, whether time or eternity is considered. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

16 COMMENTARY: Length of days is in her right hand, and in her left hand riches and honour, for this Wisdom is the Lord Jesus Christ and his salvation. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

17 COMMENTARY: Her ways are pleasantness and all her paths peace, though unbelief, sinfulness and carelessness too often lead us aside to our own hurt. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

18 COMMENTARY: The tree of life here is this section's Wisdom, the Lord Jesus Christ and his salvation, sought and obtained by faith and prayer, whose ways are pleasantness and whose paths are peace. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

19 COMMENTARY: The LORD by wisdom founded the earth and by understanding established the heavens, for Christ is that Wisdom by whom the worlds were made and are kept in being. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

20 COMMENTARY: By his knowledge the depths are broken up and the clouds drop down the dew, and he has wherewithal to make good all his promises. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

21 COMMENTARY: Let not sound wisdom and discretion depart from thine eyes, but keep Christ's words, and then we shall walk safely in his ways. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

22 COMMENTARY: They shall be life unto thy soul and grace to thy neck, with natural life under providence and spiritual life under grace. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

23 COMMENTARY: Then thou shalt walk safely and thy foot shall not stumble, kept from falling into sin or trouble. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

24 COMMENTARY: When thou liest down thou shalt not be afraid, and thy sleep shall be sweet, under God's protection. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

25 COMMENTARY: Be not afraid of sudden fear nor of the desolation of the wicked when it comes, for the LORD is thy confidence. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

26 COMMENTARY: Confidence rests on God's keeping: under the protection of his providence and grace the believer is kept from falling into sin or trouble, and his foot is not taken. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

27 COMMENTARY: Withhold not good from them to whom it is due when it is in thy power, for our business is to do justice, love mercy, and be ready for every good work. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

28 COMMENTARY: Say not to thy neighbour, Go and come again, tomorrow I will give, when thou hast it by thee, for good delayed is good withheld. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

29 COMMENTARY: Devise not evil against thy neighbour who dwells securely by thee, avoiding needless strife and bearing evils rather than seeking redress by law. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

30 COMMENTARY: The section bids the reader avoid needless strife and bear evils rather than seek redress: there is little got by striving, least of all by striving without cause. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

31 COMMENTARY: Envy not the oppressor nor choose any of his ways, for disciples of Christ do not choose the ways of prosperous oppressors. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

32 COMMENTARY: Divine favour is shown to the humble believer, while the froward is abomination: the section's contrast is favour against contempt, and the righteous stand on the favour side. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

33 COMMENTARY: The house shares its master's portion: everlasting contempt is the portion of scorners, while Divine favour rests on the humble believer's habitation, curse on one house and blessing on the other. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

34 COMMENTARY: He scorns the scorners, whose portion is everlasting contempt, but gives grace unto the lowly, on whom divine favour is shown. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

35 COMMENTARY: Henry closes the chapter with the contrast of wicked and upright: truths despised by the covetous and luxurious end in everlasting contempt for scorners, while divine favour is shown to the humble believer, and that is the glory the wise inherit and the shame that promotes fools. — Matthew Henry (Concise)
