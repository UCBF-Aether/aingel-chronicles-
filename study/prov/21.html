Study Notes — Proverbs

Chapter 21

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: The king's heart is in the hand of the LORD, as rivers of water turned whithersoever he will, for the believer perceiving the Lord rules every heart seeks his own heart and others' directed in faith, fear, and love. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

2 COMMENTARY: Every way of a man is right in his own eyes, but the LORD ponders the hearts, for we are partial in judging ourselves and our actions. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

3 COMMENTARY: To do justice and judgment is more acceptable to the LORD than sacrifice, for many deceive themselves with a conceit that outward devotions will excuse unrighteousness. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

4 COMMENTARY: A high look, a proud heart, and the plowing of the wicked is sin, for sin is the pride, ambition, glory, joy, and business of wicked men. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

5 COMMENTARY: The thoughts of the diligent tend only to plenteousness, but of the hasty only to want, for the really diligent employ foresight as well as labour. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

6 COMMENTARY: The getting of treasures by a lying tongue is a vanity tossed to and fro of them that seek death, for while men seek wealth by unlawful practices, they seek death. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

7 COMMENTARY: The robbery of the wicked shall destroy them, because they refuse to do judgment, for injustice will return upon the sinner and destroy him here and for ever. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

8 COMMENTARY: The way of man is froward and strange, but as for the pure, his work is right, for the way of mankind by nature is froward and strange. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

9 COMMENTARY: It is better to dwell in a corner of the housetop than with a brawling woman in a wide house, for it is best to shun bitter contention by pouring out the heart before God, and by prudence, patience, and constant prayer the cross may be removed. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

10 COMMENTARY: The soul of the wicked desires evil, and his neighbour finds no favour in his eyes, for the evil desires of a wicked man's heart lead to baseness in his conduct. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

11 COMMENTARY: When the scorner is punished, the simple is made wise, and when the wise is instructed, he receives knowledge, for the simple may be made wise by punishments on the wicked and by instructions to those willing to be taught. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

12 COMMENTARY: The righteous man wisely considers the house of the wicked, but God overthrows the wicked for their wickedness, for good men envy not evil-doers' prosperity, seeing a curse on them. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

13 COMMENTARY: Whoso stops his ears at the cry of the poor, he also shall cry himself but shall not be heard, for oppressors of the poor, non-relievers, and unjust authorities stop their ears at the poor's cry, though care must be used in charity. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

14 COMMENTARY: A gift in secret pacifies anger, and a reward in the bosom strong wrath, for if money can conquer the fury of passions, shall reason, God's fear, and Christ's command be too weak to bridle them. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

15 COMMENTARY: It is joy to the just to do judgment, but destruction shall be to workers of iniquity, for there is true pleasure only in the practice of religion. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

16 COMMENTARY: The man who wanders out of the way of understanding shall remain in the congregation of the dead, for of all wanderers in sin, those turning aside into darkness are in the most dangerous condition, yet there is hope in the all-sufficient Saviour if they flee without delay. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

17 COMMENTARY: He who loves pleasure shall be a poor man, and he who loves wine and oil shall not be rich, for a life of worldly pleasure brings ruin on men. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

18 COMMENTARY: The wicked shall be a ransom for the righteous, and the transgressor for the upright, for the righteous is often delivered out of trouble and the wicked comes in his stead, seeming as a ransom for him. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

19 COMMENTARY: It is better to dwell in the wilderness than with a contentious and angry woman, for unbridled passions spoil the comfort of all relations. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

20 COMMENTARY: There is treasure to be desired and oil in the dwelling of the wise, but a foolish man spends it up, for plenty obtained by prudence, industry, and frugality is desirable, while the foolish misspend what they have on lusts. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

21 COMMENTARY: He who follows after righteousness and mercy finds life, righteousness, and honour, for true repentance and faith lead him relying on God's mercy in Christ to follow righteousness and mercy in his own conduct. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

22 COMMENTARY: A wise man scales the city of the mighty and casts down the strength of its confidence, for those with wisdom often do great things even against those confident of their strength. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

23 COMMENTARY: Whoso keeps his mouth and tongue keeps his soul from troubles, for it is our great concern to keep souls from being entangled and disquieted. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

24 COMMENTARY: Proud and haughty scorner is his name, who deals in proud wrath, for pride and haughtiness make men passionate, dealing in wrath as if it were their trade to be angry. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

25 COMMENTARY: The desire of the slothful kills him, for his hands refuse to labour, and this is the misery of the slothful: hands refusing honest labour, yet hearts ceaselessly coveting riches, pleasures, and honours unobtainable without labour. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

26 COMMENTARY: He covets greedily all the day long, but the righteous gives and spares not, for the slothful covet without labouring, while the righteous and industrious have their desires satisfied. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

27 COMMENTARY: The sacrifice of the wicked is abomination, how much more when he brings it with a wicked mind, for when holiness is pretended but wickedness intended, that especially is an abomination. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

28 COMMENTARY: A false witness shall perish, but the man who hears speaks constantly, for the doom of a false witness is certain. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

29 COMMENTARY: A wicked man hardens his face, but as for the upright, he directs his way, for a wicked man bids defiance to the law's terrors and Providence's rebukes, while a good man asks what God requires of him. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

30 COMMENTARY: There is no wisdom nor understanding nor counsel against the LORD, for means are to be used, but after all our safety and salvation are only of the Lord. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

31 COMMENTARY: The horse is prepared against the day of battle, but safety is of the LORD, for in spiritual warfare we must arm with God's whole armour, but our strength must be in the Lord and the power of his might. — Matthew Henry (Concise)
