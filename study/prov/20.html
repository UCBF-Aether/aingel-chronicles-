Study Notes — Proverbs

Chapter 20

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Wine is a mocker, strong drink is raging, and whosoever is deceived thereby is not wise, for it seems hard to believe that men of greatest abilities, as well as the ignorant, render themselves fools and madmen merely for the taste of strong liquors. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

2 COMMENTARY: The fear of a king is as the roaring of a lion, and whoso provokes him sins against his own soul, for how formidable kings are to provokers, and how much more foolish to provoke the King of kings. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

3 COMMENTARY: It is an honour for a man to cease from strife, but every fool will be meddling, for engaging in quarrels is the greatest folly: yield, and even give up just demands, for peace's sake. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

4 COMMENTARY: The sluggard will not plow by reason of the cold, therefore he shall beg in harvest and have nothing, for he who labours and endures hardship in his seed-time for eternity will be properly diligent in earthly business. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

5 COMMENTARY: Counsel in the heart of man is like deep water, but a man of understanding will draw it out, for though many capable of wise counsel are silent, something may be drawn from them rewarding those who obtain it. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

6 COMMENTARY: Most men will proclaim every one his own goodness, but a faithful man who can find, for it is hard to find those who have done, and will do, more good than they speak or care to hear spoken of. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

7 COMMENTARY: The just man walks in his integrity, and his children are blessed after him, for a good man is not liable to uneasiness in contriving or reflecting, as deceitful walkers are, and his family fares better for his sake. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

8 COMMENTARY: A king sitting in the throne of judgment scatters away all evil with his eyes, for if great men are good men, they may do much good and prevent very much evil. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

9 COMMENTARY: Who can say, I have made my heart clean, I am pure from my sin, for some can say, through grace, we are cleaner than we have been, but it was the work of the Holy Spirit. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

10 COMMENTARY: Divers weights and divers measures are both abomination to the LORD, for see the various deceits men use, of which love of money is the root, and the Lord will not bless what is thus gotten. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

11 COMMENTARY: Even a child is known by his doings, whether his work be pure and right, for parents should observe their children, that they may manage them accordingly. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

12 COMMENTARY: The hearing ear and the seeing eye, the LORD has made even both of them, for all our powers and faculties are from God and are to be employed for him. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

13 COMMENTARY: Love not sleep, lest thou come to poverty, open thine eyes and be satisfied with bread, for those indulging themselves may expect to want necessaries which should have been gotten by honest labour. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

14 COMMENTARY: It is naught, it is naught, says the buyer, but when gone his way, then he boasts, for men use arts to get a good bargain and buy cheap, whereas a man ought to be ashamed of a fraud and a lie. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

15 COMMENTARY: There is gold and a multitude of rubies, but the lips of knowledge are a precious jewel, for he who prefers true knowledge to riches follows the ways of religion and happiness. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

16 COMMENTARY: Take his garment who is surety for a stranger, and take a pledge of him for a strange woman, for those ruin themselves who entangle themselves in rash suretiship, and those in league with abandoned women, and no confidence should be placed in either. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

17 COMMENTARY: Bread of deceit is sweet to a man, but afterwards his mouth shall be filled with gravel, for wealth gotten by fraud may be sweet, since the carnal mind takes pleasure in wicked devices' success, but it will be bitter in reflection. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

18 COMMENTARY: Every purpose is established by counsel, and with good advice make war, for especially we need advice in spiritual warfare, and God's word and Spirit are the best counsellors in every point. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

19 COMMENTARY: He who goes about as a talebearer reveals secrets, therefore meddle not with him who flatters with his lips, for those dearly buy their own praise who put confidence in a man because he speaks fairly. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

20 COMMENTARY: Whoso curses his father or mother, his lamp shall be put out in obscure darkness, for an undutiful child will become very miserable and must never expect peace or comfort. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

21 COMMENTARY: An inheritance may be gotten hastily at the beginning, but its end shall not be blessed, for an estate suddenly raised is often as suddenly ruined. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

22 COMMENTARY: Say not, I will recompense evil, but wait on the LORD, and he shall save thee, for we must wait on the Lord, attend his pleasure, and he will protect us. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

23 COMMENTARY: Divers weights are abomination unto the LORD, and a false balance is not good, for a bargain made by fraud will prove a losing bargain in the end. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

24 COMMENTARY: Man's goings are of the LORD, how can a man then understand his own way, for how can we form plans and conduct business independently of the Lord. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

25 COMMENTARY: It is a snare to the man who devours that which is holy, and after vows to make enquiry, for the evasions men often use with their own consciences show how false and deceitful man is. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

26 COMMENTARY: A wise king scatters the wicked and brings the wheel over them, for justice should crush the wicked and separate them from the virtuous. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

27 COMMENTARY: The spirit of man is the candle of the LORD, searching all the inward parts of the belly, for the rational soul and conscience are as a lamp within us, to examine dispositions and motives by God's revealed will. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

28 COMMENTARY: Mercy and truth preserve the king, and his throne is upheld by mercy, for mercy and truth are the glories of God's throne. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

29 COMMENTARY: The glory of young men is their strength, and the beauty of old men is the grey head, for both young and old have their advantages, and neither should despise or envy the other. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

30 COMMENTARY: The blueness of a wound cleanses away evil, so do stripes the inward parts of the belly, for severe rebukes sometimes do a great deal of good, and if God uses severe afflictions to purify hearts and fit us for service, we have cause to be thankful. — Matthew Henry (Concise)
