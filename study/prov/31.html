Study Notes — Proverbs

Chapter 31

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: The words of king Lemuel, the prophecy that his mother taught him, for when children are under the mother's eye, she has an opportunity of fashioning their minds aright, and those grown up should often call to mind the good teaching received when children. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

2 COMMENTARY: What, my son, and what, the son of my womb, and what, the son of my vows, for when children are under the mother's eye, she has an opportunity of fashioning their minds aright, and the grown should recall the good teaching received as children. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

3 COMMENTARY: Give not thy strength unto women, nor thy ways to that which destroys kings, for the many awful instances of promising characters ruined by vile women and love of wine should warn every one to avoid these evils. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

4 COMMENTARY: It is not for kings, O Lemuel, it is not for kings to drink wine, nor for princes strong drink, for all in authority should be more carefully temperate even than other men, and promising characters ruined by love of wine should warn every one to avoid this evil. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

5 COMMENTARY: Lest they drink, and forget the law, and pervert the judgment of any of the afflicted, for all in authority should be more carefully temperate than other men, and should be protectors of those unable or afraid to plead their own cause. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

6 COMMENTARY: Give strong drink unto him that is ready to perish, and wine unto those of heavy hearts, for wine is to be used for want or medicine, every creature of God is good, and though abused, wine has its use, as due praise and consolation should be used as cordials to the dejected and tempted, not to the confident and self-sufficient. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

7 COMMENTARY: Let him drink, and forget his poverty, and remember his misery no more, for wine is to be used for want or medicine, and due praise and consolation should be used as cordials to the dejected and tempted, not to the confident and self-sufficient. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

8 COMMENTARY: Open thy mouth for the dumb in the cause of all such as are appointed to destruction, for all in authority should be protectors of those unable or afraid to plead their own cause. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

9 COMMENTARY: Open thy mouth, judge righteously, and plead the cause of the poor and needy, for all in authority should be protectors of those unable or afraid to plead their own cause, and our blessed Lord did not decline the bitterest dregs of the cup of sorrow, but puts the cup of consolation into his people's hands and causes those in deepest distress to rejoice. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

10 COMMENTARY: Who can find a virtuous woman, for her price is far above rubies, for this is the description of a virtuous woman of those days, but the general outlines equally suit every age and nation. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

11 COMMENTARY: The heart of her husband does safely trust in her, so that he shall have no need of spoil, for she can be trusted, and he will leave such a wife to manage for him, and he is happy in her. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

12 COMMENTARY: She will do him good and not evil all the days of her life, for she makes it her constant business to do him good, and is careful to recommend herself to her husband's esteem and affection, to know his mind, and willing that he rule over her. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

13 COMMENTARY: She seeks wool, and flax, and works willingly with her hands, for she takes pains in her duties and takes pleasure in them, applies herself to the business proper for her, to women's business, does what she does with all her power, and trifles not. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

14 COMMENTARY: She is like the merchants' ships, she brings her food from afar, for she makes what she does turn to good account by prudent management, and provides well for her house. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

15 COMMENTARY: She rises also while it is yet night, and gives meat to her household, and a portion to her maidens, for she is careful to fill up time, that none be lost, and she rises early, and provides well for her house. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

16 COMMENTARY: She considers a field, and buys it, with the fruit of her hands she plants a vineyard, for she makes what she does turn to good account by prudent management, unlike many who undo themselves by buying without considering whether they can afford it, and she lays up for hereafter. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

17 COMMENTARY: She girds her loins with strength, and strengthens her arms, for she does what she does with all her power, and trifles not, and she takes pains in her duties and takes pleasure in them. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

18 COMMENTARY: She perceives that her merchandise is good, her candle goes not out by night, for she makes what she does turn to good account by prudent management, and she is careful to fill up time, that none be lost. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

19 COMMENTARY: She lays her hands to the spindle, and her hands hold the distaff, for she applies herself to the business proper for her, to women's business, and does what she does with all her power. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

20 COMMENTARY: She stretches out her hand to the poor, yea, she reaches forth her hands to the needy, for she is intent upon giving as upon getting, and does it freely and cheerfully. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

21 COMMENTARY: She is not afraid of the snow for her household, for all her household are clothed with scarlet, for she provides well for her house, and looks well to the ways of her household. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

22 COMMENTARY: She makes herself coverings of tapestry, her clothing is silk and purple, for she makes what she does turn to good account by prudent management, and provides well for her house. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

23 COMMENTARY: Her husband is known in the gates, when he sits among the elders of the land, for she is a great blessing to her relations, and he is happy in her, and she can be trusted to manage for him. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

24 COMMENTARY: She makes fine linen, and sells it, and delivers girdles unto the merchant, for she makes what she does turn to good account by prudent management. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

25 COMMENTARY: Strength and honour are her clothing, and she shall rejoice in time to come, for she has firmness to bear up under crosses and disappointments, shall reflect with comfort when old that she was not idle or useless when young, and shall rejoice in a world to come. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

26 COMMENTARY: She opens her mouth with wisdom, and in her tongue is the law of kindness, for she is discreet and obliging, every word she says shows she governs herself by wisdom's rules, she not only takes prudent measures herself, but gives prudent advice to others, and the law of love and kindness is written in the heart and shows itself in the tongue. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

27 COMMENTARY: She looks well to the ways of her household, and eats not the bread of idleness, for she looks well to the ways of her household, that she may oblige all to do their duty to God and one another, as well as to her. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

28 COMMENTARY: Her children arise up, and call her blessed, her husband also, and he praises her, for she is a great blessing to her relations, and if the fruit be good, the tree must have our good word. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

29 COMMENTARY: Many daughters have done virtuously, but thou excellest them all, for she is a great blessing to her relations, and every one ought to desire the honour that comes from God, by which standard we all ought to regulate our judgments. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

30 COMMENTARY: Favour is deceitful, and beauty is vain, but a woman who fears the LORD, she shall be praised, for above all, she fears the Lord: beauty recommends none to God, nor is it any proof of wisdom and goodness, but it has deceived many who chose a wife by it, while the fear of God reigning in the heart is the beauty of the soul, lasting for ever. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

31 COMMENTARY: Give her of the fruit of her hands, and let her own works praise her in the gates, for she leaves it to her own works to praise her, and this passage is to be applied to individuals, but may also be applied to the church of God, described as a virtuous spouse, formed by grace from among sinful men to possess all the excellences here described. — Matthew Henry (Concise)
