Study Notes — Proverbs

Chapter 24

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Be not envious against evil men, neither desire to be with them, for envy not sinners, and let no desire come into thy mind to shake off restraints. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

2 COMMENTARY: For their heart studies destruction, and their lips talk of mischief, for envy not sinners, and let no desire arise to shake off restraints. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

3 COMMENTARY: Through wisdom a house is built, and by understanding it is established, for piety and prudence in outward affairs both go together to complete a wise man. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

4 COMMENTARY: And by knowledge shall the chambers be filled with all precious and pleasant riches, for by knowledge the soul is filled with the graces and comforts of the Spirit, those precious and pleasant riches. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

5 COMMENTARY: A wise man is strong, and a man of knowledge increases strength, for the spirit is strengthened for spiritual work and warfare by true wisdom. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

6 COMMENTARY: For by wise counsel thou shalt make thy war, and in multitude of counsellors there is safety, for the spirit is strengthened for spiritual warfare by true wisdom. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

7 COMMENTARY: Wisdom is too high for a fool, and he opens not his mouth in the gate, for a weak man thinks wisdom too high for him and therefore will take no pains for it. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

8 COMMENTARY: He who devises to do evil shall be called a mischievous person, for it is bad to do evil, but worse to devise it. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

9 COMMENTARY: The thought of foolishness is sin, and the scorner is an abomination to men, for even the first risings of sin in the heart are sin and must be repented of, and those striving to make others hateful make themselves so. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

10 COMMENTARY: If thou faint in the day of adversity, thy strength is small, for under troubles we are apt to despair of relief, but be of good courage, and God shall strengthen thy heart. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

11 COMMENTARY: If thou forbear to deliver them drawn unto death, and those ready to be slain, remember: if a neighbour is in danger by unjust proceeding, a man is bound to do all in his power to deliver him. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

12 COMMENTARY: If thou sayest, Behold, we knew it not, does not he who ponders the heart consider it, and shall not he render to every man according to his works, for what is it to suffer immortal souls to perish when our persuasions and example may prevent it. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

13 COMMENTARY: My son, eat thou honey, because it is good, and the honeycomb sweet to thy taste, for we are quickened to wisdom's study by considering both its pleasure and profit, as all men relish what is sweet to the palate. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

14 COMMENTARY: So shall the knowledge of wisdom be unto thy soul, and when found, there shall be a reward, and thy expectation shall not be cut off, for many have no relish for things sweet to the purified soul that make us wise unto salvation. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

15 COMMENTARY: Lay not wait, O wicked man, against the dwelling of the righteous, and spoil not his resting place, for the sincere soul falls as a traveller stumbling at a stone, but gets up and goes on with more care and speed. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

16 COMMENTARY: For a just man falls seven times and rises up again, but the wicked shall fall into mischief, for the sincere soul falls as a traveller may, but gets up and goes on, rather falls into affliction than into actual sin. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

17 COMMENTARY: Rejoice not when thine enemy falls, and let not thine heart be glad when he stumbles, for the pleasure we take in an enemy's troubles is forbidden. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

18 COMMENTARY: Lest the LORD see it, and it displease him, and he turn away his wrath from him, for the pleasure taken in an enemy's troubles is forbidden. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

19 COMMENTARY: Fret not thyself because of evil men, neither be thou envious at the wicked, for envy not the wicked their prosperity, and be sure there is no true happiness in it. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

20 COMMENTARY: For there shall be no reward to the evil man, and the candle of the wicked shall be put out, for envy not the wicked their prosperity, since there is no true happiness in it. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

21 COMMENTARY: My son, fear thou the LORD and the king, and meddle not with them given to change, for the godly in the land will be quiet in the land, and though there may be cause to change for the better, have nothing to do with them given to change. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

22 COMMENTARY: For their calamity shall rise suddenly, and who knows the ruin of them both, for the godly will be quiet in the land, and those given to change face sudden calamity. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

23 COMMENTARY: These things also belong to the wise, and it is not good to have respect of persons in judgment, for the wisdom God gives renders a man fit for his station. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

24 COMMENTARY: He who says to the wicked, Thou art righteous, him shall the people curse, and nations abhor him, for the wisdom God gives renders a man fit for his station, and just judgment without partiality is its mark. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

25 COMMENTARY: But to them that rebuke him shall be delight, and a good blessing shall come upon them, for the wisdom God gives renders a man fit for his station. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

26 COMMENTARY: Every man shall kiss his lips that gives a right answer, for every one who finds the benefit of the right answer will be attached to him who gave it. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

27 COMMENTARY: Prepare thy work without, and make it fit for thyself in the field, and afterwards build thine house, for we must prefer necessaries before conveniences, and not go in debt. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

28 COMMENTARY: Be not a witness against thy neighbour without cause, and deceive not with thy lips, for there are three defaults in a witness pointed out. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

29 COMMENTARY: Say not, I will do so to him as he has done to me, I will render to the man according to his work, for there are three defaults in a witness pointed out, including retaliation by false witness. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

30 COMMENTARY: I went by the field of the slothful, and by the vineyard of the man void of understanding, for see what a blessing the husbandman's calling is, and what a wilderness earth would be without it. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

31 COMMENTARY: And, lo, it was all grown over with thorns, and nettles had covered its face, and its stone wall was broken down, for fields overgrown with thorns and fences broken down are an emblem of many souls' deplorable state. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

32 COMMENTARY: Then I saw, and considered it well, I looked upon it, and received instruction, for sloth and self-indulgence are the bane of all good, and we should show wisdom by doubling diligence in every good thing. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

33 COMMENTARY: Yet a little sleep, a little slumber, a little folding of the hands to sleep, for sloth and self-indulgence are the bane of all good, and every vile affection grows in men's hearts while they compose themselves to sleep. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

34 COMMENTARY: So shall thy poverty come as one that travels, and thy want as an armed man, for sloth and self-indulgence are the bane of all good, and diligence in every good thing is wisdom's answer. — Matthew Henry (Concise)
