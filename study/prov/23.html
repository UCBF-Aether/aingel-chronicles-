Study Notes — Proverbs

Chapter 23

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: When thou sittest to eat with a ruler, consider diligently what is before thee, for God's restraints of appetite only say, do thyself no harm. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

2 COMMENTARY: Put a knife to thy throat, if thou be a man given to appetite, for God's restraints of appetite only say, do thyself no harm. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

3 COMMENTARY: Be not desirous of his dainties, for they are deceitful meat, for God's restraints of appetite only say, do thyself no harm. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

4 COMMENTARY: Labour not to be rich, cease from thine own wisdom, for be not of those who will be rich: worldly things are not happiness or a portion for a soul. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

5 COMMENTARY: Wilt thou set thine eyes upon that which is not, for riches make themselves wings and fly away as an eagle toward heaven, for those holding worldly things ever so fast cannot hold them always, cannot hold them long. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

6 COMMENTARY: Eat thou not the bread of him who has an evil eye, neither desire his dainty meats, for do not make thyself burdensome to any, especially those not sincere. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

7 COMMENTARY: For as he thinks in his heart, so is he: Eat and drink, says he, but his heart is not with thee, for do not make thyself burdensome to the insincere, whose feast is not God's feast. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

8 COMMENTARY: The morsel thou hast eaten shalt thou vomit up, and lose thy sweet words, for do not make thyself burdensome to the insincere, unlike God's feast where souls may safely partake of the Bread of life. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

9 COMMENTARY: Speak not in the ears of a fool, for he will despise the wisdom of thy words, for it is duty to take all fit occasions to speak of Divine things, but if a wise man's words will not be heard, let him hold his peace. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

10 COMMENTARY: Remove not the old landmark, and enter not into the fields of the fatherless, for the fatherless are under God's special protection. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

11 COMMENTARY: For their redeemer is mighty, and he shall plead their cause with thee, for the fatherless' Redeemer will take their part, and he is mighty, almighty. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

12 COMMENTARY: Apply thine heart unto instruction, and thine ears to the words of knowledge, for here a parent instructs his child to give his mind to the Scriptures. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

13 COMMENTARY: Withhold not correction from the child, for if thou beatest him with the rod, he shall not die, for a parent correcting his child, accompanied with prayer and blessed of God, may prevent his destruction. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

14 COMMENTARY: Thou shalt beat him with the rod, and shalt deliver his soul from hell, for a parent's correction, accompanied with prayer and blessed of God, may prevent destruction. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

15 COMMENTARY: My son, if thine heart be wise, my heart shall rejoice, even mine, for here a parent encourages his child, telling him what would be for his good, and what comfort it would be if he answered expectation. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

16 COMMENTARY: Yea, my reins shall rejoice, when thy lips speak right things, for a parent encouraging his child tells him what would be for his good, and what comfort his answering expectation would be. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

17 COMMENTARY: Let not thine heart envy sinners, but be thou in the fear of the LORD all the day long, for the believer's expectation shall not be disappointed, and the end of his trials and of the sinner's prosperity is at hand. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

18 COMMENTARY: For surely there is an end, and thine expectation shall not be cut off, for the believer's expectation shall not be disappointed, and the end of trials and of sinners' prosperity is at hand. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

19 COMMENTARY: Hear thou, my son, and be wise, and guide thine heart in the way, for the gracious Saviour who purchased pardon and peace counsels us to hear and be wise, ready to guide our hearts in his way. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

20 COMMENTARY: Be not among winebibbers, among riotous eaters of flesh, for it is a shame to make a god of the belly, and drunkenness stupifies men and then all goes to ruin. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

21 COMMENTARY: For the drunkard and the glutton shall come to poverty, and drowsiness shall clothe a man with rags, for drunkenness stupifies men and then all goes to ruin. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

22 COMMENTARY: Hearken unto thy father that begat thee, and despise not thy mother when she is old, for here is an earnest call to young people to attend to their godly parents' advice, and if the heart be guided, the steps will be guided. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

23 COMMENTARY: Buy the truth, and sell it not, also wisdom, instruction, and understanding, for buy the truth and sell it not, parting with anything for it, but never parting with it for pleasures, honours, riches, or anything in this world. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

24 COMMENTARY: The father of the righteous shall greatly rejoice, and he who begets a wise child shall have joy of him, for the heart is what the great God requires, and a wise child's obedience is the parent's joy. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

25 COMMENTARY: Thy father and thy mother shall be glad, and she that bore thee shall rejoice, for the heart is what God requires, and godly parents rejoice in a child who gives his heart to God. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

26 COMMENTARY: My son, give me thine heart, and let thine eyes observe my ways, for the heart is what the great God requires, and we must not divide the heart between God and the world: he will have all or none. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

27 COMMENTARY: For a whore is a deep ditch, and a strange woman is a narrow pit, for licentiousness takes away the heart that should be given to God, and we must take heed of any approaches toward it, for retreat from it is very hard. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

28 COMMENTARY: She also lies in wait as for a prey, and increases the transgressors among men, for licentiousness bewitches men to their ruin, and approaches toward it must be shunned. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

29 COMMENTARY: Who has woe, sorrow, contentions, babbling, wounds without cause, redness of eyes, for Solomon warns against drunkenness, and drunkards wilfully make woe and sorrow for themselves. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

30 COMMENTARY: They that tarry long at the wine, they that go to seek mixed wine, are those with woe and sorrow, for those who would be kept from sin must keep from all its beginnings and fear coming within reach of its allurements. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

31 COMMENTARY: Look not thou upon the wine when it is red, when it gives colour in the cup, when it moves itself aright, for those who would be kept from sin must keep from its beginnings and fear its allurements. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

32 COMMENTARY: At the last it bites like a serpent and stings like an adder, for we must foresee the punishment, what it will at last end in if repentance prevents not. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

33 COMMENTARY: Thine eyes shall behold strange women, and thine heart shall utter perverse things, for drunkenness makes men impure and insolent, the tongue unruly, the heart uttering things contrary to reason, religion, and civility. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

34 COMMENTARY: Yea, thou shalt be as he who lies down in the midst of the sea, or as he who lies upon the top of a mast, for the drunkard is in danger of death and damnation, exposed yet feeling secure. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

35 COMMENTARY: They have stricken me, shalt thou say, and I was not sick, they have beaten me and I felt it not, when shall I awake, I will seek it yet again, for so lost to virtue and honour is the drunkard, his conscience so seared, that he is not ashamed to say he will seek it again. — Matthew Henry (Concise)
