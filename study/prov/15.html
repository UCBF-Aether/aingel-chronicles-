Study Notes — Proverbs

Chapter 15

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: A soft answer turns away wrath, but grievous words stir up anger, for a right cause is better pleaded with meekness than passion, and nothing stirs anger like grievous words. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

2 COMMENTARY: The tongue of the wise uses knowledge aright, but the mouth of fools pours out foolishness, for he who has knowledge is to use it aright for the good of others. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

3 COMMENTARY: The eyes of the LORD are in every place, beholding evil and good, for secret sins, services, and sorrows are under God's eye, speaking comfort to saints and terror to sinners. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

4 COMMENTARY: A wholesome tongue is a tree of life, but perverseness therein is a breach in the spirit, for a good tongue heals wounded consciences by comforting them, convinces sin-sick souls, and reconciles parties at variance. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

5 COMMENTARY: A fool despises his father's instruction, but he who regards reproof is prudent, for if instruction is despised, men should be reproved rather than suffered to go undisturbed toward ruin. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

6 COMMENTARY: In the house of the righteous is much treasure, but in the revenues of the wicked is trouble, for worldly wealth increases fears and suspicions, strengthens passions, and renders the fear of death more distressing. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

7 COMMENTARY: The lips of the wise disperse knowledge, but the heart of the foolish does not so, for we use knowledge aright when we disperse it, while the foolish heart has nothing good to disperse. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

8 COMMENTARY: The sacrifice of the wicked is abomination to the LORD, but the prayer of the upright is his delight, for the wicked put other things in place of Christ's atonement, while praying graces are his gift and his Spirit's work, with which he is well pleased. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

9 COMMENTARY: The way of the wicked is abomination to the LORD, but he loves him who follows after righteousness, for the wicked put other things in place of holy obedience, while God delights in praying graces wrought by his Spirit. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

10 COMMENTARY: Correction is grievous to him who forsakes the way, and he who hates reproof shall die, for he who hates reproof shall perish in his sins, since he would not be parted from them. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

11 COMMENTARY: Hell and destruction are before the LORD, how much more the hearts of the children of men, for nothing can be hid from God's eyes, not even man's thoughts. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

12 COMMENTARY: A scorner loves not one who reproves him, neither will he go to the wise, for a scorner cannot bear to reflect seriously within his own heart. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

13 COMMENTARY: A merry heart makes a cheerful countenance, but by sorrow of heart the spirit is broken, for a gloomy, impatient, unthankful spirit, springing from pride and attachment to worldly objects, renders a man uneasy to himself and others. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

14 COMMENTARY: The heart of him who has understanding seeks knowledge, but the mouth of fools feeds on foolishness, for a wise man seeks more wisdom, growing in grace and in knowledge of Christ, while a carnal mind rests contented, flattering itself. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

15 COMMENTARY: All the days of the afflicted are evil, but he of merry heart has a continual feast, for some are much in affliction and sorrowful spirit, to be pitied, prayed for, and comforted, while others serve God with gladness, yet should rejoice with trembling. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

16 COMMENTARY: Better is little with the fear of the LORD than great treasure and trouble therewith, for believers often have enough when worldly eyes see little, the Lord being with them without the cares and temptations attending wicked wealth. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

17 COMMENTARY: Better is a dinner of herbs where love is, than a stalled ox and hatred therewith, for believers often have enough when worldly eyes see little, the Lord with them without the troubles attending the wealth of the wicked. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

18 COMMENTARY: A wrathful man stirs up strife, but he slow to anger appeases strife, for he who is slow to anger not only prevents strife but appeases it if kindled. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

19 COMMENTARY: The way of the slothful is as a hedge of thorns, but the way of the righteous is made plain, for those with no heart to their work pretend they cannot do it without hardship and danger, and so live in doubt through neglect of duty. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

20 COMMENTARY: A wise son makes a glad father, but a foolish man despises his mother, for those treating an aged mother or father with contempt or neglect show their own folly. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

21 COMMENTARY: Folly is joy to him destitute of wisdom, but a man of understanding walks uprightly, for the truly wise study that thoughts, words, and actions should be regular, sincere, and holy. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

22 COMMENTARY: Without counsel purposes are disappointed, but in the multitude of counsellors they are established, for if men will not take time and pains to deliberate, they are not likely to bring anything to pass. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

23 COMMENTARY: A man has joy by the answer of his mouth, and a word spoken in due season, how good, for wisdom is needed to suit discourse to the occasions. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

24 COMMENTARY: The way of life is above to the wise, that he may depart from hell beneath, for a good man sets his affections on things above, and his way leads directly thither. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

25 COMMENTARY: The LORD will destroy the house of the proud, but he will establish the border of the widow, for pride is the ruin of multitudes, while those in affliction God will support. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

26 COMMENTARY: The thoughts of the wicked are abomination to the LORD, but the words of the pure are pleasant words, for the thoughts of wicked men offend him who knows the heart. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

27 COMMENTARY: He greedy of gain troubles his own house, but he who hates gifts shall live, for the covetous man lets none of his family have rest or enjoyment, and greediness often tempts to projects that bring ruin. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

28 COMMENTARY: The heart of the righteous studies to answer, but the mouth of the wicked pours out evil things, for a good man is proved wise by governing his tongue well. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

29 COMMENTARY: The LORD is far from the wicked, but he hears the prayer of the righteous, for God sets himself at a distance from those who set him at defiance. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

30 COMMENTARY: The light of the eyes rejoices the heart, and a good report makes the bones fat, for how delightful to the humbled soul to hear the good report of salvation by the Lord Jesus Christ. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

31 COMMENTARY: The ear that hears the reproof of life abides among the wise, for faithful, friendly reproofs help spiritual life and lead to eternal life. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

32 COMMENTARY: He who refuses instruction despises his own soul, but he who hears reproof gets understanding, for sinners undervalue their own souls, preferring body before soul and wronging the soul to please the body. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

33 COMMENTARY: The fear of the LORD is the instruction of wisdom, and before honour is humility, for the fear of the Lord disposes us to search the Scriptures reverently and follow the Holy Spirit, and humbly depending on God's grace we are exalted in Christ's righteousness. — Matthew Henry (Concise)
