Study Notes — Proverbs

Chapter 1

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: These are Solomon's proverbs, and Henry notes their principles are piety, charity, justice, benevolence and true prudence, whose universal purity proves them the word of God. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

2 COMMENTARY: The book teaches wisdom, piety toward God, instruction and moral discipline, giving the reader wise and prudent counsels for conduct. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

3 COMMENTARY: Its instruction in wisdom is meant to be shown in the conduct of life, above all in righteousness toward our fellow creatures. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

4 COMMENTARY: The simple are given sagacity to discern right through just principles and correct views of virtue and vice, and the young gain knowledge and discretion. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

5 COMMENTARY: Even the wise will listen and increase in learning, since these lessons benefit those who feel their own ignorance and need to be taught. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

6 COMMENTARY: The reader is led to understand proverbs, interpretations, and the words and dark sayings of the wise, pondering the precepts well so as not to err through obstinacy. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

7 COMMENTARY: The fear of the LORD is the beginning of knowledge; fools, Henry says, are those with no true wisdom who follow their own devices without reverence for God. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

8 COMMENTARY: A child is to heed a father's instruction and a mother's law, with reasons given, since children are reasonable creatures who still need law because they are corrupt and wilful. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

9 COMMENTARY: Such instruction is an ornament of grace to the head and chains about the neck: divine truths valued highly become honourable to us. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

10 COMMENTARY: When sinners entice, the answer is a flat refusal: do not say as they say, do as they do, or have fellowship with them. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

11 COMMENTARY: The gang's lure is to lie in wait for blood and lurk privily for the innocent, a pleasure in destroying another that Henry calls unthinkable. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

12 COMMENTARY: They promise to swallow victims alive like the grave, whole and unresisting, but sinners love company in sin and will have the more to answer for. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

13 COMMENTARY: The bait is precious substance and houses filled with spoil, yet Henry warns that such wealth is neither substance nor precious. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

14 COMMENTARY: They offer one purse shared alike, but men promise themselves in vain that sin will turn to their advantage. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

15 COMMENTARY: The counsel is to walk not in their way and to refrain the foot from their path, refusing even one step on these destructive paths. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

16 COMMENTARY: Their feet run to evil and make haste to shed blood, for the way of sin is downhill and men cannot stop themselves once started. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

17 COMMENTARY: A net spread in the sight of any bird is spread in vain, yet these sinners rush on heedlessly toward their own ruin. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

18 COMMENTARY: In lying in wait for others' blood they lurk privily for their own lives, since greediness of gain hurries men into practices that cut short their days. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

19 COMMENTARY: So are the ways of everyone greedy of gain: it takes away the life of its owners, for what is a man profited though he gain the world if he lose his soul? — Matthew Henry (Concise)

20 COMMENTARY: Wisdom herself cries without in the streets and lifts her voice in the chief places of concourse, and Henry identifies this Wisdom with Christ. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

21 COMMENTARY: She cries at the gates and at the entry of the city, so that no one can plead that the call of God never reached them. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

22 COMMENTARY: Three sorts are called: simple ones fond of their simple notions, scorners who jest at everything sacred, and fools who hate to be taught. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

23 COMMENTARY: The precept is plain, "Turn you at my reproof": God promises to pour out his Spirit on those who turn, for special grace is needful to sincere conversion and never denied to any who seek it. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

24 COMMENTARY: Because Wisdom called and men refused, stretching out her hand with no one regarding, the danger now is not hearkening to the calls of God. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

25 COMMENTARY: They set at nought all counsel and would none of reproof, rejecting the love of Christ and the promises mingled with his reproofs. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

26 COMMENTARY: Wisdom will laugh at their calamity and mock when their fear comes, since sinners now at ease will find their calamity come upon them. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

27 COMMENTARY: When fear comes as desolation and destruction as a whirlwind, with distress and anguish, the God once ready to hear will be sought too late. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

28 COMMENTARY: Then they shall call upon Wisdom but not be answered, and seek her early but not find her, for now God is ready to hear their prayers but then they shall cry in vain. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

29 COMMENTARY: This is because they hated knowledge and did not choose the fear of the LORD, flattering themselves in their wickedness. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

30 COMMENTARY: They would none of counsel and despised all reproof, making no right use of reproofs, which is to turn from evil to good. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

31 COMMENTARY: Therefore they shall eat of the fruit of their own way and be filled with their own devices, reaping what their choices have sown. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

32 COMMENTARY: The turning away of the simple shall slay them and the prosperity of fools shall destroy them, since the uncertainty of life and the consequences of dying without Christ make the perilous path deadly. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

33 COMMENTARY: But whoever hearkens to Wisdom shall dwell safely and be quiet from fear of evil, enjoying peace of conscience and confidence in God, free from evil in life, in death, and for ever. — Matthew Henry (Concise)
