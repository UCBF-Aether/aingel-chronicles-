Study Notes — Proverbs

Chapter 27

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Boast not thyself of tomorrow, for thou knowest not what a day may bring forth, for we know not what a day may bring forth, and this forbids presuming upon tomorrow, not preparing for it, and we must not put off conversion, that one thing needful. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

2 COMMENTARY: Let another man praise thee, and not thine own mouth, a stranger, and not thine own lips, for there may be occasion to justify ourselves, but not to praise ourselves. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

3 COMMENTARY: A stone is heavy, and the sand weighty, but a fool's wrath is heavier than them both, for those with no command of their passions sink under the load. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

4 COMMENTARY: Wrath is cruel, and anger is outrageous, but who is able to stand before envy, for those with no command of their passions sink under the load. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

5 COMMENTARY: Open rebuke is better than secret love, for plain and faithful rebukes are better, not only than secret hatred, but than love which compliments in sin, to the hurt of the soul. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

6 COMMENTARY: Faithful are the wounds of a friend, but the kisses of an enemy are deceitful, for plain and faithful rebukes are better than secret hatred, and better than love which compliments in sin, to the hurt of the soul. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

7 COMMENTARY: The full soul loathes a honeycomb, but to the hungry soul every bitter thing is sweet, for the poor have a better relish of enjoyments and are often more thankful than the rich, while the proud disdain the gospel, but those hungering after righteousness find comfort in the meanest book or sermon testifying of Christ. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

8 COMMENTARY: As a bird wandering from her nest, so is a man wandering from his place, for every man has his proper place in society, where he may be safe and comfortable. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

9 COMMENTARY: Ointment and perfume rejoice the heart, so does the sweetness of a man's friend by hearty counsel, for depend not for relief upon a kinsman merely for kindred's sake, but apply to those at hand who will help in need, and there is a Friend sticking closer than a brother, in whom we place entire confidence. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

10 COMMENTARY: Thine own friend, and thy father's friend, forsake not, neither go into thy brother's house in thy calamity, for better is a neighbour near than a brother far off, and depend not on a kinsman merely for kindred's sake, but on those at hand, and on the Friend sticking closer than a brother. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

11 COMMENTARY: My son, be wise, and make my heart glad, that I may answer him who reproaches me, for an affectionate parent urges his son to prudent conduct that should gladden his heart, and the good conduct of Christians is the best answer to all who find fault with the gospel. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

12 COMMENTARY: A prudent man foresees the evil and hides himself, but the simple pass on and are punished, for where there is temptation, if we thrust ourselves into it, there will be sin, and punishment will follow. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

13 COMMENTARY: Take his garment that is surety for a stranger, and take a pledge of him for a strange woman, for an honest man may be made a beggar, but he is not honest who makes himself one. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

14 COMMENTARY: He who blesses his friend with a loud voice, rising early in the morning, it shall be counted a curse to him, for it is folly to be fond of being praised, and it is a temptation to pride. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

15 COMMENTARY: A continual dropping in a very rainy day and a contentious woman are alike, for the contentions of a neighbour may be like a sharp shower, troublesome for a time, while a wife's contentions are like constant rain. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

16 COMMENTARY: Whosoever hides her hides the wind, and the ointment of his right hand, which bewrays itself, for the contentions of a neighbour may be like a sharp shower, while a wife's contentions are like constant rain. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

17 COMMENTARY: Iron sharpens iron, so a man sharpens the countenance of his friend, for we are cautioned to take heed whom we converse with, and directed to have in view, in conversation, to make one another wiser and better. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

18 COMMENTARY: Whoso keeps the fig tree shall eat its fruit, so he who waits on his master shall be honoured, for though a calling be laborious and despised, those who keep to it will find something to be got by it, and God, a Master, has engaged to honour those serving him faithfully. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

19 COMMENTARY: As in water face answers to face, so the heart of man to man, for one corrupt heart is like another, so are sanctified hearts: the former bear the image of the earthly, the latter the image of the heavenly, and we must carefully watch our own hearts, comparing them with God's word. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

20 COMMENTARY: Hell and destruction are never full, so the eyes of man are never satisfied, for two things are never satisfied, death and sin, and the carnal mind's appetites for profit or pleasure always desire more, while those whose eyes are toward the Lord are satisfied in him, and shall for ever be so. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

21 COMMENTARY: As the fining pot for silver, and the furnace for gold, so is a man to his praise, for silver and gold are tried by putting them into the furnace and fining-pot, so is a man tried by praising him. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

22 COMMENTARY: Though thou shouldest bray a fool in a mortar among wheat with a pestle, yet will not his foolishness depart from him, for some are so bad that even severe methods do not answer the end, and what remains but that they should be rejected, since God's new-creating grace alone can make a change. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

23 COMMENTARY: Be thou diligent to know the state of thy flocks, and look well to thy herds, for we ought to have some business to do in this world, and not to live in idleness, nor meddle with what we do not understand, but be diligent and take pains. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

24 COMMENTARY: For riches are not for ever, and does the crown endure to every generation, for we ought to have business to do and not live in idleness, and though the world cannot be secured to us, we must choose a more lasting portion. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

25 COMMENTARY: The hay appears, and the tender grass shows itself, and herbs of the mountains are gathered, for by God's blessing upon honest labours, we may expect to enjoy as much of earthly blessings as is good for us. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

26 COMMENTARY: The lambs are for thy clothing, and the goats are the price of the field, for by God's blessing upon honest labours, we may expect to enjoy as much of earthly blessings as is good for us. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

27 COMMENTARY: And thou shalt have goats' milk enough for thy food, for thy household's food, and for thy maidens' maintenance, for by God's blessing upon honest labours, we may expect to enjoy as much of earthly blessings as is good for us. — Matthew Henry (Concise)
