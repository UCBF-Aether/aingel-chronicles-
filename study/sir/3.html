Study Notes — Sirach

Chapter 3

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Listen to a father's instruction, children, and do what follows, so that you may be safe. A father's teaching is given as protection, and safety comes from doing it, not merely hearing it. — W. O. E. Oesterley

2 COMMENTARY: The Lord has given the father honour over the children and confirmed the mother's authority over the sons. Parental authority is God's own ordering of the household, not a human custom. — W. O. E. Oesterley

3 COMMENTARY: Whoever honours his father makes an atonement for his sins. Honouring a father has a cleansing, reconciling weight before God. — W. O. E. Oesterley

4 COMMENTARY: He who honours his mother is like one who lays up treasure. Honour to a mother is stored wealth, kept for the future rather than spent at once. — W. O. E. Oesterley

5 COMMENTARY: Whoever honours his father will have joy of his own children, and when he prays he will be heard. Honour runs down the generations, and it opens the way for prayer to be heard. — W. O. E. Oesterley

6 COMMENTARY: He who honours his father will have a long life, and one obedient to the Lord will be a comfort to his mother. Long life and a mother's comfort both follow from honour and obedience. — W. O. E. Oesterley

7 COMMENTARY: He who fears the Lord will honour his father and serve his parents as he would his masters. Fear of God expresses itself in treating parents with the dutiful service owed to a master. — W. O. E. Oesterley

8 COMMENTARY: Honour father and mother in both word and deed, so that a blessing may come upon you from them. Honour must be spoken and acted, and the parents' blessing is the fruit of both. — W. O. E. Oesterley

9 COMMENTARY: A father's blessing establishes the houses of children, but a mother's curse roots out foundations. Parental blessing builds a family and parental curse uproots it; both carry real power. — W. O. E. Oesterley

10 COMMENTARY: Do not glory in your father's dishonour, for his dishonour is no glory to you. A child's standing cannot be built on a parent's shame. — W. O. E. Oesterley

11 COMMENTARY: A man's glory comes from his father's honour, and a mother in dishonour is a reproach to the children. Family honour is shared: a father's honour lifts his children and a mother's disgrace shames them. — W. O. E. Oesterley

12 COMMENTARY: Help your father in his age and do not grieve him as long as he lives. Care for an aged father is a continuing duty, not a favour done once. — W. O. E. Oesterley

13 COMMENTARY: If his understanding fails, have patience with him, and do not despise him when you are in your full strength. A father's failing mind calls for patience, not contempt from a stronger child. — W. O. E. Oesterley

14 COMMENTARY: Relieving your father will not be forgotten; instead of sins it will be added to build you up. Kindness to a father is remembered by God and counts toward the child's own building up. — W. O. E. Oesterley

15 COMMENTARY: In the day of your affliction it will be remembered, and your sins will melt away like ice in fair warm weather. The mercy shown to a father returns as mercy in the child's own trouble, dissolving sins like spring ice. — W. O. E. Oesterley

16 COMMENTARY: He who forsakes his father is like a blasphemer, and he who angers his mother is cursed by God. Abandoning a father is set beside blasphemy, and provoking a mother brings God's curse. — W. O. E. Oesterley

17 COMMENTARY: Go on with your business in meekness, and you will be beloved by him who is approved. Quiet, humble work wins the love of those whose approval matters. — W. O. E. Oesterley

18 COMMENTARY: The greater you are, the more you should humble yourself, and you will find favour before the Lord. Greatness increases the duty of humility, and favour with God follows the humble great. — W. O. E. Oesterley

19 COMMENTARY: Many are in high place and of renown, but mysteries are revealed to the meek. Rank and fame do not open hidden things; meekness does. — W. O. E. Oesterley

20 COMMENTARY: The power of the Lord is great, and he is honoured by the lowly. God's greatness is not honoured by the proud but by those who take the low place. — W. O. E. Oesterley

21 COMMENTARY: Do not seek out things too hard for you, nor search things above your strength. There is a humility in refusing enquiries beyond one's measure. — W. O. E. Oesterley

22 COMMENTARY: Think with reverence on what is commanded you, for you need not see with your eyes the things that are secret. Obedience to the given command matters more than sight of hidden things. — W. O. E. Oesterley

23 COMMENTARY: Do not be curious in unnecessary matters, for more things are shown to you than men understand. Curiosity about needless secrets is rebuked: revelation already exceeds understanding. — W. O. E. Oesterley

24 COMMENTARY: Many are deceived by their own vain opinion, and an evil suspicion has overthrown their judgment. Self-flattering opinion and groundless suspicion are the two traps that wreck judgment. — W. O. E. Oesterley

25 COMMENTARY: Without eyes you will want light; do not profess knowledge you do not have. Claiming knowledge one lacks is like walking without eyes and expecting light. — W. O. E. Oesterley

26 COMMENTARY: A stubborn heart will fare evil at the last, and he who loves danger will perish in it. Stubbornness ends badly, and a taste for danger is itself the road to perishing. — W. O. E. Oesterley

27 COMMENTARY: An obstinate heart will be laden with sorrows, and the wicked man heaps sin upon sin. Obstinacy accumulates sorrow, and wickedness compounds itself. — W. O. E. Oesterley

28 COMMENTARY: In the punishment of the proud there is no remedy, for the plant of wickedness has taken root in him. Pride that has rooted itself leaves no room for remedy when punishment comes. — W. O. E. Oesterley

29 COMMENTARY: The heart of the prudent will understand a parable, and an attentive ear is the desire of a wise man. Prudence grasps pointed sayings, and the wise man's appetite is for listening well. — W. O. E. Oesterley

30 COMMENTARY: Water quenches a flaming fire, and alms make an atonement for sins. As water puts out fire, so generous giving to the needy works against sin's power. — W. O. E. Oesterley

31 COMMENTARY: He who requites good turns is mindful of what may come hereafter, and when he falls he will find a stay. Repaying kindness looks ahead to one's own future need, and such a person finds support when he himself falls. — W. O. E. Oesterley
