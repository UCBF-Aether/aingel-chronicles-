Study Notes — Sirach

Chapter 29

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: He who is merciful will lend to his neighbour, and he who strengthens his hand keeps the commandments. Merciful lending and strengthening a neighbour's hand are commandment-keeping. — W. O. E. Oesterley

2 COMMENTARY: Lend to your neighbour in time of his need, and pay your neighbour again in due season. Lending in need and repaying in due season are the two sides of neighbourly money. — W. O. E. Oesterley

3 COMMENTARY: Keep your word, and deal faithfully with him, and you will always find what is necessary for you. Word-keeping and faithful dealing secure that necessities will always be found. — W. O. E. Oesterley

4 COMMENTARY: Many, when a thing was lent them, reckoned it to be found, and put them to trouble who helped them. Many borrowers treat loans as found money and trouble their helpers. — W. O. E. Oesterley

5 COMMENTARY: Till he has received, he will kiss a man's hand, and for his neighbour's money he will speak submissively; but when he should repay, he will prolong the time, return words of grief, and complain of the time. Before receiving, the borrower kisses hands and speaks humbly; at repayment he delays, grieves, and complains. — W. O. E. Oesterley

6 COMMENTARY: If he prevails, he will hardly receive the half, and will count as if he had found it; if not, he has deprived him of his money, and gotten him an enemy without cause; he pays him with cursings and railings, and for honour pays disgrace. The lender who prevails recovers barely half, and counts himself lucky; if he fails, he loses money, gains a causeless enemy, and receives curses and disgrace for honour. — W. O. E. Oesterley

7 COMMENTARY: Many therefore have refused to lend for other men's ill dealing, fearing to be defrauded. Others' ill dealing in borrowing has made many refuse to lend, fearing fraud. — W. O. E. Oesterley

8 COMMENTARY: Yet have patience with a man in poor estate, and delay not to show him mercy. Despite bad borrowers, patience with the poor and prompt mercy are still commanded. — W. O. E. Oesterley

9 COMMENTARY: Help the poor for the commandment's sake, and do not turn him away because of his poverty. The poor are helped for the commandment's sake, not turned away for their poverty. — W. O. E. Oesterley

10 COMMENTARY: Lose your money for your brother and friend, and do not let it rust under a stone to be lost. Money risked for brother and friend is better than money rusting unused under a stone. — W. O. E. Oesterley

11 COMMENTARY: Lay up your treasure according to the commandments of the Most High, and it will bring you more profit than gold. Treasure laid up by God's commandments profits more than gold. — W. O. E. Oesterley

12 COMMENTARY: Shut up alms in your storehouses, and it will deliver you from all affliction. Alms stored up deliver from all affliction. — W. O. E. Oesterley

13 COMMENTARY: It will fight for you against your enemies better than a mighty shield and strong spear. Alms fight for the giver better than shield and spear against enemies. — W. O. E. Oesterley

14 COMMENTARY: An honest man is surety for his neighbour, but he who is impudent will forsake him. An honest man stands surety for his neighbour, while the impudent forsakes him. — W. O. E. Oesterley

15 COMMENTARY: Do not forget the friendship of your surety, for he has given his life for you. A surety's friendship must not be forgotten, since he has risked his life for you. — W. O. E. Oesterley

16 COMMENTARY: A sinner will overthrow the good estate of his surety. A sinner's default overthrows his surety's good estate. — W. O. E. Oesterley

17 COMMENTARY: He who is of an unthankful mind will leave him in danger who delivered him. An unthankful borrower abandons in danger the one who delivered him. — W. O. E. Oesterley

18 COMMENTARY: Suretyship has undone many of good estate, and shaken them as a wave of the sea; mighty men it has driven from their houses, so that they wandered among strange nations. Suretyship has ruined many good estates, shaken them like sea waves, and driven mighty men into wandering among strange nations. — W. O. E. Oesterley

19 COMMENTARY: A wicked man transgressing the commandments of the Lord will fall into suretyship, and he who undertakes and follows other men's business for gain will fall into suits. Wicked transgressors fall into suretyship, and meddlers in others' business for gain fall into lawsuits. — W. O. E. Oesterley

20 COMMENTARY: Help your neighbour according to your power, and beware that you yourself fall not into the same. Help within your power, but beware falling into the same need yourself. — W. O. E. Oesterley

21 COMMENTARY: The chief thing for life is water, bread, clothing, and a house to cover shame. Life's chief necessities are water, bread, clothing, and a house covering shame. — W. O. E. Oesterley

22 COMMENTARY: Better is the life of a poor man in a mean cottage than delicate fare in another man's house. A poor man's own mean cottage is better than delicate fare in another's house. — W. O. E. Oesterley

23 COMMENTARY: Be it little or much, hold yourself contented, that you hear not the reproach of your house. Contentment with little or much avoids hearing reproach against your household. — W. O. E. Oesterley

24 COMMENTARY: It is a miserable life to go from house to house, for where you are a stranger, you dare not open your mouth. House-to-house dependence is miserable, since a stranger dares not open his mouth. — W. O. E. Oesterley

25 COMMENTARY: You will entertain, feast, and have no thanks; moreover you will hear bitter words. The dependent entertains and feasts without thanks, and hears bitter words besides. — W. O. E. Oesterley

26 COMMENTARY: Come, you stranger, and furnish a table, and feed me of what you have ready. The dependent is ordered about: come, stranger, furnish a table and feed me from your store. — W. O. E. Oesterley

27 COMMENTARY: Give place, you stranger, to an honourable man; my brother comes to be lodged, and I have need of my house. The stranger is then displaced: give place to an honourable man, for the brother comes and the house is needed. — W. O. E. Oesterley

28 COMMENTARY: These things are grievous to a man of understanding: the upbraiding of houseroom, and reproaching of the lender. Upbraiding about lodging and reproach from a lender are grievous to an understanding man. — W. O. E. Oesterley
