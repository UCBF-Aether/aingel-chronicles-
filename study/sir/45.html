Study Notes — Sirach

Chapter 45

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: He brought out of Jacob a merciful man, who found favour in the sight of all flesh, even Moses, beloved of God and men, whose memorial is blessed. From Jacob came Moses, merciful, favoured by all flesh, beloved of God and men, blessed in memorial. — W. O. E. Oesterley

2 COMMENTARY: He made him like the glorious saints, and magnified him, so that his enemies stood in fear of him. Moses was made like glorious saints and magnified until enemies feared him. — W. O. E. Oesterley

3 COMMENTARY: By his words he caused the wonders to cease, and he made him glorious in the sight of kings, gave him a commandment for his people, and showed him part of his glory. By Moses' words wonders ceased, kings saw his glory, he received commandment for the people, and a glimpse of God's glory. — W. O. E. Oesterley

4 COMMENTARY: He sanctified him in his faithfulness and meekness, and chose him out of all men. Moses was sanctified in faithfulness and meekness, chosen out of all men. — W. O. E. Oesterley

5 COMMENTARY: He made him hear his voice, and brought him into the dark cloud, and gave him commandments before his face, even the law of life and knowledge, that he might teach Jacob his covenants, and Israel his judgments. Moses heard God's voice in the dark cloud and received the law of life and knowledge face to face, to teach Jacob covenants and Israel judgments. — W. O. E. Oesterley

6 COMMENTARY: He exalted Aaron, a holy man like him, even his brother, of the tribe of Levi. Aaron, Moses' brother of Levi, a holy man like him, was exalted. — W. O. E. Oesterley

7 COMMENTARY: An everlasting covenant he made with him and gave him the priesthood among the people; he beautified him with comely ornaments, and clothed him with a robe of glory. Aaron received an everlasting covenant and the priesthood, beautified with ornaments and clothed in a robe of glory. — W. O. E. Oesterley

8 COMMENTARY: He put upon him perfect glory, and strengthened him with rich garments, with breeches, with a long robe, and the ephod. Aaron was clothed in perfect glory: rich garments, breeches, long robe, and ephod. — W. O. E. Oesterley

9 COMMENTARY: He compassed him with pomegranates, and with many golden bells round about, that as he went there might be a sound, and a noise made that might be heard in the temple, for a memorial to the children of his people. Pomegranates and golden bells ringed Aaron, so that his going made a sound heard in the temple as a memorial to the people. — W. O. E. Oesterley

10 COMMENTARY: With a holy garment, with gold, blue silk, and purple, the work of the embroiderer, with a breastplate of judgment, and with Urim and Thummim. His holy garment of gold, blue, and purple embroidery carried the breastplate of judgment with Urim and Thummim. — W. O. E. Oesterley

11 COMMENTARY: With twisted scarlet, the work of the cunning workman, with precious stones graven like seals, set in gold, the work of the jeweller, with a writing engraved for a memorial, after the number of the tribes of Israel. Twisted scarlet, seal-graven precious stones set in gold, and engraved writing memorialised the tribes of Israel. — W. O. E. Oesterley

12 COMMENTARY: He set a crown of gold upon the mitre, wherein was engraved Holiness, an ornament of honour, a costly work, the desire of the eyes, goodly and beautiful. The golden crown on the mitre, engraved Holiness, was an honourable, costly, beautiful ornament. — W. O. E. Oesterley

13 COMMENTARY: Before him there were none such, neither did any stranger put them on, but only his children and his children's children perpetually. No one before Aaron wore such garments, and no stranger ever would, only his descendants perpetually. — W. O. E. Oesterley

14 COMMENTARY: Their sacrifices will be wholly consumed every day twice continually. Aaron's sons' sacrifices are wholly consumed twice daily, continually. — W. O. E. Oesterley

15 COMMENTARY: Moses consecrated him, and anointed him with holy oil; this was appointed to him by an everlasting covenant, and to his seed, so long as the heavens remain, that they should minister to him, execute the priesthood's office, and bless the people in his name. Moses consecrated and anointed Aaron under an everlasting covenant, for him and his seed while heavens remain, to minister, execute priesthood, and bless in God's name. — W. O. E. Oesterley

16 COMMENTARY: He chose him out of all men living to offer sacrifices to the Lord, incense, and a sweet savour, for a memorial, to make reconciliation for his people. Aaron was chosen from all living men to offer sacrifices, incense, and sweet savour as a memorial making reconciliation for the people. — W. O. E. Oesterley

17 COMMENTARY: He gave him his commandments, and authority in the statutes of judgments, that he should teach Jacob the testimonies, and inform Israel in his laws. Aaron received commandments and judicial authority, to teach Jacob testimonies and inform Israel in laws. — W. O. E. Oesterley

18 COMMENTARY: Strangers conspired together against him, and maligned him in the wilderness, even the men of Dathan's and Abiron's side, and the congregation of Core, with fury and wrath. Dathan, Abiron, and Core's congregation conspired and maligned Aaron in the wilderness with fury and wrath. — W. O. E. Oesterley

19 COMMENTARY: This the Lord saw, and it displeased him, and in his wrathful indignation they were consumed; he did wonders upon them, to consume them with fiery flame. God saw, was displeased, and consumed the conspirators with fiery flame in wrathful indignation. — W. O. E. Oesterley

20 COMMENTARY: But he made Aaron more honourable, and gave him a heritage, and divided to him the firstfruits of the increase; especially he prepared bread in abundance. Aaron was made more honourable, given heritage and firstfruits of increase, with bread in abundance. — W. O. E. Oesterley

21 COMMENTARY: For they eat of the sacrifices of the Lord, which he gave to him and his seed. Aaron and his seed eat of the Lord's sacrifices given to them. — W. O. E. Oesterley

22 COMMENTARY: However, in the land of the people he had no inheritance, neither any portion among the people, for the Lord himself is his portion and inheritance. Aaron had no land inheritance or portion among the people, because the Lord himself is his portion. — W. O. E. Oesterley

23 COMMENTARY: The third in glory is Phinees son of Eleazar, because he had zeal in the fear of the Lord, and stood up with good courage of heart when the people were turned back, and made reconciliation for Israel. Phinehas, third in glory, showed zeal in fear of God, stood with good courage when the people turned back, and made reconciliation for Israel. — W. O. E. Oesterley

24 COMMENTARY: Therefore a covenant of peace was made with him, that he should be chief of the sanctuary and of his people, and that he and his posterity should have the dignity of the priesthood for ever. Phinehas received a covenant of peace, chiefship of sanctuary and people, and perpetual priestly dignity for his posterity. — W. O. E. Oesterley

25 COMMENTARY: According to the covenant made with David son of Jesse, of the tribe of Judah, that the king's inheritance should be to his posterity alone, so the inheritance of Aaron should also be to his seed. As David's kingly inheritance belongs to his posterity alone, so Aaron's priestly inheritance belongs to his seed. — W. O. E. Oesterley

26 COMMENTARY: God give you wisdom in your heart to judge his people in righteousness, that their good things be not abolished, and that their glory may endure for ever. The chapter closes with a prayer for wisdom to judge God's people righteously, so their good things are not abolished and their glory endures for ever. — W. O. E. Oesterley
