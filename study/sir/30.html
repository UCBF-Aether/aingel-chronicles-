Study Notes — Sirach

Chapter 30

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: He who loves his son causes him often to feel the rod, that he may have joy of him in the end. A loving father uses the rod often, so that joy in his son comes at the end. — W. O. E. Oesterley

2 COMMENTARY: He who chastises his son will have joy in him, and will rejoice of him among his acquaintance. Chastisement leads to joy in the son and rejoicing among acquaintances. — W. O. E. Oesterley

3 COMMENTARY: He who teaches his son grieves the enemy, and before his friends he will rejoice of him. Teaching a son grieves enemies and gives rejoicing before friends. — W. O. E. Oesterley

4 COMMENTARY: Though his father dies, yet he is as though he were not dead, for he has left one behind him like himself. A father who leaves a son like himself is as if not dead. — W. O. E. Oesterley

5 COMMENTARY: While he lived, he saw and rejoiced in him, and when he died, he was not sorrowful. The father rejoiced in his son while living, and died without sorrow over him. — W. O. E. Oesterley

6 COMMENTARY: He left behind him an avenger against his enemies, and one who will requite kindness to his friends. The son left behind avenges enemies and repays kindness to friends. — W. O. E. Oesterley

7 COMMENTARY: He who makes too much of his son will bind up his wounds, and his bowels will be troubled at every cry. Over-indulging a son means binding his wounds and being troubled at every cry. — W. O. E. Oesterley

8 COMMENTARY: A horse not broken becomes headstrong, and a child left to himself will be wilful. An unbroken horse is headstrong, and an undisciplined child is wilful. — W. O. E. Oesterley

9 COMMENTARY: Cocker your child, and he will make you afraid; play with him, and he will bring you to heaviness. Cockering a child breeds fear in the parent, and playing with him brings heaviness. — W. O. E. Oesterley

10 COMMENTARY: Do not laugh with him, lest you have sorrow with him, and lest you gnash your teeth in the end. Laughing along with a child ends in shared sorrow and gnashing of teeth. — W. O. E. Oesterley

11 COMMENTARY: Give him no liberty in his youth, and do not wink at his follies. Youth should be given no loose liberty, and follies must not be winked at. — W. O. E. Oesterley

12 COMMENTARY: Bow down his neck while he is young, and beat him on the sides while he is a child, lest he wax stubborn, be disobedient to you, and so bring sorrow to your heart. Early discipline bows the neck and corrects the child, before stubbornness and disobedience bring sorrow to the parent's heart. — W. O. E. Oesterley

13 COMMENTARY: Chastise your son, and hold him to labour, lest his lewd behaviour be an offence to you. Chastisement joined to labour prevents lewd behaviour becoming an offence. — W. O. E. Oesterley

14 COMMENTARY: Better is the poor, being sound and strong of constitution, than a rich man afflicted in his body. Sound, strong poverty is better than bodily afflicted riches. — W. O. E. Oesterley

15 COMMENTARY: Health and good estate of body are above all gold, and a strong body above infinite wealth. Health and bodily soundness outvalue gold and infinite wealth. — W. O. E. Oesterley

16 COMMENTARY: There is no riches above a sound body, and no joy above the joy of the heart. No riches exceed a sound body, and no joy exceeds heart-joy. — W. O. E. Oesterley

17 COMMENTARY: Death is better than a bitter life or continual sickness. Death is preferable to a bitter life or unending sickness. — W. O. E. Oesterley

18 COMMENTARY: Delicates poured upon a mouth shut up are as messes of meat set upon a grave. Delicacies for a shut mouth are like meat set on a grave: useless to the dead. — W. O. E. Oesterley

19 COMMENTARY: What good does the offering to an idol do? For neither can it eat nor smell; so is he who is persecuted of the Lord. An idol's offering is useless, since it cannot eat or smell, and so is the man persecuted by God, unable to enjoy what is set before him. — W. O. E. Oesterley

20 COMMENTARY: He sees with his eyes and groans, as a eunuch embracing a virgin and sighing. He sees and groans, like a eunuch embracing a virgin and sighing, desiring what he cannot enjoy. — W. O. E. Oesterley

21 COMMENTARY: Do not give over your mind to heaviness, and do not afflict yourself in your own counsel. The mind must not be surrendered to heaviness, nor the self afflicted by its own counsel. — W. O. E. Oesterley

22 COMMENTARY: The gladness of the heart is the life of man, and the joyfulness of a man prolongs his days. Heart-gladness is man's life, and joyfulness lengthens days. — W. O. E. Oesterley

23 COMMENTARY: Love your own soul, comfort your heart, remove sorrow far from you, for sorrow has killed many, and there is no profit therein. Love of one's own soul, comforted heart, and sorrow removed are urged, since sorrow has killed many and profits nothing. — W. O. E. Oesterley

24 COMMENTARY: Envy and wrath shorten life, and carefulness brings age before the time. Envy and wrath shorten life, and anxious care ages a person prematurely. — W. O. E. Oesterley

25 COMMENTARY: A cheerful and good heart will have a care of his meat and diet. A cheerful, good heart takes care even of meat and diet. — W. O. E. Oesterley
