Study Notes — Sirach

Chapter 48

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Then stood up Elias the prophet as fire, and his word burned like a lamp. Elijah rose as fire itself, his word burning like a lamp. — W. O. E. Oesterley

2 COMMENTARY: He brought a sore famine upon them, and by his zeal he diminished their number. Elijah's zeal brought sore famine and diminished the people's number. — W. O. E. Oesterley

3 COMMENTARY: By the word of the Lord he shut up the heaven, and also three times brought down fire. By God's word Elijah shut heaven and three times brought down fire. — W. O. E. Oesterley

4 COMMENTARY: O Elias, how you were honoured in your wondrous deeds! And who may glory like you! Elijah's wondrous deeds honoured him beyond all glorying. — W. O. E. Oesterley

5 COMMENTARY: Who raised up a dead man from death, and his soul from the place of the dead, by the word of the Most High. By God's word Elijah raised a dead man, his soul returned from the dead's place. — W. O. E. Oesterley

6 COMMENTARY: Who brought kings to destruction, and honourable men from their bed. Elijah brought kings to destruction and honourable men down from their beds. — W. O. E. Oesterley

7 COMMENTARY: Who heard the rebuke of the Lord in Sinai, and in Horeb the judgment of vengeance. Elijah heard God's rebuke in Sinai and vengeance's judgment in Horeb. — W. O. E. Oesterley

8 COMMENTARY: Who anointed kings to take revenge, and prophets to succeed after him. Elijah anointed kings for revenge and prophets to succeed him. — W. O. E. Oesterley

9 COMMENTARY: Who was taken up in a whirlwind of fire, and in a chariot of fiery horses. Elijah was taken up in a fiery whirlwind, in a chariot of fiery horses. — W. O. E. Oesterley

10 COMMENTARY: Who was ordained for reproofs in their times, to pacify the wrath of the Lord's judgment before it broke forth into fury, and to turn the heart of the father to the son, and to restore the tribes of Jacob. Elijah was ordained for timely reproofs, to pacify God's wrath before fury, turn fathers' hearts to sons, and restore Jacob's tribes. — W. O. E. Oesterley

11 COMMENTARY: Blessed are they who saw you, and slept in love, for we shall surely live. Those who saw Elijah and slept in love are blessed, for they shall surely live. — W. O. E. Oesterley

12 COMMENTARY: Elias it was, who was covered with a whirlwind, and Eliseus was filled with his spirit; whilst he lived, he was not moved with the presence of any prince, neither could any bring him into subjection. Elijah was whirlwind-covered, Elisha filled with his spirit, unmoved by any prince and unsubdued by anyone while he lived. — W. O. E. Oesterley

13 COMMENTARY: No word could overcome him, and after his death his body prophesied. No word overcame Elisha, and even after death his body prophesied. — W. O. E. Oesterley

14 COMMENTARY: He did wonders in his life, and at his death his works were marvellous. Elisha's life was wonders, and his death's works were marvellous. — W. O. E. Oesterley

15 COMMENTARY: For all this the people repented not, neither departed they from their sins, till they were spoiled and carried out of their land, and scattered through all the earth; yet there remained a small people, and a ruler in the house of David. Despite all this the people did not repent or leave sins until spoiled, exiled, and scattered, though a small people and a Davidic ruler remained. — W. O. E. Oesterley

16 COMMENTARY: Of whom some did what was pleasing to God, and some multiplied sins. Among the remnant, some pleased God and some multiplied sins. — W. O. E. Oesterley

17 COMMENTARY: Ezekias fortified his city, and brought in water into the midst thereof; he dug the hard rock with iron, and made wells for waters. Hezekiah fortified his city, bringing water into its midst by digging hard rock with iron and making wells. — W. O. E. Oesterley

18 COMMENTARY: In his time Sennacherib came up, and sent Rabsaces, and lifted up his hand against Sion, and boasted proudly. In Hezekiah's time Sennacherib came up, sent Rabshakeh, lifted his hand against Sion, and boasted proudly. — W. O. E. Oesterley

19 COMMENTARY: Then trembled their hearts and hands, and they were in pain, as women in travail. Hearts and hands trembled, and the people were in pain like women in travail. — W. O. E. Oesterley

20 COMMENTARY: But they called upon the Lord who is merciful, and stretched out their hands toward him, and immediately the Holy One heard them out of heaven, and delivered them by the ministry of Esay. Calling on the merciful Lord with outstretched hands, they were immediately heard from heaven and delivered through Isaiah's ministry. — W. O. E. Oesterley

21 COMMENTARY: He smote the host of the Assyrians, and his angel destroyed them. God smote the Assyrian host, and his angel destroyed them. — W. O. E. Oesterley

22 COMMENTARY: For Ezekias had done the thing that pleased the Lord, and was strong in the ways of David his father, as Esay the prophet, who was great and faithful in his vision, had commanded him. Hezekiah had pleased God and stood strong in David's ways, as Isaiah, great and faithful in vision, had commanded. — W. O. E. Oesterley

23 COMMENTARY: In his time the sun went backward, and he lengthened the king's life. In Isaiah's time the sun went backward, and the king's life was lengthened. — W. O. E. Oesterley

24 COMMENTARY: He saw by an excellent spirit what should come to pass at the last, and he comforted those who mourned in Sion. By an excellent spirit Isaiah saw the last things, and comforted Sion's mourners. — W. O. E. Oesterley

25 COMMENTARY: He showed what should come to pass for ever, and secret things before they came. Isaiah showed everlasting things to come, and secrets before they arrived. — W. O. E. Oesterley
