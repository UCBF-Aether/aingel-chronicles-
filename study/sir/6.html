Study Notes — Sirach

Chapter 6

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Instead of a friend, do not become an enemy, for thereby you will inherit an ill name, shame, and reproach, as does a sinner with a double tongue. A friendship turned to enmity leaves a bad name, and double-tongued sin produces the same shame. — W. O. E. Oesterley

2 COMMENTARY: Do not extol yourself in the counsel of your own heart, lest your soul be torn in pieces like a bull straying alone. Self-praise in secret counsel isolates a person like a straying bull, and the soul is torn by its own pride. — W. O. E. Oesterley

3 COMMENTARY: You will eat up your leaves, lose your fruit, and leave yourself as a dry tree. Pride consumes a person's own life: leaves eaten, fruit lost, and only a dry tree left. — W. O. E. Oesterley

4 COMMENTARY: A wicked soul will destroy him who has it, and make him laughed to scorn by his enemies. Wickedness within destroys its owner and hands his enemies their laughter. — W. O. E. Oesterley

5 COMMENTARY: Sweet language multiplies friends, and a fair-speaking tongue increases kind greetings. Gracious speech is fruitful: it grows friendships and draws kind greetings. — W. O. E. Oesterley

6 COMMENTARY: Be at peace with many, but have only one counsellor of a thousand. Peaceableness should be broad, but intimate counsel should be narrow and tested. — W. O. E. Oesterley

7 COMMENTARY: If you would get a friend, prove him first, and do not be hasty to credit him. Friendship must be tested before it is trusted; haste in crediting a stranger is folly. — W. O. E. Oesterley

8 COMMENTARY: Some man is a friend for his own occasion and will not abide in the day of your trouble. A friend of convenience disappears when trouble makes friendship costly. — W. O. E. Oesterley

9 COMMENTARY: There is a friend who, turned to enmity, will in strife discover your reproach. A false friend turned enemy uses quarrels to expose your shame. — W. O. E. Oesterley

10 COMMENTARY: Again, some friend is a companion at the table and will not continue in the day of your affliction. A table companion shares feasts, not afflictions. — W. O. E. Oesterley

11 COMMENTARY: In your prosperity he will be as yourself and will be bold over your servants. The fair-weather friend acts like an equal in good times and presumes over your household. — W. O. E. Oesterley

12 COMMENTARY: If you are brought low, he will be against you and will hide himself from your face. When you fall, that same friend turns against you and avoids your face. — W. O. E. Oesterley

13 COMMENTARY: Separate yourself from your enemies, and take heed of your friends. Enemies require distance, and even friends require watchfulness, since not every friend is faithful. — W. O. E. Oesterley

14 COMMENTARY: A faithful friend is a strong defence, and he who has found such a one has found a treasure. Faithfulness in friendship is protection, and finding it is finding treasure. — W. O. E. Oesterley

15 COMMENTARY: Nothing countervails a faithful friend, and his excellency is invaluable. No price balances a faithful friend, and his worth cannot be valued. — W. O. E. Oesterley

16 COMMENTARY: A faithful friend is the medicine of life, and those who fear the Lord will find him. A faithful friend heals life's ills, and it is the God-fearing who find such friends. — W. O. E. Oesterley

17 COMMENTARY: Whoever fears the Lord will direct his friendship aright, for as he is, so will his neighbour be also. Reverence orders friendship rightly, and a person's friends tend to mirror what he himself is. — W. O. E. Oesterley

18 COMMENTARY: Gather instruction from your youth up, and you will find wisdom till your old age. Instruction gathered early becomes wisdom that lasts into old age. — W. O. E. Oesterley

19 COMMENTARY: Come to wisdom as one who ploughs and sows, and wait for her good fruits; you will not toil much in labouring about her, but you will eat of her fruits right soon. Wisdom repays farming-like labour quickly: the work is light beside the early harvest. — W. O. E. Oesterley

20 COMMENTARY: Wisdom is very unpleasant to the unlearned, and he who is without understanding will not remain with her. To the untaught, wisdom feels harsh, and the uncomprehending will not stay with her. — W. O. E. Oesterley

21 COMMENTARY: She will lie upon him as a mighty stone of trial, and he will cast her from him before long. Wisdom weighs on the unlearned like a heavy testing stone, and he soon throws her off. — W. O. E. Oesterley

22 COMMENTARY: Wisdom is according to her name, and she is not manifest to many. Wisdom lives up to her demanding name, and that is why she is not plain to the many. — W. O. E. Oesterley

23 COMMENTARY: Give ear, receive advice, and refuse not counsel. The first posture toward wisdom is listening and accepting counsel rather than refusing it. — W. O. E. Oesterley

24 COMMENTARY: Put your feet into her fetters and your neck into her chain. Wisdom's discipline is pictured as bonds willingly entered, not chains imposed by force. — W. O. E. Oesterley

25 COMMENTARY: Bow down your shoulder, bear her, and do not be grieved with her bonds. Carrying wisdom's discipline should be done without resentment at its restraint. — W. O. E. Oesterley

26 COMMENTARY: Come to her with your whole heart, and keep her ways with all your power. Half-hearted approach will not do; wisdom asks for the whole heart and full strength. — W. O. E. Oesterley

27 COMMENTARY: Search and seek, and she will be made known to you; when you have got hold of her, do not let her go. Diligent seeking is answered by wisdom revealing herself, and the find must then be held fast. — W. O. E. Oesterley

28 COMMENTARY: At the last you will find her rest, and that will be turned to your joy. The end of wisdom's discipline is rest, and that rest becomes joy. — W. O. E. Oesterley

29 COMMENTARY: Then her fetters will be a strong defence for you, and her chains a robe of glory. The bonds that once restrained become protection and honour. — W. O. E. Oesterley

30 COMMENTARY: There is a golden ornament upon her, and her bands are purple lace. Wisdom's restraints are described as gold and purple, the dress of royalty, not of slavery. — W. O. E. Oesterley

31 COMMENTARY: You will put her on as a robe of honour and put her about you as a crown of joy. Wisdom, once worn as bonds, is finally worn as honour's robe and joy's crown. — W. O. E. Oesterley

32 COMMENTARY: If you will, you will be taught, and if you apply your mind, you will be prudent. Willingness opens teaching, and applied attention produces prudence. — W. O. E. Oesterley

33 COMMENTARY: If you love to hear, you will receive understanding, and if you bow your ear, you will be wise. Love of listening brings understanding, and a bowed ear is the road to wisdom. — W. O. E. Oesterley

34 COMMENTARY: Stand in the multitude of the elders, and cleave to him who is wise. Company with elders and close attachment to a wise man are the school of wisdom. — W. O. E. Oesterley

35 COMMENTARY: Be willing to hear every godly discourse, and let not the parables of understanding escape you. Every godly discourse deserves a willing hearing, and pointed sayings of understanding should be held, not lost. — W. O. E. Oesterley

36 COMMENTARY: If you see a man of understanding, get to him betimes, and let your foot wear the steps of his door. Seek out the understanding man early and often, until your feet have worn a path to his door. — W. O. E. Oesterley

37 COMMENTARY: Let your mind be upon the ordinances of the Lord and meditate continually in his commandments; he will establish your heart and give you wisdom at your own desire. Meditation on God's ordinances steadies the heart, and God himself grants the wisdom so desired. — W. O. E. Oesterley
