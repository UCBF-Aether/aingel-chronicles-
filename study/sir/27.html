Study Notes — Sirach

Chapter 27

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Many have sinned for a small matter, and he who seeks for abundance will turn his eyes away. Small matters cause many sins, and seeking abundance turns the eyes away from right. — W. O. E. Oesterley

2 COMMENTARY: As a nail sticks fast between the joinings of the stones, so does sin stick close between buying and selling. Sin clings between buying and selling as a nail sticks between stones. — W. O. E. Oesterley

3 COMMENTARY: Unless a man holds himself diligently in the fear of the Lord, his house will soon be overthrown. Without diligent fear of God, a house is soon overthrown. — W. O. E. Oesterley

4 COMMENTARY: As when one sifts with a sieve, the refuse remains, so the filth of man in his talk. Sifting leaves refuse, and a man's talk leaves his filth behind. — W. O. E. Oesterley

5 COMMENTARY: The furnace proves the potter's vessels, so the trial of man is in his reasoning. As the furnace proves pottery, reasoning proves a man. — W. O. E. Oesterley

6 COMMENTARY: The fruit declares if the tree has been dressed, so is the utterance of a conceit in the heart of man. Fruit shows whether a tree was tended, and speech shows the conceit in a man's heart. — W. O. E. Oesterley

7 COMMENTARY: Praise no man before you hear him speak, for this is the trial of men. A man's speech is his trial, so no one should be praised before hearing him. — W. O. E. Oesterley

8 COMMENTARY: If you follow righteousness, you will obtain her, and put her on as a glorious long robe. Following righteousness leads to obtaining her and wearing her like a glorious long robe. — W. O. E. Oesterley

9 COMMENTARY: The birds will resort to their like, so will truth return to those who practise in her. Birds flock to their like, and truth returns to those who practise it. — W. O. E. Oesterley

10 COMMENTARY: As the lion lies in wait for the prey, so sin for those who work iniquity. Sin waits for workers of iniquity as a lion waits for prey. — W. O. E. Oesterley

11 COMMENTARY: The discourse of a godly man is always with wisdom, but a fool changes as the moon. A godly man's discourse stays with wisdom, while a fool changes like the moon. — W. O. E. Oesterley

12 COMMENTARY: If you are among the indiscreet, observe the time, but be continually among men of understanding. Among the indiscreet, watch the time and leave; among the understanding, stay continually. — W. O. E. Oesterley

13 COMMENTARY: The discourse of fools is irksome, and their sport is the wantonness of sin. Fools' discourse wearies, and their sport is wanton sin. — W. O. E. Oesterley

14 COMMENTARY: The talk of him who swears much makes the hair stand upright, and their brawls make one stop his ears. Much swearing makes hair stand up, and brawls make hearers stop their ears. — W. O. E. Oesterley

15 COMMENTARY: The strife of the proud is bloodshedding, and their revilings are grievous to the ear. Proud strife ends in bloodshed, and proud revilings grieve the ear. — W. O. E. Oesterley

16 COMMENTARY: Whoever discovers secrets loses his credit, and will never find a friend to his mind. Revealing secrets forfeits trust, and the revealer never finds a friend to his mind. — W. O. E. Oesterley

17 COMMENTARY: Love your friend, and be faithful to him, but if you betray his secrets, follow no more after him. Faithful love is the rule, but betrayed secrets end the pursuit of that friend. — W. O. E. Oesterley

18 COMMENTARY: For as a man has destroyed his enemy, so have you lost the love of your neighbour. Betraying secrets destroys neighbourly love as surely as destroying an enemy. — W. O. E. Oesterley

19 COMMENTARY: As one who lets a bird go out of his hand, so have you let your neighbour go, and will not get him again. A betrayed friend is like a bird loosed from the hand: gone, and not to be caught again. — W. O. E. Oesterley

20 COMMENTARY: Follow after him no more, for he is too far off; he is as a roe escaped out of the snare. Pursuit is useless, for the friend has fled like a roe out of the snare. — W. O. E. Oesterley

21 COMMENTARY: As for a wound, it may be bound up, and after reviling there may be reconcilement, but he who betrays secrets is without hope. Wounds can be bound and revilings reconciled, but betrayal of secrets leaves no hope of reconciliation. — W. O. E. Oesterley

22 COMMENTARY: He who winks with the eyes works evil, and he who knows him will depart from him. Winking eyes signal worked evil, and those who know such a man depart from him. — W. O. E. Oesterley

23 COMMENTARY: When you are present, he will speak sweetly and admire your words, but at the last he will writhe his mouth and slander your sayings. The insincere man flatters to your face, then twists his mouth and slanders what you said. — W. O. E. Oesterley

24 COMMENTARY: I have hated many things, but nothing like him, for the Lord will hate him. The writer's hatred for the insincere man surpasses all other hatreds, and God will hate him too. — W. O. E. Oesterley

25 COMMENTARY: Whoever casts a stone on high casts it on his own head, and a deceitful stroke will make wounds. A stone thrown upward falls on the thrower's head, and a deceitful blow wounds its giver. — W. O. E. Oesterley

26 COMMENTARY: Whoever digs a pit will fall therein, and he who sets a trap will be taken therein. Pit-diggers fall into their pits and trap-setters are caught in their traps. — W. O. E. Oesterley

27 COMMENTARY: He who works mischief, it will fall upon him, and he will not know whence it came to him. Mischief returns on its worker from an unknown quarter. — W. O. E. Oesterley

28 COMMENTARY: Mockery and reproach are from the proud, but vengeance, as a lion, will lie in wait for them. Mockery and reproach come from the proud, but vengeance waits for them like a lion. — W. O. E. Oesterley

29 COMMENTARY: Those who rejoice at the fall of the righteous will be taken in the snare, and anguish will consume them before they die. Rejoicing at the righteous' fall ends in snares and consuming anguish before death. — W. O. E. Oesterley

30 COMMENTARY: Malice and wrath, even these are abominations, and the sinful man will have them both. Malice and wrath are abominations, and the sinful man possesses both. — W. O. E. Oesterley
