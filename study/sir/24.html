Study Notes — Sirach

Chapter 24

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Wisdom will praise herself and glory in the midst of her people. Wisdom speaks her own praise openly among her people, not in secret. — W. O. E. Oesterley

2 COMMENTARY: In the congregation of the Most High she will open her mouth and triumph before his power. Wisdom's speech is public, in God's congregation, triumphing before his power. — W. O. E. Oesterley

3 COMMENTARY: I came out of the mouth of the Most High, and covered the earth as a cloud. Wisdom's origin is God's own mouth, and her presence covers the earth like a cloud. — W. O. E. Oesterley

4 COMMENTARY: I dwelt in high places, and my throne is in a cloudy pillar. Wisdom's dwelling is on high, enthroned in a cloudy pillar. — W. O. E. Oesterley

5 COMMENTARY: I alone compassed the circuit of heaven, and walked in the bottom of the deep. Wisdom alone spans heaven's circuit and walks the deep's bottom. — W. O. E. Oesterley

6 COMMENTARY: In the waves of the sea and in all the earth, and in every people and nation, I got a possession. Sea, earth, and every people and nation all hold something of wisdom's possession. — W. O. E. Oesterley

7 COMMENTARY: With all these I sought rest, and in whose inheritance shall I abide? Amid all peoples wisdom sought a resting place, asking in whose inheritance she should abide. — W. O. E. Oesterley

8 COMMENTARY: So the Creator of all things gave me a commandment, and he who made me caused my tabernacle to rest, and said, let your dwelling be in Jacob, and your inheritance in Israel. The Creator commanded wisdom's rest: her dwelling in Jacob and her inheritance in Israel. — W. O. E. Oesterley

9 COMMENTARY: He created me from the beginning before the world, and I shall never fail. Wisdom was created from the beginning, before the world, and will never fail. — W. O. E. Oesterley

10 COMMENTARY: In the holy tabernacle I served before him, and so was I established in Sion. Wisdom served in the holy tabernacle and was thus established in Sion. — W. O. E. Oesterley

11 COMMENTARY: Likewise in the beloved city he gave me rest, and in Jerusalem was my power. In beloved Jerusalem wisdom found rest, and there her power resides. — W. O. E. Oesterley

12 COMMENTARY: I took root in an honourable people, even in the portion of the Lord's inheritance. Wisdom rooted herself in an honourable people, the Lord's own portion. — W. O. E. Oesterley

13 COMMENTARY: I was exalted like a cedar in Libanus, and as a cypress tree upon the mountains of Hermon. Wisdom's stature is like Lebanon's cedar and Hermon's cypress, tall and enduring. — W. O. E. Oesterley

14 COMMENTARY: I was exalted like a palm tree in En-gaddi, and as a rose plant in Jericho, as a fair olive tree in a pleasant field, and grew up as a plane tree by the water. Palm, rose, olive, and plane tree together picture wisdom's beauty, fruitfulness, and flourishing by water. — W. O. E. Oesterley

15 COMMENTARY: I gave a sweet smell like cinnamon and aspalathus, and yielded a pleasant odour like the best myrrh, as galbanum, onyx, sweet storax, and as the fume of frankincense in the tabernacle. Wisdom's fragrance is like the precious spices and the frankincense of the tabernacle itself. — W. O. E. Oesterley

16 COMMENTARY: As the turpentine tree I stretched out my branches, and my branches are the branches of honour and grace. Wisdom spreads like the turpentine tree, and her branches are honour and grace. — W. O. E. Oesterley

17 COMMENTARY: As the vine I brought forth pleasant savour, and my flowers are the fruit of honour and riches. Like the vine, wisdom yields pleasant savour, and her flowers fruit into honour and riches. — W. O. E. Oesterley

18 COMMENTARY: I am the mother of fair love, fear, knowledge, and holy hope; I therefore, being eternal, am given to all my children named of him. Wisdom is mother of fair love, fear, knowledge, and holy hope, and being eternal she is given to all her children named by God. — W. O. E. Oesterley

19 COMMENTARY: Come to me, all you who are desirous of me, and fill yourselves with my fruits. Wisdom invites all who desire her to come and be filled with her fruits. — W. O. E. Oesterley

20 COMMENTARY: For my memorial is sweeter than honey, and my inheritance than the honeycomb. Wisdom's memory is sweeter than honey, and her inheritance sweeter than the honeycomb. — W. O. E. Oesterley

21 COMMENTARY: Those who eat me will yet be hungry, and those who drink me will yet be thirsty. Eating and drinking wisdom do not sate but deepen hunger and thirst for more. — W. O. E. Oesterley

22 COMMENTARY: He who obeys me will never be confounded, and those who work by me will not do amiss. Obedience to wisdom prevents confusion, and working by her prevents wrongdoing. — W. O. E. Oesterley

23 COMMENTARY: All these things are the book of the covenant of the Most High God, even the law which Moses commanded for a heritage to the congregations of Jacob. The climax of wisdom's speech is identification: all this is the book of the covenant, the law Moses commanded as Jacob's heritage. Wisdom and Law are one. — W. O. E. Oesterley

24 COMMENTARY: Do not faint to be strong in the Lord, that he may confirm you; cleave to him, for the Lord Almighty is God alone, and beside him there is no other Saviour. Strength in the Lord, confirmed by cleaving to him, rests on this: God Almighty alone is God and Saviour. — W. O. E. Oesterley

25 COMMENTARY: He fills all things with his wisdom, as Phison and as Tigris in the time of the new fruits. God's wisdom floods all things like the rivers Phison and Tigris at new-fruit season. — W. O. E. Oesterley

26 COMMENTARY: He makes understanding abound like Euphrates, and as Jordan in the time of harvest. Understanding overflows like Euphrates and like Jordan at harvest. — W. O. E. Oesterley

27 COMMENTARY: He makes the doctrine of knowledge appear as the light, and as Geon in the time of vintage. Knowledge's doctrine shines like light and flows like Geon at vintage. — W. O. E. Oesterley

28 COMMENTARY: The first man knew her not perfectly, and no more will the last find her out. Neither the first man nor the last will fully know wisdom or find her out. — W. O. E. Oesterley

29 COMMENTARY: Her thoughts are more than the sea, and her counsels profounder than the great deep. Wisdom's thoughts exceed the sea and her counsels go deeper than the great deep. — W. O. E. Oesterley

30 COMMENTARY: I also came out as a brook from a river, and as a conduit into a garden. The writer pictures himself as a brook from the river, a conduit leading into a garden. — W. O. E. Oesterley

31 COMMENTARY: I said, I will water my best garden, and will water abundantly my garden bed; and lo, my brook became a river, and my river became a sea. Watering his garden, the writer's brook grew into a river and then a sea; small teaching work overflows its bounds. — W. O. E. Oesterley

32 COMMENTARY: I will yet make doctrine shine as the morning, and will send forth her light afar off. Doctrine will yet shine like morning light, sent far off. — W. O. E. Oesterley

33 COMMENTARY: I will yet pour out doctrine as prophecy, and leave it to all ages for ever. Doctrine poured out like prophecy is left for all ages for ever. — W. O. E. Oesterley

34 COMMENTARY: Behold, I have not laboured for myself only, but for all those who seek wisdom. The writer's labour is not for himself alone but for all seekers of wisdom. — W. O. E. Oesterley
