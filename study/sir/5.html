Study Notes — Sirach

Chapter 5

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Do not set your heart upon your goods, nor say you have enough for your life. Wealth must not become the heart's anchor, and a claim of self-sufficiency is itself a danger. — W. O. E. Oesterley

2 COMMENTARY: Do not follow your own mind and strength to walk in the ways of your heart. Self-will, trusted as a guide, leads a person wherever appetite points. — W. O. E. Oesterley

3 COMMENTARY: Do not say, who shall control me for my works, for the Lord will surely avenge your pride. Boasting that no one can call you to account invites the Lord's own reckoning with pride. — W. O. E. Oesterley

4 COMMENTARY: Do not say, I have sinned, and what harm has happened to me, for the Lord is longsuffering but will in no way let you go. Delay of punishment is God's patience, not his permission, and it never means escape. — W. O. E. Oesterley

5 COMMENTARY: Concerning propitiation, do not be without fear while adding sin to sin. Presuming on forgiveness while piling up sins is a fearless folly, not faith. — W. O. E. Oesterley

6 COMMENTARY: Do not say his mercy is great and he will be pacified for the multitude of my sins, for mercy and wrath both come from him, and his indignation rests on sinners. God's mercy cannot be quoted against his wrath; both belong to him, and wrath rests on persistent sin. — W. O. E. Oesterley

7 COMMENTARY: Do not tarry to turn to the Lord, nor put it off from day to day, for suddenly his wrath will come forth, and in your security you will be destroyed and perish in the day of vengeance. Delay in repentance is perilous because judgment arrives suddenly, catching the secure unprepared. — W. O. E. Oesterley

8 COMMENTARY: Do not set your heart upon goods unjustly gotten, for they will not profit you in the day of calamity. Ill-gotten wealth fails exactly when it is most needed, in calamity. — W. O. E. Oesterley

9 COMMENTARY: Do not winnow with every wind, nor go into every way, for so does the sinner with a double tongue. Being blown about by every opinion and taking every road is the mark of a double-tongued sinner. — W. O. E. Oesterley

10 COMMENTARY: Be steadfast in your understanding, and let your word be the same. A settled mind shows itself in consistent speech. — W. O. E. Oesterley

11 COMMENTARY: Be swift to hear, let your life be sincere, and with patience give answer. Quick listening, a sincere life, and a patient answer belong together as one discipline of speech. — W. O. E. Oesterley

12 COMMENTARY: If you have understanding, answer your neighbour; if not, lay your hand upon your mouth. Answer only from real understanding; where it is lacking, silence is the right response. — W. O. E. Oesterley

13 COMMENTARY: Honour and shame are in talk, and a man's tongue is his fall. Speech is where honour is won or lost, and the tongue is the usual instrument of a fall. — W. O. E. Oesterley

14 COMMENTARY: Do not be called a whisperer, nor lie in wait with your tongue, for foul shame is upon the thief and evil condemnation upon the double tongue. Secret slander and lying in wait with words bring shame like theft and condemnation for double speech. — W. O. E. Oesterley

15 COMMENTARY: Do not be ignorant of anything in a great matter or a small. Carelessness about details, great or small, is itself a fault to be avoided. — W. O. E. Oesterley
