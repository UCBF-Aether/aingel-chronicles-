Study Notes — Sirach

Chapter 51

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: I will thank you, O Lord and King, and praise you, O God my Saviour; I give praise to your name. The closing prayer begins with thanks and praise to the Lord and King, God and Saviour, for his name. — W. O. E. Oesterley; George Leo Haydock

2 COMMENTARY: For you are my defender and helper, and have preserved my body from destruction, from the snare of the slanderous tongue, from lips that forge lies, and have been my helper against my adversaries. God defended and helped, preserving body from destruction, snares of slander, and lying lips, against adversaries. — W. O. E. Oesterley

3 COMMENTARY: You have delivered me, according to the multitude of your mercies and greatness of your name, from the teeth of those ready to devour me, out of the hands of those who sought my life, and from the manifold afflictions I had. By God's manifold mercies and great name, the writer was delivered from devouring teeth, life-seekers' hands, and manifold afflictions. — W. O. E. Oesterley

4 COMMENTARY: From the choking of fire on every side, and from the midst of the fire which I kindled not. Deliverance came from choking fire on every side, and from fire not kindled by the writer. — W. O. E. Oesterley

5 COMMENTARY: From the depth of the belly of hell, from an unclean tongue, and from lying words. Deliverance came from hell's depth, unclean tongues, and lying words. — W. O. E. Oesterley

6 COMMENTARY: By an accusation to the king from an unrighteous tongue my soul drew near even to death, my life was near to the hell beneath. Accusation to the king by an unrighteous tongue brought the writer's soul near death and his life near hell beneath. — W. O. E. Oesterley; George Leo Haydock

7 COMMENTARY: They compassed me on every side, and there was no man to help me; I looked for the succour of men, but there was none. Enemies compassed him on every side, and no man helped; human succour was sought and none found. — W. O. E. Oesterley; George Leo Haydock

8 COMMENTARY: Then I thought upon your mercy, O Lord, and upon your acts of old, how you deliver those who wait for you, and save them out of the hands of the enemies. Then he remembered God's mercy and ancient acts, delivering waiters and saving them from enemies' hands. — W. O. E. Oesterley

9 COMMENTARY: Then I lifted up my supplications from the earth, and prayed for deliverance from death. Supplications rose from the earth, praying for deliverance from death. — W. O. E. Oesterley

10 COMMENTARY: I called upon the Lord, the Father of my Lord, that he would not leave me in the days of my trouble, and in the time of the proud, when there was no help. He called on the Lord, Father of his Lord, not to leave him in trouble's days and the proud's time, when no help existed. — W. O. E. Oesterley

11 COMMENTARY: I will praise your name continually, and will sing praises with thanksgiving; and so my prayer was heard. Continual praise with thanksgiving followed, and so the prayer was heard. — W. O. E. Oesterley

12 COMMENTARY: For you saved me from destruction, and delivered me from the evil time; therefore I will give thanks, praise you, and bless your name, O Lord. Saved from destruction and the evil time, the writer gives thanks, praise, and blessing to God's name. — W. O. E. Oesterley

13 COMMENTARY: When I was yet young, before I went abroad, I desired wisdom openly in my prayer. Young, before going abroad, the writer openly desired wisdom in prayer. — W. O. E. Oesterley

14 COMMENTARY: I prayed for her before the temple, and will seek her out even to the end. He prayed for wisdom before the temple, and will seek her to the end. — W. O. E. Oesterley

15 COMMENTARY: Even from the flower till the grape was ripe my heart delighted in her; my foot went the right way, from my youth up I sought after her. From flower to ripe grape his heart delighted in wisdom, his foot went rightly, seeking her from youth. — W. O. E. Oesterley

16 COMMENTARY: I bowed down my ear a little, and received her, and got much learning. Bowing his ear a little, he received wisdom and got much learning. — W. O. E. Oesterley

17 COMMENTARY: I profited therein, therefore I will ascribe glory to him who gives me wisdom. Profiting in wisdom, he ascribes glory to its Giver. — W. O. E. Oesterley

18 COMMENTARY: For I purposed to do after her, and earnestly I followed what is good, so I will not be confounded. Purposing to follow wisdom and earnestly following good, he will not be confounded. — W. O. E. Oesterley

19 COMMENTARY: My soul has wrestled with her, and in my doings I was exact; I stretched forth my hands to heaven above, and bewailed my ignorances of her. His soul wrestled with wisdom, his doings were exact, hands stretched to heaven, bewailing ignorance of her. — W. O. E. Oesterley

20 COMMENTARY: I directed my soul to her, and found her in pureness; I have had my heart joined with her from the beginning, therefore I will not be forsaken. Directing his soul to wisdom, he found her in pureness, heart joined with her from the beginning, and will not be forsaken. — W. O. E. Oesterley

21 COMMENTARY: My heart was troubled in seeking her, therefore I have gotten a good possession. Trouble in seeking wisdom yielded a good possession. — W. O. E. Oesterley

22 COMMENTARY: The Lord has given me a tongue for my reward, and I will praise him therewith. A tongue given as reward will be used to praise God. — W. O. E. Oesterley

23 COMMENTARY: Draw near to me, you unlearned, and dwell in the house of learning. The unlearned are invited to draw near and dwell in learning's house. — W. O. E. Oesterley

24 COMMENTARY: Why are you slow, and what do you say to these things, seeing your souls are very thirsty? Slowness is challenged, since souls are very thirsty for what is offered. — W. O. E. Oesterley

25 COMMENTARY: I opened my mouth, and said, buy her for yourselves without money. Wisdom is offered to be bought without money, by the opened mouth's invitation. — W. O. E. Oesterley

26 COMMENTARY: Put your neck under the yoke, and let your soul receive instruction; she is hard at hand to find. The yoke of instruction should be taken, since wisdom is close at hand to find. — W. O. E. Oesterley

27 COMMENTARY: Behold with your eyes, how I have but little labour, and have gotten to me much rest. Little labour brought much rest, as the writer's own eyes can show. — W. O. E. Oesterley

28 COMMENTARY: Get learning with a great sum of money, and get much gold by her. Learning should be gotten at great cost, since much gold is gotten by her. — W. O. E. Oesterley

29 COMMENTARY: Let your soul rejoice in his mercy, and do not be ashamed of his praise. The soul should rejoice in God's mercy, unashamed of his praise. — W. O. E. Oesterley

30 COMMENTARY: Work your work betimes, and in his time he will give you your reward. Work should be done betimes, and in God's time the reward will be given. — W. O. E. Oesterley
