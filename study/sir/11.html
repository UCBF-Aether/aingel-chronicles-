Study Notes — Sirach

Chapter 11

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Wisdom lifts up the head of him who is of low degree and makes him sit among great men. Wisdom raises the lowly to sit with the great. — W. O. E. Oesterley

2 COMMENTARY: Do not commend a man for his beauty, nor abhor a man for his outward appearance. Beauty is no ground for praise, and outward appearance none for contempt. — W. O. E. Oesterley

3 COMMENTARY: The bee is little among flying things, but her fruit is the chief of sweet things. Smallness does not decide worth: the little bee yields the sweetest fruit. — W. O. E. Oesterley

4 COMMENTARY: Do not boast of clothing and raiment, nor exalt yourself in the day of honour, for the works of the Lord are wonderful and his works among men are hidden. Clothing and honours are no ground for boasting, since God's workings among men are wonderful and hidden. — W. O. E. Oesterley

5 COMMENTARY: Many kings have sat down upon the ground, and one never thought of has worn the crown. Kings fall to the ground while an unregarded man takes the crown; fortune reverses. — W. O. E. Oesterley

6 COMMENTARY: Many mighty men have been greatly disgraced, and the honourable delivered into other men's hands. Might ends in disgrace and honourable men fall into others' hands, showing how unstable human standing is. — W. O. E. Oesterley

7 COMMENTARY: Do not blame before you have examined the truth; understand first, and then rebuke. Blame must wait on examination; understanding comes first, rebuke second. — W. O. E. Oesterley

8 COMMENTARY: Do not answer before you have heard the cause, nor interrupt men in the midst of their talk. Answering unheard and interrupting speech are both faults of impatience. — W. O. E. Oesterley

9 COMMENTARY: Do not strive in a matter that does not concern you, and do not sit in judgment with sinners. Meddling in others' matters and judging alongside sinners are both to be avoided. — W. O. E. Oesterley

10 COMMENTARY: Do not meddle with many matters, for if you meddle much you will not be innocent; and if you follow after, you will not obtain, nor escape by fleeing. Meddling widely brings guilt, and chasing after many things ends neither in obtaining nor in escape. — W. O. E. Oesterley

11 COMMENTARY: There is one who labours, takes pains, and makes haste, and is so much the more behind. Labour, pain, and haste do not guarantee progress; some fall further behind the harder they hurry. — W. O. E. Oesterley

12 COMMENTARY: Again, there is another who is slow, has need of help, wants ability, and is full of poverty; yet the eye of the Lord looked upon him for good and set him up from his low estate. God's favourable look can raise the slow, helpless, and poor from their low estate. — W. O. E. Oesterley

13 COMMENTARY: God lifted up his head from misery, so that many who saw marvelled at him. The lowly man raised from misery becomes a marvel to onlookers. — W. O. E. Oesterley

14 COMMENTARY: Prosperity and adversity, life and death, poverty and riches, come of the Lord. All the great reversals of life are in the Lord's hand, not in man's control. — W. O. E. Oesterley

15 COMMENTARY: Wisdom, knowledge, and understanding of the law are of the Lord; love and the way of good works are from him. Wisdom and knowledge of the law, and love with good works, all come from the Lord. — W. O. E. Oesterley

16 COMMENTARY: Error and darkness had their beginning together with sinners, and evil will wax old with those who glory in it. Error and darkness begin with sinners, and evil ages alongside those who glory in it. — W. O. E. Oesterley

17 COMMENTARY: The gift of the Lord remains with the ungodly, and his favour brings prosperity for ever. Even the ungodly may keep God's gift, and his favour can bring lasting prosperity, which is why present prosperity proves nothing. — W. O. E. Oesterley

18 COMMENTARY: There is one who waxes rich by wariness and pinching, and this is the portion of his reward. A man can grow rich by careful pinching, and that pinched wealth is all his reward. — W. O. E. Oesterley

19 COMMENTARY: Whereas he says, I have found rest, and now will eat continually of my goods; yet he does not know what time will come upon him, and that he must leave those things to others and die. The self-satisfied rich man promises himself rest and feasting, not knowing that time will take his goods to others and himself to death. — W. O. E. Oesterley

20 COMMENTARY: Be steadfast in your covenant, be conversant therein, and wax old in your work. Steadfastness in covenant, familiarity with it, and growing old in one's work are the steady course. — W. O. E. Oesterley

21 COMMENTARY: Do not marvel at the works of sinners, but trust in the Lord and abide in your labour, for it is easy in the sight of the Lord suddenly to make a poor man rich. Sinners' works should not astonish; trust and steady labour are the answer, since God can enrich the poor suddenly. — W. O. E. Oesterley

22 COMMENTARY: The blessing of the Lord is in the reward of the godly, and suddenly he makes his blessing flourish. The godly are rewarded by God's blessing, which he can make flourish in a moment. — W. O. E. Oesterley

23 COMMENTARY: Do not say, what profit is there in my service, and what good things will I have hereafter? Questioning the profit of service betrays impatience with God's timing. — W. O. E. Oesterley

24 COMMENTARY: Again, do not say, I have enough and possess many things, and what evil will I have hereafter? Claiming sufficiency in possessions and denying future evil is the same folly in its prosperous form. — W. O. E. Oesterley

25 COMMENTARY: In the day of prosperity there is forgetfulness of affliction, and in the day of affliction there is no more remembrance of prosperity. Prosperity forgets affliction and affliction forgets prosperity; neither state remembers the other. — W. O. E. Oesterley

26 COMMENTARY: It is an easy thing to the Lord in the day of death to reward a man according to his ways. At death God rewards each man according to his ways, and for him this is easy. — W. O. E. Oesterley

27 COMMENTARY: The affliction of an hour makes a man forget pleasure, and in his end his deeds will be discovered. A brief affliction erases remembered pleasure, and a man's deeds are revealed at his end. — W. O. E. Oesterley

28 COMMENTARY: Judge none blessed before his death, for a man will be known in his children. Blessedness cannot be judged before death, and a man is known in what his children become. — W. O. E. Oesterley

29 COMMENTARY: Do not bring every man into your house, for the deceitful man has many trains. Indiscriminate hospitality invites deceit, which comes with many snares. — W. O. E. Oesterley

30 COMMENTARY: As a partridge taken and kept in a cage, so is the heart of the proud, and like a spy he watches for your fall. The proud heart sits caged like a partridge yet watches like a spy for a neighbour's fall. — W. O. E. Oesterley

31 COMMENTARY: For he lies in wait, turns good into evil, and in things worthy of praise will lay blame upon you. The deceitful watcher twists good into evil and blames even praiseworthy things. — W. O. E. Oesterley

32 COMMENTARY: Of a spark of fire a heap of coals is kindled, and a sinful man lies in wait for blood. Small beginnings kindle great fires, and the sinful man waits to shed blood. — W. O. E. Oesterley

33 COMMENTARY: Take heed of a mischievous man, for he works wickedness, lest he bring upon you a perpetual blot. A mischievous man must be watched, lest his wickedness leave a lasting blot on you. — W. O. E. Oesterley

34 COMMENTARY: Receive a stranger into your house, and he will disturb you and turn you out of your own. A stranger taken in can disturb a household and even turn the owner out of it. — W. O. E. Oesterley
