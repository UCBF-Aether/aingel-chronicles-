Study Notes — Sirach

Chapter 10

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: A wise judge will instruct his people, and the government of a prudent man is well ordered. Wise judging instructs, and prudent rule orders a state well. — W. O. E. Oesterley

2 COMMENTARY: As the judge of the people is himself, so are his officers, and what manner of man the ruler of the city is, such are all who dwell therein. Officers mirror their judge, and a city's people take the character of their ruler. — W. O. E. Oesterley

3 COMMENTARY: An unwise king destroys his people, but through the prudence of those in authority the city will be inhabited. Folly at the top ruins a people, while prudence in authority keeps a city inhabited. — W. O. E. Oesterley

4 COMMENTARY: The power of the earth is in the hand of the Lord, and in due time he will set over it one who is profitable. Earthly power belongs to God, and he raises up a profitable ruler in his own time. — W. O. E. Oesterley

5 COMMENTARY: In the hand of God is the prosperity of man, and upon the person of the scribe he will lay his honour. Human prosperity rests in God's hand, and honour is laid by him on the learned scribe. — W. O. E. Oesterley

6 COMMENTARY: Do not bear hatred to your neighbour for every wrong, and do nothing at all by injurious practices. Every wrong should not breed hatred, and injury must never be answered with injurious practice. — W. O. E. Oesterley

7 COMMENTARY: Pride is hateful before God and man, and by both is one found committing iniquity. Pride offends both God and man, and both find iniquity in the proud. — W. O. E. Oesterley

8 COMMENTARY: Because of unrighteous dealings, injuries, and riches got by deceit, the kingdom is translated from one people to another. Unrighteous rule, injury, and deceitful wealth move kingdoms from one people to another. — W. O. E. Oesterley

9 COMMENTARY: Why is earth and ashes proud? There is nothing more wicked than a covetous man, for such a one sets his own soul to sale, because while he lives he casts away his bowels. Man is dust, so pride in him is absurd, and covetousness is the worst wickedness: it sells the soul and hardens the heart while still alive. — W. O. E. Oesterley

10 COMMENTARY: The physician cuts off a long disease, and he who is today a king tomorrow will die. As a physician cuts off disease, so death cuts off kings; today's king is tomorrow's corpse. — W. O. E. Oesterley

11 COMMENTARY: When a man is dead, he will inherit creeping things, beasts, and worms. Death's inheritance is not glory but worms and creeping things. — W. O. E. Oesterley

12 COMMENTARY: The beginning of pride is when one departs from God, and his heart is turned away from his Maker. Pride begins in departure from God, when the heart turns from its Maker. — W. O. E. Oesterley

13 COMMENTARY: Pride is the beginning of sin, and he who has it will pour out abomination; therefore the Lord brought strange calamities upon them and overthrew them utterly. Pride breeds sin and pours out abomination, and God answers with strange calamities and utter overthrow. — W. O. E. Oesterley

14 COMMENTARY: The Lord has cast down the thrones of proud princes and set up the meek in their stead. God reverses thrones: proud princes fall and the meek are raised in their place. — W. O. E. Oesterley

15 COMMENTARY: The Lord has plucked up the roots of proud nations and planted the lowly in their place. Proud nations are uprooted and the lowly planted where they stood. — W. O. E. Oesterley

16 COMMENTARY: The Lord overthrew countries of the heathen and destroyed them to the foundations of the earth. Heathen countries are overthrown to their very foundations by the Lord. — W. O. E. Oesterley

17 COMMENTARY: He took some of them away and destroyed them, and made their memorial cease from the earth. Some nations are removed and destroyed until even their memory ceases. — W. O. E. Oesterley

18 COMMENTARY: Pride was not made for men, nor furious anger for those born of woman. Pride and furious anger do not belong to human nature as God made it. — W. O. E. Oesterley

19 COMMENTARY: Those who fear the Lord are a sure seed, and those who love him an honourable plant; those who regard not the law are a dishonourable seed, and those who transgress the commandments are a deceivable seed. Fearers of God are sure and honourable seed, while law-despisers and commandment-breakers are dishonourable and deceived seed. — W. O. E. Oesterley

20 COMMENTARY: Among brethren he who is chief is honourable, so are they who fear the Lord in his eyes. As a chief brother is honoured, so the God-fearing are honoured in God's eyes. — W. O. E. Oesterley

21 COMMENTARY: The fear of the Lord goes before the obtaining of authority, but roughness and pride are the losing of it. Reverence precedes authority, while roughness and pride lose it. — W. O. E. Oesterley

22 COMMENTARY: Whether rich, noble, or poor, their glory is the fear of the Lord. Rank does not divide glory: for rich, noble, and poor alike, glory is the fear of the Lord. — W. O. E. Oesterley

23 COMMENTARY: It is not meet to despise the poor man who has understanding, nor is it convenient to magnify a sinful man. An understanding poor man must not be despised, and a sinful man must not be magnified, whatever his station. — W. O. E. Oesterley

24 COMMENTARY: Great men, judges, and potentates will be honoured, yet none of them is greater than he who fears the Lord. High offices deserve honour, but none of their holders outranks the God-fearing man. — W. O. E. Oesterley

25 COMMENTARY: Unto the wise servant those who are free will do service, and he who has knowledge will not grudge when he is reformed. Wisdom reverses service: freemen serve the wise servant, and knowledge accepts correction without grudging. — W. O. E. Oesterley

26 COMMENTARY: Do not be overwise in doing your business, and do not boast in the time of your distress. Over-subtlety in business and boasting in distress are both rebuked. — W. O. E. Oesterley

27 COMMENTARY: Better is he who labours and abounds in all things than he who boasts himself and wants bread. A labourer who has plenty is better than a boaster who lacks bread. — W. O. E. Oesterley

28 COMMENTARY: Glorify your soul in meekness, and give it honour according to its dignity. The soul should be honoured in meekness, with the dignity that belongs to it. — W. O. E. Oesterley

29 COMMENTARY: Who will justify him who sins against his own soul, and who will honour him who dishonours his own life? Sin against one's own soul admits no justification, and a life dishonoured by its owner earns no honour. — W. O. E. Oesterley

30 COMMENTARY: The poor man is honoured for his skill, and the rich man is honoured for his riches. Honour follows skill in the poor and riches in the rich; each is honoured for what he has. — W. O. E. Oesterley

31 COMMENTARY: He who is honoured in poverty, how much more in riches? And he who is dishonourable in riches, how much more in poverty? Honour proved in poverty will grow in riches, and dishonour shown in riches will be worse in poverty. — W. O. E. Oesterley
