Study Notes — Sirach

Chapter 18

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: He who lives for ever has created all things in general. The eternal One is the creator of all things without exception. — W. O. E. Oesterley

2 COMMENTARY: The Lord only is righteous, and there is none other but he. Righteousness belongs to the Lord alone; no other shares it. — W. O. E. Oesterley

3 COMMENTARY: He governs the world with the palm of his hand, and all things obey his will, for he is the King of all, by his power dividing holy things from profane. The world is governed as if held in his palm, all obeying his will, and his power divides holy from profane. — W. O. E. Oesterley

4 COMMENTARY: To whom has he given power to declare his works, and who will find out his noble acts? No one has been given power to declare God's works fully, nor to find out all his noble acts. — W. O. E. Oesterley

5 COMMENTARY: Who will number the strength of his majesty, and who will also tell out his mercies? Majesty's strength cannot be numbered, nor mercies told out to their end. — W. O. E. Oesterley

6 COMMENTARY: As for the wondrous works of the Lord, nothing may be taken from them, nor anything put to them, neither can the ground of them be found out. God's wondrous works admit neither subtraction nor addition, and their ground cannot be discovered. — W. O. E. Oesterley

7 COMMENTARY: When a man has done, then he begins; and when he leaves off, then he will be doubtful. Human finishing is really beginning, and human ceasing ends in doubt. — W. O. E. Oesterley

8 COMMENTARY: What is man, and wherefore does he serve? What is his good, and what is his evil? Man's nature, purpose, good, and evil are all questions that humble him. — W. O. E. Oesterley

9 COMMENTARY: The number of a man's days at the most are a hundred years. A man's days reach at most a hundred years. — W. O. E. Oesterley

10 COMMENTARY: As a drop of water to the sea, and a gravelstone in comparison of the sand, so are a thousand years to the days of eternity. A thousand years beside eternity are as a drop beside the sea or a pebble beside sand. — W. O. E. Oesterley

11 COMMENTARY: Therefore God is patient with them, and pours forth his mercy upon them. Because man's days are so brief, God is patient and pours out mercy. — W. O. E. Oesterley

12 COMMENTARY: He saw and perceived their end to be evil, therefore he multiplied his compassion. Seeing man's end to be evil, God multiplied compassion rather than withdrawing it. — W. O. E. Oesterley

13 COMMENTARY: The mercy of man is toward his neighbour, but the mercy of the Lord is upon all flesh; he reproves, nurtures, teaches, and brings again, as a shepherd his flock. Human mercy reaches a neighbour; God's mercy reaches all flesh, reproving, nurturing, teaching, and restoring like a shepherd. — W. O. E. Oesterley

14 COMMENTARY: He has mercy on those who receive discipline, and who diligently seek after his judgments. Mercy is shown to those who receive discipline and diligently seek God's judgments. — W. O. E. Oesterley

15 COMMENTARY: My son, do not blemish your good deeds, nor use uncomfortable words when you give anything. Good deeds should not be spoiled, nor gifts accompanied by wounding words. — W. O. E. Oesterley

16 COMMENTARY: Will not the dew assuage the heat? So is a word better than a gift. As dew eases heat, so a kind word is better than a gift. — W. O. E. Oesterley

17 COMMENTARY: Lo, is not a word better than a gift? But both are with a gracious man. A word is indeed better than a gift, yet the gracious man gives both. — W. O. E. Oesterley

18 COMMENTARY: A fool will upbraid churlishly, and a gift of the envious consumes the eyes. A fool's giving comes with churlish upbraiding, and an envious gift pains the eyes of the receiver. — W. O. E. Oesterley

19 COMMENTARY: Learn before you speak, and use physic before you are sick. Learning should precede speech, as medicine precedes sickness: preparation comes first. — W. O. E. Oesterley

20 COMMENTARY: Before judgment examine yourself, and in the day of visitation you will find mercy. Self-examination before judgment prepares a person to find mercy when visited. — W. O. E. Oesterley

21 COMMENTARY: Humble yourself before you are sick, and in the time of sins show repentance. Humility before sickness and repentance in the time of sinning are both timely. — W. O. E. Oesterley

22 COMMENTARY: Let nothing hinder you to pay your vow in due time, and do not defer until death to be justified. Vows must be paid on time, and justification must not be deferred until death. — W. O. E. Oesterley

23 COMMENTARY: Before you pray, prepare yourself, and do not be as one who tempts the Lord. Prayer requires preparation; unprepared prayer is like tempting God. — W. O. E. Oesterley

24 COMMENTARY: Think upon the wrath that will be at the end, and the time of vengeance, when he will turn away his face. The end's wrath and vengeance, when God turns away his face, should be kept in mind. — W. O. E. Oesterley

25 COMMENTARY: When you have enough, remember the time of hunger; and when you are rich, think upon poverty and need. Plenty should remember hunger, and riches should remember poverty and need. — W. O. E. Oesterley

26 COMMENTARY: From the morning until the evening the time is changed, and all things are soon done before the Lord. Time changes from morning to evening, and all things are swiftly accomplished before God. — W. O. E. Oesterley

27 COMMENTARY: A wise man will fear in everything, and in the day of sinning he will beware of offence; but a fool will not observe time. The wise fear in everything and watch against offence, while the fool ignores the times. — W. O. E. Oesterley

28 COMMENTARY: Every man of understanding knows wisdom, and will give praise to him who found her. The understanding man knows wisdom and praises the one who found her. — W. O. E. Oesterley

29 COMMENTARY: Those who were of understanding in sayings became also wise themselves, and poured forth exquisite parables. Understanding of sayings ripens into wisdom itself, which then pours out fine parables. — W. O. E. Oesterley

30 COMMENTARY: Do not go after your lusts, but refrain yourself from your appetites. Lusts must not be followed, and appetites must be restrained. — W. O. E. Oesterley

31 COMMENTARY: If you give your soul the desires that please her, she will make you a laughingstock to your enemies who malign you. Giving the soul its pleasing desires ends in becoming a laughingstock to maligning enemies. — W. O. E. Oesterley

32 COMMENTARY: Do not take pleasure in much good cheer, nor be tied to the expense thereof. Much feasting should not be delighted in, nor its expense become a bond. — W. O. E. Oesterley

33 COMMENTARY: Do not be made a beggar by banqueting upon borrowing, when you have nothing in your purse, for you will lie in wait for your own life and be talked about. Banqueting on borrowed money when the purse is empty makes a beggar, endangers life, and becomes public talk. — W. O. E. Oesterley
