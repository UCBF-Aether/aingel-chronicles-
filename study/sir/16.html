Study Notes — Sirach

Chapter 16

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Do not desire a multitude of unprofitable children, nor delight in ungodly sons. Many children are no blessing if they are unprofitable, and ungodly sons are no delight. — W. O. E. Oesterley

2 COMMENTARY: Though they multiply, do not rejoice in them, except the fear of the Lord be with them. Numbers alone are no cause for rejoicing; only the fear of the Lord in them makes multiplication good. — W. O. E. Oesterley

3 COMMENTARY: Do not trust in their life, nor respect their multitude, for one just man is better than a thousand, and it is better to die without children than to have ungodly ones. One just person outweighs a thousand, and childlessness is better than ungodly children. — W. O. E. Oesterley

4 COMMENTARY: By one man of understanding a city will be replenished, but the kindred of the wicked will speedily become desolate. A single understanding man can fill a city, while a wicked kindred quickly empties into desolation. — W. O. E. Oesterley

5 COMMENTARY: Many such things have I seen with my eyes, and my ear has heard greater things than these. The writer appeals to seen and heard experience, greater even than the examples given. — W. O. E. Oesterley

6 COMMENTARY: In the congregation of the ungodly a fire will be kindled, and in a rebellious nation wrath is set on fire. Ungodly assemblies kindle fire, and rebellious nations set wrath ablaze. — W. O. E. Oesterley

7 COMMENTARY: He was not pacified toward the old giants, who fell away in the strength of their foolishness. God was not appeased toward the ancient giants who fell in the strength of their folly. — W. O. E. Oesterley

8 COMMENTARY: Neither did he spare the place where Lot sojourned, but abhorred them for their pride. Sodom, where Lot lived, was not spared, because God abhorred its pride. — W. O. E. Oesterley

9 COMMENTARY: He pitied not the people of perdition, who were taken away in their sins. The people of perdition, carried off in their sins, received no pity. — W. O. E. Oesterley

10 COMMENTARY: Nor the six hundred thousand footmen, gathered together in the hardness of their hearts. Even the six hundred thousand of the wilderness generation, hardened in heart, were not spared. — W. O. E. Oesterley

11 COMMENTARY: If there is one stiffnecked among the people, it is a marvel if he escapes unpunished, for mercy and wrath are with him; he is mighty to forgive and to pour out displeasure. One stiffnecked person can hardly escape, because God holds both mercy and wrath and is mighty in each. — W. O. E. Oesterley

12 COMMENTARY: As his mercy is great, so is his correction also; he judges a man according to his works. God's correction matches his mercy in greatness, and judgment follows works. — W. O. E. Oesterley

13 COMMENTARY: The sinner will not escape with his spoils, and the patience of the godly will not be frustrated. Sinners do not get away with their plunder, and godly patience is not disappointed. — W. O. E. Oesterley

14 COMMENTARY: Make way for every work of mercy, for every man will find according to his works. Mercy should be given room in every work, since each man finds according to his own works. — W. O. E. Oesterley

15 COMMENTARY: The Lord hardened Pharaoh, that he should not know him, so that his powerful works might be known to the world. Pharaoh's hardening served to make God's powerful works known to the world. — W. O. E. Oesterley

16 COMMENTARY: His mercy is manifest to every creature, and he has separated his light from the darkness with an adamant. God's mercy shows to every creature, and his light is divided from darkness as firmly as adamant divides. — W. O. E. Oesterley

17 COMMENTARY: Do not say, I will hide myself from the Lord; shall any remember me from above? I shall not be remembered among so many people, for what is my soul among such an infinite number of creatures? Hiding from God is impossible, and smallness among countless creatures is no excuse for thinking oneself unremembered. — W. O. E. Oesterley

18 COMMENTARY: Behold, the heaven and the heaven of heavens, the deep, the earth, and all therein will be moved when he visits. At God's visitation heaven, deep, earth, and all within them are shaken. — W. O. E. Oesterley

19 COMMENTARY: The mountains also and the foundations of the earth are shaken with trembling when the Lord looks upon them. Even mountains and earth's foundations tremble under God's look. — W. O. E. Oesterley

20 COMMENTARY: No heart can think upon these things worthily, and who is able to conceive his ways? The heart cannot worthily conceive these things, and no one can fully grasp God's ways. — W. O. E. Oesterley

21 COMMENTARY: It is a tempest which no man can see, for the most part of his works are hid. God's working is like an unseen tempest, since most of his works are hidden. — W. O. E. Oesterley

22 COMMENTARY: Who can declare the works of his justice, or who can endure them? For his covenant is afar off, and the trial of all things is in the end. God's just works cannot be fully declared or endured, because his covenant stretches far and the trial of all things comes at the end. — W. O. E. Oesterley

23 COMMENTARY: He who wants understanding will think upon vain things, and a foolish man erring imagines follies. Lack of understanding turns thought to vanity, and a fool's errors breed imagined follies. — W. O. E. Oesterley

24 COMMENTARY: My son, hearken to me, learn knowledge, and mark my words with your heart. The call is to listen, learn knowledge, and mark the words in the heart. — W. O. E. Oesterley

25 COMMENTARY: I will show forth doctrine in weight, and declare his knowledge exactly. Doctrine will be set out with weight and exactness, not lightly or loosely. — W. O. E. Oesterley

26 COMMENTARY: The works of the Lord are done in judgment from the beginning, and from the time he made them he disposed the parts thereof. God's works have been ordered in judgment from the beginning, each part disposed by him from creation. — W. O. E. Oesterley

27 COMMENTARY: He garnished his works for ever, and in his hand are the chief of them unto all generations; they neither labour, nor are weary, nor cease from their works. God's adorned works endure for ever in his hand across generations, never labouring, wearying, or ceasing. — W. O. E. Oesterley

28 COMMENTARY: None of them hinders another, and they will never disobey his word. Created works do not hinder each other and never disobey God's word. — W. O. E. Oesterley

29 COMMENTARY: After this the Lord looked upon the earth and filled it with his blessings. God's look upon the earth filled it with his blessings. — W. O. E. Oesterley

30 COMMENTARY: With all manner of living things he has covered the face thereof, and they will return into it again. The earth is covered with living things, and they return into it again, dust to dust. — W. O. E. Oesterley
