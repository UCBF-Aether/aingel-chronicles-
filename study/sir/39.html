Study Notes — Sirach

Chapter 39

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: But he who gives his mind to the law of the Most High, and is occupied in meditation thereof, will seek out the wisdom of all the ancient, and be occupied in prophecies. The law-meditator seeks ancient wisdom and occupies himself in prophecies. — W. O. E. Oesterley

2 COMMENTARY: He will keep the sayings of renowned men, and where subtle parables are, he will be there also. He keeps renowned men's sayings and is present wherever subtle parables are. — W. O. E. Oesterley

3 COMMENTARY: He will seek out the secrets of grave sentences, and be conversant in dark parables. He seeks grave sentences' secrets and converses in dark parables. — W. O. E. Oesterley

4 COMMENTARY: He will serve among great men, and appear before princes; he will travel through strange countries, for he has tried the good and evil among men. The learned man serves great men, appears before princes, and travels strange countries, having tried human good and evil. — W. O. E. Oesterley

5 COMMENTARY: He will give his heart to resort early to the Lord who made him, and will pray before the Most High, opening his mouth in prayer and making supplication for his sins. Early resort to the Maker, prayer before the Most High, and supplication for sins mark the learned man's heart. — W. O. E. Oesterley

6 COMMENTARY: When the great Lord wills, he will be filled with the spirit of understanding; he will pour out wise sentences, and give thanks to the Lord in his prayer. If God wills, the learned man is filled with understanding's spirit, pouring out wise sentences and thanksgiving in prayer. — W. O. E. Oesterley

7 COMMENTARY: He will direct his counsel and knowledge, and in his secrets he will meditate. He directs counsel and knowledge, meditating in secret things. — W. O. E. Oesterley

8 COMMENTARY: He will show forth what he has learned, and will glory in the law of the covenant of the Lord. What he has learned he shows forth, glorying in the covenant law. — W. O. E. Oesterley

9 COMMENTARY: Many will commend his understanding, and so long as the world endures it will not be blotted out; his memorial will not depart, and his name will live from generation to generation. Many commend his understanding, and his memorial and name endure from generation to generation. — W. O. E. Oesterley

10 COMMENTARY: Nations will show forth his wisdom, and the congregation will declare his praise. Nations publish his wisdom, and the congregation declares his praise. — W. O. E. Oesterley

11 COMMENTARY: If he dies, he will leave a greater name than a thousand, and if he lives, he will increase it. Death leaves him a name greater than a thousand, and life increases it further. — W. O. E. Oesterley

12 COMMENTARY: Yet I have more to say, which I have thought upon, for I am filled as the moon at the full. The writer, filled like the full moon, has more to say from his thoughts. — W. O. E. Oesterley

13 COMMENTARY: Hearken to me, you holy children, and bud forth as a rose growing by the brook of the field. Holy children are called to listen and bud like a rose by the field brook. — W. O. E. Oesterley

14 COMMENTARY: Give a sweet savour as frankincense, flourish as a lily, send forth a smell, sing a song of praise, and bless the Lord in all his works. Like frankincense and lily, the holy should give sweet savour, flourish, and sing praise, blessing God in all his works. — W. O. E. Oesterley

15 COMMENTARY: Magnify his name, and show forth his praise with the songs of your lips, and with harps, and in praising him you will say after this manner. God's name should be magnified with lip-songs and harps, in the praise that follows. — W. O. E. Oesterley

16 COMMENTARY: All the works of the Lord are exceeding good, and whatever he commands will be accomplished in due season. All God's works are exceeding good, and his commands are accomplished in due season. — W. O. E. Oesterley

17 COMMENTARY: None may say, what is this? Wherefore is that? For at time convenient they will all be sought out; at his commandment the waters stood as a heap, and at the words of his mouth the receptacles of waters. No one may question God's works with what or why, since all will be sought out in time, as waters stood heaped at his command. — W. O. E. Oesterley

18 COMMENTARY: At his commandment is done whatever pleases him, and none can hinder when he will save. Whatever pleases God is done at his command, and none can hinder his saving. — W. O. E. Oesterley

19 COMMENTARY: The works of all flesh are before him, and nothing can be hid from his eyes. All flesh's works lie before God, and nothing hides from his eyes. — W. O. E. Oesterley

20 COMMENTARY: He sees from everlasting to everlasting, and there is nothing wonderful before him. God sees from eternity to eternity, and nothing is wonderful, that is, too hard, before him. — W. O. E. Oesterley

21 COMMENTARY: A man need not say, what is this? Wherefore is that? For he has made all things for their uses. Questioning what or why is needless, since God made all things for their uses. — W. O. E. Oesterley

22 COMMENTARY: His blessing covered the dry land as a river, and watered it as a flood. God's blessing covers dry land like a river and waters it like a flood. — W. O. E. Oesterley

23 COMMENTARY: As he has turned the waters into saltness, so will the heathen inherit his wrath. As waters were turned salty, so heathen inherit God's wrath. — W. O. E. Oesterley

24 COMMENTARY: As his ways are plain to the holy, so are they stumbling blocks to the wicked. God's ways are plain to the holy and stumbling blocks to the wicked. — W. O. E. Oesterley

25 COMMENTARY: For the good are good things created from the beginning, so evil things for sinners. Good things were created from the beginning for the good, and evil things for sinners. — W. O. E. Oesterley

26 COMMENTARY: The principal things for the whole use of man's life are water, fire, iron, and salt, flour of wheat, honey, milk, and the blood of the grape, and oil, and clothing. Life's principal necessities are listed: water, fire, iron, salt, wheat flour, honey, milk, grape's blood, oil, and clothing. — W. O. E. Oesterley

27 COMMENTARY: All these things are for good to the godly, so to sinners they are turned into evil. The same necessities are good to the godly and turned to evil for sinners. — W. O. E. Oesterley

28 COMMENTARY: There are spirits created for vengeance, which in their fury lay on sore strokes; in the time of destruction they pour out their force, and appease the wrath of him who made them. Vengeance spirits, created for wrath, lay on sore strokes in destruction's time and appease their Maker's wrath. — W. O. E. Oesterley

29 COMMENTARY: Fire, hail, famine, and death, all these were created for vengeance. Fire, hail, famine, and death were all created as instruments of vengeance. — W. O. E. Oesterley

30 COMMENTARY: Teeth of wild beasts, scorpions, serpents, and the sword punish the wicked to destruction. Wild beasts' teeth, scorpions, serpents, and sword punish the wicked to destruction. — W. O. E. Oesterley

31 COMMENTARY: They will rejoice in his commandment, and be ready upon earth when need is, and when their time comes, they will not transgress his word. Vengeance instruments rejoice in God's command, stand ready on earth, and never transgress his word when their time comes. — W. O. E. Oesterley

32 COMMENTARY: Therefore from the beginning I was resolved, and thought upon these things, and have left them in writing. From the beginning the writer resolved and thought on these things, and left them in writing. — W. O. E. Oesterley

33 COMMENTARY: All the works of the Lord are good, and he will give every needful thing in due season. All God's works are good, and every needful thing is given in due season. — W. O. E. Oesterley

34 COMMENTARY: So a man cannot say, this is worse than that, for in time they will all be well approved. No work can be called worse than another, since in time all will be well approved. — W. O. E. Oesterley

35 COMMENTARY: Therefore praise the Lord with the whole heart and mouth, and bless the name of the Lord. The conclusion is whole-hearted, whole-mouthed praise and blessing of God's name. — W. O. E. Oesterley
