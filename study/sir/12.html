Study Notes — Sirach

Chapter 12

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: When you will do good, know to whom you do it, and you will be thanked for your benefits. Good deeds should be directed with knowledge of the recipient, and thanks follow well-placed benefits. — W. O. E. Oesterley

2 COMMENTARY: Do good to the godly man, and you will find a recompense; and if not from him, yet from the Most High. Good done to the godly is repaid, by the man if possible, and otherwise by God himself. — W. O. E. Oesterley

3 COMMENTARY: No good can come to him who is always occupied in evil, nor to him who gives no alms. A life occupied in evil and a life without almsgiving are both barren of good. — W. O. E. Oesterley

4 COMMENTARY: Give to the godly man, and do not help a sinner. Giving should be directed to the godly, not to strengthening a sinner. — W. O. E. Oesterley

5 COMMENTARY: Do well to him who is lowly, but do not give to the ungodly; hold back your bread and do not give it to him, lest he overmaster you thereby, for else you will receive twice as much evil for all the good you have done him. Help for the lowly is urged, but giving to the ungodly arms him against you, returning double evil for good. — W. O. E. Oesterley

6 COMMENTARY: The Most High hates sinners, and will repay vengeance to the ungodly, keeping them against the mighty day of their punishment. God's hatred of sinners is not forgetfulness; vengeance is kept for the great day of punishment. — W. O. E. Oesterley

7 COMMENTARY: Give to the good, and do not help the sinner. The rule is repeated plainly: give to the good, not to the sinner. — W. O. E. Oesterley

8 COMMENTARY: A friend cannot be known in prosperity, and an enemy cannot be hidden in adversity. Prosperity hides the difference between friend and flatterer, while adversity exposes enemies. — W. O. E. Oesterley

9 COMMENTARY: In a man's prosperity enemies will be grieved, but in his adversity even a friend will depart. Prosperity grieves enemies, yet adversity can scatter even friends. — W. O. E. Oesterley

10 COMMENTARY: Never trust your enemy, for as iron rusts, so does his wickedness. An enemy's wickedness is like rust in iron: constant, corrosive, and never to be trusted. — W. O. E. Oesterley

11 COMMENTARY: Though he humble himself and go crouching, take good heed and beware of him, and you will be to him as one who has wiped a looking-glass, and you will know his rust has not been altogether wiped away. A crouching, humbled enemy still carries rust beneath the polish, and the wise man sees through the wiped surface. — W. O. E. Oesterley

12 COMMENTARY: Do not set him by you, lest when he has overthrown you he stand up in your place; nor let him sit at your right hand, lest he seek to take your seat, and you at last remember these words and be pricked with them. Giving an enemy a place beside you or at your right hand invites him to seize your seat, leaving only regret for ignored warning. — W. O. E. Oesterley

13 COMMENTARY: Who will pity a charmer bitten by a serpent, or any such as come near wild beasts? One who handles serpents and wild beasts and is bitten has invited the danger and earns little pity. — W. O. E. Oesterley

14 COMMENTARY: So one who goes to a sinner and is defiled with him in his sins, who will pity? Going to a sinner and sharing his defilement is like handling serpents: pity is not owed to a chosen danger. — W. O. E. Oesterley

15 COMMENTARY: For a while he will abide with you, but if you begin to fall, he will not tarry. The sinner's companion stays only while things stand; at the first fall he leaves. — W. O. E. Oesterley

16 COMMENTARY: An enemy speaks sweetly with his lips, but in his heart imagines how to throw you into a pit; he will weep with his eyes, but if he finds opportunity he will not be satisfied with blood. Sweet lips and weeping eyes mask a heart plotting a pit, and given opportunity the enemy wants blood, not tears. — W. O. E. Oesterley

17 COMMENTARY: If adversity comes upon you, you will find him there first, and though he pretends to help you, yet he will undermine you. In adversity the enemy arrives first, and his pretended help is really undermining. — W. O. E. Oesterley

18 COMMENTARY: He will shake his head, clap his hands, whisper much, and change his countenance. The enemy's gloating shows in shaking head, clapping hands, whispering, and a changing face. — W. O. E. Oesterley
