Study Notes — Sirach

Chapter 14

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Blessed is the man who has not slipped with his mouth and is not pricked with the multitude of sins. Guarded speech is blessedness, because the slip of the mouth pricks a man with many sins. — W. O. E. Oesterley

2 COMMENTARY: Blessed is he whose conscience has not condemned him, and who has not fallen from his hope in the Lord. A clear conscience and unfallen hope in the Lord are blessed together. — W. O. E. Oesterley

3 COMMENTARY: Riches are not comely for a niggard, and what should an envious man do with money? Wealth sits ill on a miser, and money in an envious man's hands serves no good purpose. — W. O. E. Oesterley

4 COMMENTARY: He who gathers by defrauding his own soul gathers for others, who will spend his goods riotously. Wealth gathered at the soul's cost is gathered for others, who will waste it in riot. — W. O. E. Oesterley

5 COMMENTARY: He who is evil to himself, to whom will he be good? He will not take pleasure in his goods. A man who is evil even to himself cannot be good to anyone, and he takes no pleasure in his own goods. — W. O. E. Oesterley

6 COMMENTARY: There is none worse than he who envies himself, and this is a recompense of his wickedness. Self-envy is the worst condition, and it is itself the repayment of wickedness. — W. O. E. Oesterley

7 COMMENTARY: If he does good, he does it unwillingly, and at last he will declare his wickedness. Even the miser's good deeds are unwilling, and in the end his wickedness shows itself. — W. O. E. Oesterley

8 COMMENTARY: The envious man has a wicked eye; he turns away his face and despises men. Envy shows as a wicked eye, a turned face, and contempt for men. — W. O. E. Oesterley

9 COMMENTARY: A covetous man's eye is not satisfied with his portion, and the iniquity of the wicked dries up his soul. Covetousness is never satisfied with its portion, and wicked iniquity withers the soul. — W. O. E. Oesterley

10 COMMENTARY: A wicked eye envies his bread, and he is a niggard at his table. The wicked eye grudges even bread, and the covetous man is stingy at his own table. — W. O. E. Oesterley

11 COMMENTARY: According to your ability do good to yourself, and give the Lord his due offering. Ability sets the measure: do good to yourself within it, and give God his due offering. — W. O. E. Oesterley

12 COMMENTARY: Remember that death will not be long in coming, and that the covenant of the grave is not shown to you. Death comes soon and its terms are not shown in advance, so delay is folly. — W. O. E. Oesterley

13 COMMENTARY: Do good to your friend before you die, and according to your ability stretch out your hand and give to him. Good to a friend must be done before death, in proportion to ability, while the hand can still give. — W. O. E. Oesterley

14 COMMENTARY: Do not defraud yourself of the good day, and do not let the part of a good desire pass you by. The good day is not to be denied to oneself, nor a good desire allowed to slip past unused. — W. O. E. Oesterley

15 COMMENTARY: Will you not leave your travails to another, and your labours to be divided by lot? All your toils will be left to another and divided by lot, so hoarding them for yourself is vain. — W. O. E. Oesterley

16 COMMENTARY: Give and take, and sanctify your soul, for there is no seeking of dainties in the grave. Giving and receiving in this life sanctify the soul, because the grave offers no dainties to seek. — W. O. E. Oesterley

17 COMMENTARY: All flesh waxes old as a garment, for the covenant from the beginning is, you shall die the death. All flesh ages like a garment, under the original covenant of death. — W. O. E. Oesterley

18 COMMENTARY: As of the green leaves on a thick tree, some fall and some grow, so is the generation of flesh and blood: one comes to an end and another is born. Generations replace each other like leaves on a thick tree, some falling while others grow. — W. O. E. Oesterley

19 COMMENTARY: Every work rots and consumes away, and the worker thereof will go with it. Works decay, and the worker goes with them; nothing made by hands endures apart from its maker. — W. O. E. Oesterley

20 COMMENTARY: Blessed is the man who meditates good things in wisdom and reasons of holy things by his understanding. Meditation on good things in wisdom and reasoning on holy things bring blessedness. — W. O. E. Oesterley

21 COMMENTARY: He who considers her ways in his heart will also have understanding in her secrets. Considering wisdom's ways inwardly leads to understanding her secrets. — W. O. E. Oesterley

22 COMMENTARY: Go after her as one who traces, and lie in wait in her ways. Wisdom must be pursued like a tracker and waited for on her paths. — W. O. E. Oesterley

23 COMMENTARY: He who pries in at her windows will also hearken at her doors. Prying at wisdom's windows and listening at her doors picture eager, close attention to her house. — W. O. E. Oesterley

24 COMMENTARY: He who lodges near her house will also fasten a pin in her walls. Lodging near wisdom's house and fastening a pin in its walls picture settling down beside her for good. — W. O. E. Oesterley

25 COMMENTARY: He will pitch his tent near to her, and will lodge in a lodging where good things are. Camping beside wisdom means lodging where good things are found. — W. O. E. Oesterley

26 COMMENTARY: He will set his children under her shelter, and will lodge under her branches. Wisdom's shelter covers children too, and the wise man lives under her branches. — W. O. E. Oesterley

27 COMMENTARY: By her he will be covered from heat, and in her glory he will dwell. Wisdom shades from heat and her glory becomes the wise man's dwelling. — W. O. E. Oesterley
