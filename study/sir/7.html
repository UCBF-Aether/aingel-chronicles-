Study Notes — Sirach

Chapter 7

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Do no evil, and no harm will come to you. Refraining from evil is presented as the plain way to escape harm's return. — W. O. E. Oesterley

2 COMMENTARY: Depart from the unjust, and iniquity will turn away from you. Leaving unjust company is how iniquity itself is left behind. — W. O. E. Oesterley

3 COMMENTARY: Do not sow upon the furrows of unrighteousness, and you will not reap them sevenfold. Sowing in unrighteous ground yields a multiplied harvest of trouble. — W. O. E. Oesterley

4 COMMENTARY: Seek neither preeminence from the Lord nor the seat of honour from the king. Ambition for rank, whether from God or king, is not to be sought. — W. O. E. Oesterley

5 COMMENTARY: Do not justify yourself before the Lord, and do not boast of your wisdom before the king. Self-justification before God and boasting of wisdom before a king are both out of place. — W. O. E. Oesterley

6 COMMENTARY: Do not seek to be judge if you cannot take away iniquity, lest you fear the person of the mighty and stumble in the way of your uprightness. Judging without power to remove wrong invites fear of the mighty, which becomes a stumbling block to uprightness. — W. O. E. Oesterley

7 COMMENTARY: Do not offend against the multitude of a city, and then you will not cast yourself down among the people. Offending the whole city degrades a person in the people's eyes. — W. O. E. Oesterley

8 COMMENTARY: Do not bind one sin upon another, for in one you will not be unpunished. Piling sin on sin does not dilute guilt; even one sin does not go unpunished. — W. O. E. Oesterley

9 COMMENTARY: Do not say God will look upon the multitude of your oblations and accept them when offered to the Most High. Many offerings do not buy acceptance with God when the life behind them is wrong. — W. O. E. Oesterley

10 COMMENTARY: Do not be fainthearted when you make your prayer, and do not neglect to give alms. Prayer should be bold, not fainthearted, and it belongs together with almsgiving. — W. O. E. Oesterley

11 COMMENTARY: Do not laugh any man to scorn in the bitterness of his soul, for there is one who humbles and exalts. Mocking a bitter soul forgets that God both humbles and exalts, and fortunes can reverse. — W. O. E. Oesterley

12 COMMENTARY: Do not devise a lie against your brother, nor do the like to your friend. Lying devised against brother or friend is doubly condemned, since both bonds should forbid it. — W. O. E. Oesterley

13 COMMENTARY: Do not make any manner of lie, for the custom of it is not good. Lying must not become a habit, for the custom itself corrupts. — W. O. E. Oesterley

14 COMMENTARY: Do not use many words in a multitude of elders, and do not make much babbling when you pray. Brevity is fitting before elders, and prayer is not improved by babbling. — W. O. E. Oesterley

15 COMMENTARY: Do not hate laborious work, nor husbandry, which the Most High has ordained. Hard work and farming are God's own ordinances, not things beneath a wise man. — W. O. E. Oesterley

16 COMMENTARY: Do not number yourself among the multitude of sinners, but remember that wrath will not tarry long. Joining the crowd of sinners is no safety, for wrath does not delay long. — W. O. E. Oesterley

17 COMMENTARY: Humble yourself greatly, for the vengeance of the ungodly is fire and worms. Deep humility is urged because the end of the ungodly is corruption, pictured as fire and worms. — W. O. E. Oesterley

18 COMMENTARY: Do not change a friend for any good by no means, nor a faithful brother for the gold of Ophir. No material gain, not even Ophir's gold, is worth trading a friend or faithful brother. — W. O. E. Oesterley

19 COMMENTARY: Do not forego a wise and good woman, for her grace is above gold. A wise, good wife is not to be given up, since her grace outvalues gold. — W. O. E. Oesterley

20 COMMENTARY: Whereas your servant works truly, do not entreat him evil, nor the hireling who bestows himself wholly for you. Faithful service deserves fair treatment, whether from a servant or a wholly devoted hireling. — W. O. E. Oesterley

21 COMMENTARY: Let your soul love a good servant, and do not defraud him of liberty. A good servant should be loved, not cheated of the liberty that is his due. — W. O. E. Oesterley

22 COMMENTARY: If you have cattle, have an eye to them, and if they are for your profit, keep them with you. Livestock require oversight, and profitable cattle are worth keeping close. — W. O. E. Oesterley

23 COMMENTARY: If you have children, instruct them, and bow down their neck from their youth. Children need instruction and early discipline, while the neck is still young enough to bow. — W. O. E. Oesterley

24 COMMENTARY: If you have daughters, have a care of their body, and do not show yourself cheerful toward them. Daughters require careful guarding, and a father should not be lax or familiar in a way that loosens discipline. — W. O. E. Oesterley

25 COMMENTARY: Marry your daughter, and you will have performed a weighty matter, but give her to a man of understanding. Marrying a daughter well is a weighty duty, and understanding in the husband matters most. — W. O. E. Oesterley

26 COMMENTARY: If you have a wife after your mind, do not forsake her, but do not give yourself over to a light woman. A fitting wife should be kept, and a light woman must not be allowed to take possession of a man. — W. O. E. Oesterley

27 COMMENTARY: Honour your father with your whole heart, and do not forget the sorrows of your mother. Honour to a father must be wholehearted, and a mother's sorrows must be remembered. — W. O. E. Oesterley

28 COMMENTARY: Remember that you were begotten of them, and how can you recompense what they have done for you? Your very existence came from your parents, and no repayment can match what they did. — W. O. E. Oesterley

29 COMMENTARY: Fear the Lord with all your soul, and reverence his priests. Whole-souled fear of God belongs with reverence for those who serve him as priests. — W. O. E. Oesterley

30 COMMENTARY: Love him who made you with all your strength, and do not forsake his ministers. Love for the Maker, with all one's strength, includes not abandoning his ministers. — W. O. E. Oesterley

31 COMMENTARY: Fear the Lord, honour the priest, and give him his portion as commanded: the firstfruits, the trespass offering, the gift of the shoulders, the sacrifice of sanctification, and the firstfruits of the holy things. The priest's due is itemised because honour must be paid in the commanded portions, not in vague respect. — W. O. E. Oesterley

32 COMMENTARY: Stretch your hand to the poor, so that your blessing may be perfected. Giving to the poor completes a blessing; without it the blessing is unfinished. — W. O. E. Oesterley

33 COMMENTARY: A gift has grace in the sight of every man living, and for the dead do not detain it. A gift wins favour among the living, and what is owed for the dead should not be held back. — W. O. E. Oesterley

34 COMMENTARY: Do not fail to be with those who weep, and mourn with those who mourn. Presence with the weeping and shared mourning are duties, not options. — W. O. E. Oesterley

35 COMMENTARY: Do not be slow to visit the sick, for that will make you beloved. Prompt visits to the sick win love. — W. O. E. Oesterley

36 COMMENTARY: Whatever you take in hand, remember the end, and you will never do amiss. Keeping the end in view governs every undertaking and keeps it from going wrong. — W. O. E. Oesterley
