Study Notes — Sirach

Chapter 28

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: He who takes revenge will find vengeance from the Lord, and he will surely keep his sins in remembrance. Revenge-taking invites God's own vengeance, and his sins are kept in remembrance against him. — W. O. E. Oesterley

2 COMMENTARY: Forgive your neighbour the hurt he has done you, and your sins also will be forgiven when you pray. Forgiving a neighbour's hurt is the condition of one's own sins being forgiven in prayer. — W. O. E. Oesterley

3 COMMENTARY: One man bears hatred against another, and does he seek pardon from the Lord? Hatred of another sits ill with seeking pardon from God. — W. O. E. Oesterley

4 COMMENTARY: He shows no mercy to a man like himself, and does he ask forgiveness of his own sins? Mercy withheld from a fellow man makes asking forgiveness for one's own sins inconsistent. — W. O. E. Oesterley

5 COMMENTARY: If he who is but flesh nourishes hatred, who will entreat for pardon of his sins? Mere flesh nourishing hatred leaves no one to entreat pardon for its sins. — W. O. E. Oesterley

6 COMMENTARY: Remember your end, and let enmity cease; remember corruption and death, and abide in the commandments. Remembering death and corruption ends enmity and keeps a person in the commandments. — W. O. E. Oesterley

7 COMMENTARY: Remember the commandments, and bear no malice to your neighbour; remember the covenant of the Highest, and wink at ignorance. Commandments and covenant together require no malice toward neighbours and overlooking of ignorance. — W. O. E. Oesterley

8 COMMENTARY: Abstain from strife, and you will diminish your sins, for a furious man will kindle strife. Avoiding strife diminishes sins, while fury kindles strife. — W. O. E. Oesterley

9 COMMENTARY: A sinful man disquiets friends and makes debate among those who are at peace. The sinful man disturbs friends and manufactures debate among the peaceful. — W. O. E. Oesterley

10 COMMENTARY: As the matter of the fire is, so it burns; and as a man's strength is, so is his wrath; and according to his riches his anger rises; and the stronger they are who contend, the more they will be inflamed. Fire burns according to its fuel, wrath according to strength and riches, and strong contenders inflame each other the more. — W. O. E. Oesterley

11 COMMENTARY: A hasty contention kindles a fire, and hasty fighting sheds blood. Hasty contention kindles fire, and hasty fighting sheds blood. — W. O. E. Oesterley

12 COMMENTARY: If you blow the spark, it will burn; if you spit upon it, it will be quenched; and both these come out of your mouth. The mouth both blows sparks into flame and spits them out; speech decides whether strife burns or dies. — W. O. E. Oesterley

13 COMMENTARY: Curse the whisperer and double-tongued, for such have destroyed many who were at peace. Whisperers and double-tongued people deserve cursing, since they have destroyed many peaceful people. — W. O. E. Oesterley

14 COMMENTARY: A backbiting tongue has disquieted many, driven them from nation to nation, pulled down strong cities, and overthrown the houses of great men. Backbiting moves peoples, topples strong cities, and overthrows great houses. — W. O. E. Oesterley

15 COMMENTARY: A backbiting tongue has cast out virtuous women and deprived them of their labours. Backbiting drives out virtuous women and robs them of their labours. — W. O. E. Oesterley

16 COMMENTARY: Whoever hearkens to it will never find rest, and never dwell quietly. Listening to backbiting destroys rest and quiet dwelling. — W. O. E. Oesterley

17 COMMENTARY: The stroke of the whip makes marks in the flesh, but the stroke of the tongue breaks the bones. Whip strokes mark flesh, but tongue strokes break bones. — W. O. E. Oesterley

18 COMMENTARY: Many have fallen by the edge of the sword, but not so many as have fallen by the tongue. The tongue has felled more than the sword's edge. — W. O. E. Oesterley

19 COMMENTARY: Well is he who is defended through the venom thereof, who has not drawn the yoke thereof, nor been bound in her bands. Blessed is the one shielded from the tongue's venom, never yoked or bound by it. — W. O. E. Oesterley

20 COMMENTARY: The yoke thereof is a yoke of iron, and the bands thereof are bands of brass. The tongue's yoke is iron and its bands are brass, unbreakable by the victim. — W. O. E. Oesterley

21 COMMENTARY: The death thereof is an evil death; the grave were better than it. Death by the tongue is an evil death, worse than the grave itself. — W. O. E. Oesterley

22 COMMENTARY: It will not have rule over those who fear God, neither will they be burned with the flame thereof. The tongue does not rule the God-fearing, nor are they burned by its flame. — W. O. E. Oesterley

23 COMMENTARY: Those who forsake the Lord will fall into it, and it will burn in them and not be quenched; it will be sent upon them as a lion, and devour them as a leopard. Forsakers of God fall into the tongue's power, burned unquenchably, devoured like prey by lion and leopard. — W. O. E. Oesterley

24 COMMENTARY: See that you hedge your possession about with thorns, and bind up your silver and gold. Possessions are hedged with thorns and money bound up; guarding what is yours is prudent. — W. O. E. Oesterley

25 COMMENTARY: Weigh your words in a balance, and make a door and bar for your mouth. Words should be weighed in a balance, and the mouth fitted with door and bar. — W. O. E. Oesterley

26 COMMENTARY: Beware you slide not by it, lest you fall before him who lies in wait. Care is needed not to slip by the tongue, lest you fall before one lying in wait for your words. — W. O. E. Oesterley
