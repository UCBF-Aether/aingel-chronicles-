Study Notes — Sirach

Chapter 31

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Watching for riches consumes the flesh, and the care thereof drives away sleep. Anxious watching for riches wastes the flesh and banishes sleep. — W. O. E. Oesterley

2 COMMENTARY: Watching care will not let a man slumber, as a sore disease breaks sleep. Caring watchfulness breaks sleep like a sore disease. — W. O. E. Oesterley

3 COMMENTARY: The rich man has great labour in gathering riches together, and when he rests, he is filled with his delicates. Rich gathering is great labour, and rich rest is filled with delicacies. — W. O. E. Oesterley

4 COMMENTARY: The poor man labours in his poor estate, and when he leaves off, he is still needy. Poor labour ends still needy when it stops. — W. O. E. Oesterley

5 COMMENTARY: He who loves gold will not be justified, and he who follows corruption will have enough thereof. Loving gold cannot be justified, and following corruption yields plenty of it. — W. O. E. Oesterley

6 COMMENTARY: Gold has been the ruin of many, and their destruction was present. Gold has ruined many, with destruction close at hand. — W. O. E. Oesterley

7 COMMENTARY: It is a stumbling block to those who sacrifice to it, and every fool will be taken with it. Gold is a stumbling block to its worshippers, and every fool is caught by it. — W. O. E. Oesterley

8 COMMENTARY: Blessed is the rich man found without blemish, who has not gone after gold. The blameless rich man, who has not chased gold, is blessed. — W. O. E. Oesterley

9 COMMENTARY: Who is he? And we will call him blessed, for wonderful things he has done among his people. Such a man is rare enough to be sought out and called blessed for his wonderful deeds among his people. — W. O. E. Oesterley

10 COMMENTARY: Who has been tried thereby and found perfect? Then let him glory. Who might offend, and has not offended, or done evil, and has not done it? Trial by riches and perfection under it give ground for glory, as does power to offend or do evil left unused. — W. O. E. Oesterley

11 COMMENTARY: His goods will be established, and the congregation will declare his alms. The blameless rich man's goods stand firm, and the congregation proclaims his alms. — W. O. E. Oesterley

12 COMMENTARY: If you sit at a bountiful table, do not be greedy upon it, and do not say, there is much meat on it. At a bountiful table greed must be checked, and abundance must not be remarked upon greedily. — W. O. E. Oesterley

13 COMMENTARY: Remember that a wicked eye is an evil thing, and what is created more wicked than an eye? Therefore it weeps upon every occasion. A wicked eye is an evil thing, the most wicked of creations, weeping on every occasion. — W. O. E. Oesterley

14 COMMENTARY: Do not stretch your hand whithersoever it looks, and do not thrust it with him into the dish. The hand must not follow the eye everywhere, nor thrust into the dish with another. — W. O. E. Oesterley

15 COMMENTARY: Judge not your neighbour by yourself, and be discreet in every point. Neighbours must not be judged by oneself, and discretion is required in every point. — W. O. E. Oesterley

16 COMMENTARY: Eat as it becomes a man, those things set before you, and do not devour, lest you be hated. Eat becomingly what is set before you, without devouring, lest you be hated. — W. O. E. Oesterley

17 COMMENTARY: Leave off first for manners' sake, and do not be unsatiable, lest you offend. Stopping first is good manners, and insatiability offends. — W. O. E. Oesterley

18 COMMENTARY: When you sit among many, do not reach your hand out first of all. Among many, reaching first is bad manners. — W. O. E. Oesterley

19 COMMENTARY: A very little is sufficient for a man well nurtured, and he does not fetch his wind short upon his bed. A well-nurtured man needs very little, and does not lie short of breath upon his bed from overeating. — W. O. E. Oesterley

20 COMMENTARY: Sound sleep comes of moderate eating; he rises early, and his wits are with him; but the pain of watching, choler, and pangs of the belly are with an unsatiable man. Moderate eating gives sound sleep, early rising, and clear wits, while insatiability brings sleepless pain, choler, and belly pangs. — W. O. E. Oesterley

21 COMMENTARY: If you have been forced to eat, arise, go forth, vomit, and you will have rest. Overeating forced upon you is remedied by rising, going out, vomiting, and resting. — W. O. E. Oesterley

22 COMMENTARY: My son, hear me, and do not despise me, and at the last you will find as I told you; in all your works be quick, and no sickness will come to you. Hearing and not despising this teaching proves true at last, and quickness in all works keeps sickness away. — W. O. E. Oesterley

23 COMMENTARY: Whoever is liberal of his meat, men will speak well of him, and the report of his good housekeeping will be believed. Liberality with meat earns good report, and good housekeeping is believed of such a man. — W. O. E. Oesterley

24 COMMENTARY: But against him who is a niggard of his meat the whole city will murmur, and the testimonies of his niggardness will not be doubted. A niggard with meat draws the whole city's murmur, and his stinginess is not doubted. — W. O. E. Oesterley

25 COMMENTARY: Do not show your valiantness in wine, for wine has destroyed many. Valiance in wine must not be shown, since wine has destroyed many. — W. O. E. Oesterley

26 COMMENTARY: The furnace proves the edge by dipping, so does wine prove the hearts of the proud by drunkenness. As the furnace proves a blade by dipping, wine proves proud hearts by drunkenness. — W. O. E. Oesterley

27 COMMENTARY: Wine is as good as life to a man, if drunk moderately; what life is then to a man without wine? For it was made to make men glad. Moderately drunk, wine is as good as life itself, made to make men glad. — W. O. E. Oesterley

28 COMMENTARY: Wine measurably drunk and in season brings gladness of heart and cheerfulness of mind. Measured, seasonable wine brings heart-gladness and cheerful mind. — W. O. E. Oesterley

29 COMMENTARY: But wine drunk with excess makes bitterness of mind, with brawling and quarrelling. Excess wine makes bitter minds, brawling, and quarrelling. — W. O. E. Oesterley

30 COMMENTARY: Drunkenness increases the rage of a fool till he offends; it diminishes strength and makes wounds. Drunkenness feeds a fool's rage till he offends, weakens strength, and causes wounds. — W. O. E. Oesterley

31 COMMENTARY: Do not rebuke your neighbour at the wine, and do not despise him in his mirth; give him no despiteful words, and do not press upon him by urging him to drink. At wine, neighbours must not be rebuked, despised in mirth, despitefully spoken to, or pressed to drink. — W. O. E. Oesterley
