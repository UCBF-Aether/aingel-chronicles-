Study Notes — Sirach

Chapter 35

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: He who keeps the law brings offerings enough; he who takes heed to the commandment offers a peace offering. Law-keeping is offering enough, and heeding the commandment is itself a peace offering. — W. O. E. Oesterley

2 COMMENTARY: He who requites a good turn offers fine flour, and he who gives alms sacrifices praise. Repaying kindness is a fine-flour offering, and almsgiving is a sacrifice of praise. — W. O. E. Oesterley

3 COMMENTARY: To depart from wickedness is pleasing to the Lord, and to forsake unrighteousness is a propitiation. Departing from wickedness pleases God, and forsaking unrighteousness atones. — W. O. E. Oesterley

4 COMMENTARY: You will not appear empty before the Lord. No one should come before God empty-handed. — W. O. E. Oesterley

5 COMMENTARY: For all these things are to be done because of the commandment. Offerings and departures from evil are all done because God commanded them. — W. O. E. Oesterley

6 COMMENTARY: The offering of the righteous makes the altar fat, and the sweet savour thereof is before the Most High. Righteous offerings fatten the altar, and their sweet savour rises before God. — W. O. E. Oesterley

7 COMMENTARY: The sacrifice of a just man is acceptable, and the memorial thereof will never be forgotten. A just man's sacrifice is accepted, and its memorial is never forgotten. — W. O. E. Oesterley

8 COMMENTARY: Give the Lord his honour with a good eye, and do not diminish the firstfruits of your hands. God's honour should be given generously, with a good eye, and firstfruits must not be diminished. — W. O. E. Oesterley

9 COMMENTARY: In all your gifts show a cheerful countenance, and dedicate your tithes with gladness. Gifts should be given with a cheerful face, and tithes dedicated gladly. — W. O. E. Oesterley

10 COMMENTARY: Give to the Most High according as he has enriched you, and as you have gotten, give with a cheerful eye. Giving should match God's enrichment of you, given with a cheerful eye in proportion to what was gotten. — W. O. E. Oesterley

11 COMMENTARY: For the Lord recompenses, and will give you seven times as much. God repays, returning seven times as much as given. — W. O. E. Oesterley

12 COMMENTARY: Do not think to corrupt with gifts, for such he will not receive; and do not trust to unrighteous sacrifices, for the Lord is judge, and with him is no respect of persons. God cannot be bribed with gifts and unrighteous sacrifices cannot be trusted, since he judges without respect of persons. — W. O. E. Oesterley

13 COMMENTARY: He will not accept any person against a poor man, but will hear the prayer of the oppressed. God does not accept persons against the poor, but hears the oppressed man's prayer. — W. O. E. Oesterley

14 COMMENTARY: He will not despise the supplication of the fatherless, nor the widow, when she pours out her complaint. Fatherless supplication and a widow's poured-out complaint are not despised by God. — W. O. E. Oesterley

15 COMMENTARY: Do not the tears run down the widow's cheeks? And is not her cry against him who causes them to fall? The widow's tears run down her cheeks, and her cry stands against whoever caused them. — W. O. E. Oesterley

16 COMMENTARY: He who serves the Lord will be accepted with favour, and his prayer will reach to the clouds. The Lord's servant is accepted with favour, and his prayer reaches the clouds. — W. O. E. Oesterley

17 COMMENTARY: The prayer of the humble pierces the clouds, and till it comes near, he will not be comforted, and will not depart, till the Most High beholds to judge righteously and execute judgment. Humble prayer pierces the clouds and persists uncomforted until God looks down to judge righteously. — W. O. E. Oesterley

18 COMMENTARY: For the Lord will not be slack, nor will the Mighty be patient toward them, till he has smitten in sunder the loins of the unmerciful, repaid vengeance to the heathen, taken away the multitude of the proud, and broken the sceptre of the unrighteous. God will not delay or be patient toward the unmerciful, heathen, proud, and unrighteous until he breaks them and their sceptres. — W. O. E. Oesterley

19 COMMENTARY: Till he has rendered to every man according to his deeds, and to the works of men according to their devices; till he has judged the cause of his people, and made them rejoice in his mercy. Judgment renders to each according to deeds and devices, judges his people's cause, and makes them rejoice in his mercy. — W. O. E. Oesterley

20 COMMENTARY: Mercy is seasonable in the time of affliction, as clouds of rain in the time of drought. Mercy in affliction is as timely as rain clouds in drought. — W. O. E. Oesterley
