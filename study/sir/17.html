Study Notes — Sirach

Chapter 17

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: The Lord created man of the earth, and turned him into it again. Man's origin and end are both earth: created from it and returned to it. — W. O. E. Oesterley

2 COMMENTARY: He gave them few days and a short time, and power also over the things therein. Human life is brief, yet within it man is given power over earthly things. — W. O. E. Oesterley

3 COMMENTARY: He endued them with strength by themselves, and made them according to his image. Man is endowed with strength in himself and made in God's image. — W. O. E. Oesterley

4 COMMENTARY: He put the fear of man upon all flesh, and gave him dominion over beasts and fowls. All flesh fears man, and man holds dominion over beast and bird. — W. O. E. Oesterley

5 COMMENTARY: They received the use of the five operations of the Lord, and in the sixth place he imparted them understanding, and in the seventh speech, an interpreter of the thoughts thereof. The five senses are God's gift, and above them stand understanding and speech, which interprets thought. — W. O. E. Oesterley

6 COMMENTARY: Counsel, tongue, eyes, ears, and a heart he gave them to understand. Counsel, speech, sight, hearing, and heart are all given for understanding. — W. O. E. Oesterley

7 COMMENTARY: Withal he filled them with the knowledge of understanding, and showed them good and evil. Man is filled with understanding's knowledge and shown the difference between good and evil. — W. O. E. Oesterley

8 COMMENTARY: He set his eye upon their hearts, that he might show them the greatness of his works. God's eye rests on human hearts so that they may be shown the greatness of his works. — W. O. E. Oesterley

9 COMMENTARY: He gave them to glory in his marvellous acts for ever, that they might declare his works with understanding. Man is given to glory in God's marvellous acts for ever and to declare them with understanding. — W. O. E. Oesterley

10 COMMENTARY: And the elect will praise his holy name. The elect answer God's works with praise of his holy name. — W. O. E. Oesterley

11 COMMENTARY: Beside this he gave them knowledge, and the law of life for a heritage. Knowledge and the law of life are given as a heritage, an inheritance to live by. — W. O. E. Oesterley

12 COMMENTARY: He made an everlasting covenant with them, and showed them his judgments. God's covenant is everlasting, and his judgments are shown to his people. — W. O. E. Oesterley

13 COMMENTARY: Their eyes saw the majesty of his glory, and their ears heard his glorious voice. Eyes saw his glorious majesty and ears heard his glorious voice at the giving of the covenant. — W. O. E. Oesterley

14 COMMENTARY: He said to them, beware of all unrighteousness, and he gave every man commandment concerning his neighbour. The covenant command is twofold: beware unrighteousness, and keep commandment toward one's neighbour. — W. O. E. Oesterley

15 COMMENTARY: Their ways are ever before him, and will not be hid from his eyes. Human ways stand always before God and cannot be hidden from his eyes. — W. O. E. Oesterley

16 COMMENTARY: Every man from his youth is given to evil; neither could they make themselves fleshy hearts for stony. From youth man is inclined to evil, and he cannot by himself turn a stony heart into flesh. — W. O. E. Oesterley

17 COMMENTARY: In the division of the nations of the whole earth he set a ruler over every people; but Israel is the Lord's portion. Every nation has its ruler in God's division of the earth, but Israel is the Lord's own portion. — W. O. E. Oesterley

18 COMMENTARY: Whom, being his firstborn, he nourishes with discipline, and giving him the light of his love does not forsake him. Israel, as firstborn, is nourished by God's discipline and never forsaken, being given the light of his love. — W. O. E. Oesterley

19 COMMENTARY: Therefore all their works are as the sun before him, and his eyes are continually upon their ways. Israel's works lie open as the sun before God, and his eyes rest continually on their ways. — W. O. E. Oesterley

20 COMMENTARY: None of their unrighteous deeds are hid from him, but all their sins are before the Lord. No unrighteous deed of theirs is hidden; all sins stand before the Lord. — W. O. E. Oesterley

21 COMMENTARY: But the Lord, being gracious and knowing his workmanship, neither left nor forsook them, but spared them. Gracious knowledge of his own workmanship kept God from leaving or forsaking them; he spared them. — W. O. E. Oesterley

22 COMMENTARY: The alms of a man is as a signet with him, and he will keep the good deeds of man as the apple of the eye, and give repentance to his sons and daughters. Alms are kept by God as a signet, good deeds as the apple of his eye, and he grants repentance to sons and daughters. — W. O. E. Oesterley

23 COMMENTARY: Afterwards he will rise up and reward them, and render their recompense upon their heads. God will rise up to reward, rendering recompense upon the heads of the rewarded. — W. O. E. Oesterley

24 COMMENTARY: But to those who repent, he granted return, and comforted those who failed in patience. Repentance is granted return, and those who failed in patience are comforted. — W. O. E. Oesterley

25 COMMENTARY: Return to the Lord, forsake your sins, make your prayer before his face, and offend less. The call to repentance is practical: return, forsake sins, pray before his face, and sin less. — W. O. E. Oesterley

26 COMMENTARY: Turn again to the Most High, turn away from iniquity, for he will lead you out of darkness into the light of health, and hate abomination vehemently. Turning to God and away from iniquity leads out of darkness into the light of health, and abomination must be hated fiercely. — W. O. E. Oesterley

27 COMMENTARY: Who will praise the Most High in the grave, instead of those who live and give thanks? The grave offers no praise to replace the praise of the living who give thanks. — W. O. E. Oesterley

28 COMMENTARY: Thanksgiving perishes from the dead, as from one who is not; the living and sound in heart will praise the Lord. The dead cannot give thanks, as if they were not; praise belongs to the living whose hearts are sound. — W. O. E. Oesterley

29 COMMENTARY: How great is the lovingkindness of the Lord our God, and his compassion to those who turn to him in holiness! God's lovingkindness and compassion are great toward those who turn to him in holiness. — W. O. E. Oesterley

30 COMMENTARY: For all things cannot be in men, because the son of man is not immortal. Men cannot hold all things, since the son of man is not immortal. — W. O. E. Oesterley

31 COMMENTARY: What is brighter than the sun? Yet its light fails; and flesh and blood will imagine evil. Even the sun's light fails, and flesh and blood still imagine evil. — W. O. E. Oesterley

32 COMMENTARY: He views the power of the height of heaven, and all men are but earth and ashes. God views heaven's high power, while all men remain earth and ashes. — W. O. E. Oesterley
