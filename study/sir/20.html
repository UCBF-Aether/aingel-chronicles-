Study Notes — Sirach

Chapter 20

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: There is a reproof that is not comely, and again, some man holds his tongue and is wise. Reproof can be unseemly, while at other times silence itself is wisdom. — W. O. E. Oesterley

2 COMMENTARY: It is much better to reprove than to be angry secretly, and he who confesses his fault will be preserved from hurt. Open reproof beats secret anger, and confession of fault preserves from hurt. — W. O. E. Oesterley

3 COMMENTARY: How good it is, when you are reproved, to show repentance, for so you will escape wilful sin. Repentance under reproof is good because it escapes the hardening into wilful sin. — W. O. E. Oesterley

4 COMMENTARY: As is the lust of a eunuch to deflower a virgin, so is he who executes judgment with violence. Violent judgment is as fruitless and unnatural as a eunuch's lust. — W. O. E. Oesterley

5 COMMENTARY: There is one who keeps silence and is found wise, and another who by much babbling becomes hateful. Silence can prove wisdom, while much babbling proves hatefulness. — W. O. E. Oesterley

6 COMMENTARY: Some man holds his tongue because he has nothing to answer, and some keep silence knowing their time. Silence has two kinds: emptiness with nothing to say, and wisdom waiting for its time. — W. O. E. Oesterley

7 COMMENTARY: A wise man will hold his tongue till he sees opportunity, but a babbler and a fool will regard no time. The wise wait for the right moment to speak; babblers and fools speak regardless of time. — W. O. E. Oesterley

8 COMMENTARY: He who uses many words will be abhorred, and he who takes authority to himself therein will be hated. Many words breed abhorrence, and self-assumed authority in speech breeds hatred. — W. O. E. Oesterley

9 COMMENTARY: There is a sinner who has good success in evil things, and there is a gain that turns to loss. Success in evil is no proof of blessing, and some gain turns out to be loss. — W. O. E. Oesterley

10 COMMENTARY: There is a gift that will not profit you, and there is a gift whose recompense is double. Some gifts bring no profit, while others repay double. — W. O. E. Oesterley

11 COMMENTARY: There is an abasement because of glory, and there is one who lifts up his head from a low estate. Glory can bring a man low, and a low estate can be the starting point of a lifted head. — W. O. E. Oesterley

12 COMMENTARY: There is one who buys much for a little and repays it sevenfold. A sharp bargain that buys much for little may have to be repaid sevenfold. — W. O. E. Oesterley

13 COMMENTARY: A wise man by his words makes himself beloved, but the graces of fools will be poured out. Wise words win love, while a fool's graces spill out wastefully and win nothing. — W. O. E. Oesterley

14 COMMENTARY: The gift of a fool will do you no good when you have it, nor yet that of the envious for his necessity, for he looks to receive many things for one. A fool's gift profits nothing, and the envious giver's gift is really a bargain, expecting many things for one. — W. O. E. Oesterley

15 COMMENTARY: He gives little and upbraids much; he opens his mouth like a crier; today he lends, and tomorrow he will ask it again: such a one is to be hated by God and man. The grudging giver, who gives little, upbraids much, trumpets his gift, and demands it back tomorrow, is hated by God and man alike. — W. O. E. Oesterley

16 COMMENTARY: The fool says, I have no friends, I have no thanks for all my good deeds, and those who eat my bread speak evil of me. The fool's complaint, that he has no friends or thanks and that his bread-eaters speak evil, exposes his own way of giving. — W. O. E. Oesterley

17 COMMENTARY: How often, and by how many, will he be laughed to scorn, for he does not know aright what it is to have, and it is all one to him as if he had it not. Such a fool is laughed to scorn by many, because he does not know how to possess what he has; it is as if he had it not. — W. O. E. Oesterley

18 COMMENTARY: To slip upon a pavement is better than to slip with the tongue, so the fall of the wicked will come speedily. A bodily slip is better than a tongue's slip, and the wicked fall swiftly by the tongue. — W. O. E. Oesterley

19 COMMENTARY: An unseasonable tale will always be in the mouth of the unwise. The unwise always carry an ill-timed tale ready on the mouth. — W. O. E. Oesterley

20 COMMENTARY: A wise sentence will be rejected when it comes out of a fool's mouth, for he will not speak it in due season. Even a wise sentence fails from a fool's mouth, because he speaks it out of season. — W. O. E. Oesterley

21 COMMENTARY: There is one who is hindered from sinning through want, and when he takes rest he will not be troubled. Hindrance from sin by mere want is not virtue, but when such a man rests he is at least untroubled. — W. O. E. Oesterley

22 COMMENTARY: There is one who destroys his own soul through bashfulness, and by accepting persons overthrows himself. False bashfulness destroys the soul, and favouritism toward persons overthrows a man. — W. O. E. Oesterley

23 COMMENTARY: There is one who for bashfulness promises to his friend, and makes him his enemy for nothing. A promise made from bashfulness, then broken, turns a friend into an enemy for nothing. — W. O. E. Oesterley

24 COMMENTARY: A lie is a foul blot in a man, yet it is continually in the mouth of the untaught. Lying is a foul blot, yet it lives continually in the mouth of the untaught. — W. O. E. Oesterley

25 COMMENTARY: A thief is better than a man accustomed to lie, but both will have destruction for heritage. A thief is better than a habitual liar, yet both inherit destruction. — W. O. E. Oesterley

26 COMMENTARY: The disposition of a liar is dishonourable, and his shame is ever with him. A liar's disposition is dishonourable, and his shame never leaves him. — W. O. E. Oesterley

27 COMMENTARY: A wise man will promote himself to honour with his words, and he who has understanding will please great men. Wise words raise a man to honour, and understanding pleases great men. — W. O. E. Oesterley

28 COMMENTARY: He who tills his land will increase his heap, and he who pleases great men will get pardon for iniquity. Tilling increases the heap, and pleasing great men can obtain pardon for iniquity. — W. O. E. Oesterley

29 COMMENTARY: Presents and gifts blind the eyes of the wise and stop up his mouth so that he cannot reprove. Gifts blind even wise eyes and silence reproof. — W. O. E. Oesterley

30 COMMENTARY: Wisdom that is hidden, and treasure hoarded up, what profit is in them both? Hidden wisdom and hoarded treasure are alike profitless. — W. O. E. Oesterley

31 COMMENTARY: Better is he who hides his folly than a man who hides his wisdom. Hiding folly is better than hiding wisdom, since wisdom hidden helps no one. — W. O. E. Oesterley

32 COMMENTARY: Necessary patience in seeking the Lord is better than he who leads his life without a guide. Patient seeking of the Lord, even when necessary and hard, is better than living without any guide. — W. O. E. Oesterley
