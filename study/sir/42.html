Study Notes — Sirach

Chapter 42

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Of these things do not be ashamed, and accept no person to sin thereby. The things that follow are not to be shamed of, and no person's favour should lead to sinning over them. — W. O. E. Oesterley

2 COMMENTARY: Of the law of the Most High, and his covenant, and of judgment to justify the ungodly. Do not be ashamed of God's law and covenant, nor of judgment that justifies the ungodly. — W. O. E. Oesterley

3 COMMENTARY: Of reckoning with your partners and travellers, or of the gift of the heritage of friends. Do not be ashamed of honest reckoning with partners and travellers, or of gifts from friends' heritage. — W. O. E. Oesterley

4 COMMENTARY: Of exactness of balance and weights, or of getting much or little. Exact balances and weights, and getting much or little honestly, are nothing to be ashamed of. — W. O. E. Oesterley

5 COMMENTARY: And of merchants' indifferent selling; of much correction of children; and to make the side of an evil servant bleed. Impartial merchant selling, much correction of children, and severe correction of an evil servant are not shames. — W. O. E. Oesterley

6 COMMENTARY: Sure keeping is good, where an evil wife is, and shut up, where many hands are. Sure keeping is good with an evil wife, and locking up where many hands are about. — W. O. E. Oesterley

7 COMMENTARY: Deliver all things in number and weight, and put all in writing that you give out or receive. All things should be delivered by number and weight, and all givings and receivings put in writing. — W. O. E. Oesterley

8 COMMENTARY: Do not be ashamed to inform the unwise and foolish, and the extreme aged who contend with those who are young; thus you will be truly learned, and approved of all men living. Informing the unwise, foolish, and aged contenders with the young is no shame; it proves true learning and wins approval. — W. O. E. Oesterley

9 COMMENTARY: The father wakes for the daughter, when no man knows, and care for her takes away sleep: when she is young, lest she pass away the flower of her age; and being married, lest she should be hated. A father's secret wakefulness for his daughter costs sleep, fearing for her youth's flower and for hatred after marriage. — W. O. E. Oesterley

10 COMMENTARY: In her virginity, lest she should be defiled and gotten with child in her father's house; and having a husband, lest she should misbehave herself; and when she is married, lest she should be barren. A father's fears run through his daughter's stages: defilement in virginity, misbehaviour with a husband, and barrenness in marriage. — W. O. E. Oesterley

11 COMMENTARY: Keep a sure watch over a shameless daughter, lest she make you a laughingstock to your enemies, a byword in the city, a reproach among the people, and make you ashamed before the multitude. A shameless daughter needs sure watching, lest she make her father a laughingstock, byword, reproach, and public shame. — W. O. E. Oesterley

12 COMMENTARY: Do not behold everybody's beauty, and do not sit in the midst of women. Beholding every beauty and sitting among women are both to be avoided. — W. O. E. Oesterley

13 COMMENTARY: For from garments comes a moth, and from women wickedness. As moths come from garments, so wickedness is said to come from women in this proverb's rhetoric. — W. O. E. Oesterley

14 COMMENTARY: Better is the churlishness of a man than a courteous woman, a woman, I say, who brings shame and reproach. A churlish man is better than a courteous woman who brings shame and reproach. — W. O. E. Oesterley

15 COMMENTARY: I will now remember the works of the Lord, and declare the things I have seen: in the words of the Lord are his works. The writer turns to remembering and declaring God's works, which stand in his words. — W. O. E. Oesterley

16 COMMENTARY: The sun that gives light looks upon all things, and the work thereof is full of the glory of the Lord. The light-giving sun looks on all things, and its work is full of God's glory. — W. O. E. Oesterley

17 COMMENTARY: The Lord has not given power to the saints to declare all his marvellous works, which the Almighty Lord firmly settled, that whatsoever is might be established for his glory. Even saints lack power to declare all God's marvellous works, firmly settled by the Almighty so that all is established for his glory. — W. O. E. Oesterley

18 COMMENTARY: He seeks out the deep, and the heart, and considers their crafty devices, for the Lord knows all that may be known, and beholds the signs of the world. God searches deep and heart, considering crafty devices, knowing all knowable things and beholding the world's signs. — W. O. E. Oesterley

19 COMMENTARY: He declares the things that are past, and for to come, and reveals the steps of hidden things. God declares past and future, and reveals hidden things' steps. — W. O. E. Oesterley

20 COMMENTARY: No thought escapes him, neither any word is hidden from him. No thought escapes God, and no word is hidden from him. — W. O. E. Oesterley

21 COMMENTARY: He has garnished the excellent works of his wisdom, and he is from everlasting to everlasting; to him nothing may be added, nor can he be diminished, and he has no need of any counsellor. God's excellent wisdom-works are adorned, he is eternal, nothing can be added to or taken from him, and he needs no counsellor. — W. O. E. Oesterley

22 COMMENTARY: O how desirable are all his works, and that a man may see even to a spark! God's works are altogether desirable, even to the smallest spark a man may see. — W. O. E. Oesterley

23 COMMENTARY: All these things live and remain for ever for all uses, and they are all obedient. All these things live and remain for ever, for all uses, and all obey. — W. O. E. Oesterley

24 COMMENTARY: All things are double one against another, and he has made nothing imperfect. All things are paired, double one against another, and nothing God made is imperfect. — W. O. E. Oesterley

25 COMMENTARY: One thing establishes the good of another, and who will be filled with beholding his glory? One thing supports another's good, and no one can be filled with beholding God's glory. — W. O. E. Oesterley
