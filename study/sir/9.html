Study Notes — Sirach

Chapter 9

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Do not be jealous over the wife of your bosom, and do not teach her an evil lesson against yourself. Jealousy over a wife teaches her the very evil it fears, turned against the husband himself. — W. O. E. Oesterley

2 COMMENTARY: Do not give your soul to a woman to set her foot upon your substance. Surrendering yourself to a woman lets her trample your substance underfoot. — W. O. E. Oesterley

3 COMMENTARY: Do not meet with a harlot, lest you fall into her snares. Meeting a harlot is entering snares already set. — W. O. E. Oesterley

4 COMMENTARY: Do not use much the company of a woman who is a singer, lest you be taken with her attempts. Frequent company with a singing woman exposes a man to her practised allurements. — W. O. E. Oesterley

5 COMMENTARY: Do not gaze on a maid, lest you fall by those things that are precious in her. Gazing at a young woman leads to falling through attraction to her charms. — W. O. E. Oesterley

6 COMMENTARY: Do not give your soul to harlots, lest you lose your inheritance. Giving oneself to harlots costs the inheritance itself. — W. O. E. Oesterley

7 COMMENTARY: Do not look round about in the streets of the city, nor wander in its solitary places. Roving eyes in streets and lonely corners invite the very temptations this chapter warns against. — W. O. E. Oesterley

8 COMMENTARY: Turn your eye from a beautiful woman, and do not look upon another's beauty, for many have been deceived by a woman's beauty, and love is kindled by it as a fire. Beauty deceives many, and desire kindled by looking burns like fire. — W. O. E. Oesterley

9 COMMENTARY: Do not sit at all with another man's wife, nor sit down with her in your arms, nor spend your money with her at the wine, lest your heart incline to her and through desire you fall into destruction. Intimacy with another man's wife, with wine and money spent on it, bends the heart until desire ends in destruction. — W. O. E. Oesterley

10 COMMENTARY: Do not forsake an old friend, for the new is not comparable to him; a new friend is like new wine, and when it is old you will drink it with pleasure. Old friendship outvalues new, as old wine outvalues new; the new must age before it gives pleasure. — W. O. E. Oesterley

11 COMMENTARY: Do not envy the glory of a sinner, for you do not know what his end will be. A sinner's present glory should not be envied, since his end is unknown and may be ruin. — W. O. E. Oesterley

12 COMMENTARY: Do not delight in what the ungodly take pleasure in, but remember they will not go unpunished to their grave. Pleasures of the ungodly end in punishment before the grave, so they are no delight for the wise. — W. O. E. Oesterley

13 COMMENTARY: Keep far from the man who has power to kill, so you will not doubt the fear of death; and if you come to him, make no fault, lest he take away your life presently, remembering you go amid snares and walk upon the battlements of the city. Nearness to a man with power of death is walking among snares on high battlements, where a single fault costs life. — W. O. E. Oesterley

14 COMMENTARY: As near as you can, guess at your neighbour, and consult with the wise. Judge your neighbour as best you can, and take counsel with the wise rather than trusting guesswork alone. — W. O. E. Oesterley

15 COMMENTARY: Let your talk be with the wise, and all your communication in the law of the Most High. Conversation should be kept among the wise and centred on the law of God. — W. O. E. Oesterley

16 COMMENTARY: Let just men eat and drink with you, and let your glorying be in the fear of the Lord. Table company should be just men, and boasting should be in reverence for God, not in anything else. — W. O. E. Oesterley

17 COMMENTARY: For the hand of the artificer the work will be commended, and the wise ruler of the people for his speech. A craftsman is praised by his work, and a wise ruler by his speech; each is known by his proper product. — W. O. E. Oesterley

18 COMMENTARY: A man of an ill tongue is dangerous in his city, and he who is rash in his talk will be hated. An evil tongue endangers a whole city, and rash talk earns hatred. — W. O. E. Oesterley
