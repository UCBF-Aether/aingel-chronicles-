Study Notes — Sirach

Chapter 41

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: O death, how bitter is the remembrance of you to a man who lives at rest in his possessions, to the man who has nothing to vex him, and has prosperity in all things, even to him who is yet able to receive meat! Death's remembrance is bitter to the prosperous man at rest, vexed by nothing, able still to enjoy meat. — W. O. E. Oesterley

2 COMMENTARY: O death, acceptable is your sentence to the needy, to him whose strength fails, who is now in the last age, vexed with all things, and to him who despairs and has lost patience! Death's sentence is acceptable to the needy, the failing, the aged and vexed, and the despairing who have lost patience. — W. O. E. Oesterley

3 COMMENTARY: Fear not the sentence of death; remember those who have been before you and who come after, for this is the sentence of the Lord over all flesh. Death's sentence need not be feared, since it is God's sentence over all flesh, before and after us alike. — W. O. E. Oesterley

4 COMMENTARY: Why are you against the pleasure of the Most High? There is no inquisition in the grave, whether you have lived ten, a hundred, or a thousand years. Resisting God's pleasure is vain, since the grave holds no inquisition over length of life, whether ten years or a thousand. — W. O. E. Oesterley

5 COMMENTARY: The children of sinners are abominable children, and those conversant in the dwelling of the ungodly. Sinners' children are abominable, as are dwellers with the ungodly. — W. O. E. Oesterley

6 COMMENTARY: The inheritance of sinners' children will perish, and their posterity will have a perpetual reproach. Sinners' children's inheritance perishes, and their posterity bears perpetual reproach. — W. O. E. Oesterley

7 COMMENTARY: The children will complain of an ungodly father, because they will be reproached for his sake. Children complain of an ungodly father, since his sake brings them reproach. — W. O. E. Oesterley

8 COMMENTARY: Woe to you, ungodly men, who have forsaken the law of the Most High God! For if you increase, it will be to your destruction. Woe to law-forsaking ungodly men: even their increase tends to destruction. — W. O. E. Oesterley

9 COMMENTARY: If you are born, you will be born to a curse, and if you die, a curse will be your portion. For the ungodly, birth is to a curse and death's portion is a curse. — W. O. E. Oesterley

10 COMMENTARY: All that are of the earth will turn to earth again, so the ungodly will go from a curse to destruction. Earth returns to earth, and the ungodly pass from curse to destruction. — W. O. E. Oesterley

11 COMMENTARY: The mourning of men is about their bodies, but an ill name of sinners will be blotted out. Men's mourning concerns bodies, but sinners' ill names are blotted out. — W. O. E. Oesterley

12 COMMENTARY: Have regard to your name, for that will continue with you above a thousand great treasures of gold. A name deserves regard, since it continues above a thousand treasures of gold. — W. O. E. Oesterley

13 COMMENTARY: A good life has but few days, but a good name endures for ever. A good life is brief, but a good name lasts for ever. — W. O. E. Oesterley

14 COMMENTARY: My children, keep discipline in peace, for wisdom that is hidden, and a treasure not seen, what profit is in them both? Discipline should be kept in peace, since hidden wisdom and unseen treasure profit nothing. — W. O. E. Oesterley

15 COMMENTARY: A man who hides his foolishness is better than a man who hides his wisdom. Hiding folly is better than hiding wisdom. — W. O. E. Oesterley

16 COMMENTARY: Therefore be shamefaced according to my word, for it is not good to retain all shamefacedness, nor is it altogether approved in everything. Shamefacedness should follow this teaching, but not in everything, since retaining all of it is neither good nor altogether approved. — W. O. E. Oesterley

17 COMMENTARY: Be ashamed of whoredom before father and mother, and of a lie before a prince and a mighty man. Whoredom should shame before parents, and lying before princes and mighty men. — W. O. E. Oesterley

18 COMMENTARY: Of an offence before a judge and ruler; of iniquity before a congregation and people; of unjust dealing before your partner and friend. Shame is due for offences before judges, iniquity before congregations, and unjust dealing before partners and friends. — W. O. E. Oesterley

19 COMMENTARY: And of theft in regard of the place where you sojourn, and in regard of the truth of God and his covenant; and to lean with your elbow upon the meat; and of scorning to give and take. Shame is due for theft where one sojourns, for offences against God's truth and covenant, for leaning elbows on meat, and for scorning give-and-take. — W. O. E. Oesterley

20 COMMENTARY: And of silence before those who salute you; and to look upon a harlot. Shame is due for silence toward greeters and for looking upon a harlot. — W. O. E. Oesterley

21 COMMENTARY: And to turn away your face from your kinsman; or to take away a portion or a gift; or to gaze upon another man's wife. Shame is due for turning from a kinsman, taking away portion or gift, and gazing on another man's wife. — W. O. E. Oesterley

22 COMMENTARY: Or to be overbusy with his maid, and come not near her bed; or of upbraiding speeches before friends; and after you have given, do not upbraid. Shame is due for meddling with a neighbour's maid, for upbraiding speeches before friends, and for upbraiding after giving. — W. O. E. Oesterley

23 COMMENTARY: Or of iterating and speaking again what you have heard; and of revealing of secrets. Shame is due for repeating hearsay and for revealing secrets. — W. O. E. Oesterley

24 COMMENTARY: So will you be truly shamefaced and find favour before all men. Right shamefacedness in these things brings favour before all men. — W. O. E. Oesterley
