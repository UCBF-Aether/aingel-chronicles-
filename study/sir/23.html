Study Notes — Sirach

Chapter 23

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: O Lord, Father and Governor of all my whole life, leave me not to their counsels, and let me not fall by them. The prayer asks God, as Father and Governor of life, not to abandon the speaker to others' counsels or let him fall by them. — W. O. E. Oesterley

2 COMMENTARY: Who will set scourges over my thoughts, and the discipline of wisdom over my heart, that they spare me not for my ignorances, and pass not by my sins? The prayer asks for scourges over thoughts and wisdom's discipline over the heart, unsparing toward ignorances and sins. — W. O. E. Oesterley

3 COMMENTARY: Lest my ignorances increase, and my sins abound to my destruction, and I fall before my adversaries, and my enemy rejoice over me, whose hope is far from thy mercy. Unchecked ignorance and abounding sin end in destruction, falling before adversaries, and an enemy's rejoicing, far from God's mercy. — W. O. E. Oesterley

4 COMMENTARY: O Lord, Father and God of my life, give me not a proud look, but turn away from thy servants always a haughty mind. The prayer asks God not to give a proud look, and to turn a haughty mind away from his servants. — W. O. E. Oesterley

5 COMMENTARY: Turn away from me vain hopes and concupiscence, and thou wilt hold him up who is desirous always to serve thee. Vain hopes and lust must be turned away, and God upholds the one who desires always to serve him. — W. O. E. Oesterley

6 COMMENTARY: Let not greediness of the belly nor lust of the flesh take hold of me, and give not over thy servant into an impudent mind. Belly greed, fleshly lust, and an impudent mind must not be allowed to take hold of God's servant. — W. O. E. Oesterley

7 COMMENTARY: Hear, you children, the discipline of the mouth; he who keeps it will never be taken in his lips. The discipline of the mouth is taught to children because its keeper is never caught by his own lips. — W. O. E. Oesterley

8 COMMENTARY: The sinner will be left in his foolishness; both the evil speaker and the proud will fall thereby. The sinner is left in foolishness, and evil speakers and proud men fall by their own lips. — W. O. E. Oesterley

9 COMMENTARY: Do not accustom your mouth to swearing, nor use yourself to the naming of the Holy One. Swearing must not become a habit, nor the naming of the Holy One a common use. — W. O. E. Oesterley

10 COMMENTARY: For as a servant continually beaten will not be without a blue mark, so he who swears and names God continually will not be faultless. As a beaten servant always bears marks, so the continual swearer always bears fault. — W. O. E. Oesterley

11 COMMENTARY: A man who uses much swearing will be filled with iniquity, and the plague will never depart from his house; if he offends, his sin will be upon him, and if he acknowledges not his sin, he makes a double offence, and if he swears in vain, he will not be innocent, but his house will be full of calamities. Much swearing fills a man with iniquity and his house with plague and calamities, with sin upon him whether he offends, denies, or swears vainly. — W. O. E. Oesterley

12 COMMENTARY: There is a word clothed about with death; God grant it be not found in the heritage of Jacob, for all such things will be far from the godly, and they will not wallow in their sins. A death-clothed word, impure speech, must not be found among Jacob's heritage, for the godly stand far from such things and do not wallow in sins. — W. O. E. Oesterley

13 COMMENTARY: Do not use your mouth to intemperate swearing, for therein is the word of sin. Intemperate swearing must be avoided because it contains the word of sin. — W. O. E. Oesterley

14 COMMENTARY: Remember your father and mother when you sit among great men; do not be forgetful before them, and so by your custom become a fool, and wish you had not been born, and curse the day of your nativity. Among great men, forgetfulness of parents and loose custom make a man a fool who wishes he had never been born. — W. O. E. Oesterley

15 COMMENTARY: The man accustomed to opprobrious words will never be reformed all the days of his life. Habitual abusive speech hardens beyond reform for life. — W. O. E. Oesterley

16 COMMENTARY: Two sorts of men multiply sin, and the third will bring wrath: a hot mind is as a burning fire, it will never be quenched till it be consumed; a fornicator in the body of his flesh will never cease till he has kindled a fire. Hot temper and fornication multiply sin like unquenchable fire, and a third sort brings wrath. — W. O. E. Oesterley

17 COMMENTARY: All bread is sweet to a whoremonger; he will not leave off till he dies. To the whoremonger all bread tastes sweet, and he does not stop until death. — W. O. E. Oesterley

18 COMMENTARY: A man who breaks wedlock, saying in his heart, who sees me? I am compassed about with darkness, the walls cover me, and nobody sees me; what need I to fear? The Most High will not remember my sins. The adulterer comforts himself with darkness and walls, imagining no one sees and God will not remember. — W. O. E. Oesterley

19 COMMENTARY: Such a man only fears the eyes of men, and does not know that the eyes of the Lord are ten thousand times brighter than the sun, beholding all the ways of men and considering the most secret parts. He fears only human eyes, not knowing God's eyes are ten thousand times brighter than the sun, seeing all ways and secret parts. — W. O. E. Oesterley

20 COMMENTARY: He knew all things before they were created, so also after they were perfected he looked upon them all. God knew all things before creation and looks upon them all after they are made. — W. O. E. Oesterley

21 COMMENTARY: This man will be punished in the streets of the city, and where he suspects not he will be taken. The adulterer will be punished publicly in the streets and seized where he least suspects. — W. O. E. Oesterley

22 COMMENTARY: Thus will it go also with the wife who leaves her husband and brings in an heir by another. The same judgment falls on a wife who leaves her husband and brings in another man's heir. — W. O. E. Oesterley

23 COMMENTARY: For first, she has disobeyed the law of the Most High; secondly, she has trespassed against her own husband; and thirdly, she has played the whore in adultery and brought children by another man. Her guilt is threefold: against God's law, against her husband, and in adulterous children by another man. — W. O. E. Oesterley

24 COMMENTARY: She will be brought out into the congregation, and inquisition will be made of her children. She will be exposed before the congregation, and her children will be investigated. — W. O. E. Oesterley

25 COMMENTARY: Her children will not take root, and her branches will bring forth no fruit. Her children will not take root, and her branches will bear no fruit. — W. O. E. Oesterley

26 COMMENTARY: She will leave her memory to be cursed, and her reproach will not be blotted out. Her memory remains cursed, and her reproach is never blotted out. — W. O. E. Oesterley

27 COMMENTARY: And those who remain will know that there is nothing better than the fear of the Lord, and nothing sweeter than to take heed to the commandments of the Lord. Survivors will learn that nothing is better than fearing God and nothing sweeter than heeding his commandments. — W. O. E. Oesterley

28 COMMENTARY: It is great glory to follow the Lord, and to be received by him is long life. Following the Lord is great glory, and being received by him is long life. — W. O. E. Oesterley
