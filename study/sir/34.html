Study Notes — Sirach

Chapter 34

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: The hopes of a man void of understanding are vain and false, and dreams lift up fools. Understanding-less hopes are vain and false, and dreams puff up fools. — W. O. E. Oesterley

2 COMMENTARY: Whoever regards dreams is like him who catches at a shadow, and follows after the wind. Regarding dreams is catching at shadows and following wind. — W. O. E. Oesterley

3 COMMENTARY: The vision of dreams is the resemblance of one thing to another, even as the likeness of a face to a face. Dream visions merely resemble one thing by another, like face likened to face. — W. O. E. Oesterley

4 COMMENTARY: Of an unclean thing what can be cleansed, and from that thing which is false what truth can come? Nothing clean comes from the unclean, and no truth from falsehood. — W. O. E. Oesterley

5 COMMENTARY: Divinations, soothsayings, and dreams are vain, and the heart fancies, as a woman's heart in travail. Divination, soothsaying, and dreams are vain, and the heart labours in fancy like a woman in travail. — W. O. E. Oesterley

6 COMMENTARY: If they are not sent from the Most High in your visitation, do not set your heart upon them. Dreams and divinations not sent by God in visitation must not capture the heart. — W. O. E. Oesterley

7 COMMENTARY: For dreams have deceived many, and those who put their trust in them have failed. Dreams have deceived many, and trusters in them have failed. — W. O. E. Oesterley

8 COMMENTARY: The law will be found perfect without lies, and wisdom is perfection to a faithful mouth. The law is perfect without lies, and wisdom is perfection to a faithful mouth. — W. O. E. Oesterley

9 COMMENTARY: A man who has travelled knows many things, and he who has much experience will declare wisdom. Travel brings knowledge of many things, and much experience declares wisdom. — W. O. E. Oesterley

10 COMMENTARY: He who has no experience knows little, but he who has travelled is full of prudence. Without experience knowledge is little, while the travelled man is full of prudence. — W. O. E. Oesterley

11 COMMENTARY: When I travelled, I saw many things, and I understand more than I can express. The writer's travels showed him many things, beyond what he can express. — W. O. E. Oesterley

12 COMMENTARY: I was often in danger of death, yet I was delivered because of these things. Often in danger of death, the writer was delivered through these experiences. — W. O. E. Oesterley

13 COMMENTARY: The spirit of those who fear the Lord will live, for their hope is in him who saves them. The God-fearing live in spirit, because their hope is in their Saviour. — W. O. E. Oesterley

14 COMMENTARY: Whoever fears the Lord will not fear nor be afraid, for he is his hope. Fear of God removes other fear, since God himself is the fearer's hope. — W. O. E. Oesterley

15 COMMENTARY: Blessed is the soul of him who fears the Lord; to whom does he look, and who is his strength? The God-fearing soul is blessed, looking to God and strengthened by him. — W. O. E. Oesterley

16 COMMENTARY: The eyes of the Lord are upon those who love him; he is their mighty protection and strong stay, a defence from heat, a cover from the sun at noon, a preservation from stumbling, and a help from falling. God's eyes watch his lovers, and he is their protection, stay, defence from heat, noonday cover, preservation from stumbling, and help from falling. — W. O. E. Oesterley

17 COMMENTARY: He raises up the soul, lightens the eyes, and gives health, life, and blessing. God raises the soul, lightens eyes, and gives health, life, and blessing. — W. O. E. Oesterley

18 COMMENTARY: He who sacrifices of a thing wrongfully gotten, his offering is ridiculous, and the gifts of unjust men are not accepted. Offerings from wrongfully gotten things are ridiculous, and unjust men's gifts are not accepted. — W. O. E. Oesterley

19 COMMENTARY: The Most High is not pleased with the offerings of the wicked, nor is he pacified for sin by the multitude of sacrifices. God is not pleased with wicked offerings, nor pacified by many sacrifices. — W. O. E. Oesterley

20 COMMENTARY: Whoever brings an offering of the goods of the poor does as one who kills the son before his father's eyes. Offering the poor's goods is like killing a son before his father's eyes. — W. O. E. Oesterley

21 COMMENTARY: The bread of the needy is their life; he who defrauds him of it is a man of blood. The needy's bread is their life, and defrauding them of it is bloodshed. — W. O. E. Oesterley

22 COMMENTARY: He who takes away his neighbour's living slays him, and he who defrauds the labourer of his hire is a bloodshedder. Taking a neighbour's living is slaying him, and defrauding a labourer of wages is bloodshed. — W. O. E. Oesterley

23 COMMENTARY: When one builds, and another pulls down, what profit have they then but labour? Building while another pulls down leaves nothing but labour. — W. O. E. Oesterley

24 COMMENTARY: When one prays, and another curses, whose voice will the Lord hear? Prayer against cursing cancels out; which voice will God hear? — W. O. E. Oesterley

25 COMMENTARY: He who washes himself after touching a dead body, if he touches it again, what avails his washing? Washing after touching the dead is void if the dead is touched again. — W. O. E. Oesterley

26 COMMENTARY: So it is with a man who fasts for his sins, and goes again, and does the same: who will hear his prayer, or what does his humbling profit him? Fasting for sins and repeating them is like rewashing after retouching the dead; prayer goes unheard and humbling profits nothing. — W. O. E. Oesterley
