Study Notes — Sirach

Chapter 43

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: The pride of the height, the clear firmament, the beauty of heaven, with his glorious show. The hymn opens on heaven's pride of height, clear firmament, beauty, and glorious show. — W. O. E. Oesterley

2 COMMENTARY: The sun when it appears, declaring at its rising a marvellous instrument, the work of the Most High. The rising sun declares itself a marvellous instrument, the Most High's work. — W. O. E. Oesterley

3 COMMENTARY: At noon it parches the country, and who can abide the burning heat thereof? At noon the sun parches the country, and none can abide its burning heat. — W. O. E. Oesterley

4 COMMENTARY: A man blowing a furnace is in works of heat, but the sun burns the mountains three times more, breathing out fiery vapours, sending forth bright beams, and dimming the eyes. Furnace heat is small beside the sun, which burns mountains threefold, breathes fiery vapours, beams brightly, and dims eyes. — W. O. E. Oesterley

5 COMMENTARY: Great is the Lord who made it, and at his commandment it runs hastily. Great is the sun's Maker, and at his command the sun runs hastily. — W. O. E. Oesterley

6 COMMENTARY: He made the moon also to serve in her season for a declaration of times, and a sign of the world. The moon was made to serve in season, declaring times and signing the world. — W. O. E. Oesterley

7 COMMENTARY: From the moon is the sign of feasts, a light that decreases in her perfection. The moon signs the feasts, a light decreasing even in perfection. — W. O. E. Oesterley

8 COMMENTARY: The month is called after her name, increasing wonderfully in her changing, being an instrument of the armies above, shining in the firmament of heaven. The month takes the moon's name, increasing wonderfully in changing, an instrument of the heavenly armies shining in the firmament. — W. O. E. Oesterley

9 COMMENTARY: The beauty of heaven, the glory of the stars, an ornament giving light in the highest places of the Lord. Stars are heaven's beauty and glory, an ornament lighting the Lord's highest places. — W. O. E. Oesterley

10 COMMENTARY: At the commandment of the Holy One they will stand in their order, and never faint in their watches. At the Holy One's command stars stand in order and never faint in their watches. — W. O. E. Oesterley

11 COMMENTARY: Look upon the rainbow, and praise him who made it; very beautiful it is in the brightness thereof. The rainbow, very beautiful in brightness, should be looked on with praise to its Maker. — W. O. E. Oesterley

12 COMMENTARY: It compasses the heaven about with a glorious circle, and the hands of the Most High have bent it. The rainbow circles heaven gloriously, bent by the Most High's hands. — W. O. E. Oesterley

13 COMMENTARY: By his commandment he makes the snow fall apace, and sends swiftly the lightnings of his judgment. At God's command snow falls apace and judgment's lightnings fly swiftly. — W. O. E. Oesterley

14 COMMENTARY: Through this the treasures are opened, and clouds fly forth as fowls. By this the treasures are opened, and clouds fly like birds. — W. O. E. Oesterley

15 COMMENTARY: By his great power he makes the clouds firm, and the hailstones are broken small. God's great power firms clouds and breaks hailstones small. — W. O. E. Oesterley

16 COMMENTARY: At his sight the mountains are shaken, and at his will the south wind blows. Mountains shake at God's sight, and the south wind blows at his will. — W. O. E. Oesterley

17 COMMENTARY: The noise of the thunder makes the earth tremble; so does the northern storm and the whirlwind; as birds flying he scatters the snow, and its falling down is as the lighting of grasshoppers. Thunder, northern storm, and whirlwind tremble the earth, and snow scatters like flying birds, falling like alighting grasshoppers. — W. O. E. Oesterley

18 COMMENTARY: The eye marvels at the beauty of the whiteness thereof, and the heart is astonished at the raining of it. The eye marvels at snow's white beauty, and the heart is astonished at its raining down. — W. O. E. Oesterley

19 COMMENTARY: The hoarfrost also as salt he pours on the earth, and being congealed, it lies on the top of sharp stakes. Hoarfrost pours like salt on earth, congealing on sharp stakes. — W. O. E. Oesterley

20 COMMENTARY: When the cold north wind blows, and the water is congealed into ice, it abides upon every gathering of water, and clothes the water as with a breastplate. North wind congeals water into ice, clothing every gathering like a breastplate. — W. O. E. Oesterley

21 COMMENTARY: It devours the mountains, and burns the wilderness, and consumes the grass as fire. Cold devours mountains, burns wilderness, and consumes grass like fire. — W. O. E. Oesterley

22 COMMENTARY: A present remedy of all is a mist coming speedily, a dew coming after heat refreshes. Mist speedily and dew after heat are the present remedy refreshing all. — W. O. E. Oesterley

23 COMMENTARY: By his counsel he appeases the deep, and plants islands therein. By God's counsel the deep is appeased and islands planted in it. — W. O. E. Oesterley

24 COMMENTARY: Those who sail on the sea tell of its danger, and when we hear it with our ears, we marvel thereat. Sailors tell of the sea's danger, and hearers marvel at it. — W. O. E. Oesterley

25 COMMENTARY: For therein are strange and wondrous works, variety of all kinds of beasts and whales created. The sea holds strange, wondrous works, varied beasts and whales. — W. O. E. Oesterley

26 COMMENTARY: By him the end of them has prosperous success, and by his word all things consist. By God, sea creatures' ends prosper, and by his word all things consist. — W. O. E. Oesterley

27 COMMENTARY: We may speak much, and yet come short; wherefore in sum, he is all. Much speech still falls short, and in sum, God is all. — W. O. E. Oesterley

28 COMMENTARY: How shall we be able to magnify him? For he is great above all his works. Magnifying God seems impossible, since he is great above all his works. — W. O. E. Oesterley

29 COMMENTARY: The Lord is terrible and very great, and marvellous is his power. The Lord is terrible, very great, and marvellous in power. — W. O. E. Oesterley

30 COMMENTARY: When you glorify the Lord, exalt him as much as you can, for even yet he will far exceed; and when you exalt him, put forth all your strength, and do not be weary, for you can never go far enough. Glorifying God should use all strength without weariness, since he always exceeds our utmost exaltation. — W. O. E. Oesterley

31 COMMENTARY: Who has seen him, that he might tell us? And who can magnify him as he is? No one has seen God to tell of him, and no one can magnify him as he truly is. — W. O. E. Oesterley

32 COMMENTARY: There are yet hidden greater things than these, for we have seen but a few of his works. Greater things remain hidden, since we have seen only a few of God's works. — W. O. E. Oesterley

33 COMMENTARY: For the Lord has made all things, and to the godly he has given wisdom. The Lord made all things, and to the godly he has given wisdom. — W. O. E. Oesterley
