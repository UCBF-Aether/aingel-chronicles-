Study Notes — Sirach

Chapter 26

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Blessed is the man who has a virtuous wife, for the number of his days will be double. A virtuous wife doubles a man's days; blessedness is measured in length of life. — W. O. E. Oesterley

2 COMMENTARY: A virtuous woman rejoices her husband, and he will fulfil the years of his life in peace. A virtuous wife brings her husband joy, and his years are fulfilled in peace. — W. O. E. Oesterley

3 COMMENTARY: A good wife is a good portion, which will be given in the portion of those who fear the Lord. A good wife is a good portion allotted to the God-fearing. — W. O. E. Oesterley

4 COMMENTARY: Whether a man is rich or poor, if he has a good heart toward the Lord, he will at all times rejoice with a cheerful countenance. Rich or poor, a good heart toward God brings constant rejoicing with a cheerful face. — W. O. E. Oesterley

5 COMMENTARY: There are three things my heart fears, and for the fourth I was sore afraid: the slander of a city, the gathering together of an unruly multitude, and a false accusation; all these are worse than death. City slander, unruly mobs, and false accusation are feared as worse than death. — W. O. E. Oesterley

6 COMMENTARY: But a grief of heart and sorrow is a woman jealous over another woman, and a scourge of the tongue which communicates with all. A jealous woman's grief and a tongue that tells everything are heart-sorrow and scourge. — W. O. E. Oesterley

7 COMMENTARY: An evil wife is a yoke shaken to and fro; he who has hold of her is as though he held a scorpion. An evil wife is an unstable yoke, and holding her is like holding a scorpion. — W. O. E. Oesterley

8 COMMENTARY: A drunken woman and a gadder abroad cause great anger, and she will not cover her own shame. A drunken, wandering wife causes great anger and leaves her own shame uncovered. — W. O. E. Oesterley

9 COMMENTARY: The whoredom of a woman may be known in her haughty looks and eyelids. A woman's whoredom shows in haughty looks and eyelids. — W. O. E. Oesterley

10 COMMENTARY: If your daughter is shameless, keep her in straitly, lest she abuse herself through overmuch liberty. A shameless daughter must be kept strictly, lest too much liberty lead her to abuse herself. — W. O. E. Oesterley

11 COMMENTARY: Watch over an impudent eye, and do not marvel if she trespasses against you. An impudent eye must be watched, and her trespass against you should not surprise. — W. O. E. Oesterley

12 COMMENTARY: She will open her mouth, as a thirsty traveller when he has found a fountain, and drink of every water near her; by every hedge she will sit down, and open her quiver against every arrow. The shameless woman drinks at every water like a thirsty traveller and sits by every hedge, open to every arrow. — W. O. E. Oesterley

13 COMMENTARY: The grace of a wife delights her husband, and her discretion will fatten his bones. A wife's grace delights her husband, and her discretion strengthens his very bones. — W. O. E. Oesterley

14 COMMENTARY: A silent and loving woman is a gift of the Lord, and there is nothing so much worth as a mind well instructed. A silent, loving wife is God's gift, and a well-instructed mind is beyond price. — W. O. E. Oesterley

15 COMMENTARY: A shamefaced and faithful woman is a double grace, and her continent mind cannot be valued. Shamefacedness joined to faithfulness is double grace, and a continent mind is invaluable. — W. O. E. Oesterley

16 COMMENTARY: As the sun when it rises in the high heaven, so is the beauty of a good wife in the ordering of her house. A good wife's beauty in ordering her house is like the sun rising in high heaven. — W. O. E. Oesterley

17 COMMENTARY: As the clear light is upon the holy candlestick, so is the beauty of the face in ripe age. Beauty of face in ripe age is like clear light on the holy candlestick. — W. O. E. Oesterley

18 COMMENTARY: As the golden pillars are upon the sockets of silver, so are the fair feet with a constant heart. Fair feet with a constant heart stand like golden pillars on silver sockets. — W. O. E. Oesterley

19 COMMENTARY: My son, keep the flower of your age sound, and do not give your strength to strangers. Youth's flower must be kept sound, and strength must not be given to strangers. — W. O. E. Oesterley

20 COMMENTARY: When you have gotten a fruitful possession through all the field, sow it with your own seed, trusting in the goodness of your stock. A fruitful field should be sown with one's own seed, trusting the goodness of one's stock. — W. O. E. Oesterley

21 COMMENTARY: So your race which you leave will be magnified, having the confidence of their good descent. A race left behind is magnified when it can trust its good descent. — W. O. E. Oesterley

22 COMMENTARY: A harlot will be accounted as spittle, but a married woman is a tower against death to her husband. A harlot counts as spittle, while a married woman is a tower against death for her husband. — W. O. E. Oesterley

23 COMMENTARY: A wicked woman is given as a portion to a wicked man, but a godly woman is given to him who fears the Lord. Portions match character: wicked women to wicked men, godly women to God-fearing men. — W. O. E. Oesterley

24 COMMENTARY: A dishonest woman contemns shame, but an honest woman will reverence her husband. Dishonesty despises shame, while honesty reverences her husband. — W. O. E. Oesterley

25 COMMENTARY: A shameless woman will be counted as a dog, but she who is shamefaced will fear the Lord. Shamelessness counts as a dog, while shamefacedness goes with fear of the Lord. — W. O. E. Oesterley

26 COMMENTARY: A woman who honours her husband will be judged wise by all, but she who dishonours him in her pride will be counted ungodly by all. Honouring a husband earns universal judgment of wisdom, while proud dishonour earns universal judgment of ungodliness. — W. O. E. Oesterley

27 COMMENTARY: A loud crying woman and a scold will be sought out to drive away the enemies. A loud, scolding woman is even pictured as useful to drive away enemies, by sheer noise. — W. O. E. Oesterley

28 COMMENTARY: There are two things that grieve my heart, and the third makes me angry: a man of war who suffers poverty, men of understanding who are not set by, and one who returns from righteousness to sin; the Lord prepares such a one for the sword. A poor warrior, disregarded men of understanding, and an apostate from righteousness grieve and anger, and the last is prepared by God for the sword. — W. O. E. Oesterley

29 COMMENTARY: A merchant will hardly keep himself from doing wrong, and a huckster will not be freed from sin. Trade itself tempts: a merchant hardly avoids wrong, and a huckster is not freed from sin. — W. O. E. Oesterley
