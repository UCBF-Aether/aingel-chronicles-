Study Notes — Sirach

Chapter 44

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Let us now praise famous men, and our fathers who begat us. The praise of famous men begins the roll of Israel's fathers. — W. O. E. Oesterley

2 COMMENTARY: The Lord wrought great glory by them through his great power from the beginning. Their glory was God's working through his great power from the beginning. — W. O. E. Oesterley

3 COMMENTARY: Such as bore rule in their kingdoms, men renowned for power, giving counsel by understanding, and declaring prophecies. The first class praised are rulers renowned for power, counsel by understanding, and prophecy. — W. O. E. Oesterley

4 COMMENTARY: Leaders of the people by their counsels, and by their knowledge of learning meet for the people, wise and eloquent in their instructions. Next come leaders whose counsel, learning, wisdom, and eloquence instructed the people. — W. O. E. Oesterley

5 COMMENTARY: Such as found out musical tunes, and recited verses in writing. Musicians who found out tunes and recited written verses are also among the famous. — W. O. E. Oesterley

6 COMMENTARY: Rich men furnished with ability, living peaceably in their habitations. Rich men of ability living peaceably at home complete the catalogue of the honoured. — W. O. E. Oesterley

7 COMMENTARY: All these were honoured in their generations, and were the glory of their times. All these classes were honoured in their generations and were their times' glory. — W. O. E. Oesterley

8 COMMENTARY: There are some of them who have left a name behind them, that their praises might be reported. Some famous men left a name, so that their praises are reported. — W. O. E. Oesterley

9 COMMENTARY: And some there are who have no memorial, who have perished as though they had never been, and are become as though they had never been born, and their children after them. Others have no memorial and perished as if never born, with their children after them. — W. O. E. Oesterley

10 COMMENTARY: But these were merciful men, whose righteousness has not been forgotten. In contrast, the merciful men's righteousness has not been forgotten. — W. O. E. Oesterley

11 COMMENTARY: With their seed will continually remain a good inheritance, and their children are within the covenant. A good inheritance remains with their seed continually, and their children stand within the covenant. — W. O. E. Oesterley

12 COMMENTARY: Their seed stands fast, and their children for their sakes. Their seed stands fast, and their children stand for their fathers' sakes. — W. O. E. Oesterley

13 COMMENTARY: Their seed will remain for ever, and their glory will not be blotted out. Their seed remains for ever, and their glory is never blotted out. — W. O. E. Oesterley

14 COMMENTARY: Their bodies are buried in peace, but their name lives for evermore. Buried in peace in body, their names live for evermore. — W. O. E. Oesterley

15 COMMENTARY: The people will tell of their wisdom, and the congregation will show forth their praise. People tell their wisdom, and the congregation proclaims their praise. — W. O. E. Oesterley

16 COMMENTARY: Enoch pleased the Lord, and was translated, being an example of repentance to all generations. Enoch, pleasing God and translated, stands as an example of repentance to all generations. — W. O. E. Oesterley

17 COMMENTARY: Noah was found perfect and righteous; in the time of wrath he was taken in exchange for the world; therefore he was left as a remnant to the earth, when the flood came. Noah, perfect and righteous, was taken in exchange for the world in wrath's time and left as earth's remnant when the flood came. — W. O. E. Oesterley

18 COMMENTARY: An everlasting covenant was made with him, that all flesh should perish no more by the flood. God's everlasting covenant with Noah promised no more destruction of all flesh by flood. — W. O. E. Oesterley

19 COMMENTARY: Abraham was a great father of many people; in glory there was none like him. Abraham, great father of many peoples, had no equal in glory. — W. O. E. Oesterley

20 COMMENTARY: He kept the law of the Most High, and was in covenant with him; he established the covenant in his flesh, and when he was proved, he was found faithful. Abraham kept God's law, stood in covenant, established it in circumcised flesh, and when proved was found faithful. — W. O. E. Oesterley

21 COMMENTARY: Therefore he assured him by an oath, that he would bless the nations in his seed, multiply him as dust of the earth, exalt his seed as the stars, and cause them to inherit from sea to sea, and from the river to the utmost part of the land. God's oath promised blessing to nations in Abraham's seed, multiplication as dust, exaltation as stars, and inheritance from sea to sea and river to land's end. — W. O. E. Oesterley

22 COMMENTARY: With Isaac he established likewise, for Abraham his father's sake, the blessing of all men, and the covenant, and made it rest upon the head of Jacob. He acknowledged him in his blessing, gave him a heritage, and divided his portions; among the twelve tribes he parted them. Isaac received the same blessing and covenant for Abraham's sake, and it rested on Jacob's head, acknowledged in blessing, given heritage, and divided among the twelve tribes. — W. O. E. Oesterley
