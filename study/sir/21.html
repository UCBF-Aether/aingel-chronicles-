Study Notes — Sirach

Chapter 21

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: My son, have you sinned? Do so no more, but ask pardon for your former sins. The answer to sin is twofold: stop, and ask pardon for what is past. — W. O. E. Oesterley

2 COMMENTARY: Flee from sin as from the face of a serpent, for if you come too near it, it will bite you; its teeth are as the teeth of a lion, slaying the souls of men. Sin must be fled like a serpent's face, since nearness means a bite, and its teeth are lion's teeth that slay souls. — W. O. E. Oesterley

3 COMMENTARY: All iniquity is as a two-edged sword, whose wounds cannot be healed. Iniquity cuts both ways like a two-edged sword, and its wounds cannot be healed. — W. O. E. Oesterley

4 COMMENTARY: To terrify and do wrong will waste riches; thus the house of proud men will be made desolate. Terror and wrongdoing waste riches, and proud houses end desolate. — W. O. E. Oesterley

5 COMMENTARY: A prayer out of a poor man's mouth reaches to the ears of God, and his judgment comes speedily. A poor man's prayer reaches God's ears, and God's judgment on his behalf comes swiftly. — W. O. E. Oesterley

6 COMMENTARY: He who hates to be reproved is in the way of sinners, but he who fears the Lord will repent from his heart. Hatred of reproof marks the sinner's way, while fear of God leads to heartfelt repentance. — W. O. E. Oesterley

7 COMMENTARY: An eloquent man is known far and near, but a man of understanding knows when he slips. Eloquence spreads a name far and near, but understanding knows its own slips. — W. O. E. Oesterley

8 COMMENTARY: He who builds his house with other men's money is like one who gathers stones for the tomb of his burial. A house built on others' money is like gathering stones for one's own tomb. — W. O. E. Oesterley

9 COMMENTARY: The congregation of the wicked is like tow wrapped together, and the end of them is a flame of fire to destroy them. Wicked congregations are like bundled tow, and their end is a destroying flame. — W. O. E. Oesterley

10 COMMENTARY: The way of sinners is made plain with stones, but at the end thereof is the pit of hell. Sinners' way looks smoothly paved, but it ends in the pit of hell. — W. O. E. Oesterley

11 COMMENTARY: He who keeps the law of the Lord gets the understanding thereof, and the perfection of the fear of the Lord is wisdom. Law-keeping brings understanding of the law, and perfected fear of God is wisdom itself. — W. O. E. Oesterley

12 COMMENTARY: He who is not wise will not be taught, but there is a wisdom which multiplies bitterness. The unwise refuse teaching, yet there is a kind of wisdom that only multiplies bitterness. — W. O. E. Oesterley

13 COMMENTARY: The knowledge of a wise man will abound like a flood, and his counsel is like a pure fountain of life. A wise man's knowledge overflows like a flood, and his counsel is a pure fountain of life. — W. O. E. Oesterley

14 COMMENTARY: The inner parts of a fool are like a broken vessel, and he will hold no knowledge as long as he lives. A fool's inward parts are a broken vessel that can hold no knowledge for life. — W. O. E. Oesterley

15 COMMENTARY: If a skilful man hears a wise word, he will commend it and add to it; but as soon as one of no understanding hears it, it displeases him, and he casts it behind his back. The skilful receive a wise word with praise and addition, while the uncomprehending are displeased and throw it behind their backs. — W. O. E. Oesterley

16 COMMENTARY: The talking of a fool is like a burden in the way, but grace will be found in the lips of the wise. A fool's talk is a burden on the road, while grace is found on wise lips. — W. O. E. Oesterley

17 COMMENTARY: They enquire at the mouth of the wise man in the congregation, and they will ponder his words in their heart. The congregation seeks the wise man's mouth, and his words are pondered in the heart. — W. O. E. Oesterley

18 COMMENTARY: As is a house that is destroyed, so is wisdom to a fool, and the knowledge of the unwise is as talk without sense. Wisdom is to a fool like a destroyed house, useless, and the unwise man's knowledge is senseless talk. — W. O. E. Oesterley

19 COMMENTARY: Doctrine to fools is as fetters on the feet, and like manacles on the right hand. Teaching feels to fools like fetters and manacles, restraint rather than freedom. — W. O. E. Oesterley

20 COMMENTARY: A fool lifts up his voice with laughter, but a wise man scarcely smiles a little. Fools laugh loudly, while the wise barely smile. — W. O. E. Oesterley

21 COMMENTARY: Learning is to a wise man as an ornament of gold, and like a bracelet upon his right arm. To the wise, learning is gold ornament and a bracelet on the right arm, worn honourably. — W. O. E. Oesterley

22 COMMENTARY: A foolish man's foot is soon in his neighbour's house, but a man of experience is ashamed of him. Fools hurry into neighbours' houses, while experienced men are ashamed of such intrusion. — W. O. E. Oesterley

23 COMMENTARY: A fool will peep in at the door into the house, but he who is well nurtured will stand without. Peeping in at doors is folly; the well-nurtured stand outside. — W. O. E. Oesterley

24 COMMENTARY: It is the rudeness of a man to hearken at the door, but a wise man will be grieved with the disgrace. Listening at doors is rudeness, and the wise are grieved by such disgrace. — W. O. E. Oesterley

25 COMMENTARY: The lips of talkers will be telling things that do not pertain to them, but the words of those who have understanding are weighed in the balance. Talkers chatter about what is not theirs, while understanding men weigh their words in the balance. — W. O. E. Oesterley

26 COMMENTARY: The heart of fools is in their mouth, but the mouth of the wise is in their heart. Fools speak from the heart's surface, their heart in their mouth, while the wise speak from a heart that governs the mouth. — W. O. E. Oesterley

27 COMMENTARY: When the ungodly curses Satan, he curses his own soul. The ungodly man's curse on Satan falls back as a curse on his own soul. — W. O. E. Oesterley

28 COMMENTARY: A whisperer defiles his own soul, and is hated wherever he dwells. Whispering defiles the whisperer's own soul, and he is hated in every place he lives. — W. O. E. Oesterley
