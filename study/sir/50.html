Study Notes — Sirach

Chapter 50

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Simon the high priest, son of Onias, who in his life repaired the house again, and in his days fortified the temple. Simon son of Onias repaired the house in his life and fortified the temple in his days. — W. O. E. Oesterley

2 COMMENTARY: By him was built from the foundation the double height, the high fortress of the wall about the temple. Simon built from the foundation the double height, the high fortress wall about the temple. — W. O. E. Oesterley

3 COMMENTARY: In his days the cistern to receive water, being in compass as the sea, was covered with plates of brass. In Simon's days the sea-like water cistern was covered with brass plates. — W. O. E. Oesterley

4 COMMENTARY: He took care of the temple that it should not fall, and fortified the city against besieging. Simon cared for the temple against falling, and fortified the city against siege. — W. O. E. Oesterley

5 COMMENTARY: How he was honoured in the midst of the people in his coming out of the sanctuary! Simon was honoured among the people as he came out of the sanctuary. — W. O. E. Oesterley

6 COMMENTARY: He was as the morning star in the midst of a cloud, and as the moon at the full. Simon shone like the morning star amid cloud, and like the full moon. — W. O. E. Oesterley

7 COMMENTARY: As the sun shining upon the temple of the Most High, and as the rainbow giving light in the bright clouds. He was like the sun on God's temple, and the rainbow lighting bright clouds. — W. O. E. Oesterley

8 COMMENTARY: As the flower of roses in the spring of the year, as lilies by the rivers of waters, and as the branches of the frankincense tree in the time of summer. He was like spring roses, riverside lilies, and summer frankincense branches. — W. O. E. Oesterley

9 COMMENTARY: As fire and incense in the censer, and as a vessel of beaten gold set with all manner of precious stones. He was like fire and incense in the censer, and a beaten-gold vessel set with precious stones. — W. O. E. Oesterley

10 COMMENTARY: As a fair olive tree budding forth fruit, and as a cypress tree growing up to the clouds. He was like a fruiting fair olive, and a cypress growing to the clouds. — W. O. E. Oesterley

11 COMMENTARY: When he put on the robe of honour, and was clothed with the perfection of glory, when he went up to the holy altar, he made the garment of holiness honourable. Robed in honour and perfect glory, going up to the holy altar, Simon made holiness' garment honourable. — W. O. E. Oesterley

12 COMMENTARY: When he took the portions out of the priests' hands, he himself stood by the hearth of the altar, compassed about, as a young cedar in Libanus, and as palm trees compassed they him round about. Taking portions from priests' hands, Simon stood by the altar hearth, ringed like a young Lebanon cedar among palms. — W. O. E. Oesterley

13 COMMENTARY: So were all the sons of Aaron in their glory, and the oblations of the Lord in their hands, before all the congregation of Israel. So Aaron's sons stood in glory, with the Lord's oblations in hand, before all Israel's congregation. — W. O. E. Oesterley

14 COMMENTARY: Finishing the service at the altar, that he might adorn the offering of the Most High Almighty. Finishing altar service, Simon adorned the offering of the Most High Almighty. — W. O. E. Oesterley

15 COMMENTARY: He stretched out his hand to the cup, and poured of the blood of the grape; he poured out at the foot of the altar a sweet-smelling savour to the Most High King of all. He stretched hand to the cup, pouring grape's blood at the altar foot as sweet savour to the Most High King. — W. O. E. Oesterley

16 COMMENTARY: Then shouted the sons of Aaron, and sounded the silver trumpets, and made a great noise to be heard, for a remembrance before the Most High. Aaron's sons shouted and sounded silver trumpets, making great noise for remembrance before God. — W. O. E. Oesterley

17 COMMENTARY: Then all the people together hasted, and fell down to the earth upon their faces to worship their Lord God Almighty, the Most High. All the people hastened and fell face-down to worship the Lord God Almighty. — W. O. E. Oesterley

18 COMMENTARY: The singers also sang praises with their voices, with great variety of sounds was there made sweet melody. Singers sang praises with varied voices, making sweet melody. — W. O. E. Oesterley

19 COMMENTARY: The people besought the Lord, the Most High, by prayer before him who is merciful, till the solemnity of the Lord was ended, and they had finished his service. The people prayed to the merciful Most High until the solemnity ended and service finished. — W. O. E. Oesterley

20 COMMENTARY: Then he went down, and lifted up his hands over the whole congregation of the children of Israel, to give the blessing of the Lord with his lips, and to rejoice in his name. Coming down, Simon lifted hands over all Israel's congregation, blessing with his lips and rejoicing in God's name. — W. O. E. Oesterley

21 COMMENTARY: They bowed themselves down to worship the second time, that they might receive a blessing from the Most High. The people bowed again in worship, to receive blessing from the Most High. — W. O. E. Oesterley

22 COMMENTARY: Now therefore bless the God of all, who only does wondrous things everywhere, who exalts our days from the womb, and deals with us according to his mercy. Therefore bless the God of all, who alone does wonders everywhere, exalting our days from the womb and dealing by mercy. — W. O. E. Oesterley

23 COMMENTARY: He grant us joyfulness of heart, and that peace may be in our days in Israel for ever. May he grant joyful hearts, and peace in Israel's days for ever. — W. O. E. Oesterley

24 COMMENTARY: That he would confirm his mercy with us, and deliver us at his time! May he confirm his mercy with us, and deliver us in his time. — W. O. E. Oesterley

25 COMMENTARY: There are two manner of nations which my heart abhors, and the third is no nation. Two kinds of nations the writer's heart abhors, and a third that is no nation. — W. O. E. Oesterley

26 COMMENTARY: They who sit upon the mountain of Samaria, and they who dwell among the Philistines, and that foolish people who dwell in Sichem. They are Samaria's mountain-dwellers, Philistine-dwellers, and the foolish people of Shechem. — W. O. E. Oesterley

27 COMMENTARY: Jesus son of Sirach of Jerusalem has written in this book the instruction of understanding and knowledge, who out of his heart poured forth wisdom. Jesus son of Sirach of Jerusalem wrote this book's instruction of understanding and knowledge, pouring wisdom from his heart. — W. O. E. Oesterley

28 COMMENTARY: Blessed is he who will be exercised in these things, and he who lays them up in his heart will become wise. Blessed is the one exercised in these things, and laying them up in heart becomes wise. — W. O. E. Oesterley

29 COMMENTARY: For if he does them, he will be strong to all things, for the light of the Lord leads him, who gives wisdom to the godly. Blessed be the name of the Lord for ever. Amen, Amen. Doing these things makes a man strong for all things, led by the Lord's light, who gives wisdom to the godly; blessed be God's name for ever. Amen. — W. O. E. Oesterley
