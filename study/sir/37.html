Study Notes — Sirach

Chapter 37

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Every friend says, I am his friend also, but there is a friend who is only a friend in name. Many claim friendship, but some are friends in name only. — W. O. E. Oesterley

2 COMMENTARY: Is it not a grief unto death, when a companion and friend is turned to an enemy? A companion and friend turned enemy is grief unto death. — W. O. E. Oesterley

3 COMMENTARY: O wicked imagination, whence did you come in to cover the earth with deceit? Wicked imagination, covering the earth with deceit, is cried out against as an intruder. — W. O. E. Oesterley

4 COMMENTARY: There is a companion who rejoices in the prosperity of a friend, but in the time of trouble will be against him. Some companions share prosperity's joy but oppose in trouble. — W. O. E. Oesterley

5 COMMENTARY: There is a companion who helps his friend for the belly, and takes up the buckler against the enemy. Some companions help for the belly's sake and even take up shield against the enemy, yet for selfish reasons. — W. O. E. Oesterley

6 COMMENTARY: Do not forget your friend in your mind, and do not be unmindful of him in your riches. Friends must be remembered in mind and in riches alike. — W. O. E. Oesterley

7 COMMENTARY: Every counsellor extols counsel, but there is some who counsel for himself. All counsellors praise counsel, but some counsel for their own advantage. — W. O. E. Oesterley

8 COMMENTARY: Beware of a counsellor, and know before what need he has, for he will counsel for himself, lest he cast the lot upon you. A counsellor's own need must be known first, lest his self-serving counsel cast the lot upon you. — W. O. E. Oesterley

9 COMMENTARY: And say to you, your way is good, and afterward he stands on the other side, to see what will befall you. The self-serving counsellor approves your way, then stands aside to watch what befalls you. — W. O. E. Oesterley

10 COMMENTARY: Do not consult with one who suspects you, and hide your counsel from those who envy you. Counsels must not be shared with the suspicious or the envious. — W. O. E. Oesterley

11 COMMENTARY: Neither consult with a woman about her of whom she is jealous, nor with a coward in matters of war, nor with a merchant concerning exchange, nor with a buyer about selling, nor with an envious man about thankfulness, nor with an unmerciful man about kindness, nor with the slothful for any work, nor with a hireling for a year about finishing work, nor with an idle servant about much business; do not hearken to these in any matter of counsel. Counsel must be sought from the competent and disinterested, not from those whose interests or characters bias them in the matter at hand. — W. O. E. Oesterley

12 COMMENTARY: But be continually with a godly man, whom you know to keep the commandments of the Lord, whose mind is according to your mind, and who will sorrow with you if you miscarry. The right counsellor is a godly commandment-keeper, like-minded, who sorrows with you in failure. — W. O. E. Oesterley

13 COMMENTARY: Let the counsel of your own heart stand, for there is no man more faithful to you than it. Your own heart's counsel stands surest, since no man is more faithful to you than it. — W. O. E. Oesterley

14 COMMENTARY: A man's mind is sometimes wont to tell him more than seven watchmen sitting above in a high tower. One's own mind can warn more than seven watchmen in a high tower. — W. O. E. Oesterley

15 COMMENTARY: Above all this pray to the Most High, that he will direct your way in truth. Above all counsel, prayer to God to direct the way in truth is required. — W. O. E. Oesterley

16 COMMENTARY: Let reason go before every enterprise, and counsel before every action. Reason should precede enterprise, and counsel precede action. — W. O. E. Oesterley

17 COMMENTARY: The countenance is a sign of changing of the heart. The face signals the heart's changes. — W. O. E. Oesterley

18 COMMENTARY: Four manner of things appear: good and evil, life and death; but the tongue rules over them continually. Good and evil, life and death appear as four things, but the tongue rules over them continually. — W. O. E. Oesterley

19 COMMENTARY: There is one who is wise and teaches many, and yet is unprofitable to himself. A man can teach many wisely yet profit nothing himself. — W. O. E. Oesterley

20 COMMENTARY: There is one who shows wisdom in words, and is hated; he will be destitute of all food. Wisdom shown in words without grace earns hatred and leaves its speaker destitute. — W. O. E. Oesterley

21 COMMENTARY: For grace is not given him from the Lord, because he is deprived of all wisdom. Such a man lacks grace from God because he is deprived of true wisdom. — W. O. E. Oesterley

22 COMMENTARY: Another is wise to himself, and the fruits of understanding are commendable in his mouth. Another man is wise for himself, and understanding's fruits are commendable in his mouth. — W. O. E. Oesterley

23 COMMENTARY: A wise man instructs his people, and the fruits of his understanding do not fail. A wise man's instruction of his people bears unfailing fruit of understanding. — W. O. E. Oesterley

24 COMMENTARY: A wise man will be filled with blessing, and all who see him will count him happy. A wise man is filled with blessing, and all beholders count him happy. — W. O. E. Oesterley

25 COMMENTARY: The days of a man's life may be numbered, but the days of Israel are innumerable. A man's days are numbered, but Israel's days are innumerable. — W. O. E. Oesterley

26 COMMENTARY: A wise man will inherit glory among his people, and his name will be perpetual. A wise man inherits glory among his people, and his name endures perpetually. — W. O. E. Oesterley

27 COMMENTARY: My son, prove your soul in your life, and see what is evil for it, and do not give that to it. The soul should be tested in life, its evils identified, and those things withheld from it. — W. O. E. Oesterley

28 COMMENTARY: All things are not profitable for all men, nor does every soul have pleasure in everything. Not all things profit all men, and not every soul takes pleasure in everything. — W. O. E. Oesterley

29 COMMENTARY: Do not be unsatiable in any dainty thing, nor too greedy upon meats. Dainties and meats must not be pursued insatiably or greedily. — W. O. E. Oesterley

30 COMMENTARY: Excess of meats brings sickness, and surfeiting will turn into choler. Excess eating brings sickness, and surfeiting turns to choler. — W. O. E. Oesterley

31 COMMENTARY: By surfeiting many have perished, but he who takes heed prolongs his life. Surfeiting has killed many, while heedfulness prolongs life. — W. O. E. Oesterley
