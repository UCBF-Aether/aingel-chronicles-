Study Notes — Sirach

Chapter 47

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: After him rose up Nathan to prophesy in the time of David. After Samuel, Nathan rose to prophesy in David's time. — W. O. E. Oesterley

2 COMMENTARY: As the fat is taken away from the peace offering, so was David chosen out of the children of Israel. David was chosen from Israel as the fat is set apart from the peace offering. — W. O. E. Oesterley

3 COMMENTARY: He played with lions as with kids, and with bears as with lambs. David played with lions as with kids, and bears as with lambs. — W. O. E. Oesterley

4 COMMENTARY: Did he not slay a giant when he was yet but young? And did he not take away reproach from the people, when he lifted up his hand with the stone in the sling, and beat down the boasting of Goliath? Young David slew the giant, removing reproach from the people by sling stone, beating down Goliath's boasting. — W. O. E. Oesterley

5 COMMENTARY: For he called upon the Most High Lord, and he gave him strength in his right hand to slay that mighty warrior, and set up the horn of his people. David called on God, who strengthened his right hand to slay the warrior and raise his people's horn. — W. O. E. Oesterley

6 COMMENTARY: So the people honoured him with ten thousands, and praised him in the blessings of the Lord, in that he gave him a crown of glory. The people honoured David with ten thousands and praised him in God's blessings, for God gave him a crown of glory. — W. O. E. Oesterley

7 COMMENTARY: For he destroyed the enemies on every side, and brought to nought the Philistines his adversaries, and broke their horn in sunder to this day. David destroyed surrounding enemies, brought the Philistines to nought, and broke their horn to this day. — W. O. E. Oesterley

8 COMMENTARY: In all his works he praised the Holy One Most High with words of glory; with his whole heart he sang songs, and loved him who made him. In all his works David praised God with glorious words, singing with his whole heart and loving his Maker. — W. O. E. Oesterley

9 COMMENTARY: He set singers also before the altar, that by their voices they might make sweet melody, and daily sing praises in their songs. David set singers before the altar for sweet melody and daily praise songs. — W. O. E. Oesterley

10 COMMENTARY: He beautified their feasts, and set in order the solemn times until the end, that they might praise his holy name, and that the temple might sound from morning. David beautified feasts and ordered solemn times to the end, so God's holy name was praised and the temple sounded from morning. — W. O. E. Oesterley

11 COMMENTARY: The Lord took away his sins, and exalted his horn for ever; he gave him a covenant of kings, and a throne of glory in Israel. God took away David's sins, exalted his horn for ever, and gave him a kings' covenant and a throne of glory in Israel. — W. O. E. Oesterley

12 COMMENTARY: After him rose up a wise son, and for his sake he dwelt at large. After David rose a wise son, Solomon, and for his sake Israel dwelt at large. — W. O. E. Oesterley

13 COMMENTARY: Solomon reigned in a peaceable time, and was honoured, for God made all quiet round about him, that he might build a house in his name, and prepare his sanctuary for ever. Solomon reigned in peace, honoured, with God quieting all around so he could build God's house and prepare the sanctuary for ever. — W. O. E. Oesterley

14 COMMENTARY: How wise you were in your youth, and as a flood, filled with understanding! Solomon's youthful wisdom is praised: like a flood, filled with understanding. — W. O. E. Oesterley

15 COMMENTARY: Your soul covered the whole earth, and you filled it with dark parables. Solomon's soul covered the earth, filling it with dark parables. — W. O. E. Oesterley

16 COMMENTARY: Your name went far to the islands, and for your peace you were beloved. Solomon's name reached far islands, and his peace made him beloved. — W. O. E. Oesterley

17 COMMENTARY: The countries marvelled at you for your songs, proverbs, parables, and interpretations. Countries marvelled at Solomon's songs, proverbs, parables, and interpretations. — W. O. E. Oesterley

18 COMMENTARY: By the name of the Lord God, called the Lord God of Israel, you gathered gold as tin and multiplied silver as lead. By God's name Solomon gathered gold like tin and multiplied silver like lead. — W. O. E. Oesterley

19 COMMENTARY: You bowed your loins to women, and by your body you were brought into subjection. Solomon bowed his loins to women, and by his body was brought into subjection. — W. O. E. Oesterley

20 COMMENTARY: You stained your honour, and polluted your seed, so that you brought wrath upon your children, and were grieved for your folly. Solomon stained his honour, polluted his seed, brought wrath on his children, and grieved over his folly. — W. O. E. Oesterley

21 COMMENTARY: So the kingdom was divided, and out of Ephraim ruled a rebellious kingdom. The kingdom divided, and a rebellious kingdom ruled out of Ephraim. — W. O. E. Oesterley

22 COMMENTARY: But the Lord will never leave off his mercy, nor will any of his works perish, nor will he abolish the posterity of his elect, and the seed of him who loves him he will not take away; therefore he gave a remnant to Jacob, and out of him a root to David. God's mercy never ceases, his works do not perish, and his elect's posterity is not abolished; so he gave Jacob a remnant and David a root. — W. O. E. Oesterley

23 COMMENTARY: Thus rested Solomon with his fathers, and of his seed he left behind him Roboam, even the foolishness of the people, and one who had no understanding, who turned away the people through his counsel. There was also Jeroboam son of Nebat, who caused Israel to sin, and showed Ephraim the way of sin. Solomon rested with his fathers, leaving Rehoboam, the people's folly, a man without understanding who turned the people away by his counsel, and Jeroboam son of Nebat, who caused Israel to sin and showed Ephraim sin's way. — W. O. E. Oesterley

24 COMMENTARY: And their sins were multiplied exceedingly, that they were driven out of the land. Their sins multiplied exceedingly until they were driven out of the land. — W. O. E. Oesterley

25 COMMENTARY: For they sought out all wickedness, till the vengeance came upon them. They sought out all wickedness until vengeance came upon them. — W. O. E. Oesterley
