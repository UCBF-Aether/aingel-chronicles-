Study Notes — Sirach

Chapter 46

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Jesus son of Nave was valiant in the wars, and was the successor of Moses in prophecies, who according to his name was made great for the saving of the elect of God, taking vengeance on the enemies who rose up against them, that he might set Israel in their inheritance. Joshua son of Nun, valiant in war and Moses' prophetic successor, was made great, as his name implies, to save God's elect, avenge their enemies, and settle Israel in its inheritance. — W. O. E. Oesterley

2 COMMENTARY: How great glory he got, when he lifted up his hands, and stretched out his sword against the cities! Great glory came to Joshua when he lifted hands and stretched sword against the cities. — W. O. E. Oesterley

3 COMMENTARY: Who before him so stood to it? For the Lord himself brought his enemies to him. None before Joshua stood so firmly, because the Lord himself brought his enemies to him. — W. O. E. Oesterley

4 COMMENTARY: Did not the sun go back by his means? And was not one day as long as two? By Joshua's means the sun went back, and one day became as long as two. — W. O. E. Oesterley

5 COMMENTARY: He called upon the Most High Lord, when the enemies pressed upon him on every side, and the great Lord heard him. Pressed by enemies on every side, Joshua called on the Most High, and the great Lord heard him. — W. O. E. Oesterley

6 COMMENTARY: With hailstones of mighty power he made the battle fall violently upon the nations, and in the descent of Beth-horon he destroyed those who resisted, that the nations might know all their strength, because he fought in the sight of the Lord, and followed the Mighty One. Mighty hailstones fell violently on the nations, and at Beth-horon's descent resisters were destroyed, so nations might know their strength was nothing, since Joshua fought before God and followed the Mighty One. — W. O. E. Oesterley

7 COMMENTARY: In the time of Moses also he did a work of mercy, he and Caleb son of Jephunne, in that they withstood the congregation, withheld the people from sin, and appeased the wicked murmuring. With Caleb, Joshua did mercy in Moses' time, withstanding the congregation, withholding the people from sin, and appeasing wicked murmuring. — W. O. E. Oesterley

8 COMMENTARY: Of six hundred thousand people on foot, they two were preserved to bring them into the heritage, even to the land flowing with milk and honey. Of six hundred thousand footmen, only Joshua and Caleb were preserved to bring the people into the milk-and-honey heritage. — W. O. E. Oesterley

9 COMMENTARY: The Lord gave strength also to Caleb, who remained with him to his old age, so that he entered upon the high places of the land, and his seed obtained it for a heritage. Caleb, remaining faithful to old age, was strengthened to enter the land's high places, and his seed inherited them. — W. O. E. Oesterley

10 COMMENTARY: That all the children of Israel might see that it is good to follow the Lord. Caleb's reward shows all Israel that following the Lord is good. — W. O. E. Oesterley

11 COMMENTARY: And concerning the judges, every one by name, whose heart did not go whoring, nor depart from the Lord, let their memory be blessed. The judges, named one by one, whose hearts did not whore after idols nor depart from God, are blessed in memory. — W. O. E. Oesterley

12 COMMENTARY: Let their bones flourish out of their place, and let the name of those who were honoured be continued upon their children. The judges' bones should flourish from their place, and honoured names continue upon their children. — W. O. E. Oesterley

13 COMMENTARY: Samuel, the prophet of the Lord, beloved of his Lord, established a kingdom, and anointed princes over his people. Samuel, God's beloved prophet, established a kingdom and anointed princes over the people. — W. O. E. Oesterley

14 COMMENTARY: By the law of the Lord he judged the congregation, and the Lord had respect to Jacob. Samuel judged the congregation by God's law, and God had respect to Jacob. — W. O. E. Oesterley

15 COMMENTARY: By his faithfulness he was found a true prophet, and by his word he was known to be faithful in vision. Samuel's faithfulness proved him a true prophet, and his word showed faithfulness in vision. — W. O. E. Oesterley

16 COMMENTARY: He called upon the mighty Lord, when his enemies pressed upon him on every side, when he offered the sucking lamb. Pressed by enemies, Samuel called on the mighty Lord while offering a sucking lamb. — W. O. E. Oesterley

17 COMMENTARY: The Lord thundered from heaven, and with a great noise made his voice to be heard. God answered with thunder from heaven and a great voice. — W. O. E. Oesterley

18 COMMENTARY: He destroyed the rulers of the Tyrians, and all the princes of the Philistines. God destroyed Tyrian rulers and all Philistine princes. — W. O. E. Oesterley

19 COMMENTARY: Before his long sleep he made protestations in the sight of the Lord and his anointed, I have not taken any man's goods, so much as a shoe, and no man accused him. Before death Samuel protested before God and his anointed that he had taken no man's goods, not even a shoe, and none accused him. — W. O. E. Oesterley

20 COMMENTARY: After his death he prophesied, and showed the king his end, and lifted up his voice from the earth in prophecy, to blot out the wickedness of the people. Even after death Samuel prophesied, showing the king his end and lifting his voice from the earth to blot out the people's wickedness. — W. O. E. Oesterley
