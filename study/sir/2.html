Study Notes — Sirach

Chapter 2

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Whoever comes to serve the Lord should prepare his soul for temptation. Service of God is not an escape from trial but an entry into it, and readiness is part of the service. — W. O. E. Oesterley

2 COMMENTARY: Set your heart aright, endure constantly, and do not make haste in time of trouble. A steadied heart and patient endurance, not panic, are the right response when trouble comes. — W. O. E. Oesterley

3 COMMENTARY: Cleave to the Lord and do not depart, so that you may be increased at your last end. Holding fast through trial is what brings growth, and the increase is seen at the end of life. — W. O. E. Oesterley

4 COMMENTARY: Take cheerfully whatever is brought upon you, and be patient when you are changed to a low estate. Losses of standing are to be accepted without resentment, as part of the discipline. — W. O. E. Oesterley

5 COMMENTARY: Gold is tried in fire, and acceptable men in the furnace of adversity. Hardship is the assay that proves whether a person is genuine, as fire proves gold. — W. O. E. Oesterley

6 COMMENTARY: Believe in him and he will help you; order your way aright and trust in him. Trust is shown in an ordered life, and God's help meets those who both believe and walk straight. — W. O. E. Oesterley

7 COMMENTARY: You who fear the Lord, wait for his mercy and do not go aside, lest you fall. Waiting on God's mercy, rather than turning off the path, is what keeps a person from falling. — W. O. E. Oesterley

8 COMMENTARY: You who fear the Lord, believe him, and your reward will not fail. Trust in God's word carries a sure reward; it is not a gamble. — W. O. E. Oesterley

9 COMMENTARY: You who fear the Lord, hope for good, and for everlasting joy and mercy. The hope held out to the God-fearing is not temporary relief but lasting joy and mercy. — W. O. E. Oesterley

10 COMMENTARY: Look at the generations of old: did anyone trust in the Lord and was confounded, or abide in his fear and was forsaken, or call on him and was despised? History itself is the evidence that trust in God has never yet ended in shame. — W. O. E. Oesterley

11 COMMENTARY: The Lord is full of compassion and mercy, longsuffering and very pitiful, forgiving sins and saving in time of affliction. God's character is the ground of that history: he is slow to anger, ready to forgive, and present in trouble. — W. O. E. Oesterley

12 COMMENTARY: Woe to fearful hearts, faint hands, and the sinner who goes two ways. A divided, cowardly course earns woe because it is neither trust nor obedience. — W. O. E. Oesterley

13 COMMENTARY: Woe to the fainthearted, for he does not believe and therefore will not be defended. Unbelief leaves a person without the defence that trust would have secured. — W. O. E. Oesterley

14 COMMENTARY: Woe to those who have lost patience; what will they do when the Lord visits them? Impatience now leaves nothing to stand on when God comes to examine a life. — W. O. E. Oesterley

15 COMMENTARY: Those who fear the Lord will not disobey his word, and those who love him will keep his ways. Fear shows itself in obedience and love in keeping God's ways. — W. O. E. Oesterley

16 COMMENTARY: Those who fear the Lord will seek what is well-pleasing to him, and those who love him will be filled with the law. Reverence aims at pleasing God, and love fills a life with his law rather than with itself. — W. O. E. Oesterley

17 COMMENTARY: Those who fear the Lord will prepare their hearts and humble their souls in his sight. True preparation for God is inward: a heart made ready and a soul humbled before him. — W. O. E. Oesterley

18 COMMENTARY: We will fall into the hands of the Lord and not into the hands of men, for as his majesty is, so is his mercy. God's hands are safer than men's because his mercy matches his greatness. — W. O. E. Oesterley
