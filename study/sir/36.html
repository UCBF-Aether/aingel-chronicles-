Study Notes — Sirach

Chapter 36

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Have mercy upon us, O Lord God of all, and behold us. The prayer opens with a plea for God's mercy and his beholding gaze upon his people. — W. O. E. Oesterley

2 COMMENTARY: Send your fear upon all the nations that do not seek after you. God's fear should be sent upon nations that do not seek him. — W. O. E. Oesterley

3 COMMENTARY: Lift up your hand against the strange nations, and let them see your power. God's hand lifted against strange nations will show them his power. — W. O. E. Oesterley

4 COMMENTARY: As you were sanctified in us before them, so be magnified among them before us. As God was sanctified in Israel before the nations, so he should be magnified among the nations before Israel. — W. O. E. Oesterley

5 COMMENTARY: Let them know you, as we have known you, that there is no God but only you, O God. The nations should come to know, as Israel knows, that there is no God but God alone. — W. O. E. Oesterley

6 COMMENTARY: Show new signs, and make other strange wonders; glorify your hand and your right arm, that they may set forth your wondrous works. New signs and strange wonders should glorify God's hand and arm, displaying his wondrous works. — W. O. E. Oesterley

7 COMMENTARY: Raise up indignation, and pour out wrath; take away the adversary, and destroy the enemy. Indignation raised and wrath poured out remove adversary and enemy. — W. O. E. Oesterley

8 COMMENTARY: Make the time short, remember the covenant, and let them declare your wonderful works. Shortened time and remembered covenant will let God's wonderful works be declared. — W. O. E. Oesterley

9 COMMENTARY: Let him who escapes be consumed by the rage of the fire, and let them perish who oppress the people. Escapers should be consumed by fiery rage, and oppressors of the people should perish. — W. O. E. Oesterley

10 COMMENTARY: Smite in sunder the heads of the rulers of the heathen, who say, there is none other but we. Heathen rulers who claim there is none but themselves should have their heads smitten asunder. — W. O. E. Oesterley

11 COMMENTARY: Gather all the tribes of Jacob together, and inherit them, as from the beginning. Jacob's tribes should be gathered and inherited as from the beginning. — W. O. E. Oesterley

12 COMMENTARY: O Lord, have mercy upon the people called by your name, and upon Israel, whom you have named your firstborn. Mercy is asked for the people called by God's name, Israel his firstborn. — W. O. E. Oesterley

13 COMMENTARY: O be merciful to Jerusalem, your holy city, the place of your rest. Mercy is asked for Jerusalem, the holy city and place of God's rest. — W. O. E. Oesterley

14 COMMENTARY: Fill Sion with your unspeakable oracles, and your people with your glory. Sion should be filled with God's unspeakable oracles and his people with his glory. — W. O. E. Oesterley

15 COMMENTARY: Give testimony to those you have possessed from the beginning, and raise up prophets who have been in your name. Testimony should be given to God's ancient possession, and prophets raised up in his name. — W. O. E. Oesterley

16 COMMENTARY: Reward those who wait for you, and let your prophets be found faithful. Waiters for God should be rewarded, and prophets found faithful. — W. O. E. Oesterley

17 COMMENTARY: O Lord, hear the prayer of your servants, according to the blessing of Aaron over your people, that all who dwell upon the earth may know you are the Lord, the eternal God. The prayer asks to be heard according to Aaron's blessing, so that all on earth may know the Lord as eternal God. — W. O. E. Oesterley

18 COMMENTARY: The belly devours all meats, yet one meat is better than another. The belly takes all meats, yet meats differ in worth. — W. O. E. Oesterley

19 COMMENTARY: As the palate tastes divers kinds of venison, so does a heart of understanding taste false speeches. As the palate distinguishes meats, an understanding heart distinguishes false speeches. — W. O. E. Oesterley

20 COMMENTARY: A froward heart causes heaviness, but a man of experience will recompense him. A perverse heart brings heaviness, and an experienced man will repay it in kind. — W. O. E. Oesterley

21 COMMENTARY: A woman will receive every man, yet one daughter is better than another. Women may receive every man, yet daughters differ, one better than another. — W. O. E. Oesterley

22 COMMENTARY: The beauty of a woman cheers the countenance, and a man loves nothing better. A woman's beauty cheers the face, and a man loves nothing better. — W. O. E. Oesterley

23 COMMENTARY: If there is kindness, meekness, and comfort in her tongue, then her husband is not like other men. A tongue of kindness, meekness, and comfort sets a husband apart from other men. — W. O. E. Oesterley

24 COMMENTARY: He who gets a wife begins a possession, a help like himself, and a pillar of rest. Getting a wife begins a possession: a help like oneself and a pillar of rest. — W. O. E. Oesterley

25 COMMENTARY: Where no hedge is, the possession is spoiled, and he who has no wife will wander up and down mourning. Without a hedge a possession is spoiled, and without a wife a man wanders mourning. — W. O. E. Oesterley

26 COMMENTARY: Who will trust a thief well appointed, who skips from city to city? So who will believe a man who has no house, and lodges wherever the night takes him? A roving thief cannot be trusted, and neither can a houseless man lodging wherever night finds him. — W. O. E. Oesterley
