Study Notes — Sirach

Chapter 4

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Do not defraud the poor of his living, and do not make needy eyes wait long. Justice to the poor is urgent: withholding what they live on, and delaying help, are both wrongs. — W. O. E. Oesterley

2 COMMENTARY: Do not make a hungry soul sorrowful, nor provoke a man in his distress. Hunger and distress already weigh heavily, and adding sorrow or provocation to them is cruelty. — W. O. E. Oesterley

3 COMMENTARY: Do not add trouble to a vexed heart, and do not defer giving to him who is in need. A troubled heart needs relief, not more weight, and need calls for prompt giving. — W. O. E. Oesterley

4 COMMENTARY: Do not reject the supplication of the afflicted, nor turn your face from a poor man. Affliction's plea deserves a hearing, and turning away the face is itself a refusal. — W. O. E. Oesterley

5 COMMENTARY: Do not turn your eye from the needy, and give him no occasion to curse you. Looking away from need invites the needy person's bitter curse. — W. O. E. Oesterley

6 COMMENTARY: If he curses you in the bitterness of his soul, his prayer will be heard by him who made him. The Maker hears the poor man's bitter prayer, so wronging the needy is dangerous before God. — W. O. E. Oesterley

7 COMMENTARY: Win the love of the congregation, and bow your head to a great man. A good name among the people and deference to those in authority both belong to a well-ordered life. — W. O. E. Oesterley

8 COMMENTARY: Do not grudge bowing your ear to the poor, but give him a friendly answer with meekness. Listening to the poor is a duty, and the answer should be friendly and gentle, not grudging. — W. O. E. Oesterley

9 COMMENTARY: Deliver him who suffers wrong from the oppressor's hand, and do not be fainthearted when you sit in judgment. Rescuing the wronged takes courage, especially when judging against power. — W. O. E. Oesterley

10 COMMENTARY: Be as a father to the fatherless and instead of a husband to their mother, and you will be as a son of the Most High, loved by him more than by your mother. Fatherly care for the fatherless is the way to sonship with God himself. — W. O. E. Oesterley

11 COMMENTARY: Wisdom exalts her children and lays hold of those who seek her. Wisdom lifts those who belong to her and takes hold of seekers rather than waiting passively. — W. O. E. Oesterley

12 COMMENTARY: He who loves wisdom loves life, and those who seek her early will be filled with joy. Loving wisdom is loving life itself, and seeking her early fills life with joy. — W. O. E. Oesterley

13 COMMENTARY: He who holds her fast will inherit glory, and wherever she enters, the Lord will bless. Clinging to wisdom brings glory as an inheritance, and her presence brings God's blessing. — W. O. E. Oesterley

14 COMMENTARY: Those who serve wisdom minister to the Holy One, and those who love her are loved by the Lord. Serving wisdom is service of God, and loving her draws God's love in return. — W. O. E. Oesterley

15 COMMENTARY: Whoever gives ear to wisdom will judge the nations, and he who attends to her will dwell securely. Listening to wisdom qualifies a person for great judgment and gives secure dwelling. — W. O. E. Oesterley

16 COMMENTARY: If a man commits himself to wisdom, he will inherit her, and his generation will hold her in possession. Wholehearted commitment makes wisdom an inheritance passed down through the family. — W. O. E. Oesterley

17 COMMENTARY: At first wisdom walks with a man by crooked ways, bringing fear, dread, and torment with her discipline, until she can trust his soul and try him by her laws. Wisdom's first schooling feels harsh and winding, because it tests whether the learner can be trusted. — W. O. E. Oesterley

18 COMMENTARY: Then she will return by the straight way to him, comfort him, and show him her secrets. Once tested and trusted, the learner finds wisdom coming straight to him with comfort and hidden knowledge. — W. O. E. Oesterley

19 COMMENTARY: But if he goes wrong, she will forsake him and give him over to his own ruin. Turning aside forfeits wisdom's company, and the deserter is left to the ruin he chose. — W. O. E. Oesterley

20 COMMENTARY: Observe the opportunity, beware of evil, and do not be ashamed when your soul is concerned. Right timing matters, evil must be watched for, and shame should never silence a matter of conscience. — W. O. E. Oesterley

21 COMMENTARY: There is a shame that brings sin, and a shame that is glory and grace. False shame leads into sin, while the shame that owns fault before God brings honour and grace. — W. O. E. Oesterley

22 COMMENTARY: Accept no person against your soul, and let no man's reverence cause you to fall. Neither favour nor fear of any person should be allowed to work against your own soul. — W. O. E. Oesterley

23 COMMENTARY: Do not refrain from speaking when there is occasion to do good, and do not hide your wisdom in her beauty. Wisdom kept silent when good could be spoken is wisdom hidden, not wisdom used. — W. O. E. Oesterley

24 COMMENTARY: By speech wisdom is known, and learning by the word of the tongue. Wisdom becomes visible in speech, and learning is proved by what the tongue can rightly say. — W. O. E. Oesterley

25 COMMENTARY: In no way speak against the truth, but be abashed at the error of your ignorance. Truth must never be contradicted, and ignorance should bring embarrassment rather than bold speech. — W. O. E. Oesterley

26 COMMENTARY: Do not be ashamed to confess your sins, and do not force the course of the river. Confession should be unashamed, and no one should fight against the natural course of things as if forcing a river. — W. O. E. Oesterley

27 COMMENTARY: Do not make yourself an underling to a foolish man, nor accept the person of the mighty. Subservience to folly and favouritism toward power both corrupt judgment. — W. O. E. Oesterley

28 COMMENTARY: Strive for the truth unto death, and the Lord will fight for you. Truth is worth contending for to the end, and God himself takes up the fight of those who do. — W. O. E. Oesterley

29 COMMENTARY: Do not be hasty in your tongue, and slack and remiss in your deeds. Speech should not outrun action, nor action lag behind speech. — W. O. E. Oesterley

30 COMMENTARY: Do not be as a lion in your house, nor frantic among your servants. A tyrant at home, raging over servants, is the opposite of a governed household. — W. O. E. Oesterley

31 COMMENTARY: Do not let your hand be stretched out to receive and shut when you should repay. Taking eagerly while refusing to give back in turn is a grasping, dishonest pattern. — W. O. E. Oesterley
