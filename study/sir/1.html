Study Notes — Sirach

Chapter 1

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: All wisdom belongs to the Lord alone and has always been with him. Oesterley stresses this opening as the text for all that follows: wisdom is God's peculiar possession, dispensed as he wills. — W. O. E. Oesterley

2 COMMENTARY: No one can count the sand, the raindrops, or the days of eternity, and the same limit bounds human knowledge. The verse piles up uncountables to show that divine wisdom cannot be measured by man. — W. O. E. Oesterley

3 COMMENTARY: The height of heaven, the breadth of earth, and the deep are all beyond man's measuring, and wisdom is more unsearchable still. Even the created world defeats our instruments, so its Maker's wisdom must lie further beyond us. — W. O. E. Oesterley

4 COMMENTARY: Wisdom existed before anything was made, and prudent understanding is from everlasting. Wisdom is not a human discovery but a reality older than creation itself. — W. O. E. Oesterley

5 COMMENTARY: The word of God most high is the fountain from which wisdom flows, and her ways are his everlasting commandments. Wisdom is therefore found not apart from God's word but in obeying it. — W. O. E. Oesterley

6 COMMENTARY: The root of wisdom has been revealed to no one by his own power, and her wise counsels are known to no unaided mind. Oesterley notes that root here means the essence of wisdom, not merely its origin. — W. O. E. Oesterley

7 COMMENTARY: The knowledge of wisdom and her great experience have not been laid open to man as a possession. The bracketed verse repeats the question of verse 6: wisdom is received, not seized. — W. O. E. Oesterley

8 COMMENTARY: There is only one who is wise and greatly to be feared: the Lord seated on his throne. All claims to wisdom are judged against this one sovereign wisdom. — W. O. E. Oesterley

9 COMMENTARY: God himself created wisdom, saw her, numbered her, and poured her out upon all his works. Wisdom is woven into creation by God's own ordering, portioned out to every creature. — W. O. E. Oesterley

10 COMMENTARY: Wisdom is present with all flesh in measured gift, but God gives her freely to those who love him. Oesterley explains the contrast: all receive something, while those who love God receive without restriction. — W. O. E. Oesterley

11 COMMENTARY: Fearing the Lord brings honour, glory, gladness, and a crown of rejoicing. Reverence for God is not a burden but the source of a person's true dignity and joy. — W. O. E. Oesterley

12 COMMENTARY: The fear of the Lord makes a merry heart and gives joy, gladness, and long life. A life ordered by reverence is cheerful and enduring, not pinched and fearful. — W. O. E. Oesterley

13 COMMENTARY: Whoever fears the Lord will find it goes well with him at the last and will find favour in the day of his death. The payoff of reverence is seen at the end, when a life is summed up. — W. O. E. Oesterley

14 COMMENTARY: Fearing the Lord is the beginning of wisdom, planted with the faithful from the womb. Wisdom does not start with cleverness but with reverence, and it grows with a person from the start of life. — W. O. E. Oesterley

15 COMMENTARY: Wisdom has built an everlasting foundation among men and will continue with their seed. What is founded on the fear of God outlasts one generation and passes to the next. — W. O. E. Oesterley

16 COMMENTARY: The fear of the Lord is the fullness of wisdom and fills men with her fruits. Reverence is not a thin beginning but the complete substance of wisdom, yielding real fruit in life. — W. O. E. Oesterley

17 COMMENTARY: Wisdom fills the house of the God-fearing with desirable things and the storehouses with increase. The fruit of wisdom shows up in a well-ordered, provided-for household. — W. O. E. Oesterley

18 COMMENTARY: The fear of the Lord is a crown of wisdom that makes peace and perfect health flourish, both gifts of God, enlarging the joy of those who love him. Reverence crowns a life with wholeness rather than mere success. — W. O. E. Oesterley

19 COMMENTARY: Wisdom rains down skill and knowledge of understanding and exalts to honour those who hold her fast. Wisdom is a downpour from above, and honour follows those who cling to it. — W. O. E. Oesterley

20 COMMENTARY: The root of wisdom is the fear of the Lord, and its branches are long life. Reverence is the root system; a long, steady life is what grows from it. — W. O. E. Oesterley

21 COMMENTARY: The fear of the Lord drives away sins, and where it is present it turns away wrath. Reverence works as a guard, checking both wrongdoing and the anger that follows it. — W. O. E. Oesterley

22 COMMENTARY: A furious man cannot be justified, for the sway of his fury becomes his own destruction. Uncontrolled anger is not strength; it masters its owner and ruins him. — W. O. E. Oesterley

23 COMMENTARY: A patient man will bear for a time, and afterward joy springs up for him. Patience is not defeat but endurance that waits out trouble until gladness returns. — W. O. E. Oesterley

24 COMMENTARY: The patient man hides his words for a time, and the lips of many will afterwards declare his wisdom. Restraint in speech lets a person's wisdom be recognised by others rather than advertised by himself. — W. O. E. Oesterley

25 COMMENTARY: The parables of knowledge lie in the treasures of wisdom, but godliness is an abomination to a sinner. Wisdom's storehouse is open, yet the sinner hates the very godliness that would let him in. — W. O. E. Oesterley

26 COMMENTARY: If you desire wisdom, keep the commandments, and the Lord will give her to you. The way to wisdom is practical obedience, and the gift follows the keeping. — W. O. E. Oesterley

27 COMMENTARY: The fear of the Lord is wisdom and instruction, and faith and meekness are his delight. God delights not in showy religion but in trusting, humble obedience. — W. O. E. Oesterley

28 COMMENTARY: Do not distrust the fear of the Lord when you are poor, and do not come to him with a double heart. Poverty is no reason to abandon reverence, and divided loyalty cancels the approach to God. — W. O. E. Oesterley

29 COMMENTARY: Do not be a hypocrite before men, and take good heed what you speak. Outward pretence and careless speech both betray a heart that is not single toward God. — W. O. E. Oesterley

30 COMMENTARY: Do not exalt yourself, lest you fall and bring dishonour on your soul, and God expose your secrets and cast you down in the congregation. Pride that comes to God without truth ends in public disgrace, because the heart was full of deceit rather than fear. — W. O. E. Oesterley
