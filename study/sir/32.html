Study Notes — Sirach

Chapter 32

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: If you are made master of a feast, do not lift yourself up, but be among them as one of the rest; take diligent care for them, and so sit down. A feast-master should serve humbly among the guests, caring diligently before sitting down. — W. O. E. Oesterley

2 COMMENTARY: When you have done all your office, take your place, that you may be merry with them, and receive a crown for your well ordering of the feast. After the work is done, merriment follows, and good ordering earns a crown. — W. O. E. Oesterley

3 COMMENTARY: Speak, you who are the elder, for it becomes you, but with sound judgment, and do not hinder music. Elders should speak, as befits them, with sound judgment, without stopping the music. — W. O. E. Oesterley

4 COMMENTARY: Do not pour out words where there is a musician, and do not show forth wisdom out of time. Words should not be poured out over music, nor wisdom shown out of season. — W. O. E. Oesterley

5 COMMENTARY: A concert of music in a banquet of wine is as a signet of carbuncle set in gold. Music at a wine banquet is like a carbuncle signet set in gold, precious ornament. — W. O. E. Oesterley

6 COMMENTARY: As a signet of an emerald set in a work of gold, so is the melody of music with pleasant wine. Melody with pleasant wine is like an emerald signet in gold work. — W. O. E. Oesterley

7 COMMENTARY: Speak, young man, if there is need of you, and yet scarcely when you are twice asked. A young man should speak only if needed, and scarcely even when twice asked. — W. O. E. Oesterley

8 COMMENTARY: Let your speech be short, comprehending much in few words; be as one who knows and yet holds his tongue. Youthful speech should be short, much in few words, like one who knows yet holds his tongue. — W. O. E. Oesterley

9 COMMENTARY: If you are among great men, do not make yourself equal with them, and when ancient men are in place, do not use many words. Among the great, equality must not be claimed, and before ancients, words must be few. — W. O. E. Oesterley

10 COMMENTARY: Before the thunder goes lightning, and before a shamefaced man will go favour. As lightning precedes thunder, so favour precedes the shamefaced man. — W. O. E. Oesterley

11 COMMENTARY: Rise up betimes, and do not be the last, but get home without delay. Leave in good time, not last, and go home without delay. — W. O. E. Oesterley

12 COMMENTARY: There take your pastime, and do what you will, but do not sin by proud speech. At home, pastime and freedom are allowed, but not sin by proud speech. — W. O. E. Oesterley

13 COMMENTARY: For these things bless him who made you, and has replenished you with his good things. For all these enjoyments, bless the Maker who replenished you with good things. — W. O. E. Oesterley

14 COMMENTARY: Whoever fears the Lord will receive his discipline, and those who seek him early will find favour. Fear of God brings his discipline, and early seeking finds favour. — W. O. E. Oesterley

15 COMMENTARY: He who seeks the law will be filled with it, but the hypocrite will be offended at it. Law-seekers are filled with law, while hypocrites are offended by it. — W. O. E. Oesterley

16 COMMENTARY: Those who fear the Lord will find judgment, and will kindle justice as a light. The God-fearing find judgment and kindle justice like a light. — W. O. E. Oesterley

17 COMMENTARY: A sinful man will not be reproved, but finds an excuse according to his will. The sinful man refuses reproof and invents excuses to suit his will. — W. O. E. Oesterley

18 COMMENTARY: A man of counsel will be considerate, but a strange and proud man is not daunted with fear, even when he has done without counsel. Counsel makes a man considerate, while the strange, proud man fears nothing even after acting without counsel. — W. O. E. Oesterley

19 COMMENTARY: Do nothing without advice, and when you have once done, do not repent. Nothing should be done without advice, and once done on advice, it should not be repented of. — W. O. E. Oesterley

20 COMMENTARY: Do not go in a way where you may fall, and do not stumble among the stones. Dangerous ways and stony paths must be avoided. — W. O. E. Oesterley

21 COMMENTARY: Do not be confident in a plain way. Even a plain way should not breed overconfidence. — W. O. E. Oesterley

22 COMMENTARY: Beware of your own children. Even one's own children require wariness, since family can also endanger. — W. O. E. Oesterley

23 COMMENTARY: In every good work trust your own soul, for this is the keeping of the commandments. In every good work, trusting one's own soul is the keeping of the commandments. — W. O. E. Oesterley

24 COMMENTARY: He who believes in the Lord takes heed to the commandment, and he who trusts in him will fare never the worse. Belief in God shows in heeding commandments, and trust in him never fares worse. — W. O. E. Oesterley
