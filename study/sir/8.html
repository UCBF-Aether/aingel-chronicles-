Study Notes — Sirach

Chapter 8

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Do not strive with a mighty man, lest you fall into his hands. Contention with power ends in being at its mercy. — W. O. E. Oesterley

2 COMMENTARY: Do not be at variance with a rich man, lest he overweigh you, for gold has destroyed many and perverted the hearts of kings. Wealth can outweigh right, and it has corrupted even kings, so quarrelling with the rich is perilous. — W. O. E. Oesterley

3 COMMENTARY: Do not strive with a man full of tongue, and do not heap wood upon his fire. Arguing with a talkative man only feeds his fire. — W. O. E. Oesterley

4 COMMENTARY: Do not jest with a rude man, lest your ancestors be disgraced. Jesting with the rude drags disgrace beyond yourself, onto your family name. — W. O. E. Oesterley

5 COMMENTARY: Do not reproach a man who turns from sin, but remember that we are all worthy of punishment. A repentant man should not be reproached, since all stand deserving of punishment. — W. O. E. Oesterley

6 COMMENTARY: Do not dishonour a man in his old age, for even some of us wax old. Old age deserves honour, if only because we ourselves grow old. — W. O. E. Oesterley

7 COMMENTARY: Do not rejoice over your greatest enemy being dead, but remember that we all die. Death is common to all, so even an enemy's death is no occasion for rejoicing. — W. O. E. Oesterley

8 COMMENTARY: Do not despise the discourse of the wise, but acquaint yourself with their proverbs, for from them you will learn instruction and how to serve great men with ease. The wise men's sayings teach both instruction and the ease of serving the great. — W. O. E. Oesterley

9 COMMENTARY: Do not miss the discourse of the elders, for they also learned from their fathers, and from them you will learn understanding and how to give answer as need requires. Elders pass on what they received, and their discourse teaches understanding and timely answers. — W. O. E. Oesterley

10 COMMENTARY: Do not kindle the coals of a sinner, lest you be burnt with the flame of his fire. Stirring a sinner's passions burns the stirrer himself. — W. O. E. Oesterley

11 COMMENTARY: Do not rise up in anger at the presence of an injurious person, lest he lie in wait to entrap you in your words. Angry rising against an injurious man gives him the chance to trap you by your own words. — W. O. E. Oesterley

12 COMMENTARY: Do not lend to him who is mightier than yourself, for if you lend to him, count it but lost. A loan to the mightier cannot be enforced, so it should be counted as lost from the start. — W. O. E. Oesterley

13 COMMENTARY: Do not be surety above your power, for if you are surety, take care to pay it. Standing surety beyond your means is folly, and once bound, payment must be seen to. — W. O. E. Oesterley

14 COMMENTARY: Do not go to law with a judge, for they will judge for him according to his honour. Litigation against a judge is hopeless, since his peers judge in his favour out of regard for his office. — W. O. E. Oesterley

15 COMMENTARY: Do not travel by the way with a bold fellow, lest he become grievous to you, for he will do according to his own will, and you will perish with him through his folly. A bold companion acts on his own will, and his folly can drag a fellow-traveller to ruin. — W. O. E. Oesterley

16 COMMENTARY: Do not strive with an angry man, and do not go with him into a solitary place, for blood is as nothing in his sight, and where there is no help he will overthrow you. An angry man sets no value on blood, and a lonely place leaves no help against him. — W. O. E. Oesterley

17 COMMENTARY: Do not consult with a fool, for he cannot keep counsel. A fool is useless as a counsellor because he cannot keep what is entrusted to him. — W. O. E. Oesterley

18 COMMENTARY: Do no secret thing before a stranger, for you do not know what he will bring forth. A stranger's use of a secret cannot be predicted, so secrets should not be done before him. — W. O. E. Oesterley

19 COMMENTARY: Do not open your heart to every man, lest he requite you with a shrewd turn. An open heart to everyone invites a cunning return for trust. — W. O. E. Oesterley
