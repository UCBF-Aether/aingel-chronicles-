Study Notes — Ruth

Chapter 1

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: In the days of the judges a famine drove a Bethlehem family to sojourn in Moab. Henry warns that trying to escape a cross laid in our way by changing place seldom mends matters. — Matthew Henry (Concise); Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: Elimelech, Naomi, and their sons Mahlon and Chilion were Ephrathites of Bethlehemjudah who settled in Moab. JFB notes the names mean, respectively, "My God is king" and "fair or pleasant." — Matthew Henry (Concise); Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: Elimelech died in Moab, leaving Naomi a widow with her two sons. Henry draws the general lesson that death continually mars all our outward comforts, which are of short continuance. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

4 COMMENTARY: The sons married Moabite women, Orpah and Ruth, and lived there about ten years. JFB notes these marriages violated the Mosaic law, and Jewish writers regarded the young men's early deaths as judgments for those unlawful connections. — Matthew Henry (Concise); Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: Then both sons also died, and Naomi was left without husband or sons. Henry observes that the removal which was meant to provide for the family ended in the wasting of it. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

6 COMMENTARY: Hearing that the LORD had visited his people in giving them bread, Naomi arose with her daughters-in-law to return from Moab. JFB notes the aged widow longed to enjoy again the privileges of Israel once the famine had ceased. — Matthew Henry (Concise); Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: Naomi set out from Moab toward the land of Judah with her two daughters-in-law. Henry remarks that when death comes into a family it ought to reform what is amiss there, and earth is made bitter so that heaven may be made dear. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

8 COMMENTARY: Naomi urged each daughter-in-law to return to her mother's house, praying the LORD would deal kindly with them as they had dealt with the dead. JFB explains the mother's house as the women's own apartments in Eastern custom. — Matthew Henry (Concise); Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: Naomi prayed the LORD would grant each of them rest in the house of a husband, then kissed them and they wept aloud. JFB glosses this rest as a life of tranquillity, free from the peculiar troubles of widowhood. — Matthew Henry (Concise); Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: Both daughters-in-law answered that they would surely return with Naomi to her people. Henry notes Naomi dismissed them with prayer, as friends parting in love should part. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

11 COMMENTARY: Naomi asked whether she had any more sons in her womb who could become their husbands. JFB explains the allusion to the ancient custom, later sanctioned in the law of Moses, by which a younger son married his deceased brother's widow. — Matthew Henry (Concise); Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: Naomi pressed them to turn back, saying she was too old to have a husband and had no hope of sons for them. JFB calls this dissuasion wise: it prevented hopes that could not be realized and tested whether their conversion to the true religion was sincere. — Matthew Henry (Concise); Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: Naomi added that it grieved her for their sakes that the hand of the LORD had gone out against her. JFB explains that she was so reduced in circumstances she could not bear to subject them to privation with her. — Matthew Henry (Concise); Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: Orpah kissed her mother-in-law and went back, but Ruth clave to Naomi. Henry contrasts them: Orpah loved Naomi but not enough to leave Moab, while many likewise value Christ yet will not forsake other things for him. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

15 COMMENTARY: Naomi pointed out that Ruth's sister-in-law had gone back to her people and to her gods, and urged Ruth to return after her. Henry frames this as the moment of decision between Moab and the God of Israel. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

16 COMMENTARY: Ruth answered with her great declaration: where Naomi goes she will go, Naomi's people will be her people, and Naomi's God her God. Henry calls this an example of grace inclining the soul to choose the better part. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

17 COMMENTARY: Ruth swore that only death would part her from Naomi, calling down the LORD's judgment on herself if she did otherwise. Henry notes the power of such resolution: it silences temptation, while an unsteady mind stands like a door half open. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

18 COMMENTARY: Seeing Ruth was steadfastly minded to go with her, Naomi stopped urging her. Henry observes that Naomi could desire no more than this solemn declaration. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

19 COMMENTARY: When the two came to Bethlehem, all the city was moved about them and asked, "Is this Naomi?" JFB notes the painful contrast between her former flourishing state and her present condition as a forlorn widow. — Matthew Henry (Concise); Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

20 COMMENTARY: Naomi asked to be called Mara, bitterness, because the Almighty had dealt very bitterly with her. Henry glosses Naomi as "pleasant" and Mara as "bitter," marking her as a woman of a sorrowful spirit. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

21 COMMENTARY: Naomi said she went out full and the LORD had brought her home empty, testifying against her and afflicting her. Henry adds that she owned the affliction came from God, and that it is affliction rightly borne, not affliction itself, that does us good. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

22 COMMENTARY: Naomi returned with Ruth the Moabitess at the beginning of barley harvest. JFB dates this to the end of March, and Henry notes that though Naomi came home empty and childless, believers have a fulness of which they can never be emptied. — Matthew Henry (Concise); Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
