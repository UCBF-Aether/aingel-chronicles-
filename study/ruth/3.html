Study Notes — Ruth

Chapter 3

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Naomi said she would seek rest for Ruth, that it might be well with her. Henry notes the married state should be a rest that fixes the affections for life, and should be entered with seriousness and prayer for God's direction. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

2 COMMENTARY: Naomi reminded Ruth that Boaz, with whose maidens she had been, was their kindred, and was winnowing barley that night in the threshingfloor. Henry frames Naomi's advice as a parent's careful counsel in an important concern, seeking what is best for Ruth's soul. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

3 COMMENTARY: Naomi directed Ruth to wash, anoint herself, put on her raiment, and go down to the floor, not making herself known until Boaz had eaten and drunk. Henry stresses this course was according to the laws and usages of Israel, not to be judged by modern rules. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

4 COMMENTARY: Ruth was to mark where Boaz lay down, uncover his feet, and lie down, and he would tell her what to do. Henry notes that law and custom gave Ruth, now proselyted to the true religion, a legal claim upon Boaz, which widows customarily asserted. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

5 COMMENTARY: Ruth answered, "All that thou sayest unto me I will do." Henry observes that if there had been any evil in the proposal, Ruth, a woman of too much virtue and sense, would not have listened to it. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

6 COMMENTARY: Ruth went down to the floor and did all her mother-in-law had bidden her. Henry's outline treats this obedience as Ruth doing all that was fit for her to do before patiently waiting the event. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

7 COMMENTARY: After eating, drinking, and lying down at the end of the heap of corn, Boaz was approached as Ruth came softly and uncovered his feet and lay down. Henry notes that what would be improper in one age or nation is not always so in another. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

8 COMMENTARY: At midnight Boaz was afraid and turned himself, and behold, a woman lay at his feet. Henry praises Boaz's conduct throughout: he did not suspect Ruth of ill intentions nor take advantage of her. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

9 COMMENTARY: Boaz asked who she was, and Ruth answered, "I am Ruth thine handmaid: spread therefore thy skirt over thine handmaid; for thou art a near kinsman." Henry notes Boaz, as a judge of Israel, would tell Ruth what she should do and whether he had the right of redemption. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

10 COMMENTARY: Boaz blessed Ruth, saying her latter kindness exceeded the former, in not following young men, poor or rich. Henry observes Boaz did not disdain her as a poor, destitute stranger, but spoke honourably of her. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

11 COMMENTARY: Boaz told Ruth not to fear, for he would do all she required, since all the city knew she was a virtuous woman. Henry calls Boaz's conduct worthy of the highest praise, honouring Ruth's virtue rather than suspecting her. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

12 COMMENTARY: Boaz confirmed it was true that he was a near kinsman, but there was a kinsman nearer than he. Henry notes Boaz made his promise conditional, since the right of redemption belonged first to that nearer kinsman. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

13 COMMENTARY: Boaz told Ruth to tarry that night, and in the morning, if the nearer kinsman would redeem her, well, but if not, he would do the kinsman's part, swearing by the LORD. Henry stresses Boaz's honest, conditional promise, made without taking advantage. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

14 COMMENTARY: Ruth lay at his feet until morning, then rose before one could know another, because Boaz said it should not be known that a woman came into the floor. Henry notes Boaz sent her away as soon as morning arrived, protecting her reputation. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

15 COMMENTARY: Boaz told Ruth to bring her veil, hold it, and he measured six measures of barley into it and laid it on her. Henry presents this as Boaz's present to her mother-in-law, given with the promise. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

16 COMMENTARY: When Ruth came to her mother-in-law, Naomi asked who she was, and Ruth told her all the man had done for her. Henry's lesson is that having done all fit for her to do, Ruth must now patiently wait the event. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

17 COMMENTARY: Ruth reported Boaz's gift of six measures of barley, with his words, "Go not empty unto thy mother in law." Henry notes Boaz, having undertaken the matter, would be sure to manage it well. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

18 COMMENTARY: Naomi told Ruth to sit still until she knew how the matter would fall, for the man would not rest until he had finished the thing that day. Henry draws the believer's lesson: our strength is to sit still, casting our care on God who has promised to care for us, and Christ himself is our near Kinsman with the right to redeem. — Matthew Henry (Concise)
