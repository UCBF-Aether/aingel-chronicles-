Study Notes — Romans

Chapter 3

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: The question follows: what advantage has the Jew, or what profit is there in circumcision? Henry notes that Paul does not deny Jewish privilege; he defines it. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: Much every way, chiefly because to them were committed the oracles of God. JFB stresses that possessing God's word is the chief privilege, and also the chief trust. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: If some did not believe, shall their unbelief make God's faithfulness without effect? Henry answers no: human unfaithfulness cannot cancel God's faithfulness. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: God forbid; let God be true and every man a liar, as it is written, that God may be justified in his sayings and overcome when judged. JFB notes that God's truth stands even when every human witness fails. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: If our unrighteousness commends God's righteousness, is God unrighteous in taking vengeance? Paul speaks as men speak, to expose the objection. Henry notes the objection mistakes God's use of sin for approval of it. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: God forbid; for then how shall God judge the world? JFB observes that denying God's right to punish would deny his office as judge of all. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: If God's truth abounds through my lie to his glory, why am I still judged as a sinner? Henry notes that a good end does not excuse a false means. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: Some slanderously report that Paul teaches to do evil that good may come; their damnation is just. JFB stresses that no end, however good, licenses evil means. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: Are we better than they? No, for Paul has already charged both Jews and Gentiles as all under sin. Henry observes that universality of sin levels every claim of superiority. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: As it is written, there is none righteous, no, not one. JFB notes that Scripture, not Paul's opinion, delivers this verdict on humanity. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: There is none that understands, none that seeks after God. Henry stresses that fallen man does not naturally seek God; he flees him. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: All have gone out of the way and become unprofitable; none does good, no, not one. JFB notes the picture of a road abandoned by all travellers alike. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: Their throat is an open sepulchre; with their tongues they have used deceit; the poison of asps is under their lips. Henry observes that corrupt speech reveals a corrupt heart. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: Their mouth is full of cursing and bitterness. JFB notes that the mouth, meant for praise, becomes an instrument of malediction. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: Their feet are swift to shed blood. Henry stresses that sin is not idle; it hurries toward violence. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: Destruction and misery are in their ways. JFB notes that the path of sin leaves ruin behind it wherever it goes. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: The way of peace they have not known. Henry observes that sinners cannot find peace because they have forsaken its way. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

18 COMMENTARY: There is no fear of God before their eyes. JFB stresses that this is the root of all the rest: where reverence is gone, restraint is gone. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

19 COMMENTARY: Whatever the law says, it says to those under the law, that every mouth may be stopped and all the world become guilty before God. Henry notes that the law's first work is to silence every excuse. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

20 COMMENTARY: By deeds of the law no flesh shall be justified in God's sight, for by the law is the knowledge of sin. JFB stresses that the law diagnoses sin; it does not cure it. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

21 COMMENTARY: But now God's righteousness apart from the law is manifested, witnessed by the law and the prophets. Henry notes that the gospel righteousness was promised before it was published. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

22 COMMENTARY: This righteousness of God comes through faith in Jesus Christ to all and on all who believe, for there is no difference. JFB stresses that faith is the one door for every sinner. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

23 COMMENTARY: All have sinned and fall short of the glory of God. Henry observes that the shortfall is universal, so the remedy must be universal too. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

24 COMMENTARY: Believers are justified freely by God's grace through the redemption in Christ Jesus. JFB stresses the word freely: justification is a gift, not a wage. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

25 COMMENTARY: God set forth Christ as a propitiation through faith in his blood, to declare his righteousness for the remission of past sins. Henry notes that the cross both pardons and vindicates God's justice. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

26 COMMENTARY: God declares his righteousness at this time, that he might be just and the justifier of him who believes in Jesus. JFB stresses the double wonder: God remains just while justifying the unjust. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

27 COMMENTARY: Where is boasting then? It is excluded. By what law? Of works? No, but by the law of faith. Henry observes that faith leaves nothing for man to boast in. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

28 COMMENTARY: A man is justified by faith without the deeds of the law. JFB notes this is the epistle's thesis: faith alone lays hold of Christ's righteousness. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

29 COMMENTARY: Is God only the God of the Jews? Is he not also of the Gentiles? Yes, of the Gentiles also. Henry stresses that one God implies one way of salvation for all. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

30 COMMENTARY: One God will justify the circumcised by faith and the uncircumcised through faith. JFB notes the slight change of preposition still points to the same faith. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

31 COMMENTARY: Do we then make void the law through faith? God forbid; we establish the law. Henry observes that faith honours the law by fulfilling its demands in Christ and writing it on the heart. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
