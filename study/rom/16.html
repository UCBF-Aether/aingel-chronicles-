Study Notes — Romans

Chapter 16

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Paul commends Phebe, our sister, a servant of the church at Cenchrea. Henry notes that commendation of a woman servant shows women's honoured place in the early church. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: Receive her in the Lord, as becomes saints, and assist her in whatever business she needs, for she has been a succourer of many, and of Paul also. JFB notes that hospitality repays hospitality. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: Greet Priscilla and Aquila, Paul's helpers in Christ Jesus. Henry observes that this couple's help was personal and costly. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: They laid down their own necks for Paul's life, to whom not only Paul gives thanks but all the Gentile churches. JFB notes that risking life for an apostle earned the thanks of whole churches. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: Greet the church in their house, and Epaenetus, Paul's beloved, the firstfruits of Achaia to Christ. Henry notes that a house church and a first convert are both remembered by name. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: Greet Mary, who bestowed much labour on us. JFB observes that labour for the apostles is labour worthy of record. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: Greet Andronicus and Junia, Paul's kinsmen and fellow prisoners, of note among the apostles, who were in Christ before Paul. Henry notes that kinship, imprisonment, and early faith are all honoured. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: Greet Amplias, Paul's beloved in the Lord. JFB notes the warmth of personal affection in these brief greetings. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: Greet Urbane, our helper in Christ, and Stachys, Paul's beloved. Henry observes that helpers and beloved friends are named side by side. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: Greet Apelles, approved in Christ, and those of Aristobulus's household. JFB notes that approval in Christ is higher praise than any office. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: Greet Herodion, Paul's kinsman, and those of Narcissus's household who are in the Lord. Henry observes that only those in the Lord in a household are greeted, a quiet discrimination. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: Greet Tryphena and Tryphosa, who labour in the Lord, and Persis, the beloved, who laboured much in the Lord. JFB notes that women's labour in the Lord is twice remembered. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: Greet Rufus, chosen in the Lord, and his mother and Paul's. Henry observes that Rufus's mother had been a mother to Paul himself. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: Greet Asyncritus, Phlegon, Hermas, Patrobas, Hermes, and the brethren with them. JFB notes that groups of lesser-known believers are greeted together. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: Greet Philologus and Julia, Nereus and his sister, and Olympas, and all the saints with them. Henry observes that the list closes with a general greeting to all the saints. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: Salute one another with a holy kiss; the churches of Christ salute you. JFB notes that the holy kiss was a sign of fellowship, and the greeting is from all the churches. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: Mark those who cause divisions and offences contrary to the doctrine learned, and avoid them. Henry stresses that division-makers are to be noted and avoided, not debated endlessly. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

18 COMMENTARY: Such serve not our Lord Jesus Christ but their own belly, and by good words and fair speeches deceive simple hearts. JFB notes that fair speech is the deceiver's tool against the simple. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

19 COMMENTARY: The Romans' obedience has come abroad to all men, and Paul is glad; but he would have them wise unto good and simple concerning evil. Henry observes that wisdom toward good and simplicity toward evil are the wise combination. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

20 COMMENTARY: The God of peace shall bruise Satan under your feet shortly; the grace of our Lord Jesus Christ be with you. JFB notes that peace's God is also the warrior who crushes Satan. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

21 COMMENTARY: Timothy, Paul's workfellow, and Lucius, Jason, and Sosipater, his kinsmen, salute you. Henry notes that Paul's companions join his greeting. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

22 COMMENTARY: Tertius, who wrote this epistle, salutes you in the Lord. JFB notes the amanuensis steps forward for a single greeting, a glimpse of how the letter was written. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

23 COMMENTARY: Gaius, Paul's host and the whole church's, and Erastus, the city's chamberlain, and Quartus, a brother, salute you. Henry observes that host, city treasurer, and brother greet together. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

24 COMMENTARY: The grace of our Lord Jesus Christ be with you all. Amen. JFB notes that grace, the letter's theme, is also its farewell. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

25 COMMENTARY: To him who is of power to establish you according to Paul's gospel and the preaching of Jesus Christ, according to the mystery kept secret since the world began, be glory. Henry stresses that establishment comes by the gospel of the long-hidden mystery. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

26 COMMENTARY: The mystery is now made manifest, and by the prophetic Scriptures, according to the everlasting God's commandment, made known to all nations for the obedience of faith. JFB notes that the mystery is now published to all nations. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

27 COMMENTARY: To God only wise, be glory through Jesus Christ for ever. Amen. Henry observes that the letter ends where it began: in the glory of God alone, through Christ. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
