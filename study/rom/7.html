Study Notes — Romans

Chapter 7

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: The law has dominion over a man as long as he lives; Paul speaks to those who know the law. JFB notes that death ends legal obligation, which is the key to the whole chapter. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: A married woman is bound by law to her husband while he lives, but if he dies she is loosed from her husband's law. Henry observes that death dissolves even the strongest legal bond. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: While her husband lives, marriage to another makes her an adulteress, but if he dies she is free, even though married to another. JFB notes the illustration prepares the claim that believers died to the law. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: Likewise, brethren, you are become dead to the law by Christ's body, that you should be married to another, to him raised from the dead, that we should bring forth fruit to God. Henry stresses that death to the law is not lawlessness but remarriage to Christ. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: When we were in the flesh, sinful motions stirred by the law worked in our members to bring forth fruit unto death. JFB notes that the law, by forbidding sin, can stir the very sin it forbids in a fallen heart. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: Now we are delivered from the law, being dead to that in which we were held, that we should serve in newness of spirit, not in oldness of the letter. Henry observes that service continues, but its spring is the Spirit, not the letter. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: What shall we say then? Is the law sin? God forbid. Paul would not have known lust except the law had said, you shall not covet. JFB stresses that the law exposes sin; it does not create the sinfulness it exposes. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: Sin, taking occasion by the commandment, worked in Paul all manner of concupiscence, for without the law sin was dead. Henry notes that a forbidden thing wakens desire in a corrupt heart. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: Paul was alive without the law once, but when the commandment came, sin revived and he died. JFB reads this as the awakening of conscience: the law made him feel his death. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: The commandment, ordained to life, was found by Paul to be unto death. Henry observes that a law meant to guide to life condemns the one who breaks it. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: Sin, taking occasion by the commandment, deceived Paul and by it slew him. JFB notes sin's deceit: it promises freedom and delivers death. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: Therefore the law is holy, and the commandment holy, just, and good. Henry stresses that the fault lies in sin, not in the law that condemns it. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: Was what is good made death to Paul? God forbid; but sin, that it might appear sin, worked death in him by what is good, that sin might become exceedingly sinful. JFB notes that the law's goodness makes sin's evil unmistakable. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: The law is spiritual, but Paul is carnal, sold under sin. Henry observes that the law's spirituality condemns a nature that is fleshly. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: What Paul does he does not allow, for what he would he does not, but what he hates he does. JFB notes the inner war: the renewed will and the remaining corruption contend. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: If Paul does what he would not, he consents to the law that it is good. Henry observes that even unwilling sin testifies that the law is right. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: It is no more Paul that does it, but sin dwelling in him. JFB stresses that indwelling sin, not the new man, is the author of the evil done. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

18 COMMENTARY: In Paul's flesh dwells no good thing, for to will is present with him, but how to perform what is good he finds not. Henry notes the painful gap between willing good and doing it. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

19 COMMENTARY: The good Paul would he does not, but the evil he would not, that he does. JFB observes that this repeated confession is the experience of a believer, not an excuse for sin. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

20 COMMENTARY: If Paul does what he would not, it is no more he that does it, but sin dwelling in him. Henry stresses the distinction between the man and the corruption that still lodges in him. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

21 COMMENTARY: Paul finds a law: when he would do good, evil is present with him. JFB notes that this law of indwelling sin opposes the law of God in the mind. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

22 COMMENTARY: Paul delights in God's law after the inward man. Henry observes that delight in the law marks the new nature, whatever the flesh does. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

23 COMMENTARY: He sees another law in his members, warring against the law of his mind and bringing him into captivity to sin's law in his members. JFB notes the warfare is real and lifelong, though not hopeless. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

24 COMMENTARY: O wretched man that I am! Who shall deliver me from the body of this death? Henry notes that the cry of misery is itself the doorway to deliverance. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

25 COMMENTARY: Paul thanks God through Jesus Christ our Lord; so with the mind he serves God's law, but with the flesh sin's law. JFB stresses that deliverance is in Christ, while the conflict itself continues in this life. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
