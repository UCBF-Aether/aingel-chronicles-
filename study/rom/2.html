Study Notes — Romans

Chapter 2

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Whoever judges another condemns himself, for he does the same things he judges. Henry notes that judging others does not exempt the judge; it witnesses against him. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: God's judgment is according to truth against those who commit such things. JFB stresses that God's verdict follows fact, not reputation or profession. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: The man who judges others while doing the same cannot expect to escape God's judgment. Henry observes that self-flattery is no shelter at the bar of God. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: Men despise the riches of God's goodness, forbearance, and longsuffering, not knowing that his goodness leads to repentance. JFB notes that patience is meant to soften, not to embolden, sinners. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: Through hardness and an impenitent heart, a man treasures up wrath for himself against the day of wrath and revelation of God's righteous judgment. Henry stresses that every unrepented sin adds to the store. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: God will render to every man according to his deeds. JFB observes that this is the rule of judgment, not a denial that salvation is by grace. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: To those who by patient continuance in well-doing seek glory, honour, and immortality, God gives eternal life. Henry notes that perseverance, not a single act, marks the seeker. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: To the contentious, who obey not the truth but obey unrighteousness, belong indignation and wrath. JFB stresses that wilful resistance, not ignorance, draws this sentence. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: Tribulation and anguish fall on every soul that does evil, Jew first and also Gentile. Henry observes that privilege does not lessen judgment; it increases responsibility. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: Glory, honour, and peace belong to every man who works good, Jew first and also Gentile. JFB notes the same impartial order governs reward as governs punishment. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: There is no respect of persons with God. Henry stresses that God judges persons by deeds and truth, not by nation, name, or outward standing. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: Those who sinned without the law will perish without the law, and those who sinned under the law will be judged by the law. JFB notes that each is judged by the light he actually had. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: Not hearers of the law are just before God, but doers of the law shall be justified. Henry observes that hearing without doing is self-deception. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: Gentiles who do not have the law yet do by nature the things in the law are a law to themselves. JFB notes that conscience supplies a law where no written law was given. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: They show the work of the law written in their hearts, their conscience bearing witness, and their thoughts accusing or excusing one another. Henry stresses that conscience is God's inward court. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: This will be manifest in the day when God judges the secrets of men by Jesus Christ, according to Paul's gospel. JFB notes that judgment reaches hidden thoughts, not only open deeds. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: The Jew who is called a Jew, rests in the law, and boasts in God, is addressed next. Henry observes that a name and a privilege are no substitute for obedience. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

18 COMMENTARY: Such a man knows God's will and approves what is excellent, being instructed out of the law. JFB notes that knowledge increases accountability rather than excusing disobedience. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

19 COMMENTARY: He is confident that he is a guide of the blind, a light to those in darkness. Henry warns that a teacher who does not practise what he teaches leads others astray with himself. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

20 COMMENTARY: He claims to be an instructor of the foolish, a teacher of babes, having the form of knowledge and truth in the law. JFB notes the word form: the shape of truth without its power. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

21 COMMENTARY: The teacher who preaches that a man should not steal is asked whether he himself steals. Henry stresses that the preacher's life is his loudest sermon. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

22 COMMENTARY: The man who says not to commit adultery, and who abhors idols, is asked whether he commits adultery or robs temples. JFB notes that selective obedience exposes the whole profession. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

23 COMMENTARY: The man who boasts in the law dishonours God by breaking the law. Henry observes that boasting in a law one breaks turns privilege into shame. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

24 COMMENTARY: Through such conduct God's name is blasphemed among the Gentiles, as it is written. JFB stresses that the sins of God's people dishonour him before the world. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

25 COMMENTARY: Circumcision profits if one keeps the law, but if one breaks the law it becomes uncircumcision. Henry notes that a sign without obedience is an empty seal. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

26 COMMENTARY: If the uncircumcised keep the law's righteousness, his uncircumcision is counted as circumcision. JFB stresses that God regards the reality, not the rite. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

27 COMMENTARY: The uncircumcised who fulfils the law will judge the circumcised law-breaker who has the letter and circumcision. Henry observes that obedient outsiders shame disobedient insiders. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

28 COMMENTARY: He is not a Jew who is one outwardly, nor is circumcision outward in the flesh. JFB notes that true Jewish identity, like true religion, is inward. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

29 COMMENTARY: He is a Jew who is one inwardly, and circumcision is of the heart, in the spirit, not in the letter, whose praise is from God, not men. Henry stresses that God, not public applause, is the judge of inward religion. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
