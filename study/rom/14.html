Study Notes — Romans

Chapter 14

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Receive him who is weak in the faith, but not to doubtful disputations. Henry notes that the weak are to be welcomed, not interrogated into disputes. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: One believes he may eat all things; another, who is weak, eats herbs. JFB stresses that both are believers; their difference is about conscience, not about Christ. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: Let not him who eats despise him who eats not, and let not him who eats not judge him who eats, for God has received him. Henry observes that God has received both, so neither may reject the other. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: Who are you to judge another man's servant? To his own master he stands or falls, and he shall be held up, for God is able to make him stand. JFB notes that the servant answers to his own master, not to a fellow servant. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: One man esteems one day above another; another esteems every day alike; let every man be fully persuaded in his own mind. Henry stresses that in indifferent matters conscience, fully persuaded, must rule. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: He who regards the day regards it to the Lord, and he who regards it not does so to the Lord; likewise he who eats eats to the Lord, giving thanks, and he who eats not does so to the Lord. JFB notes that both parties act for the Lord, which sanctifies their difference. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: None of us lives to himself, and no man dies to himself. Henry observes that the Christian life is not private property; it belongs to the Lord. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: Whether we live, we live to the Lord, and whether we die, we die to the Lord; whether we live or die, we are the Lord's. JFB notes that life and death alike are lived under Christ's lordship. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: To this end Christ both died and rose and revived, that he might be Lord both of the dead and the living. Henry stresses that Christ's lordship spans both worlds. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: Why do you judge your brother, or why do you set at nought your brother? For we shall all stand before Christ's judgment seat. JFB notes that a coming common judgment forbids present private judging. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: As it is written, as I live, says the Lord, every knee shall bow to me, and every tongue shall confess to God. Henry observes that universal confession will vindicate God's judgment. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: Every one of us shall give account of himself to God. JFB stresses the personal account: each answers for himself, not for his brother. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: Let us not judge one another any more, but judge this rather, that no man put a stumbling block or occasion to fall in his brother's way. Henry notes the better judgment: to judge one's own conduct lest it trip another. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: Paul knows and is persuaded by the Lord Jesus that nothing is unclean of itself, but to him who esteems anything unclean, to him it is unclean. JFB stresses that conscience makes a thing unclean to the man who believes it so. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: If your brother is grieved with your meat, you no longer walk charitably; do not destroy with your meat him for whom Christ died. Henry observes that love outweighs liberty when a brother's soul is at stake. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: Let not your good be evil spoken of. JFB notes that even lawful liberty, used unlovingly, brings reproach on the gospel. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: God's kingdom is not meat and drink, but righteousness, peace, and joy in the Holy Ghost. Henry stresses that the kingdom's substance is inward and spiritual, not dietary. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

18 COMMENTARY: He who in these things serves Christ is acceptable to God and approved of men. JFB notes that kingdom service wins both God's acceptance and men's approval. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

19 COMMENTARY: Let us follow things that make for peace, and things that edify one another. Henry observes that peace and edification are the twin aims of Christian conduct. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

20 COMMENTARY: For meat do not destroy God's work; all things indeed are pure, but it is evil for the man who eats with offence. JFB notes that a pure thing becomes evil when it offends a brother. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

21 COMMENTARY: It is good neither to eat flesh, nor to drink wine, nor anything by which your brother stumbles, is offended, or made weak. Henry stresses that abstaining for a brother's sake is better than exercising liberty. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

22 COMMENTARY: Have you faith? Have it to yourself before God. Happy is he who does not condemn himself in what he allows. JFB notes that a settled conscience before God is the happy man's possession. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

23 COMMENTARY: He who doubts is damned if he eats, because he eats not of faith, for whatever is not of faith is sin. Henry observes that acting against conscience, even in indifferent things, is sin. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
