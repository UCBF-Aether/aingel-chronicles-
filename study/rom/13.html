Study Notes — Romans

Chapter 13

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Let every soul be subject to the higher powers, for there is no power but of God, and the powers that are ordained of God. Henry notes that civil authority itself is God's ordinance, whatever the ruler's character. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: Whoever resists the power resists God's ordinance, and those who resist shall receive damnation to themselves. JFB stresses that resistance to lawful authority is resistance to God. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: Rulers are not a terror to good works but to evil; if you do good you shall have praise from the power. Henry observes that government is meant to encourage good, not to frighten it. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: The ruler is God's minister to you for good, but if you do evil, be afraid, for he bears not the sword in vain, being God's minister and avenger of wrath on evildoers. JFB notes that the sword is God's instrument of justice, not mere force. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: Therefore you must be subject, not only for wrath but also for conscience' sake. Henry stresses that conscience, not fear of punishment alone, grounds obedience. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: For this cause you pay tribute also, for rulers are God's ministers attending continually to this very thing. JFB notes that taxes support the ministry of government God ordained. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: Render to all their dues: tribute to whom tribute is due, custom to whom custom, fear to whom fear, honour to whom honour. Henry observes that every due, fiscal or personal, is owed in its proper place. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: Owe no man anything except to love one another, for he who loves another has fulfilled the law. JFB notes that love is the one debt that is never discharged. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: The commandments against adultery, murder, theft, false witness, and covetousness, and every other, are summed up in this: you shall love your neighbour as yourself. Henry observes that love gathers all the second table into one word. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: Love works no ill to its neighbour, therefore love is the fulfilling of the law. JFB stresses that love does not abolish the law; it performs it. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: Knowing the time, that now it is high time to awake out of sleep, for our salvation is nearer than when we believed. Henry notes that nearness of salvation is a reason for wakefulness, not slumber. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: The night is far spent, the day is at hand; let us cast off works of darkness and put on the armour of light. JFB pictures the Christian as a soldier dressing for dawn. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: Let us walk honestly, as in the day, not in rioting and drunkenness, not in chambering and wantonness, not in strife and envying. Henry observes that daylight conduct has nothing to hide. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: Put on the Lord Jesus Christ, and make no provision for the flesh, to fulfil its lusts. JFB notes that putting on Christ leaves no room for planning sin. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
