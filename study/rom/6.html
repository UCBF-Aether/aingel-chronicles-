Study Notes — Romans

Chapter 6

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: What shall we say then? Shall we continue in sin that grace may abound? Henry notes that the question exposes a perversion of grace, not a consequence of it. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: God forbid. How shall we who are dead to sin live any longer in it? JFB stresses that death to sin is the believer's new identity, not a mere resolution. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: As many as were baptized into Jesus Christ were baptized into his death. Henry notes that baptism pictures union with Christ in his death. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: We are buried with him by baptism into death, that as Christ was raised by the Father's glory, so we should walk in newness of life. JFB stresses that burial implies a resurrection life, not a return to the grave. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: If we have been planted together in the likeness of his death, we shall also be in the likeness of his resurrection. Henry observes that union with Christ covers both halves of his work. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: Our old man is crucified with him, that the body of sin might be destroyed, so we should no longer serve sin. JFB notes that the old self is executed, not merely reformed. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: He that is dead is freed from sin. Henry stresses that death ends sin's legal claim, as it ends a master's claim over a servant. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: If we are dead with Christ, we believe we shall also live with him. JFB notes that death with Christ is the pledge of life with him. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: Christ, being raised from the dead, dies no more; death has no more dominion over him. Henry observes that a risen Christ can never die again, and neither can the life he gives. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: In that he died, he died unto sin once; in that he lives, he lives unto God. JFB stresses the once-for-all character of his death and the Godward direction of his life. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: Likewise reckon yourselves dead indeed unto sin, but alive unto God through Jesus Christ our Lord. Henry notes that reckoning is faith acting on the fact of union with Christ. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: Let not sin therefore reign in your mortal body, that you should obey its lusts. JFB stresses that sin may remain as a rebel, but must not reign as a king. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: Neither yield your members as instruments of unrighteousness to sin, but yield yourselves to God as alive from the dead, and your members as instruments of righteousness. Henry observes that the body is a weapon for whichever master it serves. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: Sin shall not have dominion over you, for you are not under law but under grace. JFB notes that grace, not law, is the power that breaks sin's dominion. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: What then? Shall we sin because we are not under law but under grace? God forbid. Henry observes that freedom from law as a covenant is not freedom to sin. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: To whom you yield yourselves servants to obey, his servants you are, whether of sin unto death or of obedience unto righteousness. JFB stresses that every man serves some master; neutrality is impossible. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: God be thanked that you were servants of sin, but have obeyed from the heart the form of doctrine delivered to you. Henry notes that the change is inward obedience, not outward profession alone. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

18 COMMENTARY: Being made free from sin, you became servants of righteousness. JFB observes that Christian freedom is a change of masters, not an absence of one. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

19 COMMENTARY: Paul speaks after the manner of men because of the infirmity of their flesh: as they yielded members to uncleanness, so now they are to yield them to holiness. Henry notes that the analogy of slavery is used because human weakness needs plain speech. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

20 COMMENTARY: When you were servants of sin, you were free from righteousness. JFB notes the irony: freedom from righteousness is the worst bondage. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

21 COMMENTARY: What fruit had you then in things you are now ashamed of? For their end is death. Henry observes that shame over past sin is itself evidence of a changed heart. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

22 COMMENTARY: Now, made free from sin and become servants of God, you have your fruit unto holiness, and the end everlasting life. JFB notes that holiness is both the fruit and the road to life. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

23 COMMENTARY: The wages of sin is death, but the gift of God is eternal life through Jesus Christ our Lord. Henry stresses the contrast: death is earned wages, life is a free gift. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
