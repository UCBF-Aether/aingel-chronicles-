Study Notes — Romans

Chapter 15

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: We who are strong ought to bear the infirmities of the weak, and not please ourselves. Henry notes that strength is given for bearing, not for boasting. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: Let every one of us please his neighbour for his good to edification. JFB stresses that pleasing others is bounded by their good, not by their whim. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: Even Christ pleased not himself, but, as it is written, the reproaches of those who reproached you fell on me. Henry observes that Christ is the pattern of self-forgetting service. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: Whatever things were written before were written for our learning, that we through patience and comfort of the Scriptures might have hope. JFB notes that the Old Testament is written for Christian hope, not merely for history. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: The God of patience and consolation grant you to be like-minded one toward another according to Christ Jesus. Henry notes that unity is God's gift, prayed for, not man's achievement alone. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: That you may with one mind and one mouth glorify God, the Father of our Lord Jesus Christ. JFB stresses that unity of mind issues in unity of worship. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: Therefore receive one another, as Christ also received us to God's glory. Henry observes that Christ's reception of us is the measure of our reception of one another. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: Jesus Christ was a minister of the circumcision for God's truth, to confirm the promises made to the fathers. JFB notes that Christ's ministry to Israel confirms, not cancels, the promises. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: The Gentiles might glorify God for his mercy, as it is written, for this cause I will confess to you among the Gentiles and sing to your name. Henry observes that Gentile praise is the fruit of mercy, not of merit. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: Again he says, rejoice, you Gentiles, with his people. JFB notes that Gentiles rejoice with Israel, not instead of Israel. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: Again, praise the Lord, all you Gentiles, and laud him, all you people. Henry observes that the call to praise is universal because mercy is universal. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: Isaiah says, there shall be a root of Jesse, and he who shall rise to reign over the Gentiles; in him shall the Gentiles trust. JFB notes that the Messiah's reign and the Gentiles' trust are joined in prophecy. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: The God of hope fill you with all joy and peace in believing, that you may abound in hope through the Holy Ghost's power. Henry stresses that joy, peace, and hope all grow in believing, by the Spirit's power. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: Paul is persuaded of the Romans that they are full of goodness, filled with all knowledge, and able to admonish one another. JFB notes that Paul commends them before he instructs them. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: He has written more boldly to them in some sort, as putting them in mind, because of the grace given him of God. Henry observes that boldness in reminding is consistent with respect for those reminded. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: Paul is a minister of Jesus Christ to the Gentiles, ministering God's gospel, that the Gentiles' offering might be acceptable, sanctified by the Holy Ghost. JFB pictures Paul as a priest presenting the Gentiles as an offering to God. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: He has therefore reason to glory through Jesus Christ in things pertaining to God. Henry notes that Paul's glorying is through Christ, not in himself. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

18 COMMENTARY: He will not dare to speak of things Christ has not wrought by him, to make the Gentiles obedient, by word and deed. JFB stresses that Paul claims only what Christ actually did through him. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

19 COMMENTARY: Through mighty signs and wonders, by the Spirit's power, Paul has fully preached Christ's gospel from Jerusalem round about to Illyricum. Henry observes that the gospel's spread from Jerusalem to Illyricum testifies to its power. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

20 COMMENTARY: He has strived to preach where Christ was not named, lest he build on another man's foundation. JFB notes that Paul's ambition was pioneer work, not work on others' foundations. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

21 COMMENTARY: As it is written, to whom he was not spoken of, they shall see, and they who have not heard shall understand. Henry observes that pioneer preaching fulfils prophecy. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

22 COMMENTARY: For this cause also Paul has been much hindered from coming to them. JFB notes that the very work that kept him away was the work that prepared his coming. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

23 COMMENTARY: Having no more place in these parts, and having a great desire these many years to come to them, Paul plans his visit. Henry observes that a finished work in one region frees the worker for another. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

24 COMMENTARY: Whenever he takes his journey into Spain, he will come to them, hoping to be brought on his way by them, after first being filled with their company. JFB notes that Rome is a stopping place on the way to Spain, not merely a destination. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

25 COMMENTARY: Now he goes to Jerusalem to minister to the saints. Henry notes that the journey to Jerusalem interrupts his Roman plan, because service to the poor saints comes first. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

26 COMMENTARY: Macedonia and Achaia were pleased to make a contribution for the poor saints at Jerusalem. JFB stresses that the gift was willing, not extorted. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

27 COMMENTARY: It pleased them, and they are debtors, for if Gentiles shared Israel's spiritual things, their duty is to minister to them in carnal things. Henry observes that spiritual debt is repaid in material generosity. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

28 COMMENTARY: When Paul has performed this and sealed to them this fruit, he will come by them into Spain. JFB notes that Paul treats the collection as sealed fruit, delivered faithfully. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

29 COMMENTARY: He knows that when he comes to them, he shall come in the fullness of the blessing of Christ's gospel. Henry stresses that Paul expects his coming to be a blessing, not a burden. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

30 COMMENTARY: Paul beseeches them, for the Lord Jesus Christ's sake and the Spirit's love, to strive together with him in prayers to God for him. JFB notes that prayer is striving together, a joint labour. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

31 COMMENTARY: He asks prayer that he may be delivered from unbelievers in Judea, and that his service for Jerusalem may be accepted by the saints. Henry observes that even an apostle asks prayer for safety and acceptance. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

32 COMMENTARY: That he may come to them with joy by God's will, and may with them be refreshed. JFB notes that refreshment in fellowship is the hoped-for end of a perilous journey. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

33 COMMENTARY: The God of peace be with you all. Amen. Henry observes that the prayer for peace fittingly closes a letter that began with grace and peace. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
