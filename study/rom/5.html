Study Notes — Romans

Chapter 5

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Being justified by faith, we have peace with God through our Lord Jesus Christ. Henry notes that justification ends the war between God and the sinner. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: By Christ we have access by faith into this grace in which we stand, and rejoice in hope of God's glory. JFB stresses that standing in grace is a present possession, not a future hope alone. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: We glory in tribulations also, knowing that tribulation works patience. Henry observes that affliction, in grace, becomes a workman rather than a destroyer. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: Patience works experience, and experience hope. JFB notes the chain: tried character is the evidence on which hope rests. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: Hope does not make ashamed, because God's love is shed abroad in our hearts by the Holy Ghost given to us. Henry stresses that the Spirit makes God's love a felt reality, not a mere doctrine. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: When we were yet without strength, in due time Christ died for the ungodly. JFB notes the timing and the objects: helpless sinners, not deserving saints. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: Scarcely for a righteous man will one die, yet perhaps for a good man some would dare to die. Henry observes that even the best human love stops short of Christ's. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: God commends his love toward us, in that while we were yet sinners, Christ died for us. JFB stresses that the proof of divine love is given at man's worst, not his best. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: Much more, being now justified by his blood, we shall be saved from wrath through him. Henry notes the argument from the greater to the less: if his death justifies, his life saves. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: If when we were enemies we were reconciled to God by his Son's death, much more, being reconciled, we shall be saved by his life. JFB stresses that reconciliation is the ground of final salvation. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: We also joy in God through our Lord Jesus Christ, by whom we have now received the atonement. Henry notes that joy in God himself, not only in his gifts, is the fruit of reconciliation. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: As by one man sin entered the world, and death by sin, so death passed upon all men, for all have sinned. JFB stresses that Adam's fall is the fountain from which universal death flows. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: Until the law, sin was in the world, but sin is not imputed when there is no law. Henry observes that sin existed before Moses, even though it was not yet codified. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: Death reigned from Adam to Moses, even over those who had not sinned after Adam's transgression, Adam being the figure of him who was to come. JFB notes that universal death proves universal sin, inherited from Adam. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: The free gift is not like the offence: if many died by one man's offence, much more God's grace and the gift by grace abounded to many by one man, Jesus Christ. Henry stresses that grace outmeasures the fall. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: The gift is not as by one who sinned: judgment came by one offence to condemnation, but the free gift is of many offences to justification. JFB notes that grace covers a multitude of sins, not one alone. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: If by one man's offence death reigned, much more those who receive abundance of grace and the gift of righteousness shall reign in life by one, Jesus Christ. Henry observes that believers do not merely escape death; they reign. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

18 COMMENTARY: As by one offence judgment came on all men to condemnation, so by one righteousness the free gift came on all men to justification of life. JFB stresses the parallel of the two heads, Adam and Christ. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

19 COMMENTARY: As by one man's disobedience many were made sinners, so by one man's obedience many shall be made righteous. Henry notes that representation, not imitation alone, is the ground in both cases. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

20 COMMENTARY: The law entered that the offence might abound, but where sin abounded, grace did much more abound. JFB stresses that the law exposed sin's depth so that grace's height might be seen. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

21 COMMENTARY: As sin reigned unto death, so grace reigns through righteousness unto eternal life by Jesus Christ our Lord. Henry observes that grace now occupies sin's throne, ruling toward life instead of death. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
