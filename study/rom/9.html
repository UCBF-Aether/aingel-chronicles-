Study Notes — Romans

Chapter 9

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Paul says the truth in Christ, he lies not, his conscience bearing witness in the Holy Ghost. Henry notes that so solemn an oath fits so painful a subject. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: He has great heaviness and continual sorrow in his heart. JFB stresses that Paul's grief for Israel is constant, not a passing feeling. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: He could wish himself accursed from Christ for his brethren, his kinsmen according to the flesh. Henry observes that this is love's hyperbole, not a literal offer, showing how deeply he loves his people. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: They are Israelites, to whom belong adoption, glory, covenants, law-giving, service, and promises. JFB notes that Israel's privileges are real and many, which makes their unbelief the more tragic. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: Theirs are the fathers, and from them, as concerning the flesh, Christ came, who is over all, God blessed for ever. Henry stresses that Christ's descent from Israel crowns all their privileges. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: Not as though God's word has taken none effect, for not all Israel are Israel. JFB notes the distinction between outward Israel and the true Israel of promise. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: Neither because they are Abraham's seed are they all children, but in Isaac shall your seed be called. Henry observes that God's promise, not mere descent, defines the seed. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: The children of the flesh are not God's children, but the children of promise are counted for the seed. JFB stresses that promise, not nature, makes a child of God. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: This is the word of promise: at this time I will come, and Sarah shall have a son. Henry notes that Isaac's birth was itself a work of promise, not of nature. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: Rebecca also conceived by one, Isaac our father, and the choice was made before the twins were born. JFB notes that election here precedes any works of the children. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: Before the children had done good or evil, that God's purpose according to election might stand, not of works but of him who calls, it was said to her. Henry stresses that the timing excludes works as the ground. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: The elder shall serve the younger. JFB notes that God's choice reverses human expectation of primogeniture. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: As it is written, Jacob I loved, but Esau I hated. Henry observes that love and hatred here speak of elective preference in the covenant, not of mere feeling. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: What shall we say then? Is there unrighteousness with God? God forbid. JFB stresses that God's sovereign choice cannot be unrighteous, since he owes mercy to none. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: God said to Moses, I will have mercy on whom I will have mercy, and compassion on whom I will have compassion. Henry notes that mercy is God's free gift, not man's claim. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: So then it is not of him who wills, nor of him who runs, but of God who shows mercy. JFB stresses that salvation's source is God's mercy, not human effort or resolve. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: Scripture says to Pharaoh, for this purpose I raised you up, that I might show my power in you and declare my name in all the earth. Henry observes that God overrules even a hardened king to display his power. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

18 COMMENTARY: Therefore God has mercy on whom he will, and whom he will he hardens. JFB notes that hardening is God's judicial giving-over of one who has already resisted him. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

19 COMMENTARY: The objector asks, why does God still find fault, for who has resisted his will? Henry notes that Paul does not flinch from the hardest question his doctrine raises. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

20 COMMENTARY: O man, who are you to reply against God? Shall the thing formed say to him who formed it, why have you made me thus? JFB stresses that the creature has no standing to arraign the Creator. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

21 COMMENTARY: Has not the potter power over the clay, to make one vessel to honour and another to dishonour from the same lump? Henry observes that the potter's right illustrates God's sovereignty, not his arbitrariness. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

22 COMMENTARY: God, willing to show his wrath and make his power known, endured with much longsuffering vessels of wrath fitted to destruction. JFB notes that even wrath's vessels are endured patiently before judgment. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

23 COMMENTARY: He did this that he might make known the riches of his glory on vessels of mercy, which he had before prepared to glory. Henry stresses that mercy, not wrath, is the end God delights to display. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

24 COMMENTARY: These vessels of mercy are us, whom he called, not of the Jews only but also of the Gentiles. JFB notes that the calling now breaks the bounds of one nation. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

25 COMMENTARY: As Hosea says, I will call them my people who were not my people, and her beloved who was not beloved. Henry observes that Hosea's words to apostate Israel now welcome the Gentiles. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

26 COMMENTARY: In the place where it was said to them, you are not my people, there they shall be called children of the living God. JFB notes the reversal: the place of rejection becomes the place of sonship. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

27 COMMENTARY: Isaiah cries concerning Israel, though the number of Israel's children be as the sand of the sea, a remnant shall be saved. Henry stresses that a remnant, not the whole nation, is the pattern of God's saving work. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

28 COMMENTARY: The Lord will finish the work and cut it short in righteousness, because a short work will the Lord make upon the earth. JFB notes that judgment, when it comes, is swift and decisive. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

29 COMMENTARY: As Isaiah said before, except the Lord of Sabaoth had left us a seed, we would have been as Sodom and Gomorrah. Henry observes that Israel's survival itself is mercy, not merit. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

30 COMMENTARY: What shall we say then? The Gentiles, who did not follow righteousness, have attained righteousness, the righteousness of faith. JFB notes the paradox: seekers missed it, while non-seekers received it by faith. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

31 COMMENTARY: Israel, following the law of righteousness, has not attained to the law of righteousness. Henry observes that pursuing righteousness by law misses the righteousness that comes by faith. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

32 COMMENTARY: Why? Because they sought it not by faith but as it were by works of the law, for they stumbled at the stumbling stone. JFB notes that Christ, the cornerstone, becomes a stumbling stone to self-righteous seekers. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

33 COMMENTARY: As it is written, behold, I lay in Zion a stumbling stone and rock of offence, and whoever believes on him shall not be ashamed. Henry stresses that faith in Christ is the one ground that never shames its holder. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
