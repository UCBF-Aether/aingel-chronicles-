Study Notes — Romans

Chapter 8

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: There is now no condemnation to those in Christ Jesus, who walk not after the flesh but after the Spirit. Henry notes that union with Christ removes the sentence the law pronounced. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: The law of the Spirit of life in Christ Jesus has made Paul free from the law of sin and death. JFB stresses that a higher law, the Spirit's life, liberates from the lower law of sin. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: What the law could not do, being weak through the flesh, God did, sending his Son in sinful flesh's likeness and for sin, condemning sin in the flesh. Henry observes that God condemned sin where sin had reigned: in human flesh. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: This was done that the law's righteousness might be fulfilled in us, who walk not after the flesh but after the Spirit. JFB notes that fulfilment comes through a walk empowered by the Spirit, not by the letter. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: Those after the flesh mind fleshly things, but those after the Spirit mind spiritual things. Henry observes that a man's mind reveals his master. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: To be carnally minded is death, but to be spiritually minded is life and peace. JFB stresses that the carnal mind's end is death because it is enmity against God. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: The carnal mind is enmity against God, for it is not subject to God's law, nor indeed can be. Henry notes that the unrenewed mind cannot submit; it must be renewed. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: Those in the flesh cannot please God. JFB observes that no fleshly service, however earnest, is acceptable while the nature remains unrenewed. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: Believers are not in the flesh but in the Spirit, if God's Spirit dwells in them; whoever lacks Christ's Spirit is none of his. Henry stresses that the Spirit's indwelling is the test of belonging to Christ. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: If Christ is in you, the body is dead because of sin, but the Spirit is life because of righteousness. JFB notes the paradox: a dying body houses a living spirit. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: If the Spirit of him who raised Jesus dwells in you, he who raised Christ shall also quicken your mortal bodies by his Spirit. Henry observes that the resurrection of Jesus is the pledge of the believer's own. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: Therefore, brethren, we are debtors, not to the flesh, to live after the flesh. JFB notes that obligation runs to the Spirit, who gives life, not to the flesh, which gives death. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: If you live after the flesh you shall die, but if through the Spirit you mortify the body's deeds, you shall live. Henry stresses that mortification is the Spirit's work through the believer's effort. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: As many as are led by God's Spirit, they are God's sons. JFB notes that being led, not merely taught, marks sonship. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: Believers received not a spirit of bondage again to fear, but the Spirit of adoption, whereby we cry, Abba, Father. Henry observes that adoption replaces slavery's fear with a child's cry. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: The Spirit himself bears witness with our spirit that we are God's children. JFB notes that assurance comes from the Spirit's joint testimony, not from feeling alone. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: If children, then heirs, heirs of God and joint-heirs with Christ, if we suffer with him, that we may also be glorified together. Henry stresses that suffering with Christ is the road to glory with him. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

18 COMMENTARY: The sufferings of this present time are not worthy to be compared with the glory to be revealed in us. JFB notes that the comparison is not close: present pain is outweighed beyond measure. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

19 COMMENTARY: The earnest expectation of the creature waits for the manifestation of God's sons. Henry observes that creation itself longs for the day of redemption's completion. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

20 COMMENTARY: The creature was made subject to vanity, not willingly, but by him who subjected it in hope. JFB notes that creation's frustration is temporary and purposeful, not meaningless. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

21 COMMENTARY: The creature itself shall be delivered from corruption's bondage into the glorious liberty of God's children. Henry notes that creation shares in the freedom of the sons it serves. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

22 COMMENTARY: The whole creation groans and travails in pain together until now. JFB pictures creation as in labour, awaiting a birth, not merely enduring decay. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

23 COMMENTARY: We ourselves, who have the firstfruits of the Spirit, groan within ourselves, waiting for adoption, the redemption of our body. Henry observes that even Spirit-filled believers groan for the body's redemption. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

24 COMMENTARY: We are saved by hope, but hope that is seen is not hope, for what a man sees, why does he yet hope for it? JFB notes that hope's nature is to wait for the unseen. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

25 COMMENTARY: If we hope for what we see not, then with patience we wait for it. Henry stresses that patience is hope's steadfast posture. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

26 COMMENTARY: Likewise the Spirit helps our infirmities, for we know not what to pray for as we ought, but the Spirit makes intercession for us with groanings that cannot be uttered. JFB notes that the Spirit prays within us where words fail. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

27 COMMENTARY: He who searches hearts knows the Spirit's mind, because he makes intercession for the saints according to God's will. Henry observes that the Spirit's prayers can never miss God's will. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

28 COMMENTARY: We know that all things work together for good to those who love God, to those called according to his purpose. JFB stresses that all things, not only pleasant things, are overruled for the called. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

29 COMMENTARY: Those God foreknew he predestined to be conformed to his Son's image, that Christ might be firstborn among many brethren. Henry notes that conformity to Christ is the goal of predestination. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

30 COMMENTARY: Those he predestined he called, those he called he justified, and those he justified he glorified. JFB notes the golden chain: each link holds to the next, from purpose to glory. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

31 COMMENTARY: What shall we say to these things? If God is for us, who can be against us? Henry observes that God's being for us settles every opposition. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

32 COMMENTARY: He who spared not his own Son but delivered him up for us all, how shall he not with him freely give us all things? JFB notes the argument: the greatest gift guarantees the lesser ones. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

33 COMMENTARY: Who shall lay anything to the charge of God's elect? It is God who justifies. Henry stresses that God's justification answers every accusation. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

34 COMMENTARY: Who is he that condemns? Christ died, yea rather is risen, and is at God's right hand, making intercession for us. JFB notes that the Judge himself is the believer's advocate. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

35 COMMENTARY: Who shall separate us from Christ's love? Tribulation, distress, persecution, famine, nakedness, peril, or sword? Henry observes that the list of enemies is also a list of things love survives. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

36 COMMENTARY: As it is written, for your sake we are killed all the day long; we are accounted as sheep for the slaughter. JFB notes that suffering is the believer's normal lot, not a sign of abandonment. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

37 COMMENTARY: In all these things we are more than conquerors through him who loved us. Henry stresses that victory comes through Christ's love, not through escape from trouble. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

38 COMMENTARY: Paul is persuaded that neither death nor life, angels nor principalities nor powers, things present nor things to come, can separate us. JFB notes that the catalogue spans every power in heaven, earth, and time. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

39 COMMENTARY: Neither height nor depth, nor any other creature, shall separate us from God's love in Christ Jesus our Lord. Henry observes that the love from which nothing separates is anchored in Christ himself. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
