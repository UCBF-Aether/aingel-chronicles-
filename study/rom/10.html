Study Notes — Romans

Chapter 10

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Brethren, Paul's heart's desire and prayer to God for Israel is that they might be saved. Henry notes that doctrine about Israel never cools Paul's prayer for Israel. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: He bears them record that they have a zeal of God, but not according to knowledge. JFB stresses that zeal without knowledge is dangerous, not commendable. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: Being ignorant of God's righteousness and seeking to establish their own, they have not submitted to God's righteousness. Henry observes that self-righteousness is a rival righteousness, not a supplement. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: Christ is the end of the law for righteousness to everyone who believes. JFB notes that Christ ends the law as a way of righteousness, not as a revelation of God's will. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: Moses describes the righteousness of the law, that the man who does those things shall live by them. Henry observes that law-righteousness demands perfect doing. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: The righteousness of faith speaks otherwise: do not say in your heart, who shall ascend into heaven, to bring Christ down. JFB notes that faith does not need to fetch Christ from afar; he has already come. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: Nor, who shall descend into the deep, to bring Christ up from the dead. Henry observes that faith does not need to raise Christ again; he is already risen. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: The word is near you, in your mouth and in your heart, the word of faith Paul preaches. JFB stresses the nearness of the gospel: it is to be believed and confessed, not fetched from afar. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: If you confess with your mouth the Lord Jesus, and believe in your heart that God raised him from the dead, you shall be saved. Henry notes that confession and belief together mark the saved. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: With the heart man believes unto righteousness, and with the mouth confession is made unto salvation. JFB stresses that inward faith and outward confession belong together. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: Scripture says, whoever believes on him shall not be ashamed. Henry observes that the promise is open to whoever, without exception of nation. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: There is no difference between Jew and Greek, for the same Lord over all is rich to all who call on him. JFB notes that one Lord's riches are open to every caller. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: Whoever shall call on the name of the Lord shall be saved. Henry stresses that calling on the Lord is the plain, open door of salvation. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: How shall they call on him in whom they have not believed? How shall they believe in him of whom they have not heard? How shall they hear without a preacher? JFB notes the chain that makes preaching necessary to faith. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: How shall they preach except they be sent? As it is written, how beautiful are the feet of those who preach the gospel of peace and bring glad tidings of good things. Henry observes that the preacher's feet are beautiful because of the message they carry. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: Not all obeyed the gospel, for Isaiah says, Lord, who has believed our report? JFB notes that unbelief in the face of preaching fulfils, not frustrates, prophecy. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: Faith comes by hearing, and hearing by the word of God. Henry stresses that the word preached, not wonders or visions, is faith's ordinary means. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

18 COMMENTARY: Have they not heard? Yes, their sound went into all the earth, and their words to the ends of the world. JFB notes that Israel cannot plead ignorance of the message. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

19 COMMENTARY: Did not Israel know? Moses says, I will provoke you to jealousy by a people who are no people, and by a foolish nation I will anger you. Henry observes that Gentile blessing was meant to stir Israel to jealousy. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

20 COMMENTARY: Isaiah is very bold and says, I was found by those who did not seek me; I was made manifest to those who did not ask for me. JFB notes the boldness: God found those who were not even looking. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

21 COMMENTARY: To Israel he says, all day long I have stretched forth my hands to a disobedient and gainsaying people. Henry observes that God's hands stayed open all day to a people who talked back. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
