Study Notes — Romans

Chapter 12

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: By God's mercies, Paul beseeches the brethren to present their bodies a living sacrifice, holy, acceptable to God, their reasonable service. Henry notes that the body itself, not only the soul, is offered in gospel worship. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: Be not conformed to this world, but be transformed by the renewing of the mind, that you may prove God's good, acceptable, and perfect will. JFB stresses that transformation begins in the mind, not in outward imitation. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: Through the grace given to Paul, he tells every man not to think of himself more highly than he ought, but soberly, as God has dealt to each the measure of faith. Henry observes that humility is the first fruit of renewed thinking. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: As we have many members in one body, and all members do not have the same office, so we, being many, are one body in Christ. JFB notes that diversity of function does not break unity of body. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: We are every one members one of another. Henry stresses that mutual membership makes mutual service a duty, not an option. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: Having gifts differing according to the grace given us, whether prophecy, let us prophesy according to the proportion of faith. JFB notes that gifts are measured by grace, and must be used within that measure. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: Or ministry, let us wait on our ministering; or he that teaches, on teaching. Henry observes that faithfulness in one's own calling matters more than reaching for another's. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: He that exhorts, on exhortation; he that gives, with simplicity; he that rules, with diligence; he that shows mercy, with cheerfulness. JFB notes that each gift has its proper manner: giving without ostentation, mercy without gloom. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: Let love be without dissimulation; abhor what is evil; cleave to what is good. Henry stresses that unfeigned love hates evil as truly as it loves good. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: Be kindly affectioned to one another with brotherly love, in honour preferring one another. JFB notes that honour is to be given, not grasped. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: Not slothful in business, fervent in spirit, serving the Lord. Henry observes that diligence in ordinary work and fervour in spirit belong together. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: Rejoicing in hope, patient in tribulation, continuing instant in prayer. JFB notes the threefold posture: joy for the future, patience in the present, prayer always. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: Distributing to the necessity of saints, given to hospitality. Henry stresses that generosity to fellow believers and welcome to strangers are both marks of love. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: Bless those who persecute you; bless, and curse not. JFB notes that blessing persecutors is the direct opposite of the natural heart. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: Rejoice with those who rejoice, and weep with those who weep. Henry observes that sympathy in both joy and sorrow is love's truest test. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: Be of the same mind one toward another; mind not high things, but condescend to men of low estate; be not wise in your own conceits. JFB notes that lowliness, not ambition, preserves unity. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: Recompense no man evil for evil; provide things honest in the sight of all men. Henry stresses that Christian conduct must be honest even before unbelievers. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

18 COMMENTARY: If it is possible, as much as lies in you, live peaceably with all men. JFB notes the double qualification: peace is pursued as far as possible and as far as it depends on us. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

19 COMMENTARY: Do not avenge yourselves, but give place to wrath, for it is written, vengeance is mine, I will repay, says the Lord. Henry observes that leaving vengeance to God is faith, not weakness. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

20 COMMENTARY: If your enemy hungers, feed him; if he thirsts, give him drink; in so doing you shall heap coals of fire on his head. JFB notes that kindness is the fire that melts an enemy, not that tortures him. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

21 COMMENTARY: Be not overcome of evil, but overcome evil with good. Henry stresses the final rule: evil is conquered only by good, never by evil. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
