Study Notes — 2 Samuel

Chapter 17

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Ahithophel asks Absalom to let him choose twelve thousand men and arise to pursue David that very night. Speed is the counsel's strength: strike before the fugitives can regroup. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

2 COMMENTARY: He will come upon David while weary and weak-handed, make him afraid, scatter his people, and smite the king only. A single blow at the king would end the war without a general battle. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

3 COMMENTARY: He will bring back all the people to Absalom, as if all returned, so all shall be in peace. The plan promises a bloodless settlement after one assassination. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

4 COMMENTARY: The saying pleases Absalom well and all the elders of Israel. The best counsel in the room is recognised at once, and that makes its rejection the more remarkable. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

5 COMMENTARY: Absalom says, Call now Hushai the Archite also, and let us hear likewise what he says. Here is the wonderful effect of Providence, Henry notes, blinding Absalom's mind and influencing his heart, so he cannot rest in Ahithophel's counsel but desires Hushai's advice. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

6 COMMENTARY: When Hushai comes, Absalom lays Ahithophel's counsel before him and asks whether to follow it, or else speak. Hushai is invited to judge the very counsel he was planted to defeat. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

7 COMMENTARY: Hushai says Ahithophel's counsel is not good at this time. The qualification matters: he does not call it foolish in itself, only ill-timed, which makes his alternative persuasive. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

8 COMMENTARY: Hushai warns that David and his men are mighty men, chafed as a bear robbed of her whelps, and David is a man of war who will not lodge with the people. A cornered, experienced force is the worst target for a night attack. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

9 COMMENTARY: David is hid now in some pit or place, and if some of Absalom's men fall at the first, all who hear will say there is a slaughter among Absalom's followers. A small reverse at the start would break the new army's nerve. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

10 COMMENTARY: Even the valiant man whose heart is as a lion's shall utterly melt, for all Israel knows David is mighty and his men valiant. Reputation itself becomes a weapon for the defence. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

11 COMMENTARY: Hushai counsels instead that all Israel be gathered from Dan to Beersheba, as the sand by the sea, and that Absalom go to battle in his own person. Delay disguised as thoroughness is the real counsel: time is what David needs. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

12 COMMENTARY: Then they shall come upon David where he is found and light on him as dew falls on the ground, leaving none of his men remaining. The grand army promises total victory, later, instead of a swift stroke now. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

13 COMMENTARY: If David gets into a city, all Israel shall bring ropes and draw it into the river until not one small stone is found. The boastful image of dragging a city away flatters Absalom's sense of overwhelming force. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

14 COMMENTARY: Absalom and all Israel say Hushai's counsel is better than Ahithophel's, for the Lord had appointed to defeat Ahithophel's good counsel, to bring evil upon Absalom. There is no contending with God, Henry says, who can arm a man against himself and destroy him by his own mistakes; Ahithophel's former counsel was followed because God intended to correct David, but this one was not, because God meant not to destroy him. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

15 COMMENTARY: Hushai tells Zadok and Abiathar what Ahithophel counselled and what he counselled. The double agent reports both counsels to David's priests, so the warning carries the full picture. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

16 COMMENTARY: Send quickly, Hushai says, and tell David not to lodge this night in the plains of the wilderness, but speedily pass over, lest the king be swallowed up. Even a rejected counsel is dangerous until David is across the Jordan. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

17 COMMENTARY: Jonathan and Ahimaaz stay by Enrogel, not daring to be seen entering the city, and a wench goes and tells them, and they go to tell David. The messenger chain David set up in chapter 15 now works. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

18 COMMENTARY: A lad sees them and tells Absalom, but they hurry to a man's house in Bahurim with a well in his court. The escape is narrow, and the warning nearly fails at the last link. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

19 COMMENTARY: The woman spreads a covering over the well's mouth and ground corn on it, and the thing is not known. A household's quick wit hides the king's messengers. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

20 COMMENTARY: When Absalom's servants ask where Ahimaaz and Jonathan are, the woman says they are gone over the brook of water. The misdirection sends the search the wrong way. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

21 COMMENTARY: After the servants depart, the messengers climb out of the well and tell David to arise and pass quickly over Jordan, for Ahithophel counselled thus against him. The warning arrives in time because Hushai's delay gave the messengers their chance. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

22 COMMENTARY: David and all his people pass over Jordan, and by morning light not one is left uncrossed. The whole company escapes intact, and whatever wisdom men employ, Henry notes, success is from God alone, who will not let his people perish. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

23 COMMENTARY: When Ahithophel sees his counsel is not followed, he saddles his ass, goes home to his city, puts his household in order, hangs himself, and dies, buried in his father's sepulchre. He hangs himself for vexation, Henry says: what breaks a proud man's heart will not break a humble man's sleep, and he does justice on himself to prevent a public execution. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

24 COMMENTARY: David comes to Mahanaim, and Absalom passes over Jordan with all Israel. The pursuit resumes, but now on David's chosen ground and on his timetable. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

25 COMMENTARY: Absalom makes Amasa captain of the host instead of Joab, Amasa being the son of Ithra who went in to Abigail, David's sister. A new commander replaces the general who stayed loyal to David. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

26 COMMENTARY: Israel and Absalom pitch in the land of Gilead. The rebel army masses across the Jordan, preparing for the battle Hushai's counsel made inevitable. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

27 COMMENTARY: When David comes to Mahanaim, Shobi of Rabbah, Machir of Lodebar, and Barzillai the Gileadite meet him. God, Henry observes, sometimes makes up to his people from strangers the comfort they are disappointed of in their own families. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

28 COMMENTARY: They bring beds, basins, earthen vessels, wheat, barley, flour, parched corn, beans, and lentils. The fugitive army is furnished for a camp, not merely fed for a night. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

29 COMMENTARY: They bring honey, butter, sheep, and cheese of kine for David and his people, saying the people are hungry, weary, and thirsty in the wilderness. What is done for the least of the King's brethren, Henry notes, is accepted and recompensed as if done to himself. — Matthew Henry (Concise)
