Study Notes — 2 Samuel

Chapter 7

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: The king sits in his house, and the Lord has given him rest round about from all his enemies. David, at rest in his palace, considers how he might best employ his leisure and prosperity in the service of God. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

2 COMMENTARY: David tells Nathan he dwells in a house of cedar while the ark dwells within curtains. Having built a palace for himself and a city for his servants, he now thinks of building a habitation for the ark. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

3 COMMENTARY: Nathan answers, Go, do all that is in thine heart, for the Lord is with thee. Nathan here speaks not as a prophet but as a godly man encouraging David by his private judgment, and Henry urges us likewise to forward the good designs of others. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

4 COMMENTARY: That night the word of the Lord comes to Nathan. Private encouragement gives way to a prophetic message that will both accept David's purpose and countermand its performance. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

5 COMMENTARY: The Lord asks, Shalt thou build me a house for me to dwell in? The question reverses David's plan: God will be the builder, not David. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

6 COMMENTARY: God has not dwelt in any house since bringing Israel out of Egypt, but walked in a tent and a tabernacle. His presence travelled with his people rather than settling in a fixed house. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

7 COMMENTARY: In all his walkings with Israel, God never asked any tribe he commanded to feed his people why they had not built him a house of cedar. The absence of such a command shows David's plan, though devout, was not required. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

8 COMMENTARY: God recounts taking David from the sheepcote, from following sheep, to be ruler over Israel. Every honour David holds is traced to God's choosing, not to his own rising. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

9 COMMENTARY: God was with David wherever he went, cut off his enemies, and made him a great name like the great men of the earth. The past record of God's presence is the ground for the promises that follow. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

10 COMMENTARY: God will appoint a place for Israel and plant them, that they may dwell in their own place and move no more, no longer afflicted by the children of wickedness. The promise secures the people, not only the king. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

11 COMMENTARY: As since the judges, God has given David rest from his enemies, and now the Lord tells him that he will make David a house. The house David wanted to build for God becomes the house God builds for David. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

12 COMMENTARY: When David sleeps with his fathers, God will set up his seed after him, proceeding out of his bowels, and establish his kingdom. These blessings relate to Solomon, David's immediate successor, and to the royal line of Judah. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

13 COMMENTARY: The seed shall build a house for God's name, and God will establish the throne of his kingdom for ever. Henry notes the everlasting throne can be applied to no other than Christ and his kingdom, for David's house and kingdom long since came to an end. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

14 COMMENTARY: God will be his father and he his son; if he commits iniquity, he will be chastened with the rod of men. The committing of iniquity cannot be applied to the Messiah himself, Henry observes, but to his spiritual seed, who have infirmities yet are not cast off. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

15 COMMENTARY: God's mercy shall not depart from him as it was taken from Saul, whom God put away before David. Unlike Saul's house, David's line is held in mercy even under chastening. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

16 COMMENTARY: David's house and kingdom shall be established for ever before him, and his throne for ever. The promise reaches beyond Solomon to Christ, who is often called David and the Son of David, and who builds the gospel temple, the spiritual temple of true believers. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

17 COMMENTARY: According to all these words and this vision, Nathan speaks to David. Nathan faithfully delivers a message that contradicts his own earlier words, once he is better informed of God's mind. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

18 COMMENTARY: David goes in, sits before the Lord, and asks, Who am I, O Lord God, and what is my house, that thou hast brought me hitherto? His prayer is full of devout affection, with low thoughts of his own merits and all he has counted as divine gift. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

19 COMMENTARY: This was yet a small thing in God's sight, but he has spoken also of David's house for a great while to come. David is amazed that God should deal with man as he does, considering what man is. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

20 COMMENTARY: David asks what more he can say, for the Lord God knows his servant. He knows us better than we know ourselves, Henry notes, so we may be satisfied with what he has done for us. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

21 COMMENTARY: For his word's sake and according to his own heart, God has done all these great things to make his servant know them. David ascribes all to the free grace of God, both the great things done for him and the great things made known to him. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

22 COMMENTARY: Therefore thou art great, O Lord God, for there is none like thee, neither any God beside thee. David speaks very highly and honourably of the Lord's favours, as one who has received far beyond desert. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

23 COMMENTARY: What one nation is like Israel, whom God redeemed to be a people to himself, to make him a name, doing great and terrible things for their land? The promise to David's house rests on the same redeeming grace shown to the whole nation. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

24 COMMENTARY: God has confirmed Israel to himself as a people for ever, and has become their God. The covenant with the house stands inside the covenant with the people. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

25 COMMENTARY: David prays that the word spoken concerning his servant and his house be established for ever, and that God do as he has said. He builds his faith and hopes to speed upon the sureness of God's promise, for with God saying and doing are not two things. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

26 COMMENTARY: Let God's name be magnified for ever, saying, The Lord of hosts is the God over Israel, and let David's house be established before him. The prayer seeks God's glory first, and the house's continuance as its witness. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

27 COMMENTARY: Because God revealed that he will build David a house, David has found in his heart to pray this prayer. His heart was found, Henry says, fixed and gathered from its wanderings, not a prayer of the tongue only. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

28 COMMENTARY: Thou art that God, and thy words are true, and thou hast promised this goodness to thy servant. The promise of Christ includes all, Henry observes: if the Lord God be ours, what more can we ask or think of? — Matthew Henry (Concise)

29 COMMENTARY: Let it please thee to bless the house of thy servant, that it may continue for ever before thee, for thou hast spoken it. The promises of God are not made to us by name as to David, Henry notes, but they belong to all who believe in Jesus Christ and plead them in his name. — Matthew Henry (Concise)
