Study Notes — 2 Samuel

Chapter 20

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: There happens to be a man of Belial, Sheba son of Bichri, a Benjamite, who blows a trumpet and says, We have no part in David, nor inheritance in the son of Jesse; every man to his tents, O Israel. One trial arises after another, Henry notes, and angry disputers misconstrue one another's words until a quarrel becomes a rebellion. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

2 COMMENTARY: Every man of Israel goes up from David and follows Sheba, but Judah cleaves to the king from Jordan to Jerusalem. The favour of the many is not to be depended upon, Henry observes, as Hosanna to the Son of David was soon changed to Crucify him. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

3 COMMENTARY: David comes to his house at Jerusalem and puts the ten concubines he left to keep the house in ward, feeding them but going not in to them, shut up to the day of their death, living in widowhood. The women Absalom publicly dishonoured live out their days in a living widowhood. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

4 COMMENTARY: David tells Amasa to assemble Judah within three days and be present. The new captain, promised Joab's place, is given his first command: raise the army against Sheba. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

5 COMMENTARY: Amasa goes to assemble Judah but tarries longer than the set time appointed. Delay in a rebellion is dangerous, and the king will not wait for his new captain. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

6 COMMENTARY: David tells Abishai that Sheba will do more harm than Absalom did, and bids him take the lord's servants and pursue, lest Sheba get fenced cities and escape. A second rebellion must be crushed before it fortifies itself. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

7 COMMENTARY: Joab's men, the Cherethites, Pelethites, and all the mighty men go out from Jerusalem to pursue Sheba. The pursuit is led in fact by the displaced captain, whatever the formal command. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

8 COMMENTARY: At the great stone in Gibeon, Amasa comes before them, and Joab's garment is girded with a girdle holding a sword that falls out as he goes. The stage is set for a murder disguised as a greeting. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

9 COMMENTARY: Joab asks Amasa, Art thou in health, my brother? and takes him by the beard with his right hand to kiss him. The kiss of peace is the cover for the sword, and the more plot there is in a sin, Henry says, the worse it is. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

10 COMMENTARY: Amasa takes no heed of the sword in Joab's hand, so Joab smites him in the fifth rib, sheds his bowels to the ground, and strikes not again, and Amasa dies. Joab barbarously murders Amasa, Henry says, contentedly sacrificing king and kingdom to personal revenge, with a seared conscience. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

11 COMMENTARY: One of Joab's men stands by Amasa and says, He that favours Joab and is for David, let him go after Joab. The corpse is passed by with a slogan, and loyalty to Joab is made the test of loyalty to David. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

12 COMMENTARY: Amasa wallows in blood in the highway, and when the man sees all the people stand still, he removes Amasa into the field and casts a cloth on him. The body blocks the road, and the army halts at the sight of its murdered captain. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

13 COMMENTARY: When Amasa is removed from the highway, all the people go on after Joab to pursue Sheba. With the corpse hidden, the pursuit resumes, and murder is covered by momentum. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

14 COMMENTARY: Sheba goes through all the tribes to Abel and Bethmaachah, and all the Berites gather and follow him. The rebel gathers a following across the north before he is cornered. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

15 COMMENTARY: Joab's army comes and besieges Sheba in Abel of Bethmaachah, casting up a bank against the city in the trench, and all the people with Joab batter the wall to throw it down. Justly is that place attacked, Henry notes, which dares to harbour a traitor. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

16 COMMENTARY: A wise woman cries out of the city, Hear, hear, and asks them to tell Joab to come near that she may speak with him. A single voice from the wall interrupts a siege. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

17 COMMENTARY: When Joab comes near, the woman asks if he is Joab, and he answers, I am he, and she asks him to hear her handmaid's words. The negotiation begins with identification, not with terms. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

18 COMMENTARY: She speaks the old saying, They shall surely ask counsel at Abel, and so they ended the matter. Abel's ancient reputation for counsel is invoked against a siege that never asked. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

19 COMMENTARY: She says she is one of the peaceable and faithful in Israel, and asks why Joab seeks to destroy a city and a mother in Israel, swallowing up the Lord's inheritance. Her plea reframes the siege as destruction of God's own people, not of a rebel stronghold. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

20 COMMENTARY: Joab answers, Far be it from me to swallow up or destroy. The captain disavows the very purpose his siege was pursuing. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

21 COMMENTARY: The matter is not so, Joab says: a man of Ephraim, Sheba son of Bichri, lifted his hand against the king; deliver him only, and Joab will depart from the city. The single condition of peace is the surrender of the traitor, Henry notes, as in God's dealing with the soul: part with the beloved sin, and all shall be well. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

22 COMMENTARY: The woman goes to all the people in her wisdom, they cut off Sheba's head and cast it out to Joab, and he blows the trumpet and his men depart from the city to their tents. A discreet woman, Henry says, by prudent management satisfies Joab and saves the city; wisdom is not confined to rank or sex, but consists in understanding how to act as matters arise. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

23 COMMENTARY: Joab is over all Israel's host, and Benaiah over the Cherethites and Pelethites. The restored court keeps the murderer as its commander, a fact the chapter records without approving. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

24 COMMENTARY: Adoram is over the tribute, and Jehoshaphat son of Ahilud is recorder. Revenue and records have their officers in the restored kingdom. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

25 COMMENTARY: Sheva is scribe, and Zadok and Abiathar are priests. Writing and worship stand beside the army in David's court. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

26 COMMENTARY: Ira the Jairite is also a chief ruler about David. The list closes with a courtier whose office is nearness to the king, and Henry concludes it is well when able men are appointed to public duties, to be performed as faithful servants to the Son of David. — Matthew Henry (Concise)
