Study Notes — 2 Samuel

Chapter 9

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: David asks whether any is left of Saul's house, that he may show him kindness for Jonathan's sake. This comes a great while after his accession, Henry notes, and it is good to bethink ourselves whether any promises we have neglected should be made good, better late than never. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: A servant of Saul's house named Ziba is called, and David asks if he is Ziba; he answers, Thy servant is he. Ziba, an old retainer, knows the state of Saul's family and can inform the king. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: David asks whether there is any left of Saul's house to whom he may show the kindness of God. Henry explains this kindness as covenant kindness after God's example, greater than one can ordinarily expect from men, done with an eye to God's honour. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: Ziba answers that Jonathan has yet a son lame on his feet, living in Machir's house in Lodebar. The son lives in obscurity, forgotten as a dead man out of mind, bearing it the more easily because he remembers little of the honour he fell from. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: David sends and fetches him out of Machir's house in Lodebar. In sending for him David eases Machir's trouble, and Machir, a generous man who sheltered the reduced son of a prince, will later furnish David himself at Mahanaim. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: Mephibosheth, son of Jonathan son of Saul, comes to David, falls on his face, and does reverence, answering, Behold thy servant. Though lame, he presents himself with all respect due to the king's character, and those who show respect in inferior relations shall have respect shown to them when advanced. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: David says, Fear not, for he will surely show kindness for Jonathan's sake, restore all Saul's land, and Mephibosheth shall eat bread at the king's table continually. Great men, Henry notes, should not take pleasure in the timorous approaches of inferiors, but encourage them; true friendship will be generous, giving the estate and a constant place at table. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: Mephibosheth bows and asks what his servant is, that David should look upon such a dead dog as he is. He accepts kindness with great humility, magnifying David's favour and vilifying himself, and Henry notes it is good to have the heart humble under humbling providences. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: David calls Ziba and gives him all that pertained to Saul and to all his house, for his master's son. The grant of the father's estate is confirmed, with Ziba called as witness to it. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: Ziba, his sons, and his servants shall till the land and bring in the fruits, that Mephibosheth may have food, while Mephibosheth eats at the king's table. Ziba, who knows the estate and has hands enough for it, manages it, though Henry warns that all who dwelt in Ziba's house lived upon Mephibosheth's estate. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: Ziba answers that as the king commanded, so his servant will do, and the king adds that Mephibosheth shall eat at his table as one of the king's sons. David will have him at his own table, not merely at Ziba's, and Mephibosheth is as pleased with his post as Ziba with his. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: Mephibosheth has a young son named Micha, and all who dwell in Ziba's house are servants to Mephibosheth. The household that serves the estate also lives upon it, a reminder that as goods increase, so do those who eat them. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: So Mephibosheth dwells in Jerusalem, eating continually at the king's table, lame on both his feet. Because David was a type of Christ, Henry says, his kindness to Mephibosheth illustrates the Saviour's kindness to fallen man: the Son of God seeks a race that sought not him, restores the forfeited inheritance, and sets them with his children at his table. — Matthew Henry
