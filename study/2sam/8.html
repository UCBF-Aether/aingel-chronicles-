Study Notes — 2 Samuel

Chapter 8

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: After this David smites the Philistines, subdues them, and takes Methegammah from their hand. They had long been troublesome to Israel, and Henry sees in their subduing a type of the Son of David treading the powers of darkness underfoot. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

2 COMMENTARY: He smites Moab and measures them with a line, putting two lines to death and keeping one full line alive, so the Moabites become servants bringing gifts. Henry notes the line kept alive, though but one, is ordered to be a full line: let the line of mercy be stretched to the utmost. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

3 COMMENTARY: David smites Hadadezer king of Zobah as he goes to recover his border at the Euphrates. The campaign pushes Israel's frontier to the river promised long before. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

4 COMMENTARY: David takes a thousand chariots, seven hundred horsemen, and twenty thousand footmen, houghing the chariot horses but reserving a hundred chariots. In all these wars, Henry notes, David was protected, and in his psalms he often gives glory to God for it. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

5 COMMENTARY: When the Syrians of Damascus come to succour Hadadezer, David slays twenty-two thousand of them. An ally's rescue attempt only widens the defeat. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

6 COMMENTARY: David puts garrisons in Syria of Damascus, the Syrians become his servants bringing gifts, and the Lord preserves David wherever he goes. Preservation in every campaign is the refrain of the chapter. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

7 COMMENTARY: David takes the shields of gold from Hadadezer's servants and brings them to Jerusalem. The precious spoil of a defeated king becomes treasure in the capital. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

8 COMMENTARY: From Betah and Berothai, cities of Hadadezer, David takes exceeding much brass. The raw material of war is gathered for a purpose beyond war. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

9 COMMENTARY: Toi king of Hamath hears David smote all Hadadezer's host. News of the victory travels to neighbouring kings, who must now reckon with David. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

10 COMMENTARY: Toi sends his son Joram to salute and bless David, because he had fought and smitten Hadadezer, who had wars with Toi, bringing vessels of silver, gold, and brass. An old enemy of David's enemy becomes his congratulator. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

11 COMMENTARY: All these things David dedicates to the Lord, with the silver and gold he had dedicated from all nations he subdued. Henry notes that in the conquest of a soul, what opposes God must be destroyed, but what may glorify him must be dedicated, its property altered. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

12 COMMENTARY: The dedication gathers spoil of Syria, Moab, Ammon, the Philistines, Amalek, and Hadadezer son of Rehob. Every conquered nation's tribute is redirected to the temple David's son will build. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

13 COMMENTARY: David gains a name when he returns from smiting the Syrians in the valley of salt, eighteen thousand men. God employs his servants variously, Henry observes: some, as David, in spiritual battles, and others, as Solomon, in spiritual buildings, one preparing work for the other. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

14 COMMENTARY: He puts garrisons throughout Edom, and all Edom becomes his servants, and the Lord preserves David wherever he goes. The southern frontier is secured as the northern one was. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

15 COMMENTARY: David reigns over all Israel and executes judgment and justice to all his people. He neither did wrong nor denied or delayed right to any, Henry notes, showing close application to business and readiness to hear every appeal, with no respect of persons. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

16 COMMENTARY: Joab is over the host, and Jehoshaphat son of Ahilud is recorder. The kingdom's wars and records each have a named officer, marking an ordered government. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

17 COMMENTARY: Zadok and Ahimelech are priests, and Seraiah is scribe. Worship and writing stand beside warfare in David's administration. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

18 COMMENTARY: Benaiah is over the Cherethites and Pelethites, and David's sons are chief rulers. Henry notes David made his sons chief rulers, but all believers, Christ's spiritual seed, are better preferred, made kings and priests to God. — Matthew Henry (Concise)
