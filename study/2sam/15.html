Study Notes — 2 Samuel

Chapter 15

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Absalom prepares chariots and horses and fifty men to run before him. David allows Absalom's pomp, Henry notes, and parents who indulge a proud humour in their children know not what they do: many young people are ruined by pride. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

2 COMMENTARY: Absalom rises early and stands beside the way of the gate, calling to every man with a controversy for the king. The gate is where justice is sought, and Absalom stations himself there to intercept it. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

3 COMMENTARY: Absalom tells each man his matters are good and right, but no man is deputed by the king to hear him. The flattery is twofold: the suit is praised, and the king is blamed for neglecting it. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

4 COMMENTARY: Absalom wishes he were made judge in the land, that every man with a suit might come to him and he would do him justice. Those most eager for authority, Henry observes, commonly least understand its duties. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

5 COMMENTARY: When any man comes to do obeisance, Absalom puts forth his hand, takes him, and kisses him. Royal distance is replaced with a studied familiarity that buys affection. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

6 COMMENTARY: In this manner Absalom does to all Israel that come for judgment, and so steals the hearts of the men of Israel. Four years of gate-courtship, not one act, win the nation. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

7 COMMENTARY: After forty years, Absalom asks the king to let him go and pay his vow to the Lord in Hebron. The long interval and the pious pretext show how willing tender parents are to believe the best concerning their children. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

8 COMMENTARY: He claims he vowed in Geshur that if the Lord brought him back to Jerusalem, he would serve the Lord. Religion is made the show that deceives a good parent, Henry warns, and children easily take advantage of good parents this way. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

9 COMMENTARY: The king says, Go in peace, and Absalom goes to Hebron. The blessing is given to a conspiracy, because the father believes the son's vow. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

10 COMMENTARY: Absalom sends spies through all Israel, saying that at the trumpet's sound they shall say, Absalom reigns in Hebron. The vow journey is revealed as a coronation plot, timed to a signal. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

11 COMMENTARY: Two hundred men go with Absalom from Jerusalem, called and going in simplicity, knowing nothing. Pious persons are glad to see others appear religious, Henry notes, and this gives occasion for deceptions that draw good persons to countenance base designs. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

12 COMMENTARY: Absalom sends for Ahithophel the Gilonite, David's counsellor, from Giloh while offering sacrifices, and the conspiracy grows strong as the people increase with Absalom. The defection of David's own counsellor gives the revolt its brains. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

13 COMMENTARY: A messenger tells David that the hearts of the men of Israel are after Absalom. The report is blunt and total: not a faction, but the hearts of the nation. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

14 COMMENTARY: David tells his servants at Jerusalem to arise and flee, lest Absalom overtake them, smite the city, and leave no escape. He determines to quit Jerusalem as a penitent submitting to the rod, Henry says: before unrighteous Absalom he could justify himself, but before the righteous God he must condemn himself and yield. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

15 COMMENTARY: The king's servants answer that they are ready to do whatever the king appoints. In flight, David still has a household that chooses him freely. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

16 COMMENTARY: The king goes forth with all his household, leaving ten concubines to keep the house. The abandoned house will become the stage for Absalom's public insult. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

17 COMMENTARY: The king goes forth and all the people after him, tarrying in a place far off. The exodus from Jerusalem is orderly, not a rout, and David leads it himself. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

18 COMMENTARY: All his servants pass beside him, with the Cherethites, Pelethites, and six hundred Gittites who came from Gath. The foreign guard that followed David from Gath stays with him when Israel leaves. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

19 COMMENTARY: David asks Ittai the Gittite why he goes also, urging him to return and abide with the king, since he is a stranger and exile. Good men, Henry notes, when they suffer, are anxious that others not be led to suffer with them, and David cannot bear that a new convert meet hard usage. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

20 COMMENTARY: David reminds Ittai he came but yesterday, and asks whether he should be made to wander when David himself goes whither he may, bidding him return with mercy and truth. He compels none, Henry observes, and thus Christ enlists none but willing followers. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

21 COMMENTARY: Ittai swears that where the king shall be, whether in death or life, there his servant will be. He is a friend indeed, Henry says, who loves at all times and adheres in adversity, and so should we cleave to the Son of David. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

22 COMMENTARY: David says, Go and pass over, and Ittai passes over with all his men and little ones. The exile's household is accepted into David's wandering company. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

23 COMMENTARY: All the country weeps aloud, and all the people pass over, the king himself passing over Kidron toward the wilderness. The nation's weeping marks a king leaving his capital on foot. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

24 COMMENTARY: Zadok and all the Levites come bearing the ark of God, set it down, and Abiathar goes up until all the people pass over. The ark itself is carried into exile with the king. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

25 COMMENTARY: David tells Zadok to carry the ark back into the city: if he finds favour in the Lord's eyes, God will bring him again and show him both it and his habitation. David is very careful for the ark's safety, Henry notes, and it is right to be more concerned for the church's prosperity than our own. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

26 COMMENTARY: But if God says, I have no delight in thee, behold, here am I, let him do to me as seems good to him. With satisfaction and submission David speaks of the divine disposal, and Henry urges us cheerfully to acquiesce in God's will and see all events in God's hand. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

27 COMMENTARY: David tells Zadok the priest, as a seer, to return into the city in peace with his son Ahimaaz and Jonathan son of Abiathar. The priests become his eyes in the city he has left. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

28 COMMENTARY: David will tarry in the plains of the wilderness until word comes from them to certify him. The fugitive king sets up a line of messengers rather than marching blind. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

29 COMMENTARY: Zadok and Abiathar carry the ark back to Jerusalem and tarry there. The ark returns to the city while the king waits in the wilderness. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

30 COMMENTARY: David goes up the ascent of Olivet weeping, head covered, barefoot, and all the people with him cover every man his head and weep as they go. He never wept thus when Saul hunted him, Henry notes, but a wounded conscience makes troubles lie heavy, and his sin was ever before him. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

31 COMMENTARY: One tells David that Ahithophel is among the conspirators, and David prays, O Lord, turn the counsel of Ahithophel into foolishness. He prays not against Ahithophel's person but against his counsel, believing God has all hearts and tongues in his hand. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

32 COMMENTARY: At the top of the mount where he worshipped God, Hushai the Archite meets David with his coat rent and earth on his head. Worship on the height is interrupted by a loyal friend in mourning dress. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

33 COMMENTARY: David tells Hushai that if he passes on with him he will be a burden. Even in flight David weighs the cost his followers bear. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

34 COMMENTARY: But if Hushai returns to the city and offers Absalom the service he gave his father, he may defeat Ahithophel's counsel for David. Prayer is seconded with endeavour, Henry insists, else we tempt God. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

35 COMMENTARY: Zadok and Abiathar the priests are there, David says, and whatever Hushai hears in the king's house he shall tell them. A network of priests and sons will carry the intelligence out. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

36 COMMENTARY: Their two sons, Ahimaaz and Jonathan, are there, and by them Hushai shall send David everything he hears. The chain of messengers is fixed before Hushai enters the city. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

37 COMMENTARY: So Hushai, David's friend, comes into the city, and Absalom comes into Jerusalem. Friend and usurper enter the capital on the same day, and the contest for counsel begins. — Matthew Henry (Concise)
