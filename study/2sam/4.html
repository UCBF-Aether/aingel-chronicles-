Study Notes — 2 Samuel

Chapter 4

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: When Saul's son hears Abner is dead in Hebron, his hands grow feeble and all Israel is troubled. Henry's lesson is that difficulties which should sharpen our endeavours, when they dispirit us instead, betray both heavenly crowns and earthly lives. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

2 COMMENTARY: Saul's son has two captains of bands, Baanah and Rechab, sons of Rimmon the Beerothite of Benjamin. The men positioned to guard a king are the very men who will betray him. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

3 COMMENTARY: The Beerothites had fled to Gittaim and remained sojourners there. The aside explains the family's displaced standing, a clan already uprooted before its sons turn to murder. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

4 COMMENTARY: Jonathan's son Mephibosheth, lame in his feet since a fall at five years old when his nurse fled with the tidings from Jezreel, is introduced here. His helplessness stands in contrast to the strong men who plot around the throne. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

5 COMMENTARY: Rechab and Baanah come at midday heat to Ishbosheth's house while he lies on his bed at noon. Henry warns against loving sleep lest we come to poverty and ruin, for the idle soul is an easy prey to the destroyer. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

6 COMMENTARY: They enter as though to fetch wheat and smite him under the fifth rib, then escape. Murder is disguised as an ordinary errand, and no hour is safe: we know not when or from what hand the death-blow may come. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

7 COMMENTARY: They find him on his bed in his bedchamber, slay and behead him, and carry his head away through the plain all night. The grisly trophy is meant to purchase favour with David. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

8 COMMENTARY: They present Ishbosheth's head to David, claiming the Lord has avenged his lord on Saul's house. A man may be glad to obtain his just wishes, Henry notes, and yet truly regret the means by which he receives them. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

9 COMMENTARY: David answers by the Lord who redeemed his soul out of all adversity. He will not be beholden to anyone who helps him by unlawful practices, trusting God to crown and complete his own work. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

10 COMMENTARY: David recalls the man who brought tidings of Saul's death expecting a reward, and whom he slew at Ziklag. The precedent is set: David does not pay for blood, even the blood of his enemy. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

11 COMMENTARY: David asks how much more these wicked men, who slew a righteous person in his own house upon his bed, deserve to have their blood required. They shed innocent blood from the basest motives, and David justly executes vengeance upon them. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

12 COMMENTARY: David's young men slay them, cut off their hands and feet, and hang them over the pool in Hebron, while Ishbosheth's head is buried in Abner's sepulchre. The public punishment answers the public crime, and the murdered king receives an honourable burial. — Matthew Henry (Concise)
