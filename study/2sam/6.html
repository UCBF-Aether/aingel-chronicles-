Study Notes — 2 Samuel

Chapter 6

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: David again gathers all the chosen men of Israel, thirty thousand. Now that David is settled on the throne, Henry notes, the honour of the ark begins to revive after long neglect. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

2 COMMENTARY: David goes with all the people from Baale of Judah to bring up the ark, named by the name of the Lord of hosts dwelling between the cherubim. The ark is the token of God's presence, and Henry says it typified Christ and his mediation, in which Jehovah's name and glories are displayed. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

3 COMMENTARY: They set the ark on a new cart from Abinadab's house, driven by Uzzah and Ahio. Henry warns that the priests should have carried it on their shoulders: Philistines might carry it in a cart unpunished, but Israelites who disobey God's appointment do so at their peril. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

4 COMMENTARY: The ark is brought out with Ahio going before it. The procession honours the ark outwardly while still breaking the appointed manner of carrying it. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

5 COMMENTARY: David and all Israel play before the Lord on instruments of fir wood, harps, psalteries, timbrels, cornets, and cymbals. Joy before God is expressed with the whole body and every endowment, Henry observes, though joy must be joined to reverence. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

6 COMMENTARY: At Nachon's threshingfloor the oxen shake the cart, and Uzzah puts forth his hand and takes hold of the ark. Familiarity even with what is most awful, Henry warns, is apt to breed contempt. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

7 COMMENTARY: The Lord's anger is kindled against Uzzah, and God smites him there for his error, and he dies by the ark. God saw presumption and irreverence in Uzzah's heart, not merely a helpful hand. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

8 COMMENTARY: David is displeased because the Lord made a breach upon Uzzah, and names the place Perezuzzah. The breach interrupts the celebration and teaches that God's holiness is not to be managed by good intentions. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

9 COMMENTARY: David is afraid of the Lord that day and asks how the ark shall come to him. Fear replaces festivity, and the question is honest: a holy God cannot be brought in on man's terms. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

10 COMMENTARY: David will not bring the ark into the city of David, but carries it aside into Obededom the Gittite's house. Delay, for David, is better than a second breach. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

11 COMMENTARY: The ark remains in Obededom's house three months, and the Lord blesses Obededom and all his household. The same hand that punished Uzzah's proud presumption rewarded Obededom's humble boldness, and it is good to live in a family that entertains the ark. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

12 COMMENTARY: David hears the Lord blessed Obededom's house because of the ark, and goes to bring it up with gladness. It became evident, Henry says, that happy is the man who has the ark near him. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

13 COMMENTARY: When the bearers have gone six paces, David sacrifices oxen and fatlings. At first setting out he offered sacrifices to God, for we are likely to speed in our enterprises when we begin with God and seek peace with him. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

14 COMMENTARY: David dances before the Lord with all his might, girded with a linen ephod. He lays aside his royal robes for a plain linen dress, serving God with his whole body and soul. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

15 COMMENTARY: David and all Israel bring up the ark with shouting and the sound of the trumpet. The second procession is marked by worship that has learned reverence from the first failure. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

16 COMMENTARY: Michal, watching from a window, sees David leaping and dancing and despises him in her heart. Exercises of religion appear mean, Henry notes, in the eyes of those who have little or no religion themselves. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

17 COMMENTARY: The ark is set in the tabernacle David pitched for it, and he offers burnt and peace offerings before the Lord. Our joy in God, Henry reminds, must be connected with repentance and faith in the Redeemer's atoning blood. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

18 COMMENTARY: Having finished the offerings, David blesses the people in the name of the Lord of hosts. He prays with and for the people and, as a prophet, solemnly blesses them. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

19 COMMENTARY: David deals to every person, women and men alike, a cake of bread, a good piece of flesh, and a flagon of wine, and the people depart. The national mercy is shared as a feast, so that all Israel tastes the blessing. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

20 COMMENTARY: David returns to bless his household, but Michal meets him mocking how glorious the king was, uncovering himself before his servants' eyes. Even princes' palaces, Henry observes, are not free from family troubles. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

21 COMMENTARY: David answers that it was before the Lord, who chose him before her father and all his house to rule Israel, that he will play. If we can approve ourselves to God in what we do in religion, Henry says, we need not heed reproach. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

22 COMMENTARY: David will be yet more vile and base in his own sight, and expects honour from the maidservants Michal mentioned. It is angels' work to worship God, and that cannot lower the greatest of men. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

23 COMMENTARY: Therefore Michal has no child to the day of her death. David does not further reprove her, Henry notes, but God punishes her: those who honour God he will honour, but those who despise him and his service shall be lightly esteemed. — Matthew Henry (Concise)
