Study Notes — 2 Samuel

Chapter 10

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: After this the king of Ammon dies, and his son Hanun reigns in his stead. A change of king brings a change of temper, and David's kindness will meet a very different reception. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

2 COMMENTARY: David resolves to show kindness to Hanun as his father Nahash showed kindness to him, sending servants to comfort him. Nahash had been an enemy to Israel, Henry notes, yet had shown David kindness, and David gratefully returns it. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

3 COMMENTARY: Ammon's princes persuade Hanun that David's comforters are really spies come to search and overthrow the city. Those who bear ill-will to their neighbours, Henry observes, are resolved not to believe their neighbours bear any good-will to them. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

4 COMMENTARY: Hanun shaves off half the servants' beards, cuts their garments in the middle, and sends them away. By the usages of those days, Henry says, this was the most contemptuous treatment of ambassadors possible. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

5 COMMENTARY: David, told of it, sends to meet the ashamed men and bids them tarry at Jericho until their beards grow. He shows much concern for his servants, and Henry draws the lesson not to lay unjust reproaches to heart: they wear off, and reputation wrongfully hurt grows again, as these beards did. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

6 COMMENTARY: Seeing they stink before David, the Ammonites hire Syrians of Bethrehob and Zoba, twenty thousand footmen, with the king of Maacah and Ishtob. They that are at war with the Son of David, Henry notes, not only give provocation but begin the war. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

7 COMMENTARY: David hears of it and sends Joab and all the host of the mighty men. The insult to ambassadors is answered with the full army, not with a protest. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

8 COMMENTARY: The Ammonites array at the gate, while the Syrians of Zoba, Rehob, Ishtob, and Maacah stand by themselves in the field. Israel faces a two-front battle, city before and field behind. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

9 COMMENTARY: Joab, seeing battle before and behind, chooses the choice men of Israel and arrays them against the Syrians. The best troops are set where the danger is greatest. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

10 COMMENTARY: The rest he delivers to Abishai his brother, to array against Ammon. Command is divided so neither front is left without a captain. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

11 COMMENTARY: Joab pledges mutual help: if the Syrians are too strong for him, Abishai shall help him, and if Ammon is too strong for Abishai, Joab will come. Christ's soldiers, Henry says, should strengthen one another's hands in their spiritual warfare. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

12 COMMENTARY: Be of good courage, and let us play the men for our people and the cities of our God, and let the Lord do what seems good to him. When we make conscience of doing our duty, Henry notes, we may leave the event with God, hoping for his salvation in his own way and time. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

13 COMMENTARY: Joab draws near against the Syrians, and they flee before him. The first front collapses without a prolonged fight. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

14 COMMENTARY: When Ammon sees the Syrians fled, they flee before Abishai into the city, and Joab returns to Jerusalem. The second front follows the first, and the campaign ends for the season. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

15 COMMENTARY: The Syrians, seeing they were smitten before Israel, gather themselves together again. Even a baffled cause, Henry observes, will make head as long as there is any life in it. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

16 COMMENTARY: Hadarezer brings out the Syrians beyond the river to Helam, with Shobach captain of his host before them. The enemy regroups across the Euphrates for a second, larger attempt. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

17 COMMENTARY: David gathers all Israel, passes over Jordan to Helam, and the Syrians array against him. This time the king himself leads, and the battle is joined on the far side of the river. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

18 COMMENTARY: The Syrians flee before Israel; David slays men of seven hundred chariots and forty thousand horsemen, and smites Shobach, who dies there. With this the promise to Abraham and Joshua, that Israel's borders should extend to the Euphrates, is performed. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

19 COMMENTARY: All the kings who served Hadarezer, seeing they were smitten, make peace with Israel and serve them, and the Syrians fear to help Ammon any more. Henry's lesson is that it is dangerous to help those who have God against them, for when they fall, their helpers fall with them. — Matthew Henry (Concise)
