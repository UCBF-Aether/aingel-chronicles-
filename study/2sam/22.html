Study Notes — 2 Samuel

Chapter 22

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: David speaks the words of this song to the Lord in the day the Lord delivered him from all his enemies and from Saul. It has often been the lot of God's people to have many enemies, Henry notes, and David, a man after God's heart but not after men's, was hated by many; yet those who trust God in duty find him a present help in their greatest dangers, and every new mercy should put a new song in the mouth. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: The Lord is my rock, my fortress, and my deliverer. If God is my God, Henry says, then he is my rock, my strength and power, the rock under which I take shelter and on which I build my hope. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: The God of my rock, in whom I trust, is shield, horn of salvation, high tower, refuge, and saviour who saves from violence. David often hid in a rock, Henry observes, but God was his chief hiding-place, and the horn of salvation both protects strongly and pushes enemies strongly. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: I will call on the Lord, worthy to be praised, and so shall I be saved from my enemies. What we find in God worthy of praise, Henry notes, should engage us to pray to him and give him glory. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: The waves of death compass him, and the floods of ungodly men make him afraid. The danger was very great, Henry says: surrounded with death on every side, threatened to be overwhelmed, seeing no way of escape. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: The sorrows of hell compass him, and the snares of death prevent him. The floods of Belial and his instruments made him afraid, as if earth, death, and hell were all in arms against him. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: In distress he calls on the Lord and cries to his God, and God hears his voice out of his temple, and his cry enters his ears. His deliverance was an answer to prayer, Henry notes, and he leaves the example of crying to God with importunity, as frightened children cry to their parents. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: Then the earth shakes and trembles, and heaven's foundations move because God is wroth. The expressions are borrowed from Sinai, Henry says, to set forth God's glory manifested in the deliverance and his displeasure against his enemies. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: Smoke goes up from his nostrils, fire from his mouth devours, and coals are kindled by it. God's anger is set forth in these metaphors, and who knows the power and terror of his wrath? — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: He bows the heavens, comes down, and darkness is under his feet. God, who seemed at a distance, comes near in judgment and rescue. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: He rides upon a cherub and flies, seen upon the wings of the wind. God hastens to his succour with seasonable relief, Henry notes, though he had seemed far off. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: He makes darkness pavilions round about him, dark waters and thick clouds of the skies. God hides himself in darkness for the amazement of his enemies and the protection of his people. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: Through the brightness before him coals of fire are kindled. Light and fire together mark the divine appearing: glory to the delivered, terror to the opposer. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: The Lord thunders from heaven, and the Most High utters his voice. The voice from above answers the cry from below, and the battle turns on a word. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: He sends out arrows and scatters them, lightning, and discomfits them. The extraordinary confusion of his enemies is as if the earth trembled and the world's foundations were discovered. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: The channels of the sea appear and the world's foundations are discovered at the Lord's rebuking, at the blast of his nostrils. Who can stand before God when he is angry, Henry asks. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: He sends from above, takes David, and draws him out of many waters. Rescue reaches down from heaven into the flood that was drowning him. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: He delivers David from his strong enemy and from haters too strong for him. The strong enemy is Saul, Henry says, who would have been too hard for David had God not succoured him. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: They prevent him in the day of his calamity, but the Lord is his stay. Burdened and ready to sink, he is supported by the Lord, who holds him up. — Matthew Henry

20 COMMENTARY: He brings David into a large place and delivers him because he delights in him. The deliverance comes not from common providence but covenant love, Henry notes, and David is herein a type of Christ, whom God upheld because he delighted in him. — Matthew Henry

21 COMMENTARY: The Lord rewards David according to his righteousness, according to the cleanness of his hands. God recompenses him according to his righteousness, Henry explains, not as if righteousness merited anything, but as what God is well pleased with and has an eye to. — Matthew Henry

22 COMMENTARY: He has kept the ways of the Lord and has not wickedly departed from his God. His conscience witnesses that he kept to God's word as his rule, taking his religion wherever he went. — Matthew Henry

23 COMMENTARY: All God's judgments are before him, and as for his statutes, he does not depart from them. Wherever he was, God's judgments were his guide, and he did not depart from the statutes even when exiled. — Matthew Henry

24 COMMENTARY: He is upright before God and keeps himself from his iniquity. Careful abstaining from our own iniquity, Henry says, is one of the best evidences of integrity, and David reflected with more comfort on victories over his own iniquity than on Goliath. — Matthew Henry

25 COMMENTARY: Therefore the Lord recompenses him according to his righteousness, according to his cleanness in God's eyesight. The witness of his own heart to uprightness is sweeter, Henry notes, than the song that David slew his ten thousands. — Matthew Henry

26 COMMENTARY: With the merciful thou wilt show thyself merciful, and with the upright thou wilt show thyself upright. As we are found toward God, Henry says, he will be found toward us, and even the merciful need mercy and shall obtain it. — Matthew Henry

27 COMMENTARY: With the pure thou wilt show thyself pure, and with the froward thou wilt show thyself unsavoury. God's purity is the joy of the pure, Henry notes, but with the froward he wrestles, and those with whom God wrestles are sure to be foiled. — Matthew Henry

28 COMMENTARY: The afflicted people thou wilt save, but thine eyes are on the haughty to bring them down. The afflicted are saved out of or by their afflictions, while the haughty are marked out, for God resists the proud. — Matthew Henry

29 COMMENTARY: Thou art my lamp, O Lord, and the Lord will lighten my darkness. In the dark, benighted and at a loss, David finds God his lamp to show the way and dispel darkness. — Matthew Henry

30 COMMENTARY: By thee he runs through a troop, and by his God leaps over a wall. Military swiftness and boldness are both credited to God, not to David's own force. — Matthew Henry

31 COMMENTARY: As for God, his way is perfect; the word of the Lord is tried, a buckler to all who trust in him. Men begin in kindness but end not, promise but perform not, Henry observes, but God finishes his work, and his word is tried and trustworthy. — Matthew Henry

32 COMMENTARY: Who is God save the Lord, and who is a rock save our God? All others adored as deities are counterfeits, Henry says; none is to be relied on but he, for they disappoint worshippers when most needed, but the Lord lives. — Matthew Henry

33 COMMENTARY: God is his strength and power, and makes his way perfect. Called to fight God's battles, David was qualified for the service, and God gave him success in all his undertakings. — Matthew Henry

34 COMMENTARY: He makes David's feet like hinds' feet and sets him on his high places. Swiftness in charging and retreating, Henry notes, is of great advantage, and the high places denote both safety and dignity. — Matthew Henry

35 COMMENTARY: He teaches David's hands to war, so that a bow of steel is broken by his arms. Though bred to the crook, David was instructed in war, and his ingenuity was as good as strength, breaking steel by dexterity. — Matthew Henry

36 COMMENTARY: God gives the shield of salvation, and his gentleness makes David great. God's grace and tender mercy, Henry says, gave him wealth, authority, and a name like the great men of the earth. — Matthew Henry

37 COMMENTARY: God enlarges David's steps under him, so his feet do not slip. Brought into a large place, he has room to thrive and stir, no longer straitened and confined. — Matthew Henry

38 COMMENTARY: He pursues his enemies, destroys them, and turns not again until he has consumed them. The victories are speedy, Henry notes, and complete: the enemies are wounded, destroyed, consumed, and fall under his feet. — Matthew Henry

39 COMMENTARY: He consumes and wounds them so they cannot arise, fallen under his feet. Disabled to rise, they lie at his mercy, trampled and spent. — Matthew Henry

40 COMMENTARY: God girds him with strength for battle and subdues those who rise against him. David gives God the glory of all his courage and ability for service. — Matthew Henry

41 COMMENTARY: God gives him the necks of his enemies, that he may destroy those who hate him. Victory is described as necks laid at the victor's mercy. — Matthew Henry

42 COMMENTARY: They look, but there is none to save, even to the Lord, but he answers them not. They cry to earth and heaven in vain, Henry says, for they are not on God's side and cry only at the last extremity. — Matthew Henry

43 COMMENTARY: He beats them as small as the dust of the earth, stamps them as mire in the street, and spreads them abroad. Abandoned, they become an easy prey, scattered like dust by wind and trodden by every foot. — Matthew Henry

44 COMMENTARY: God delivers him from the strivings of his people and keeps him to be head of the heathen; a people he knew not shall serve him. His preservations show he was reserved for something great: to rule all Israel despite the people's strivings. — Matthew Henry

45 COMMENTARY: Strangers submit to him; as soon as they hear, they obey him. The sons of the stranger, hearing the report of his victories and God's presence with him, are possessed with fear and forced to submit, though feignedly. — Matthew Henry

46 COMMENTARY: Strangers fade away and are afraid out of their close places. Past successes are earnests of more, Henry notes, and thus the Son of David goes on conquering and to conquer. — Matthew Henry

47 COMMENTARY: The Lord lives, blessed be his rock, and exalted be the God of the rock of his salvation. The living God stands against dead counterfeits that disappoint their worshippers. — Matthew Henry

48 COMMENTARY: It is God who avenges David and brings down the people under him. Vengeance is God's work, not David's private revenge, and the subduing of peoples is credited to him. — Matthew Henry

49 COMMENTARY: God brings David forth from his enemies, lifts him above those who rise against him, and delivers him from the violent man. After all his dangers, Henry says, he gained his point and was lifted as high as the throne. — Matthew Henry

50 COMMENTARY: Therefore David will give thanks to the Lord among the heathen and sing praises to his name. Among the heathen he will neither be afraid nor ashamed to own his obligations to the God of Israel. — Matthew Henry

51 COMMENTARY: He is the tower of salvation for his king, and shows mercy to his anointed, to David and his seed for evermore. The tower of salvation can never be scaled, battered, or undermined, Henry says, and David's hopes terminate in Christ, who alone is his seed for evermore, whose throne still continues. — Matthew Henry
