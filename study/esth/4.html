Study Notes — Esther

Chapter 4

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: When Mordecai perceived all that was done, Mordecai rent his clothes, and put on sackcloth with ashes, and went out into the midst of the city, and cried with a loud and bitter cry. Henry notes Mordecai mourns in public, not in private, so that the city hears what the decree means. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: And came even before the king's gate: for none might enter into the king's gate clothed with sackcloth. JFB notes the limit of grief: sackcloth may cry in the street, but it may not enter the gate, so Mordecai stops where Esther can hear of him. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: And in every province, whithersoever the king's commandment and decree came, there was great mourning among the Jews, and fasting, and weeping, and wailing; and many lay in sackcloth and ashes. Henry notes the mourning is empire-wide, matching the decree's empire-wide reach. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: So Esther's maids and chamberlains came and told it her. Then was the queen exceedingly grieved; and she sent raiment to clothe Mordecai, and to take away his sackcloth from him: but he received it not. JFB notes Esther first tries to comfort Mordecai by removing the sign, but he refuses: the cause of grief must be removed, not its clothing. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: Then called Esther for Hatach, one of the king's chamberlains, whom he had appointed to attend upon her, and gave him a commandment to Mordecai, to know what it was, and why it was. Henry notes Esther, shut in the palace, must learn through a messenger what the whole city already knows. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: So Hatach went forth to Mordecai unto the street of the city, which was before the king's gate. The street before the gate becomes the meeting place of palace and people. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: And Mordecai told him of all that had happened unto him, and of the sum of the money that Haman had promised to pay to the king's treasuries for the Jews, to destroy them. JFB notes Mordecai names the price: the Jews are told they have been sold, not merely condemned. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: Also he gave him the copy of the writing of the decree given at Shushan to destroy them, to show it unto Esther, and to declare it unto her, and to charge her that she should go in unto the king, to make supplication unto him, and to make request before him for her people. Henry notes Mordecai sends not only news but the document, and with it a charge: Esther must go to the king herself. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: And Hatach came and told Esther the words of Mordecai. The message is delivered whole, charge and all. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: Again Esther spake unto Hatach, and gave him commandment unto Mordecai. Esther answers, but her answer is a difficulty, not a refusal. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: All the king's servants, and the people of the provinces, do know, that whosoever, whether man or woman, shall come unto the king into the inner court, who is not called, there is one law of his to put him to death, except such to whom the king shall hold out the golden sceptre, that he may live: but I have not been called to come in unto the king these thirty days. JFB notes the law is stated exactly: uncalled entry means death unless the sceptre is held out, and thirty days without summons makes the risk real. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: And they told to Mordecai Esther's words. The difficulty is carried back faithfully, without softening. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: Then Mordecai commanded to answer Esther, Think not with thyself that thou shalt escape in the king's house, more than all the Jews. Henry notes the warning strips away palace safety: the decree names all Jews, and a queen's chamber is no refuge from it. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: For if thou altogether holdest thy peace at this time, then shall there enlargement and deliverance arise to the Jews from another place; but thou and thy father's house shall be destroyed: and who knows whether thou art come to the kingdom for such a time as this? JFB notes Mordecai's faith: deliverance does not depend on Esther, but her place in it may be the very reason she is queen. Henry calls this the hinge of the book: providence appoints the person to the hour. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: Then Esther bade them return Mordecai this answer. Esther's answer comes after Mordecai's challenge, not before it. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: Go, gather together all the Jews that are present in Shushan, and fast ye for me, and neither eat nor drink three days, night or day: I also and my maidens will fast likewise; and so will I go in unto the king, which is not according to the law: and if I perish, I perish. Henry notes the resolution joins fasting and action: the Jews fast, Esther goes, and the risk is accepted in the same breath as the resolve. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: So Mordecai went his way, and did according to all that Esther had commanded him. JFB notes the reversal is complete: Mordecai, who charged Esther, now obeys Esther's command, and the fast begins. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
