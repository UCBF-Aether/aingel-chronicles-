Study Notes — Esther

Chapter 9

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Now in the twelfth month, Adar, on the thirteenth day, when the king's commandment and decree drew near to be put in execution, in the day that the enemies of the Jews hoped to have power over them, though it was turned to the contrary, that the Jews had rule over them that hated them. Henry notes the chapter's thesis in one sentence: the day hoped for by enemies becomes the day of their overthrow. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: The Jews gathered themselves together in their cities throughout all the provinces of Ahasuerus, to lay hand on such as sought their hurt: and no man could withstand them; for the fear of them fell upon all people. JFB notes the Jews act together, in their cities, under the king's licence, not as a mob. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: And all the rulers of the provinces, lieutenants, deputies and officers of the king, helped the Jews; because the fear of Mordecai fell upon them. Henry notes the officials read the court: Mordecai's rise makes helping the Jews the safe course. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: For Mordecai was great in the king's house, and his fame went out throughout all the provinces: for this man Mordecai waxed greater and greater. JFB notes Mordecai's growing greatness is the political fact behind the Jews' safety. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: Thus the Jews smote all their enemies with the stroke of the sword, and slaughter, and destruction, and did what they would unto those that hated them. Henry notes the victory is complete but defensive in law: they strike those who sought their hurt, under the counter-decree. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: And in Shushan the palace the Jews slew and destroyed five hundred men. The capital's toll is counted first, as the place where the decree was first published. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: And Parshandatha, and Dalphon, and Aspatha. These are the first three of Haman's ten sons, named at the head of the list of the slain. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: The ten sons of Haman son of Hammedatha, the enemy of the Jews, slew they; but on the spoil laid they not their hand. JFB notes the sons share their father's fate, and Henry stresses the restraint: though the decree allowed the spoil, the Jews take none, showing the fight was for life, not plunder. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: On that day the number of those slain in Shushan the palace was brought before the king. The toll is reported to the king the same day, as a matter of state. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: Henry notes the king, hearing the capital's number, again opens his favour to Esther and invites a further request, which she will use to ask for a second day in Shushan. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: Then said Esther, If it please the king, let it be granted to the Jews in Shushan to do to morrow also according unto this day's decree, and let Haman's ten sons be hanged upon the gallows. JFB notes Esther asks for a second day in the capital and for the public hanging of the sons' bodies, to break the remaining resistance in Shushan. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: And the king commanded it so to be done: and the decree was given at Shushan; and they hanged Haman's ten sons. The second decree is issued and executed in the capital. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: For the Jews in Shushan gathered themselves together on the fourteenth day also of Adar, and slew three hundred men at Shushan; but on the prey they laid not their hand. Henry notes the second day's restraint repeats the first: victory again without plunder. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: But the other Jews in the king's provinces gathered themselves together, and stood for their lives, and had rest from their enemies, and slew of their foes seventy-five thousand, but they laid not their hands on the prey. JFB notes the provinces' number is vast, yet the same restraint holds everywhere, which the narrator repeats as a point of honour. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: On the thirteenth day of Adar; and on the fourteenth day rested they, and made it a day of feasting and gladness. The provinces rest and feast the day after the fighting, turning a day of slaughter into a day of gladness. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

18 COMMENTARY: But the Jews at Shushan assembled together on the thirteenth day, and on the fourteenth; and on the fifteenth day they rested, and made it a day of feasting and gladness. Henry notes the capital's extra day of fighting explains its later feast day: Shushan rests on the fifteenth, the provinces on the fourteenth. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

19 COMMENTARY: Therefore the Jews of the villages, that dwelt in the unwalled towns, made the fourteenth day of Adar a day of gladness and feasting, and a good day, and of sending portions one to another. JFB notes the village Jews, without walls, keep the fourteenth, and the feast is social: portions sent to neighbours, not only eaten at home. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

20 COMMENTARY: And Mordecai wrote these things, and sent letters unto all the Jews in all the provinces of Ahasuerus, both nigh and far. Henry notes Mordecai the deliverer becomes Mordecai the recorder, writing the deliverance down so it will not depend on memory. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

21 COMMENTARY: To stablish this among them, that they should keep the fourteenth day of Adar, and the fifteenth day of the same, yearly. The letters fix both days, covering both the provinces' and the capital's observance. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

22 COMMENTARY: As the days wherein the Jews rested from their enemies, and the month which was turned unto them from sorrow to joy, and from mourning into a good day: that they should make them days of feasting and joy, and of sending portions one to another, and gifts to the poor. JFB notes the feast's character: joy shared, with portions to neighbours and gifts to the poor, so that gladness reaches those who cannot feast themselves. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

23 COMMENTARY: And the Jews undertook to do as they had begun, and as Mordecai had written unto them. The people accept in writing what they had begun in practice, making custom into ordinance. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

24 COMMENTARY: Because Haman son of Hammedatha, the Agagite, the enemy of all the Jews, had devised against the Jews to destroy them, and had cast Pur, that is, the lot, to consume them, and to destroy them. Henry notes the letter rehearses the cause: the feast is named for the lot that was meant to choose the Jews' destruction. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

25 COMMENTARY: But when Esther came before the king, he commanded by letters that his wicked device, which he devised against the Jews, should return upon his own head, and that he and his sons should be hanged on the gallows. JFB notes the reversal is stated as law: the device returns on its deviser, by the king's own letters. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

26 COMMENTARY: Wherefore they called these days Purim after the name of Pur. Therefore for all the words of this letter, and of that which they had seen concerning this matter, and which had come unto them. The name Purim turns the lot, an instrument of chance, into a memorial of providence. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

27 COMMENTARY: The Jews ordained, and took upon them, and upon their seed, and upon all such as joined themselves unto them, so as it should not fail, that they would keep these two days according to their writing, and according to their appointed time every year. Henry notes the ordinance binds seed and proselytes alike, and is made perpetual: 'so as it should not fail.' — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

28 COMMENTARY: And that these days should be remembered and kept throughout every generation, every family, every province, and every city; and that these days of Purim should not fail from among the Jews, nor the memorial of them perish from their seed. JFB notes the fourfold spread, generation, family, province, city, guards the memory against any single failure. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

29 COMMENTARY: Then Esther the queen, the daughter of Abihail, and Mordecai the Jew, wrote with all authority, to confirm this second letter of Purim. Henry notes the second letter carries both authorities: the queen's and Mordecai's, confirming what Mordecai's first letter established. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

30 COMMENTARY: And he sent the letters unto all the Jews, to the hundred and twenty-seven provinces of Ahasuerus, with words of peace and truth. JFB notes the letters' character is named: peace and truth, not triumph alone. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

31 COMMENTARY: To confirm these days of Purim in their times appointed, according as Mordecai the Jew and Esther the queen had enjoined them, and as they had decreed for themselves and for their seed, the matters of the fastings and their cry. Henry notes the confirmation joins the feast to the fast: Purim remembers both the cry that went up and the joy that answered it. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

32 COMMENTARY: And the decree of Esther confirmed these matters of Purim; and it was written in the book. The book closes its Purim legislation with a written record, so that the feast rests on a document, not on tradition alone. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

No note for verse(s) 8, 9 — the public-domain sources consulted give no distinct explanation for these verses beyond the list itself, so nothing was invented for them.
