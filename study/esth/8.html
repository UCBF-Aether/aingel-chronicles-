Study Notes — Esther

Chapter 8

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: On that day did the king Ahasuerus give the house of Haman the Jews' enemy unto Esther the queen. And Mordecai came before the king; for Esther had told what he was unto her. JFB notes the transfer is immediate: Haman's estate passes to the queen, and Mordecai, now known as her kinsman, is admitted to the king. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: And the king took off his ring, which he had taken from Haman, and gave it unto Mordecai. And Esther set Mordecai over the house of Haman. Henry notes the ring passes from enemy to deliverer: the seal that signed the Jews' death now seals their deliverance. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: And Esther spake yet again before the king, and fell down at his feet, and besought him with tears to put away the mischief of Haman the Agagite, and his device that he had devised against the Jews. JFB notes Esther's work is half done: Haman is dead, but his decree lives, and she weeps for a law, not only for a man. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: Then the king held out the golden sceptre toward Esther. So Esther arose, and stood before the king. The sceptre is held out a second time, and Esther, who once risked death to enter, now stands by grace to plead. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: And said, If it please the king, and if I have favour in his sight, and the thing seem right before the king, and I be pleasing in his eyes, let it be written to reverse the letters devised by Haman, which he wrote to destroy the Jews in all the king's provinces. Henry notes Esther asks for reversal in the full language of court deference, though she knows Persian law cannot be reversed. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: For how can I endure to see the evil that shall come unto my people? or how can I endure to see the destruction of my kindred? JFB notes the plea is personal and unanswerable: a queen cannot watch her kindred perish and call herself queen. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: Then the king Ahasuerus said unto Esther the queen and to Mordecai the Jew, Behold, I have given Esther the house of Haman, and him they have hanged upon the gallows, because he laid his hand upon the Jews. Henry notes the king recites what is done, as if that settled the matter, while the decree still stands. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: Write ye also for the Jews, as it likes you, in the king's name, and seal it with the king's ring: for the writing written in the king's name, and sealed with the king's ring, may no man reverse. JFB notes the solution: since the first writing cannot be undone, a second writing, equally royal, arms the Jews to defend themselves on the same day. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: Then were the king's scribes called in the third month, Sivan, on the twenty-third day; and it was written according to all that Mordecai commanded unto the Jews, and to the lieutenants, deputies and rulers of the provinces from India to Ethiopia, a hundred and twenty-seven provinces, each in its own writing and language, and to the Jews according to their writing and language. Henry notes Mordecai now commands the scribes, and the Jews receive the decree in their own tongue, a detail the first decree lacked. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: And he wrote in king Ahasuerus's name, and sealed it with the king's ring, and sent letters by posts on horseback, and riders on mules, camels, and young dromedaries. JFB notes the swiftest posts of the empire carry the counter-decree, as they once carried the decree of death. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: Wherein the king granted the Jews in every city to gather themselves together, and to stand for their life, to destroy, to slay and to cause to perish, all the power of the people and province that would assault them, both little ones and women, and to take the spoil of them for a prey. Henry notes the grant mirrors the first decree's language, turning its weapons back on the attackers. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: Upon one day in all the provinces of king Ahasuerus, namely, upon the thirteenth day of the twelfth month, the month Adar. The same day is named, so that deliverance and danger meet on a single date. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: The copy of the writing for a commandment to be given in every province was published unto all people, and that the Jews should be ready against that day to avenge themselves on their enemies. JFB notes publication matters: enemies must know the Jews are now lawfully armed, not merely privately warned. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: So the posts that rode upon mules and camels went out, being hastened and pressed on by the king's commandment. And the decree was given at Shushan the palace. Henry notes the haste matches the first decree's haste, but now it carries life. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: And Mordecai went out from the presence of the king in royal apparel of blue and white, and with a great crown of gold, and with a garment of fine linen and purple: and the city of Shushan rejoiced and was glad. JFB notes Mordecai's dress is the visible sign of the reversal: the Jew at the gate now walks out in royal colours, and the city that was perplexed rejoices. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: The Jews had light, and gladness, and joy, and honour. Henry notes the fourfold summary: light after darkness, gladness after mourning, joy after fear, honour after contempt. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: And in every province, and in every city, whithersoever the king's commandment and decree came, the Jews had joy and gladness, a feast and a good day. And many of the people of the land became Jews; for the fear of the Jews fell upon them. JFB notes the counter-decree's effect spreads like the first one's mourning, and fear now draws converts, as mourning once drew only pity. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
