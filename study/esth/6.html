Study Notes — Esther

Chapter 6

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: On that night could not the king sleep, and he commanded to bring the book of records of the chronicles; and they were read before the king. Henry notes the sleepless night is providence, not accident: the king who cannot sleep reaches for the book that will undo Haman. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: And it was found written, that Mordecai had told of Bigthana and Teresh, two of the king's chamberlains, keepers of the door, who sought to lay hand on king Ahasuerus. JFB notes the record is read at exactly the passage Haman would have wished forgotten, on the very night before he asks for Mordecai's death. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: And the king said, What honour and dignity has been done to Mordecai for this? Then said the king's servants that ministered unto him, There is nothing done for him. Henry notes the forgotten service is discovered by the king himself, not by Mordecai's complaint. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: And the king said, Who is in the court? Now Haman was come into the outward court, to speak unto the king to hang Mordecai on the gallows he had prepared. JFB notes the timing is exact: Haman arrives to ask a hanging at the moment the king asks about honouring. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: And the king's servants said unto him, Behold, Haman stands in the court. And the king said, Let him come in. Haman is admitted at once, as a favourite, to a question he will answer against himself. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: Henry notes Haman's heart answers the king's question before his mouth does, assuming every honour must be meant for himself, which is why his prescription will be turned against him. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: And Haman answered the king, For the man whom the king delights to honour. Haman begins his prescription without asking whose honour it is. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: Let the royal apparel be brought which the king uses to wear, and the horse that the king rides upon, and the crown royal which is set upon his head. JFB notes Haman asks for the king's own clothes, horse and crown, honours that border on royalty itself. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: And let this apparel and horse be delivered to one of the king's most noble princes, that they may array the man, and bring him on horseback through the street of the city, and proclaim before him, Thus shall it be done to the man whom the king delights to honour. Henry notes the procession is designed for envy: a public proclamation through the streets, where all can see. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: Then the king said to Haman, Make haste, and take the apparel and the horse, as thou hast said, and do even so to Mordecai the Jew, that sits at the king's gate: let nothing fail of all that thou hast spoken. JFB notes the reversal is spoken in Haman's own words, and 'Mordecai the Jew' is named to make the irony complete. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: Then took Haman the apparel and the horse, and arrayed Mordecai, and brought him on horseback through the street of the city, and proclaimed before him, Thus shall it be done unto the man whom the king delights to honour. Henry notes Haman becomes the herald of his enemy's honour, proclaiming with his own mouth what he had plotted to prevent. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: And Mordecai came again to the king's gate. But Haman hasted to his house mourning, and having his head covered. JFB notes Mordecai returns quietly to his post, while Haman flees home in mourning, his head covered as a man shamed. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: And Haman told Zeresh his wife and all his friends everything that had befallen him. Then said his wise men and Zeresh his wife unto him, If Mordecai be of the seed of the Jews, before whom thou hast begun to fall, thou shalt not prevail against him, but shalt surely fall before him. Henry notes even Haman's own household reads the omen: a fall before a Jew is the beginning of a total fall. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: And while they were yet talking with him, came the king's chamberlains, and hasted to bring Haman unto the banquet that Esther had prepared. JFB notes the summons cuts off the counsel: Haman is hurried from his own house of mourning to the queen's banquet, where his fall will be completed. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
