Study Notes — Esther

Chapter 7

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: So the king and Haman came to banquet with Esther the queen. The second banquet gathers the same three, but the night between has changed everything for Haman. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: Henry notes the repeated offer shows the king's impatience to grant, and Esther, having deferred once, can delay no longer at this second banquet. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: Then Esther the queen answered and said, If I have found favour in thy sight, O king, and if it please the king, let my life be given me at my petition, and my people at my request. JFB notes the petition is at last plain: not half a kingdom, but a life and a people. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: For we are sold, I and my people, to be destroyed, to be slain, and to perish. But if we had been sold for bondmen and bondwomen, I had held my tongue, although the enemy could not countervail the king's damage. Henry notes Esther names the sale: Haman's ten thousand talents bought a people, and bondage she could have borne, but extermination she cannot. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: Then the king Ahasuerus answered and said unto Esther the queen, Who is he, and where is he, that durst presume in his heart to do so? JFB notes the king's anger is genuine surprise: he had signed the decree without knowing it touched his own queen. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: And Esther said, The adversary and enemy is this wicked Haman. Then Haman was afraid before the king and the queen. Henry notes the naming is the turning point: one sentence makes the favourite the enemy, and fear falls on him at his own feast. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: And the king arising from the banquet of wine in his wrath went into the palace garden: and Haman stood up to make request for his life to Esther the queen; for he saw that there was evil determined against him by the king. JFB notes the king withdraws to master his wrath, and Haman, left alone with the queen, begs for the life he had denied to others. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: Henry notes the king's return misreads a suppliant's fall as an assault on the queen, and the covered face that follows is the sign of a condemned man. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: And Harbonah, one of the chamberlains, said before the king, Behold also, the gallows fifty cubits high, which Haman had made for Mordecai, who had spoken good for the king, stands in the house of Haman. Then the king said, Hang him thereon. JFB notes a chamberlain supplies the instrument: the gallows built for Mordecai stands ready in Haman's own house. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: So they hanged Haman on the gallows that he had prepared for Mordecai. Then was the king's wrath pacified. Henry notes the proverb is fulfilled in history: the pit dug for another receives the digger, and the king's wrath rests because justice has been done. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
