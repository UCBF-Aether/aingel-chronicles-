Study Notes — Esther

Chapter 10

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: And the king Ahasuerus laid a tribute upon the land, and upon the isles of the sea. Henry notes the book ends with the king's power, not the Jews' triumph: Ahasuerus still taxes land and isles, and the story's deliverance happened inside his empire, not by overthrowing it. Clarke adds the tribute fell on all one hundred and twenty-seven provinces. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown; Adam Clarke

2 COMMENTARY: And all the acts of his power and might, and the declaration of the greatness of Mordecai, whereunto the king advanced him, are they not written in the book of the chronicles of the kings of Media and Persia? JFB notes the appeal to the Persian chronicles, now lost, while the sacred story lives: human chronicles perish, but this record remains. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: For Mordecai the Jew was next unto king Ahasuerus, and great among the Jews, and accepted of the multitude of his brethren, seeking the wealth of his people, and speaking peace to all his seed. Henry notes the final portrait: second to the king, great among his own, accepted because he sought their wealth and spoke their peace. Clarke adds he was prime minister under Ahasuerus, and studied to settle his people's prosperity on a permanent basis. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown; Adam Clarke
