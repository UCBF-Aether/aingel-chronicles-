Study Notes — Esther

Chapter 5

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Now it came to pass on the third day, that Esther put on her royal apparel, and stood in the inner court of the king's house, over against the king's house: and the king sat upon his royal throne in the royal house, over against the gate of the house. Henry notes the third day ends the fast, and Esther comes in royal dress, not in sackcloth, to a king who must be won, not confronted. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: And when the king saw Esther standing in the court, she obtained favour in his sight: and the king held out to Esther the golden sceptre in his hand. So Esther drew near, and touched the top of the sceptre. JFB notes the sceptre decides the case: life, not death, and Esther's touch of it is submission to the king's grace. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: Henry notes the king's offer is royal generosity, and also the measure of Esther's favour: half a kingdom is laid open before she asks anything, which makes her restraint in asking only for a banquet all the more deliberate. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: And Esther answered, If it seem good unto the king, let the king and Haman come this day unto the banquet that I have prepared for him. JFB notes Esther does not ask at once: a banquet invitation buys time, and brings Haman face to face with the king under her roof. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: Then the king said, Cause Haman to make haste, that he may do as Esther has said. So the king and Haman came to the banquet that Esther had prepared. Henry notes Haman is hastened to his own undoing, summoned eagerly to a feast that will expose him. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: The offer is repeated over wine at the first banquet, pressing Esther to speak, yet she still defers her petition to a second banquet rather than asking at once. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: Then answered Esther, and said, My petition and my request is. Esther pauses on the threshold of her petition, holding the king's attention for a second banquet. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: If I have found favour in the sight of the king, and if it please the king to grant my petition, and to perform my request, let the king and Haman come to the banquet that I shall prepare for them, and I will do to morrow as the king has said. Henry notes the delay is wisdom, not fear: one more day lets Haman's pride ripen and the night's providence work. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: Then went Haman forth that day joyful and with a glad heart: but when Haman saw Mordecai in the king's gate, that he stood not up, nor moved for him, he was full of indignation against Mordecai. JFB notes the same gate that saw Mordecai's loyalty now sees his refusal, and Haman's feast-day joy curdles at one standing man. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: Nevertheless Haman refrained himself: and when he came home, he sent and called for his friends, and Zeresh his wife. Henry notes Haman restrains himself in public and unburdens himself at home, where flattery will feed what restraint starved. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: And Haman told them of the glory of his riches, and the multitude of his children, and all the things wherein the king had promoted him, and how he had advanced him above the princes and servants of the king. JFB notes the catalogue of blessings is recited to an audience that already knows it, because pride loves to hear itself. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: Haman said moreover, Yea, Esther the queen did let no man come in with the king unto the banquet that she had prepared but myself; and to morrow am I invited unto her also with the king. Henry notes Haman counts the queen's banquet as his highest honour, not knowing it is his summons to judgment. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: Yet all this availeth me nothing, so long as I see Mordecai the Jew sitting at the king's gate. JFB notes the sentence exposes pride's arithmetic: an empire's honours weigh nothing against one man's refusal. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: Then said Zeresh his wife and all his friends unto him, Let a gallows be made of fifty cubits high, and to morrow speak thou unto the king that Mordecai may be hanged thereon: then go thou in merrily with the king unto the banquet. And the thing pleased Haman; and he caused the gallows to be made. Henry notes the gallows is built fifty cubits high to make the hanging public, and Haman goes to bed pleased with a plan that will hang himself. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
