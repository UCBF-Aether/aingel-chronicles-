Study Notes — Luke

Chapter 7

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: When he had ended his sayings before the people, he entered Capernaum. Teaching gives way to a test of faith in that same town. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: A centurion's servant, dear to him, was sick and ready to die. Henry notes servants should study to endear themselves to masters, and masters ought to take particular care of servants when sick. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: Hearing of Jesus, the centurion sent elders of the Jews, beseeching him to come and heal his servant. Henry urges that we may still, by faithful and fervent prayer, apply to Christ when sickness is in our families. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: The elders besought him instantly, saying he was worthy for whom he should do this. Intercessors plead the centurion's worth, while the centurion himself will plead unworthiness. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: For he loves our nation, and has built us a synagogue. Henry notes building places for worship is a good work, an instance of love to God and his people. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: Jesus went with them, but not far from the house the centurion sent friends, saying, Lord, trouble not thyself, for I am not worthy that thou shouldest enter under my roof. Worthiness claimed by others is disowned by the man himself. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: Neither thought I myself worthy to come unto thee; but say in a word, and my servant shall be healed. Distance is no barrier to a word of command, and humility asks for the word, not the visit. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: I also am a man under authority, having soldiers under me; I say, Go, and he goes; Come, and he comes; Do this, and he does it. The soldier reasons from command to command: as his word rules men, Christ's word rules disease. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: Jesus marvelled at him and said to the following people, I have not found so great faith, no, not in Israel. Henry notes the Lord was pleased with the centurion's faith, and never fails to answer the expectations of faith that honours his power and love. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: Those sent, returning to the house, found the servant whole that had been sick. The cure was soon wrought and perfect, without Jesus entering the house. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: The day after, he went to Nain, with many disciples and much people. The crowd that follows will become witnesses of something greater than a healing. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: Near the gate, a dead man was carried out, the only son of his widowed mother, with much people of the city with her. Double loss — husband gone, only son dead — meets the procession at the gate. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: When the Lord saw her, he had compassion on her, and said, Weep not. Henry notes Christ's compassion on the poor widow, and his power over death itself. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: He came and touched the bier, the bearers stood still, and he said, Young man, I say unto thee, Arise. The gospel call, Henry says, to all people, and young people particularly, is, arise from the dead, and Christ shall give you light and life. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: He that was dead sat up and began to speak, and he delivered him to his mother. Henry notes life appeared by sitting up and speaking: whenever Christ gives spiritual life, he opens lips in prayer and praise. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: Fear came on all, and they glorified God, saying, a great prophet is risen among us, and God has visited his people. Henry urges that when dead souls are raised by divine power with the gospel, we must glorify God and see it as a gracious visit to his people. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: This rumour went forth throughout all Judaea and the region round about. The raising at Nain becomes news across the country. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: John's disciples showed him all these things. The imprisoned forerunner hears reports, and must weigh them. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: John called two disciples and sent them to Jesus, asking, Art thou he that should come, or look we for another? The question is honest inquiry, not settled unbelief, and Jesus answers it with deeds, not titles. — Matthew Henry

20 COMMENTARY: When the men came, they repeated John's question: Art thou he that should come, or look we for another? The messengers deliver the question exactly as sent. — Matthew Henry

21 COMMENTARY: In that same hour he cured many of infirmities, plagues, and evil spirits, and gave sight to many blind. The answer begins before words, in healings done while the questioners watch. — Matthew Henry

22 COMMENTARY: Go and tell John what ye have seen and heard: the blind see, the lame walk, lepers are cleansed, the deaf hear, the dead are raised, to the poor the gospel is preached. Henry notes that to Christ's miracles in nature he adds this in grace: to the poor the gospel is preached, which points out the spiritual nature of his kingdom. — Matthew Henry

23 COMMENTARY: Blessed is he, whosoever shall not be offended in me. Offence at a Messiah who heals rather than conquers is the stumbling block named. — Matthew Henry

24 COMMENTARY: When John's messengers departed, he asked the people, what went ye out into the wilderness to see? A reed shaken with the wind? John was no reed bending to opinion. — Matthew Henry

25 COMMENTARY: What went ye out to see? A man clothed in soft raiment? Those gorgeously apparelled and living delicately are in kings' courts, not in the wilderness. — Matthew Henry

26 COMMENTARY: What went ye out to see? A prophet? Yea, and much more than a prophet. John's office exceeds prophecy because he prepares the way in person. — Matthew Henry

27 COMMENTARY: This is he of whom it is written, Behold, I send my messenger before thy face, to prepare thy way before thee. Scripture names John's role before he ever preached. — Matthew Henry

28 COMMENTARY: Among those born of women there is not a greater prophet than John, but the least in the kingdom of God is greater than he. Henry notes John's ministry was preaching repentance and reformation of heart and life, preparing the way for a kingdom greater than his own place in it. — Matthew Henry

29 COMMENTARY: All the people and the publicans, hearing him, justified God, being baptized with John's baptism. The common people and outcasts owned God's way; the respectable refused it. — Matthew Henry

30 COMMENTARY: But the Pharisees and lawyers rejected God's counsel against themselves, being not baptized of him. Refusal of John's baptism was refusal of God's counsel itself. — Matthew Henry

31 COMMENTARY: Whereunto shall I liken the men of this generation, and to what are they like? The generation will be pictured by its own inconsistency. — Matthew Henry

32 COMMENTARY: They are like children in the marketplace calling, we piped to you and ye did not dance, we mourned to you and ye did not weep. Henry notes the just blame of those wrought upon neither by John's ministry nor by Christ's: they made a jest of God's methods to do them good. — Matthew Henry

33 COMMENTARY: John came neither eating bread nor drinking wine, and ye say, he has a devil. Austerity is called madness. — Matthew Henry

34 COMMENTARY: The Son of man is come eating and drinking, and ye say, behold a glutton and winebibber, a friend of publicans and sinners! The opposite manner is condemned with the opposite insult. — Matthew Henry

35 COMMENTARY: But wisdom is justified of all her children. Henry's counsel is to prove ourselves children of Wisdom by attending God's word, and adoring mysteries that infidels and Pharisees deride. — Matthew Henry

36 COMMENTARY: One Pharisee desired him to eat with him, and he went into the Pharisee's house and sat down to meat. Jesus accepts the table of a critic, and the scene that follows will test host and guest alike. — Matthew Henry

37 COMMENTARY: A woman in the city, a sinner, knowing Jesus sat at meat in the Pharisee's house, brought an alabaster box of ointment. Henry notes none can truly perceive how precious Christ is, and the glory of the gospel, except the broken-hearted. — Matthew Henry

38 COMMENTARY: Standing at his feet behind him weeping, she washed his feet with tears, wiped them with her hair, kissed them, and anointed them with ointment. Tears, hair, kisses, ointment: repentance expresses itself without words. — Matthew Henry

39 COMMENTARY: The Pharisee thought within himself, if he were a prophet he would know who and what manner of woman touches him, for she is a sinner. Henry notes the Pharisee, instead of rejoicing in tokens of her repentance, confined his thoughts to her former bad character. — Matthew Henry

40 COMMENTARY: Jesus answered, Simon, I have somewhat to say unto thee; and he said, Master, say on. The unspoken thought is answered aloud, and Simon must listen. — Matthew Henry

41 COMMENTARY: A creditor had two debtors, one owing five hundred pence, the other fifty. The parable sets up a simple arithmetic of debt. — Matthew Henry

42 COMMENTARY: When they had nothing to pay, he frankly forgave them both. Tell me, which will love him most? Henry notes sin is a debt, all are debtors, some greater, but every debt is more than we can pay, and God is ready to forgive. — Matthew Henry

43 COMMENTARY: Simon answered, I suppose he to whom he forgave most; and Jesus said, thou hast rightly judged. The Pharisee judges the parable rightly, and thereby judges the woman's case. — Matthew Henry

44 COMMENTARY: Turning to the woman, Jesus told Simon, I entered thine house, thou gavest no water for my feet, but she washed my feet with tears and wiped them with her hair. Henry notes Christ forced Simon to acknowledge that the greater sinner, pardoned, ought to show the greater love. — Matthew Henry

45 COMMENTARY: Thou gavest me no kiss, but this woman since I came in has not ceased to kiss my feet. Courtesy withheld by the host is supplied, exceeded, by the sinner. — Matthew Henry

46 COMMENTARY: My head with oil thou didst not anoint, but this woman has anointed my feet with ointment. The contrast is complete: host's neglect against guest's devotion. — Matthew Henry

47 COMMENTARY: Her sins, which are many, are forgiven, for she loved much; but to whom little is forgiven, the same loves little. Henry's rule follows: the more we express sorrow for sin and love to Christ, the clearer evidence we have of forgiveness. — Matthew Henry

48 COMMENTARY: And he said unto her, Thy sins are forgiven. The word spoken to Simon about her becomes a word spoken to her herself. — Matthew Henry

49 COMMENTARY: They that sat at meat began to say within themselves, who is this that forgives sins also? The table now asks the same question the scribes asked over the paralytic. — Matthew Henry

50 COMMENTARY: He said to the woman, Thy faith has saved thee; go in peace. Henry closes with the wonderful change grace makes on a sinner's heart and life, and on her state before God, by full remission through faith in Christ. — Matthew Henry
