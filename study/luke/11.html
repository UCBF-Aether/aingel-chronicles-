Study Notes — Luke

Chapter 11

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: As he prayed in a certain place and ceased, a disciple asked, Lord, teach us to pray, as John also taught his disciples. Henry calls this a good and very needful prayer, for only Christ by word and Spirit can teach us how to pray, what to pray for, and what to say. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: When ye pray, say, Our Father which art in heaven, hallowed be thy name; thy kingdom come; thy will be done, as in heaven, so in earth. Henry notes Christ taught much the same prayer as in the Sermon on the Mount, differences in words being of no moment, and urges coming to our heavenly Father confiding in his power and goodness. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: Give us day by day our daily bread. Daily bread is asked daily, not stored in advance. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: Forgive us our sins, for we also forgive every one indebted to us; and lead us not into temptation, but deliver us from evil. Forgiveness received and forgiveness given are bound together in the same petition. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: Which of you shall have a friend, and go to him at midnight saying, Friend, lend me three loaves? The parable begins with an awkward hour and a simple need. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: For a friend of mine in his journey is come to me, and I have nothing to set before him. The midnight visitor's need is hospitality, not luxury. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: He from within shall answer, Trouble me not: the door is shut, my children are with me in bed; I cannot rise and give thee. Every excuse is respectable, and still the request stands. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: Though he will not rise because he is his friend, yet because of his importunity he will rise and give him as many as he needs. Henry's lesson is fervency and constancy: if God does not answer speedily, yet he will in due time if we continue to pray. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: Ask, and it shall be given you; seek, and ye shall find; knock, and it shall be opened unto you. Asking, seeking, knocking are rising degrees of the same earnestness. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: For every one that asks receives, he that seeks finds, and to him that knocks it shall be opened. The promise is stated universally, to encourage continuance. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: If a son asks bread of a father, will he give him a stone? Or if he asks a fish, will he for a fish give him a serpent? A father does not mock hunger with harm. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: Or if he asks an egg, will he offer him a scorpion? The third example completes the point: gifts may be small, but they are not cruel. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: If ye then, being evil, know how to give good gifts to your children, how much more shall your heavenly Father give the Holy Spirit to them that ask him? Henry notes we must ask for the Holy Spirit above all, since all spiritual blessings are included in that one, and the Father is more ready to bestow than an indulgent parent to feed a hungry child. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: He was casting out a devil, and it was dumb; when the devil went out, the dumb spoke, and the people wondered. Casting out is here the destroying of the devil's power, not a mere display. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: But some said, he casts out devils through Beelzebub, chief of the devils. The miracle is admitted and its source is slandered. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: Others, tempting him, sought of him a sign from heaven. A sign already given is not enough for those who demand a different kind. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: Knowing their thoughts, he said, every kingdom divided against itself is brought to desolation, and a house divided against a house falls. A divided kingdom cannot be the explanation of its own defeat. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: If Satan also be divided against himself, how shall his kingdom stand? Because ye say I cast out devils through Beelzebub. The charge collapses on its own logic. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: If I by Beelzebub cast out devils, by whom do your sons cast them out? Therefore shall they be your judges. Their own exorcists become witnesses against the charge. — Matthew Henry

20 COMMENTARY: But if I with the finger of God cast out devils, no doubt the kingdom of God is come upon you. The exorcism is not a trick but an arrival: the kingdom has come. — Matthew Henry

21 COMMENTARY: When a strong man armed keeps his palace, his goods are in peace. Henry pictures the unconverted heart as the devil's palace, where he dwells and rules, and the sinner feels a false peace, secure with no dread of judgment. — Matthew Henry

22 COMMENTARY: But when a stronger than he comes and overcomes him, he takes all his armour wherein he trusted, and divides his spoils. Conversion, Henry says, is Christ's victory over the devil in that soul, restoring it to liberty and recovering his own interest and power over it. — Matthew Henry

23 COMMENTARY: He that is not with me is against me, and he that gathers not with me scatters. Neutrality is not an option in this war. — Matthew Henry

24 COMMENTARY: When the unclean spirit goes out, he walks through dry places seeking rest, finds none, and says, I will return to my house whence I came out. Expulsion without occupation leaves the house empty for return. — Matthew Henry

25 COMMENTARY: When he comes, he finds it swept and garnished. Henry warns the house may be swept from common sins by forced confession, feigned contrition, or partial reformation, yet not washed; sweeping takes only loose dirt, while the besetting sin is untouched, and garnish is paint, not true grace. — Matthew Henry

26 COMMENTARY: Then he takes seven other spirits more wicked than himself, and they enter and dwell there, and the last state is worse than the first. Henry urges us not to rest in what a man may have yet come short of heaven, and to pray earnestly to be delivered from such an awful state. — Matthew Henry

27 COMMENTARY: As he spoke, a woman lifted her voice: blessed is the womb that bore thee, and the paps thou hast sucked. Henry notes that while scribes despised his discourses, this woman admired them and his wisdom and power. — Matthew Henry

28 COMMENTARY: But he said, yea rather, blessed are they that hear the word of God, and keep it. Christ leads her to a higher consideration: hearing, kept in memory and kept as way and rule, is the true blessedness. — Matthew Henry

29 COMMENTARY: When people gathered thick, he began: this is an evil generation; they seek a sign, and no sign shall be given but the sign of Jonah the prophet. Henry notes Christ promised one more sign, Jonah, explained in Matthew as the resurrection, and warned them to improve it. — Matthew Henry

30 COMMENTARY: For as Jonah was a sign to the Ninevites, so shall the Son of man be to this generation. The sign is a prophet delivered and preaching, not a spectacle on demand. — Matthew Henry

31 COMMENTARY: The queen of the south shall rise in judgment with this generation and condemn them, for she came from earth's utmost parts to hear Solomon's wisdom, and behold, a greater than Solomon is here. Distance travelled to hear wisdom condemns those who will not hear at home. — Matthew Henry

32 COMMENTARY: The men of Nineveh shall rise in judgment with this generation and condemn it, for they repented at Jonah's preaching, and behold, a greater than Jonah is here. Repentance at a lesser prophet condemns impenitence before a greater. — Matthew Henry

33 COMMENTARY: No man lighting a candle puts it in a secret place or under a bushel, but on a candlestick, that entrants may see the light. Light is given to be seen, not hidden. — Matthew Henry

34 COMMENTARY: The light of the body is the eye: when thine eye is single, thy whole body is full of light; but when thine eye is evil, thy body is full of darkness. The eye governs the body's light. — Matthew Henry

35 COMMENTARY: Take heed therefore that the light in thee be not darkness. Henry warns especially that if our leading principles are wrong, judgment and practice must become more so. — Matthew Henry

36 COMMENTARY: If thy whole body be full of light, having no part dark, the whole shall be full of light, as when a candle's bright shining gives thee light. Undivided light makes the whole person luminous. — Matthew Henry

37 COMMENTARY: As he spoke, a Pharisee besought him to dine with him, and he went in and sat down to meat. The table becomes the next pulpit. — Matthew Henry

38 COMMENTARY: When the Pharisee saw it, he marvelled that he had not first washed before dinner. Outward washing is treated as the measure of piety. — Matthew Henry

39 COMMENTARY: Now do ye Pharisees make clean the outside of cup and platter, but your inward part is full of ravening and wickedness. Henry's counsel for the whole section is to look to our hearts, that they may be cleansed and new-created, attending to great things of law and gospel without neglecting the smallest matter God appointed. — Matthew Henry

40 COMMENTARY: Ye fools, did not he that made what is without make what is within also? The Maker of outside is Maker of inside, so outside cleansing cannot excuse inside filth. — Matthew Henry

41 COMMENTARY: But rather give alms of such things as ye have, and behold, all things are clean unto you. Mercy, not ritual, cleanses what is held. — Matthew Henry

42 COMMENTARY: Woe unto you, Pharisees! For ye tithe mint, rue, and all herbs, and pass over judgment and the love of God; these ought ye to have done, and not to leave the other undone. Small exactness cannot substitute for judgment and love. — Matthew Henry

43 COMMENTARY: Woe unto you, Pharisees! For ye love the uppermost seats in synagogues, and greetings in markets. Honour-seeking is named as a woe, not a weakness. — Matthew Henry

44 COMMENTARY: Woe unto you, scribes and Pharisees, hypocrites! For ye are as graves which appear not, and men walking over them are not aware. Hidden corruption defiles the unaware, like an unmarked grave. — Matthew Henry

45 COMMENTARY: Then a lawyer answered, Master, thus saying thou reproachest us also. The lawyer hears his own profession in the woes. — Matthew Henry

46 COMMENTARY: Woe unto you also, ye lawyers! For ye lade men with burdens grievous to be borne, and ye yourselves touch not the burdens with one finger. Burdens imposed on others, untouched by imposers, draw a woe of their own. — Matthew Henry

47 COMMENTARY: Woe unto you! For ye build the sepulchres of the prophets, and your fathers killed them. Honouring dead prophets while sharing their killers' spirit is the indictment. — Matthew Henry

48 COMMENTARY: Truly ye bear witness that ye allow your fathers' deeds, for they killed them, and ye build their sepulchres. Building tombs testifies to continuity, not to repentance. — Matthew Henry

49 COMMENTARY: Therefore also said the wisdom of God, I will send them prophets and apostles, and some they shall slay and persecute. The pattern continues into the present generation. — Matthew Henry

50 COMMENTARY: That the blood of all prophets, shed from the foundation of the world, may be required of this generation. The generation that completes the pattern bears its reckoning. — Matthew Henry

51 COMMENTARY: From Abel's blood unto Zacharias, who perished between altar and temple: verily, it shall be required of this generation. From first martyr to last, the whole account is charged to those who repeat it. — Matthew Henry

52 COMMENTARY: Woe unto you, lawyers! For ye have taken away the key of knowledge: ye entered not in yourselves, and them entering in ye hindered. Keeping the key while barring the door is the lawyers' worst woe. — Matthew Henry

53 COMMENTARY: As he said these things, scribes and Pharisees began to urge him vehemently, and provoke him to speak of many things. Reproof provokes a cross-examination. — Matthew Henry

54 COMMENTARY: Laying wait for him, seeking to catch something out of his mouth, that they might accuse him. Henry's prayer for such moments is for prudence and patience, that evil purposes be disappointed and the Spirit rest upon us. — Matthew Henry
