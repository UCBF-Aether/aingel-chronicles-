Study Notes — Luke

Chapter 12

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Meanwhile an innumerable multitude gathered, treading on one another, and he began to say first to his disciples, beware of the leaven of the Pharisees, which is hypocrisy. The first warning to disciples, before the crowd, is against the leaven that spreads unseen. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: For there is nothing covered that shall not be revealed, nor hid that shall not be known. Hypocrisy fails because concealment is temporary. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: Therefore whatever ye have spoken in darkness shall be heard in the light, and what ye have spoken in the ear in closets shall be proclaimed upon housetops. Private words become public proclamation. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: I say to you, my friends, be not afraid of them that kill the body, and after that have no more that they can do. Henry grounds courage in providence: a firm belief in God's universal providence satisfies us in peril and encourages trust in the way of duty. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: But I will forewarn you whom ye shall fear: fear him, who after he has killed has power to cast into hell; yea, fear him. The fear that matters is not of men whose power ends at death. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: Are not five sparrows sold for two farthings, and not one of them is forgotten before God? Providence notices the meanest creatures, Henry notes, and therefore the smallest interests of Christ's disciples. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: But even the very hairs of your head are all numbered. Fear not therefore: ye are of more value than many sparrows. Numbered hairs measure a care that counts the smallest. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: Whosoever shall confess me before men, him shall the Son of man also confess before the angels of God. Henry notes those who confess Christ now shall be owned by him in the great day. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: But he that denies me before men shall be denied before the angels of God. Denial now is answered by denial then. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: Whosoever speaks a word against the Son of man, it shall be forgiven him, but unto him that blasphemes against the Holy Ghost it shall not be forgiven. Henry distinguishes trembling, penitent backsliding, which may obtain forgiveness, from determined enmity against the Spirit, which shall never be forgiven because it will never be repented of. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: When they bring you to synagogues, magistrates, and powers, take no thought how or what ye shall answer, or what ye shall say. Defence is not to be rehearsed in anxiety. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: For the Holy Ghost shall teach you in the same hour what ye ought to say. The hour of trial carries its own teaching. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: One of the company said, Master, speak to my brother, that he divide the inheritance with me. A family dispute is brought to Jesus as if he were a divider of estates. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: Man, who made me a judge or a divider over you? Henry notes Christ's kingdom is spiritual, not of this world; Christianity obliges all to do justly, but worldly dominion is not founded in grace, nor does religion promise worldly advantage. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: Take heed, and beware of covetousness, for a man's life consists not in the abundance of things he possesses. Covetousness, Henry warns, is a sin needing constant warning, for happiness does not depend on worldly wealth. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: The ground of a certain rich man brought forth plentifully. The parable begins with blessing, not with wrongdoing. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: He thought within himself, what shall I do, because I have no room where to bestow my fruits? Henry notes that instead of thanking God or rejoicing to do more good, he afflicts himself; the poorest beggar could not have said a more anxious word, and the more men have, the more perplexity they have with it. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: This will I do: I will pull down my barns, build greater, and there bestow all my fruits and goods. The solution to plenty is larger storage, not larger generosity. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: I will say to my soul, Soul, thou hast much goods laid up for many years; take thine ease, eat, drink, and be merry. Henry calls it folly to make no other use of plenty than indulging flesh and gratifying appetite, with no thought of doing good to others. — Matthew Henry

20 COMMENTARY: But God said unto him, Thou fool, this night thy soul shall be required of thee; then whose shall those things be, which thou hast provided? Henry notes carnal worldlings are fools, and God will call them by their own name; thy soul shall be required, as a guilty soul to be punished without delay. — Matthew Henry

21 COMMENTARY: So is he that lays up treasure for himself, and is not rich toward God. The parable's verdict generalises the rich fool into a type. — Matthew Henry

22 COMMENTARY: Therefore I say unto you, take no thought for your life, what ye shall eat, nor for the body, what ye shall put on. Henry notes Christ largely insisted on this caution against disquieting, perplexing cares, urging us to cast care upon God, the right way to get ease. — Matthew Henry

23 COMMENTARY: The life is more than meat, and the body is more than raiment. Life and body exceed their provisions, so anxiety about provisions mistakes the lesser for the greater. — Matthew Henry

24 COMMENTARY: Consider the ravens: they neither sow nor reap, have neither storehouse nor barn, and God feeds them; how much more are ye better than the fowls? Provision without storehouse is the ravens' lesson. — Matthew Henry

25 COMMENTARY: Which of you with taking thought can add to his stature one cubit? Anxiety cannot add even the least measure. — Matthew Henry

26 COMMENTARY: If ye then cannot do that which is least, why take ye thought for the rest? Inability in the least exposes anxiety's uselessness in the rest. — Matthew Henry

27 COMMENTARY: Consider the lilies how they grow: they toil not, they spin not, yet Solomon in all his glory was not arrayed like one of these. Beauty without toil outshines royal glory. — Matthew Henry

28 COMMENTARY: If God so clothes the grass, today in the field and tomorrow cast into the oven, how much more will he clothe you, O ye of little faith? Short-lived grass argues for lasting care of disciples. — Matthew Henry

29 COMMENTARY: Seek not what ye shall eat or drink, neither be ye of doubtful mind. Seeking food as life's aim breeds a doubtful, tossed mind. — Matthew Henry

30 COMMENTARY: For all these things do the nations of the world seek after, and your Father knows that ye have need of these things. Gentile seeking is not the disciples' pattern, and the Father already knows the need. — Matthew Henry

31 COMMENTARY: But rather seek ye the kingdom of God, and all these things shall be added unto you. Seeking the kingdom first reorders the rest as addition, not aim. — Matthew Henry

32 COMMENTARY: Fear not, little flock, for it is your Father's good pleasure to give you the kingdom. Henry urges us to examine whether we belong to this little flock, whose Master gives the kingdom by good pleasure, not by purchase. — Matthew Henry

33 COMMENTARY: Sell that ye have, and give alms; provide yourselves bags which wax not old, a treasure in the heavens that fails not, where no thief approaches, neither moth corrupts. Treasure is moved, not merely renounced, to a place thieves and moth cannot reach. — Matthew Henry

34 COMMENTARY: For where your treasure is, there will your heart be also. Treasure locates the heart, whether on earth or in heaven. — Matthew Henry

35 COMMENTARY: Let your loins be girded about, and your lights burning. Readiness is pictured as dressed and lit, not idle. — Matthew Henry

36 COMMENTARY: And ye yourselves like men that wait for their lord, when he will return from the wedding, that when he comes and knocks, they may open immediately. Henry notes we are waiting servants, sitting up while he stays out late, ready to receive him. — Matthew Henry

37 COMMENTARY: Blessed are those servants, whom the lord when he comes shall find watching: verily, he shall gird himself, make them sit down to meat, and come forth and serve them. Watchfulness is rewarded by a reversal: the lord serves the servants. — Matthew Henry

38 COMMENTARY: If he comes in the second watch, or in the third watch, and finds them so, blessed are those servants. Late watches do not excuse sleep. — Matthew Henry

39 COMMENTARY: This know, if the goodman of the house had known what hour the thief would come, he would have watched, and not suffered his house to be broken through. Henry notes if men thus care for houses, we should be thus wise for souls. — Matthew Henry

40 COMMENTARY: Be ye therefore ready also, for the Son of man comes at an hour when ye think not. Readiness is constant because the hour is unknown. — Matthew Henry

41 COMMENTARY: Then Peter said, Lord, speakest thou this parable unto us, or even to all? The question asks whether watchfulness is for apostles or for everyone. — Matthew Henry

42 COMMENTARY: Who then is that faithful and wise steward, whom his lord shall make ruler over his household, to give them their portion of meat in due season? Henry notes all are to take Christ's word to themselves and inquire concerning it; no one is left so ignorant as not to know many wrongs done and rights neglected, so all are without excuse. — Matthew Henry

43 COMMENTARY: Blessed is that servant, whom his lord when he comes shall find so doing. Blessing rests on being found at the assigned work. — Matthew Henry

44 COMMENTARY: Of a truth, he will make him ruler over all that he has. Faithful stewardship in a household leads to rule over all. — Matthew Henry

45 COMMENTARY: But if that servant says in his heart, my lord delays his coming, and begins to beat menservants and maidens, and to eat and drink and be drunken, delay becomes cruelty and indulgence. — Matthew Henry

46 COMMENTARY: The lord of that servant will come in a day when he looks not for him, and at an hour when he is not aware, and will cut him in sunder, and appoint him his portion with the unbelievers. The servant who counted on delay is caught by the very coming he discounted. — Matthew Henry

47 COMMENTARY: That servant, who knew his lord's will, and prepared not himself, neither did according to his will, shall be beaten with many stripes. Knowledge without preparation increases stripes. — Matthew Henry

48 COMMENTARY: But he that knew not, and did things worthy of stripes, shall be beaten with few stripes. For unto whomsoever much is given, of him shall be much required, and to whom men have committed much, of him they will ask the more. Ignorance lessens but does not remove accountability, and gift measures requirement. — Matthew Henry

49 COMMENTARY: I am come to send fire on the earth, and what will I, if it be already kindled? Henry notes the gospel's coming would occasion desolations, not because Christ's religion tends that way, being pure, peaceable, and loving, but because it contradicts men's pride and lusts. — Matthew Henry

50 COMMENTARY: But I have a baptism to be baptized with, and how am I straitened till it be accomplished! Henry notes before the gospel's wide publication, Christ had a baptism far different from water and Spirit: he must endure sufferings and death. — Matthew Henry

51 COMMENTARY: Suppose ye that I am come to give peace on earth? I tell you, Nay, but rather division. Peace is not the immediate effect where pride resists the gospel. — Matthew Henry

52 COMMENTARY: For from henceforth there shall be five in one house divided, three against two, and two against three. Division reaches inside the household itself. — Matthew Henry

53 COMMENTARY: The father shall be divided against the son, and the son against the father; the mother against the daughter, and the daughter against the mother; the mother in law against her daughter in law, and the daughter in law against her mother in law. Every household bond is named, because none is exempt from gospel division. — Matthew Henry

54 COMMENTARY: When ye see a cloud rise out of the west, straightway ye say, there comes a shower, and so it is. Weather wisdom is accurate in its own sphere. — Matthew Henry

55 COMMENTARY: When ye see the south wind blow, ye say, there will be heat, and it comes to pass. The second sign confirms the first: they read signs well. — Matthew Henry

56 COMMENTARY: Ye hypocrites, ye can discern the face of sky and earth, but how is it that ye do not discern this time? Henry notes Christ would have people as wise in soul concerns as in outward affairs. — Matthew Henry

57 COMMENTARY: Yea, and why even of yourselves judge ye not what is right? Discernment of the time should lead to self-judgment about what is right. — Matthew Henry

58 COMMENTARY: When thou goest with thine adversary to the magistrate, as thou art in the way, give diligence that thou mayest be delivered from him, lest he hale thee to the judge, and the judge deliver thee to the officer, and the officer cast thee into prison. Henry urges hastening to obtain peace with God before it is too late, applying to God in Christ reconciling the world. — Matthew Henry

59 COMMENTARY: I tell thee, thou shalt not depart thence, till thou hast paid the very last mite. The way with an adversary ends in exact payment, so settlement must come before judgment. — Matthew Henry
