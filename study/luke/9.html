Study Notes — Luke

Chapter 9

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: He called the twelve together and gave them power and authority over all devils, and to cure diseases. Henry notes they were now able to teach others what they had received, and Jesus is the fountain of power and authority to whom all creatures must be subject. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: He sent them to preach the kingdom of God and to heal the sick. Preaching and healing go together as the mission's two hands. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: Take nothing for the journey, neither staves, scrip, bread, money, nor two coats apiece. Henry notes they must not commend themselves by outward appearance, but go as they were, trusting that he who goes with his ministers in power will care for their wants. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: Whatever house ye enter, there abide, and thence depart. Contentment with one lodging is part of the mission's discipline. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: Whoever will not receive you, shake off the very dust from your feet for a testimony against them when ye leave that city. Henry warns that where truth and love go together yet the message is rejected, it leaves men without excuse and turns to testimony against them. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: They departed, went through towns, preaching the gospel and healing everywhere. The mission in practice matches the commission. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: Herod heard all that was done and was perplexed, because some said John was risen from the dead. Henry notes Herod's guilty conscience was ready to conclude John risen, since he had killed him. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: Some said Elijah had appeared, others that one of the old prophets was risen again. Rumour tries to fit Jesus into familiar prophetic shapes. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: Herod said, John have I beheaded, but who is this of whom I hear such things? And he desired to see him. Henry asks why he did not go and see him: probably pride, or unwillingness to meet another reprover of sin, and delay hardened his heart. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: The apostles returned and told him all they had done, and he took them aside privately to a desert place belonging to Bethsaida. Report and retreat follow mission. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: The people followed him, and he received them, spoke of the kingdom of God, and healed those needing healing. Henry notes though they came unseasonably, he gave them what they came for. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: When day wore away, the twelve said, send the multitude away to lodge and get victuals in towns round about, for we are in a desert place. The disciples' practical solution is dismissal. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: Give ye them to eat. They answered, we have no more but five loaves and two fishes, except we buy meat for all this people. The command exposes the gap between need and resource. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: They were about five thousand men, and he said, make them sit down by fifties in a company. Order precedes miracle: the crowd is arranged before it is fed. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: They did so and made them all sit down. Obedience in arrangement is the disciples' part of the feeding. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: He took the five loaves and two fishes, looked up to heaven, blessed, broke, and gave to the disciples to set before the multitude. Henry notes creature-comforts must be acknowledged as received from God, unworthily received, and owed wholly to Christ's mediation, by whom the curse is taken away. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: They ate and were all filled, and twelve baskets of fragments remained. Henry notes Christ's blessing makes a little go a great way, fills every hungry soul abundantly, and in our Father's house there is bread enough and to spare; we are not straitened in Christ. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: As he was alone praying, with disciples by, he asked, whom say the people that I am? Prayer precedes the great confession. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: They answered, John the Baptist, some say Elijah, others one of the old prophets risen again. Public opinion repeats Herod's rumours. — Matthew Henry

20 COMMENTARY: But whom say ye that I am? Peter answered, The Christ of God. Henry calls it unspeakable comfort that Jesus is God's Anointed, appointed to be Messiah and qualified for it. — Matthew Henry

21 COMMENTARY: He straitly charged and commanded them to tell no man that thing. The confession is true but premature for proclamation. — Matthew Henry

22 COMMENTARY: The Son of man must suffer many things, be rejected by elders, chief priests, and scribes, be slain, and be raised the third day. Henry notes Jesus discourses concerning his own sufferings, and disciples must prepare for their own, not plot to prevent his. — Matthew Henry

23 COMMENTARY: If any man will come after me, let him deny himself, take up his cross daily, and follow me. Henry notes crosses meet us in the way of duty: not to be pulled upon our heads, but when laid for us, to be taken up and carried after Christ. — Matthew Henry

24 COMMENTARY: Whosoever will save his life shall lose it, but whosoever will lose his life for my sake shall save it. Saving and losing are reversed by his sake. — Matthew Henry

25 COMMENTARY: What is a man advantaged, if he gain the whole world and lose himself, or be cast away? Henry notes it is well or ill with us according as it is well or ill with our souls; the body cannot be happy if the soul is miserable in the other world. — Matthew Henry

26 COMMENTARY: Whosoever shall be ashamed of me and my words, of him shall the Son of man be ashamed, when he comes in his own glory, his Father's, and the holy angels'. Henry adds we must never be ashamed of Christ and his gospel. — Matthew Henry

27 COMMENTARY: Some standing here shall not taste death till they see the kingdom of God. The kingdom's coming is near enough for hearers to witness it. — Matthew Henry

28 COMMENTARY: About eight days after, he took Peter, John, and James up into a mountain to pray. The transfiguration is framed, like the confession, by prayer. — Matthew Henry

29 COMMENTARY: As he prayed, the fashion of his countenance was altered, and his raiment was white and glistering. Henry notes prayer is a transfiguring, transforming duty, which makes the face shine. — Matthew Henry

30 COMMENTARY: Behold, two men talked with him, Moses and Elijah. Law and prophets stand with the Son on the mountain. — Matthew Henry

31 COMMENTARY: They appeared in glory and spoke of his decease to be accomplished at Jerusalem. Henry notes even in transfiguration Jesus was willing to speak concerning his death; in our greatest earthly glories, remember we have no continuing city here. — Matthew Henry

32 COMMENTARY: Peter and they with him were heavy with sleep, but when awake they saw his glory and the two men standing with him. Henry notes the disciples, after a while awake, could give a full account of what passed. — Matthew Henry

33 COMMENTARY: As they departed, Peter said, Master, it is good for us to be here; let us make three tabernacles, for thee, Moses, and Elijah, not knowing what he said. Henry notes those who talk of making tabernacles on earth for glorified saints in heaven know not what they say. — Matthew Henry

34 COMMENTARY: While he spoke, a cloud overshadowed them, and they feared as they entered the cloud. Glory that invites tabernacles also inspires fear. — Matthew Henry

35 COMMENTARY: A voice out of the cloud said, This is my beloved Son: hear him. The Father's command replaces Peter's plan: listen, do not build. — Matthew Henry

36 COMMENTARY: When the voice was past, Jesus was found alone, and they kept it close and told no man in those days what they had seen. Transfiguration is a specimen, Henry says, of the glory in which he will come to judge the world, and an encouragement to suffer for him. — Matthew Henry

37 COMMENTARY: On the next day, coming down from the hill, much people met him. Mountain glory descends to a waiting crowd. — Matthew Henry

38 COMMENTARY: A man cried out, Master, look upon my son, for he is mine only child. The father's plea names the child's uniqueness, as Jairus's did. — Matthew Henry

39 COMMENTARY: A spirit takes him, he suddenly cries out, it tears him till he foams, and bruising him hardly departs. Henry notes how deplorable the child's case, under an evil spirit, more frightful than natural disease, and what mischief Satan does where he gets possession. — Matthew Henry

40 COMMENTARY: I besought thy disciples to cast him out, and they could not. The disciples' failure is confessed openly before the crowd. — Matthew Henry

41 COMMENTARY: O faithless and perverse generation, how long shall I be with you and suffer you? Bring thy son hither. The rebuke falls on the generation, and the command brings the boy to the one who can heal. — Matthew Henry

42 COMMENTARY: As he was yet coming, the devil threw him down and tore him; Jesus rebuked the unclean spirit, healed the child, and delivered him again to his father. Henry notes a word from Christ healed the child, and happy are those with access to Christ, who can do what his disciples cannot. — Matthew Henry

43 COMMENTARY: All were amazed at the mighty power of God, but while they wondered at all Jesus did, he said to his disciples, let these sayings sink into your ears, for the Son of man shall be delivered into men's hands. Wonder at power must make room for the prediction of suffering. — Matthew Henry

44 COMMENTARY: Let these sayings sink down into your ears: for the Son of man shall be delivered into the hands of men. The saying is repeated because it will not sink in. — Matthew Henry

45 COMMENTARY: They understood not this saying, it was hid from them that they perceived it not, and they feared to ask him. Henry notes the prediction was plain enough, but they would not understand it because it agreed not with their notions. — Matthew Henry

46 COMMENTARY: Then there arose a reasoning among them, which of them should be greatest. From a prediction of suffering they turn at once to a contest for greatness. — Matthew Henry

47 COMMENTARY: Jesus, perceiving the thought of their heart, took a child and set him by him. The answer to ambition is a child placed beside him. — Matthew Henry

48 COMMENTARY: Whosoever receives this child in my name receives me, and whosoever receives me receives him that sent me; for he that is least among you all, the same shall be great. Henry notes a little child is the emblem by which Christ teaches simplicity and humility. — Matthew Henry

49 COMMENTARY: John answered, Master, we saw one casting out devils in thy name, and we forbade him, because he follows not with us. Exclusiveness follows ambition: outsiders doing the work must be stopped. — Matthew Henry

50 COMMENTARY: Forbid him not, for he that is not against us is for us. Henry notes that even the twelve, with reason to silence outsiders if any had, were warned not to; faithful followers may be found who do not follow with us. — Matthew Henry

51 COMMENTARY: When the time came that he should be received up, he steadfastly set his face to go to Jerusalem. The journey to the cross begins with a fixed face. — Matthew Henry

52 COMMENTARY: He sent messengers before his face, who entered a Samaritan village to make ready for him. Preparation precedes the rejected welcome. — Matthew Henry

53 COMMENTARY: They did not receive him, because his face was as though he would go to Jerusalem. National and religious prejudice, not enmity to God's word alone, shapes the refusal, Henry notes. — Matthew Henry

54 COMMENTARY: James and John asked, Lord, wilt thou that we command fire from heaven to consume them, even as Elijah did? Zeal borrows Elijah's fire for a Samaritan village. — Matthew Henry

55 COMMENTARY: He turned and rebuked them, saying, ye know not what manner of spirit ye are of. Henry notes they were ignorant of their own hearts' motives, which were pride and carnal ambition, and the gospel dispensation is marked by miracles of mercy. — Matthew Henry

56 COMMENTARY: For the Son of man is not come to destroy men's lives, but to save them. And they went to another village. The rebuke's reason is the mission itself: saving, not destroying. — Matthew Henry

57 COMMENTARY: As they went, a man said, Lord, I will follow thee whithersoever thou goest. Forward willingness is tested by the next verse. — Matthew Henry

58 COMMENTARY: Foxes have holes, and birds have nests, but the Son of man has not where to lay his head. Henry notes the hasty follower had not counted the cost, and must lay aside thoughts of great things in the world. — Matthew Henry

59 COMMENTARY: He said to another, Follow me; but he said, Lord, suffer me first to go and bury my father. The call is direct, the delay is pious and reasonable. — Matthew Henry

60 COMMENTARY: Let the dead bury their dead, but go thou and preach the kingdom of God. Henry notes religion teaches kindness and piety at home, but we must not make these an excuse for neglecting duty to God. — Matthew Henry

61 COMMENTARY: Another said, I will follow thee, but let me first go bid farewell to those at home. Farewell seems small, yet it holds the heart back. — Matthew Henry

62 COMMENTARY: No man, having put his hand to the plough and looking back, is fit for the kingdom of God. Henry warns that looking back leads to drawing back, and drawing back to perdition; he only that endures to the end shall be saved. — Matthew Henry
