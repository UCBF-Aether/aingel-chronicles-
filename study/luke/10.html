Study Notes — Luke

Chapter 10

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: The Lord appointed other seventy also, and sent them two and two before his face into every city and place whither he himself would come. Henry notes they were sent in pairs, that they might strengthen and encourage one another. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: The harvest truly is great, but labourers are few; pray the Lord of the harvest to send forth labourers. The first work of mission is prayer for workers, not strategy. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: Go your ways: behold, I send you forth as lambs among wolves. The sending itself acknowledges danger without softening it. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: Carry neither purse, scrip, nor shoes, and salute no man by the way. Unencumbered and undistracted, the messengers travel light and urgent. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: Into whatever house ye enter, first say, Peace be to this house. Peace is the mission's greeting before any demand. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: If the son of peace be there, your peace shall rest upon it; if not, it shall turn to you again. Peace offered is not wasted when refused; it returns to the giver. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: In the same house remain, eating and drinking such things as they give, for the labourer is worthy of his hire; go not from house to house. Settled gratitude replaces shopping for better lodgings. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: Into whatever city ye enter and they receive you, eat such things as are set before you. Reception is honoured by accepting what is offered. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: Heal the sick therein, and say, the kingdom of God is come nigh unto you. Healing and announcement together declare the kingdom's nearness. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: Into whatever city ye enter and they receive you not, go out into its streets and say, even the dust of your city cleaving to us we wipe off against you; notwithstanding, be sure the kingdom of God is come nigh unto you. Rejection does not cancel the announcement; it sharpens it. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: Even the very dust of your city, which cleaveth on us, we do wipe off against you: notwithstanding be ye sure of this, that the kingdom of God is come nigh unto you. The wiped dust is testimony, and the repeated nearness is the point. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: It shall be more tolerable in that day for Sodom than for that city. Henry warns the doom of those who receive God's grace in vain will be very fearful, measured by light refused. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: Woe unto thee, Chorazin! Woe unto thee, Bethsaida! For if the mighty works done in you had been done in Tyre and Sidon, they had long ago repented in sackcloth and ashes. Privileged towns are judged by works they saw and spurned. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: It shall be more tolerable for Tyre and Sidon at the judgment than for you. Gentile cities with less light fare better than towns that saw the works. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: And thou, Capernaum, exalted to heaven, shalt be thrust down to hell. Exaltation by privilege becomes the measure of the fall. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: He that hears you hears me, and he that despises you despises me, and he that despises me despises him that sent me. Henry notes despisers of faithful ministers will be reckoned despisers of God and Christ. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: The seventy returned with joy, saying, Lord, even the devils are subject unto us through thy name. Joy in power is the report brought home. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: I beheld Satan as lightning fall from heaven. Their small victories are part of a great fall, seen by Christ before they report it. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: Behold, I give you power to tread on serpents and scorpions, and over all the power of the enemy, and nothing shall by any means hurt you. Power over the enemy is given, not seized. — Matthew Henry

20 COMMENTARY: Notwithstanding, rejoice not that spirits are subject unto you, but rather rejoice because your names are written in heaven. Henry warns against spiritual pride, destruction of many, and redirects joy from power to written names. — Matthew Henry

21 COMMENTARY: In that hour Jesus rejoiced in spirit, saying, I thank thee, Father, Lord of heaven and earth, that thou hast hid these things from the wise and prudent and revealed them unto babes; even so, Father, for so it seemed good in thy sight. Henry notes it was fit to mark that hour of joy, for he was a man of sorrows, and in that hour, seeing Satan fall and his ministers succeed, he rejoiced; he ever resists the proud and gives grace to the humble. — Matthew Henry

22 COMMENTARY: All things are delivered to me of my Father; no man knows who the Son is but the Father, and who the Father is but the Son, and he to whom the Son will reveal him. Henry notes the more simply dependent we are on the Son's teaching, help, and blessing, the more we shall know both Father and Son. — Matthew Henry

23 COMMENTARY: Turning to his disciples privately, he said, blessed are the eyes which see the things that ye see. Private blessing follows public joy. — Matthew Henry

24 COMMENTARY: Many prophets and kings desired to see what ye see and have not seen, and to hear what ye hear and have not heard. The disciples' blessedness is measured by prophets' and kings' unfulfilled desire. — Matthew Henry

25 COMMENTARY: A lawyer stood up and tempted him, saying, Master, what shall I do to inherit eternal life? The question tests Jesus, and will test the questioner. — Matthew Henry

26 COMMENTARY: What is written in the law? How readest thou? Jesus answers a law question with the law itself, and with how it is read. — Matthew Henry

27 COMMENTARY: Thou shalt love the Lord thy God with all heart, soul, strength, and mind, and thy neighbour as thyself. Henry warns that speaking of eternal life carelessly takes God's name in vain, and no one loves God and neighbour with pure spiritual love without converting grace. — Matthew Henry

28 COMMENTARY: Thou hast answered right: this do, and thou shalt live. Right answer and right doing are not the same, and the lawyer knows it. — Matthew Henry

29 COMMENTARY: But he, willing to justify himself, said, and who is my neighbour? Self-justification narrows the command by narrowing the neighbour. — Matthew Henry

30 COMMENTARY: A man went down from Jerusalem to Jericho, fell among thieves, who stripped, wounded, and departed, leaving him half dead. Henry notes we were like this poor traveller: Satan robbed and wounded us, such is sin's mischief. — Matthew Henry

31 COMMENTARY: By chance a priest came down that way, saw him, and passed by on the other side. Henry laments how selfishness governs all ranks, and how many excuses men make to avoid trouble or expense in relieving others. — Matthew Henry

32 COMMENTARY: Likewise a Levite, at the place, came, looked on him, and passed by on the other side. Looking is not helping, and the second passer proves the first was no exception. — Matthew Henry

33 COMMENTARY: But a Samaritan, journeying, came where he was, and when he saw him, had compassion on him. Henry notes he was slighted by those who should have been friends, and cared for by a stranger, a Samaritan of the nation Jews most despised and would have no dealings with. — Matthew Henry

34 COMMENTARY: He went to him, bound up his wounds, pouring in oil and wine, set him on his own beast, brought him to an inn, and took care of him. Compassion becomes hands, oil, wine, beast, inn, and care. — Matthew Henry

35 COMMENTARY: On the morrow he took out two pence, gave them to the host, and said, take care of him, and whatever thou spendest more, when I come again I will repay thee. The Samaritan's care extends beyond his presence, at his own future cost. — Matthew Henry

36 COMMENTARY: Which of these three, thinkest thou, was neighbour to him that fell among thieves? The question is turned around: neighbour is not a category to define, but a deed to recognise. — Matthew Henry

37 COMMENTARY: He that showed mercy on him. Then Jesus said, go, and do thou likewise. Henry's conclusion is that the parable explains loving neighbour as self, without regard to nation, party, or distinction, and sets forth Christ's kindness to sinful, miserable men; having shown mercy, he bids us go and do likewise. — Matthew Henry

38 COMMENTARY: As they went, he entered a village, and Martha received him into her house. Hospitality opens the next scene. — Matthew Henry

39 COMMENTARY: She had a sister called Mary, who also sat at Jesus' feet and heard his word. Henry notes sitting at Christ's feet signifies readiness to receive his word and submission to its guidance. — Matthew Henry

40 COMMENTARY: Martha was cumbered about much serving, and said, Lord, dost thou not care that my sister has left me to serve alone? Bid her therefore help me. Service becomes complaint when it is much and alone. — Matthew Henry

41 COMMENTARY: Martha, Martha, thou art careful and troubled about many things. Henry notes Martha's respect and right care of household affairs, but she was for much serving, plenty, variety, exactness, and worldly business is a snare when it hinders serving God and getting good to our souls. — Matthew Henry

42 COMMENTARY: But one thing is needful, and Mary has chosen that good part, which shall not be taken away from her. Henry declares the favour of God needful to happiness and Christ's salvation needful to safety; where this is attended to, all else will be rightly pursued, and nothing, neither men nor devils, can take that part away. — Matthew Henry
