Study Notes — Luke

Chapter 13

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Some told him of Galileans whose blood Pilate had mingled with their sacrifices. Henry notes this tragical story is briefly related here and met with in no historians, yet it becomes the text for repentance. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: Suppose ye that these Galileans were sinners above all Galileans, because they suffered such things? The question challenges the link between spectacular suffering and spectacular sin. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: I tell you, Nay: but except ye repent, ye shall all likewise perish. Henry cautions not to blame great sufferers as therefore great sinners, and founds on sudden removals a call to repentance: the same Jesus who bids us repent because the kingdom is at hand bids us repent, or we shall perish. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: Or those eighteen, upon whom the tower in Siloam fell and slew them, think ye they were sinners above all men in Jerusalem? A second sudden death, by a tower built for safety, makes the same point. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: I tell you, Nay: but except ye repent, ye shall all likewise perish. Henry notes no place or employment secures us from death's stroke, so others' sudden removals are warnings to ourselves. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: A man had a fig tree planted in his vineyard, came seeking fruit, and found none. Planting in a vineyard implies care and expectation, not wild growth. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: Behold, these three years I come seeking fruit on this fig tree and find none: cut it down; why cumbereth it the ground? Three years of seeking frame the sentence: the tree occupies ground without fruit. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: Lord, let it alone this year also, till I shall dig about it, and dung it. The dresser pleads for one more year of cultivation, not for acquittal. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: And if it bear fruit, well; and if not, then after that thou shalt cut it down. Henry notes the parable enforces the warning just given: the barren tree, except it bear fruit, will be cut down; it refers first to Israel, yet awakens all who enjoy means of grace, for God may bear a little longer, but not always. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: He was teaching in one of the synagogues on the sabbath. Henry notes the Lord attended public worship on sabbaths, and even bodily infirmities, unless very grievous, should not keep us from it. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: There was a woman with a spirit of infirmity eighteen years, bowed together, unable to lift herself. Eighteen years bent is the measure of her bondage. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: When Jesus saw her, he called her and said, Woman, thou art loosed from thine infirmity. She came to be taught and get good to her soul, Henry notes, and then he relieved her bodily infirmity. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: He laid hands on her, and immediately she was made straight, and glorified God. Henry notes this cure represents grace upon the soul, and when crooked souls are made straight, they show it by glorifying God. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: The ruler answered with indignation, because Jesus healed on the sabbath, and said, there are six days to work; in them come and be healed, not on the sabbath day. Indignation cloaks enmity with sabbath zeal. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: Thou hypocrite, doth not each one of you on the sabbath loose his ox or ass from the stall and lead him away to watering? The ruler's own sabbath practice condemns his objection. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: And ought not this woman, a daughter of Abraham, whom Satan has bound eighteen years, be loosed from this bond on the sabbath day? Henry notes Christ knew the ruler's real enmity, cloaked with pretended sabbath zeal, for he would not have them healed any day; yet if Jesus speaks and puts forth healing power, sinners are set free, and such deliverance often wrought on the Lord's day agrees with that day's design. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: When he had said these things, all his adversaries were ashamed, and all the people rejoiced for all the glorious things done by him. Shame and rejoicing divide the audience by their response to mercy. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: Then said he, Unto what is the kingdom of God like, and whereunto shall I resemble it? The question introduces two parables of progress. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: It is like a grain of mustard seed, which a man took and cast into his garden; it grew into a great tree, and fowls lodged in its branches. Henry notes the progress of the gospel foretold here: may grace grow in our hearts, faith and love exceedingly, giving undoubted evidence of reality. — Matthew Henry

20 COMMENTARY: And again he said, Whereunto shall I liken the kingdom of God? The second likeness repeats the question for a different image. — Matthew Henry

21 COMMENTARY: It is like leaven, which a woman took and hid in three measures of meal, till the whole was leavened. Henry prays the saints' example be blessed to neighbours, and grace flow heart to heart, until the little one becomes a thousand. — Matthew Henry

22 COMMENTARY: He went through cities and villages, teaching, and journeying toward Jerusalem. Teaching on the way to Jerusalem frames the strait-gate exhortation that follows. — Matthew Henry

23 COMMENTARY: Then said one, Lord, are there few that be saved? And he said unto them. Henry notes the Saviour came to guide consciences, not gratify curiosity: ask not how many shall be saved, but shall I be one? — Matthew Henry

24 COMMENTARY: Strive to enter in at the strait gate, for many, I say, will seek to enter in, and shall not be able. Henry stresses the direction is to each of us, strive ye; all who will be saved must enter the strait gate and undergo a change of the whole man. — Matthew Henry

25 COMMENTARY: When once the master of the house is risen and shut the door, and ye stand without knocking, Lord, Lord, open unto us, he shall answer, I know you not whence ye are. A shut door answers late knocking with non-recognition. — Matthew Henry

26 COMMENTARY: Then shall ye begin to say, We have eaten and drunk in thy presence, and thou hast taught in our streets. Proximity to his presence and streets is pleaded as acquaintance. — Matthew Henry

27 COMMENTARY: But he shall say, I tell you, I know you not whence ye are; depart from me, all ye workers of iniquity. Eating in his presence without doing his will is counted iniquity, not intimacy. — Matthew Henry

28 COMMENTARY: There shall be weeping and gnashing of teeth, when ye shall see Abraham, Isaac, Jacob, and all prophets in the kingdom, and you yourselves thrust out. Seeing the patriarchs inside while oneself is thrust out is the torment pictured. — Matthew Henry

29 COMMENTARY: And they shall come from east, west, north, and south, and sit down in the kingdom of God. The kingdom's table gathers from every compass point. — Matthew Henry

30 COMMENTARY: Behold, there are last which shall be first, and first which shall be last. Henry warns none to despond for self or others, for if we reach heaven we shall meet many we little thought to meet, and miss many we expected to find. — Matthew Henry

31 COMMENTARY: The same day certain Pharisees said, Get thee out and depart hence, for Herod will kill thee. The warning may be friendly or a trap; Jesus answers it as a message to Herod. — Matthew Henry

32 COMMENTARY: Go ye, and tell that fox, Behold, I cast out devils, and I do cures today and tomorrow, and the third day I shall be perfected. Henry notes Christ, calling Herod a fox, gave him his true character, though it is not an example for us; and I shall be perfected means, when I die, I shall have completed my undertaking. — Matthew Henry

33 COMMENTARY: Nevertheless I must walk today, tomorrow, and the day following, for it cannot be that a prophet perish out of Jerusalem. His timetable is set, and Jerusalem is the appointed place of a prophet's death. — Matthew Henry

34 COMMENTARY: O Jerusalem, Jerusalem, which killest the prophets and stonest them sent unto thee; how often would I have gathered thy children together, as a hen gathers her brood under her wings, and ye would not! Henry notes the wickedness of places professing religion especially grieves the Lord, and urges welcoming all who come in the Lord's name to call us to salvation. — Matthew Henry

35 COMMENTARY: Behold, your house is left unto you desolate, and verily ye shall not see me, until ye shall say, Blessed is he that comes in the name of the Lord. Desolation follows refusal, until blessing replaces rejection. — Matthew Henry
