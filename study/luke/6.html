Study Notes — Luke

Chapter 6

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: On the second sabbath after the first, going through corn fields, his disciples plucked ears of corn and ate, rubbing them in their hands. Henry notes Christ justifies a work of necessity for themselves on the sabbath, here hunger met in the field. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: Certain Pharisees asked why they did what is not lawful on sabbath days. The charge turns a hungry handful of grain into a legal offence. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: Have ye not read what David did when he and his men were hungry? Jesus answers charge with Scripture, not with a new rule. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: David went into God's house, took and ate the shewbread, and gave to his men, though lawful only for priests. Necessity, in David's precedent, outweighed ceremonial restriction. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: The Son of man is Lord also of the sabbath. Henry's caution follows: we must not mistake this liberty for leave to sin, for it is his day, to be spent in his service and to his honour. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: On another sabbath he entered the synagogue and taught, and there was a man whose right hand was withered. Teaching and a test case arrive together. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: The scribes and Pharisees watched whether he would heal on the sabbath, to find an accusation. Mercy itself is watched as evidence. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: He knew their thoughts and told the man, rise up and stand forth in the midst. Henry notes Christ was neither ashamed nor afraid to own the purposes of his grace, though he knew enemies would take advantage. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: Is it lawful on sabbath days to do good or evil, to save life or destroy it? The question reframes the sabbath as a day for doing good, not for withholding it. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: Stretch forth thy hand: he did so, and his hand was restored whole as the other. A work of mercy proves suitable to the sabbath day. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: They were filled with madness and communed what they might do to Jesus. Henry marvels that men should be so wicked as to rage at a healing, and urges us not to be drawn from duty or usefulness by opposition. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: He went out into a mountain to pray, and continued all night in prayer to God. Henry contrasts our half hour of secret prayer with Christ's whole nights in these duties, and urges making the end of one good duty the beginning of another. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: When it was day he called his disciples, and of them chose twelve, whom he named apostles. The night of prayer issues in the day of choosing. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: Simon, named Peter, and Andrew his brother, James and John, Philip and Bartholomew. Henry notes never were men so privileged as these twelve, yet the list already carries its shadow. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: Matthew and Thomas, James son of Alphaeus, and Simon called Zelotes. The twelve span tax collector, doubter, and zealot alike. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: Judas brother of James, and Judas Iscariot, who also was the traitor. Henry's sober note: one of the twelve had a devil and proved a traitor, privilege being no proof against betrayal. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: He came down and stood in the plain, with his disciples and a great multitude from Judaea, Jerusalem, and the Tyre and Sidon coast, come to hear and be healed. Henry notes those without faithful preaching nearby had better travel far than be without it. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: They vexed with unclean spirits were healed. Bodily and spiritual afflictions are brought to the same healer. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: The whole multitude sought to touch him, for virtue went out of him and healed them all. Henry notes a fulness of grace in Christ, and healing virtue ready to go out, enough for all and enough for each. — Matthew Henry

20 COMMENTARY: Blessed be ye poor, for yours is the kingdom of God. Henry notes believers who take the gospel's precepts to themselves may take its promises to themselves, and those Christ blesses are blessed indeed. — Matthew Henry

21 COMMENTARY: Blessed are ye that hunger now, for ye shall be filled; blessed are ye that weep now, for ye shall laugh. Present hunger and tears are not the last word for Christ's disciples. — Matthew Henry

22 COMMENTARY: Blessed are ye when men hate, separate, reproach, and cast out your name as evil, for the Son of man's sake. Blessing is tied to his sake, not to suffering in general. — Matthew Henry

23 COMMENTARY: Rejoice in that day and leap for joy, for your reward is great in heaven, for so did their fathers to the prophets. Persecution joins disciples to the prophets, and reward, not revenge, is the answer. — Matthew Henry

24 COMMENTARY: Woe unto you that are rich, for ye have received your consolation. Henry warns woes are denounced against prosperous sinners as miserable, though the world envies them. — Matthew Henry

25 COMMENTARY: Woe to you that are full, for ye shall hunger; woe to you that laugh now, for ye shall mourn and weep. Present fullness and laughter are reversed, as present hunger and weeping were. — Matthew Henry

26 COMMENTARY: Woe when all men speak well of you, for so did their fathers to the false prophets. Universal praise is a warning sign, not a credential. — Matthew Henry

27 COMMENTARY: I say to you which hear, love your enemies, do good to them that hate you. Henry calls these hard lessons to flesh and blood, made easy only by being thoroughly grounded in Christ's love. — Matthew Henry

28 COMMENTARY: Bless them that curse you, and pray for them that despitefully use you. Blessing and prayer replace retaliation word for word. — Matthew Henry

29 COMMENTARY: To him that smites one cheek offer also the other; him that takes thy cloak forbid not thy coat also. Non-retaliation is pushed past patience into generosity. — Matthew Henry

30 COMMENTARY: Give to every man that asks, and of him that takes thy goods ask them not again. Giving is freed from the ledger of return. — Matthew Henry

31 COMMENTARY: As ye would that men should do to you, do ye also to them likewise. The golden rule sums the whole ethic in one sentence. — Matthew Henry

32 COMMENTARY: If ye love only them that love you, what thank have ye? For sinners also love those that love them. Love that only returns love is common, not Christian. — Matthew Henry

33 COMMENTARY: If ye do good only to them that do good to you, what thank have ye? Sinners do even the same. Reciprocal kindness earns no distinctive praise. — Matthew Henry

34 COMMENTARY: If ye lend only to them of whom ye hope to receive, what thank have ye? Sinners lend to sinners to receive as much again. Lending for return is business, not mercy. — Matthew Henry

35 COMMENTARY: Love your enemies, do good, and lend, hoping for nothing again; your reward shall be great, and ye shall be children of the Highest, for he is kind to the unthankful and evil. Henry's aim is to be merciful even according to our heavenly Father's mercy to us. — Matthew Henry

36 COMMENTARY: Be ye therefore merciful, as your Father also is merciful. The Father's mercy is both the model and the measure. — Matthew Henry

37 COMMENTARY: Judge not, and ye shall not be judged; condemn not, and ye shall not be condemned; forgive, and ye shall be forgiven. Henry urges care when we blame others, for we need allowance ourselves, and a giving, forgiving spirit reaps its own benefit. — Matthew Henry

38 COMMENTARY: Give, and it shall be given to you, good measure, pressed down, shaken together, running over, men shall give into your bosom; with the same measure ye mete it shall be measured to you again. Providence, Henry notes, does what should encourage us in doing good, though full returns are made in another world, not this. — Matthew Henry

39 COMMENTARY: Can the blind lead the blind? Shall they not both fall into the ditch? A blind guide doubles the fall. — Matthew Henry

40 COMMENTARY: The disciple is not above his master, but every one that is perfect shall be as his master. The ceiling of discipleship is likeness to Christ, not superiority over him. — Matthew Henry

41 COMMENTARY: Why behold the mote in thy brother's eye, but perceive not the beam in thine own? The fault-finder's eye is sharp outward and blind inward. — Matthew Henry

42 COMMENTARY: How canst thou say, let me pull out thy mote, when thou beholdest not thy beam? Hypocrite, cast out first thy beam, then shalt thou see clearly to pull out thy brother's mote. Self-correction precedes, and enables, brotherly correction. — Matthew Henry

43 COMMENTARY: A good tree brings not forth corrupt fruit, nor a corrupt tree good fruit. Fruit reveals the tree, whatever its leaves claim. — Matthew Henry

44 COMMENTARY: Every tree is known by his own fruit; of thorns men do not gather figs, nor of brambles grapes. Henry's prayer follows: may Christ's word be grafted in our hearts, that we may be fruitful in every good word and work. — Matthew Henry

45 COMMENTARY: A good man out of the good treasure of his heart brings forth good, and an evil man evil, for of the abundance of the heart his mouth speaks. Speech is the overflow of treasure, good or evil. — Matthew Henry

46 COMMENTARY: Why call ye me, Lord, Lord, and do not the things which I say? Title without obedience is the contradiction the whole sermon exposes. — Matthew Henry

47 COMMENTARY: Whosoever comes to me, hears my sayings, and does them, I will show you to whom he is like. Hearing plus doing is the definition of the hearer Christ pictures. — Matthew Henry

48 COMMENTARY: He is like a man who built a house, dug deep, and laid the foundation on a rock; when the flood arose the stream beat vehemently and could not shake it, for it was founded on rock. Henry notes those who take pains in religion found hope on Christ, the Rock of Ages, and in death and judgment are safe, kept through faith unto salvation. — Matthew Henry

49 COMMENTARY: He that hears and does not is like a man who built without foundation upon the earth; the stream beat vehemently, immediately it fell, and the ruin was great. Only those who think, speak, and act according to Christ's words, Henry says, make sure work for souls and eternity. — Matthew Henry
