Study Notes — Luke

Chapter 1

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Luke opens by noting that many had already drawn up accounts of the gospel events. Henry stresses that Luke will not write about matters Christians may safely differ over, but about things surely believed; JFB adds that these earlier narratives rested on eyewitness testimony. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: The earlier accounts came from those who were eyewitnesses from the beginning and ministers of the word. JFB notes that "from the beginning" here points to the start of Christ's public ministry, as the following narrative makes plain. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: Luke claims exact knowledge traced from the very first, and writes in order to Theophilus. JFB observes that this claim to trace all things from their earliest rise is a virtual claim that his Gospel should supersede the many partial narratives. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: The purpose is certainty: Theophilus, already instructed, should know the sureness of what he was taught. Henry notes the doctrine of Christ is what the wisest and best have ventured their souls upon with confidence. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: The story begins under Herod with Zacharias, a priest of the course of Abia, and Elisabeth, herself of Aaron's line. Henry notes both were sinners saved like all others, yet eminent for piety and integrity. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: Both husband and wife are described as righteous before God, walking blamelessly in his commandments and ordinances. Henry's point is that their piety was real and obedient, not mere reputation. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: Their blameless life was shadowed by childlessness, and both were now well advanced in years. Henry notes it could not be expected that Elisabeth should bear a child in her old age, which makes what follows pure mercy. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: Zacharias was executing the priest's office before God in the order of his course. The setting matters: this is ordinary, faithful temple service, not a staged occasion, when God breaks in. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: By lot, according to priestly custom, Zacharias was chosen to burn incense in the temple. Henry connects the incense with the people's prayers offered without, acceptable only through Christ's intercession above. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: While Zacharias burned incense, the whole multitude prayed outside at that hour. Henry draws the lesson that we cannot expect an interest in Christ's intercession if we do not pray earnestly ourselves. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: An angel of the Lord appeared standing at the right side of the altar of incense. The place is fitting: the answer to long prayer comes while the priest stands at the very symbol of intercession. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: Zacharias was troubled and fear fell upon him at the sight. Henry's account treats this as the natural awe of a godly man confronted with a heavenly messenger, not as a rebuke in itself. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: The angel's first word is reassurance: Zacharias's prayer is heard, and Elisabeth shall bear a son to be called John. Henry notes that prayers of faith are filed in heaven and not forgotten, even when answered late. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: The child will bring joy and gladness, and many shall rejoice at his birth. Henry observes that mercies given in answer to prayer are doubly sweet, and this mercy will gladden more than one household. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: John shall be great in the Lord's sight, abstaining from wine and strong drink, and filled with the Holy Ghost from his mother's womb. Henry sees in him a man set apart from earthly pleasures for a holy work. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: Many of Israel shall John turn to the Lord their God. Henry stresses that John will be instrumental in converting many souls to God and preparing them to receive the gospel. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: John will go before the Lord in the spirit and power of Elijah, turning fathers' hearts to children and the disobedient to the wisdom of the just. Henry explains this as bringing the rebellious to attend to the wisdom of the Just One who was coming. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: Zacharias answers with unbelief, asking how he shall know this, since he and his wife are old. Henry is blunt: his unbelief spoke, objecting against God's word even after hearing the angel. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: The angel names himself Gabriel, who stands in God's presence and was sent to speak these glad tidings. The name and standing underline that this is no private fancy but a message from God himself. — Matthew Henry

20 COMMENTARY: Because Zacharias did not believe, he shall be dumb until the things are performed in their season. Henry reads this as both just and kind: just, for objecting against God's word, and kind, since it stopped further distrustful words and confirmed his faith. — Matthew Henry

21 COMMENTARY: The people waited and marvelled that Zacharias tarried so long in the temple. The delay itself becomes a sign that something out of the ordinary has happened within. — Matthew Henry

22 COMMENTARY: Coming out unable to speak, Zacharias beckoned, and the people perceived he had seen a vision. Henry notes that even real believers can dishonour God by unbelief, and then find their mouths stopped in silence and confusion. — Matthew Henry

23 COMMENTARY: As soon as the days of his ministration were accomplished, Zacharias departed to his own house. Duty done, he carries the silent sign home with him, waiting for God's word to ripen. — Matthew Henry

24 COMMENTARY: After those days Elisabeth conceived and hid herself five months. Henry's theme is God's gracious regard: he has looked on his people with compassion, and deals with them accordingly. — Matthew Henry

25 COMMENTARY: Elisabeth reads her conception as the Lord taking away her reproach among men. Henry closes the section with the lesson to observe God's gracious dealings and mark his compassion in them. — Matthew Henry

26 COMMENTARY: In the sixth month Gabriel was sent from God to Nazareth in Galilee. The same messenger who announced John now announces one far greater, and the lowly town sets the tone of the whole story. — Matthew Henry

27 COMMENTARY: The angel comes to a virgin espoused to Joseph, of the house of David, whose name was Mary. Henry notes that Christ must be born miraculously, and David's line is named to mark the promised King. — Matthew Henry

28 COMMENTARY: Gabriel greets Mary as highly favoured, with the Lord present with her and blessing her among women. Henry explains the address as meaning she is especially chosen and favoured of the Most High, an honour Jewish mothers had long desired. — Matthew Henry

29 COMMENTARY: Mary was troubled at the salutation and pondered what manner of greeting it was. Henry notes the wondrous salutation and appearance troubled her, a modest response to an unlooked-for honour. — Matthew Henry

30 COMMENTARY: The angel reassures her: fear not, for she has found favour with God. The assurance comes before the announcement, as it did for Zacharias, because fear would otherwise drown the message. — Matthew Henry

31 COMMENTARY: Mary shall conceive and bear a son, and call his name Jesus. Henry dwells on the name: Jesus, a Saviour, sweet to speak and sweet to hear, refreshing the fainting spirits of humbled sinners. — Matthew Henry

32 COMMENTARY: He shall be great, called the Son of the Highest, and given the throne of his father David. Henry stresses that he is one in nature and perfection with the Lord God, not merely a favoured man. — Matthew Henry

33 COMMENTARY: He shall reign over the house of Jacob for ever, and of his kingdom there shall be no end. The everlasting kingdom answers every earthly throne's rise and fall. — Matthew Henry

34 COMMENTARY: Mary asks how this shall be, seeing she knows not a man. Henry contrasts this with Zacharias: her reply is the language of faith and humble admiration, and she asks no sign to confirm her faith. — Matthew Henry

35 COMMENTARY: The Holy Ghost shall come upon her and the power of the Highest overshadow her, so the child shall be called the Son of God. Henry calls this a great mystery, fit for one taken into union with the divine nature. — Matthew Henry

36 COMMENTARY: Elisabeth, called barren, has also conceived in her old age, now in her sixth month. The sign is given unasked: God is already doing in Mary's own family what nature calls impossible. — Matthew Henry

37 COMMENTARY: With God nothing shall be impossible. Henry makes this the rule for all conflicts: as we read and hear God's promises, we should turn them into prayers. — Matthew Henry

38 COMMENTARY: Mary answers as the Lord's handmaid: be it unto me according to thy word. Henry holds this up as guiding our desires by God's word, and the angel departs once consent is given. — Matthew Henry

39 COMMENTARY: Mary arose and went with haste into the hill country, to a city of Judah. Henry notes it is very good for those in whom grace has begun to communicate with one another, and Mary does so at once. — Matthew Henry

40 COMMENTARY: Mary entered the house of Zacharias and saluted Elisabeth. The meeting of the two mothers is the meeting of the two promises, John's and Jesus'. — Matthew Henry

41 COMMENTARY: At Mary's salutation the babe leaped in Elisabeth's womb, and Elisabeth was filled with the Holy Ghost. Henry notes Elisabeth was conscious of the approach of the mother of the great Redeemer, and spoke under the Spirit's influence. — Matthew Henry

42 COMMENTARY: Elisabeth cries out that Mary is blessed among women, and blessed is the fruit of her womb. Henry reads this as declaring Mary and her expected child most blessed, as peculiarly honoured of and dear to the Most High. — Matthew Henry

43 COMMENTARY: Elisabeth wonders that the mother of her Lord should come to her. The wonder is humility: the greater honour comes to the lesser house, and she knows it. — Matthew Henry

44 COMMENTARY: As soon as Elisabeth heard Mary's voice, the babe leaped for joy. The unborn John already does his forerunner's work, rejoicing at the presence of Christ. — Matthew Henry

45 COMMENTARY: Blessed is she that believed, for there shall be a performance of what was told her from the Lord. Henry's contrast is plain: Mary believed where Zacharias doubted, and faith is here pronounced blessed. — Matthew Henry

46 COMMENTARY: Mary answers, my soul doth magnify the Lord. Henry notes that, animated by Elisabeth's address and under the Holy Ghost, Mary broke out into joy, admiration, and gratitude. — Matthew Henry

47 COMMENTARY: Her spirit rejoices in God her Saviour. Henry stresses that Mary knew herself a sinner who needed a Saviour, and could rejoice in God only as interested in salvation through the promised Messiah. — Matthew Henry

48 COMMENTARY: God has regarded the low estate of his handmaiden, so all generations shall call her blessed. The blessing rests on God's regard for lowliness, not on any claim of her own. — Matthew Henry

49 COMMENTARY: He that is mighty has done great things for her, and holy is his name. Mary's praise turns from her own case to the might and holiness of the God who acted. — Matthew Henry

50 COMMENTARY: His mercy is on them that fear him from generation to generation. The mercy Mary received is not a private exception but the standing way God deals with the fearing. — Matthew Henry

51 COMMENTARY: He has showed strength with his arm and scattered the proud in the imagination of their hearts. God's arm reverses human pride at its very root, in proud imaginations. — Matthew Henry

52 COMMENTARY: He has put down the mighty from their seats and exalted them of low degree. The song's reversals are the gospel's pattern: the self-sufficient are sent away, the lowly lifted. — Matthew Henry

53 COMMENTARY: He has filled the hungry with good things, and sent the rich empty away. Henry applies it spiritually: those who see their need of Christ he fills with the best things, while the self-sufficient go empty. — Matthew Henry

54 COMMENTARY: He has helped his servant Israel, in remembrance of his mercy. What God does for Mary is what he promised to his people: mercy remembered, not newly invented. — Matthew Henry

55 COMMENTARY: As he spoke to the fathers, to Abraham and his seed for ever. The promise to Abraham is the anchor: this birth fulfils an old word, not a new idea. — Matthew Henry

56 COMMENTARY: Mary abode with Elisabeth about three months, then returned to her own house. The visit ends quietly, with the two mothers parting to await their appointed days. — Matthew Henry

57 COMMENTARY: Elisabeth's full time came, and she brought forth a son. Henry notes the great joy among all the relations of the family at John's birth. — Matthew Henry

58 COMMENTARY: Neighbours and cousins heard how the Lord had showed great mercy to Elisabeth, and rejoiced with her. Mercy shared becomes joy shared, and the whole neighbourhood enters into it. — Matthew Henry

59 COMMENTARY: On the eighth day they came to circumcise the child, and called him Zacharias after his father. Custom and family expectation point one way; the parents' obedience will point another. — Matthew Henry

60 COMMENTARY: His mother answered, not so; he shall be called John. Elisabeth holds to the angel's word against the pressure of custom, naming the child by God's appointment. — Matthew Henry

61 COMMENTARY: They object that none of her kindred is called by this name. The objection shows how new God's way is: the name breaks family precedent because the child breaks ordinary expectation. — Matthew Henry

62 COMMENTARY: They made signs to his father, asking how he would have the child called. Zacharias, still dumb, is appealed to as the one whose silence has already spoken. — Matthew Henry

63 COMMENTARY: He asked for a writing table and wrote, His name is John, and they marvelled all. Henry notes the name means "Gracious," because John shall bring in the gospel wherein God's grace shines most bright. — Matthew Henry

64 COMMENTARY: Immediately his mouth was opened, his tongue loosed, and he spoke and praised God. Henry's summary is memorable: unbelief closed his mouth, and believing opened it again; when God opens our lips, our mouths must show forth his praise. — Matthew Henry

65 COMMENTARY: Fear came on all round about, and all these sayings were noised abroad through the hill country of Judaea. The private household wonder becomes public talk, preparing the country for what is coming. — Matthew Henry

66 COMMENTARY: All who heard laid these things up in their hearts, asking what manner of child this shall be, for the hand of the Lord was with him. Henry urges us to observe God's dealings and wait the event, as they did. — Matthew Henry

67 COMMENTARY: Zacharias was filled with the Holy Ghost and prophesied. The dumb priest becomes a prophet: the same Spirit that sealed his mouth for unbelief now opens it for praise and prophecy. — Matthew Henry

68 COMMENTARY: Blessed be the Lord God of Israel, for he has visited and redeemed his people. Henry reads the song as prophecy concerning the kingdom and salvation of the Messiah, now at hand. — Matthew Henry

69 COMMENTARY: God has raised up a horn of salvation in the house of his servant David. The horn is strength and kingship: salvation comes through David's promised heir, not through a new dynasty. — Matthew Henry

70 COMMENTARY: As he spoke by the mouth of his holy prophets since the world began. The new salvation is the old promise, spoken all along by prophets, now arriving. — Matthew Henry

71 COMMENTARY: That we should be saved from our enemies and from all that hate us. Salvation here is deliverance with a purpose, not escape alone, as the following verses spell out. — Matthew Henry

72 COMMENTARY: To perform the mercy promised to the fathers and to remember his holy covenant. JFB notes there is not a word in this noble burst of song about Zacharias's own child: like Elisabeth, he loses sight of self in the glory of a Greater. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

73 COMMENTARY: The oath which he swore to Abraham. The covenant is grounded in God's oath, so its fulfilment is as sure as God's own word. — Matthew Henry

74 COMMENTARY: That we, being delivered from our enemies, might serve him without fear. Deliverance is for service: freedom from enemies frees God's people for fearless worship. — Matthew Henry

75 COMMENTARY: In holiness and righteousness before him all the days of our life. The service is lifelong and inward as well as outward, holiness and righteousness together. — Matthew Henry

76 COMMENTARY: And thou, child, shalt be called prophet of the Highest, going before the Lord to prepare his ways. Only now does the song turn to John, and his whole calling is to prepare the way for another. — Matthew Henry

77 COMMENTARY: To give knowledge of salvation to his people by the remission of their sins. John's preparation is not ceremony but knowledge: salvation known as forgiveness. — Matthew Henry

78 COMMENTARY: Through the tender mercy of our God, whereby the dayspring from on high has visited us. JFB explains the dayspring as Christ himself, the Sun of righteousness arising on a dark world, or the light he sheds; the sense is one. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

79 COMMENTARY: To give light to them that sit in darkness and in the shadow of death, to guide our feet into the way of peace. Henry calls the gospel discovering, reviving, and directing: it shows what we were dark about, revives condemned prisoners, and guides feet into peace. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

80 COMMENTARY: The child grew and waxed strong in spirit, and was in the deserts till the day of his showing unto Israel. JFB pictures a concluding paragraph of grandeur, tracing John's bodily and mental growth in the wilderness, alone with God, until his public appearance. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
