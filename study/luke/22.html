Study Notes — Luke

Chapter 22

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Now the feast of unleavened bread drew nigh, which is called the Passover. The feast that frames the passion draws near. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: The chief priests and scribes sought how they might kill him, for they feared the people. Killing is sought, but fear of the people restrains the manner. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: Then entered Satan into Judas surnamed Iscariot, being of the number of the twelve. Henry notes Christ knew all men and had wise and holy ends in taking Judas as disciple, yet how one who knew Christ so well came to betray him is told here: Satan entered into Judas. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: He went his way, and communed with the chief priests and captains, how he might betray him unto them. The betrayer initiates the communing, not the priests alone. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: They were glad, and covenanted to give him money. Gladness and covenant seal the betrayal with money. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: He promised, and sought opportunity to betray him in the absence of the multitude. Opportunity sought is absence of the multitude, whose presence restrains. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: Then came the day of unleavened bread, when the passover must be killed. The day of killing the passover arrives, and the Lamb's own killing approaches. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: He sent Peter and John, saying, Go and prepare us the passover, that we may eat. Preparation is delegated to Peter and John. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: They said, Where wilt thou that we prepare? The question asks for place, and the answer will be a sign, not an address. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: Behold, when ye are entered into the city, there shall a man meet you, bearing a pitcher of water; follow him into the house where he enters in. Henry notes those who go upon Christ's word need not fear disappointment, and according to orders given, the disciples got all ready. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: Ye shall say unto the goodman of the house, The Master says unto thee, Where is the guestchamber, where I shall eat the passover with my disciples? The Master's question claims the room. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: He shall show you a large upper room furnished: there make ready. A large furnished upper room awaits, already prepared. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: They went, and found as he had said unto them, and they made ready the passover. Finding as said, they make ready. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: When the hour was come, he sat down, and the twelve apostles with him. The hour, not merely the day, governs the sitting down. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: With desire I have desired to eat this passover with you before I suffer. Henry notes Jesus bids this passover welcome and desired it, though he knew sufferings would follow, because it was in order to his Father's glory and man's redemption. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: For I say unto you, I will not any more eat thereof, until it be fulfilled in the kingdom of God. This passover is his last until fulfilment in the kingdom. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: He took the cup, and gave thanks, and said, Take this, and divide it among yourselves. The cup is shared among them, divided, not drunk alone. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: For I say unto you, I will not drink of the fruit of the vine, until the kingdom of God shall come. Henry notes he takes leave of all passovers, signifying doing away with ceremonial ordinances, of which passover was earliest and chief, because now in God's kingdom the substance was come. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: He took bread, gave thanks, broke it, and gave unto them, saying, This is my body which is given for you: this do in remembrance of me. Henry notes the supper is a sign and memorial of Christ already come, who by dying delivered us, and the breaking of his body as sacrifice is brought to remembrance by breaking of bread. — Matthew Henry

20 COMMENTARY: Likewise also the cup after supper, saying, This cup is the new testament in my blood, which is shed for you. Henry notes the shedding of blood, by which atonement was made, is represented by the wine, and nothing is more nourishing to the soul than the doctrine of Christ's atonement and assurance of interest in it. — Matthew Henry

21 COMMENTARY: But behold, the hand of him that betrays me is with me on the table. The betrayer's hand shares the table with the betrayed. — Matthew Henry

22 COMMENTARY: And truly the Son of man goes, as it was determined, but woe unto that man by whom he is betrayed! Determination does not remove woe from the betrayer. — Matthew Henry

23 COMMENTARY: They began to enquire among themselves, which of them it was that should do this thing. The table turns to self-enquiry, each fearing himself. — Matthew Henry

24 COMMENTARY: There was also a strife among them, which of them should be accounted the greatest. Henry notes how unbecoming worldly ambition of being greatest is to a follower of Jesus, who took the form of a servant and humbled himself to the cross's death. — Matthew Henry

25 COMMENTARY: The kings of the Gentiles exercise lordship over them, and they that exercise authority upon them are called benefactors. Gentile lordship and its flattering title are the pattern rejected. — Matthew Henry

26 COMMENTARY: But ye shall not be so, but he that is greatest among you, let him be as the younger, and he that is chief, as he that does serve. Greatness is redefined as youth and service. — Matthew Henry

27 COMMENTARY: For whether is greater, he that sits at meat, or he that serves? Is not he that sits at meat? But I am among you as he that serves. The greater by custom sits, but Christ among them serves. — Matthew Henry

28 COMMENTARY: Ye are they which have continued with me in my temptations. Continuance through temptations is credited to the disputing disciples. — Matthew Henry

29 COMMENTARY: And I appoint unto you a kingdom, as my Father has appointed unto me. Appointment passes from Father to Son to disciples. — Matthew Henry

30 COMMENTARY: That ye may eat and drink at my table in my kingdom, and sit on thrones judging the twelve tribes of Israel. Table and thrones together: fellowship and judgment. — Matthew Henry

31 COMMENTARY: And the Lord said, Simon, Simon, behold, Satan has desired to have you, that he may sift you as wheat. Henry notes in the way to happiness we must expect to be assaulted and sifted by Satan, who if he cannot destroy will try to disgrace or distress. — Matthew Henry

32 COMMENTARY: But I have prayed for thee, that thy faith fail not, and when thou art converted, strengthen thy brethren. Henry notes if believers were left to themselves they would fall, but they are kept by God's power and Christ's prayer; and Peter, converted, must strengthen brethren. — Matthew Henry

33 COMMENTARY: And he said, Lord, I am ready to go with thee, both into prison, and to death. Readiness is professed for prison and death together. — Matthew Henry

34 COMMENTARY: And he said, I tell thee, Peter, the cock shall not crow this day, before thou shalt thrice deny that thou knowest me. Henry warns nothing more certainly forebodes a fall than self-confidence with disregard to warnings and contempt of danger. — Matthew Henry

35 COMMENTARY: When I sent you without purse, scrip, and shoes, lacked ye anything? And they said, Nothing. The earlier mission lacked nothing, proving provision without purse. — Matthew Henry

36 COMMENTARY: But now, he that has a purse, let him take it, and likewise his scrip, and he that has no sword, let him sell his garment, and buy one. Henry notes a very great change of circumstances approaches: friends will not be as kind as before, enemies more fierce, so purse and scrip are now needed. — Matthew Henry

37 COMMENTARY: For I say unto you, that this that is written must yet be accomplished in me, And he was reckoned among the transgressors, for the things concerning me have an end. Henry notes the apostles understood real weapons, but he spoke only of spiritual warfare's weapons, the sword of the Spirit. — Matthew Henry

38 COMMENTARY: And they said, Lord, behold, here are two swords. And he said, It is enough. Two swords are enough to close the discussion, not to arm a revolt. — Matthew Henry

39 COMMENTARY: He came out, and went, as he was wont, to the mount of Olives, and his disciples also followed him. Custom leads him to Olivet, even on this night. — Matthew Henry

40 COMMENTARY: When he was at the place, he said unto them, Pray that ye enter not into temptation. The garden's first word is prayer against temptation. — Matthew Henry

41 COMMENTARY: He was withdrawn from them about a stone's cast, and kneeled down, and prayed. Withdrawal by a stone's cast, kneeling, praying: the agony begins in posture. — Matthew Henry

42 COMMENTARY: Saying, Father, if thou be willing, remove this cup from me: nevertheless not my will, but thine, be done. Henry notes every description of his state of mind proves the tremendous nature of the assault and his perfect foreknowledge of its terrors. — Matthew Henry

43 COMMENTARY: There appeared an angel unto him from heaven, strengthening him. Henry notes this as one of three things peculiar to Luke: an angel strengthening him, part of his humiliation to be strengthened by a ministering spirit. — Matthew Henry

44 COMMENTARY: Being in an agony he prayed more earnestly, and his sweat was as it were great drops of blood falling down to the ground. Henry notes the second and third peculiar things: in agony he prayed more earnestly, and his sweat was as great drops of blood, showing the travail of his soul. — Matthew Henry

45 COMMENTARY: When he rose up from prayer, and was come to his disciples, he found them sleeping for sorrow. Sorrow, not indifference, sleeps the disciples. — Matthew Henry

46 COMMENTARY: And said, Why sleep ye? Rise and pray, lest ye enter into temptation. Henry warns unless we watch and pray always, we may be drawn in the day into sins we in the morning most resolved against. — Matthew Henry

47 COMMENTARY: While he yet spoke, behold a multitude, and he that was called Judas, one of the twelve, went before them, and drew near unto Jesus to kiss him. Henry notes nothing is a greater affront to Jesus than to be betrayed by professed followers who say they love him. — Matthew Henry

48 COMMENTARY: But Jesus said, Judas, betrayest thou the Son of man with a kiss? The kiss, sign of love, is named as the betrayal's instrument. — Matthew Henry

49 COMMENTARY: When they which were about him saw what would follow, they said, Lord, shall we smite with the sword? The disciples ask for sword action at the arrest. — Matthew Henry

50 COMMENTARY: One of them smote the servant of the high priest, and cut off his right ear. The sword asked for is used at once, on the high priest's servant. — Matthew Henry

51 COMMENTARY: And Jesus answered, Suffer ye thus far. And he touched his ear, and healed him. Henry notes Jesus gave an illustrious example of doing good to those that hate us, as afterwards of praying for those that despitefully use us. — Matthew Henry

52 COMMENTARY: Then Jesus said unto the chief priests, captains of the temple, and elders, which were come to him, Be ye come out, as against a thief, with swords and staves? The arrest's manner is questioned: swords and staves, as against a thief. — Matthew Henry

53 COMMENTARY: When I was daily with you in the temple, ye stretched forth no hands against me, but this is your hour, and the power of darkness. Henry notes Christ was willing to wait for triumphs till warfare was accomplished, and the hour and power of darkness were short, as wicked triumphs always will be. — Matthew Henry

54 COMMENTARY: Then took they him, and led him, and brought him into the high priest's house. And Peter followed afar off. Following afar off begins Peter's fall. — Matthew Henry

55 COMMENTARY: When they had kindled a fire in the midst of the hall, and were set down together, Peter sat down among them. The fire's circle becomes the place of denial. — Matthew Henry

56 COMMENTARY: But a certain maid beheld him as he sat by the fire, and earnestly looked upon him, and said, This man was also with him. A maid's earnest look names him as with Jesus. — Matthew Henry

57 COMMENTARY: And he denied him, saying, Woman, I know him not. Henry notes Peter's fall was denying he knew Christ and was his disciple, disowning him because of distress and danger. — Matthew Henry

58 COMMENTARY: After a little while another saw him, and said, Thou art also of them. And Peter said, Man, I am not. The second denial follows after a little while, to another witness. — Matthew Henry

59 COMMENTARY: About the space of one hour after, another confidently affirmed, Of a truth this fellow also was with him, for he is a Galilaean. An hour later, Galilean speech confirms what Peter denies. — Matthew Henry

60 COMMENTARY: And Peter said, Man, I know not what thou sayest. And immediately, while he yet spoke, the cock crew. The third denial is cut short by the cock's crow. — Matthew Henry

61 COMMENTARY: And the Lord turned, and looked upon Peter. And Peter remembered the word of the Lord, how he had said, Before the cock crow, thou shalt deny me thrice. Henry lists the look's sixfold character: convincing, chiding, expostulating, compassionate, directing, and significant, conveying grace to enable repentance. — Matthew Henry

62 COMMENTARY: And Peter went out, and wept bitterly. Henry notes grace works in and by the word, bringing it to mind and setting it home on conscience, giving the soul its happy turn; and it was not the mere look, but divine grace with it, that restored Peter. — Matthew Henry

63 COMMENTARY: And the men that held Jesus mocked him, and smote him. Mocking and smiting follow Peter's weeping, in the same house. — Matthew Henry

64 COMMENTARY: When they had blindfolded him, they struck him on the face, and asked, Prophesy, who is it that smote thee? Blindfolded prophecy is demanded as a game. — Matthew Henry

65 COMMENTARY: And many other things blasphemously spoke they against him. Mockery multiplies into many blasphemous speeches. — Matthew Henry

66 COMMENTARY: As soon as it was day, the elders, chief priests, and scribes came together, and led him into their council, saying, Art thou the Christ? Tell us. Daylight brings the council and its direct question. — Matthew Henry

67 COMMENTARY: Art thou the Christ? Tell us. And he said, If I tell you, ye will not believe. The answer names their unbelief before their verdict. — Matthew Henry

68 COMMENTARY: And if I also ask you, ye will not answer me, nor let me go. Questions from him would meet neither answer nor release. — Matthew Henry

69 COMMENTARY: Hereafter shall the Son of man sit on the right hand of the power of God. The future seat at God's right hand is the proof offered. — Matthew Henry

70 COMMENTARY: Then said they all, Art thou then the Son of God? And he said, Ye say that I am. Henry notes he owns himself Son of God, though he knew he should suffer for it, and upon this they ground his condemnation. — Matthew Henry

71 COMMENTARY: And they said, What need we any further witness? For we ourselves have heard of his own mouth. His own mouth becomes their witness, and they need no further. — Matthew Henry
