Study Notes — Luke

Chapter 2

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: A decree from Caesar Augustus ordered all the world to be taxed. Henry notes the fulness of time was now come: God sends forth his Son, made of a woman and under the law, and even an emperor's decree serves that timing. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: This taxing was first made when Cyrenius was governor of Syria. The historical detail anchors the birth in real imperial administration, not in legend. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: All went to be taxed, each to his own city. The universal movement sets the stage for Joseph's journey to the city of his lineage. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: Joseph went up from Nazareth to Bethlehem, the city of David, because he was of David's house and lineage. The census thus brings the promised King's heir to David's city. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: Joseph travelled to be taxed with Mary, his espoused wife, who was great with child. The journey itself is part of the lowliness of the birth that follows. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: While they were in Bethlehem, the days were accomplished for Mary to be delivered. Henry stresses the circumstances were very mean, yet this is the appointed hour. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: Mary brought forth her firstborn son, wrapped him in swaddling clothes, and laid him in a manger, because there was no room in the inn. Henry notes Christ came to sojourn as at an inn, and the manger checks our vanity, ambition, and envy. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: Shepherds were abiding in the field, keeping watch over their flock by night. Henry observes the angels were sent not to the great but to poor, humble, pious, industrious shepherds in the business of their calling. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: The angel of the Lord came upon them, and the glory of the Lord shone round about them, and they were sore afraid. Divine glory breaks into ordinary night work, and fear is the first honest response. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: Fear not: the angel brings good tidings of great joy, which shall be to all people. The message is joy before it is doctrine, and its scope is all peoples, not one class. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: Unto you is born this day in the city of David a Saviour, which is Christ the Lord. Henry notes it is still proclaimed in our ears that to us is born a Saviour, Christ the Lord, and these should be glad tidings to all. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: The sign is a babe wrapped in swaddling clothes, lying in a manger. The sign matches the message: the Saviour is found in lowliness, not in splendour. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: Suddenly a multitude of the heavenly host joined the angel, praising God. One herald becomes a choir, because the birth deserves heaven's own praise. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: Glory to God in the highest, and on earth peace, good will toward men. Henry explains that God's good-will in sending the Messiah redounds to his glory in the highest and brings peace, here put for all the good flowing from Christ taking our nature. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: When the angels departed, the shepherds said, let us go to Bethlehem and see this thing which the Lord has made known. Henry notes they lost no time, but came with haste; we are not out of the way of divine visits when employed in an honest calling. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: They came with haste and found Mary, Joseph, and the babe lying in a manger. What was told them matches exactly what they find, confirming the word. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: Having seen, they made known abroad the saying told them concerning this child. Henry notes they were satisfied, and spread word that he was the Saviour, even Christ the Lord. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: All who heard wondered at the things told them by the shepherds. Wonder is the right first response of hearers, even before full understanding. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: But Mary kept all these things and pondered them in her heart. Henry urges the same pondering: we should be more delivered from errors in judgment and practice if we more fully ponder these things in our hearts. — Matthew Henry

20 COMMENTARY: The shepherds returned, glorifying and praising God for all they had heard and seen, as it was told them. Their return to the flock is itself worship, because the word proved true. — Matthew Henry

21 COMMENTARY: At eight days the child was circumcised, and his name called Jesus, as the angel had named him before conception. Henry notes he did not need circumcision's mortification of a corrupt nature, but it pledged his future perfect obedience to the whole law, even unto death for us. — Matthew Henry

22 COMMENTARY: When the days of purification were accomplished, they brought him to Jerusalem to present him to the Lord. Mary goes up to offer the appointed sacrifices, and Joseph presents the firstborn son according to the law. — Matthew Henry

23 COMMENTARY: As written in the law, every male that opens the womb shall be called holy to the Lord. The presentation owns the child as the Lord's before he is ever the parents'. — Matthew Henry

24 COMMENTARY: They offered a pair of turtledoves or two young pigeons, according to the law. Henry's application is to present our children to the Lord who gave them, beseeching him to redeem them from sin and death. — Matthew Henry

25 COMMENTARY: Simeon in Jerusalem was just and devout, waiting for the consolation of Israel, and the Holy Ghost was upon him. Henry notes the same Spirit that supported Simeon's hope also provided for his joy. — Matthew Henry

26 COMMENTARY: It was revealed to Simeon by the Holy Ghost that he should not see death before he had seen the Lord's Christ. His long waiting had a promise attached to it, and the promise held him. — Matthew Henry

27 COMMENTARY: Simeon came by the Spirit into the temple, just as the parents brought the child to do for him after the custom of the law. Henry notes those who would see Christ must go to his temple. — Matthew Henry

28 COMMENTARY: Then Simeon took the child up in his arms and blessed God. The old man's arms hold the salvation he had waited for, and blessing is his first act. — Matthew Henry

29 COMMENTARY: Lord, now lettest thou thy servant depart in peace, according to thy word. Henry observes how poor this world looks to one with Christ in his arms, and how comfortable is the death of a good man who departs in peace with God and his conscience. — Matthew Henry

30 COMMENTARY: For mine eyes have seen thy salvation. Simeon confesses this child in his arms is the Saviour, the salvation itself, of God's appointing. — Matthew Henry

31 COMMENTARY: Which thou hast prepared before the face of all people. Salvation is prepared openly, not as a secret for one nation alone. — Matthew Henry

32 COMMENTARY: A light to lighten the Gentiles, and the glory of thy people Israel. The same child is light to outsiders and glory to Israel, holding both promises together. — Matthew Henry

33 COMMENTARY: Joseph and Mary marvelled at the things spoken of him. Even parents who knew the angels' words afresh marvel at what Simeon sees in their child. — Matthew Henry

34 COMMENTARY: Simeon blessed them and told Mary this child is set for the fall and rising again of many in Israel, and for a sign spoken against. Henry notes Jesus, his doctrine, and his people are still spoken against, and his preached word remains the touchstone of men's characters. — Matthew Henry

35 COMMENTARY: A sword shall pierce through Mary's own soul also, that the thoughts of many hearts may be revealed. Henry adds that Jesus shall be a suffering Saviour, and his mother shall suffer with him because of the nearness of her relation and affection. — Matthew Henry

36 COMMENTARY: Anna, a prophetess of the tribe of Aser, was of great age, having lived seven years with her husband from her virginity. She is introduced as a second aged witness, matching Simeon. — Matthew Henry

37 COMMENTARY: Widowed about fourscore and four years, Anna departed not from the temple, but served God with fastings and prayers night and day. Henry notes she was always in a praying spirit, and God left himself not without witness even when much evil was in the church. — Matthew Henry

38 COMMENTARY: Coming in that instant, Anna gave thanks to the Lord and spoke of him to all who looked for redemption in Jerusalem. Henry's lesson is that those to whom Christ is made known have great reason to thank the Lord and to teach others concerning him. — Matthew Henry

39 COMMENTARY: When they had performed all things according to the law, they returned to Galilee, to Nazareth. Obedience to the law frames the whole infancy: nothing is skipped, even by the law's own Lord. — Matthew Henry

40 COMMENTARY: The child grew, waxed strong in spirit, filled with wisdom, and the grace of God was upon him. Henry notes he passed through infancy and childhood as other children, yet without sin, and with manifest proofs of the divine nature, everything said and done wisely, above his years. — Matthew Henry

41 COMMENTARY: His parents went to Jerusalem every year at the feast of the passover. Yearly worship was the family's habit, and the boy is raised inside it. — Matthew Henry

42 COMMENTARY: When he was twelve years old, they went up after the custom of the feast. Henry notes it is for the honour of Christ that children attend public worship, and his parents stayed all seven days of the feast. — Matthew Henry

43 COMMENTARY: When the days were fulfilled, as they returned, the child Jesus tarried behind in Jerusalem, unknown to Joseph and Mary. The loss is real, and it happens at the end of an ordinance faithfully kept. — Matthew Henry

44 COMMENTARY: Supposing him in the company, they went a day's journey and sought him among kinsfolk and acquaintance. Henry's counsel applies: those who have lost their comforts in Christ must bethink themselves where, when, and how they lost them. — Matthew Henry

45 COMMENTARY: Not finding him, they turned back to Jerusalem, seeking him. Recovery requires returning, not pressing on and hoping he will catch up. — Matthew Henry

46 COMMENTARY: After three days they found him in the temple, sitting among the doctors, hearing them and asking questions. Henry notes they found him where the doctors kept their schools, hearkening, proposing questions, and answering with wisdom that delighted his hearers. — Matthew Henry

47 COMMENTARY: All who heard him were astonished at his understanding and answers. The astonishment is at a twelve-year-old's grasp, not at display: he listens and asks as well as answers. — Matthew Henry

48 COMMENTARY: When they saw him they were amazed, and his mother asked why he had thus dealt with them, since she and Joseph had sought him sorrowing. Henry notes those who seek Christ in sorrow shall find him with the greater joy. — Matthew Henry

49 COMMENTARY: How is it that ye sought me? Wist ye not that I must be about my Father's business? Henry quotes the saying in full: I ought to be in my Father's house, at my Father's work, and children of God must make all other concerns give way to it. — Matthew Henry

50 COMMENTARY: They understood not the saying he spoke to them. Henry adds that we must not think God's sayings obscure merely because at first they are dark; what is dark may afterwards become plain. — Matthew Henry

51 COMMENTARY: He went down with them to Nazareth and was subject to them, but his mother kept all these sayings in her heart. Henry draws the example of true greatness: to know one's own place and office, even for the Son of God, who obeyed earthly parents. — Matthew Henry

52 COMMENTARY: Jesus increased in wisdom and stature, and in favour with God and man. The hidden years end as they began: real human growth, in wisdom and favour alike, under God's grace. — Matthew Henry
