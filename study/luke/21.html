Study Notes — Luke

Chapter 21

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: He looked up, and saw the rich men casting their gifts into the treasury. The treasury scene is watched, not just passed. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: He saw also a certain poor widow casting in thither two mites. Two mites, the smallest gift, are seen alongside rich gifts. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: Of a truth, this poor widow has cast in more than they all. Henry notes what we rightly give for the poor's relief and God's worship is given unto God, and the Saviour sees with pleasure whatever we have in our hearts to give. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: For all these have of their abundance cast in unto God's offerings, but she of her penury has cast in all the living that she had. Henry prays the poorest servants, who have two mites, a soul and a body, may offer both unto him. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: As some spoke of the temple, how it was adorned with goodly stones and gifts, he said. Admiration of the temple's adornment prompts its doom. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: As for these things which ye behold, the days will come in which there shall not be left one stone upon another, that shall not be thrown down. Goodly stones will be thrown down stone by stone. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: They asked, Master, but when shall these things be? And what sign will there be when these things shall come to pass? Henry notes curiosity about timing, and Christ answers with clearness as far as necessary to teach duty, for all knowledge is desirable as far as it is in order to practice. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: Take heed that ye be not deceived, for many shall come in my name, saying, I am Christ, and the time draws near: go ye not therefore after them. Deception, not dating, is the first danger. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: When ye shall hear of wars and commotions, be not terrified, for these things must first come to pass, but the end is not by and by. Wars are necessary first things, not the end itself. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: Nation shall rise against nation, and kingdom against kingdom. The prophecy widens from Jerusalem to nations. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: Great earthquakes shall be in divers places, and famines, and pestilences, and fearful sights and great signs shall there be from heaven. Earth, hunger, disease, and heaven all give signs. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: But before all these, they shall lay hands on you, and persecute you, delivering you to synagogues and prisons, being brought before kings and rulers for my name's sake. Henry notes Christ tells what hard things they should suffer for his name, encouraging them to bear up and go on in their work despite opposition. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: And it shall turn to you for a testimony. Persecution becomes testimony, not merely suffering. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: Settle it therefore in your hearts, not to meditate before what ye shall answer. Defence is settled beforehand by not meditating beforehand. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: For I will give you a mouth and wisdom, which all your adversaries shall not be able to gainsay nor resist. Henry notes God will stand by, own, and assist them, remarkably fulfilled after the Spirit's pouring out, giving wisdom and utterance. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: Ye shall be betrayed both by parents, brethren, kinsfolks, and friends, and some of you shall they cause to be put to death. Betrayal comes from the nearest bonds, and some betrayals end in death. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: Ye shall be hated of all men for my name's sake. Hatred is universalised, and its cause is his name. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: But there shall not a hair of your head perish. Henry notes though we may be losers for Christ, we shall not, we cannot be losers by him in the end. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: In your patience possess ye your souls. Henry notes it is by Christian patience we keep possession of our souls, keeping out impressions that would put us out of temper, and securing our souls' safety in perilous times. — Matthew Henry

20 COMMENTARY: When ye shall see Jerusalem compassed with armies, then know that the desolation thereof is nigh. Armies around Jerusalem are the sign of desolation near, not far. — Matthew Henry

21 COMMENTARY: Then let them in Judaea flee to the mountains, and let them in the midst depart out, and let not them in the countries enter thereinto. Flight, departure, and non-entry are the three commands for the siege. — Matthew Henry

22 COMMENTARY: For these be the days of vengeance, that all things written may be fulfilled. Vengeance days fulfil what was written. — Matthew Henry

23 COMMENTARY: But woe unto them with child, and to them that give suck, in those days! For there shall be great distress in the land, and wrath upon this people. Mothers and infants bear the siege's woe most sharply. — Matthew Henry

24 COMMENTARY: They shall fall by the edge of the sword, and be led captive into all nations, and Jerusalem shall be trodden down of the Gentiles, until the times of the Gentiles be fulfilled. Henry notes the destruction of Jerusalem and dispersion of the Jews would be a type of Christ's second coming, and the city is set as an example that sins will not pass unpunished. — Matthew Henry

25 COMMENTARY: There shall be signs in sun, moon, and stars, and upon earth distress of nations, with perplexity, the sea and waves roaring. Cosmic signs join earthly distress and perplexity. — Matthew Henry

26 COMMENTARY: Men's hearts failing them for fear, and for looking after those things coming on the earth, for the powers of heaven shall be shaken. Fear fails hearts before the events themselves arrive. — Matthew Henry

27 COMMENTARY: And then shall they see the Son of man coming in a cloud with power and great glory. The shaken powers give way to the Son of man coming in cloud, power, and glory. — Matthew Henry

28 COMMENTARY: When these things begin to come to pass, then look up, and lift up your heads, for your redemption draws nigh. Henry notes when Christ came to destroy the Jews, he came to redeem persecuted Christians, and when he comes to judge, he will redeem all his from troubles; so beginning terrors are a signal to lift heads, not bow them. — Matthew Henry

29 COMMENTARY: He spoke a parable: Behold the fig tree, and all the trees. The fig tree is joined by all trees as the sign's picture. — Matthew Henry

30 COMMENTARY: When they now shoot forth, ye see and know of yourselves that summer is now nigh at hand. Shooting forth is self-evident summer, needing no interpreter. — Matthew Henry

31 COMMENTARY: So likewise ye, when ye see these things come to pass, know ye that the kingdom of God is nigh at hand. Signs seen are kingdom nearness known. — Matthew Henry

32 COMMENTARY: Verily, this generation shall not pass away, till all be fulfilled. The generation hearing will live to see fulfilment. — Matthew Henry

33 COMMENTARY: Heaven and earth shall pass away, but my words shall not pass away. Henry notes though heaven and earth pass, Jesus' words shall not pass away, and scattered Jews preach Christianity's truth. — Matthew Henry

34 COMMENTARY: Take heed to yourselves, lest at any time your hearts be overcharged with surfeiting, drunkenness, and cares of this life, and so that day come upon you unawares. Henry cautions against being secure and sensual, overpowered by temptations or betrayed by corruptions, lest death and judgment come unprepared. — Matthew Henry

35 COMMENTARY: For as a snare shall it come on all them that dwell on the face of the whole earth. The day comes as a snare on all earth-dwellers, not on a few. — Matthew Henry

36 COMMENTARY: Watch ye therefore, and pray always, that ye may be accounted worthy to escape all these things that shall come to pass, and to stand before the Son of man. Henry urges watching against sin, in every duty, making most of every opportunity to do good, and praying always, for those who live a life of prayer here shall be accounted worthy to live a life of praise hereafter. — Matthew Henry

37 COMMENTARY: In the daytime he was teaching in the temple, and at night he went out, and abode in the mount called Olivet. Days in the temple, nights on Olivet: the rhythm before the passion. — Matthew Henry

38 COMMENTARY: All the people came early in the morning to him in the temple, for to hear him. Early crowds close the chapter, listening before the arrest. — Matthew Henry
