Study Notes — Luke

Chapter 17

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Then said he unto the disciples, It is impossible but that offences will come, but woe unto him through whom they come! Henry notes it is no abatement of guilt, nor lessening of punishment, that offences will come; inevitability does not excuse the offender. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: It were better for him that a millstone were hanged about his neck, and he cast into the sea, than that he should offend one of these little ones. Drowning with a millstone is better than causing a little one to stumble. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: Take heed to yourselves: If thy brother trespass against thee, rebuke him, and if he repent, forgive him. Rebuke and forgiveness are paired duties, not alternatives. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: If he trespass against thee seven times in a day, and seven times turn again saying, I repent, thou shalt forgive him. Henry notes faith in God's pardoning mercy enables us to get over the greatest difficulties in forgiving brethren, even sevenfold in a day. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: And the apostles said unto the Lord, Increase our faith. Forgiving seven times a day drives the apostles to pray for more faith, not for easier brethren. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: If ye had faith as a grain of mustard seed, ye might say unto this sycamine tree, Be thou plucked up by the root, and be thou planted in the sea, and it should obey you. Henry notes as with God nothing is impossible, so all things are possible to him that can believe, and small faith, truly placed, moves what is rooted. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: But which of you, having a servant plowing or feeding cattle, will say unto him when he comes from the field, Go and sit down to meat? The servant's field work does not earn immediate table service. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: And will not rather say, Make ready wherewith I may sup, and gird thyself, and serve me, till I have eaten and drunken, and afterward thou shalt eat and drink? Service continues after labour, without complaint. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: Does he thank that servant because he did the things commanded him? I trow not. Obedience commanded earns no special thanks. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: So likewise ye, when ye shall have done all commanded you, say, We are unprofitable servants: we have done that which was our duty to do. Henry notes the Lord has such a property in every creature as no man can have in another; he cannot be in debt for services, nor do they deserve return from him, so deep humility is the disciples' need. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: As he went to Jerusalem, he passed through the midst of Samaria and Galilee. The journey to Jerusalem passes through border country, where the next healing will find a Samaritan. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: As he entered a certain village, there met him ten men that were lepers, which stood afar off. Leprosy keeps them at a distance, even from the healer. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: They lifted up their voices, saying, Jesus, Master, have mercy on us. Henry notes a sense of spiritual leprosy should make us very humble when we draw near to Christ, and it is enough to refer ourselves to his compassions, for they fail not. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: When he saw them, he said, Go show yourselves unto the priests. And it came to pass, as they went, they were cleansed. Henry notes we may look for God to meet us with mercy when found in the way of obedience; cleansing comes on the way, not before it. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: One of them, when he saw he was healed, turned back, and with a loud voice glorified God. The one who turns back does so loudly, glorifying God, not quietly slipping away. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: And fell down on his face at his feet, giving him thanks, and he was a Samaritan. Henry notes only one returned to give thanks, and Christ noticed the one who distinguished himself: he was a Samaritan. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: Jesus answering said, Were there not ten cleansed? But where are the nine? The question counts the absent, not just the present. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: There are not found that returned to give glory to God, save this stranger. The stranger alone gives glory, while the nine keep only the outward cure. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: And he said unto him, Arise, go thy way: thy faith has made thee whole. Henry notes the others only got the outward cure, he alone got the spiritual blessing, and it becomes us to be very humble in thanksgivings as well as in prayers. — Matthew Henry

20 COMMENTARY: When demanded of the Pharisees when the kingdom of God should come, he answered, The kingdom of God comes not with observation. A date and a spectacle are refused as the kingdom's marks. — Matthew Henry

21 COMMENTARY: Neither shall they say, Lo here! or, lo there! For behold, the kingdom of God is within you. Henry notes the kingdom was among the Jews, or rather within some of them, a spiritual kingdom set up in the heart by divine grace. — Matthew Henry

22 COMMENTARY: And he said unto the disciples, The days will come, when ye shall desire to see one of the days of the Son of man, and ye shall not see it. Desire for his days will outrun their presence. — Matthew Henry

23 COMMENTARY: And they shall say to you, See here, or, see there: go not after them, nor follow them. False locations of the Son of man are to be ignored, not investigated. — Matthew Henry

24 COMMENTARY: For as lightning, lightening out of one part under heaven, shines unto the other part under heaven, so shall also the Son of man be in his day. Lightning, not a rumour, is the image of his coming: sudden, visible, universal. — Matthew Henry

25 COMMENTARY: But first must he suffer many things, and be rejected of this generation. Before revelation comes rejection, and suffering precedes glory. — Matthew Henry

26 COMMENTARY: And as it was in the days of Noah, so shall it be also in the days of the Son of man. Noah's days become the pattern for the Son of man's days. — Matthew Henry

27 COMMENTARY: They did eat, drank, married wives, were given in marriage, until the day Noah entered the ark, and the flood came and destroyed them all. Ordinary life continued until the flood, and Henry notes sinners of every age go on securely in evil ways, remembering not their latter end. — Matthew Henry

28 COMMENTARY: Likewise also as it was in the days of Lot: they did eat, drank, bought, sold, planted, builded. Lot's days add commerce and building to eating and marrying, all ordinary, all uninterrupted. — Matthew Henry

29 COMMENTARY: But the same day Lot went out of Sodom it rained fire and brimstone from heaven, and destroyed them all. Deliverance and destruction fall on the same day. — Matthew Henry

30 COMMENTARY: Even thus shall it be in the day when the Son of man is revealed. Henry notes what a dreadful surprise destruction will be to the secure and sensual, and thus shall it be when the Son of man is revealed. — Matthew Henry

31 COMMENTARY: In that day, he which shall be upon the housetop, and his stuff in the house, let him not come down to take it away, and he that is in the field, let him likewise not return back. Possessions must be abandoned without retrieval when the day comes. — Matthew Henry

32 COMMENTARY: Remember Lot's wife. Two words sum the warning: looking back is fatal. — Matthew Henry

33 COMMENTARY: Whosoever shall seek to save his life shall lose it, and whosoever shall lose his life shall preserve it. Saving and losing are again reversed. — Matthew Henry

34 COMMENTARY: I tell you, in that night there shall be two men in one bed; the one shall be taken, and the other shall be left. The closest companionship does not guarantee the same fate. — Matthew Henry

35 COMMENTARY: Two women shall be grinding together; the one shall be taken, and the other left. Shared labour, like shared bed, divides at the revelation. — Matthew Henry

36 COMMENTARY: Two men shall be in the field; the one shall be taken, and the other left. Field, mill, and bed: every ordinary scene becomes a scene of separation. — Matthew Henry

37 COMMENTARY: And they answered, Where, Lord? And he said, Wheresoever the body is, thither will the eagles be gathered together. Henry notes wherever the wicked are, marked for eternal ruin, they shall be found by God's judgments, as eagles find the body. — Matthew Henry
