Study Notes — Luke

Chapter 14

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: As he went into the house of one of the chief Pharisees to eat bread on the sabbath, they watched him. Henry notes this Pharisee, like others, seems to have had an ill design in entertaining Jesus, yet the Lord would not be hindered from healing, though a clamour would be raised at doing it on the sabbath. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: Behold, there was a certain man before him which had the dropsy. The sick man is placed before Jesus, in the watching house. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: Jesus answering spoke to lawyers and Pharisees, Is it lawful to heal on the sabbath day? The question is put before the healing, to the watchers themselves. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: They held their peace. And he took him, healed him, and let him go. Silence answers the question, and healing follows it. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: Which of you shall have an ass or ox fallen into a pit, and will not straightway pull him out on the sabbath day? Necessity for beasts argues necessity for a man. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: And they could not answer him again to these things. The pit argument leaves no reply. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: He put forth a parable to those bidden, when he marked how they chose out the chief rooms. Henry notes Christ marks what we do even in common actions, not only in religious assemblies, but at our tables. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: When thou art bidden to a wedding, sit not down in the highest room, lest a more honourable man than thou be bidden. Choosing the highest room risks public displacement. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: And he that bade thee and him come and say, Give this man place, and thou begin with shame to take the lowest room. The shame is the lesson: self-exaltation ends in demotion before witnesses. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: But when thou art bidden, go and sit in the lowest room, that when he that bade thee comes, he may say, Friend, go up higher; then shalt thou have worship in the presence of them that sit at meat with thee. Low seating leaves room for honour given, not seized. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: For whosoever exalts himself shall be abased, and he that humbles himself shall be exalted. Henry's rule is that pride brings low, and before honour is humility. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: Then said he to him that bade him, When thou makest a dinner or supper, call not friends, brethren, kinsmen, nor rich neighbours, lest they also bid thee again, and a recompense be made thee. Reciprocal hospitality is not charity. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: But when thou makest a feast, call the poor, the maimed, the lame, the blind. The guest list is inverted toward those who cannot repay. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: And thou shalt be blessed, for they cannot recompense thee, for thou shalt be recompensed at the resurrection of the just. Henry notes works of charity are better than works of show, and the precept of doing good to poor and afflicted should be observed from love to Christ, not for proud liberality's reward. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: When one that sat at meat heard these things, he said, Blessed is he that shall eat bread in the kingdom of God. The table talk turns to the kingdom's feast. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: Then said he, A certain man made a great supper, and bade many. The kingdom feast is pictured as a great supper with many bidden. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: And sent his servant at supper time to say to them that were bidden, Come, for all things are now ready. The second call announces readiness, not mere invitation. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: They all with one consent began to make excuse. The first said, I have bought a piece of ground, and I must needs go and see it; I pray thee have me excused. Henry notes all found some pretence to put off attendance, reproving Israel's neglect of Christ's grace offers and our backwardness to close with the gospel call. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: Another said, I have bought five yoke of oxen, and I go to prove them; I pray thee have me excused. Business, newly bought, becomes the excuse. — Matthew Henry

20 COMMENTARY: Another said, I have married a wife, and therefore I cannot come. Marriage, itself good, is pleaded as impossibility. — Matthew Henry

21 COMMENTARY: So the servant showed his lord these things. Then the master, being angry, said, Go out quickly into streets and lanes of the city, and bring in hither the poor, maimed, halt, and blind. Anger at slighted grace turns the invitation to the city's streets. — Matthew Henry

22 COMMENTARY: And the servant said, Lord, it is done as thou hast commanded, and yet there is room. Obedience reports room remaining, and the house is not yet full. — Matthew Henry

23 COMMENTARY: And the lord said, Go out into highways and hedges, and compel them to come in, that my house may be filled. Henry notes the apostles were to turn to Gentiles when Jews refused, and with them the church was filled; the provision for precious souls has not been made in vain, for if some reject, others thankfully accept. — Matthew Henry

24 COMMENTARY: For I say unto you, that none of those men which were bidden shall taste of my supper. Refusal, once settled, excludes from tasting. — Matthew Henry

25 COMMENTARY: And there went great multitudes with him, and he turned and said unto them. Multitudes following prompt a hard word about discipleship. — Matthew Henry

26 COMMENTARY: If any man come to me, and hate not his father, mother, wife, children, brethren, sisters, yea, and his own life also, he cannot be my disciple. Henry notes disciples are not all crucified, yet all bear their cross in the way of duty, and must count upon it. — Matthew Henry

27 COMMENTARY: And whosoever does not bear his cross, and come after me, cannot be my disciple. Cross-bearing is the condition, not an optional extra. — Matthew Henry

28 COMMENTARY: For which of you, intending to build a tower, sits not down first and counts the cost, whether he have sufficient to finish it? Henry explains the first similitude: we must consider the expenses of religion. — Matthew Henry

29 COMMENTARY: Lest haply, after he has laid the foundation and is not able to finish it, all that behold it begin to mock him. An unfinished tower invites mockery. — Matthew Henry

30 COMMENTARY: Saying, This man began to build, and was not able to finish. The mockers' sentence is the warning itself. — Matthew Henry

31 COMMENTARY: Or what king, going to make war against another king, sits not down first and consults whether he be able with ten thousand to meet him that comes with twenty thousand? Henry explains the second similitude: we must consider the perils of religion. — Matthew Henry

32 COMMENTARY: Or else, while the other is yet a great way off, he sends an ambassage and desires conditions of peace. The outnumbered king seeks peace before battle, and Henry notes the proudest sinner cannot stand against God, so it is our interest to seek peace with him, whose conditions are offered to our advantage. — Matthew Henry

33 COMMENTARY: So likewise, whosoever he be of you that forsakes not all that he has, he cannot be my disciple. Counting cost ends in forsaking all, or not being a disciple. — Matthew Henry

34 COMMENTARY: Salt is good, but if the salt has lost his savour, wherewith shall it be seasoned? Salt without savour has lost its use. — Matthew Henry

35 COMMENTARY: It is neither fit for the land, nor yet for the dunghill, but men cast it out. He that has ears to hear, let him hear. Henry urges seeking to be disciples indeed, careful not to grow slack or afraid of the cross, that we may be good salt seasoning those around us with Christ's savour. — Matthew Henry
