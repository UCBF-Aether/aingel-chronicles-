Study Notes — Luke

Chapter 3

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: In the fifteenth year of Tiberius, with Pilate governing Judaea, Herod tetrarch of Galilee, Philip of Ituraea and Trachonitis, and Lysanias of Abilene, the scene is dated by rulers. John's ministry begins inside real imperial and local history, not outside it. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: Annas and Caiaphas being high priests, the word of God came to John, son of Zacharias, in the wilderness. The word bypasses the temple establishment and comes to a prophet in the desert. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: John came into all the country about Jordan, preaching the baptism of repentance for the remission of sins. Henry stresses the scope of his ministry: to bring people from their sins to their Saviour, with water baptism as the outward sign of inward cleansing and renewal. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: As written in Isaiah, the voice crying in the wilderness says, prepare the way of the Lord, make his paths straight. Henry sees Scripture fulfilled in John's ministry: way is made for the gospel by levelling high thoughts into obedience to Christ. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: Every valley shall be filled, every mountain and hill brought low, the crooked made straight, the rough ways smooth. The levelling is inward: whatever hinders Christ and his grace in the soul must be removed. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: And all flesh shall see the salvation of God. The preparation ends in a universal sight: salvation is not for one party but shown to all flesh. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: To the multitude coming to be baptized, John says, O generation of vipers, who warned you to flee from the wrath to come? Henry calls the guilty, corrupted race a generation of vipers, hateful to God and hating one another, with no flight from wrath but by repentance. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: Bring forth fruits worthy of repentance, and do not say, we have Abraham to our father, for God can raise up children to Abraham from stones. Henry warns that profession and relation to God's church stand us in no stead without real holiness in heart and life. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: The axe is laid to the root of the trees: every tree not bringing forth good fruit is hewn down and cast into the fire. Fruit, not foliage, is the test John sets, and judgment follows barrenness. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: The people asked, what shall we do then? John's warnings produce the right question: not debate about doctrine, but what repentance looks like in practice. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: He who has two coats, let him impart to him who has none; and he who has meat, let him do likewise. Henry notes the gospel requires mercy, not sacrifice, and repentance must show itself by reformation according to each person's place and condition. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: Publicans also came to be baptized, asking, Master, what shall we do? Even tax collectors, despised as collaborators, are welcomed to the same question and the same repentance. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: Exact no more than that which is appointed you. John's instruction to publicans is exact justice: forego unjust gain, and the same principle leads to restoring what was gained by wrong. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: Soldiers asked what they should do, and John answered: do violence to no man, accuse none falsely, and be content with wages. Henry notes men must be cautioned against the temptations of their own employments, and these answers form a test of sincerity. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: The people were in expectation, musing whether John were the Christ. Expectation is high because John's preaching has awakened Israel to look for the promised one. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: John answered all: I baptize with water, but one mightier than I comes, whose shoe latchet I am not worthy to loose; he shall baptize with the Holy Ghost and with fire. Henry stresses John could only exhort and assure forgiveness on repentance, but Christ can give the Spirit to cleanse within, as fire clears dross and casts metal anew. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: His fan is in his hand; he will thoroughly purge his floor, gather the wheat into his garner, and burn the chaff with unquenchable fire. The mightier one's baptism is also a separation, and John does not soften it. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: With many other things John exhorted and preached to the people. Henry describes him as affectionate, practical, popular, and evangelical: in all exhortations directing people to Christ, for righteousness and strength, and shunning not the whole counsel of God. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: Herod the tetrarch, reproved by John for Herodias, his brother Philip's wife, and for all his evils, heard the same plain preaching in his own court. John's boldness did not stop at the crowds. — Matthew Henry

20 COMMENTARY: Herod added above all that he shut up John in prison. Henry notes a full stop was put to John's preaching in the midst of his usefulness, and those who injure God's faithful servants add greater guilt to their other sins. — Matthew Henry

21 COMMENTARY: When all the people were baptized, Jesus also being baptized and praying, the heaven was opened. Henry notes Christ had no sin to confess, but he prayed as others did, keeping communion with his Father, and the witness from heaven came while he was praying. — Matthew Henry

22 COMMENTARY: The Holy Ghost descended in bodily shape like a dove upon him, and a voice from heaven said, Thou art my beloved Son; in thee I am well pleased. Henry sees here a proof of the Holy Trinity, the Three Persons in the Godhead, given at Christ's baptism. — Matthew Henry

23 COMMENTARY: Jesus began to be about thirty years of age, being, as was supposed, the son of Joseph, who was the son of Heli. Henry notes Luke traces the line beginning with Heli, the father not of Joseph but of Mary, showing Jesus as the Seed of the woman. — Matthew Henry

24 COMMENTARY: Heli's line continues through Matthat, Levi, Melchi, Janna, and Joseph. Each link carries the line one step further from Jesus toward its ancient root. — Matthew Henry

25 COMMENTARY: The line continues through Mattathias, Amos, Naum, Esli, and Nagge. Luke's ascent passes through names otherwise little known, because the line itself, not each man's fame, is the point. — Matthew Henry

26 COMMENTARY: The line continues through Maath, Mattathias, Semei, Joseph, and Juda. The chain is unbroken as it climbs toward the royal house. — Matthew Henry

27 COMMENTARY: The line continues through Joanna, Rhesa, Zorobabel, Salathiel, and Neri. Zorobabel stands in the line at the return from exile, tying Jesus to the post-exilic hope. — Matthew Henry

28 COMMENTARY: The line continues through Melchi, Addi, Cosam, Elmodam, and Er. The ascent moves steadily backward through generations that bridge exile and kingdom. — Matthew Henry

29 COMMENTARY: The line continues through Jose, Eliezer, Jorim, Matthat, and Levi. Each named father is another step in Luke's backward trace toward David. — Matthew Henry

30 COMMENTARY: The line continues through Simeon, Juda, Joseph, Jonan, and Eliakim. The repeated familiar names show an ordinary human lineage, not a list of only the famous. — Matthew Henry

31 COMMENTARY: The line continues through Melea, Menan, Mattatha, and Nathan, who was the son of David. Henry's contrast with Matthew matters here: Luke follows Nathan's branch, yet still arrives at David, heir to the throne and promise. — Matthew Henry

32 COMMENTARY: David was the son of Jesse, of Obed, of Booz, of Salmon, of Naasson. The royal line is now traced into the Bethlehem household of Ruth's story. — Matthew Henry

33 COMMENTARY: The line continues through Aminadab, Aram, Esrom, Phares, and Juda. From David the ascent passes back through Judah, the tribe of the promised ruler. — Matthew Henry

34 COMMENTARY: The line continues through Jacob, Isaac, Abraham, Thara, and Nachor. With Abraham the line reaches the father of the promise, in whom all families of the earth are blessed. — Matthew Henry

35 COMMENTARY: The line continues through Saruch, Ragau, Phalec, Heber, and Sala. Beyond Abraham the trace runs into the generations after the flood. — Matthew Henry

36 COMMENTARY: The line continues through Cainan, Arphaxad, Sem, Noe, and Lamech. Sem and Noe carry the line through the flood itself to the new beginning of mankind. — Matthew Henry

37 COMMENTARY: The line continues through Mathusala, Enoch, Jared, Maleleel, and Cainan. The antediluvian names take the line back toward the first man. — Matthew Henry

38 COMMENTARY: The line ends: Enos, Seth, Adam, who was the son of God. Henry's conclusion is the point of the whole list: Christ was both the son of Adam and the Son of God, a proper Mediator between God and the sons of Adam, bringing them to be sons of God. — Matthew Henry
