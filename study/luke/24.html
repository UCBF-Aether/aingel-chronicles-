Study Notes — Luke

Chapter 24

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Now upon the first day of the week, very early in the morning, they came unto the sepulchre, bringing the spices which they had prepared, and certain others with them. Henry notes the affection and respect the women showed to Christ after he was dead and buried, coming very early with prepared spices. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: They found the stone rolled away from the sepulchre. The stone they expected to move is already rolled away. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: They entered in, and found not the body of the Lord Jesus. Entering, they find absence, not the body. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: As they were much perplexed thereabout, behold, two men stood by them in shining garments. Perplexity meets two men in shining garments. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: As they were afraid, and bowed down their faces to the earth, they said unto them, Why seek ye the living among the dead? Henry notes Christians often perplex themselves about what should comfort them, looking rather to find their Master in grave-clothes than angels in shining garments. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: He is not here, but is risen: remember how he spoke unto you when he was yet in Galilee. The angels bring no new gospel, Henry notes, but remind the women of Christ's words and teach them to apply them; he is risen by his own power. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: Saying, The Son of man must be delivered into the hands of sinful men, and be crucified, and the third day rise again. The remembered words name delivery, crucifixion, and third-day rising as musts. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: And they remembered his words. Remembering is the turn from perplexity to report. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: And returned from the sepulchre, and told all these things unto the eleven, and to all the rest. Return becomes report to the eleven and all the rest. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: It was Mary Magdalene and Joanna, and Mary the mother of James, and other women with them, which told these things unto the apostles. The witnesses are named: Magdalene, Joanna, Mary mother of James, and others. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: Their words seemed to them as idle tales, and they believed them not. The apostles hear resurrection as idle tales and do not believe. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: Then arose Peter, and ran unto the sepulchre, and stooping down, he beheld the linen clothes laid by themselves, and departed, wondering in himself at that which was come to pass. Henry notes Peter, who so lately ran from his Master, now ran to the sepulchre, and was amazed; many puzzling things would be plain if we rightly understood Christ's words. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: Behold, two of them went that same day to a village called Emmaus, which was from Jerusalem about threescore furlongs. The same day, two disciples walk to Emmaus, threescore furlongs from Jerusalem. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: They talked together of all these things which had happened. Talking together of death and resurrection is their employment on the road. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: While they communed together and reasoned, Jesus himself drew near, and went with them. Henry notes it well becomes disciples to talk of his death and resurrection, improving one another's knowledge and stirring devout affections, and where but two are well employed thus, he will come and make a third. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: But their eyes were holden that they should not know him. Eyes holden, not absent, keep recognition back. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: And he said unto them, What manner of communications are these that ye have one to another, as ye walk, and are sad? The stranger's question names their sadness. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: The one of them, whose name was Cleopas, answering said, Art thou only a stranger in Jerusalem, and hast not known the things which are come to pass there in these days? Henry notes those are strangers in Jerusalem who know not of Jesus' death and sufferings, and Cleopas treats the stranger as the only ignorant one. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: And he said, What things? And they said, Concerning Jesus of Nazareth, which was a prophet mighty in deed and word before God and all the people. The summary names a prophet mighty in deed and word. — Matthew Henry

20 COMMENTARY: And how the chief priests and our rulers delivered him to be condemned to death, and have crucified him. Delivery, condemnation, and crucifixion by rulers complete the summary's tragedy. — Matthew Henry

21 COMMENTARY: But we trusted that it had been he which should have redeemed Israel, and beside all this, today is the third day since these things were done. Trust in past tense and the third day: hope deferred by the calendar. — Matthew Henry

22 COMMENTARY: Yea, and certain women also of our company made us astonished, which were early at the sepulchre. The women's early visit astonished the company, without convincing it. — Matthew Henry

23 COMMENTARY: When they found not his body, they came, saying, that they had also seen a vision of angels, which said that he was alive. Empty tomb plus angels' vision, reported secondhand, still leaves him unseen. — Matthew Henry

24 COMMENTARY: Certain of them which were with us went to the sepulchre, and found it even so as the women had said, but him they saw not. The men's visit confirms the women's words and repeats the absence: him they saw not. — Matthew Henry

25 COMMENTARY: Then he said unto them, O fools, and slow of heart to believe all that the prophets have spoken. Henry notes the Lord reproved them for weakness of faith in Old Testament Scriptures. — Matthew Henry

26 COMMENTARY: Ought not Christ to have suffered these things, and to enter into his glory? Suffering as the appointed way to glory is the question that reframes everything. — Matthew Henry

27 COMMENTARY: Beginning at Moses and all the prophets, he expounded unto them in all the scriptures the things concerning himself. Henry notes Christ, the best expositor, led them not by new notions but by showing Scripture fulfilled; a golden thread of gospel grace runs through the whole Old Testament web, and we cannot go far in any part without meeting something concerning Christ. — Matthew Henry

28 COMMENTARY: They drew nigh unto the village, whither they went, and he made as though he would have gone further. The stranger makes as though to go further, testing whether they will constrain him. — Matthew Henry

29 COMMENTARY: But they constrained him, saying, Abide with us, for it is toward evening, and the day is far spent. And he went in to tarry with them. Henry notes if we would have Christ dwell with us, we must be earnest with him, and those who have tasted communion cannot but desire more of his company. — Matthew Henry

30 COMMENTARY: As he sat at meat with them, he took bread, and blessed it, and brake, and gave to them. The familiar manner — took, blessed, broke, gave — precedes recognition. — Matthew Henry

31 COMMENTARY: Their eyes were opened, and they knew him, and he vanished out of their sight. Henry notes Christ by Spirit and grace makes himself known, opening Scriptures and meeting at table, known in breaking of bread, but the work is completed by opening eyes of mind, and our views here are short, for in heaven we shall see him for ever. — Matthew Henry

32 COMMENTARY: And they said one to another, Did not our heart burn within us, while he talked with us by the way, and while he opened to us the scriptures? Henry notes they had found the preaching powerful even when they knew not the preacher, and Scriptures speaking of Christ will warm true disciples' hearts. — Matthew Henry

33 COMMENTARY: They rose up the same hour, and returned to Jerusalem, and found the eleven gathered together, and them that were with them. The same hour's return reverses the day's walk from Jerusalem. — Matthew Henry

34 COMMENTARY: Saying, The Lord is risen indeed, and has appeared to Simon. The gathered company already confesses: risen indeed, appeared to Simon. — Matthew Henry

35 COMMENTARY: And they told what things were done in the way, and how he was known of them in breaking of bread. Henry notes it is the duty of those to whom he has shown himself to let others know what he has done, and of great use to compare experiences and tell them to each other. — Matthew Henry

36 COMMENTARY: As they thus spoke, Jesus himself stood in the midst of them, and says unto them, Peace be unto you. Henry notes Jesus appeared miraculously, assuring disciples of his peace, though they had so lately forsaken him, promising spiritual peace with every blessing. — Matthew Henry

37 COMMENTARY: But they were terrified and affrighted, and supposed that they had seen a spirit. Presence produces terror: they suppose a spirit. — Matthew Henry

38 COMMENTARY: And he said, Why are ye troubled? And why do thoughts arise in your hearts? Henry notes many troublesome thoughts disquieting minds rise from mistakes concerning Christ, and all thoughts rising in hearts are known to the Lord and displeasing to him. — Matthew Henry

39 COMMENTARY: Behold my hands and my feet, that it is I myself: handle me, and see, for a spirit has not flesh and bones, as ye see me have. Hands, feet, handling, seeing: flesh and bones answer the spirit theory. — Matthew Henry

40 COMMENTARY: When he had thus spoken, he showed them his hands and his feet. Showing follows saying, wounds as evidence. — Matthew Henry

41 COMMENTARY: While they yet believed not for joy, and wondered, he said, Have ye here any meat? Unbelief for joy is answered with a request for food. — Matthew Henry

42 COMMENTARY: They gave him a piece of a broiled fish, and of a honeycomb. Fish and honeycomb are given, ordinary food for an extraordinary proof. — Matthew Henry

43 COMMENTARY: He took it, and did eat before them. Eating before them completes the bodily proof. — Matthew Henry

44 COMMENTARY: And he said, These are the words which I spoke unto you, while I was yet with you, that all things must be fulfilled, which were written in the law of Moses, and in the prophets, and in the psalms, concerning me. Henry notes nothing had passed but what was foretold by prophets and necessary for sinners' salvation, and even good men need understandings opened. — Matthew Henry

45 COMMENTARY: Then opened he their understanding, that they might understand the scriptures. Opening understanding, not merely opening the book, is the gift. — Matthew Henry

46 COMMENTARY: And said, Thus it is written, and thus it behoved Christ to suffer, and to rise from the dead the third day. Written necessity joins suffering and third-day rising. — Matthew Henry

47 COMMENTARY: And that repentance and remission of sins should be preached in his name among all nations, beginning at Jerusalem. Henry notes all men should be taught repentance's nature and necessity, in order to forgiveness, sought by faith in Jesus' name. — Matthew Henry

48 COMMENTARY: And ye are witnesses of these things. The opened, fed, and taught disciples are now witnesses. — Matthew Henry

49 COMMENTARY: And behold, I send the promise of my Father upon you, but tarry ye in the city of Jerusalem, until ye be endued with power from on high. Tarrying in Jerusalem precedes power from on high. — Matthew Henry

50 COMMENTARY: He led them out as far as to Bethany, and he lifted up his hands, and blessed them. Henry notes Christ ascended from Bethany, near Olivet, where his sufferings began; those who would go to heaven must ascend from the house of sufferings. — Matthew Henry

51 COMMENTARY: While he blessed them, he was parted from them, and carried up into heaven. Blessing and parting coincide: he goes away in blessing, not displeasure. — Matthew Henry

52 COMMENTARY: They worshipped him, and returned to Jerusalem with great joy. Henry notes this fresh display of glory drew fresh acknowledgments, and they returned with great joy, for Christ's glory is the joy of true believers even here. — Matthew Henry

53 COMMENTARY: And were continually in the temple, praising and blessing God. Amen. Henry closes: while waiting for God's promises, we must meet them with praises, for nothing better prepares the mind for the Holy Ghost; fears silenced, sorrows sweetened, hopes kept up, and the Father's throne is the throne of grace because it is also our Mediator's throne. — Matthew Henry
