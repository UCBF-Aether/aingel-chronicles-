Study Notes — Luke

Chapter 8

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Afterward he went through every city and village, preaching and showing the glad tidings of the kingdom of God, and the twelve were with him. Henry notes teaching the gospel was the constant business of his life. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: Certain women healed of evil spirits and infirmities attended him, Mary called Magdalene, out of whom went seven devils. Healing becomes following: the healed become his company. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: Joanna wife of Chuza, Herod's steward, and Susanna, and many others, ministered to him of their substance. Henry notes it showed his mean condition that he needed their kindness, and his great humility that he accepted it, though rich, yet for our sakes becoming poor. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: When much people gathered from every city, he spoke by a parable. The parable will teach how hearing itself is tested. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: A sower went out to sow his seed; as he sowed, some fell by the wayside, was trodden down, and fowls devoured it. Seed on a path never enters the soil. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: Some fell on a rock, and as soon as it sprang up it withered, because it lacked moisture. Quick growth without root is the danger pictured. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: Some fell among thorns, and thorns sprang up with it and choked it. Growth is possible yet fruitless when rivals grow faster. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: Other fell on good ground, sprang up, and bore fruit a hundredfold; and he cried, he that has ears to hear, let him hear. Henry urges heed of things that hinder profiting by the word: careless hearing, prejudice against it, and losing after hearing what was gained. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: His disciples asked, what might this parable be? The question itself marks them as hearers who want understanding, not amusement. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: Unto you it is given to know the mysteries of the kingdom, but to others in parables, that seeing they might not see, and hearing they might not understand. Henry counts us happy and indebted to free grace if what is a parable to others, amusing only, is plain truth to us by which we are taught and governed. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: Now the parable is this: the seed is the word of God. Every soil in the story is a kind of hearing of that one seed. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: Those by the wayside hear, then the devil comes and takes away the word from their hearts, lest they believe and be saved. The theft aims at belief itself. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: They on the rock receive the word with joy, have no root, believe for a while, and in temptation fall away. Joy without root collapses under pressure. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: That among thorns are they who, having heard, go forth and are choked with cares, riches, and pleasures of this life, bringing no fruit to perfection. The choke is slow and ordinary: cares, riches, pleasures. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: That on good ground are they who, in an honest and good heart, having heard the word, keep it, and bring forth fruit with patience. Henry notes great encouragement to faithful hearers who prove themselves doers: Christ owns them as his relations. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: No man lighting a candle covers it with a vessel or puts it under a bed, but sets it on a candlestick, that entrants may see the light. Henry adds it is not enough not to hold truth in unrighteousness; we should hold forth the word of life and shine to all around. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: Nothing is secret that shall not be made manifest, nor hid that shall not be known and come abroad. Hidden things are hidden only for a time. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: Take heed therefore how ye hear: whosoever has, to him shall be given, and whosoever has not, from him shall be taken even what he seems to have. Henry notes gifts continue or not as we use them for God's glory and our brethren's good. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: His mother and brethren came, and could not come at him for the press. Family ties meet the crowd that now surrounds him. — Matthew Henry

20 COMMENTARY: It was told him, thy mother and brethren stand without, desiring to see thee. The message asks for family privilege at the door. — Matthew Henry

21 COMMENTARY: My mother and my brethren are these which hear the word of God, and do it. Kinship is redefined by hearing and doing, not by blood alone. — Matthew Henry

22 COMMENTARY: On a certain day he went into a ship with his disciples, saying, let us go over to the other side of the lake, and they launched forth. The crossing is at his word, before any storm is known. — Matthew Henry

23 COMMENTARY: As they sailed he fell asleep, and a storm of wind came down on the lake; they were filled with water and in jeopardy. Henry warns those who put to sea in a calm, even at Christ's word, must yet prepare for a storm and great peril in it. — Matthew Henry

24 COMMENTARY: They awoke him, saying, Master, master, we perish. He arose, rebuked wind and raging water, and they ceased, and there was a calm. Henry notes no relief for souls under guilt and fear of wrath but to go to Christ, call him Master, and say, I am undone if thou dost not help me. — Matthew Henry

25 COMMENTARY: Where is your faith? They, afraid, wondered, what manner of man is this, for he commands winds and water, and they obey him. Henry adds that when dangers are over, we should take shame for our fears and give Christ glory for deliverance. — Matthew Henry

26 COMMENTARY: They arrived at the country of the Gadarenes, over against Galilee. The calm crossing lands them in Gentile territory, where the next deliverance waits. — Matthew Henry

27 COMMENTARY: Out of the city met him a man who had devils long time, wore no clothes, abode in no house, but in the tombs. Henry notes the malignant spirits' enmity to man and all his comforts, and that those under the devil are furiously driven, while those under Christ are sweetly led with bands of love. — Matthew Henry

28 COMMENTARY: Seeing Jesus, he cried out, fell down before him, and said with a loud voice, what have I to do with thee, Jesus, thou Son of God most high? I beseech thee, torment me not. Even the possessed man confesses the Son of God most high. — Matthew Henry

29 COMMENTARY: For he had commanded the unclean spirit to come out. Often it had caught him; bound with chains and fetters, he broke the bands and was driven by the devil into the wilderness. Human chains cannot hold what only Christ's word can expel. — Matthew Henry

30 COMMENTARY: Jesus asked, what is thy name? And he said, Legion, because many devils were entered into him. Henry notes these malignant spirits are very numerous, and it is a miracle of mercy if those Satan possesses are not brought to destruction. — Matthew Henry

31 COMMENTARY: They besought him not to command them to go out into the deep. The spirits plead against the abyss, revealing their fear of judgment. — Matthew Henry

32 COMMENTARY: A herd of many swine fed on the mountain, and they besought him to suffer them to enter into them, and he suffered them. Permission is granted, and the consequence will show the spirits' nature. — Matthew Henry

33 COMMENTARY: The devils went out of the man into the swine, and the herd ran violently down a steep place into the lake and were choked. Deliverance for the man is destruction for the swine, and the trade is stark. — Matthew Henry

34 COMMENTARY: They that fed them saw what was done, fled, and told it in city and country. The herdsmen become unwilling evangelists of a loss. — Matthew Henry

35 COMMENTARY: People went out to see, and found the man, out of whom devils departed, sitting at Jesus' feet, clothed, and in his right mind, and they were afraid. The transformation is total: sitting, clothed, sane — and it frightens the onlookers. — Matthew Henry

36 COMMENTARY: They also which saw it told them by what means the possessed man was healed. Witnesses confirm the means: not chains, but Christ's word. — Matthew Henry

37 COMMENTARY: The whole multitude of the Gadarenes besought him to depart, for they were taken with great fear, and he went up into the ship and returned. Henry warns Christ will not stay with those who slight him, and may no more return to them while others wait glad to receive him. — Matthew Henry

38 COMMENTARY: The man out of whom devils departed besought him that he might be with him, but Jesus sent him away, saying, return to thine own house and show how great things God has done unto thee. Staying with Jesus is refused in favour of witness at home. — Matthew Henry

39 COMMENTARY: He went his way and published throughout the whole city how great things Jesus had done unto him. The sent man becomes the preacher to his own city. — Matthew Henry

40 COMMENTARY: When Jesus returned, the people gladly received him, for they were all waiting for him. On the other shore, waiting replaces the Gadarenes' fear. — Matthew Henry

41 COMMENTARY: Jairus, a ruler of the synagogue, fell at Jesus' feet and besought him to come into his house. A ruler publicly humbles himself for his child's sake. — Matthew Henry

42 COMMENTARY: He had one only daughter, about twelve years old, and she lay dying; but as he went, the people thronged him. Henry notes we should not complain of crowd and hurry while in the way of duty and doing good. — Matthew Henry

43 COMMENTARY: A woman having an issue of blood twelve years, who had spent all her living on physicians and could not be healed of any, was hidden in that crowd. Twelve years of dying daughter and twelve years of bleeding woman meet in one throng. — Matthew Henry

44 COMMENTARY: She came behind him, touched the border of his garment, and immediately her issue stanched. Henry notes many a poor soul is healed and saved by Christ hidden in a crowd, and nobody notices it. — Matthew Henry

45 COMMENTARY: Jesus said, who touched me? Peter answered, Master, the multitude throng and press thee, and sayest thou, who touched me? The crowd presses, but one touch differs from all pressing. — Matthew Henry

46 COMMENTARY: Somebody has touched me, for I perceive virtue is gone out of me. Power given is power felt, even in a throng. — Matthew Henry

47 COMMENTARY: Seeing she was not hid, the woman came trembling, fell down before him, and declared before all for what cause she touched him and how she was healed immediately. Henry notes this woman came trembling, yet her faith saved her; there may be trembling where yet there is saving faith. — Matthew Henry

48 COMMENTARY: Daughter, be of good comfort: thy faith has made thee whole; go in peace. Trembling faith is named, comforted, and sent in peace. — Matthew Henry

49 COMMENTARY: While he yet spoke, one came from the ruler's house, saying, thy daughter is dead; trouble not the Master. The interruption seems to end Jairus's hope at the worst moment. — Matthew Henry

50 COMMENTARY: When Jesus heard it, he answered, fear not, believe only, and she shall be made whole. Henry quotes his comfortable words to Jairus, and notes in perfect faith there is no fear; the more we fear, the less we believe. — Matthew Henry

51 COMMENTARY: Coming into the house, he suffered none to go in save Peter, James, John, and the father and mother of the maiden. The inner circle is small for the work of raising. — Matthew Henry

52 COMMENTARY: All wept and bewailed her, but he said, weep not, she is not dead, but sleeps. Death is renamed sleep by the one about to wake her. — Matthew Henry

53 COMMENTARY: They laughed him to scorn, knowing she was dead. Knowledge of death mocks the claim, and will be answered by the event. — Matthew Henry

54 COMMENTARY: He put them all out, took her by the hand, and called, Maid, arise. Henry notes the hand of Christ's grace goes with the calls of his word, to make them effectual. — Matthew Henry

55 COMMENTARY: Her spirit came again, she arose straightway, and he commanded to give her meat. Henry notes, as babes newborn, so those newly raised from sin desire spiritual food, that they may grow thereby. — Matthew Henry

56 COMMENTARY: Her parents were astonished, but he charged them to tell no man what was done. Astonishment is left without a publicity campaign; the family keeps the wonder. — Matthew Henry
