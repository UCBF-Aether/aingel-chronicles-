Study Notes — Luke

Chapter 4

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Full of the Holy Ghost, Jesus returned from Jordan and was led by the Spirit into the wilderness. Henry notes the wilderness gave the tempter an advantage of solitude, yet Jesus, made like his brethren, lived in dependence on divine providence and promise. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: Forty days tempted of the devil, eating nothing, he afterward hungered. The long fast is the ground of the first temptation: bodily need meets a spiritual test. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: If thou be the Son of God, command this stone to be made bread. The tempter attacks sonship through appetite, urging Jesus to provide for himself apart from the Father's way. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: It is written, man shall not live by bread alone, but by every word of God. Henry's rule follows: the word of God is our sword, and faith in that word our shield, and God has many ways of providing for his people in the way of duty. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: The devil showed him all the kingdoms of the world in a moment of time. The second temptation is spectacle and speed: all glory offered at once, without the cross. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: All this power and glory will I give thee, for it is delivered unto me, and to whomsoever I will I give it. Henry warns that Satan's promises are deceitful, and he uses worldly glory as bait to ensnare men to destruction. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: If thou wilt worship me, all shall be thine. The price is named plainly: worship for kingdoms, a bargain Henry says we must reject at once and with abhorrence, seeking riches and honour in God's worship and service only. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: Get thee behind me, Satan: it is written, thou shalt worship the Lord thy God, and him only shalt thou serve. Christ will not worship Satan, nor, when the kingdoms are delivered to him by his Father, suffer any remains of devil-worship in them. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: Set on the pinnacle of the temple, Jesus is told, if thou be the Son of God, cast thyself down. The third temptation is presumption dressed as trust, urging him to force God's hand in the holy place itself. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: For it is written, he shall give his angels charge over thee, to keep thee. Satan now abuses Scripture, quoting promise without its condition, and Henry warns that such abuse must not abate our esteem for Scripture or its use. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: And in their hands they shall bear thee up, lest thou dash thy foot against a stone. The quoted promise is real, but it is a promise for the path of duty, not a warrant for a self-chosen leap. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: It is said, thou shalt not tempt the Lord thy God. Jesus answers Scripture with Scripture, refusing an unfitting confidence in his Father's protection such as he had no warrant for. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: When the devil had ended all the temptation, he departed for a season. Henry notes Christ let him try all his force and defeated him, for Satan found nothing in Christ for his darts to fasten on; yet he departed only till the season when he would return as persecutor, not tempter. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: Jesus returned in the power of the Spirit into Galilee, and fame of him went through all the region. Temptation resisted becomes ministry empowered, and the report spreads. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: He taught in their synagogues, being glorified of all. Henry notes Christ taught in their places of public worship, where they met to read, expound, and apply the word, to pray and praise. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: He came to Nazareth, where he had been brought up, and as his custom was went into the synagogue on the sabbath and stood up to read. Customary worship, not novelty, is the frame of his public claim. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: The book of Isaiah was delivered to him, and he found the place where it was written. The text he opens is the prophet's own announcement of the anointed one. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: The Spirit of the Lord is upon me, because he has anointed me to preach the gospel to the poor, heal the brokenhearted, preach deliverance to captives, recovering of sight to the blind, and liberty to the bruised. Henry explains that by Christ sinners are loosed from guilt's bonds, and by his Spirit from corruption's bondage; by his word light comes to those in darkness, and by his grace sight to the blind. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: To preach the acceptable year of the Lord. Henry urges sinners to attend the Saviour's invitation now, while liberty is thus proclaimed. — Matthew Henry

20 COMMENTARY: He closed the book, gave it to the minister, and sat down, and all eyes in the synagogue were fastened on him. The pause after the reading is itself proclamation: all wait to hear what this text means. — Matthew Henry

21 COMMENTARY: This day is this scripture fulfilled in your ears. The prophecy is not distant history but present fact, spoken in their hearing. — Matthew Henry

22 COMMENTARY: All bare him witness and wondered at the gracious words from his mouth, yet said, is not this Joseph's son? Henry notes Christ's name was Wonderful, nowhere more than in the word of his grace, yet prejudice furnishes an objection against the humbling doctrine of the cross. — Matthew Henry

23 COMMENTARY: Ye will surely say, Physician, heal thyself: whatever we heard done in Capernaum, do also here in thy country. The demand is for wonders as proof, on their terms, in his hometown. — Matthew Henry

24 COMMENTARY: Verily, no prophet is accepted in his own country. Familiarity, not evidence, is the barrier Jesus names. — Matthew Henry

25 COMMENTARY: Many widows were in Israel in Elijah's days, when heaven was shut three years and six months and great famine covered the land. The example is chosen to sting: need was widespread in Israel. — Matthew Henry

26 COMMENTARY: Unto none of them was Elijah sent, save unto Sarepta in Sidon, to a widow woman. God's favour went outside Israel, to a Gentile widow, while Israel's widows went unvisited. — Matthew Henry

27 COMMENTARY: Many lepers were in Israel in Elisha's time, and none was cleansed saving Naaman the Syrian. Again the healing passed Israel by and rested on a foreigner. — Matthew Henry

28 COMMENTARY: All in the synagogue, hearing these things, were filled with wrath. Henry notes the doctrine of God's sovereignty, his right to do his will, provokes proud men, who are angry when others receive favours they neglect. — Matthew Henry

29 COMMENTARY: They rose up, thrust him out of the city, and led him to the brow of the hill to cast him down headlong. Wonder at gracious words has turned, within minutes, to murderous rage. — Matthew Henry

30 COMMENTARY: But he passing through the midst of them went his way. The rejection is real, but his hour is not yet, and he walks out untouched. — Matthew Henry

31 COMMENTARY: He came down to Capernaum and taught them on the sabbath days. Rejected at Nazareth, he carries the same teaching to Capernaum, and Henry notes his preaching much affected the people, with a working power on their consciences. — Matthew Henry

32 COMMENTARY: They were astonished at his doctrine, for his word was with power. Authority is felt before it is analysed: the word itself carries weight. — Matthew Henry

33 COMMENTARY: In the synagogue was a man with a spirit of an unclean devil, crying out with a loud voice. Unclean power confronts Jesus in the very place of worship. — Matthew Henry

34 COMMENTARY: Let us alone; what have we to do with thee, Jesus of Nazareth? Art thou come to destroy us? I know thee, the Holy One of God. The demon's confession is true and unwelcome: it knows him, and fears destruction. — Matthew Henry

35 COMMENTARY: Hold thy peace, and come out of him. The devil threw the man down in the midst, then came out and hurt him not. Henry notes these miracles show Christ as controller and conqueror of Satan, and a healer of diseases. — Matthew Henry

36 COMMENTARY: All were amazed, saying, what a word is this! For with authority and power he commands unclean spirits, and they come out. Teaching with power is now confirmed by command over spirits. — Matthew Henry

37 COMMENTARY: The fame of him went out into every place round about. Word of the synagogue deliverance spreads faster than any planned announcement. — Matthew Henry

38 COMMENTARY: He entered Simon's house, where Simon's wife's mother was taken with a great fever, and they besought him for her. Henry's application is to beseech Christ on behalf of those diseased in body or mind, and use our influence to bring sinners to him. — Matthew Henry

39 COMMENTARY: He stood over her, rebuked the fever, and it left her; immediately she arose and ministered to them. Henry notes that where Christ gives new life in recovery, it should be a new life spent more than ever in his service, to his glory. — Matthew Henry

40 COMMENTARY: At sunset all with sick of divers diseases brought them to him, and he laid hands on every one and healed them. No case is too varied or too many: each one receives his hands. — Matthew Henry

41 COMMENTARY: Devils came out of many, crying, thou art Christ the Son of God; he rebuked them and suffered them not to speak, for they knew he was Christ. Testimony from demons is silenced, because its source discredits the truth it speaks. — Matthew Henry

42 COMMENTARY: At day he departed to a desert place, and the people sought him and stayed him, that he should not depart. Henry notes a desert is no desert if we are with Christ there, and the people's clinging shows how deeply the healings had moved them. — Matthew Henry

43 COMMENTARY: I must preach the kingdom of God to other cities also, for therefore am I sent. Healing must not confine him: preaching the kingdom is the mission that sends him onward. — Matthew Henry

44 COMMENTARY: And he preached in the synagogues of Galilee. The mission widens from one town's synagogue to the synagogues of the whole region. — Matthew Henry
