Study Notes — Micah

Chapter 3

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: The heads of Jacob and princes of Israel are called to hear, for it is their business above all to know judgment. Henry notes how seldom wholesome truths reach the ears of those in high stations or authority. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

2 COMMENTARY: These rulers hate the good and love the evil, plucking the skin and flesh from the people they should protect. Men cannot expect to do ill and fare well; they will find done to them what they did to others. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

3 COMMENTARY: They eat the flesh of God's people, flaying their skin and chopping their bones as meat for the pot. Such cruelty to the people is the very behaviour that will be repaid in kind. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

4 COMMENTARY: Then they will cry to the Lord, but he will not hear them and will hide his face, as they behaved ill in their doings. Those who deceive others are preparing confusion for their own faces. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

5 COMMENTARY: The prophets who make God's people err cry "Peace" while they bite with their teeth, and prepare war against anyone who will not feed them. Their smooth words serve their appetites, not the truth. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

6 COMMENTARY: Therefore night will come on these prophets: no vision, no divining, the sun gone down over them and the day dark. The light they abused is taken away. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

7 COMMENTARY: The seers will be ashamed and the diviners confounded, all covering their lips, for there is no answer of God. Deceivers are left speechless when God stops answering. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

8 COMMENTARY: But Micah is truly full of power by the Spirit of the Lord, with judgment and might, to declare Jacob's transgression and Israel's sin. Henry notes his strength was not from himself: ardent love for God and souls, and zeal against sin, kept him at his work despite difficulties, and those who act honestly may act boldly. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

9 COMMENTARY: Hear this, heads of Jacob and princes of Israel who abhor judgment and pervert all equity. Those who come to hear God's word must be willing to be told of their faults, and take it kindly. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

10 COMMENTARY: They build up Zion with blood and Jerusalem with iniquity. Henry says Zion's walls owe no thanks to builders who raise them by bloodshed: the sin of man works not the righteousness of God. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

11 COMMENTARY: Heads judge for reward, priests teach for hire, prophets divine for money, yet they lean on the Lord, saying none evil can come upon them. Henry distinguishes faith, which rests in the Lord as the soul's foundation, from presumption, which only leans on him as a prop to serve a turn; even good acts done for filthy lucre become abomination. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

12 COMMENTARY: Therefore Zion will be plowed as a field for their sake, Jerusalem become heaps, and the mountain of the house as the high places of the forest. Henry notes this was exactly fulfilled at Jerusalem's destruction by the Romans: if sacred places are polluted by sin, they will be wasted by God's judgments. — Matthew Henry (Concise)
