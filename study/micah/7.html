Study Notes — Micah

Chapter 7

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Woe is me, says the prophet: he is like one after the summer fruits are gathered and the vintage gleaned, with no cluster to eat, though his soul desired the firstripe fruit. Henry reads this as the prophet bemoaning that he lived among a people ripening apace for ruin, in which many good persons would suffer. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

2 COMMENTARY: The good man has perished from the earth and none is upright: all lie in wait for blood, hunting every man his brother with a net. Universal corruption shows itself in neighbours preying on neighbours. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

3 COMMENTARY: They do evil with both hands earnestly: prince asks, judge asks for a reward, and the great man utters his mischievous desire, and together they wrap it up. Corruption is a joint work of rulers, judges and powerful men. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

4 COMMENTARY: The best of them is as a brier, the most upright sharper than a thorn hedge; the day of the watchmen and of visitation comes, and now will be their perplexity. Even the best of a corrupt people prick and wound. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

5 COMMENTARY: Trust not in a friend, put no confidence in a guide, and keep the doors of your mouth from her who lies in your bosom. Men had no comfort or satisfaction, Henry notes, in their own families or nearest relations. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

6 COMMENTARY: The son dishonours the father, the daughter rises against her mother, the daughter-in-law against her mother-in-law, and a man's enemies are the men of his own house. Contempt and violation of domestic duties are a sad symptom of universal corruption, and those undutiful to their parents are never likely to come to good. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

7 COMMENTARY: Therefore I will look to the Lord; I will wait for the God of my salvation: my God will hear me. The prophet saw no safety or comfort but in looking to the Lord and waiting on God his salvation, and under trials we should look continually to our Divine Redeemer. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

8 COMMENTARY: Rejoice not against me, O my enemy: when I fall, I shall arise; when I sit in darkness, the Lord will be a light to me. Though cast down, Henry says, we are not destroyed; we may join hope in God's mercy with submission to his correction. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

9 COMMENTARY: I will bear the indignation of the Lord because I have sinned against him, until he pleads my cause and executes judgment for me, bringing me forth to the light where I shall behold his righteousness. Those truly penitent for sin see great reason to be patient under affliction, and when we complain of the badness of the times we ought to complain against ourselves for the badness of our hearts. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

10 COMMENTARY: Then the enemy who said, "Where is the Lord thy God?" will see it and be covered with shame, and will be trodden down as the mire of the streets. Though enemies triumph and insult, they will be silenced and put to shame. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

11 COMMENTARY: In the day that your walls are to be built, the decree will be far removed. Though Zion's walls may long be in ruins, there will come a day when they are repaired. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

12 COMMENTARY: In that day he will come to you from Assyria and the fortified cities, from fortress to river, from sea to sea and mountain to mountain. Israel will come from all the remote parts, not turning back for discouragements, and no hindrances can prevent the favours the Lord intends for his church. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

13 COMMENTARY: Notwithstanding, the land will be desolate because of those who dwell in it, for the fruit of their doings. Deliverance does not cancel the desolation sin earned. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

14 COMMENTARY: Feed your people with your rod, the flock of your heritage dwelling solitarily in the wood, in the midst of Carmel, and let them feed in Bashan and Gilead as in days of old. Henry applies the prayer spiritually to Christ, the great Shepherd of the sheep, to take care of his church and go before them while they are in this world as in a wood, in it but not of it. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

15 COMMENTARY: According to the days of coming out of Egypt, God will show marvellous things. God promises, in answer to prayer, to repeat the miracles of former ages for his people. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

16 COMMENTARY: The nations will see and be confounded at all their might, laying hand on mouth with ears deaf. As sin brought them into bondage, God's pardoning their sin brings them out, and the nations are silenced before it. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

17 COMMENTARY: They will lick the dust like a serpent and move out of their holes like worms of the earth, afraid of the Lord our God and fearing because of his people. Enemies who seemed to prevail are humbled to the dust. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

18 COMMENTARY: Who is a God like you, pardoning iniquity and passing by the transgression of the remnant of his heritage, not retaining anger for ever because he delights in mercy? All who find pardoning mercy, Henry says, cannot but wonder at that mercy. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

19 COMMENTARY: He will turn again and have compassion on us, subduing our iniquities and casting all their sins into the depths of the sea. When the Lord takes away the guilt of sin, he breaks its power so it may not have dominion; and he casts sins not near the shore, where they may appear again, but into the depth, never to rise again, forgiving all. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

20 COMMENTARY: He will perform the truth to Jacob and the mercy to Abraham, sworn to the fathers from days of old. These engagements relate to Christ and the success of the gospel to the end of time, the future restoration of Israel, and the final prevailing of true religion in all lands; not one jot or tittle of his promised truth and mercy will fall to the ground. — Matthew Henry (Concise)
