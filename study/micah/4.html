Study Notes — Micah

Chapter 4

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: In the last days the mountain of the Lord's house will be established above the mountains, and peoples will flow to it. Henry reads this as a precious promise of the gospel church, which Christ himself will build upon a rock, to be more and more fulfilled because he who promised is faithful. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

2 COMMENTARY: Many nations will come, urging one another up to the mountain of the Lord, to be taught his ways and walk in his paths, for the law goes out from Zion. Henry's exhortation fits here: stir one another up to attend God's ordinances, that we may learn his ways and receive his law written on our hearts. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

3 COMMENTARY: He will judge among many peoples and rebuke strong nations afar off, and they will beat swords into plowshares and spears into pruninghooks, learning war no more. Henry notes the nations have not yet so submitted to the Prince of Peace; this peace belongs to the kingdom of Christ. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

4 COMMENTARY: Each will sit under his own vine and fig tree, and none will make them afraid, for the Lord of hosts has spoken it. The promise is certain because it rests on God's own word. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

5 COMMENTARY: While all peoples walk each in the name of his god, God's people will walk in the name of the Lord their God for ever and ever. The Gentiles once worshipped idols; in the promised period God's people cleave to the Lord with full purpose of heart and delight in doing his will. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

6 COMMENTARY: In that day the Lord will assemble her that halts and gather her that is driven out and afflicted. Henry explains "halteth" as walking not according to God's word, and the gathering of the Babylonian captives as an earnest of the church's healing, purifying and prospering. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

7 COMMENTARY: Her that halted will become a remnant, and her that was cast far off a strong nation, and the Lord will reign over them in Mount Zion for ever. Christ's reign continues until it is succeeded by the everlasting kingdom of heaven. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

8 COMMENTARY: To the tower of the flock, the stronghold of the daughter of Zion, the first dominion will come: the kingdom will come to the daughter of Jerusalem. The glorious church for God set up in the world is the fulfilment of this first dominion restored. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

9 COMMENTARY: Why does Zion cry aloud, as if she had no king and her counsellor had perished, in pangs like a woman in travail? Henry's counsel for distress fits here: believers should cry aloud under distress with the prayer of faith, not with despondency. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

10 COMMENTARY: Daughter of Zion must labour like a woman in travail: she will go out of the city, dwell in the field, and go even to Babylon, and there she will be delivered and redeemed from her enemies. The Babylonian captivity itself becomes the place of deliverance. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

11 COMMENTARY: Now many nations gather against Zion, saying let her be defiled and let their eye look upon her. They assemble to rejoice in her calamities. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

12 COMMENTARY: But they do not know the Lord's thoughts or understand his counsel: he gathers them as sheaves into the threshing floor. Their assembly against Zion is, unknown to them, their own gathering for judgment. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

13 COMMENTARY: Arise and thresh, daughter of Zion, with a horn of iron and hoofs of brass, beating many peoples in pieces and consecrating their gain to the Lord of the whole earth. When God has conquering work for his people, Henry notes, he furnishes them with strength and ability for it; nothing yet in the Jewish church's history has matched this prediction. — Matthew Henry (Concise)
