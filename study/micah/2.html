Study Notes — Micah

Chapter 2

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Woe to those who devise evil by night on their beds and rise early to carry it out. Henry stresses it is bad to do mischief on a sudden thought, and much worse to do it with design and forethought. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

2 COMMENTARY: They covet fields and houses and take them by violence, oppressing a man, his house and his heritage. Where covetousness reigns in the heart, Henry says, compassion is banished, and violence and fraud occupy the hands. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

3 COMMENTARY: Therefore the Lord devises an evil against this family from which they cannot remove their necks, and they will walk haughtily no more. Woe to those from whom God turns away; the haughty and secure in prosperity are commonly most ready to despair in adversity. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

4 COMMENTARY: In that day a taunting lament will be taken up: utterly spoiled, the people's portion changed, their fields divided to strangers. Such a calamity cuts them off from the congregation of the Lord and its privileges. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

5 COMMENTARY: Therefore none of them will cast a cord by lot in the congregation of the Lord. The sorest part of the calamity is being cut short in the enjoyment of the congregation's privileges. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

6 COMMENTARY: They say to the prophets, "Prophesy not," hoping to escape shame. Henry answers that God will take them at their word, and their sin will become their punishment: the physician no longer attends the patient who will not be healed. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

7 COMMENTARY: The house of Jacob asks whether the Lord's Spirit is straitened, but his doings are not the problem; his words do good to the upright. Those who silence good ministers and stop the means of grace are enemies not only to God but to their country. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

8 COMMENTARY: God's own people have risen up as an enemy, stripping the robe from travellers passing securely, as from men averse to war. Henry asks what bonds will hold those who have no reverence for God's word. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

9 COMMENTARY: The women of God's people are cast out of their pleasant houses, and his glory is taken from their children for ever. Sinners cannot expect to rest in a land they have polluted. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

10 COMMENTARY: Arise and depart, for this is not your rest: the polluted land will destroy you with a sore destruction. Henry applies it beyond Israel: this world is our passage, not our portion, our inn, not our home, and we have here no continuing city. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

11 COMMENTARY: A man who walks in falsehood, prophesying wine and strong drink, will be exactly the prophet such a people wants. Since they will be deceived, Henry says, let them be deceived; teachers who recommend self-indulgence best suit such sinners. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

12 COMMENTARY: The Lord will surely assemble all Jacob and gather the remnant of Israel together, like sheep of Bozrah in the fold, a multitude making a great noise. Henry reads this of return from captivity, and beyond it of the conversion of the Jews to Christ. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

13 COMMENTARY: The breaker has gone up before them; they break through the gate and go out, their king passing before them and the Lord at their head. Henry reads the breaker as the Lord Jesus opening the way to God and breaking Satan's fetters, so his people follow through their enemies. — Matthew Henry (Concise)
