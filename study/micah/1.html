Study Notes — Micah

Chapter 1

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Micah, from Moresheth in Judah, prophesied under Jotham, Ahaz and Hezekiah, contemporary with Isaiah. Gill notes Samaria stands for the ten tribes and Jerusalem for Judah and Benjamin, and Samaria is named first because it was first in transgression and therefore first in punishment. — John Gill; Matthew Henry (Concise)

2 COMMENTARY: All the people, and the whole land with its inhabitants, are summoned to hear, as in a court. Gill reads the Lord from his holy temple as the all-knowing witness who will testify that this message is God's own word, not the prophet's. — John Gill; Matthew Henry (Concise)

3 COMMENTARY: The Lord comes forth from heaven, not by changing place but by changing his dealing with his people, from mercy to judgment. Gill notes he treads down the high places: proud fortified cities and proud men, and the idol shrines that provoked his wrath. — John Gill; Matthew Henry (Concise)

4 COMMENTARY: Mountains melting like wax and valleys splitting picture kingdoms and their great men losing heart and glory under God's judgment, while the lowly people of the valleys share the calamity. Neither high nor low escapes. — John Gill; Matthew Henry (Concise)

5 COMMENTARY: All this judgment comes for Jacob's transgression, above all idolatry. Gill traces it to Samaria, the calf-worship and the kings and nobles who encouraged it, and to Jerusalem, where even the temple was defiled under kings like Ahaz. — John Gill; Matthew Henry (Concise)

6 COMMENTARY: Samaria will be reduced to a heap of stones in a field, its walls poured into the valley and its foundations laid bare, so that no trace of the city remains. Gill notes the total desolation was fulfilled when Hyrcanus dug the city up until its site could hardly be known. — John Gill; Matthew Henry (Concise)

7 COMMENTARY: Samaria's graven images will be smashed and her idol-hires burned, her idols left desolate. Henry's point is that what is given to idols never prospers: what one lust gains, another wastes. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

8 COMMENTARY: The prophet answers the sentence with wailing, going stripped and naked like a mourner, crying like desert creatures. Henry reads this as lament over a case grown desperate. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

9 COMMENTARY: Her wound is incurable, and the judgment has reached Judah itself, even to the gate of Jerusalem. The blow that fell on Samaria is now at the capital's door. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

10 COMMENTARY: The sorrow is not to be declared at Gath or wept over before mockers who would rejoice at Israel's shame. Instead, in the house of Aphrah, a "house of dust," the people are to roll in the dust as mourners humble themselves under God's hand. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

11 COMMENTARY: Saphir's inhabitant goes out in naked shame, Zaanan does not come forth, and Bethezel's mourning leaves its neighbours without help. Henry notes these place-names themselves point to the miseries coming on them, meant to awaken a holy fear. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

12 COMMENTARY: Maroth waited anxiously for good, but evil came down from the Lord to the gate of Jerusalem. Hope of relief is answered by judgment arriving at the city's entrance. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

13 COMMENTARY: Lachish is told to bind the chariot to the swift beast, a vain preparation for flight. Henry's heading calls such precautions vain: Lachish was the beginning of the sin to Zion, for Israel's transgressions were found in her. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

14 COMMENTARY: Parting presents must be given to Moreshethgath, and the houses of Achzib will prove a lie to the kings of Israel. Henry's warning fits here: refuges other than Christ are refuges of lies to those who trust them. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

15 COMMENTARY: Yet an heir, a dispossessor, will be brought against Mareshah, and the glory of Israel will flee to Adullam. Henry notes other heirs succeed to every inheritance but that of heaven. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

16 COMMENTARY: The people are to shave themselves bald in mourning for their delicate children, enlarging their baldness like the eagle's, because those children have gone into captivity. All glory is turned to shame except the honour that comes from God alone. — Matthew Henry (Concise)
