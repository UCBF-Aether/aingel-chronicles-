Study Notes — Micah

Chapter 5

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: The daughter of troops gathers herself in troops because siege is laid against her, and the judge of Israel is smitten with a rod on the cheek. Henry sets this low point of the house of David as the dark ground against which the Messiah's prediction shines. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

2 COMMENTARY: But Bethlehem Ephratah, little among the thousands of Judah, will give forth the ruler of Israel, whose goings forth are from of old, from everlasting. Henry calls this perhaps the most important single prophecy in the Old Testament: it distinguishes his human birth at Bethlehem from his existence from eternity, and was universally known among the Jews as his birthplace. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

3 COMMENTARY: Therefore God will give them up until she who travails has brought forth, and then the remnant of his brethren will return to the children of Israel. Henry reads here the rejection of Israel for a season and their final restoration. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

4 COMMENTARY: He will stand and feed his flock in the strength of the Lord and the majesty of his God's name, and they will abide, for he will be great to the ends of the earth. Henry notes Christ's government is very happy for his subjects: they will be safe and easy, and we may trust our Shepherd's care and power. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

5 COMMENTARY: This man will be the peace when the Assyrian comes into the land and treads in the palaces, and seven shepherds and eight principal men will be raised against him. Henry explains Christ is our Peace as Priest, making atonement and reconciling us to God, and as King, conquering our enemies, so our souls may dwell at ease in him. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

6 COMMENTARY: They will waste the land of Assyria with the sword, and the land of Nimrod at its entrances, and he will deliver his people from the Assyrian within their borders. Under the shadow of protection from the Assyrians lies a promise of protection for the gospel church from the powers of darkness, and those who threaten ruin to God's church soon bring ruin on themselves. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

7 COMMENTARY: The remnant of Jacob among many peoples will be like dew from the Lord and showers on the grass, which do not wait for man. Henry reads the remnant converted to Christ in the earliest times as drops of dew, made instruments in calling a large increase of spiritual worshippers. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

8 COMMENTARY: The same remnant among the Gentiles will be like a lion among forest beasts and a young lion among flocks, treading down and tearing in pieces, with none to deliver. To those who neglected or opposed this salvation, Henry says, they would cause terror, their doctrine condemning them. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

9 COMMENTARY: Your hand will be lifted up upon your adversaries, and all your enemies will be cut off. Victory in personal conflicts comes, Henry says, as we simply depend upon the Lord our salvation. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

10 COMMENTARY: In that day the Lord will cut off their horses and destroy their chariots. This is part of the purification Henry describes: God strips away the instruments of warlike self-trust. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

11 COMMENTARY: He will cut off the cities of the land and throw down all its strongholds. Human fortifications are removed along with the horses and chariots, so that trust rests in the Lord alone. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

12 COMMENTARY: He will cut off witchcrafts from their hand, and they will have no more soothsayers. The purification of the church includes ending all resort to forbidden arts. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

13 COMMENTARY: Graven images and standing images will be cut off from their midst, and they will no more worship the work of their hands. Reformation means idols gone, not merely reformed. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

14 COMMENTARY: He will pluck up their groves from their midst and destroy their cities. The idol groves fall with the idolatrous cities they served. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

15 COMMENTARY: He will execute vengeance in anger and fury on the heathen, such as they have not heard. Henry's warning stands behind this: those who threaten ruin to the church of God soon bring ruin on themselves, in the ruin of all antichristian powers. — Matthew Henry (Concise)
