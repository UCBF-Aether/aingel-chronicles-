Study Notes — Micah

Chapter 6

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Hear what the Lord says: arise and contend before the mountains, and let the hills hear. The controversy is pleaded openly, before the most enduring witnesses. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

2 COMMENTARY: Mountains and strong foundations of the earth are called to hear the Lord's controversy, for he will plead with Israel. Sin causes the controversy between God and man, and God reasons with us to teach us to reason with ourselves. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

3 COMMENTARY: O my people, what have I done to you, and how have I wearied you? Testify against me. The people are called to declare why they were weary of God's worship and prone to idolatry, when he has given them no cause. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

4 COMMENTARY: He brought them up out of Egypt and redeemed them from the house of servants, sending Moses, Aaron and Miriam before them. Let them remember God's many favours to them and their fathers, and compare their unworthy, ungrateful conduct toward him. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

5 COMMENTARY: Remember what Balak king of Moab consulted and what Balaam answered him, from Shittim to Gilgal, that they may know the righteousness of the Lord. The whole journey from the wilderness to the promised land testifies to God's righteous dealings. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

6 COMMENTARY: With what shall I come before the Lord and bow before the high God: with burnt offerings, with calves a year old? Henry reads this as the substance of Balak's consultation with Balaam about how to obtain Israel's God's favour, now asked by a conscience convicted of guilt and seeking peace and pardon. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

7 COMMENTARY: Will the Lord be pleased with thousands of rams, or ten thousands of rivers of oil, or with a firstborn given for transgression? The proposals betray ignorance, though they show zeal: they offer what is rich and costly, and a convicted sinner would give all the world for peace and pardon, yet they do not offer aright, for the blood of bulls and goats could never take away sin. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

8 COMMENTARY: He has shown you, O man, what is good: what the Lord requires is to do justly, to love mercy, and to walk humbly with your God. The good God requires is not paying a price for pardon, Henry says, but love to himself; moral duties are commanded because they are good for man, and must be kept as penitent sinners depending on the Redeemer and his atonement. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

9 COMMENTARY: The Lord's voice cries to the city, and the man of wisdom will see his name: hear the rod, and who appointed it. Henry's rule fits here: hear the rod when it is coming, before you feel it, and hear it when it has come; the voice of God is to be heard in the rod of God. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

10 COMMENTARY: Are there yet treasures of wickedness in the house of the wicked, and the scant, abominable measure? Having shown how necessary it is to do justly, God now shows how plain it is that they have done unjustly. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

11 COMMENTARY: Shall he count them pure with wicked balances and a bag of deceitful weights? Those dishonest in their dealings will never be reckoned pure, whatever shows of devotion they make. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

12 COMMENTARY: The rich men of the city are full of violence, its inhabitants speak lies, and their tongue is deceitful in their mouth. What is got by fraud and oppression cannot be kept or enjoyed with satisfaction. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

13 COMMENTARY: Therefore God will make them sick in smiting them, making them desolate because of their sins. What they hold closest they commonly lose soonest, and sin is a root of bitterness soon planted but not soon plucked up. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

14 COMMENTARY: They will eat but not be satisfied, their casting down will be in their midst, they will seize but not deliver, and what they deliver will be given to the sword. Gain got by wickedness slips away in the very keeping. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

15 COMMENTARY: They will sow but not reap, tread olives but not anoint themselves with oil, and make sweet wine but not drink it. The fruit of their labour is denied them, as the fruit of fraud cannot be enjoyed. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

16 COMMENTARY: The statutes of Omri are kept and all the works of Ahab's house, and they walk in their counsels, so God will make them a desolation and their inhabitants a hissing, and they will bear the reproach of his people. Their having once been God's people, Henry notes, now that they are backsliders, turns to their reproach. — Matthew Henry (Concise)
