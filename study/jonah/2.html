Study Notes — Jonah

Chapter 2

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Affliction at last brought Jonah to prayer, under a sense of sin and of God's displeasure. Henry notes he prayed once he was kept alive by miracle, a sign he was reserved for further mercy, and that God's goodwill toward him despite his offence opened lips closed by guilt and dread. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: Jonah cried out of his affliction and God heard him, even from what he calls the belly of hell, the fish as grave and prison for his disobedience. Henry's point is that no depth in this world bars crying to God with hope of being heard. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: Though sailors cast him in, Jonah saw God's hand casting him into the heart of the seas, with floods compassing him on every side. The billows and waves were God's own, commissioned to afflict and terrify him but not to destroy him, in words borrowed from David's complaint. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: Jonah sank toward despair, thinking himself cast out of God's sight, which Henry calls the very misery of the lost. Yet faith corrected fear with one resolve: he would look again toward God's holy temple, hoping to live and worship there again. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: The waters threatened his very life and spirit, tokens of God's displeasure that reached to his soul, and the depths closed round him with seaweed wrapped about his head. Henry reads this helpless entanglement as teaching trust not in oneself but in God who raises the dead. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: Jonah went down to the bottoms of the mountains, with the earth barred about him as if for ever, cut off from any hope of return. Henry sets this utter helplessness against the assurance that follows: God brought his life up from corruption. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: When his soul fainted within him, Jonah remembered the Lord, and his prayer came into God's holy temple. Henry calls this hope an anchor of the soul: though perplexed, he was not in despair, because faith had the last word. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: Those who observe lying vanities forsake their own mercy. Henry's concise warning applies Jonah's own case: those who run away from the work of their place and day run away from the comfort of it. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

9 COMMENTARY: Jonah vows sacrifice with thanksgiving and payment of his vows, giving all praise to God. The concise note sums the chapter's encouragement: Jonah's experience encourages others in all ages to trust in God as the God of salvation. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

10 COMMENTARY: The Lord spoke to the fish and it vomited Jonah onto dry land, safe and sound. Henry frames the verse as the completion of the chapter's arc: God heard him praying, and now Nineveh will hear him preaching. — Matthew Henry
