Study Notes — Jubilees

Chapter 44

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Israel took his journey from his house on the new moon of the third month, went by the way of the Well of the Oath, and offered a sacrifice to the God of his father Isaac on the seventh of the month. Henry notes that even for undertakings that appear most joyful, we should seek counsel, assistance, and blessing from the Lord, attending his ordinances and receiving the pledges of his covenant love. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: Jacob remembered the dream he had seen at Bethel and feared to go down into Egypt. The fear was not unbelief but the weight of leaving the promised land, and Henry notes that in all removals we should be reminded of our removal out of this world, encouraged only by God's presence. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: While Jacob thought of sending word to Joseph to come to him instead of going down, he remained seven days, hoping to see a vision whether he should remain or go down. The waiting is itself the counsel Henry commends: seek the Lord's direction before a great removal, rather than rushing on fear or hope alone. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: On the sixteenth the Lord appeared to Jacob and said, Jacob, Jacob, and he answered, Here am I. The double call and the ready answer recall the patriarchs' encounters with God, and the vision Jacob waited seven days to receive. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: God said he was the God of Jacob's fathers, Abraham and Isaac, and told him not to fear going down into Egypt, for there he would make of him a great nation. Henry notes that the promise to Abraham was 215 years old, and the family had only increased to seventy, yet the fulfilling of promises is always sure, though often slow. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: God promised to go down with Jacob, bring him up again, bury him in this land, and let Joseph put his hands upon his eyes. The promise covers the whole Egyptian sojourn: presence in it, return from it, burial in Canaan, and a son's hand closing his eyes. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: The command is repeated: fear not, go down into Egypt. The repetition seals the vision's answer to Jacob's seven days of waiting, turning fear into obedience. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: Jacob's sons and grandsons rose up and placed their father and their possessions upon wagons. Henry notes the particular account of Jacob's family going to Egypt, the seventy whose increase would show God's power in making them a vast multitude. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: Israel rose up from the Well of the Oath on the sixteenth of the third month and went to Egypt. The departure from the place of sacrifice, dated precisely, begins the sojourn God had just promised to bless. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: Israel sent Judah before him to his son Joseph to examine the land of Goshen, for Joseph had told his brothers they should dwell there near him. Henry notes that Joseph contrived to settle his brethren to their advantage, rendering good for evil, and that Goshen lay nearest to Canaan, keeping them near the promise. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: Goshen was the goodliest land in Egypt and near to Joseph, for all of them and also for their cattle. Henry notes that Joseph would have his brethren live by themselves in Goshen, not ashamed of their shepherd calling, lest court preferment tempt them to forget Canaan and the promise. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: These are the names of the sons of Jacob who went into Egypt with their father. Henry observes that a particular account of the family is kept, to show God's power in making these seventy become a vast multitude. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: Reuben, the first-born of Israel, is listed with his sons Enoch, Pallu, Hezron, and Carmi, five souls. The first-born's line opens the register, though the birthright had passed from him. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: Simeon and his sons are listed: Jemuel, Jamin, and Ohad. The list continues tribe by tribe, the family counted as it enters Egypt. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: This unit is the translator's footnote, explaining the purpose of sending Judah: to show the way, citing Genesis 46:28. Charles's note glosses the mission, that Judah went ahead to prepare the road to Goshen. — R. H. Charles

18 COMMENTARY: Simeon's sons continue: Jachin, Zohar, and Shaul, son of the Zephathite woman, seven souls in all. The counting includes the son of the foreign woman, counted among Israel without distinction. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: Levi and his sons are listed: Gershon, Kohath, and Merari, four souls. The priestly tribe enters Egypt as a small family, whose descendants would carry the ark and the tabernacle. — Matthew Henry

20 COMMENTARY: Judah and his sons are listed: Shela, Perez, and Zerah, four souls. The royal tribe enters with the twins born to Tamar, Perez the ancestor of the Messiah among them. — Matthew Henry

21 COMMENTARY: Issachar and his sons are listed: Tola, Phua, Jasub, and Shimron, five souls. The tribe's quiet register gives no deeds, only names, the seed of a people not yet a nation. — Matthew Henry

22 COMMENTARY: Zebulon and his sons are listed: Sered, Elon, and Jahleel, four souls. Each tribe's brief entry builds the seventy, name by name. — Matthew Henry

23 COMMENTARY: These are the sons of Jacob and their sons whom Leah bore in Mesopotamia, six sons and their one sister Dinah, and all the souls of Leah's sons and their sons who went into Egypt were twenty-nine, and with Jacob their father, thirty. Henry notes the purpose of the particular account: to show the power of God in making these seventy a vast multitude. — Matthew Henry

24 COMMENTARY: The sons of Zilpah, Leah's handmaid and Jacob's wife, whom she bore to Jacob, were Gad and Asher. The handmaid's sons are counted with the household, their lines continuing into Egypt. — Matthew Henry

25 COMMENTARY: The sons of Gad who went into Egypt are named: Ziphion, Haggi, Shuni, Ezbon, Eri, Areli, and Arodi, eight souls. Gad's line enters as a troop, as his name foretold. — Matthew Henry

26 COMMENTARY: The sons of Asher are named: Imnah, Ishvah, Ishvi, Beriah, and Serah their one sister, six souls. Asher's register includes a daughter among the sons, the family counted whole. — Matthew Henry

27 COMMENTARY: All the souls of Zilpah's line were fourteen, and all those of Leah were forty-four. The totals gather the tribes into their mothers' houses, Leah's house the largest of the four. — Matthew Henry

28 COMMENTARY: The sons of Rachel, Jacob's wife, were Joseph and Benjamin. The two sons of the beloved wife stand at the head of Rachel's house, one already in Egypt, one arriving with his father. — Matthew Henry

29 COMMENTARY: Joseph's sons, born in Egypt before his father came, were Manasseh and Ephraim, born to Asenath daughter of Potiphar priest of Heliopolis, three souls with Joseph. The Egyptian-born grandsons are counted among Israel, the fruit of Joseph's exile. — Matthew Henry

30 COMMENTARY: The sons of Benjamin are named: Bela, Becher, Ashbel, Gera, Naaman, Ehi, Rosh, Muppim, Huppim, and Ard, eleven souls. Benjamin, the youngest, arrives with the largest list of sons, the tribe that would keep with Judah. — Matthew Henry

31 COMMENTARY: All the souls of Rachel were fourteen, the first reckoning of her house. The number counts the heads of the house, before the fuller reckoning that follows. — Matthew Henry

32 COMMENTARY: The sons of Bilhah, Rachel's handmaid and Jacob's wife, whom she bore to Jacob, were Dan and Naphtali. The last mother's house completes the four houses of Jacob's family. — Matthew Henry

33 COMMENTARY: The sons of Bilhah are introduced: Dan and Naphtali, whose own sons follow. The introduction gathers the handmaid's lines under their mothers' names. — Matthew Henry

34 COMMENTARY: These are the names of the sons of Bilhah's sons who went into Egypt, the register now descending to the grandsons. The naming continues the particular account Henry finds kept to show God's power in the seventy. — Matthew Henry

35 COMMENTARY: The sons of Dan were Hushim, Samon, Asudi, Ijaka, and Salomon, six souls. Dan's line enters Egypt with six names, though Jubilees will add that only Hushim remained. — Matthew Henry

37 COMMENTARY: These are the names of the sons of Naphtali: Jahziel, Guni, Jezer, Shallum, and Iv. Naphtali's five names close the tribal registers, the last of the seventy's branches. — Matthew Henry

39 COMMENTARY: All the souls of Rachel were twenty-six, the fuller reckoning of her house including the grandsons. The two reckonings, fourteen and twenty-six, count the house at different depths, heads and branches together. — Matthew Henry

40 COMMENTARY: All the souls of Jacob who went into Egypt were seventy souls. Henry notes the point of the number: that branch of Abraham's seed to which the promise was made sure had only increased to seventy, yet God would make these seventy a vast multitude. — Matthew Henry

41 COMMENTARY: These are his children and his children's children, seventy in all, but five died in Egypt before Joseph and had no children. The seventy is the reckoned number, though death thinned it even in Egypt, and the promise waited on God's power, not on arithmetic. — Matthew Henry

No note for verse(s) 4, 5, 36, 38, 42 — the public-domain sources consulted give no distinct explanation for these verses beyond the list itself, so nothing was invented for them.
