Study Notes — Jubilees

Chapter 23

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: He placed two fingers of Jacob on his eyes and he blessed the God of gods, and he covered his face and stretched out his feet and slept the sleep of eternity and was gathered to his fathers. Abraham's death: two fingers of Jacob on his eyes, blessing the God of gods, face covered, feet stretched out, the sleep of eternity, gathered to his fathers. — R. H. Charles

2 COMMENTARY: Notwithstanding all this Jacob was lying in his bosom, and knew not that Abraham, his father's father, was dead. Jacob, lying in his bosom, does not know his grandfather is dead. — R. H. Charles

3 COMMENTARY: Jacob awoke from his sleep, and behold Abraham was cold as ice, and he said: 'Father, father!'; but there was none that spake, and he knew that he was dead. Waking to find Abraham cold as ice, Jacob's cry 'Father, father!' goes unanswered, and he knows he is dead. — R. H. Charles

4 COMMENTARY: He arose from his bosom and ran and told Rebecca his mother; and Rebecca went to Isaac in the night and told him; and they went together, and Jacob with them, and a lamp was in his hand, and when they had gone in they found Abraham lying dead. Jacob runs to tell Rebekah, Rebekah tells Isaac in the night, and together with Jacob and a lamp they find Abraham lying dead. — R. H. Charles

5 COMMENTARY: Isaac fell on the face of his father, and wept and kissed him. Isaac falls on his father's face, weeping and kissing him. — R. H. Charles

6 COMMENTARY: The voices were heard in the house of Abraham, and Ishmael his son arose, and went to Abraham his father, and wept over. Voices are heard in Abraham's house, and Ishmael arises, goes to his father, and weeps over him, completed by the next verse. — R. H. Charles

7 COMMENTARY: 'Abraham his father, he and all the house of Abraham, and they wept with a great weeping.' Ishmael and all Abraham's house weep with a great weeping over Abraham. — R. H. Charles

8 COMMENTARY: His sons Isaac and Ishmael buried him in the double cave near Sarah his wife, and they wept for him forty days, all the men of his house, and Isaac and Ishmael, and all their sons, and all the sons of Keturah in their places; and the days of weeping for Abraham were ended. Isaac and Ishmael bury Abraham in the double cave near Sarah, and forty days of weeping by all the house, Isaac, Ishmael, all their sons and all Keturah's sons, end. — R. H. Charles

9 COMMENTARY: He lived three jubilees and four weeks of years, one hundred and seventy-five years, and completed the days of his life, being old and full of days. Abraham's life: three jubilees and four weeks of years, 175 years, completed, old and full of days. — R. H. Charles

10 COMMENTARY: This unit is the heading for the prophecy that follows: the decreasing years and increasing corruption of mankind. It divides Abraham's burial from the eschatological prophecy. — R. H. Charles

11 COMMENTARY: For the days of the forefathers, of their life, were nineteen jubilees; and after the Flood they began to grow less than nineteen jubilees, and to decrease in jubilees, and to grow old quickly, and to be full of their days by reason of manifold tribulation and the wickedness of their ways, with the exception of Abraham. The forefathers lived nineteen jubilees; after the Flood life lessens in jubilees, grows old quickly, and is full of days through manifold tribulation and wickedness, with the exception of Abraham. — R. H. Charles

12 COMMENTARY: For Abraham was perfect in all his deeds with the Lord, and well-pleasing in righteousness all the days of his life; and behold, he did not complete four jubilees in his life, when he had grown old by reason of the wickedness and was full of his days. Abraham was perfect in all deeds with the Lord and well-pleasing in righteousness all his life, yet did not complete four jubilees, grown old by reason of the wickedness and full of his days. — R. H. Charles

13 COMMENTARY: All the generations which will arise from this time until the day of the great judgment will grow old quickly, before they complete two jubilees, and their knowledge will forsake them by reason of their old age, and all their knowledge will vanish away. Generations until the great judgment grow old quickly before two jubilees, and knowledge forsakes them with old age and vanishes away. — R. H. Charles

14 COMMENTARY: In those days, if a man live a jubilee and a half of years, they will say regarding him: 'He hath lived long, and the greater part of his days are pain and sorrow and.' A jubilee and a half of years counts as living long, and the greater part of such days are pain and sorrow, completed by the next verse. — R. H. Charles

15 COMMENTARY: 'Tribulation and there is no peace: for calamity followeth on calamity, and wound on wound, and tribulation on tribulation, and evil tidings on evil tidings, and illness on illness, and all evil judgments such as these, one with another, illness and overthrow, and snow and frost and ice, and fever, and chills, and torpor, and famine, and death, and sword, and captivity, and all kinds of calamities and pains.' Tribulation without peace: calamity on calamity, wound on wound, tribulation on tribulation, evil tidings on evil tidings, illness on illness, and the catalogue of overthrow, snow, frost, ice, fever, chills, torpor, famine, death, sword, captivity, and all calamities and pains. — R. H. Charles

16 COMMENTARY: All these will come on an evil generation, which transgresseth on the earth: their works are uncleanness and fornication, and pollution and abominations. All these evils come on an evil generation transgressing on earth, whose works are uncleanness, fornication, pollution and abominations. — R. H. Charles

17 COMMENTARY: Then they will say: 'The days of the forefathers were many, even unto a thousand years, and were good; but, behold, the days of our life, if a man hath lived many, are three score years and ten, and, if he is strong, four score years, and those evil and there is no peace in the days of this evil generation.' The forefathers' thousand good years are set against threescore years and ten, or fourscore for the strong, and those evil, with no peace in the evil generation's days. — R. H. Charles

18 COMMENTARY: In that generation the sons will convict their fathers and their elders of sin and unrighteousness and of the words of their mouth and the great wickednesses which they perpetrate, and concerning their forsaking the covenant which the Lord made between them and him, that they should observe and do all his commandments and his ordinances and all his laws, without departing either to the right hand or to the left. Sons convict fathers and elders of sin, unrighteousness, words of mouth and great wickednesses, and of forsaking the covenant to observe and do all commandments, ordinances and laws without departing right or left. — R. H. Charles

19 COMMENTARY: For all have done evil and every mouth speaketh iniquity and all their works are an uncleanness and an abomination, and all their ways are pollution, uncleanness and destruction. All have done evil, every mouth speaks iniquity, all works are uncleanness and abomination, all ways pollution, uncleanness and destruction. — R. H. Charles

COMMENTARY: This unit is the heading for the prophecy that follows: the Messianic woes. It divides the indictment of the evil generation from the woes. — R. H. Charles

22 COMMENTARY: Behold the earth will be destroyed on account of all their works, and there will be no seed of the vine, and no oil; for their works are altogether faithless, and they will all perish together, beasts and cattle and birds, and all the fish of the sea on account of the children of men. The earth will be destroyed for their works, with no vine seed and no oil, for their works are altogether faithless, and beasts, cattle, birds and all sea fish perish together on account of the children of men. — R. H. Charles

23 COMMENTARY: They will strive one with another, the young with the old, and the old with the young, the poor with the rich, and the lowly with the great, and the beggar with the prince, on account of the law and the covenant; for they have forgotten commandment, and covenant, and feasts, and months, and Sabbaths, and jubilees, and all judgments. Universal strife, young with old, poor with rich, lowly with great, beggar with prince, over law and covenant, for commandment, covenant, feasts, months, Sabbaths, jubilees and all judgments are forgotten. — R. H. Charles

24 COMMENTARY: They will stand with bows and swords and war to turn them back into the way; but they will not return until much blood hath been shed on the earth, one by another. Bows, swords and war to turn them back into the way fail until much blood is shed on earth, one by another. — R. H. Charles

25 COMMENTARY: Those who have escaped will not return from their wickedness to the way of righteousness, but they will all exalt themselves to deceit and wealth, that they may each take all that is his neighbour's, and they will name the great name, but not in truth and not in righteousness, and they will defile the holy of holies with their uncleanness and the corruption of their pollution. The escaped do not return from wickedness to righteousness, but exalt themselves to deceit and wealth, seizing each his neighbour's all, naming the great name not in truth or righteousness, and defiling the holy of holies with uncleanness and corruption of pollution. — R. H. Charles

26 COMMENTARY: 'And a great punishment will befall the deeds of this generation.' A great punishment will befall the deeds of this generation, completed by the next verse. — R. H. Charles

27 COMMENTARY: 'From the Lord, and he will give them over to the sword and to judgment and to captivity, and to be plundered and devoured.' From the Lord: sword, judgment, captivity, plunder and devouring. — R. H. Charles

28 COMMENTARY: He will wake up against them the sinners of the Gentiles, who have neither mercy nor compassion and who will respect the person of none, neither old nor young, nor any one, for they are more wicked and strong to do evil than all the children of men. Gentile sinners are awakened against them: merciless, compassionless, respecting none, old or young, more wicked and strong to do evil than all the children of men. — R. H. Charles

29 COMMENTARY: 'And they will use violence against Israel and.' Violence against Israel and transgression against Jacob, completed by the next verse. — R. H. Charles

30 COMMENTARY: "Transgression against Jacob, and much blood will be shed upon the earth, and there will be none to gather and none to bury." Transgression against Jacob, much blood shed on earth, and none to gather and none to bury. — R. H. Charles

31 COMMENTARY: 'In those days they will cry aloud.' In those days they cry aloud, calling and praying to be saved, completed by the next verses. — R. H. Charles

32 COMMENTARY: 'And call and pray that they may be saved from.' They call and pray to be saved from the hand of the Gentile sinners, completed by the next verse. — R. H. Charles

33 COMMENTARY: 'The hand of the sinners, the Gentiles; but none will be saved.' From the hand of the Gentile sinners, but none will be saved. — R. H. Charles

34 COMMENTARY: 'And the heads of the children will be white with.' The heads of the children will be white with grey hair, completed by the next verse. — R. H. Charles

35 COMMENTARY: 'Grey hair and a child of three weeks will appear old like a.' Grey hair on children's heads, and a child of three weeks appearing old like a man of one hundred years, completed by the next verse. — R. H. Charles

36 COMMENTARY: 'Man of one hundred years, and their stature will be destroyed by tribulation.' A child of three weeks like a man of one hundred years, stature destroyed by tribulation and oppression, completed by the next verse. — R. H. Charles

37 COMMENTARY: 'And oppression.' Stature destroyed by tribulation and oppression. — R. H. Charles

38 COMMENTARY: This unit is the heading for the renewal that follows: renewed study of the law followed by a renewal of mankind. It divides the woes from the renewal. — R. H. Charles

39 COMMENTARY: This unit is the heading for the kingdom that follows: the Messianic kingdom and the blessedness of the righteous. It divides the renewal heading from the kingdom prophecy. — R. H. Charles

40 COMMENTARY: 'And in those days the children will begin to.' In those days the children begin to study the laws, seek the commandments and return to righteousness, completed by the next verse. — R. H. Charles

41 COMMENTARY: 'Study the laws, and to seek the commandments, and to return to the path of righteousness.' The children study the laws, seek the commandments, and return to the path of righteousness. — R. H. Charles

42 COMMENTARY: 'And the days will begin to grow many and.' The days begin to grow many and increase among the children of men, completed by the next verses. — R. H. Charles

43 COMMENTARY: 'Increase amongst those children of men.' Days increase among the children of men, until they draw nigh to one thousand years, completed by the next verse. — R. H. Charles

44 COMMENTARY: 'Till their days draw nigh to one thousand years and to a greater number of years than before was the number of the days.' Days draw nigh to one thousand years, and to a greater number of years than before was the number of the days. — R. H. Charles

45 COMMENTARY: 'And there will be no old man.' There will be no old man, nor one unsatisfied with his days, completed by the next verse. — R. H. Charles

46 COMMENTARY: 'Nor one who is not satisfied with his days, for all will be as children and youths.' No one unsatisfied with his days, for all will be as children and youths. — R. H. Charles

47 COMMENTARY: 'And all their days they will complete and live.' All their days they complete and live in peace and joy, completed by the next verse. — R. H. Charles

48 COMMENTARY: 'In peace and in joy and there will be no Satan nor any evil.' Living in peace and joy, with no Satan nor any evil destroyer, completed by the next verse. Charles's notes on this passage state there is no Satan in the coming kingdom. — R. H. Charles

49 COMMENTARY: 'Destroyer; for all their days will be days of blessing and.' No evil destroyer, for all their days will be days of blessing and healing, completed by the next verse. — R. H. Charles

50 COMMENTARY: 'Healing.' All their days will be days of blessing and healing. — R. H. Charles

51 COMMENTARY: 'And at that time the Lord will heal his servants, and they will rise up and see great peace, and drive out their adversaries.' At that time the Lord heals his servants, who rise up, see great peace, and drive out their adversaries. — R. H. Charles

52 COMMENTARY: 'And the righteous will see and be thankful, and rejoice with joy for ever and ever, and will see all their judgments and all their curses on their enemies.' The righteous see and are thankful, rejoicing with joy forever and ever, seeing all their judgments and curses on their enemies. — R. H. Charles

53 COMMENTARY: 'And their bones will rest in the earth, and their spirits will have much joy.' Their bones rest in the earth, and their spirits have much joy; Charles's note on this passage is that the bodies rest, but the spirits rise. — R. H. Charles

54 COMMENTARY: 'And they will know that it is the Lord who.' They will know it is the Lord who executes judgment and shows mercy, completed by the next verses. — R. H. Charles

55 COMMENTARY: 'Executeth judgment, and showeth mercy to hundreds and thousands.' He executes judgment and shows mercy to hundreds and thousands, and to all that love him, completed by the next verse. — R. H. Charles

56 COMMENTARY: 'And to all that love him.' Mercy to hundreds and thousands, and to all that love him. — R. H. Charles

57 COMMENTARY: 'And do thou, Moses, write down these words; for thus are they written, and they record them on the heavenly tables for a testimony for the generations.' Moses is to write down these words, for thus they are written and recorded on the heavenly tables for a testimony for the generations forever, completed by the next verse. — R. H. Charles

58 COMMENTARY: 'For ever.' For a testimony for the generations forever. — R. H. Charles

No note for verse(s) 21 — the public-domain sources consulted give no distinct explanation for these verses beyond the list itself, so nothing was invented for them.
