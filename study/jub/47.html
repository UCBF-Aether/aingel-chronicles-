Study Notes — Jubilees

Chapter 47

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: In the seventh week, in the seventh year, in the forty-seventh jubilee, Moses' father went forth from Canaan, and Moses was born in the fourth week, in the sixth year thereof, in the forty-eighth jubilee, in the time of tribulation on Israel. Henry notes the order of providence: just when Pharaoh's cruelty rose to its height by ordering the Hebrew children drowned, the deliverer was born, for when men contrive the ruin of the church, God prepares its salvation. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: Pharaoh, king of Egypt, issued a command regarding the Israelites, that their male children should be dealt with. Henry notes that Pharaoh gave orders to drown all the male children of the Hebrews, the enemy attempting to destroy the church in its infant state. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: The command was that all male children born should be cast into the river. Henry observes that the murdering of the infants was Pharaoh's cruelty at its height, and that the deliverer was born at that very time. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: The Israelites cast their children into the river for seven months, until the day Moses was born. The seven months of drowning precede the birth that would end the drowning, the tribulation reaching its height as the deliverer arrived. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: Moses' mother hid him for three months, and word was told regarding her. Henry notes that Moses' parents hid him by faith, relying on the promise that Israel should be preserved, and that faith quickens the use of lawful means for obtaining mercy. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: His mother made an ark, covered it with pitch and asphalt, placed it in the flags on the river's bank, placed Moses in it seven days, and came by night to suckle him, while by day Miriam his sister guarded him from the birds. Henry notes that at three months' end, when they could hide the infant no longer, they put him in an ark of bulrushes by the river's brink and set his sister to watch, and that Moses never had stronger protection than when he lay alone, a helpless babe upon the waves. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: In those days Tharmuth, Pharaoh's daughter, came to bathe in the river, heard the child's voice crying, and told her maidens to bring him forth, and they brought him to her. Henry notes that providence brought Pharaoh's daughter to the place where the forlorn infant lay, inclining her heart to pity it, which she dared do when none else durst. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: Pharaoh's daughter took Moses out of the ark and had compassion on him. Henry observes that Pharaoh cruelly sought to destroy Israel, but his own daughter had pity on a Hebrew child, and without knowing it preserved Israel's deliverer. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: Moses' sister asked Pharaoh's daughter whether she should go and call one of the Hebrew women to nurse and suckle the babe, and she said go. Henry notes that the sister brought the mother into the place of a nurse, so that Moses was provided with a good nurse, even his own mother. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: The sister went and called Moses' mother Jochebed, and Pharaoh's daughter gave her wages, and she nursed him. Henry notes God's care of Moses in his infancy, and that his own mother, hired as nurse, could teach him the faith of his people under the princess's protection. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: Afterwards, when Moses was grown up, they brought him to Pharaoh's daughter, and he became her son, and Amram his father taught him writing, and after he had completed three weeks they brought him into the royal court. Henry notes that Moses was treated as the son of Pharaoh's daughter, and that many obscure and poor by birth are raised high by surprising providences, to make men know that God rules. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: Moses was three weeks of years at court, until he went forth and saw an Egyptian smiting his friend of the children of Israel, and slew him and hid him in the sand. Henry notes that Moses boldly owned the cause of God's people, in faith, with the full purpose of leaving the honours, wealth, and pleasures of his rank among the Egyptians. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: On the second day Moses found two Israelites striving together, and said to the wrongdoer, why dost thou smite thy brother? Henry notes that Moses tried to end a dispute between two Hebrews, and that the reproof he gave may still be of use to disputants who divide and weaken the church, forgetting they are brethren. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: The wrongdoer was angry and indignant, asking who had made Moses a prince and a judge over them. Henry observes that it is a sign of guilt to be angry at reproof, and that men know not what enemies they are to themselves when they resist faithful reprovers. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: He asked whether Moses thought to kill him as he had killed the Egyptian yesterday. The taunt exposed the secret deed, and the knowledge that the killing was known changed Moses' standing at court in a moment. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: Moses feared and fled on account of these words. Henry notes that Moses was obliged to flee into Midian, and that God ordered this for wise and holy ends, the flight beginning the exile that would end at the burning bush. — Matthew Henry

No note for verse(s) 3 — the public-domain sources consulted give no distinct explanation for these verses beyond the list itself, so nothing was invented for them.
