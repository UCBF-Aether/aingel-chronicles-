Study Notes — Jubilees

Chapter 20

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: In the forty-second jubilee, in the first year of the seventh week, Abraham called Ishmael, and his twelve sons and Isaac and his two sons, and the six sons of Keturah, and their sons. The farewell charge gathers the whole family: Ishmael and his twelve sons, Isaac and his two sons, Keturah's six sons and their sons. — R. H. Charles

2 COMMENTARY: He commanded them that they should observe the way of the Lord; that they should work righteousness, and love each his neighbour, and act on this manner amongst all men. The command opens: observe the way of the Lord, work righteousness, love each his neighbour, acting so among all men. — R. H. Charles

3 COMMENTARY: This verse completes the command: that they should each so walk with regard to them as to do judgment and righteousness on the earth. Walking so among all men means doing judgment and righteousness on earth. — R. H. Charles

4 COMMENTARY: That they should circumcise their sons according to the covenant which he had made with them, and not deviate to the right hand or the left of all the paths which the Lord had commanded us; and that we should keep ourselves from all fornication and uncleanness, and renounce from amongst us all fornication and uncleanness. Circumcision according to the covenant, no deviation right or left from the commanded paths, and renunciation of all fornication and uncleanness are the charge's three marks. — R. H. Charles

5 COMMENTARY: If any woman or maid commit fornication amongst you, burn her with fire, and let them not commit fornication with her after their eyes and their heart; and let them not take to themselves wives from the daughters of Canaan; for the seed of Canaan will be rooted out of the land. Fornication is answered by burning with fire, not by following eyes and heart into it, and Canaanite wives are forbidden, for Canaan's seed will be rooted out of the land. — R. H. Charles

6 COMMENTARY: He told them of the judgment of the giants, and the judgment of the Sodomites, how they had been judged on account of their wickedness, and had died on account of their fornication, and uncleanness, and mutual corruption through fornication. The giants and the Sodomites are the warnings: judged for wickedness, dead for fornication, uncleanness and mutual corruption through fornication. — R. H. Charles

7 COMMENTARY: 'And guard yourselves from all fornication and.' The poetic charge opens: guard yourselves from all fornication, completed by the next verse. — R. H. Charles

8 COMMENTARY: 'Uncleanness, and from all pollution of sin, lest ye make our name a curse, and your whole life a hissing and all your sons be destroyed by the sword, and ye become accursed like Sodom, and all your remnant as the sons of Gomorrah.' Guard against uncleanness and all pollution of sin, lest the name become a curse, life a hissing, sons destroyed by the sword, and the people accursed like Sodom with a remnant like Gomorrah's sons. — R. H. Charles

9 COMMENTARY: 'I implore you, my sons, love the God of.' The imploring opens: love the God of heaven, completed by the next verse. — R. H. Charles

10 COMMENTARY: 'Heaven, and cleave ye to all his commandments.' Love of the God of heaven means cleaving to all his commandments. — R. H. Charles

11 COMMENTARY: 'And walk not after their idols, and after their.' The prohibition opens: walk not after their idols and after their uncleannesses, completed by the next verse. — R. H. Charles

12 COMMENTARY: 'Uncleannesses, and make not for yourselves molten or graven.' Walk not after uncleannesses, and make no molten or graven gods, completed by the next verse. — R. H. Charles

13 COMMENTARY: 'Gods; for they are vanity, and there is no spirit in them; for they are work of men's hands, and all who trust in them, trust in nothing.' Molten and graven gods are vanity with no spirit in them, work of men's hands, and trust in them is trust in nothing. — R. H. Charles

14 COMMENTARY: 'Serve them not, nor worship them.' Idols are neither to be served nor worshipped. — R. H. Charles

15 COMMENTARY: 'But serve ye the Most High God, and worship.' The positive command opens: serve the Most High God and worship him, completed by the next verse. — R. H. Charles

16 COMMENTARY: 'Him continually: and hope for his countenance always, and work uprightness and righteousness before.' Worship the Most High continually, hope for his countenance always, and work uprightness and righteousness before him. — R. H. Charles

17 COMMENTARY: 'Him, that he may have pleasure in you and grant.' Uprightness and righteousness before him are for his pleasure and his granting, completed by the next verse. — R. H. Charles

18 COMMENTARY: 'You his mercy, and send rain upon you morning and evening, and bless all your works which ye have wrought.' His mercy comes with rain morning and evening and blessing on all works wrought, completed by the next verse. — R. H. Charles

19 COMMENTARY: 'Upon the earth, and bless thy bread and thy water and bless the fruit of thy womb and the fruit.' Blessing covers works on earth, bread and water, fruit of womb and fruit of land, completed by the next verse. — R. H. Charles

20 COMMENTARY: 'Of thy land, and the herds of thy cattle, and the flocks of.' Blessing covers fruit of land, herds of cattle and flocks, completed by the next verse. — R. H. Charles

21 COMMENTARY: 'Thy sheep.' The blessing completes: flocks of sheep. — R. H. Charles

22 COMMENTARY: 'And ye will be for a blessing on the earth, and all nations of the earth will desire you, and bless your sons in my name.' Obedience makes them a blessing on earth, desired by all nations, whose sons are blessed in Abraham's name. — R. H. Charles

23 COMMENTARY: 'That they may be blessed as I am.' The charge closes: that they may be blessed as Abraham is. — R. H. Charles

24 COMMENTARY: He gave to Ishmael and to his sons, and to the sons of Keturah, gifts, and sent them away from Isaac his son, and he gave everything to Isaac his son. JFB on Genesis 25:5-6 notes while the chief part of the inheritance went to Isaac, the other sons, Ishmael included, migrated to the East country, Arabia, but received each a portion of the patrimony, perhaps in cattle and other things; here the gifts and the sending away accompany everything given to Isaac. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

25 COMMENTARY: This unit is the heading for the narrative that follows: the dwelling-places of the Ishmaelites and of the sons of Keturah. It divides the farewell charge from the settlement notice. — R. H. Charles

26 COMMENTARY: Ishmael and his sons, and the sons of Keturah and their sons, went together and dwelt from Paran to the entering in of Babylon in all the land which is towards the East facing the desert. The sent-away sons settle together from Paran to Babylon's entering, all the land eastward facing the desert. — R. H. Charles

27 COMMENTARY: These mingled with each other, and their name was called Arabs, and Ishmaelites. Mingled together, they are called Arabs and Ishmaelites. — R. H. Charles

28 COMMENTARY: This unit is the heading for the narrative that follows: Abraham's last words to Isaac. It divides the settlement notice from the charge to Isaac in chapter 21. — R. H. Charles
