Study Notes — Jubilees

Chapter 2

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: The angel of the presence speaks to Moses by the Lord's word, commanding him to write the complete history of creation: six days of work and a seventh-day Sabbath hallowed for all ages as a sign. Charles presents the whole book as this dictated history, and JFB notes creation was gradual for the instruction of man. — R. H. Charles; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: On the first day God created the heavens, the earth, the waters, and all the spirits that serve before him, angels of the presence and of sanctification, spirits of winds, clouds, darkness, snow, hail, thunder and lightning, the seasons, the abysses, darkness, light, dawn and day. JFB on Genesis 1:1-2 reads heaven and earth as the universe, created from nothing, with the Spirit brooding over the waste; Jubilees adds the angelic hosts as first-day creatures, opening the book's angelology. — R. H. Charles; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: The angels saw God's works and praised and lauded him for them, for seven great works were made on the first day. Creation is framed as worship: the hosts that will later keep the Sabbath with God already praise him at the beginning. — R. H. Charles

4 COMMENTARY: On the second day God created the firmament in the midst of the waters, half going up above and half down below it over the face of the whole earth. JFB on Genesis 1:6-8 explains the firmament as the atmosphere, drawing the lighter waters up as clouds while the heavier mass stays below. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: This was the only work created on the second day. The day's singleness marks the firmament's unique office: dividing the waters above from those below. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: On the third day God commanded the waters to pass from the face of the whole earth into one place and the dry land to appear. JFB on Genesis 1:9 describes the waters rushing into hollows left for them until dry land stood out. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: The waters obeyed as commanded, retiring into one place outside the firmament, and the dry land appeared. Obedience is the note: the waters keep the place appointed them rather than returning as chaos. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: On that day God created the seas by their gathering-places, rivers, mountain waters, lakes, dew, seed, sprouting things, fruit trees, trees of the wood, and the garden of Eden in Eden. JFB on Genesis 1:11 stresses the plants grew at once by divine power, not by slow vegetation; Jubilees names Eden itself among the third day's plantings. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: These four great works God created on the third day. The book's habit of counting its works, here and in verses 21 and 31, is part of its symmetrical ordering of creation. — R. H. Charles

10 COMMENTARY: On the fourth day God created the sun, moon and stars and set them in the firmament to give light on all the earth, rule day and night, and divide light from darkness. JFB on Genesis 1:14-19 explains the lights as appointed luminaries, unveiled to regulate the divisions of time. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: God appointed the sun as a great sign on earth for days, sabbaths, months, feasts, years, sabbaths of years, jubilees and all seasons of years. This verse states the book's calendar doctrine: the sun, not the moon, marks the feasts and jubilees, the point on which the calendar apostasy of 1:17 turns. — R. H. Charles

12 COMMENTARY: This verse continues the sun's office: it divides the light from the darkness. The division made on the first day is now entrusted to a visible sign that governs the whole year. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: The verse completes the sun's work: its light brings prosperity, so that all things shooting and growing on the earth may prosper. The sign is not only a calendar but a source of fruitfulness. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: These three kinds were made on the fourth day: sun, moon and stars. The count closes the day as the earlier counts closed the first and third days. — R. H. Charles

15 COMMENTARY: On the fifth day God created great sea monsters in the depths, the first things of flesh made by his hands, with fish, everything moving in the waters, and every flying bird after its kind. JFB on Genesis 1:20-23 notes the rapid, prodigious increase of these creatures, from small fish to great sea monsters and birds of the air. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: The sun rose above the new creatures to prosper them, and above everything on earth, everything shooting from the earth, fruit trees and all flesh. The fourth day's sign now serves the fifth day's life, linking light to fruitfulness. — R. H. Charles

17 COMMENTARY: These three kinds were created on the fifth day: sea monsters, water creatures and birds. The count matches the book's pattern of tallying each day's works. — R. H. Charles

18 COMMENTARY: On the sixth day God created all the animals of the earth, all cattle, and everything moving on the earth. JFB on Genesis 1:24 groups land animals into cattle fit for domestication, wild beasts, and creeping things, all made in one day's work. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

19 COMMENTARY: After all this God created man, male and female, and gave him dominion over all on earth, in the seas, over flying things, beasts, cattle, movers on the earth, and the whole earth. JFB on Genesis 1:26-28 stresses the image lies not in body or intellect but in moral uprightness, knowledge, righteousness and true holiness, and dominion is stewardship as God's representative. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

20 COMMENTARY: These four kinds were created on the sixth day, closing with humanity. Man is counted among the day's kinds yet set over them all by the dominion of the previous verse. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

21 COMMENTARY: There were altogether two and twenty kinds. The book's tally of creation's kinds will be matched in verse 31 by two and twenty heads of mankind from Adam to Jacob. — R. H. Charles

22 COMMENTARY: God finished all his work on the sixth day, all in heavens, earth, seas, abysses, light, darkness and everything. JFB on Genesis 2:1 reads 'finished' as brought to completion, with no new species or law of nature added since. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

23 COMMENTARY: God gave a great sign, the Sabbath day: work six days but keep Sabbath on the seventh from all work. JFB on Genesis 2:2-3 explains that God rested not from exhaustion but by ceasing from working, an example equivalent to a command that we also cease. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

24 COMMENTARY: All the angels of the presence and all the angels of sanctification, two great classes, were bidden to keep the Sabbath with God. Charles's introduction states the book's premise: before the law was revealed on earth it was kept in heaven by the angels. — R. H. Charles

25 COMMENTARY: This verse completes the command: the angels keep the Sabbath with God in heaven and on earth. The earthly Sabbath that follows is a share in a heavenly observance, not a new invention. — R. H. Charles

26 COMMENTARY: God declares he will separate a people from among all peoples, and these will keep the Sabbath day. Election in this book is tied to Sabbath observance: the separated people are recognised by the day they keep. — R. H. Charles

27 COMMENTARY: God will sanctify them as his people and bless them, just as he sanctified the Sabbath day and sanctifies it to himself; they will be his people and he their God. JFB on Genesis 2:3 notes the seventh day was blessed and set apart above the other six for sacred purposes, and here that blessing passes to the people who keep it. — R. H. Charles; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

28 COMMENTARY: God chose the seed of Jacob from all he had seen, wrote him down as his firstborn son, and sanctified him forever, and will teach them the Sabbath day to keep it from all work. Firstborn sonship names Israel's election, and Sabbath observance is its taught mark. — R. H. Charles

29 COMMENTARY: Thus God created a sign by which they should keep Sabbath with the angels on the seventh day, eating and drinking and blessing him who created all things, as he blessed and sanctified a peculiar people above all peoples to keep Sabbath together with the angels. Earthly and heavenly Sabbath are one observance. — R. H. Charles

30 COMMENTARY: God caused his commands to ascend as a sweet savour acceptable before him all the days. Sabbath observance itself is presented as an offering, acceptable to God like the sacrifices of the sanctuary. — R. H. Charles

31 COMMENTARY: There were two and twenty heads of mankind from Adam to Jacob, and two and twenty kinds of work were made until the seventh day. The correspondence is deliberate: the generations of humanity match the kinds of creation, both crowned by the Sabbath. — R. H. Charles

33 COMMENTARY: The seventh day is blessed and holy, and the former also is blessed and holy, and this one serves with that one for sanctification and blessing. Day and people serve together: the blessed Sabbath sanctifies the blessed seed, and the seed's keeping hallows the day. — R. H. Charles

34 COMMENTARY: To Jacob and his seed it was granted that they should always be the blessed and holy ones of the first testimony and law, even as God sanctified and blessed the Sabbath day on the seventh day. Israel's standing as first testimony and law rests on the same blessing that made the Sabbath holy. — R. H. Charles

35 COMMENTARY: God created heaven and earth and all he created in six days and made the seventh day holy for all his works, therefore commanding that whoever does any work thereon shall die and whoever defiles it shall surely die. The death penalty marks the Sabbath's holiness as absolute, not advisory. — R. H. Charles

36 COMMENTARY: Therefore Moses is to command Israel to observe this day, keep it holy, do no work thereon, and not defile it, as it is holier than all other days. The creation ordinance now becomes Israel's commanded law. — R. H. Charles

37 COMMENTARY: Whoever profanes it shall surely die, and whoever does any work thereon shall die eternally, so that Israel may observe this day throughout their generations and not be rooted out of the land, for it is a holy and blessed day. Sabbath observance is tied to remaining in the land itself. — R. H. Charles

38 COMMENTARY: Everyone who observes and keeps Sabbath thereon from all his work will be holy and blessed throughout all days, like unto the angels. The Sabbath-keeper shares the angels' own blessedness, the promise of verse 29 fulfilled in the keeper. — R. H. Charles

39 COMMENTARY: Moses must declare to Israel the law of this day: keep Sabbath thereon, do not forsake it in the error of their hearts, do no unseemly work or their own pleasure, prepare nothing to eat or drink, draw no water, and carry no burden through their gates that was not prepared on the sixth day in their dwellings. Charles notes the book gives earlier forms of enactments that later appear in the Mishnah, and this detailed Sabbath halakhah is such a form. — R. H. Charles

40 COMMENTARY: This verse completes the preparation rule: burdens must have been prepared for themselves on the sixth day in their dwellings. Sixth-day preparation is what makes seventh-day rest possible. — R. H. Charles

41 COMMENTARY: They shall not bring in or take out from house to house on that day, for it is more holy and blessed than any jubilee day of the jubilees, and on it the angels kept Sabbath in the heavens before it was made known to any flesh to keep Sabbath on earth. The heavenly Sabbath precedes its earthly revelation, as Charles's introduction states. — R. H. Charles

42 COMMENTARY: The Creator blessed this day but did not sanctify all peoples and nations to keep Sabbath thereon, but Israel alone, them alone permitting to eat and drink and keep Sabbath on earth. The Sabbath is Israel's exclusive gift, not a universal ordinance. — R. H. Charles

43 COMMENTARY: The Creator blessed this day, which he had created for a blessing and a sanctification and a glory above all days. The chapter closes its Sabbath teaching with a doxology on the day itself. — R. H. Charles

44 COMMENTARY: This law and testimony was given to the children of Israel as a law forever unto their generations. The Sabbath stands as everlasting testimony, matching Charles's account of the book's thesis that the law is of everlasting validity. — R. H. Charles

No note for verse(s) 32 — the public-domain sources consulted give no distinct explanation for these verses beyond the list itself, so nothing was invented for them.
