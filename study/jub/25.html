Study Notes — Jubilees

Chapter 25

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: In the second year of this week in this jubilee, Rebecca called Jacob her son, and spake unto him, saying: 'My son, do not take thee a wife of the daughters of Canaan, as Esau thy brother, who took him two wives of the daughters of Canaan and they have embittered my soul with all their unclean deeds: for all their deeds are fornication and lust, and there is no righteousness with them, for their deeds are evil.' JFB on Genesis 26:34 notes the pious feelings of Abraham recoiled from the idea of Isaac forming a matrimonial connection with a Canaanitish woman, and the devout patriarch would be equally opposed to such a union on the part of his children; the pious heart was wounded and family peace destroyed when the favorite but wayward son brought two idolatrous wives among them, an additional proof Esau neither desired the blessing nor dreaded the curse. — R. H. Charles; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: 'And I, my son, love thee exceedingly, and my heart and my affection bless thee every hour of the day and watch of the night.' Rebekah loves Jacob exceedingly, and her heart and affection bless him every hour of the day and watch of the night. — R. H. Charles

3 COMMENTARY: 'And now, my son, hearken to my voice, and do the will of thy mother, and do not take thee a wife of the daughters of this land, but only of the house of my father, and of my father's kindred.' Hearken to the mother's voice and do her will: no wife from the daughters of this land, only from her father's house and kindred. — R. H. Charles

4 COMMENTARY: 'Thou wilt take thee a wife of the house of my father, and the Most High God will bless thee, and thy children will be a righteous generation and a holy seed.' A wife from her father's house brings the Most High's blessing, and children a righteous generation and a holy seed. — R. H. Charles

5 COMMENTARY: Then spake Jacob to Rebecca his mother, and said unto her: 'Behold, mother, I am nine weeks of years old, and I neither know nor have I touched any woman, nor have I betrothed myself to any, nor even think of taking me a wife of the daughters of Canaan.' Jacob is nine weeks of years old, has neither known nor touched any woman, is betrothed to none, and does not even think of taking a Canaanite wife. — R. H. Charles

6 COMMENTARY: 'For I remember.' Jacob remembers, completed by the next verse. — R. H. Charles

7 COMMENTARY: 'Mother, the words of Abraham, our father, for he commanded me not to take a wife of the daughters of Canaan, but to take me a wife from the seed of my father's house and from my kindred.' He remembers Abraham's command: no Canaanite wife, but a wife from the seed of his father's house and from his kindred. — R. H. Charles

8 COMMENTARY: 'I have heard before that daughters have been born to Laban, thy brother, and I have set my heart on them to take a wife from amongst them.' Daughters born to Laban her brother are heard of, and Jacob has set his heart on taking a wife from among them. — R. H. Charles

9 COMMENTARY: 'And for this reason I have guarded myself in my spirit against sinning or being corrupted in all my ways throughout all the days of my life; for with regard to lust and fornication, Abraham, my father, gave me many commands.' For this reason Jacob has guarded himself in spirit against sinning or being corrupted in all his ways all his life, for Abraham gave him many commands regarding lust and fornication. — R. H. Charles

10 COMMENTARY: 'And, despite all that he hath commanded me, these two and twenty years my brother hath striven with me, and spoken frequently to me and said: "My brother, take to wife a sister of my two wives"; but I refuse to do as he hath done.' For twenty-two years Esau has striven with Jacob, frequently urging him to take a sister of his two wives, but Jacob refuses to do as Esau has done. — R. H. Charles

11 COMMENTARY: 'I swear before thee, mother, that all the days of my life I will not take me a wife from the daughters of the seed of Canaan, and I will not act wickedly as my brother hath done.' Jacob swears before his mother: all his life no wife from Canaan's daughters, and no wicked action as his brother has done. — R. H. Charles

12 COMMENTARY: 'Fear not, mother; be assured that I shall do thy will and walk in uprightness, and not corrupt my ways for ever.' Fear not: he will do her will, walk in uprightness, and never corrupt his ways. — R. H. Charles

13 COMMENTARY: Thereupon she lifted up her face to heaven and extended the fingers of her hands, and opened her mouth and blessed the Most High God, who had created the heaven and the earth, and she gave him thanks and praise. Face lifted to heaven, fingers extended, mouth opened, Rebekah blesses the Most High God, Creator of heaven and earth, with thanks and praise. — R. H. Charles

14 COMMENTARY: She said: 'Blessed be the Lord God, and may his holy name be blessed for ever and ever, who hath given me Jacob as a pure son and a holy seed; for he is thine, and thine shall his seed be continually and throughout all the generations for evermore.' Blessed be the Lord God, his holy name blessed forever and ever, who gave Jacob as a pure son and holy seed; he is God's, and God's shall his seed be continually through all generations forevermore. — R. H. Charles

15 COMMENTARY: 'Bless him, O Lord, and place in my mouth the blessing of righteousness, that I may bless him.' Bless him, O Lord, and place the blessing of righteousness in her mouth, that she may bless him. — R. H. Charles

16 COMMENTARY: At that hour, when the spirit of righteousness descended into her mouth, she placed both her hands on the head of Jacob, and said. At that hour the spirit of righteousness descends into her mouth, she places both hands on Jacob's head, and the blessing begins. — R. H. Charles

17 COMMENTARY: 'Blessed art thou, Lord of righteousness and.' Blessed art thou, Lord of righteousness and God of the ages, completed by the next verse. — R. H. Charles

18 COMMENTARY: 'God of the ages.' God of the ages. — R. H. Charles

19 COMMENTARY: 'And may he bless thee beyond all the generations of men.' May he bless Jacob beyond all the generations of men. — R. H. Charles

20 COMMENTARY: 'May he give thee, my son, the path of righteousness.' May he give Jacob the path of righteousness, and reveal righteousness to his seed, completed by the next verse. — R. H. Charles

21 COMMENTARY: 'And reveal righteousness to thy seed.' Righteousness revealed to Jacob's seed. — R. H. Charles

22 COMMENTARY: 'And may he make thy sons many during thy.' Sons made many during Jacob's life, arising according to the months of the year, completed by the next verses. — R. H. Charles

23 COMMENTARY: 'Life, and may they arise according to the number of the.' Sons arising according to the number of the months of the year, completed by the next verse. — R. H. Charles

24 COMMENTARY: 'Months of the year.' According to the number of the months of the year. — R. H. Charles

25 COMMENTARY: 'And may their sons become many and great.' Their sons becoming many and great beyond the stars of heaven, completed by the next verses. — R. H. Charles

26 COMMENTARY: 'Beyond the stars of heaven, and their numbers be more than the sand of the.' Beyond the stars of heaven, numbers more than the sand of the sea, completed by the next verse. — R. H. Charles

27 COMMENTARY: 'Sea.' More than the sand of the sea. — R. H. Charles

28 COMMENTARY: 'And may he give them this goodly land, as he.' This goodly land given, as he said he would give it to Abraham and his seed after him always, completed by the next verse. — R. H. Charles

29 COMMENTARY: 'Said he would give it to Abraham and to his seed after him alway, and may they hold it as a possession for ever.' As said to Abraham and his seed after him always, may they hold it as a possession forever. — R. H. Charles

30 COMMENTARY: 'And may I see born unto thee, my son, blessed.' May Rebekah see blessed children born unto Jacob during her life, completed by the next verse. — R. H. Charles

31 COMMENTARY: 'Children during my life, and a blessed and holy seed may all thy seed be.' Blessed children during her life, and all his seed a blessed and holy seed. — R. H. Charles

32 COMMENTARY: 'And as thou hast refreshed thy mother's spirit.' As Jacob refreshed his mother's spirit during her life, completed by the next verse. — R. H. Charles

33 COMMENTARY: 'During her life, the womb of her that bare thee blesseth thee, my affection and my breasts bless thee and my mouth and my tongue praise thee greatly.' During her life, the womb that bore him blesses him, affection and breasts bless him, and mouth and tongue praise him greatly. — R. H. Charles

34 COMMENTARY: 'Increase and spread over the earth.' Increase and spread over the earth. — R. H. Charles

35 COMMENTARY: 'And may thy seed be perfect in the joy of heaven.' Seed perfect in the joy of heaven and earth forever, completed by the next verses. — R. H. Charles

36 COMMENTARY: 'And earth for ever; and may thy seed rejoice, and on the great day of peace may it have.' Earth forever, seed rejoicing, and on the great day of peace may it have peace, completed by the next verse. — R. H. Charles

37 COMMENTARY: 'Peace.' On the great day of peace may it have peace. — R. H. Charles

38 COMMENTARY: 'And may thy name and thy seed endure to all.' Name and seed enduring to all the ages, completed by the next verses. — R. H. Charles

39 COMMENTARY: 'The ages, and may the Most High God be their God, and may the God of righteousness dwell with.' The ages, the Most High God their God, the God of righteousness dwelling with them, completed by the next verses. — R. H. Charles

40 COMMENTARY: 'Them.' The God of righteousness dwelling with them. — R. H. Charles

41 COMMENTARY: 'And by them may his sanctuary be built unto all the ages.' By them his sanctuary built unto all the ages. — R. H. Charles

42 COMMENTARY: 'Blessed be he that blesseth thee.' Blessed be he that blesses Jacob. — R. H. Charles

43 COMMENTARY: 'And all flesh that curseth thee falsely may it be cursed.' All flesh cursing Jacob falsely may it be cursed. — R. H. Charles

44 COMMENTARY: She kissed him, and said to him. She kisses him and speaks, completed by the next verse. — R. H. Charles

45 COMMENTARY: 'May the Lord of the world love thee as the heart of thy mother and her affection rejoice in thee and bless thee.' May the Lord of the world love Jacob as his mother's heart and affection rejoice in him and bless him. — R. H. Charles

46 COMMENTARY: She ceased from blessing. The blessing ceases. — R. H. Charles

47 COMMENTARY: This unit is the heading for the narrative that follows: Jacob obtains the blessing of the firstborn. It divides Rebekah's blessing from the firstborn blessing of chapter 26, which belongs to the other crew's chapters. — R. H. Charles
