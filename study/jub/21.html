Study Notes — Jubilees

Chapter 21

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: In the sixth year of the seventh week of this jubilee Abraham called Isaac his son and commanded him, saying: 'I am become old, and know not the day of my death and am full of my days.' Old age, an unknown day of death, and fullness of days open the charge to Isaac. — R. H. Charles

2 COMMENTARY: 'And behold, I am one hundred and seventy-five years old and throughout all the days of my life I have remembered the Lord, and sought with all my heart to do his will, and to walk uprightly in all his ways.' The life summary: 175 years, remembering the Lord, seeking his will with all his heart, walking uprightly in all his ways. — R. H. Charles

3 COMMENTARY: 'My soul hath hated idols, and I have despised those that served them, and I have given my heart and spirit, that I might observe to do the will of him who created me.' Hatred of idols, despising their servants, and heart and spirit given, all that he might observe the Creator's will. — R. H. Charles

4 COMMENTARY: 'For he is the living God, and he is holy and faithful, and he is righteous beyond all, and there is with him no accepting of men's persons and no accepting of gifts; for God is righteous, and executeth.' The living God is holy, faithful, righteous beyond all, with no accepting of persons and no accepting of gifts, and his righteousness executes judgment, completed by the next verse. — R. H. Charles

5 COMMENTARY: 'Judgment on all those who transgress his commandments and despise his covenant.' God's judgment falls on all who transgress his commandments and despise his covenant. — R. H. Charles

6 COMMENTARY: 'And do thou, my son, observe his commandments and his ordinances and his judgments, and walk not after the abominations and after the graven images and after the molten images.' Isaac is to observe commandments, ordinances and judgments, and walk not after abominations, graven images or molten images. — R. H. Charles

7 COMMENTARY: 'And eat no blood at all of animals or cattle, or of any bird which flieth in the heaven.' The blood prohibition is total: no blood of animals, cattle, or any bird of heaven. — R. H. Charles

8 COMMENTARY: 'And if thou dost slay a victim as an acceptable peace-offering, slay ye it, and pour out its blood upon the altar, and all the fat of the offering offer on the altar with fine flour and the meat-offering mingled with oil with its drink-offering, offer them all together on the altar of burnt-offering; it is a sweet savour before the Lord.' The peace-offering rite: blood poured on the altar, all fat offered with fine flour mingled with oil and the drink-offering, all together on the altar of burnt-offering, a sweet savour before the Lord. — R. H. Charles

9 COMMENTARY: 'And thou wilt offer the fat of the sacrifice of thank-offerings on the fire which is upon the altar, and the fat which is on the belly, and all the fat on the inwards and the two kidneys, and all the fat that is upon them, and upon the loins and liver thou shalt remove, together with the kidneys.' The thank-offering fat is specified: belly fat, inward fat, the two kidneys with all fat upon them, and loins and liver removed together with the kidneys. — R. H. Charles

10 COMMENTARY: 'And offer all these for a sweet savour acceptable before the Lord, with its meat-offering and with its drink-offering, for a sweet savour, the bread of the offering unto the Lord.' All these fats are offered as a sweet savour acceptable before the Lord, with meat-offering and drink-offering, the bread of the offering unto the Lord. — R. H. Charles

11 COMMENTARY: 'And eat its meat on that day and on the second day, and let not the sun on the second day go down upon it till it is eaten, and let nothing be left over for the third day; for it is not acceptable, for it is not approved, and let it no longer be eaten, and all who eat thereof will bring sin upon themselves; for thus I have found it written in the books of my forefathers, and in the words of Enoch, and in the words of Noah.' The meat is eaten on the day and the second day, never left to the third day, for third-day eating is not acceptable and brings sin, as written in the forefathers' books and in the words of Enoch and Noah. — R. H. Charles

12 COMMENTARY: This verse opens the salt command: 'And on all.' The sentence is completed by the oblations of the next verse. — R. H. Charles

13 COMMENTARY: 'Thy oblations thou shalt strew salt, and let not the salt of the covenant be lacking in all thy oblations before the Lord.' Salt is strewn on all oblations, and the salt of the covenant must never be lacking in any oblation before the Lord. — R. H. Charles

14 COMMENTARY: 'And as regards the wood of the sacrifices, beware lest thou bring other wood for the altar in addition to these: cypress, defran, sagad, pine, fir, cedar, savin, palm, olive, myrrh, laurels and citron, juniper, and balsam.' The altar woods are a closed list of fourteen: cypress, defran, sagad, pine, fir, cedar, savin, palm, olive, myrrh, laurels, citron, juniper and balsam; no other wood is to be brought. — R. H. Charles

15 COMMENTARY: 'And of these kinds of wood lay upon the altar under the sacrifice, such as have been tested as to their appearance, and do not lay thereon any split or dark wood, but hard and clean, without fault, a sound and new growth; and do not lay thereon old wood, for its fragrance is gone, for there is no longer fragrance in it as before.' The wood laid under the sacrifice must be tested in appearance, hard, clean, without fault, sound new growth; no split or dark wood, and no old wood whose fragrance is gone. — R. H. Charles

16 COMMENTARY: 'Besides these kinds of wood there is none other that thou shalt place on the altar, for the fragrance is dispersed, and the smell of its fragrance goeth not up to heaven.' No other wood may be placed on the altar, for its fragrance is dispersed and does not go up to heaven. — R. H. Charles

17 COMMENTARY: 'Observe this commandment and do it, my son, that thou mayst be upright in all thy deeds.' Observing and doing this commandment makes Isaac upright in all his deeds. — R. H. Charles

18 COMMENTARY: 'And at all times be clean in thy body, and wash thyself with water before thou approachest to offer on the altar, and wash thy hands and thy feet before thou drawest near to the altar; and when thou art done sacrificing, wash again thy hands and thy feet.' Bodily cleanness governs sacrifice: wash with water before offering, wash hands and feet before drawing near the altar, and wash hands and feet again when done sacrificing. — R. H. Charles

19 COMMENTARY: 'And let no blood appear upon you nor upon your clothes; be on thy guard, my son, against blood, be on thy guard exceedingly; cover it with dust.' No blood may appear on person or clothes; guard against blood exceedingly, and cover it with dust. — R. H. Charles

20 COMMENTARY: 'And do not eat any blood, for it is the soul; eat no blood whatever.' Blood is the soul, so no blood whatever is to be eaten. — R. H. Charles

21 COMMENTARY: 'And take no gifts for the blood of man, lest it be shed with impunity, without judgment; for it is the blood that.' No gifts may be taken for the blood of man, lest it be shed with impunity and without judgment; the shed blood causes the earth to sin, completed by the next verse. — R. H. Charles

22 COMMENTARY: 'Is shed that causeth the earth to sin, and the earth cannot be cleansed from the blood of man save by the blood of him who shed it.' Shed blood makes the earth sin, and the earth is cleansed from man's blood only by the blood of him who shed it. — R. H. Charles

23 COMMENTARY: This verse repeats the prohibition's opening: 'And take no.' The sentence is completed by the present or gift of the next verse. — R. H. Charles

24 COMMENTARY: 'Present or gift for the blood of man: blood for blood, that thou mayest be accepted before the Lord, the Most High God; for he is the defence of the good: and that thou mayest be preserved from all evil, and that he may save thee from every kind of death.' No present or gift for man's blood: blood for blood, that Isaac may be accepted before the Most High God, the defence of the good, preserved from all evil and saved from every kind of death. — R. H. Charles

25 COMMENTARY: This verse opens the vision: 'I see, my son.' What Abraham sees follows in the next verses. — R. H. Charles

26 COMMENTARY: 'That all the works of the children of men are.' All the works of the children of men are sin and wickedness, completed by the next verses. — R. H. Charles

27 COMMENTARY: 'Sin and wickedness, and all their deeds are uncleanness and an.' Their works are sin and wickedness, their deeds uncleanness and an abomination, completed by the next verse. — R. H. Charles

28 COMMENTARY: 'Abomination and a pollution; and there is no righteousness with them.' Their deeds are abomination and pollution, and there is no righteousness with them. — R. H. Charles

29 COMMENTARY: 'Beware, lest thou shouldest walk in their ways and tread in their paths.' Beware walking in their ways and treading in their paths. — R. H. Charles

30 COMMENTARY: 'And sin a sin unto death before the Most High.' Walking their ways means sinning a sin unto death before the Most High God, completed by the next verse. — R. H. Charles

31 COMMENTARY: 'God.' The sin unto death is before the Most High God. — R. H. Charles

32 COMMENTARY: 'Else he will hide his face from thee, and give thee back into the hands of thy.' Else God will give Isaac back into the hands of his transgression, completed by the next verse. — R. H. Charles

33 COMMENTARY: 'Transgression, and root thee out of the land, and thy seed.' Given back into transgression's hands, he will be rooted out of the land, and his seed likewise, completed by the next verse. — R. H. Charles

34 COMMENTARY: 'Likewise from under heaven, and thy name and thy seed will perish from.' Seed rooted out from under heaven, name and seed perishing from the whole earth, completed by the next verse. — R. H. Charles

35 COMMENTARY: 'The whole earth.' Name and seed perish from the whole earth. — R. H. Charles

36 COMMENTARY: 'Turn away from all their deeds and all their.' Turn away from all their deeds and all their uncleanness, completed by the next verse. — R. H. Charles

37 COMMENTARY: 'Uncleanness, and observe the ordinance of the Most High God, and do his will and be upright in all things.' Turn from uncleanness, observe the Most High's ordinance, do his will, and be upright in all things. — R. H. Charles

38 COMMENTARY: 'And he will bless thee in all thy deeds.' The Lord will bless Isaac in all his deeds. — R. H. Charles

39 COMMENTARY: 'And will raise up from thee the plant of righteousness through all the earth, throughout.' He will raise up from Isaac the plant of righteousness through all the earth, throughout all generations, completed by the next verse. — R. H. Charles

40 COMMENTARY: 'All generations of the earth, and my name and thy name will not be forgotten under heaven for ever.' The plant of righteousness runs through all generations of the earth, and Abraham's name and Isaac's name will not be forgotten under heaven forever. — R. H. Charles

41 COMMENTARY: 'Go, my son, in peace.' The charge closes with dismissal in peace. — R. H. Charles

42 COMMENTARY: 'May the Most High God, my God and thy God.' The final blessing opens with the Most High God, Abraham's God and Isaac's God. — R. H. Charles

43 COMMENTARY: 'Strengthen thee to do his will, and may he bless all thy seed and the residue.' May he strengthen Isaac to do his will, and bless all his seed and the residue of his seed, completed by the next verse. — R. H. Charles

44 COMMENTARY: 'Of thy seed for the generations for ever, with.' The blessing covers the residue of his seed for the generations forever, with all righteous blessings, completed by the next verse. — R. H. Charles

45 COMMENTARY: 'All righteous blessings, that thou mayest be a blessing on all the earth.' All righteous blessings, that Isaac may be a blessing on all the earth. — R. H. Charles

46 COMMENTARY: And he went out from him rejoicing. Isaac goes out from Abraham rejoicing. — R. H. Charles

47 COMMENTARY: This unit is the heading for the narrative that follows: Isaac, Ishmael and Jacob join in festival with Abraham for the last time. It divides the charge to Isaac from the last festival of chapter 22. — R. H. Charles

48 COMMENTARY: This unit is the heading for the prayer that follows: Abraham's prayer. It marks the prayer embedded in chapter 22. — R. H. Charles
