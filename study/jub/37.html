Study Notes — Jubilees

Chapter 37

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: On the day Isaac died, the sons of Esau heard that Isaac had given the portion of the elder to Jacob, and they were very angry. The anger of the next generation reopens the grievance the fathers had settled by oath, and Henry's warning applies: men may fret at God's counsels, but cannot change them. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: Esau's sons strove with their father, asking why Isaac had given Jacob the elder's portion and passed over Esau, though Esau was elder and Jacob younger. The question repeats Esau's own old complaint, now carried by his sons against the father's settlement. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: Esau answered that he had sold his birthright to Jacob for a small mess of lentils, and that on the day Isaac sent him hunting, Jacob came with guile, brought food and drink, and Isaac blessed him and put him under his hand. Henry's account agrees on both facts: Esau sold his title for what was of no value, and Jacob gained the blessing by deceit that God nevertheless overruled. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: Esau added that their father Isaac had caused them to swear, he and Jacob, that they would not mutually devise evil against his brother. The oath of the previous chapter is pleaded here as the standing peace between the houses. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: The oath bound the brothers to continue in love and peace with each other and not to make their ways corrupt. The terms are those of Isaac's charge: affection, righteousness, and prosperity together, or destruction for the breaker. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: Esau was filled with wrath and indignation at seeing his sons force him to lead them against Jacob his brother. The father's anger shows he knew the war was wrong, even as he was dragged into it. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: Afterwards Esau remembered all the evil hidden in his heart against Jacob, and forgot the oath sworn to his father and mother that he would devise no evil against Jacob all his days. Henry's portrait of Esau fits: he bore malice on account of the blessing, in the way of Cain, and old malice, once remembered, drowned the oath. — Matthew Henry

23 COMMENTARY: Jacob stood on the battlements and spoke to Esau, saying noble was the comfort with which he had come to comfort him for his dead wife. The bitter irony names the truth: an army is no comfort for a mourner, and the visit's pretext is exposed. — Matthew Henry

24 COMMENTARY: Jacob asked whether this was the oath Esau had sworn to his father and again to his mother before they died. The double oath, to father and to mother, is the measure of the breach: both parents' settlements are broken at once. — Matthew Henry

25 COMMENTARY: Jacob said Esau had broken the oath, and that from the moment he swore to his father he was condemned. The condemnation follows Isaac's own sanction: the brother who devises evil falls into his brother's hand. — Matthew Henry
