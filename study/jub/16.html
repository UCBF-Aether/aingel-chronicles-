Study Notes — Jubilees

Chapter 16

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: On the new moon of the fourth month the angels appeared unto Abraham at the oak of Mamre, and talked with him, and announced to him that a son would be given to him by Sarah his wife. JFB on Genesis 18:1-2 notes another manifestation of the divine presence, more familiar than any yet narrated, at the terebinth or oak of Mamre, where Abraham ran to meet the three men as a host meets visitors of superior rank. — R. H. Charles; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: Sarah laughed, for she heard that the angels had spoken these words with Abraham, and they admonished her, and she became afraid, and denied that she had laughed on account of the words. JFB on Genesis 18:12 notes long delay seems to have weakened faith, so Sarah treated the announcement as incredible, and when taxed with the silent sneer she added falsehood to distrust, an aggravated offence saved only by grace. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: The angels told her the name of her son, as his name is ordained and written in the heavenly tables: Isaac. Isaac's name is pre-ordained and written before his birth, like the laws on the tables. — R. H. Charles

4 COMMENTARY: When the angels returned to her at a set time, she would have conceived a son. The set time governs the promise: return and conception are fixed together. — R. H. Charles

5 COMMENTARY: In this month the Lord executed his judgments on Sodom, Gomorrah, Zeboim and all the region of the Jordan, burning them with fire and brimstone and destroying them until this day, even as declared: all their works are wicked and sinners exceedingly, defiling themselves, committing fornication in their flesh, and working uncleanness on the earth. JFB on Genesis 18:17, 21 notes the Lord disclosed to Abraham the awful doom for the cities' enormous wickedness, making them examples of God's severity, with ample proof the judgment was neither rash nor excessive. — R. H. Charles; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: In like manner God will execute judgment on the places where they have done according to the uncleanness of the Sodomites, like unto the judgment of Sodom. Sodom stands as the pattern judgment for every place sharing its uncleanness. — R. H. Charles

7 COMMENTARY: But Lot was saved, for God remembered Abraham and sent him out from the midst of the overthrow. Lot's deliverance is for Abraham's sake: God remembers Abraham and sends Lot out of the overthrow. — R. H. Charles

8 COMMENTARY: He and his daughters committed sin upon the earth, such as had not been on the earth since the days of Adam till his time, for the man lay with his daughters. The cave sin is unmatched since Adam's days: a father lying with his daughters. — R. H. Charles

9 COMMENTARY: This verse opens the decree: behold, it was commanded and engraven concerning all his seed, on the heavenly tables, to remove them and root them out. The sentence is completed by the Sodom-like judgment of the next verse. — R. H. Charles

10 COMMENTARY: The decree is to execute judgment upon them like the judgment of Sodom, and to leave no seed of the man on earth on the day of condemnation. Lot's seed faces Sodom's judgment and total removal on the condemnation day. — R. H. Charles

11 COMMENTARY: In this month Abraham moved from Hebron, and departed and dwelt between Kadesh and Shur in the mountains of Gerar. After Sodom, Abraham moves to the Gerar mountains between Kadesh and Shur. — R. H. Charles

12 COMMENTARY: In the middle of the fifth month he moved from thence and dwelt at the Well of the Oath. The move to the Well of the Oath, Beersheba, follows the Gerar stay by a month's middle. — R. H. Charles

13 COMMENTARY: In the middle of the sixth month the Lord visited Sarah and did unto her as he had spoken, and she conceived. Visitation and conception fall in the sixth month's middle, at the spoken time. — R. H. Charles

14 COMMENTARY: She bare a son in the third month and in the middle of the month, at the time of which the Lord had spoken to Abraham, on the festival of the firstfruits of the harvest Isaac was born. Isaac is born on the firstfruits festival, third month's middle day, matching the feast of chapter 15 and the fifteenth-day list in Charles's notes on chapter 1. — R. H. Charles

15 COMMENTARY: Abraham circumcised his son on the eighth day: he was the first that was circumcised according to the covenant which is ordained forever. Isaac is the first eighth-day circumcision under the everlasting covenant of chapter 15. — R. H. Charles

16 COMMENTARY: In the sixth year of the fourth week the angels came to Abraham, to the Well of the Oath, and appeared unto him, as they had told Sarah that they should return to her, and she would have conceived a son. The return visit at the Well of the Oath, sixth year of the fourth week, recalls the set-time promise to Sarah. — R. H. Charles

17 COMMENTARY: The angels returned in the seventh month and found Sarah with child before them, and blessed Abraham, announcing all things decreed concerning him: that he should not die till he should beget six sons more and see them before he died, but that in Isaac should his name and seed be called. The decree promises six more sons seen before death, while name and seed are called in Isaac alone. — R. H. Charles

18 COMMENTARY: All the seed of his sons should be Gentiles and be reckoned with the Gentiles. The other sons' seed counts among the Gentiles, outside the chosen reckoning. — R. H. Charles

19 COMMENTARY: But from the sons of Isaac one should become a holy seed, and should not be reckoned among the Gentiles. From Isaac's sons one holy seed stands apart from the Gentile reckoning. — R. H. Charles

20 COMMENTARY: For he should become the portion of the Most High, and all his seed had fallen into the possession of God, that it should be unto the Lord a people for his possession above all nations, and that it should become a kingdom and priests and a holy nation. And the angels went their way and announced to Sarah all they had told him, and both rejoiced with exceeding great joy. And he built there an altar to the Lord who had delivered him and was making him rejoice in the land of his sojourning, and celebrated a festival of joy in this month seven days, near the altar built at the Well of the Oath. He built booths for himself and his servants on this festival, and was the first to celebrate the feast of tabernacles on the earth. During these seven days he brought each day to the altar a burnt offering of two oxen, two rams, seven sheep and one he-goat for a sin offering, to atone for himself and his seed, and as a thank-offering seven rams, seven kids, seven sheep and seven he-goats, with fruit and drink offerings, burning all the fat on the altar as a chosen offering unto the Lord for a sweet smelling savour. The holy seed becomes God's possession above all nations, a kingdom, priests and holy nation, and Abraham answers with the first tabernacles on earth: seven days of booths, daily burnt and thank offerings, and fat burnt as a sweet savour. — R. H. Charles

21 COMMENTARY: Morning and evening he burnt fragrant substances, frankincense and galbanum, and stacte, and nard, and myrrh, and spice, and costum, all these seven offered crushed, mixed together in equal parts and pure. The morning and evening incense joins seven substances, crushed, equally mixed and pure. — R. H. Charles

22 COMMENTARY: He celebrated this feast during seven days, rejoicing with all his heart and all his soul, he and all those in his house, and there was no stranger with him, nor any that was uncircumcised. The seven-day feast is household joy with heart and soul, and no stranger or uncircumcised person shares it: the covenant household alone keeps it. — R. H. Charles

23 COMMENTARY: He blessed his Creator who had created him in his generation, for he had created him according to his good pleasure, for he knew and perceived that from him would arise the plant of righteousness for the eternal generations. Abraham blesses the Creator who made him by good pleasure, foreknowing the plant of righteousness from him. — R. H. Charles

24 COMMENTARY: This verse continues the blessing: from him a holy seed, so that it should become like him who had made all things. The holy seed's destiny is likeness to its Maker. — R. H. Charles

25 COMMENTARY: This verse completes the blessing: the holy seed should become like him who made all things. Likeness to the Creator is the seed's appointed end. — R. H. Charles

26 COMMENTARY: He blessed and rejoiced, and called the name of this festival the festival of the Lord, a joy acceptable to the Most High God. The feast is named the festival of the Lord, a joy acceptable to the Most High. — R. H. Charles

27 COMMENTARY: The angels blessed him forever, and all his seed after him throughout all the generations of the earth, because he celebrated this festival in its season, according to the testimony of the heavenly tables. The angels' perpetual blessing answers seasonal observance according to the heavenly tables' testimony. — R. H. Charles

28 COMMENTARY: For this reason it is ordained on the heavenly tables concerning Israel, that they shall celebrate the feast of tabernacles seven days with joy, in the seventh month, acceptable before the Lord, a statute forever throughout their generations every year. Tabernacles is ordained for Israel: seventh month, seven days of joy, yearly forever. — R. H. Charles

29 COMMENTARY: To this there is no limit of days, for it is ordained forever regarding Israel that they should celebrate it and dwell in booths, and set wreaths upon their heads and take leafy boughs, and willows from the brook. Booths, wreaths, leafy boughs and brook willows are the perpetual observances, without limit of days. — R. H. Charles

30 COMMENTARY: Abraham took branches of palm trees, and the fruit of goodly trees, and every day going round the altar with the branches seven times in the morning, he praised and gave thanks to his God for all things in joy. The daily sevenfold altar circuit with palm branches and goodly fruit, morning praise and thanks in joy, is Abraham's own tabernacles rite. — R. H. Charles

31 COMMENTARY: This verse completes the circuit notice: going round the altar seven times a day in the morning, praising and giving thanks. The sevenfold morning circuit is the feast's daily rhythm. — R. H. Charles

32 COMMENTARY: This unit is the heading for the narrative that follows: the expulsion of Hagar and Ishmael. It divides the tabernacles feast from the expulsion that opens chapter 17. — R. H. Charles
