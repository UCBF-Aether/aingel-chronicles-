Study Notes — Jubilees

Chapter 15

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: In the fifth year of the fourth week of this jubilee, in the third month, in the middle of the month, Abram celebrated the feast of the firstfruits of the grain harvest. Charles's notes on chapter 1 mark the fifteenth of the third month as the feast of firstfruits, the same day later marked for Isaac's birth and Abraham's death. — R. H. Charles

2 COMMENTARY: He offered new offerings on the altar, the first-fruits of the produce unto the Lord, an heifer and a goat and a sheep on the altar as a burnt sacrifice, with their fruit-offerings and drink-offerings upon the altar with frankincense. The firstfruits offering follows the full pattern of chapter 6 and 7: burnt sacrifice, fruit and drink offerings, and frankincense. — R. H. Charles

3 COMMENTARY: The Lord appeared to Abram and said: 'I am God Almighty; approve thyself before me and be thou perfect.' JFB on Genesis 17:1 notes Abram was ninety-nine, thirteen years after Ishmael's birth, an interval of communion with no special revelation, probably on account of his hasty and blameable marriage with Hagar; the appearance was some visible manifestation, probably the Shekinah, and 'Almighty' conveys 'all-sufficient,' while walking perfect means upright and sincere in heart, speech and behaviour. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: This verse opens the covenant promise: 'And I will make my covenant between me and thee, and I will multiply thee exceedingly.' The sentence is completed by Abram falling on his face in the next verse. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: Abram fell on his face, and God talked with him. JFB on Genesis 17:3 describes falling on the face as the attitude of profoundest reverence among Eastern people, the prostrate body with forehead touching the ground, expressing conscious humility. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: 'Behold my ordinance is with thee.' The covenant is presented as an ordinance already with Abram, the foundation of all that follows. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: 'And thou wilt be the father of many nations.' Fatherhood of many nations is the ordinance's first content, before any name change. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: 'Neither will thy name any more be called Abram, but thy name from henceforth, even forever, shall be Abraham.' JFB on Genesis 17:5 notes that in Eastern countries a change of name advertises some new circumstance in history, rank or religion, and God adapted his procedure to the ideas of the age: Abram, 'a high father,' becomes Abraham, 'father of a multitude of nations.' — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: This verse completes the renaming: from henceforth, even forever, his name shall be Abraham. The new name is perpetual, not temporary. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: 'For the father of many nations have I made thee.' The name's reason is stated as already accomplished: father of many nations he has been made. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: 'And I shall make thee very great, and I shall make thee into nations, and kings will come forth from thee.' Greatness expands into nations and then into kings, the promise's three steps. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: 'And I shall establish my covenant between me and thee, and thy seed after thee, throughout their generations, for an eternal covenant, so that I may be a God unto thee, and to thy seed after thee.' JFB on Genesis 17:4 notes the renewed mention of the covenant as the foundation of the communication, the covenant of grace made with all who believe. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: 'And I shall give to thee and to thy seed after thee the land where thou hast been a sojourner, the land of Canaan, that thou mayst possess it forever, and I shall be their God.' JFB on Genesis 17:8 notes the land, previously promised, is here promised as an everlasting possession, and therefore a type of heaven, the better country. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: The Lord said unto Abraham: 'And as for thee, do thou keep my covenant, thou and thy seed after thee; and circumcise ye every male among you, and circumcise your foreskins, and it will be a token of an eternal covenant between me and you.' JFB on Genesis 17:10 notes circumcision was the sign in the Old Testament Church as baptism is in the New, hence the covenant is called the covenant of circumcision, with terms that Abraham and his seed observe circumcision while God gives Canaan perpetually, is their God, and blesses all nations in him and his seed. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: 'And the child on the eighth day ye will circumcise, every male throughout your generations, him that is born in the house, or whom ye have bought with money from any stranger, whom ye have acquired who is not of thy seed.' The eighth-day rule covers every male of the household, born or bought, seed or stranger. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: 'He that is born in thy house will surely be circumcised, and those whom thou hast bought with money will be circumcised, and my covenant will be in your flesh for an eternal ordinance.' The covenant is placed in the flesh itself, an eternal ordinance carried on the body. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: 'And the uncircumcised male who is not circumcised in the flesh of his foreskin on the eighth day, that soul will be cut off from his people, for he hath broken my covenant.' The penalty matches the sign: breaking the flesh covenant cuts the soul off from the people. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

18 COMMENTARY: God said unto Abraham: 'As for Sarai thy wife, her name will no more be called Sarai, but Sarah will be her name.' Sarai's renaming parallels Abraham's, marking the same new circumstance for the mother of the promised son. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

19 COMMENTARY: 'And I shall bless her, and give thee a son by her, and I shall bless him and he will become a nation, and kings of nations will proceed from him.' JFB on Genesis 17:15-16 notes God's purposes are gradually made known: a son was long promised, but now for the first time Abraham is informed it will be a child of Sarai. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

20 COMMENTARY: Abraham fell on his face, and rejoiced, and said in his heart: 'Shall a son be born to him that is a hundred years old, and shall Sarah, who is ninety years old, bring forth?' JFB on Genesis 17:17 notes this was not the sneer of unbelief but a smile of delight at the improbability of the event. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

21 COMMENTARY: Abraham said unto God: 'O that Ishmael might live before thee!' JFB on Genesis 17:18 reads this as the natural solicitude of a parent, while God's thoughts are not as man's thoughts. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

22 COMMENTARY: God said: 'Yea, and Sarah also will bear thee a son, and thou wilt call his name Isaac, and I shall establish my covenant with him, an everlasting covenant, and for his seed after him.' JFB on Genesis 17:19-20 notes the blessings of the covenant are reserved for Isaac, while common blessings are abundantly promised to Ishmael. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

23 COMMENTARY: 'And as for Ishmael also have I heard thee, and behold I shall bless him, and make him great, and multiply him exceedingly, and he will beget twelve princes, and I shall make him a great nation.' Ishmael hears a blessing of greatness, multiplication and twelve princes, but not the covenant. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

24 COMMENTARY: 'But my covenant shall I establish with Isaac, whom Sarah will bear to thee, in these days, in the next year.' The covenant is fixed on Isaac, and the birth is timed: in these days, in the next year. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

25 COMMENTARY: He left off speaking with him, and God went up from Abraham. The theophany ends with God's ascent, as the appearance began with his descent to Abram. — R. H. Charles

26 COMMENTARY: Abraham did according as God had said unto him, and he took Ishmael his son, and all born in his house, and whom he had bought with his money, every male in his house, and circumcised the flesh of their foreskin. Obedience is immediate and household-wide, covering son, home-born and bought alike. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

27 COMMENTARY: This verse completes the obedience: Abraham circumcised the flesh of their foreskin. The act matches the command of verse 14 without delay. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

28 COMMENTARY: On the selfsame day was Abraham circumcised, and all the men of his house, those born in the house, and all those bought with money from the children of the stranger, were circumcised with him. The whole household is circumcised on the selfsame day as Abraham himself. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

29 COMMENTARY: This law is for all the generations forever, and there is no circumcision of the days and no omission of one day out of the eight days, for it is an eternal ordinance, ordained and written on the heavenly tables. The eighth day admits no shifting and no omission: the ordinance is eternal and written on the heavenly tables. — R. H. Charles

30 COMMENTARY: Everyone that is born, whose foreskin's flesh is not circumcised on the eighth day, belongs not to the children of the covenant which the Lord made with Abraham, but to the children of destruction; nor is there any sign on him that he is the Lord's, but he is destined to be destroyed and slain from the earth, and rooted out of the earth, for he has broken the covenant of the Lord our God. The uncircumcised man belongs to the children of destruction, bears no sign of belonging to the Lord, and faces destruction and rooting out. — R. H. Charles

31 COMMENTARY: For all the angels of the presence and all the angels of sanctification have been so created from the day of their creation, and before the angels of the presence and the angels of sanctification he has sanctified Israel, that they should be with him and with his holy angels. Israel's circumcision conforms them to the angels' created state, and Israel was sanctified before those angelic orders to dwell with God and his holy angels. — R. H. Charles

32 COMMENTARY: Moses is to command the children of Israel and let them observe the sign of this covenant for their generations as an eternal ordinance, and they will not be rooted out of the land. Observance keeps them in the land, the reverse of verse 30's rooting out. — R. H. Charles

33 COMMENTARY: For the command is ordained for a covenant, that they should observe it forever among all the children of Israel. The command's perpetual observance among all Israel is the covenant's own terms. — R. H. Charles

34 COMMENTARY: For Ishmael and his sons and his brothers and Esau, the Lord did not cause to approach him, and he chose them not because they are the children of Abraham, because he knew them, but he chose Israel to be his people. Election narrows within Abraham's children: Ishmael's line and Esau are not brought near, because God knew them, and Israel alone is chosen as his people. — R. H. Charles

35 COMMENTARY: This verse opens the election doctrine: he sanctified Israel and gathered it from amongst all the children of men. The sentence is completed by the spirits over the nations in the next verse. — R. H. Charles

36 COMMENTARY: For there are many nations and many peoples, and all are his, and over all he has placed spirits in authority to lead them astray from him. The nations have spirits set over them in authority, leading them astray, while Israel, as the next verse states, has no such intermediary. — R. H. Charles

37 COMMENTARY: But over Israel he did not appoint any angel or spirit, for he alone is their ruler, and he will preserve them and require them at the hand of his angels and his spirits, and at the hand of all his powers, in order that he may preserve them and bless them, and that they may be his and he may be theirs from henceforth forever. Israel's sole ruler is God himself, who preserves and blesses directly and holds his own angels, spirits and powers accountable for them. — R. H. Charles

38 COMMENTARY: 'And now I announce unto thee that the children of Israel will not keep true to this ordinance, and they will not circumcise their sons according to all this law; for in the flesh of their circumcision they will omit this circumcision of their sons, and all of them, sons of Beliar, will leave their sons uncircumcised as they were born.' The prophecy announces apostasy: sons of Beliar will leave their sons uncircumcised as born, omitting the covenant sign. — R. H. Charles

39 COMMENTARY: 'And there will be great wrath from the Lord against the children of Israel, because they have forsaken his covenant and turned aside from his word, and provoked and blasphemed, inasmuch as they do not observe the ordinance of this law; for they have treated their members like the Gentiles, so that they may be removed and rooted out of the land.' Great wrath follows forsaking the covenant and treating their members like the Gentiles, ending in removal and rooting out of the land. — R. H. Charles

40 COMMENTARY: 'And there will no more be pardon or forgiveness unto them, so that there should be forgiveness and pardon, for all the sin of this eternal error.' No pardon remains for this eternal error, the breaking of the everlasting covenant sign, as the bracketed gloss in the text itself acknowledges the wording's difficulty. — R. H. Charles

No note for verse(s) 41 — the public-domain sources consulted give no distinct explanation for these verses beyond the list itself, so nothing was invented for them.
