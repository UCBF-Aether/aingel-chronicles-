Study Notes — Jubilees

Chapter 49

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Remember the commandment concerning the passover: celebrate it in its season on the fourteenth of the first month, kill it before evening, and eat it by night on the evening of the fifteenth from sunset. Henry notes that God appointed that on the night of going out of Egypt each family should kill a lamb, and that the blood was to be sprinkled on the door-posts to mark Israel's houses from Egypt's. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: On this night, the beginning of the festival and the beginning of the joy, Israel was eating the passover in Egypt when all the powers of Mastema were let loose to slay all the first-born in Egypt, from Pharaoh's first-born to the first-born of the captive maidservant. Henry notes that the plague struck the first-born, the joy and hope of families, reaching from throne to dungeon, prince and peasant alike before God's judgments. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: The slaying extended to the first-born of the captive maidservant in the mill, and to the cattle. No rank and no kind was exempt: the judgment fell on every first-born in the land except where the blood marked the house. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: The sign the Lord gave was this: into every house on whose lintels they saw the blood of a yearling lamb, they should not enter to slay, but pass by, so that all in the house were saved because the blood was on its lintels. Henry explains the name: the angel, destroying Egypt's first-born, would pass over the houses marked by the lamb's blood, hence the holy feast's name, and the blood was the believer's protection from wrath. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: The powers of the Lord did as commanded, passing by all Israel, and the plague came not upon them to destroy any soul, whether cattle, man, or dog. Henry notes that the destroying angel had nothing to do where the blood was, and that Israel's safety was not a reward of their righteousness but the gift of mercy. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: The plague was very grievous in Egypt, and there was no house without one dead, with weeping and lamentation. Henry bids the reader imagine the cry that rang through Egypt, the long loud shriek of agony from every dwelling, leaving not a house without one dead. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: All Israel was eating the paschal lamb's flesh, drinking wine, lauding, blessing, and thanking the Lord God of their fathers, ready to go forth from under Egypt's yoke and evil bondage. Henry notes the lamb was to be eaten standing, with staves in hand, as ready to depart, and that the feast was kept with the bitterness of bondage remembered and deliverance at hand. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: Remember this day all the days of life, and observe it year to year, once a year, on its day, according to all its law, without adjourning it from day to day or month to month. Henry notes that the passover was to be kept every year as a remembrance of preservation and deliverance, and as a type of Christ, and that the keeping of the solemnity looked backward to old mercies and forward to the Lamb of God. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: It is an eternal ordinance, engraven on the heavenly tables for all Israel, to be observed every year on its day once a year throughout all generations, with no limit of days, ordained for ever. Henry agrees on the perpetuity: it was by an ordinance for ever, so long as we live we must continue feeding on Christ and rejoicing in what he has done. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: The man free from uncleanness who does not come to observe the passover on its day, to bring an acceptable offering and eat and drink before the Lord on its festival, that man, clean and close at hand, will be cut off, because he offered not the Lord's oblation in its appointed season. Henry notes that in times to come all the congregation must keep the passover, and that all who share God's mercies should join in thankful praise, none neglecting the ordinance. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: The negligent man will take the guilt upon himself. The guilt is the penalty's ground: the appointed season was known, the offering was due, and the failure was his own. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: Let Israel come and observe the passover on its fixed time, the fourteenth day of the first month, between the evenings, from the third part of the day to the third part of the night, for two portions of the day are given to light and a third part to the evening. Henry notes the lamb was to be set apart and slain as directed, and Jubilees fixes the between-the-evenings window by its thirds of day and night. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: That is what the Lord commanded: observe it between the evenings. The window is the ordinance's centre, neither in the full light nor in the full night, but at the passage between them. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: It is not permissible to slay the lamb during any period of light, but during the period bordering on evening, and they must eat it at evening until the third part of the night, burning with fire whatever flesh remains from the third part of the night onward. Henry notes the lamb was to be eaten at once, not put by till morning, and what remained was to be burned, for the feast allowed no delay. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: They shall not cook it with water nor eat it raw, but roast it on the fire, eating with diligence its head with its inwards and its feet roasted with fire, breaking no bone, for of Israel no bone shall be crushed. Henry notes the lamb was to be roasted with fire, denoting Christ's sufferings even unto death, and that not a bone of it must be broken, which was fulfilled in Christ, denoting his unbroken strength. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: For this reason the Lord commanded Israel to observe the passover on its fixed time, breaking no bone, for it is a festival day and a commanded day. The unbroken bone is both a rule of eating and a sign: the lamb whole, the people whole, the deliverance whole. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: There may be no passing over from day to day or month to month, but on the day of its festival let it be observed. The fixed day is repeated because the whole ordinance stands on it: adjourning the passover would unfix the memorial. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: Moses is commanded to have Israel observe the passover throughout their days, every year, once a year on its fixed time, and it will come as a memorial well pleasing before the Lord, with no plague coming to slay or smite in the year they celebrate it in its season according to his command. Henry notes that the great things God does for his people are to be remembered throughout all ages, and that the passover night was a night of the Lord, much to be observed. — Matthew Henry

26 COMMENTARY: Moses is commanded to declare to Israel every year the ordinances of the passover, the day of its days, and the festival of unleavened bread, eating unleavened bread seven days, observing its festival, and bringing an oblation every day during those seven days of joy before the Lord on the altar. Henry notes that the feast of unleavened bread was typical of the Christian life, kept in charity without malice's leaven and in sincerity without hypocrisy's leaven, and that in after-times parents should carefully teach their children the service's meaning. — Matthew Henry

27 COMMENTARY: Israel celebrated this festival with haste when they went forth from Egypt until they entered the wilderness of Shur, for on the shore of the sea they completed it. Henry notes the lamb was eaten standing, with staves in hand, as ready to depart, and that the haste of the first passover became the pattern of its yearly keeping. — Matthew Henry

No note for verse(s) 19, 20, 21, 22, 23, 24, 25, 28 — the public-domain sources consulted give no distinct explanation for these verses beyond the list itself, so nothing was invented for them.
