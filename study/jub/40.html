Study Notes — Jubilees

Chapter 40

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: In those days Pharaoh dreamed two dreams in one night concerning a famine to come in all the land, awoke, and called all Egypt's interpreters and magicians to tell them his dreams. Henry notes that the dreams showed they were sent of God, since Pharaoh awoke with his spirit troubled. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: The interpreters and magicians were not able to declare Pharaoh's dreams. Human wisdom failed where God's meaning was hidden, preparing the way for the prisoner who gave honour to God. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: Then the chief butler remembered Joseph, spoke of him to the king, and Joseph was brought forth from prison and told the two dreams before Pharaoh. Henry notes that God's time for the enlargement of his people is the fittest time, and that if the butler had freed Joseph earlier, he would likely have gone back to the Hebrews and never become the blessing to his family that he proved. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: Joseph said before Pharaoh that the two dreams were one: seven years of plenty would come over all Egypt, and after them seven years of famine such as had not been in the land. Henry stresses the goodness of God in sending the plenty before the famine, that provision might be made, and warns that we cannot be sure tomorrow shall be as today. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: Joseph counselled Pharaoh to appoint overseers in all Egypt to store up food in every city through the years of plenty, so that there would be food for the seven years of famine and the land would not perish, for the famine would be very severe. Henry notes that fair warning should always be followed by good counsel, and that we must provide accordingly for the day of trial before us. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: The Lord gave Joseph favour and mercy in Pharaoh's eyes, and Pharaoh said to his servants that no man so wise and discreet could be found, for the spirit of the Lord was with him. Henry records Pharaoh's honourable testimony: he is a man in whom the spirit of God is, and such men ought to be valued. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: Pharaoh appointed Joseph second in all his kingdom, gave him authority over all Egypt, and caused him to ride in Pharaoh's second chariot. The preferment encourages all to trust in God, Henry notes, since the prisoner of yesterday becomes the ruler of today. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: Pharaoh clothed Joseph with byssus garments, put a gold chain on his neck, had a herald proclaim before him, placed a ring on his hand, made him ruler over all his house, and said only on the throne would he be greater. Henry notes the marks of honour Pharaoh put upon Joseph, including a new name speaking the value he had for him, a revealer of secrets. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: Joseph ruled over all Egypt, and all Pharaoh's princes, servants, and king's business loved him, for he walked in uprightness, without pride or arrogance, with no respect of persons, accepting no gifts, and judging all the people in uprightness. The character of the ruler matches the wisdom of the interpreter: the same uprightness governed both. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: The land of Egypt was at peace before Pharaoh because of Joseph, the sentence beginning here and completed in the next verse. Peace under Joseph's rule was the fruit of the stored plenty and the just judgments that distributed it. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: Egypt was at peace because the Lord was with Joseph, giving him favour and mercy for all his generations before all who knew him and heard of him, and Pharaoh's kingdom was well ordered, with no Satan and no evil person therein. Henry's account of the plenty years agrees: Joseph was diligent in laying up while plenty lasted, and prudent in giving out when famine came. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: The king called Joseph's name Sephantiphans and gave him as wife the daughter of Potiphar, priest of Heliopolis, the chief cook. Henry notes the new name given by Pharaoh, Zaphnath-paaneah, a revealer of secrets, and some translate it saviour of the world, a title Henry applies in its highest sense to Christ. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: On the day Joseph stood before Pharaoh he was thirty years old. Thirteen years had passed since he was sold at seventeen, and Henry's remark stands: God's time for enlargement is the fittest time, neither early nor late. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: In that year Isaac died, the same year Joseph stood before Pharaoh. The death of the patriarch in Canaan and the exaltation of his grandson in Egypt fall together, closing one generation's story as another's opens. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: As Joseph had said in interpreting his two dreams, there were seven years of plenty over all Egypt, and the land produced abundantly, one measure producing eighteen hundred measures. Henry notes that the seven plenteous years came and were ended, and that we must not be secure in prosperity nor slothful in making good use of opportunity. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: Joseph gathered food into every city until they were full of corn beyond counting and measuring for its multitude. Henry observes that Joseph was diligent in laying up while the plenty lasted, and that the great harvests of plenty, though they seemed very much, would only just serve to keep the people alive through famine. — Matthew Henry
