Study Notes — Jubilees

Chapter 9

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Ham divided amongst his sons, and the first portion came forth for Cush towards the east, and to the west of him for Mizraim. JFB on Genesis 10:6 notes Ham's sons emigrated southward, Cush in Arabia and Mizraim in Egypt, Upper and Lower, and the division runs westward from Cush. — R. H. Charles; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: To the west of Mizraim came Put, and to the west thereof, on the sea, came Canaan. The westward order completes Ham's four sons: Cush in the east, then Mizraim, Put, and Canaan on the sea. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: Shem also divided amongst his sons, and the first portion came forth for Elam and his sons, to the east of the river Tigris. The Shemite division begins with Elam in the east, as Ham's began with Cush. — R. H. Charles

4 COMMENTARY: Elam's portion runs till it approaches the east, the whole land of India, and on the Red Sea on its coast, and the waters of Dedan, and all the mountains of Mebri and Ela, and all the land of Susan and all on the side of Pharnak to the Red Sea and the river Tina. Elam holds the far east, from India and the Red Sea coast to Susan and the Tina. — R. H. Charles

5 COMMENTARY: For Asshur came forth the second portion, all the land of Asshur and Nineveh and Shinar and to the border of India. Asshur's portion centres on Assyria, Nineveh and Shinar, reaching toward India. — R. H. Charles

6 COMMENTARY: This verse continues Asshur's portion: it ascends and skirts the river. The boundary follows the river's course, the Tigris line of verse 3. — R. H. Charles

7 COMMENTARY: This verse completes Asshur's boundary: it ascends and skirts the river. The river-skirted border closes the second Shemite portion. — R. H. Charles

8 COMMENTARY: For Arpachshad came forth the third portion, all the land of the region of the Chaldees to the east of the Euphrates, bordering on the Red Sea, and all the waters of the desert close to the tongue of the sea. JFB on Genesis 10:24 places Arphaxad's posterity in the extensive valley of Shinar on the Tigris, toward southern Mesopotamia, including the region on the east side of the river. — R. H. Charles; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: This verse continues Arpachshad's portion: the tongue of the sea which looks towards Egypt. The desert waters reach the sea-tongue facing Egypt. — R. H. Charles

10 COMMENTARY: The portion closes with all the land of Lebanon and Sanir and 'Amana to the border of the Euphrates. Lebanon, Sanir and Amanah bring Arpachshad's portion west to the Euphrates border. — R. H. Charles

11 COMMENTARY: For Aram came forth the fourth portion, all the land of Mesopotamia between the Tigris and the Euphrates, to the north of the Chaldees, to the border of the mountains of Asshur and the land of 'Arara. JFB on Genesis 10:23 notes that in the general division of the earth, Armenia, Mesopotamia and Syria fell to Aram's descendants. — R. H. Charles; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: For Lud came forth the fifth portion, the mountains of Asshur and all appertaining to them, till it reaches the Great Sea. Lud holds Asshur's mountains westward to the Great Sea. — R. H. Charles

13 COMMENTARY: This verse completes Lud's portion: till it reaches the east of Asshur his brother. Lud's land borders his brother Asshur's land on the east, closing the Shemite division. — R. H. Charles

14 COMMENTARY: Japheth also divided the land of his inheritance amongst his sons. The third division begins, matching Shem's and Ham's divisions before it. — R. H. Charles

15 COMMENTARY: The first portion came forth for Gomer to the east from the north side to the river Tina, and in the north there came forth for Magog all the inner portions of the north until it reaches the sea of Me'at. Gomer holds the east-north to the Tina, Magog the inner north to the Me'at sea. — R. H. Charles

16 COMMENTARY: This verse is the hinge to the next portion: and for Madai came forth his portion. The sentence is completed by verse 18's islands and coasts. — R. H. Charles

18 COMMENTARY: For Madai came forth as his portion that he should possess from the west of his two brothers to the islands and to the coasts of the islands. Madai holds west of his brothers, out to the islands and their coasts. — R. H. Charles

19 COMMENTARY: For Javan came forth the fourth portion, every island and the islands towards the border of Lud. Javan holds the islands, reaching toward Lud's border, the island portion of Japheth's inheritance. — R. H. Charles

20 COMMENTARY: For Tubal came forth the fifth portion, in the midst of the tongue which approaches towards the border of the portion of Lud, to the second tongue, to the region beyond the second tongue unto the third tongue. Tubal's portion is counted by tongues of land, from Lud's border through the second tongue to the third. — R. H. Charles

21 COMMENTARY: For Meshech came forth the sixth portion, all the region beyond the third tongue till it approaches the east of Gadir. Meshech holds beyond the third tongue eastward to Gadir, which Charles's note identifies with Cadiz. — R. H. Charles

22 COMMENTARY: For Tiras came forth the seventh portion, four great islands in the midst of the sea, which reach to the portion of Ham, and the islands of Kamaturi came out by lot for the sons of Arpachshad as his inheritance. Tiras holds four great islands toward Ham's portion, with the bracketed Kamaturi islands falling by lot to Arpachshad's sons. — R. H. Charles

23 COMMENTARY: Thus the sons of Noah divided unto their sons in the presence of Noah their father, and he bound them all by an oath, imprecating a curse on everyone that sought to seize the portion which had not fallen to him by his lot. The threefold division is sealed by oath and curse: seizing another's lot is accursed. — R. H. Charles

24 COMMENTARY: They all said, 'So be it; so be it,' for themselves and their sons forever throughout their generations till the day of judgment, on which the Lord God shall judge them with a sword and with fire, for all the unclean wickedness of their errors, wherewith they have filled the earth with transgression, uncleanness, fornication and sin. The double amen binds every generation till the judgment by sword and fire, the sanction behind the oath of verse 23. — R. H. Charles

25 COMMENTARY: This unit is the heading for the narrative that follows: Noah's sons led astray by evil spirits, Noah's prayer, Mastema, and the death of Noah. It divides the division of the earth from the demonology and death that open chapter 10. — R. H. Charles

No note for verse(s) 17 — the public-domain sources consulted give no distinct explanation for these verses beyond the list itself, so nothing was invented for them.
