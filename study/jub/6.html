Study Notes — Jubilees

Chapter 6

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: On the new moon of the third month Noah went forth from the ark and built an altar on that mountain. JFB on Genesis 8:20 notes the altar was literally a high place, probably a mound of earth, and Noah's first care was to return thanks for the signal mercy he and his family had experienced. — R. H. Charles; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: He made atonement for the earth and took a kid and made atonement by its blood for the guilt of the earth. The first act after the flood is atonement, not merely thanksgiving: the earth itself needs cleansing. — R. H. Charles

3 COMMENTARY: This verse completes the atonement: it was for all the guilt of the earth, for everything on it had been destroyed save those in the ark with Noah. The survivors alone are left to make atonement for a drowned world. — R. H. Charles

4 COMMENTARY: He placed the fat on the altar, and took an ox, a goat, a sheep and kids, salt, a turtle-dove and the young of a dove, placing a burnt sacrifice on the altar, pouring an offering mingled with oil, sprinkling wine and strewing frankincense over everything, causing a goodly savour acceptable before the Lord. JFB on Genesis 8:20-21 notes Noah took of every clean beast and fowl for so unparalleled a deliverance, and the sacrifice of a righteous man in faith was acceptable as the most fragrant incense; Jubilees gives the full offering system, salt, oil, wine and frankincense, before Sinai. — R. H. Charles; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: The Lord smelt the goodly savour and made a covenant that there should not be any more a flood to destroy the earth, that seed-time and harvest should never cease, and cold and heat, summer and winter, day and night should not change their order nor cease forever. JFB on Genesis 8:21-22 reads 'the Lord said in his heart' as a sworn promise that the waters of Noah should no more go over the earth, holding good while the earth remains. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: 'And you, increase ye and multiply upon the earth, and become many upon it, and be a blessing upon it.' JFB on Genesis 9:1 notes this republishes the law of nature announced to Adam, the original blessing reannounced in the very same words as at first. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: 'The fear of you and the dread of you I shall inspire in everything that is on earth and in the sea.' JFB on Genesis 9:2 explains dominion is re-established, now founded not in love and kindness as at first but in terror, a dread that keeps beasts from man's haunts except those employed in his service. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: 'Behold I have given unto you all beasts, and all winged things, and everything that moveth on the earth, and the fish in the waters, and all things for food; as the green herbs, I have given you all things to eat.' JFB on Genesis 9:3 notes man was for the first time allowed animal food, but the grant came with one restriction, given in the next verse. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: 'But flesh, with the life thereof, with the blood, ye shall not eat; for the life of all flesh is in the blood, lest your blood of your lives be required. At the hand of every man, at the hand of every beast, shall I require the blood of man.' JFB on Genesis 9:4-5 explains the prohibition as preventing the ferocity of eating flesh from living animals, and the requirement of blood as a new power for protecting life. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: 'Whoso sheddeth man's blood by man shall his blood be shed; for in the image of God made He man.' JFB on Genesis 9:6 notes the image has been injured by the fall but is not lost, so a high value attaches to the life of every man, even the poorest, and an awful criminality lies in destroying it. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: 'And you, increase ye, and multiply on the earth.' The covenant blessing is repeated to close the quoted words, framing the blood law between two blessings of fruitfulness. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: This verse opens the oath: Noah and his sons swore that they would not eat any blood in any flesh. The sentence is completed by the covenant of the next verse. — R. H. Charles

13 COMMENTARY: Noah made a covenant before the Lord God forever, throughout all the generations of the earth, in this month. The blood oath is sealed as an everlasting covenant in the third month, the month of the exodus from the ark. — R. H. Charles

14 COMMENTARY: On this account God spoke to Moses to make a covenant with Israel in this month upon the mountain with an oath, and to sprinkle blood upon them because of all the words of the covenant the Lord made with them forever. Noah's third-month blood covenant is the pattern for Sinai's third-month blood sprinkling. — R. H. Charles

15 COMMENTARY: This testimony is written concerning you, that you should observe it continually, never eating on any day any blood of beasts or birds or cattle during all the days of the earth, and the man who eats such blood during all the days of the earth, he and his seed shall be rooted out of the land. The prohibition is perpetual and its penalty reaches the seed. — R. H. Charles

16 COMMENTARY: Moses is to command Israel to eat no blood, so that their names and their seed may be before the Lord continually. Abstinence from blood keeps their names standing before God without break. — R. H. Charles

17 COMMENTARY: For this law there is no limit of days, for it is forever. Unlike the seasonal feasts, the blood law is not bounded by a calendar: it binds every day. — R. H. Charles

18 COMMENTARY: They shall observe it throughout their generations, so that they may continue supplicating on your behalf with blood before the altar, and every day, morning and evening, they shall seek forgiveness on your behalf perpetually before the Lord, that they may keep it and not be rooted out. Daily morning and evening altar blood becomes perpetual supplication and forgiveness for the people. — R. H. Charles

19 COMMENTARY: God gave Noah and his sons a sign that there should not again be a flood on the earth. The sign follows the covenant, sealing the promise that the seasons will not cease. — R. H. Charles

20 COMMENTARY: He set his bow in the cloud for a sign of the eternal covenant that there should not again be a flood on the earth to destroy it all the days of the earth. JFB on Genesis 9:13 explains 'set' as constituted or appointed: a common, familiar phenomenon made the pledge of peace, welcomed with joy whenever showers begin to fall. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

21 COMMENTARY: This verse introduces the ordinance: for this reason it is ordained and written on the heavenly tables that they should celebrate the feast of weeks. The sentence is completed by the next verse's annual renewal. — R. H. Charles

22 COMMENTARY: The feast of weeks is to be celebrated in this month once a year, to renew the covenant every year. The annual feast renews Noah's covenant, as the bow renews its sign in the cloud. — R. H. Charles

23 COMMENTARY: This whole festival was celebrated in heaven from the day of creation until the days of Noah, twenty-six jubilees and five weeks of years, and Noah and his sons observed it for seven jubilees and one week of years until Noah's death, but from his death his sons did away with it until the days of Abraham, and they ate blood. Charles notes the feast of weeks was first observed on earth in connection with Noah's covenant, designed to celebrate that covenant's institution, whereas later Judaism held it celebrated the Sinai legislation; here its observance lapses after Noah, and blood-eating returns with the lapse. — R. H. Charles

24 COMMENTARY: But Abraham observed it, and Isaac and Jacob and his children observed it up to Moses' days, and in Moses' days the children of Israel forgot it until they celebrated it anew on this mountain. The patriarchal chain preserves the feast, Israel forgets it, and Sinai restores it. — R. H. Charles

25 COMMENTARY: Moses is to command Israel to observe this festival in all their generations as a commandment: one day in the year, in this month, they shall celebrate the festival. The feast is fixed to a single annual day in the third month. — R. H. Charles

26 COMMENTARY: For it is the feast of weeks and the feast of firstfruits: this feast is twofold and of a double nature, and according to what is written and engraven concerning it they are to celebrate it. Two names mark one feast: weeks for the counting, firstfruits for the harvest it celebrates. — R. H. Charles

27 COMMENTARY: For it is written in the book of the first law, in that which was written for Moses, that he should celebrate it in its season, one day in the year, and its sacrifices were explained to him so that Israel should remember and celebrate it throughout their generations in this month, one day in every year. Charles's note explains 'the book of the first law' as the Pentateuch, in which the feast's season and sacrifices stand written. — R. H. Charles

28 COMMENTARY: This verse opens the season-days list: on the new moon of the first month, and on the new moon of the fourth month. The sentence is completed by the seventh and tenth months in verse 30. — R. H. Charles

30 COMMENTARY: On the new moon of the seventh month and on the new moon of the tenth month are the days of remembrance, and the days of the seasons in the four divisions of the year. Four new moons quarter the year, each marking a season's turn. — R. H. Charles

31 COMMENTARY: These are written and ordained as a testimony forever. The four season days stand as everlasting testimony, like the Sabbath and the blood law. — R. H. Charles

32 COMMENTARY: Noah ordained them for himself as feasts for the generations forever, so that they have become thereby a memorial unto him. Noah's personal ordinance becomes a generational memorial, tied to his own flood dates. — R. H. Charles

33 COMMENTARY: On the new moon of the first month Noah was bidden to make for himself an ark, and on that day the earth became dry and he opened the ark and saw the earth. The first month's new moon is doubly memorial: the ark's command and the earth's drying both fall on it. — R. H. Charles

34 COMMENTARY: On the new moon of the fourth month the mouths of the depths of the abysses beneath were closed. The fourth month's new moon recalls the shutting of the deep's fountains in chapter 5. — R. H. Charles

35 COMMENTARY: On the new moon of the seventh month all the mouths of the abysses of the earth were opened, and the waters began to descend into them. The seventh month's new moon recalls the waters' descent into the deep. — R. H. Charles

36 COMMENTARY: On the new moon of the tenth month the tops of the mountains were seen, and Noah was glad. The tenth month's new moon recalls the first sight of the mountains' tops and Noah's gladness. — R. H. Charles

37 COMMENTARY: On this account he ordained them for himself as feasts for a memorial forever, and thus are they ordained. The flood's dated turning points become feasts, so the calendar itself remembers the deluge. — R. H. Charles

38 COMMENTARY: They placed them on the heavenly tables, each season having thirteen weeks, and from one to another passed their memorial, from the first to the second, from the second to the third, and from the third to the fourth. Four seasons of thirteen weeks each carry the memorial in unbroken succession through the year. — R. H. Charles

39 COMMENTARY: All the days of the commandment will be two and fifty weeks of days, and these will make the entire year complete. Fifty-two weeks complete the year with nothing left over and nothing lacking. — R. H. Charles

40 COMMENTARY: Thus it is engraven and ordained on the heavenly tables. The 52-week year is engraved, not devised: the calendar, like the laws, comes from the tables. — R. H. Charles

41 COMMENTARY: This verse is the hinge to the no-neglect rule: there is no neglecting this commandment. The sentence is completed by the next verse's ban on gap years. — R. H. Charles

42 COMMENTARY: There is no neglecting this commandment for a single year or from year to year. Calendar observance admits no gap year; one missed year breaks the reckoning. — R. H. Charles

43 COMMENTARY: Moses is to command Israel to observe the years according to this reckoning, three hundred and sixty-four days, constituting a complete year, not disturbing its time from its days and feasts, for everything will fall out according to their testimony, leaving out no day and disturbing no feast. The 364-day year keeps every feast on its appointed day. — R. H. Charles

44 COMMENTARY: But if they neglect and do not observe them according to his commandment, they will disturb all their seasons, and the years will be dislodged from this order, disturbing the seasons and dislodging the years, and neglecting their ordinances. Neglect unseats seasons and years together, and ordinances fall with them. — R. H. Charles

45 COMMENTARY: All the children of Israel will forget, and will not find the path of the years, and will forget the new moons, seasons and sabbaths, and will go wrong as to all the order of the years. Forgetting the calendar means losing the year's path itself, the apostasy foretold in chapter 1. — R. H. Charles

46 COMMENTARY: For God knows and will declare it to Moses, and it is not of his own devising, for the book lies written before him, and on the heavenly tables the division of days is ordained, lest they forget the feasts of the covenant and walk according to the feasts of the Gentiles after their error and ignorance. The calendar is revealed, not invented, and its purpose is to keep Israel on covenant feasts rather than Gentile ones. — R. H. Charles

47 COMMENTARY: For there will be those who will assuredly make observations of the moon, how it disturbs the seasons and comes in from year to year ten days too soon. Charles's note explains the arithmetic: a lunar year of 354 days falls ten days short of the 364-day year, so moon reckoning shifts the seasons year by year. — R. H. Charles

48 COMMENTARY: This verse opens the consequence: for this reason the years will come upon them when they will disturb the order. The sentence is completed by verse 52's confounding of holy and unclean days. — R. H. Charles

52 COMMENTARY: They will make an abominable day the day of testimony, and an unclean day a feast day, and will confound all the days, the holy with the unclean and the unclean with the holy, for they will go wrong as to the months, sabbaths, feasts and jubilees. Moon reckoning ends in inverted holiness: abominable days kept as testimony, unclean days kept as feasts. — R. H. Charles

53 COMMENTARY: For this reason God commands and testifies to Moses that he may testify to them, for after his death his children will disturb them, so that they will not make the year three hundred and sixty-four days only, and for this reason they will go wrong as to new moons, seasons, sabbaths and festivals, and will eat all kinds of blood with all kinds of flesh. Moses' testimony anticipates the post-Mosaic disturbance, and the chapter's two laws fall together: calendar error ends in blood-eating. — R. H. Charles

No note for verse(s) 29, 49, 50, 51 — the public-domain sources consulted give no distinct explanation for these verses beyond the list itself, so nothing was invented for them.
