Study Notes — Jubilees

Chapter 22

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: In the first week in the forty-fourth jubilee, in the second year, the year in which Abraham died, Isaac and Ishmael came from the Well of the Oath to celebrate the feast of weeks, the feast of the first-fruits of the harvest, to Abraham their father, and Abraham rejoiced because his two sons had come. The last festival gathers both sons to Abraham in the year of his death. — R. H. Charles

2 COMMENTARY: Isaac had many possessions in Beersheba, and Isaac was wont to go and see his possessions and to return to his father. Isaac's comings from Beersheba to his father frame the gathering. — R. H. Charles

3 COMMENTARY: In those days Ishmael came to see his father, and they both came together, and Isaac offered a sacrifice for a burnt-offering, and presented it on the altar of his father which he had made in Hebron. Both sons come together, and Isaac offers a burnt-offering on Abraham's own altar at Hebron. — R. H. Charles

4 COMMENTARY: He offered a thank-offering and made a feast of joy before Ishmael his brother, and Rebecca made new cakes from the new grain, and gave them to Jacob her son, to take them to Abraham his father, from the first-fruits of the land, that he might eat and bless the Creator of all things before he died. The thank-offering feast of joy is joined by Rebekah's new cakes from the new grain, carried by Jacob as first-fruits for Abraham to eat and bless the Creator before his death. — R. H. Charles

5 COMMENTARY: Isaac, too, sent by the hand of Jacob to Abraham a best thank-offering, that he might eat and drink. Isaac sends his best thank-offering by Jacob's hand, that Abraham might eat and drink. — R. H. Charles

6 COMMENTARY: He ate and drank, and blessed the Most High God. Abraham eats and drinks and blesses the Most High God, whose praise follows in the next verse. — R. H. Charles

7 COMMENTARY: 'Who hath created heaven and earth, who hath made all the fat things of the earth, and given them to the children of men that they might eat and drink and bless their Creator.' The Most High created heaven and earth, made all the fat things of the earth, and gave them to men to eat and drink and bless their Creator. — R. H. Charles

8 COMMENTARY: 'And now I give thanks unto thee, my God, because thou hast caused me to see this day: behold, I am one hundred three score and fifteen years, an old man and full of days and all my days have been unto me peace.' Thanksgiving for seeing this day: 175 years, an old man full of days, all his days peace. — R. H. Charles

9 COMMENTARY: 'The sword of the adversary hath not overcome me in all that thou hast given me and my children all the days of my life until this day.' The adversary's sword has not overcome him in all God gave him and his children, all his life until this day. — R. H. Charles

10 COMMENTARY: 'My God, may thy mercy and thy peace be upon thy servant, and upon the seed of his sons, that they may be to thee a chosen nation and an inheritance from amongst all the nations of the earth from henceforth unto all the days of the generations of the earth, unto all the ages.' Mercy and peace are prayed upon the servant and the seed of his sons, that they may be a chosen nation and inheritance from among all nations henceforth unto all ages. — R. H. Charles

11 COMMENTARY: This unit is the heading for the narrative that follows: Abraham's last words to and blessings of Jacob. It divides the thanksgiving prayer from the blessing of Jacob. — R. H. Charles

12 COMMENTARY: He called Jacob and said: 'My son Jacob, may the God of all bless thee and strengthen thee to do righteousness, and his will before him, and may he choose thee and thy seed that ye may become a people for his inheritance according to his will alway.' The God of all is to bless and strengthen Jacob to do righteousness and God's will, choosing him and his seed as a people for God's inheritance according to his will always. — R. H. Charles

13 COMMENTARY: 'And do thou, my son, Jacob, draw near and kiss me.' The blessing is sealed with a kiss: draw near and kiss me. — R. H. Charles

14 COMMENTARY: He drew near and kissed him, and he said. Jacob draws near and kisses Abraham, and the blessing poem begins. — R. H. Charles

15 COMMENTARY: 'Blessed be my son Jacob.' The blessing opens: blessed be Jacob. — R. H. Charles

16 COMMENTARY: 'And all the sons of God Most High, unto all the ages.' Blessing extends to all the sons of God Most High, unto all ages. — R. H. Charles

17 COMMENTARY: 'May God give unto thee a seed of righteousness.' God is to give Jacob a seed of righteousness. — R. H. Charles

18 COMMENTARY: 'And some of thy sons may he sanctify in the midst of the whole earth.' Some of Jacob's sons are to be sanctified in the midst of the whole earth. — R. H. Charles

19 COMMENTARY: 'May nations serve thee.' Nations are to serve Jacob. — R. H. Charles

20 COMMENTARY: 'And all the nations bow themselves before thy seed.' All nations are to bow before Jacob's seed. — R. H. Charles

21 COMMENTARY: 'Be strong in the presence of men.' Jacob is to be strong in men's presence. — R. H. Charles

22 COMMENTARY: 'And exercise authority over all the seed of Seth.' Jacob is to exercise authority over all the seed of Seth. — R. H. Charles

23 COMMENTARY: 'Then thy ways and the ways of thy sons will be.' Then Jacob's ways and his sons' ways will be justified, completed by the next verse. — R. H. Charles

24 COMMENTARY: 'Justified, so that they shall become a holy nation.' Ways justified, so that they become a holy nation. — R. H. Charles

25 COMMENTARY: 'May the Most High God give thee all the blessings wherewith he hath blessed me.' The Most High is to give Jacob all the blessings wherewith he blessed Abraham, and Noah and Adam, completed by the next verse. — R. H. Charles

26 COMMENTARY: 'And wherewith he blessed Noah and Adam; may they rest on the sacred head of thy seed from generation to generation for ever.' The blessings of Noah and Adam are to rest on the sacred head of Jacob's seed from generation to generation forever. — R. H. Charles

27 COMMENTARY: 'And may he cleanse thee from all unrighteous-.' Cleansing from all unrighteousness and impurity is prayed, completed by the next verse. — R. H. Charles

28 COMMENTARY: 'Ness and impurity.' Cleansing from all unrighteousness and impurity. — R. H. Charles

29 COMMENTARY: 'That thou mayest be forgiven all thy transgressions; and thy sins of ignorance.' Forgiveness is prayed for all transgressions and sins of ignorance. — R. H. Charles

30 COMMENTARY: 'And may he strengthen thee.' Strengthening is prayed for Jacob. — R. H. Charles

31 COMMENTARY: 'And bless thee.' Blessing is prayed for Jacob. — R. H. Charles

32 COMMENTARY: 'And mayest thou inherit the whole earth.' Jacob is to inherit the whole earth. — R. H. Charles

33 COMMENTARY: 'And may he renew his covenant with thee, that thou mayest be to him a nation for his.' Covenant renewal is prayed, that Jacob may be a nation for God's inheritance, completed by the next verse. — R. H. Charles

34 COMMENTARY: 'Inheritance for all the ages, and that he may be to thee and to thy seed a God in truth and righteousness throughout all the days of the earth.' The nation for his inheritance for all ages, and God to Jacob and his seed in truth and righteousness throughout all the days of the earth. — R. H. Charles

35 COMMENTARY: 'And do thou, my son Jacob, remember my words, and observe the commandments of Abraham, thy.' Jacob is to remember Abraham's words and observe his commandments, beginning with separation, completed by the next verse. — R. H. Charles

36 COMMENTARY: 'Father: separate thyself from the nations, and eat not with them: and do not according to their works, and become not their associate; for their works are unclean.' Separate from the nations, eat not with them, do not according to their works, become not their associate, for their works are unclean. — R. H. Charles

37 COMMENTARY: 'And all their ways are a pollution and an abomination and uncleanness.' All their ways are pollution, abomination and uncleanness. — R. H. Charles

38 COMMENTARY: 'They offer their sacrifices to the dead and they worship evil spirits.' Their sacrifices go to the dead, and they worship evil spirits. — R. H. Charles

39 COMMENTARY: 'And they eat over the graves.' They eat over the graves. — R. H. Charles

40 COMMENTARY: 'And all their works are vanity and nothingness.' All their works are vanity and nothingness. — R. H. Charles

41 COMMENTARY: 'They have no heart to understand.' They have no heart to understand. — R. H. Charles

42 COMMENTARY: 'And their eyes do not see what their works are, and how they err in saying to a piece of wood.' Their eyes do not see what their works are, nor how they err in calling wood their god, completed by the next verse. — R. H. Charles

43 COMMENTARY: "'Thou art my God,' and to a stone: 'Thou art my Lord and thou art my deliverer.' And they have no heart." To wood they say 'Thou art my God,' to stone 'Thou art my Lord and my deliverer,' and they have no heart. — R. H. Charles

44 COMMENTARY: 'And as for thee, my son Jacob, may the Most High God help thee and the God of heaven bless thee.' For Jacob, the Most High God is to help and the God of heaven to bless, completed by the next verse. — R. H. Charles

45 COMMENTARY: 'And remove thee from their uncleanness and from all their error.' God is to remove Jacob from their uncleanness and from all their error. — R. H. Charles

46 COMMENTARY: 'Be thou ware, my son Jacob, of taking a wife.' Beware of taking a wife from Canaan's seed, completed by the next verse. — R. H. Charles

47 COMMENTARY: 'From any seed of the daughters of Canaan; for all his seed is to be rooted out of the earth.' No wife from any seed of Canaan's daughters, for all his seed is to be rooted out of the earth. — R. H. Charles

48 COMMENTARY: 'For, owing to the transgression of Ham, Canaan.' Owing to Ham's transgression, Canaan erred, completed by the next verse. — R. H. Charles

49 COMMENTARY: 'Erred and all his seed will be destroyed from off the.' Canaan erred, and all his seed will be destroyed from off the earth, completed by the next verse. — R. H. Charles

50 COMMENTARY: 'Earth and all the residue thereof, and none springing from him will be saved on.' Earth and all its residue, and none springing from him saved on the day of judgment, completed by the next verse. — R. H. Charles

51 COMMENTARY: 'The day of judgment.' None springing from Canaan will be saved on the day of judgment. — R. H. Charles

52 COMMENTARY: 'And as for all the worshippers of idols and the.' For all idol worshippers and the profane, there is no hope in the land of the living, completed by the next verses. — R. H. Charles

53 COMMENTARY: 'Profane: there will be no hope for them in the land of the living.' Idol worshippers and the profane have no hope in the land of the living. — R. H. Charles

54 COMMENTARY: 'And there will be no remembrance of them on.' No remembrance of them on earth, and descent into Sheol and the place of condemnation, completed by the next verses; the lettering in the text marks lines reordered in the source. — R. H. Charles

55 COMMENTARY: 'The earth; for they will descend into Sheol, and into the place of condemnation will they go as the children of Sodom were taken away from.' They descend into Sheol and the place of condemnation, as Sodom's children were taken from the earth, completed by the next verse. — R. H. Charles

56 COMMENTARY: 'The earth so will all those who worship idols be taken away.' As Sodom's children were taken from the earth, so will all idol worshippers be taken away. — R. H. Charles

57 COMMENTARY: 'Fear not, my son Jacob.' Fear not, my son Jacob, completed by the next verses. — R. H. Charles

58 COMMENTARY: 'And be not dismayed, son of Abraham: may the Most High God preserve thee from.' Be not dismayed, son of Abraham: may the Most High preserve from destruction, completed by the next verse. — R. H. Charles

59 COMMENTARY: 'Destruction, and from all the paths of error may he deliver.' Preservation from destruction and deliverance from all paths of error, completed by the next verse. — R. H. Charles

60 COMMENTARY: 'Thee.' Deliverance from all paths of error for thee. — R. H. Charles

61 COMMENTARY: 'This house have I built for myself that I might put my name upon it in the earth: it is given to thee and to thy seed for ever, and it will be named the house of Abraham; it is given to thee and to thy seed for ever; for thou wilt build my house and establish my name before God for ever: thy seed and thy name will stand throughout all generations of the earth.' The house Abraham built to put his name upon it in the earth is given to Jacob and his seed forever, named the house of Abraham, for Jacob will build his house and establish his name before God forever, seed and name standing through all generations. — R. H. Charles

62 COMMENTARY: And he ceased commanding him and blessing him. The commanding and blessing cease. — R. H. Charles

63 COMMENTARY: And the two lay together on one bed, and Jacob slept in the bosom of Abraham, his father's father, and he kissed him seven times, and his affection and his heart rejoiced over him. The two lie together on one bed, Jacob sleeping in his grandfather's bosom, kissed seven times, affection and heart rejoicing over him. — R. H. Charles

64 COMMENTARY: And he blessed him with all his heart and said: 'The Most High God, the God of all, and Creator of all, who brought me forth from Ur of the Chaldees, that he might give me this land to inherit it for ever, and that I might establish a holy seed, blessed be the Most High for ever.' With all his heart Abraham blesses: the Most High God, God of all, Creator of all, who brought him from Ur of the Chaldees to give him this land forever and establish a holy seed, blessed be the Most High forever. — R. H. Charles

65 COMMENTARY: And he blessed Jacob and said: 'My son, over whom with all my heart and my affection I rejoice, may thy grace and thy mercy.' The final blessing opens: over Jacob, whom he rejoices in with all heart and affection, grace and mercy are prayed, completed by the next verse. — R. H. Charles

66 COMMENTARY: 'Be lift up upon him and upon his seed alway.' Grace and mercy lifted up upon Jacob and his seed always. — R. H. Charles

67 COMMENTARY: 'And do not forsake him, nor set him at nought from henceforth unto the days of eternity, and may thine eyes be opened upon him and upon his seed, that thou mayest preserve him, and bless him, and mayest sanctify him as a nation for thine inheritance; and bless him with all thy blessings from henceforth unto all the days of eternity, and renew thy covenant and thy grace with him and with his seed according to all thy good pleasure unto all the generations of the earth.' Do not forsake or set him at nought henceforth unto eternity; eyes opened upon him and his seed to preserve, bless and sanctify him as a nation for God's inheritance, with all blessings henceforth unto all days of eternity, covenant and grace renewed with him and his seed according to all God's good pleasure unto all generations of the earth. — R. H. Charles

68 COMMENTARY: This unit is the heading for the narrative that follows: the death and burial of Abraham. It divides the blessing of Jacob from Abraham's death in chapter 23. — R. H. Charles
