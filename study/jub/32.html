Study Notes — Jubilees

Chapter 32

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Levi dreamed at Bethel that they had ordained and made him priest of the Most High God, him and his sons for ever, and he awoke and blessed the Lord. The dream confirms at Bethel what Isaac's blessing had spoken: Levi's line would serve before God continually. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: Jacob rose early on the fourteenth of the month and gave a tithe of all that came with him, men, cattle, gold, vessels, and garments, tithes of all. Henry notes that the tenth is a fit proportion to devote to God, and that Jacob's vow at Bethel is now paid in full. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: In those days Rachel became pregnant with her son Benjamin. The pregnancy comes late in the journey, after the Bethel vow, and its end will bring both joy and grief to the household. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: Jacob counted his sons from the youngest upwards, and Levi fell to the portion of the Lord, so his father clothed him in the garments of the priesthood and filled his hands. The counting is a lot cast for God's portion, and Levi's investiture makes the dream and the blessing visible. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: On the fifteenth of the month Jacob brought to the altar fourteen oxen, twenty-eight rams, forty-nine sheep, seven lambs, and twenty-one kids as a burnt offering, well pleasing for a sweet savour before God. The scale of the offering matches the scale of the deliverance it thanked God for. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: This was Jacob's offering in consequence of the vow he had vowed, to give a tenth with fruit-offerings and drink-offerings. Henry stresses that a vow must not be deferred, and Jacob's offering is the payment of the Bethel vow made when he fled from Esau. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: The offering was given in consequence of the vow, tying the sacrifice to the promise made at Bethel. Vow and offering belong together: the promise made in trouble is paid in safety. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: When the fire had consumed the burnt offering, Jacob burnt incense and offered a thank-offering of oxen, rams, sheep, he-goats, and yearling sheep and kids, daily for seven days. The seven days of thanksgiving turn the vow-payment into a feast, not a bare transaction. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: Jacob, his sons, and his men ate with joy for seven days, blessing and thanking the Lord who had delivered him out of all his tribulation and given him his vow. Henry notes that the comfort of holy ordinances lies in meeting God in them, and the feast is the household's joy in that meeting. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: Jacob tithed all the clean animals and made a burnt sacrifice, but the unclean animals he did not give to Levi, and he gave him all the souls of the men. The distinction keeps the priest's portion holy, clean animals for sacrifice and men for service, while the unclean are excluded. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: Levi discharged the priestly office at Bethel before his father, in preference to his ten brothers, and Jacob gave his vow, tithing the tithe again and sanctifying it. Henry's reading of Levi's blessing fits: the tribe set apart to God as priests began its service here, before Jacob, at Bethel. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: On the following night, on the twenty-second day of the month, Jacob resolved to build that place, surround the court with a wall, and sanctify it for ever for himself and his children. The resolve shows Jacob's desire to make the place of his vision a permanent sanctuary. — Matthew Henry

20 COMMENTARY: The Lord appeared to Jacob by night, blessed him, and said his name would no longer be Jacob but Israel. Henry notes that God appeared to Jacob at Bethel, renewed the covenant, and changed his name: Jacob the supplanter became Israel, a prince with God. — Matthew Henry

21 COMMENTARY: God said he was the Lord who created heaven and earth, and would increase and multiply Jacob exceedingly, with kings coming forth from him. Henry notes the two promises renewed at Bethel: a great nation and a good land, with a spiritual sense, Christ the promised Seed and heaven the promised land. — Matthew Henry

22 COMMENTARY: God promised Jacob's seed all the earth under heaven, judging the nations and at last possessing the whole earth and inheriting it for ever. The promise widens the land of Canaan into a universal inheritance, the full scope of the covenant with Abraham. — Matthew Henry

23 COMMENTARY: God finished speaking and went up from Jacob, who looked until he had ascended into heaven. The ascending God closes the Bethel theophany as the ladder had opened it: heaven and earth meet, and then part, leaving Jacob with the promise. — Matthew Henry

27 COMMENTARY: The angel commanded Jacob to go to the house of Abraham his father and dwell with Isaac his father until the day of his father's death. Henry's account of Genesis 35 records the fulfilment: Jacob came to Isaac at Hebron, and Isaac's death and burial by his two sons closed the patriarch's life. — Matthew Henry

35 COMMENTARY: On the night of the twenty-third of the month Deborah, Rebecca's nurse, died, and they buried her beneath the city under the oak of the river, calling the place the river of Deborah and the oak the oak of Deborah's mourning. Henry notes that Deborah died much lamented, and that old servants in a family who have been faithful and useful ought to be respected. — Matthew Henry

36 COMMENTARY: Rebecca went and returned to her house to Isaac, and Jacob sent by her hand rams, sheep, and he-goats for a meal for his father such as he desired. The provision honours the aged father, the same care Henry finds in Jacob's household throughout his return. — Matthew Henry

38 COMMENTARY: Rachel bore a son in the night and called him son of my sorrow, for she suffered in giving him birth, but his father called him Benjamin, on the eleventh of the eighth month in the first of the sixth week of this jubilee. Henry explains the two names: Ben-oni, son of sorrow, from the mother's dying lips, changed by Jacob to Benjamin, son of the right hand, so that sorrow would not be renewed every time the boy was called. — Matthew Henry

39 COMMENTARY: Rachel died there and was buried in Ephrath, that is Bethlehem, and Jacob built a pillar on her grave on the road above it. Henry notes that Rachel, who had passionately said give me children or else I die, now died having children, and that God alone knows what is best for his people. — Matthew Henry

No note for verse(s) 12, 13, 14, 15, 16, 17, 18, 24, 25, 26, 28, 29, 30, 31, 32, 33, 34, 37 — the public-domain sources consulted give no distinct explanation for these verses beyond the list itself, so nothing was invented for them.
