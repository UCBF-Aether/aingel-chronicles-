Study Notes — Jubilees

Chapter 50

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: After this law the angel made known to Moses the days of the Sabbaths in the desert of Sin, between Elim and Sinai. Henry notes that the fourth commandment's form, Remember, shows the Sabbath was not first given at Sinai but was known by the people before, and that one day in seven is to be kept holy. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: The angel told Moses of the Sabbaths of the land on Mount Sinai, and of the jubilee years in the sabbaths of years, but the year of the jubilee was not told until Israel entered the land to be possessed. Henry notes that all labour was to cease in the seventh year, as daily labour ceases on the seventh day, and that the jubilee of the fiftieth year proclaimed liberty, lands returning to their families. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: The land also will keep its sabbaths while Israel dwells upon it, and they will know the jubilee year. Henry observes that the year of rest taught willing dependence on God's providence, with Israel as the Lord's tenants and stewards, and that the jubilee preserved tribes and families distinct till the Messiah's coming. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: The angel ordained for Moses the year-weeks, the years, and the jubilees: forty-nine jubilees from Adam until this day, and one week and two years, with yet forty years to come for learning the commandments. Henry's account of the jubilee structure fits the reckoning: sabbaths of years counted toward the fiftieth year of liberty, and the forty years are the wilderness schooling before the land. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: The forty years are described as distant, years for learning the commandments of the Lord. The wilderness generation would learn the law before possessing the land, the schooling preceding the inheritance. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: The learning would last until Israel passed over into Canaan, crossing the Jordan to the west. The crossing is the goal of the forty years: commandments learned in the desert, kept in the land. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: The jubilees will pass until Israel is cleansed from all guilt of fornication, uncleanness, pollution, sin, and error, dwelling with confidence in all the land, with no more Satan or evil one, and the land clean from that time for evermore. Henry notes that the jubilee typified redemption by Christ from the slavery of sin and Satan, and being brought again to the liberty of the children of God, the cleansing this verse awaits. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: Behold the commandment regarding the Sabbaths, written down for Moses, with all the judgments of its laws. The Sabbath law is presented as a written code, its judgments spelled out in the verses that follow. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: Six days shalt thou labour, but on the seventh day is the Sabbath of the Lord thy God. Henry notes that six days are allotted to worldly business, but not so as to neglect God's service and the soul's care, and that on those days we must do all our work and leave none to be done on the Sabbath day. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: In the Sabbath ye shall do no manner of work, ye, your sons, menservants, maidservants, all cattle, and the sojourner with you. Henry notes that the Sabbath rest covers the whole household, and that the day is blessed, with men blessed by it and in it, the blessing spoken of the Sabbath day itself. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: The man who does any work on the Sabbath shall die: whoever desecrates the day, lies with his wife, says he will do something on it, sets out on a journey regarding buying or selling, draws water he had not prepared on the sixth day, or takes up any burden to carry out of tent or house, shall die. Henry lists the same forbidden works, trading, paying wages, settling accounts, journeys, and worldly business, as not keeping the day holy, and notes that the wages of sin is death; Jubilees attaches the death sentence to the whole list in its severer halakha. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: Ye shall do no work whatever on the Sabbath save what ye prepared for yourselves on the sixth day, so as to eat, drink, rest, and keep Sabbath from all work, blessing the Lord who gave a festival day and a holy day, a day of the holy kingdom for all Israel among their days for ever. Henry notes that we must do all our work beforehand and leave none for the Sabbath, and that the Sabbath should be a day of rest from worldly labour and rest in the service of God. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: Great is the honour the Lord has given Israel, that they should eat, drink, and be satisfied on this festival day, resting from all labour belonging to the labour of men, save burning frankincense and bringing oblations and sacrifices before the Lord for days and Sabbaths. Henry notes that the Sabbath's advantages, to health, happiness, and the soul's care, show the commandment's excellency, and that works of piety, unlike works of luxury or business, belong to the day. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: This work alone shall be done on Sabbath-days in the sanctuary of the Lord, that they may atone for Israel with sacrifice continually from day to day, as a memorial well pleasing before the Lord, receiving them always as commanded. Henry allows works of necessity, charity, and piety on the Sabbath, for the Sabbath was made for man, and the sanctuary's continual sacrifice is the piety the day requires. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: Every man who does any work on the Sabbath, goes a journey, tills his farm at home or elsewhere, lights a fire, rides any beast, travels by ship on the sea, strikes or kills anything, slaughters beast or bird, catches animal, bird, or fish, fasts, or makes war on the Sabbaths, breaks the law. Henry's forbidden list covers journeys, trading, and worldly business, and Jubilees extends it to fire, riding, sailing, hunting, fishing, fasting, and war, the fullest form of its Sabbath strictness. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: The man who does any of these things on the Sabbath shall die, so that Israel shall observe the Sabbaths according to the commandments regarding the Sabbaths of the land, as written in the tables given into Moses' hands, that he should write out the laws of the seasons and the seasons according to the division of their days. Henry notes that the land's sabbath year taught dependence on providence, and that the Sabbath laws, like the land laws, were given to be kept as written, not varied, since to omit or vary anything in God's law is sin. — Matthew Henry

No note for verse(s) 17 — the public-domain sources consulted give no distinct explanation for these verses beyond the list itself, so nothing was invented for them.
