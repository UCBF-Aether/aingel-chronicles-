Study Notes — Jubilees

Chapter 8

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: In the twenty-ninth jubilee, in the first week, in the beginning thereof, Arpachshad took Rasu'eja, daughter of Susan, daughter of Elam, to wife, and she bare him a son in the third year in this week, and he called his name Kainam. The Shemite line continues through Arpachshad, married into Elam, with Kainam's birth dated to week and year. — R. H. Charles

2 COMMENTARY: This verse opens Kainam's upbringing: the son grew, and his father taught him writing. The sentence is completed by verse 6, where the taught youth goes seeking a city to seize. — R. H. Charles

6 COMMENTARY: He went to seek for himself a place where he might seize for himself a city. The youth taught writing turns to seizure: literacy and ambition arrive together in Kainam. — R. H. Charles

7 COMMENTARY: He found a writing which former generations had carved on the rock, and he read what was thereon, and he transcribed it and sinned owing to it, for it contained the teaching of the Watchers, in accordance with which they used to observe the omens of the sun, moon and stars in all the signs of heaven. Kainam's sin is the recovery of forbidden Watcher teaching, astrology by sun, moon and stars, in contrast to Enoch's licit heavenly writing. — R. H. Charles

8 COMMENTARY: He wrote it down and said nothing regarding it, for he was afraid to speak to Noah about it lest Noah should be angry with him on account of it. Concealment betrays knowledge of guilt: the transcription is hidden from the patriarch. — R. H. Charles

9 COMMENTARY: In the thirtieth jubilee, in the second week, in the first year thereof, he took Melka, daughter of Madai, son of Japheth, to wife, and in the fourth year he begat a son and called his name Shelah, saying, 'Truly I have been sent.' Shelah's name, 'sent,' is explained by his father's own words, and the marriage crosses into Japheth's line. — R. H. Charles

10 COMMENTARY: In the fourth year he was born, and Shelah grew up and took Mu'ak, daughter of Kesed, his father's brother, in the thirty-first jubilee, in the fifth week, in the first year thereof. The bracketed birth year and the dated marriage carry the line from Kainam to Shelah. — R. H. Charles

11 COMMENTARY: She bare him a son in the fifth year thereof, and he called his name Eber, and he took unto himself a wife, Azurad, daughter of Nebrod, in the thirty-second jubilee, in the seventh week, in the third year thereof. Eber's birth and marriage continue the dated genealogy toward Peleg. — R. H. Charles

12 COMMENTARY: In the sixth year thereof she bare him a son, and he called his name Peleg, for in the days when he was born the children of Noah began to divide the earth amongst themselves, and for this reason he called his name Peleg. JFB on Genesis 10:25 notes Peleg's name was given in memory of the division, made by divine instruction probably through Eber, and cites Deuteronomy 32:8 and Acts 17:26 for God apportioning the nations. — R. H. Charles; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: They divided the earth secretly amongst themselves. A first, secret division precedes the public, lot-cast division under Noah in verse 15. — R. H. Charles

14 COMMENTARY: They told it to Noah. The secret division is disclosed to the patriarch, who will then oversee the division by lot. — R. H. Charles

15 COMMENTARY: In the beginning of the thirty-third jubilee they divided the earth into three parts, for Shem, Ham and Japheth, according to the inheritance of each, in the first year in the first week, when one of the angels who had been sent was with them. JFB on Genesis 10:32 notes the division was made in the most orderly manner, the sons ranged by nations, every nation assigned its territory; Jubilees dates the formal division and sets an angel as witness. — R. H. Charles; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: He called his sons, and they drew nigh to him, they and their children, and he divided the earth into the lots which his three sons were to take in possession, and they reached forth their hands and took the writing out of the bosom of Noah, their father. The lots are drawn as a writing from Noah's bosom, so the division is received, not seized. — R. H. Charles

17 COMMENTARY: There came forth on the writing as Shem's lot the middle of the earth, which he should take as an inheritance for himself and his sons for the generations of eternity, from the middle of the mountain range of Rafa, from the mouth of the water from the river Tina, his portion going west through the midst of this river until it reaches the water of the abysses, out of which this river goes forth and pours its waters into the sea Me'at, and this river flows into the great sea. Shem receives the middle earth, bounded by the Rafa range and the Tina river system running to the great sea. — R. H. Charles

18 COMMENTARY: All that is towards the north is Japheth's, and all that is towards the south belongs to Shem. The verse fixes the orientation of Shem's middle lot: Japheth to the north, the south to Shem's side of the division. — R. H. Charles

19 COMMENTARY: His portion extends till it reaches Karaso, in the bosom of the tongue which looks towards the south. Karaso on the southward tongue marks a boundary point of Shem's lot. — R. H. Charles

20 COMMENTARY: His portion extends along the great sea, in a straight line till it reaches the west of the tongue which looks towards the south, for this sea is named the tongue of the Egyptian Sea. The western boundary runs along the great sea to the tongue of the Egyptian Sea. — R. H. Charles

21 COMMENTARY: It turns from here towards the south, towards the mouth of the great sea on the shore of its waters, and extends west to 'Afra, and till it reaches the waters of the river Gihon, and to the south of the waters of Gihon, to the banks of this river. The southern boundary runs by 'Afra to the Gihon river's banks. — R. H. Charles

22 COMMENTARY: It extends towards the east till it reaches the Garden of Eden, to the south thereof, and from the east of the whole land of Eden and of the whole east, it turns to the east and proceeds till it reaches the east of the mountain named Rafa, and descends to the bank of the mouth of the river Tina. The eastern boundary returns by Eden to Rafa and the Tina's mouth, closing Shem's circuit where it began. — R. H. Charles

23 COMMENTARY: This portion came forth by lot for Shem and his sons, that they should possess it forever unto his generations forevermore. The lot makes Shem's portion a perpetual inheritance, not a temporary camp. — R. H. Charles

24 COMMENTARY: This verse opens Noah's joy: he rejoiced that this portion came forth for Shem and his sons. The sentence is completed by the prophecy remembered in the next verses. — R. H. Charles

25 COMMENTARY: This verse continues Noah's rejoicing over Shem's portion and his sons. The joy is explained by the remembered prophecy that follows. — R. H. Charles

26 COMMENTARY: He remembered all that he had spoken with his mouth in prophecy, for he had said, 'Blessed be the Lord God of Shem, and may the Lord dwell in the dwelling of Shem.' The lot fulfils Noah's blessing of chapter 7: Shem's middle portion is the dwelling where the Lord will dwell. — R. H. Charles

27 COMMENTARY: This verse gives the prophecy itself, quoted from chapter 7: blessed be the Lord God of Shem, and may the Lord dwell in Shem's dwelling. The blessing is now seen fulfilled in the lot. — R. H. Charles

28 COMMENTARY: He knew that the Garden of Eden is the holy of holies and the dwelling of the Lord, and Mount Sinai the centre of the desert, and Mount Zion the centre of the navel of the earth, and these three were created as holy places facing each other. Eden, Sinai and Zion form a triad of holy places, centres of garden, desert and earth, facing one another; chapter 4 adds the Mount of the East as a fourth. — R. H. Charles

29 COMMENTARY: He blessed the God of gods, who had put the word of the Lord into his mouth, and the Lord forevermore. Noah blesses God for the prophetic word now fulfilled in the lot. — R. H. Charles

30 COMMENTARY: This verse opens the catalogue: he knew that a blessed portion and a blessing had come to Shem and his sons unto the generations forever. The sentence is completed by the lands list of the next verse. — R. H. Charles

31 COMMENTARY: The blessed portion is the whole land of Eden and the whole land of the Red Sea, the whole land of the east, India, the Red Sea and its mountains, all the land of Bashan, Lebanon, the islands of Kaftur, the mountains of Sanir and 'Amana, the mountains of Asshur in the north, all the land of Elam, Asshur, Babel, Susan and Ma'edai, all the mountains of Ararat, and all the region beyond the sea beyond the mountains of Asshur towards the north, a blessed and spacious land, all in it very good. Shem's catalogue runs from Eden and the Red Sea east to India, and north through Bashan, Lebanon, Elam, Asshur, Babel, Susan, Media and Ararat to the beyond-sea north, a spacious land pronounced very good. — R. H. Charles

32 COMMENTARY: For Ham came forth the second portion, beyond the Gihon towards the south to the right of the Garden, extending south to all the mountains of fire, west to the sea of 'Atel, and west till it reaches the sea of Ma'uk, that sea into which everything which is not destroyed descends. Ham receives the south beyond the Gihon, to the mountains of fire and the western seas; Charles's notes gloss the sea of Ma'uk as the great ocean stream in the extreme west. — R. H. Charles

33 COMMENTARY: It goes forth towards the north to the limits of Gadir, and to the coast of the waters of the sea, to the waters of the great sea, till it draws near to the river Gihon, and goes along the river Gihon till it reaches the right of the Garden of Eden. Ham's boundary returns north by Gadir, which Charles's note identifies with Cadiz, along the great sea to the Gihon and Eden's right side. — R. H. Charles

34 COMMENTARY: This verse introduces Ham's portion as the land which came forth for him to occupy forever for himself and his sons unto their generations. The sentence is completed by verse 42's 'forever.' — R. H. Charles

42 COMMENTARY: This verse completes Ham's portion: for himself and his sons unto their generations forever. Like Shem's, Ham's lot is a perpetual inheritance, and Charles's summary notes it covers all Africa and certain parts of Asia. — R. H. Charles

43 COMMENTARY: For Japheth came forth the third portion beyond the river Tina to the north of the outflow of its waters, extending north-easterly to the whole region of Gog and to all the country east thereof. Japheth receives the north beyond the Tina, toward Gog's region and the eastern country. — R. H. Charles

44 COMMENTARY: It extends northerly to the north, to the mountains of Qelt towards the north, towards the sea of Ma'uk, and goes forth to the east of Gadir as far as the region of the waters of the sea. Japheth's boundary runs by the Qelt mountains and the Ma'uk sea to the east of Gadir, Cadiz's region. — R. H. Charles

45 COMMENTARY: It extends until it approaches the west of Fara, and returns towards 'Aferag, and extends easterly to the waters of the sea of Me'at. The circuit turns at Fara and 'Aferag back east to the Me'at sea. — R. H. Charles

46 COMMENTARY: It extends to the region of the river Tina in a north-easterly direction until it approaches the boundary of its waters towards the mountain Rafa, and turns round towards the north. The circuit closes at the Tina and Rafa, mirroring Shem's starting point and turning north. — R. H. Charles

47 COMMENTARY: This is the land which came forth for Japheth and his sons as the portion of his inheritance which he should possess for himself and his sons, for their generations forever: five great islands, and a great land in the north. Japheth's perpetual portion is five great islands and a great northern land, matching JFB's isles of the Gentiles for Japheth's settlements. — R. H. Charles; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

48 COMMENTARY: But it is cold, and the land of Ham is hot, and the land of Shem is neither hot nor cold, but of blended cold and heat. Climate matches portion: Japheth's north is cold, Ham's south hot, and Shem's middle temperate, blended cold and heat. — R. H. Charles

No note for verse(s) 3, 4, 5, 35, 36, 37, 38, 39, 40, 41 — the public-domain sources consulted give no distinct explanation for these verses beyond the list itself, so nothing was invented for them.
