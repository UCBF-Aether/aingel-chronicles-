Study Notes — Jubilees

Chapter 7

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: In the seventh week, in the first year thereof, in this jubilee, Noah planted vines on the mountain where the ark had rested, named Lubar, one of the Ararat mountains. The vineyard rises on the grounding mountain itself, and the planting is dated to week and year. — R. H. Charles

2 COMMENTARY: The vines produced fruit in the fourth year, and Noah guarded their fruit. The fourth-year fruit matters: verse 49 will make fourth-year fruit holy, and Noah's vines follow that rule from the start. — R. H. Charles

3 COMMENTARY: He gathered the fruit in this year, in the seventh month. The gathering falls in the seventh month, the harvest season of the book's calendar. — R. H. Charles

4 COMMENTARY: He made wine from the fruit and put it into a vessel. The first wine after the flood is made from Lubar's vines, kept for a set time before use. — R. H. Charles

5 COMMENTARY: He kept the wine until the fifth year, until the first day, on the new moon of the first month. The keeping runs to a new moon of the first month, the book's memorial new moon. — R. H. Charles

6 COMMENTARY: This verse completes the keeping notice: the wine was kept until the first day, on the new moon of the first month. The date fixes the feast that follows. — R. H. Charles

7 COMMENTARY: He celebrated with joy the day of this feast, and made a burnt sacrifice to the Lord, one young ox and one ram, and seven sheep each a year old, and a kid of the goats, to make atonement for himself and his sons. The firstfruits feast joins joy, burnt sacrifice and atonement for Noah and his sons. — R. H. Charles

8 COMMENTARY: He prepared the kid first, and placed some of its blood on the flesh that was on the altar he had made. The kid comes first, and its blood is placed on the altar's flesh before the rest of the offering. — R. H. Charles

9 COMMENTARY: All the fat he laid on the altar where he made the burnt sacrifice, with the ox, ram and sheep, laying all their flesh upon the altar. The whole burnt offering follows the kid's blood rite. — R. H. Charles

10 COMMENTARY: He placed all their offerings mingled with oil upon it, afterwards sprinkled wine on the fire he had previously made on the altar, and placed incense on the altar, causing a sweet savour to ascend acceptable before the Lord his God. Oil, wine and incense complete the offering, as in chapter 6's sacrifice, and the savour is acceptable. — R. H. Charles

11 COMMENTARY: He rejoiced and drank of this wine, he and his children with joy. Feast joy is shared: Noah and his children drink the new wine together after the sacrifice. — R. H. Charles

12 COMMENTARY: It was evening, and he went into his tent, and being drunken he lay down and slept, and was uncovered in his tent as he slept. JFB on Genesis 9:21 calls this a solitary stain on the character of so eminently pious a man, believed to be the result of age or inadvertency, perhaps at the festivities of the vintage season. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: Ham saw Noah his father naked, and went forth and told his two brethren without. The offence is twofold: seeing and then publishing it to his brothers outside. — R. H. Charles

14 COMMENTARY: Shem took his garment and arose, he and Japheth, and they placed the garment on their shoulders and went backward and covered the shame of their father, their faces backward. Reverence is shown by averted faces: they cover what Ham exposed, without looking. — R. H. Charles

15 COMMENTARY: Noah awoke from his sleep and knew all that his younger son had done to him, and he cursed his son, saying, 'Cursed be Canaan; an enslaved servant shall he be unto his brethren.' JFB on Genesis 9:25 notes the doom was fulfilled in the destruction of the Canaanites, with Ham's other descendants degraded and enslaved; Jubilees directs the curse at Canaan, Ham's younger son. — R. H. Charles; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: He blessed Shem, saying, 'Blessed be the Lord God of Shem, and Canaan shall be his servant.' JFB on Genesis 9:26 reads this as an intimation that Shem's descendants would be peculiarly honoured in the service of the true God, his Church established among them, and Canaan's people made their servants by conquest or submission. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: 'God shall enlarge Japheth, and God shall dwell in the dwelling of Shem, and Canaan shall be his servant.' JFB on Genesis 9:27 reads the enlargement as a vast increase in posterity and possessions, and dwelling in Shem's tents as a prophecy fulfilled as the nations come under Shem's blessing. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

18 COMMENTARY: Ham knew his father had cursed his younger son, and was displeased that he had cursed his son, and parted from his father, he and his sons with him, Cush and Mizraim and Put and Canaan. The curse divides the family: Ham departs with all four sons, beginning the parting Noah will warn against in verse 35. — R. H. Charles

19 COMMENTARY: Ham built for himself a city and called its name after the name of his wife Ne'elatama'uk. The departed son founds his own city, named for his wife. — R. H. Charles

20 COMMENTARY: Japheth saw it, became envious of his brother, and he too built for himself a city, calling its name after his wife 'Adataneses. Envy drives imitation: Japheth's city answers Ham's, the very envy Noah names in verse 35. — R. H. Charles

21 COMMENTARY: Shem dwelt with his father Noah, and built a city close to his father on the mountain. Unlike Ham who departed and Japheth who envied, Shem stays beside his father. — R. H. Charles

22 COMMENTARY: Shem too called his city's name after the name of his wife Sedeqetelebab. All three cities are named for wives, but Shem's alone stands close to his father's mountain. — R. H. Charles

23 COMMENTARY: Behold these three cities are near Mount Lubar: Sedeqetelebab fronting the mountain on its east, Na'eltama'uk on the south, and 'Adatan'eses towards the west. The three cities ring Lubar on three sides, east, south and west, around the ark's mountain. — R. H. Charles

24 COMMENTARY: These are the sons of Shem: Elam, Asshur, and Arpachshad, this son born two years after the flood, and Lud, and Aram. JFB on Genesis 10 notes Shem is introduced with marked distinction as the father of Eber, ancestor of the Hebrews, and Aram's descendants received Armenia, Mesopotamia and Syria. — R. H. Charles; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

25 COMMENTARY: The sons of Japheth: Gomer, Magog, Madai, Javan, Tubal, Meshech and Tiras; these are the sons of Noah. JFB on Genesis 10:1, 5 notes the catalogue is not arranged by seniority, and Japheth's descendants settled the isles of the Gentiles, the sea-accessible countries of Europe, Asia Minor and the east of the Euxine. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

26 COMMENTARY: This verse opens Noah's testament: in the twenty-eighth jubilee Noah began to enjoin upon his sons' sons the ordinances, commandments and judgments he knew. The sentence is completed by the exhortation of the next verse. — R. H. Charles

27 COMMENTARY: He exhorted his sons to observe righteousness, cover the shame of their flesh, bless their Creator, honour father and mother, love their neighbour, and guard their souls from fornication, uncleanness and all iniquity. Noah's testament gathers the moral law: righteousness, modesty, worship, family honour, neighbour love, and purity. — R. H. Charles

28 COMMENTARY: For owing to these three things came the flood upon the earth, namely the fornication wherein the Watchers, against the law of their ordinances, went whoring after the daughters of men and took wives of all they chose, and they made the beginning of uncleanness. Noah names the flood's causes, beginning with the Watchers' fornication against their own ordinances. — R. H. Charles

29 COMMENTARY: They begat sons, the Naphidim, and they were all unlike, and they devoured one another: the Giants slew the Naphil, the Naphil slew the Eljo, and the Eljo slew mankind, and one man slew another. Charles names the three classes of giants, Giants, Naphil and Eljo, destroying each other in descending order until mankind itself is slain. — R. H. Charles

30 COMMENTARY: This verse continues the corruption: everyone sold himself to work iniquity and to shed much blood. The giants' mutual slaughter becomes mankind's universal self-sale to iniquity and bloodshed. — R. H. Charles

31 COMMENTARY: The earth was filled with iniquity. The sentence begun in verse 30 closes here: iniquity fills the earth as the waters soon will. — R. H. Charles

32 COMMENTARY: This verse opens the next corruption: after this they sinned against the beasts and birds, and all that moves and walks on the earth. The sentence is completed by the bloodshed and evil imagination of the next verse. — R. H. Charles

33 COMMENTARY: Much blood was shed on the earth, and every imagination and desire of men imagined vanity and evil continually. Corruption spreads from men to beasts, and inward imagination matches outward bloodshed, echoing chapter 5's account of the flood generation. — R. H. Charles

34 COMMENTARY: The Lord destroyed everything from off the face of the earth, because of the wickedness of their deeds and because of the blood shed in the midst of the earth. Noah's retrospective names the flood's double cause: wicked deeds and shed blood. — R. H. Charles

35 COMMENTARY: 'And we were left, I and you, my sons, and everything that entered with us into the ark, and behold I see your works before me that ye do not walk in righteousness: for in the path of destruction ye have begun to walk, and ye are parting one from another, and are envious one of another, and so it comes that ye are not in harmony, my sons, each with his brother.' Noah warns that parting and envy, already seen in verses 18-20, repeat the pre-flood path of destruction. — R. H. Charles

36 COMMENTARY: 'For I see, and behold the demons have begun their seductions against you and against your children, and now I fear on your behalf, that after my death ye will shed the blood of men upon the earth, and that ye, too, will be destroyed from the face of the earth. For whoso sheddeth man's blood, and whoso eateth the blood of any flesh, shall all be destroyed from the earth.' Demons' seduction succeeds the Watchers, and Noah's fear is specific: bloodshed and blood-eating will destroy his sons as the flood destroyed the world. — R. H. Charles

37 COMMENTARY: There will not be left any man that eateth blood. The curse's first line: blood-eaters will be utterly removed. — R. H. Charles

38 COMMENTARY: Nor any that sheddeth the blood of man on the earth. The second line extends the removal to all manslayers. — R. H. Charles

39 COMMENTARY: Nor will there be left to him any seed or descendants living under heaven. The third line cuts off the offender's seed and descendants from life under heaven. — R. H. Charles

40 COMMENTARY: For into Sheol will they go. The fourth line sends the offenders to Sheol, the realm of the dead. — R. H. Charles

41 COMMENTARY: And into the place of condemnation will they descend. The fifth line names their destination as condemnation, not mere death. — R. H. Charles

42 COMMENTARY: And into the darkness of the deep will they all be removed by a violent death. The sixth line ends the curse in the darkness of the deep, by violent death matching their violent deeds. — R. H. Charles

43 COMMENTARY: 'There shall be no blood seen upon you of all the blood there shall be all the days in which ye have killed any beasts or cattle or whatever flieth upon the earth, and work ye a good work to your souls by covering that which hath been shed on the face of the earth.' Blood shed in lawful killing must be covered, and covering it is a good work for the soul. — R. H. Charles

44 COMMENTARY: 'And ye shall not be like him who eateth with blood, but guard yourselves that none may eat blood before you: cover the blood, for thus have I been commanded to testify to you and your children, together with all flesh.' Guarding against blood-eating and covering shed blood is Noah's commanded testimony to his children and to all flesh. — R. H. Charles

45 COMMENTARY: 'And suffer not the soul to be eaten with the flesh, that your blood, which is your life, may not be required at the hand of any flesh that sheddeth it on the earth.' The soul is the blood, the life itself, and eating soul with flesh brings bloodguilt under chapter 6's requirement. — R. H. Charles

46 COMMENTARY: 'For the earth will not be clean from the blood which hath been shed upon it; for only through the blood of him that shed it will the earth be purified throughout all its generations.' Shed blood pollutes the earth, and only the shedder's blood purifies it, generation after generation. — R. H. Charles

47 COMMENTARY: 'And now, my children, hearken: work judgment and righteousness that ye may be planted in righteousness over the face of the whole earth, and your glory lifted up before my God, who saved me from the waters of the flood.' After warnings comes the positive charge: judgment and righteousness plant them over the whole earth, with glory before the God who saved Noah. — R. H. Charles

48 COMMENTARY: 'And behold, ye will go and build for yourselves cities, and plant in them all the plants that are upon the earth, and moreover all fruit-bearing trees.' Settlement and planting follow the testament: cities and orchards are the work of the obedient generations. — R. H. Charles

49 COMMENTARY: 'For three years the fruit of everything that is eaten will not be gathered: and in the fourth year its fruit will be accounted holy, and they will offer the first-fruits, acceptable before the Most High God, who created heaven and earth and all things.' Three ungathered years and a holy fourth year of firstfruits order every planting, as Noah's own vines in verse 2 followed the fourth-year rule. — R. H. Charles

50 COMMENTARY: This verse completes the fourth-year rule: the holy fruit is acceptable before the Most High God, creator of heaven and earth and all things. The offering's acceptability rests on the Creator's own claim over all planting. — R. H. Charles

51 COMMENTARY: 'Let them offer in abundance the first of the wine and oil as first-fruits on the altar of the Lord, who receiveth it, and what is left let the servants of the house of the Lord eat before the altar which receiveth it.' Wine and oil firstfruits go on the altar in abundance, and the remainder feeds the Lord's house servants before the altar. — R. H. Charles

52 COMMENTARY: This verse opens the fifth-year rule: and in the fifth year make the release. The sentence is completed by the next verse's righteous release. — R. H. Charles

53 COMMENTARY: 'Make ye the release so that ye release it in righteousness and uprightness, and ye shall be righteous, and all that you plant will prosper.' The fifth-year release, made righteously, brings righteousness and prosperity to all planting. — R. H. Charles

54 COMMENTARY: 'For thus did Enoch, the father of your father, command Methuselah, his son, and Methuselah his son Lamech, and Lamech commanded me all the things which his fathers commanded him.' Noah's testament is not new: it is the chain of command from Enoch through Methuselah and Lamech to Noah himself. — R. H. Charles

55 COMMENTARY: 'And I also will give you commandment, my sons, as Enoch commanded his son in the first jubilees: whilst still living, the seventh in his generation, he commanded and testified to his son and to his sons' sons until the day of his death.' Noah mirrors Enoch, seventh in his generation, giving living testimony to sons and sons' sons until death. — R. H. Charles

COMMENTARY: This unit is the heading for the narrative that follows: the genealogy of Shem's descendants, and Noah and his sons dividing the earth. It divides Noah's testament from the division of the earth in chapters 8 and 9. — R. H. Charles
