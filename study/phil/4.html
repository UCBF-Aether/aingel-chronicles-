Study Notes — Philippians

Chapter 4

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Therefore, my brethren dearly beloved and longed for, my joy and crown, so stand fast in the Lord, my dearly beloved. Dearly beloved and longed for, his joy and crown, they are to stand fast in the Lord, for a minister's crown is a standing church, and love's terms urge steadfastness. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: I beseech Euodia, and beseech Syntyche, that they be of the same mind in the Lord. Euodia and Syntyche, each besought by name, are to be of the same mind in the Lord, for two honoured women at variance endanger the church's unity, and Paul pleads, not commands. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: I entreat you also, true yokefellow, help those women which laboured with me in the gospel, with Clement also, and with other fellow labourers, whose names are in the book of life. The true yokefellow is entreated to help these labouring women, with Clement and fellow labourers whose names are in life's book, for past labour deserves present help, and heaven's book remembers what men forget. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: Rejoice in the Lord always, and again I say, Rejoice. Rejoicing in the Lord always, repeated as again rejoice, is the standing command, for joy in the Lord, not in circumstances, can be always. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: Let your moderation be known unto all men. The Lord is at hand. Moderation known to all men, with the Lord at hand, is the church's witness, for gentleness before all, under a near Lord, restrains both passion and fear. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: Be careful for nothing, but in everything by prayer and supplication with thanksgiving let your requests be made known unto God. Careful for nothing, but in everything by prayer, supplication, and thanksgiving, requests are made known to God, for prayer replaces anxiety, and thanksgiving seasons every petition. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: The peace of God, which passes all understanding, shall keep your hearts and minds through Christ Jesus. God's peace, passing all understanding, shall keep hearts and minds through Christ, for peace beyond reason guards what reason cannot, like a garrison around the soul. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: Finally, brethren, whatever things are true, whatever things are honest, whatever things are just, whatever things are pure, whatever things are lovely, whatever things are of good report, if there be any virtue, and if there be any praise, think on these things. True, honest, just, pure, lovely, and of good report, with any virtue and praise, are to fill thought, for the mind's furniture decides the life's course. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: Those things, which you have both learned, and received, and heard, and seen in me, do, and the God of peace shall be with you. Learned, received, heard, and seen in Paul, these things done bring the God of peace with them, for practice, not only precept, secures God's presence. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: But I rejoiced in the Lord greatly, that now at the last your care of me has flourished again, wherein you were also careful, but you lacked opportunity. He rejoiced greatly that their care flourished again, for they were careful but lacked opportunity, and their revived gift is like a plant flowering after winter. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: Not that I speak in respect of want, for I have learned, in whatever state I am, therewith to be content. Not speaking of want, he has learned contentment in whatever state, for contentment is learned, not natural, and the lesson holds in every condition. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: I know both how to be abased, and I know how to abound: everywhere and in all things I am instructed both to be full and to be hungry, both to abound and to suffer need. Abased and abounding, full and hungry, abounding and in need, he is instructed in all, for contentment's school has two lessons, poverty and plenty, and he has learned both. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: I can do all things through Christ which strengthens me. All things through Christ who strengthens him is the secret of that contentment, for strength is not his own but Christ's, and through it every state is bearable. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: Notwithstanding you have well done, that you did communicate with my affliction. They did well to communicate with his affliction, for sharing a prisoner's suffering by gift is fellowship in deed, and Paul commends what he did not need. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: Now you Philippians know also, that in the beginning of the gospel, when I departed from Macedonia, no church communicated with me as concerning giving and receiving, but you only. In the gospel's beginning, on leaving Macedonia, no church but Philippi shared giving and receiving with him, for their generosity was singular from the start. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: For even in Thessalonica you sent once and again unto my necessity. Even in Thessalonica they sent once and again to his necessity, for their giving was repeated, not single, and distance did not cool it. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: Not because I desire a gift, but I desire fruit that may abound to your account. Not desiring a gift, he desires fruit abounding to their account, for the giver's profit, not the receiver's, is the apostle's aim, and generosity is credited to heaven's ledger. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

18 COMMENTARY: But I have all, and abound: I am full, having received of Epaphroditus the things which were sent from you, an odour of a sweet smell, a sacrifice acceptable, well pleasing to God. Having all and abounding, full by Epaphroditus' delivery, he counts their gift a sweet-smelling sacrifice, acceptable and pleasing to God, for charity to a minister is worship to God. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

19 COMMENTARY: But my God shall supply all your need according to his riches in glory by Christ Jesus. His God shall supply all their need according to riches in glory by Christ, for the God who received their gift will return supply from his own abundance, measured by glory, not by their gift. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

20 COMMENTARY: Now unto God and our Father be glory for ever and ever. Amen. To God and Father be glory for ever and ever, Amen, for supply, sacrifice, and all grace end in doxology, and eternity is the only fit measure for God's glory. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

21 COMMENTARY: Salute every saint in Christ Jesus. The brethren which are with me greet you. Every saint is to be saluted, and the brethren with him greet them, for greetings bind the churches, and no saint is too small for a salutation. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

22 COMMENTARY: All the saints salute you, chiefly they that are of Caesar's household. All saints salute them, chiefly Caesar's household, for the gospel had entered the palace itself, and Paul's bonds had won saints in the emperor's house. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

23 COMMENTARY: The grace of our Lord Jesus Christ be with you all. Amen. Grace of the Lord Jesus Christ with them all, Amen, closes the letter where it began, for grace is both its greeting and its farewell, and all the church's blessings are summed in it. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
