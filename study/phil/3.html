Study Notes — Philippians

Chapter 3

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Finally, my brethren, rejoice in the Lord. To write the same things to you, to me indeed is not grievous, but for you it is safe. Rejoicing in the Lord, repeated without weariness, is safe teaching, for repetition of the same truths guards, not bores, the church. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: Beware of dogs, beware of evil workers, beware of the concision. Three beware's warn against dogs, evil workers, and the concision, for Judaizing teachers, who bark, work evil, and cut the flesh, must be thrice warned against. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: For we are the circumcision, who worship God in the spirit, and rejoice in Christ Jesus, and have no confidence in the flesh. True circumcision worships in spirit, rejoices in Christ, and trusts not the flesh, for the inward mark, not the outward cut, makes God's people. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: Though I might also have confidence in the flesh. If any other man thinks he has whereof he might trust in the flesh, I more. Paul might trust the flesh, and more than any other, for his pedigree outdoes every rival's claim, and he lists it to show its worthlessness. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: Circumcised the eighth day, of the stock of Israel, of the tribe of Benjamin, a Hebrew of the Hebrews; as touching the law, a Pharisee. Eighth-day circumcision, Israel's stock, Benjamin's tribe, Hebrew parentage, and Pharisee strictness together make a flawless Jewish pedigree, the best the flesh can offer. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: Concerning zeal, persecuting the church; touching the righteousness which is in the law, blameless. Zeal that persecuted the church and a blameless legal righteousness complete the list, for even his sins for Judaism and his keeping of it were exemplary. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: But what things were gain to me, those I counted loss for Christ. Gain turned to loss for Christ is the reckoning of conversion, for all that once profited him now counts against him beside Christ. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: Yea doubtless, and I count all things but loss for the excellency of the knowledge of Christ Jesus my Lord, for whom I have suffered the loss of all things, and do count them but dung, that I may win Christ. All things are loss beside the excellency of knowing Christ his Lord, and he counts them dung, for he suffered their loss to win Christ, and the exchange is no loss at all. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: And be found in him, not having my own righteousness, which is of the law, but that which is through the faith of Christ, the righteousness which is of God by faith. Found in him, his righteousness is not law's own but God's by faith in Christ, for the righteousness that saves is received, not achieved. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: That I may know him, and the power of his resurrection, and the fellowship of his sufferings, being made conformable unto his death. Knowing him means knowing resurrection's power and sufferings' fellowship, conformed to his death, for Christ is known in both triumph and cross. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: If by any means I might attain unto the resurrection of the dead. Attaining the resurrection of the dead is the goal of this knowing, for conformity to his death leads to likeness in his rising. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: Not as though I had already attained, either were already perfect, but I follow after, if that I may apprehend that for which also I am apprehended of Christ Jesus. Not yet attained nor perfect, he follows after to apprehend what Christ apprehended him for, for grace that seized him now drives him to seize its prize. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: Brethren, I count not myself to have apprehended, but this one thing I do, forgetting those things which are behind, and reaching forth unto those things which are before. Not counting himself to have apprehended, he does one thing: forgetting behind and reaching before, for the race requires a single eye and a forward lean. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: I press toward the mark for the prize of the high calling of God in Christ Jesus. Pressing toward the mark, he runs for the prize of God's high calling in Christ, for the Christian life is a race with a mark, a prize, and a caller above. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: Let us therefore, as many as be perfect, be thus minded, and if in anything you be otherwise minded, God shall reveal even this to you. The perfect, that is, the mature, are to be thus minded, and if any differ, God shall reveal it, for unity in pursuit leaves lesser differences to God's teaching. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: Nevertheless, whereto we have already attained, let us walk by the same rule, let us mind the same thing. Where we have attained, let us walk by the same rule and mind the same thing, for common attainment demands common walking, and agreement in what is known binds until more is revealed. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: Brethren, be followers together of me, and mark them which walk so as you have us for an example. Followers together of Paul, and markers of those who walk by his example, are the church's discipline, for example is the living rule beside the written one. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

18 COMMENTARY: For many walk, of whom I have told you often, and now tell you even weeping, that they are the enemies of the cross of Christ. Many walk as enemies of Christ's cross, told of often and now weeping, for Paul weeps over enemies whom others admire, and tears are the apostle's comment on apostasy. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

19 COMMENTARY: Whose end is destruction, whose God is their belly, and whose glory is in their shame, who mind earthly things. Their end is destruction, their god is their belly, their glory is shame, and their mind is earthly things, for appetite, shame, and earth together mark the cross's enemies. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

20 COMMENTARY: For our conversation is in heaven, from whence also we look for the Saviour, the Lord Jesus Christ. Our citizenship is in heaven, from where we look for the Saviour, for the cross's friends live as heaven's citizens on earth, awaiting their Lord's return. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

21 COMMENTARY: Who shall change our vile body, that it may be fashioned like unto his glorious body, according to the working whereby he is able even to subdue all things unto himself. He shall change our vile body into his glorious body's likeness, by the power that subdues all things, for resurrection completes what conversion began. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
