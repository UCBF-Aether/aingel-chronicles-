Study Notes — Philippians

Chapter 2

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: If there be therefore any consolation in Christ, if any comfort of love, if any fellowship of the Spirit, if any bowels and mercies. Consolation in Christ, comfort of love, fellowship of the Spirit, and bowels of mercy are the grounds of his appeal, for all gospel comforts urge unity. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: Fulfil my joy, that you be likeminded, having the same love, being of one accord, of one mind. Fulfilling his joy requires likemindedness, same love, one accord, and one mind, for joy in a church is complete only when its members are one. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: Let nothing be done through strife or vainglory, but in lowliness of mind let each esteem other better than themselves. Nothing through strife or vainglory, but lowliness esteeming others better, is the rule that kills both party spirit and pride at their root. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: Look not every man on his own things, but every man also on the things of others. Looking also on others' things, not only one's own, is the practice of lowliness, for self-interest blinded is the enemy of unity. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: Let this mind be in you, which was also in Christ Jesus. The mind that was in Christ is the pattern for ours, for the church's unity must copy the Saviour's humility, not merely admire it. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: Who, being in the form of God, thought it not robbery to be equal with God. Being in God's form, he did not count equality with God a prize to be grasped, for his divine nature was his own, not a theft to be defended. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: But made himself of no reputation, and took upon him the form of a servant, and was made in the likeness of men. He emptied himself of reputation, took a servant's form, and was made in men's likeness, for the Lord of all became the servant of all. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: Being found in fashion as a man, he humbled himself, and became obedient unto death, even the death of the cross. Found as a man, he humbled himself further, to obedience unto death, even the cross's death, for humility's lowest step is a criminal's execution. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: Wherefore God also has highly exalted him, and given him a name which is above every name. Wherefore God highly exalted him and gave a name above every name, for humiliation's depth is answered by exaltation's height, and the cross leads to the throne. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: That at the name of Jesus every knee should bow, of things in heaven, and things in earth, and things under the earth. At Jesus' name every knee bows, in heaven, earth, and under earth, for his lordship is universal, and no creature is exempt from homage. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: That every tongue should confess that Jesus Christ is Lord, to the glory of God the Father. Every tongue confesses Jesus Christ is Lord, to the Father's glory, for the Son's exaltation glorifies the Father who exalted him. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: Wherefore, my beloved, as you have always obeyed, not as in my presence only, but now much more in my absence, work out your own salvation with fear and trembling. Obedience in absence, more than in presence, must work out salvation with fear and trembling, for the work is theirs to do, though God works in them. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: For it is God who works in you both to will and to do of his good pleasure. God works in them both to will and to do, so fear and trembling are not servile dread but reverent dependence, for the willing and the doing are both his good pleasure's gift. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: Do all things without murmurings and disputings. All things without murmurings and disputings is the outward mark of inward grace, for a church that disputes its duties dishonours the God who works in it. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: That you may be blameless and harmless, sons of God, without rebuke, in the midst of a crooked and perverse nation, among whom you shine as lights in the world. Blameless and harmless sons of God, without rebuke among a crooked nation, shine as lights, for holiness in darkness is itself a witness. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: Holding forth the word of life, that I may rejoice in the day of Christ, that I have not run in vain, neither laboured in vain. Holding forth life's word, they give Paul ground to rejoice in Christ's day that his running and labour were not vain, for a church's steadfastness is its minister's reward. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: Yea, and if I be offered upon the sacrifice and service of your faith, I joy, and rejoice with you all. If he is poured out as a drink offering upon their faith's sacrifice, he joys and rejoices with them, for a minister's death, spent on his people's faith, is itself a festival. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

18 COMMENTARY: For the same cause also do you joy, and rejoice with me. For the same cause they are to joy and rejoice with him, for his offering and their faith are one sacrifice, and joy is shared, not solitary. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

19 COMMENTARY: But I trust in the Lord Jesus to send Timothy shortly to you, that I also may be of good comfort, when I know your state. He trusts in the Lord to send Timothy shortly, that knowing their state may comfort him, for a minister's comfort is news of his people's welfare. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

20 COMMENTARY: For I have no man likeminded, who will naturally care for your state. No man likeminded cares naturally for their state as Timothy does, for his care is not forced but natural, like a son's for his father's house. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

21 COMMENTARY: For all seek their own, not the things which are Jesus Christ's. All seek their own, not Christ's things, is the general charge that makes Timothy rare, for self-seeking is the common disease even among ministers. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

22 COMMENTARY: But you know the proof of him, that, as a son with the father, he has served with me in the gospel. They know Timothy's proof: as a son with a father he served with Paul in the gospel, for tried service, not untried promise, commends him. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

23 COMMENTARY: Him therefore I hope to send presently, so soon as I shall see how it will go with me. Timothy will be sent as soon as Paul sees how his own case goes, for the servant's mission waits on the master's trial, and both are in the Lord's hands. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

24 COMMENTARY: But I trust in the Lord that I also myself shall come shortly. He trusts in the Lord that he himself shall come shortly, for the same confidence that sends Timothy hopes for his own visit, and both hopes rest on the Lord. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

25 COMMENTARY: Yet I supposed it necessary to send to you Epaphroditus, my brother, and companion in labour, and fellow soldier, but your messenger, and he that ministered to my wants. Epaphroditus, brother, fellow labourer, fellow soldier, their messenger and his minister, is sent as necessary, for his titles together describe a complete minister. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

26 COMMENTARY: For he longed after you all, and was full of heaviness, because you had heard that he had been sick. He longed after them and was heavy because they heard of his sickness, for his grief was not for his illness but for their sorrow at it. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

27 COMMENTARY: For indeed he was sick near unto death, but God had mercy on him, and not on him only, but on me also, lest I should have sorrow upon sorrow. Sick near death, he found God's mercy, and mercy to him was mercy to Paul also, lest sorrow be added to sorrow in the apostle's prison. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

28 COMMENTARY: I sent him therefore the more carefully, that, when you see him again, you may rejoice, and that I may be the less sorrowful. He is sent the more carefully, that their sight of him may bring joy and lessen Paul's sorrow, for a restored minister is a double comfort. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

29 COMMENTARY: Receive him therefore in the Lord with all gladness, and hold such in reputation. Receive him in the Lord with all gladness, and hold such in reputation, for ministers who hazard life deserve honour, and glad reception is their due reward. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

30 COMMENTARY: Because for the work of Christ he was near unto death, not regarding his life, to supply your lack of service toward me. Near death for Christ's work, not regarding his life, he supplied their lack of service, for what the church could not do in person, its messenger did at hazard of life. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
