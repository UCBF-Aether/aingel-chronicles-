Study Notes — James

Chapter 4

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Wars and fightings come from lusts that war in our members. Since all wars and fightings come from the corruptions of our own hearts, Henry says, it is right to mortify those lusts. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

2 COMMENTARY: We lust and have not, kill and desire to have and cannot obtain, fight and war, yet have not because we ask not. Worldly and fleshly lusts are distempers, Henry notes, which will not allow content or satisfaction. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

3 COMMENTARY: We ask and receive not because we ask amiss, to consume it upon our lusts. When men ask prosperity with wrong aims and intentions, it is just in God to deny them; prayers that are the language of lusts rather than graces will return empty. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

4 COMMENTARY: Adulterers and adulteresses, friendship with the world is enmity with God. Here is a decided warning against criminal friendships with this world: an enemy may be reconciled, but enmity never can be. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

5 COMMENTARY: Do ye think the scripture speaks in vain? The spirit that dwells in us lusts to envy. Natural corruption shows itself by envying, Henry says, while the Holy Spirit teaches us to be willing to do good to all about us. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

6 COMMENTARY: God gives more grace: he resists the proud but gives grace unto the humble. The proud resist God in understanding, will, and passions, so no wonder God resists the proud; the humble see their need of grace, pray for it, and are thankful for it. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

7 COMMENTARY: Submit yourselves therefore to God; resist the devil, and he will flee from you. Submit understanding to the truth of God and will to the will of his precept and providence; if we yield to temptations the devil will follow us, but if we stand out against him he will leave us. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

8 COMMENTARY: Draw nigh to God, and he will draw nigh to you; cleanse your hands, ye sinners, and purify your hearts, ye double minded. Sinners are to submit to God and seek his grace and favour, resisting the devil. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

9 COMMENTARY: Be afflicted, and mourn, and weep; let laughter be turned to mourning and joy to heaviness. All sin must be wept over, Henry says, here in godly sorrow, or hereafter in eternal misery. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

10 COMMENTARY: Humble yourselves in the sight of the Lord, and he shall lift you up. The Lord will not refuse to comfort one who really mourns for sin, or to exalt one who humbles himself before him. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

11 COMMENTARY: Speak not evil one of another, brethren; he that speaks evil of his brother and judges his brother speaks evil of the law and judges the law. Our lips must be governed by the law of kindness as well as truth and justice, Henry says. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

12 COMMENTARY: There is one lawgiver, who is able to save and to destroy; who art thou that judgest another? We have the law of God as a rule to all, and must not set up our own notions as a rule to those about us, lest we be condemned of the Lord. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

13 COMMENTARY: Go to now, ye that say, today or tomorrow we will go into such a city, continue a year, buy and sell, and get gain. "Go to now" is a call to consider such conduct as wrong, Henry notes, and how apt contriving men are to leave God out of their plans. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

14 COMMENTARY: Whereas ye know not what shall be on the morrow; what is your life? It is even a vapour that appears for a little time and then vanishes away. The frailty, shortness, and uncertainty of life ought to check the vanity of all projects for futurity. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

15 COMMENTARY: For that ye ought to say, if the Lord will, we shall live, and do this, or that. We are always to depend on the will of God; our times are not in our own hands but at the disposal of God, and all we design and do should be with submissive dependence on him. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

16 COMMENTARY: But now ye rejoice in your boastings, and all such rejoicing is evil. It is foolish and hurtful to boast of worldly things and aspiring projects; it will bring great disappointment and prove destruction in the end. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

17 COMMENTARY: To him that knows to do good and does it not, to him it is sin. Omissions are sins which will be brought into judgment as well as commissions, Henry stresses. — Matthew Henry (Concise)
