Study Notes — James

Chapter 3

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Be not many masters, knowing we shall receive the greater condemnation. Henry's caution against proud behaviour opens here: those who take the teacher's place invite stricter judgment. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

2 COMMENTARY: In many things we offend all, and if any offend not in word, he is a perfect man able also to bridle the whole body. We are taught to dread an unruly tongue as one of the greatest evils, Henry says. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

3 COMMENTARY: Bits in horses' mouths turn about their whole body. The tongue, small as a bit, steers the whole course of a life, which is why its government matters so much. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

4 COMMENTARY: Ships, though great and driven of fierce winds, are turned about with a very small helm. So the tongue, though little, directs great affairs, and the affairs of mankind are thrown into confusion by the tongues of men. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

5 COMMENTARY: The tongue is a little member that boasts great things, and a little fire kindles a great matter. Every age and every condition of life, private or public, affords examples of this mischief. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

6 COMMENTARY: The tongue is a fire, a world of iniquity, defiling the whole body and setting on fire the course of nature, itself set on fire of hell. Hell has more to do in promoting the fire of the tongue than men generally think, Henry warns. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

7 COMMENTARY: Every kind of beasts, birds, serpents, and sea creatures is tamed and has been tamed of mankind, but the tongue can no man tame. No man can tame it without divine grace and assistance; the apostle does not call it impossible, but extremely difficult. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

8 COMMENTARY: The tongue is an unruly evil, full of deadly poison. Other sins decay with age, Henry notes, but this many times gets worse, as we grow more froward and fretful when natural strength decays. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

9 COMMENTARY: With the tongue we bless God, even the Father, and with it we curse men made after the similitude of God. That man's tongue confutes itself, Henry says, which at one time pretends to adore God and at another condemns even good men for not using the same words. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

10 COMMENTARY: Out of the same mouth proceed blessing and cursing, which ought not so to be. True religion will not admit of contradictions, Henry insists; how many sins would be prevented if men would always be consistent! — Matthew Henry (Concise)

11 COMMENTARY: Does a fountain send forth at the same place sweet water and bitter? Pious and edifying language is the genuine produce of a sanctified heart, and none who understand Christianity expect curses, lies, boastings, and revilings from a true believer's mouth. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

12 COMMENTARY: Can the fig tree bear olive berries, or a vine figs? So no fountain can yield both salt water and fresh. We look for the fruit of one tree from another no more than we expect such contradictions from a believer. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

13 COMMENTARY: Who is wise and endued with knowledge among you? Let him show out of a good conversation his works with meekness of wisdom. He who thinks well or talks well is not wise in Scripture's sense, Henry says, if he does not live and act well. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

14 COMMENTARY: If ye have bitter envying and strife in your hearts, glory not and lie not against the truth. Those who live in malice, envy, and contention live in confusion and are liable to be hurried to any evil work. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

15 COMMENTARY: This wisdom descends not from above, but is earthly, sensual, devilish. Such wisdom springs up from earthly principles, acts on earthly motives, and is intent on serving earthly purposes. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

16 COMMENTARY: Where envying and strife is, there is confusion and every evil work. The wisdom that is lifted up in envy and contention stands far from Christian love. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

17 COMMENTARY: The wisdom from above is first pure, then peaceable, gentle, easy to be entreated, full of mercy and good fruits, without partiality and without hypocrisy. It has no disguise or deceit, Henry says; it is sincere, open, steady, uniform, and consistent with itself. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

18 COMMENTARY: The fruit of righteousness is sown in peace of them that make peace. May the purity, peace, gentleness, teachableness, and mercy shown in all our actions, and the fruits of righteousness abounding in our lives, prove that God has bestowed this excellent gift. — Matthew Henry (Concise)
