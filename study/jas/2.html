Study Notes — James

Chapter 2

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Believers must not hold the faith of the Lord Jesus Christ, the Lord of glory, with respect of persons. Henry notes that professing faith in the lowly Jesus does not agree with favouring people for mere outward circumstances and appearances. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

2 COMMENTARY: If a man with a gold ring and goodly apparel comes into the assembly, and a poor man in vile raiment also comes, the two must not be treated by appearance. Henry allows civil respect, but never respect that influences church offices, censures, or any matter of religion. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

3 COMMENTARY: Saying to the rich man, sit here in a good place, while telling the poor man to stand or sit under a footstool, is partiality in action. Were all persons more spiritually-minded, Henry says, the poor would be treated with more attention in worshipping congregations. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

4 COMMENTARY: Such partiality makes believers judges of evil thoughts. Questioning ourselves is of great use in every part of the holy life, Henry urges, and we should take occasion to discourse with our souls. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

5 COMMENTARY: God has chosen the poor of this world to be rich in faith and heirs of the kingdom promised to them that love him. A lowly state, Henry notes, is most favourable for inward peace and growth in holiness. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

6 COMMENTARY: Yet believers have despised the poor, while rich men oppress them and draw them before judgment seats. Consider how often riches lead to vice and mischief, Henry says, and what reproaches are thrown upon God and religion by men of wealth and worldly greatness. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

7 COMMENTARY: It is the rich who blaspheme the worthy name by which believers are called. This makes the sin of partiality appear very sinful and foolish. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

8 COMMENTARY: If ye fulfil the royal law, thou shalt love thy neighbour as thyself, ye do well. This law is royal because it comes from the King of kings. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

9 COMMENTARY: If ye have respect to persons, ye commit sin, and are convinced of the law as transgressors. Christians who act unjustly are convicted by the very law they profess. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

10 COMMENTARY: Whoever keeps the whole law yet offends in one point is guilty of all. According to the covenant of works, one breach of any one command brings a man under condemnation from which no obedience, past, present, or future, can deliver him. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

11 COMMENTARY: He that said, do not commit adultery, also said, do not kill; so breaking one command makes a man a transgressor of the law. To think our good deeds will atone for our bad deeds, Henry notes, plainly puts us upon looking for another atonement. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

12 COMMENTARY: So speak and so do, as they that shall be judged by the law of liberty. This shows the happiness of those in Christ, Henry says: we may serve him without slavish fear, for God's restraints are not a bondage, but our own corruptions are. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

13 COMMENTARY: He shall have judgment without mercy that has shown no mercy, and mercy rejoices against judgment. God deems it his glory and joy to pardon and bless those who might justly be condemned, and his grace teaches those who partake of his mercy to copy it in their conduct. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

14 COMMENTARY: What does it profit if a man says he has faith but has not works? Can faith save him? A bare profession may gain the good opinion of pious people, Henry says, but what profit is it to gain the whole world and lose one's soul? — Matthew Henry (Concise)

15 COMMENTARY: If a brother or sister is naked and destitute of daily food, words alone do not meet the need. All things should be accounted profitable or unprofitable as they tend to forward or hinder the salvation of our souls. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

16 COMMENTARY: Saying depart in peace, be warmed and filled, without giving the things needful to the body, profits nothing. An opinion or assent to the gospel without works is not faith, Henry insists. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

17 COMMENTARY: Even so faith, if it has not works, is dead, being alone. There is no way to show we really believe in Christ, Henry says, but by being diligent in good works from gospel motives and for gospel purposes. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

18 COMMENTARY: One may say, thou hast faith and I have works; show me thy faith without thy works, and I will show thee my faith by my works. Men may boast to others and be conceited of what they really have not; true believing is not an act of the understanding only, but a work of the whole heart. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

19 COMMENTARY: Thou believest there is one God; thou doest well, but the devils also believe and tremble. Mere assent to doctrine or historical belief of facts wholly differs from saving faith, Henry notes. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

20 COMMENTARY: O vain man, know that faith without works is dead. Those are wrong who put a mere notional belief of the gospel for the whole of evangelical religion. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

21 COMMENTARY: Was not Abraham our father justified by works when he offered Isaac his son upon the altar? Abraham believed God and it was reckoned to him for righteousness, and faith producing such works advanced him to peculiar favours. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

22 COMMENTARY: Faith wrought with Abraham's works, and by works was faith made perfect. Faith is the root and good works are the fruits, Henry says, and we must see to it that we have both. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

23 COMMENTARY: The scripture was fulfilled which says Abraham believed God and it was imputed to him for righteousness, and he was called the Friend of God. By works a man is justified, Henry explains, not by bare opinion or profession, but by having such faith as produces good works. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

24 COMMENTARY: Ye see then how that by works a man is justified, and not by faith only. To deny one's own reason, affections, and interests, as Abraham did, is an action fit to try a believer. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

25 COMMENTARY: Likewise Rahab the harlot was justified by works when she received the messengers and sent them out another way. Rahab's conduct proved her faith to be living and powerful, showing she believed with her heart, not merely by assent of the understanding. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

26 COMMENTARY: As the body without the spirit is dead, so faith without works is dead also. The best works without faith are dead, Henry warns, for they want root and principle; and faith without fruit is as though the root were dead. — Matthew Henry (Concise)
