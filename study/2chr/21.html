Study Notes — 2 Chronicles

Chapter 21

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Now Jehoshaphat slept with his fathers, and was buried with his fathers in the city of David, and Jehoram his son reigned in his stead. Henry notes never did a kingdom change its king so much for the worse, when Jehoram, one of the vilest, succeeded Jehoshaphat, one of the best, a punishment for not making better use of Jehoshaphat's good government and coldness to his reformation. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: And he had brethren, sons of Jehoshaphat: Azariah, Jehiel, Zechariah, Azariah, Michael and Shephatiah, all sons of Jehoshaphat king of Israel. Henry notes Jehoshaphat's many sons were better than Jehoram, with more wisdom and virtue, living up to their education, which Jehoram went counter to, and any of them was more fit for the crown. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: And their father gave them great gifts of silver, gold and precious things, with fenced cities in Judah, but the kingdom gave he to Jehoram, because he was the firstborn. Henry notes Jehoshaphat, like Abraham with Isaac, secured the kingdom to the firstborn and portioned his brethren so they would give no disturbance, though whether wisely, given Jehoram's unfitness, is questionable, for power is sacred and the people's security is the first consideration. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: Now when Jehoram was risen up to the kingdom, he strengthened himself, and slew all his brethren with the sword, and divers also of the princes of Israel. Henry notes Jehoram, as soon as settled, murdered all his brethren, by false accusation or assassination, pretending he could not be safe till they were gone, hating them as Cain hated Abel because their piety condemned his impiety, and with them slew princes of Judah who had taught God's knowledge. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: Jehoram was thirty-two years old when he began to reign, and he reigned eight years in Jerusalem. Henry notes his reign was short and miserable, eight years ending in disgrace, a foundation in blood that would sink the superstructure. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: And he walked in the way of the kings of Israel, like as did the house of Ahab, for he had Ahab's daughter to wife, and he wrought evil in the eyes of the Lord. Henry notes Jehoram corrupted and debauched his kingdom, ruining his father and grandfather's reformation, walking in Ahab's way through marriage to Ahab's daughter. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: Howbeit the Lord would not destroy the house of David, because of the covenant made with David, and as he promised to give a light to him and his sons for ever. Henry notes God was tender of his covenant: David's seed should be visited for transgressions, but the covenant should never be broken. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: In his days the Edomites revolted from under Judah's dominion, and made themselves a king. Henry notes when Jehoram forsook God, his subjects withdrew allegiance: tributary provinces abroad revolted, Edom first, as God permitted it because he had forsaken the Lord. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: Then Jehoram went forth with his princes and all his chariots, and rose up by night, and smote the Edomites which compassed him in, and the captains of the chariots. Henry notes Jehoram chastised Edom by night attack, yet could not reduce them, for the revolt was God's providence ordering it so. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: So the Edomites revolted from under Judah unto this day. The same time also did Libnah revolt from under his hand, because he had forsaken the Lord God of his fathers. Henry notes Libnah, a city of his own kingdom, revolted and set up as a free state, because he had forsaken God and become an idolater, and they could not continue subject without danger of being drawn away, so while he adhered to God they adhered to him, but when he cast God off, they cast him off. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: Moreover he made high places in the mountains of Judah and caused Jerusalem's inhabitants to commit fornication, and compelled Judah thereto. Henry notes he made high places the people were forward to make, drew Jerusalem easily into spiritual whoredom, seducing them to eat idol sacrifices, and drove the country people by force, using power given for edification for destruction of the church. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: And there came a writing to him from Elijah the prophet, saying, Thus saith the Lord God of David thy father, Because thou hast not walked in the ways of Jehoshaphat thy father, nor in the ways of Asa king of Judah. Henry notes Elijah, near his departure and unable to go in person, left this writing probably with Elisha to be sent at first opportunity, either to reclaim Jehoram or witness against him, sent in the name of the Lord God of David, upbraiding him with a relation that was his honour and his degeneracy's aggravation. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: But hast walked in the way of the kings of Israel, and hast made Judah and Jerusalem to go a whoring, like the whoredoms of Ahab's house, and also hast slain thy brethren of thy father's house, which were better than thyself. Henry notes the indictment's heads: departure from his father and grandfather's good ways, conformity to Ahab's impious family, setting up and enforcing idolatry, and murdering brethren better than himself. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: Behold, with a great plague will the Lord smite thy people, and thy children, and thy wives, and all thy goods. Henry notes judgment was given: his sin should ruin kingdom and family, with a heavy stroke of war and captivity on people, children, wives and goods, for bad men bring God's judgments on all about them. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: And thou shalt have great sickness by disease of thy bowels, until thy bowels fall out by reason of the sickness day by day. Henry notes judgment on his health and life was foretold before, that his blood might be on his own head and the fulfilment might show it came not by chance but as punishment, and had he humbled himself like Ahab on Elijah's message, he might have obtained at least a reprieve, but he threw it by as waste paper. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: Moreover the Lord stirred up against Jehoram the spirit of the Philistines, and of the Arabians near the Ethiopians. Henry notes God stirred up neighbours who had loved and feared Jehoshaphat but hated and despised Jehoram, looking on changing gods as scandalous, and they invaded his country, aiming at the king's house only. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: And they came up into Judah, and brake into it, and carried away all the substance found in the king's house, and his sons also, and his wives, so that never a son was left him, save Jehoahaz, the youngest. Henry notes the controversy was evidently with Jehoram and his house: only wives and sons were carried, and in chapter 22 we learn his sons were slain, blood for blood, for he had slain his brethren to strengthen himself, and now all but one son were slain, weakening him, yet David's house was not wholly extirpated because a blessing, the Messiah, was in it. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: And after all this the Lord smote him in his bowels with an incurable disease. Henry notes he was tormented with sore, long-continued diseases threatened in the law, in his bowels with continual griping and complications, incurable though he was in his prime, not forty, and he who had no bowels of compassion was plagued in his bowels till they fell out. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: And it came to pass, in process of time, after the end of two years, his bowels fell out by reason of his sickness, so he died of sore diseases. And his people made no burning for him, like the burning of his fathers. Henry notes two years he continued ill with no relief, disabled from recovering his family or revenging the injury, and at death they made no burning for him, showing little affection, for his memory was far from sweet, though his loathsome body needed perfume. — Matthew Henry

20 COMMENTARY: Thirty-two years old was he when he began to reign, and he reigned in Jerusalem eight years, and departed without being desired. Howbeit they buried him in the city of David, but not in the sepulchres of the kings. Henry notes he departed without being desired, nobody valuing him in life or lamenting him in death, and they would not bury him in the kings' sepulchres, thinking him unworthy, an intimation of the wicked's everlasting separation from just men, for those who despise God shall be lightly esteemed. — Matthew Henry
