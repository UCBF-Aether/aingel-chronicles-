Study Notes — 2 Chronicles

Chapter 15

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: And the Spirit of God came upon Azariah son of Oded. Henry notes a prophet was sent to Asa and his army returning victorious, not to compliment them but to quicken them to duty, which is the proper business of God's ministers even with princes, and the Spirit came both to instruct him what to say and enable him to say it boldly. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: And he went out to meet Asa, and said, Hear ye me, Asa, and all Judah and Benjamin: The Lord is with you, while ye be with him, and if ye seek him, he will be found of you, but if ye forsake him, he will forsake you. Henry notes this is both comfort and caution: those who keep close to God shall have his presence, but continuance depends on perseverance, and present triumphs are no security if God departs. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: Now for a long season Israel hath been without the true God, and without a teaching priest, and without law. Henry notes Israel, forsaking duty, was overrun with atheism and impiety, and in the Judges' days worshipped false gods, with priests who did not teach and no law to any purpose, while the ten tribes under Jeroboam's calves came likewise to be without the true God, for it is next to impossible to keep up religion without a preaching ministry. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: But when they in their trouble did turn unto the Lord God of Israel, and sought him, he was found of them. Henry notes that when troubles drove them to God by repentance, prayer and reformation, he raised up deliverers, verifying that God is with us while we are with him, and what was written aforetime was written for our admonition. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: And in those times there was no peace to him that went out, nor to him that came in, but great vexations were upon all inhabitants of the countries. Henry notes forsaking God brought vexatious and destroying wars, foreign and domestic, with no peace in going out or coming in. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: And nation was destroyed of nation, and city of city, for God did vex them with all adversity. Henry notes they provoked God with all iniquity, and he vexed them with all adversity, nation against nation and city against city, as neighbours' harms to warn Judah. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: Be ye strong therefore, and let not your hands be weak, for your work shall be rewarded. Henry notes God's work should be done with diligence, cheerfulness and resolution, and the assurance of reward should quicken religion, for the work will not go unrewarded, and indeed is its own reward. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: And when Asa heard these words, and the prophecy of Oded the prophet, he took courage, and put away the abominable idols out of all Judah and Benjamin, and out of the cities taken from Mount Ephraim, and renewed the altar of the Lord before the porch. Henry notes the sermon gave the good cause life: Asa grew bolder, ventured to destroy all abominable idols as far as his power went, and renewed the altar gone out of repair, though only thirty-five years since Solomon erected it. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: And he gathered all Judah and Benjamin, and the strangers with them out of Ephraim, Manasseh and Simeon, for they fell to him out of Israel in abundance, when they saw that the Lord his God was with him. Henry notes strangers coming was great encouragement, for they came because they saw God was with Asa, and it is good to be with those who live in God's fear and favour. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: So they gathered themselves together at Jerusalem in the third month, in the fifteenth year of Asa's reign. Henry notes Asa gave orders for gathering, yet they gathered themselves, so forward were they to obey, and the meeting was held in the third month, probably at Pentecost. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: And they offered unto the Lord the same time, of the spoil they had brought, seven hundred oxen and seven thousand sheep. Henry notes these sacrifices were thanksgiving for favours received and supplication for further favours, giving God his share of the spoil, though nothing to Solomon's offering, owing to diminished zeal or wealth. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: And they entered into a covenant to seek the Lord God of their fathers with all their heart and with all their soul. Henry notes penitents and converts should renew covenants, and the motion seems to have come from the people themselves as volunteers, engaging to seek God diligently, as their fathers sought him, with all heart and soul, for God will have all the heart or none. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: That whosoever would not seek the Lord God of Israel should be put to death, whether small or great, whether man or woman. Henry notes they agreed to oblige others to seek God, executing God's law in Deuteronomy, not a new law of their own, for if that law had been duly executed there would not have been so many idols in Judah. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: And they sware unto the Lord with a loud voice, and with shouting, and with trumpets, and with cornets. Henry notes they swore not secretly, as if ashamed or afraid of binding themselves, but with loud voice to express zeal and animate one another, as a bridegroom plights troth in marriage. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: And all Judah rejoiced at the oath, for they had sworn with all their heart, and sought him with their whole desire, and he was found of them, and the Lord gave them rest round about. Henry notes they rejoiced because they were hearty in it, and those only experience religion's pleasure who are sincere in it, and national reformation gives general satisfaction to all that are good. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: And also concerning Maachah mother of Asa the king, he removed her from being queen, because she had made an idol in a grove, and Asa cut down her idol, stamped it, and burnt it at the brook Kidron. Henry notes Asa bravely would not connive at idolatry in those nearest him, honouring God more than his grandmother, and deposing the queen-mother, though she may have subscribed the covenant and so escaped death. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: But the high places were not taken away out of Israel: nevertheless the heart of Asa was perfect all his days. Henry's chapter summary states it plainly: all was well, but that the high places were permitted, yet Asa's heart was perfect all his days. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: And he brought into the house of God the things his father had dedicated, and that he himself had dedicated, silver, gold and vessels. Henry notes it is honesty to render to God the things that are his: what has long been designed and laid by for him should at length be laid out for him, and a man must not rob God or make slow payment. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: And there was no more war unto the five and thirtieth year of the reign of Asa. Henry notes God did well for them: he was found of them and gave them rest round about, so there was no open general war for a long time after, for national piety procures national blessings. — Matthew Henry
