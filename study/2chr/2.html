Study Notes — 2 Chronicles

Chapter 2

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Solomon determined to build first a house for the name of the Lord, and then a house for his kingdom. Henry notes the order matters: he who is first should be served first, and in both buildings Solomon aimed at the public good, not merely his own convenience. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: Solomon told out seventy thousand burden-bearers, eighty thousand hewers in the mountain, and thirty-six hundred overseers. Henry notes the vast distribution, one hundred and fifty thousand in all, and that so large a fabric, begun and ended in seven years, found work for so many hands. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: Solomon sent to Huram king of Tyre, asking him to deal with him as he had dealt with David, who received cedars for his house. Henry notes we may expect kindness from our fathers' friends, and should cultivate correspondence with them. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: The house was to be dedicated to God for sweet incense, continual showbread, and burnt offerings morning and evening, on sabbaths, new moons and feasts, an ordinance for ever. Henry notes the house was built to be used in God's service, and that the mysteries of true religion, unlike Gentile superstition, coveted not concealment. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: The house Solomon built would be great, for great is our God above all gods. Henry notes the house must be great in proportion to the esteem held for God, though between finite and infinite there can be no true proportion. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: Yet who is able to build God a house, seeing the heaven of heavens cannot contain him? Henry notes Solomon guarded against misapprehension: God does not dwell in temples made with hands, and the house was only for the convenience of priests and worshippers to burn sacrifice before him, with Solomon counting himself unworthy of the work. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: Solomon asked Huram to send a cunning man to work in gold, silver, brass, iron, purple, crimson and blue, and to engrave with the cunning men David had provided. Henry notes that since temple-work must be the best of its kind, Solomon sought the best workmen that could be got, even from Tyre. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: He also asked for cedar, fir and algum trees out of Lebanon, since Huram's servants could skill to cut timber there, with his own servants working alongside. Henry notes no cost or contrivance was to be spared, for the house must be wonderfully great. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: Timber was to be prepared in abundance, for the house Solomon was about to build would be wonderful great. Henry's point is that magnificence here was not vanity but proportion: a great God deserved a stately house, built without sparing. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: Solomon engaged to give the hewers twenty thousand measures each of beaten wheat and barley, and twenty thousand baths each of wine and oil. Henry notes he fed his workmen not with bread and water but plentifully, and that employers should see labourers well paid and well provided for. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: Huram answered in writing that because the Lord loved his people, he had made Solomon king over them. Henry notes princes must see themselves preferred for the public good, not personal satisfaction, and rule so as to prove they were given in love and not in anger. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: Huram blessed the Lord God of Israel, maker of heaven and earth, who had given David a wise son endued with prudence and understanding. Henry notes Huram seems proselyted to Israel's religion, and that when Israel kept close to God's law, neighbouring nations were willing to be instructed by them in true religion. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: Huram sent a cunning man endued with understanding, of Huram his father's. Henry notes he would not fail Solomon's expectations in anything, a workman answering every requirement of the temple work. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: The workman was the son of a woman of Dan, his father a Tyrian, skilful in gold, silver, brass, iron, stone, timber, purple, blue, fine linen and crimson, and in all engraving. Henry notes the mingling of Jewish and Gentile blood as a good omen of Jew and Gentile united in the gospel temple. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: Huram asked that the wheat, barley, oil and wine Solomon had spoken of be sent to his servants. Henry notes Huram signified his dependence on Solomon for the maintenance of the workmen, as Solomon had promised. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: Huram undertook to cut as much wood out of Lebanon as Solomon needed and bring it in floats by sea to Joppa, whence Solomon would carry it up to Jerusalem. Henry notes this agreement matches the account in Kings, with timber delivered at Joppa for the building. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: Solomon numbered all the strangers in Israel, after David's numbering, and they were a hundred and fifty-three thousand six hundred. Henry notes these were proselyted strangers without lands of inheritance, who applied themselves to trades and were bound in gratitude to serve the temple. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: He set seventy thousand to bear burdens, eighty thousand to hew in the mountain, and thirty-six hundred overseers to set the people to work. Henry notes free-born Israelites were not employed in this drudgery, and that the strangers, though bound to serve, were well paid and not to be oppressed. — Matthew Henry
