Study Notes — 2 Chronicles

Chapter 29

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Hezekiah began to reign when he was five and twenty years old, and he reigned nine and twenty years in Jerusalem. And his mother's name was Abijah, daughter of Zechariah. Henry notes Hezekiah came to the crown at twenty-five, of age unlike Joash at seven and Josiah at eight, so he applied himself immediately to reformation, having wept in secret over his father's idolatry and vowed to redress it when he received the congregation. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: And he did that which was right in the sight of the Lord, according to all that David his father had done. Henry notes he did right like David, with David's integrity and zeal and as hearty an affection for the ark and law of God as David ever had, whereas several predecessors did right but not like David. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: He in the first year of his reign, in the first month, opened the doors of the house of the Lord, and repaired them. Henry notes the first thing he did was open the Lord's house doors, for he found Judah low and naked yet made it not his first business to revive civil interests but to restore religion, and those who begin with God begin at the right end and will prosper accordingly. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: And he brought in the priests and the Levites, and gathered them together into the east street. Henry notes Hezekiah immediately threw the church doors open and brought in the priests and Levites, whereas they had not made application to him, which bespeaks their coldness as much as his zeal. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: And said unto them, Hear me, ye Levites, sanctify now yourselves, and sanctify the house of the Lord God of your fathers, and carry forth the filthiness out of the holy place. Henry notes his exhortation begins and ends with engaging the Levites: they must sanctify themselves first, by repenting, reforming and renewing covenant, and then sanctify God's house, making it clean from disuse and profanation and setting it up for its purposes. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: For our fathers have trespassed, and done evil in the eyes of the Lord our God, and have forsaken him, and have turned away their faces from the habitation of the Lord, and turned their backs. Henry notes he laid before them religion's desolations, saying not 'my father' out of tenderness for his father's name, but that God's house had been deserted and his worship let fall, for those who turn their backs on God's ordinances forsake God himself. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: Also they have shut up the doors of the porch, and put out the lamps, and have not burned incense nor offered burnt offerings in the holy place unto the God of Israel. Henry notes the instituted worship had been let fall: lamps not lighted, incense not burnt, and still such neglects are culpable when the word is not duly read and opened, signified by lamps, and prayers and praises not duly offered, signified by incense. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: Wherefore the wrath of the Lord was upon Judah and Jerusalem, and he hath delivered them to trouble, to astonishment, and to hissing, as ye see with your eyes. Henry notes he showed the sad consequences of religion's neglect: this was the cause of all calamities, for God in anger delivered them to trouble, sword and captivity, and under providence's rebukes we should enquire whether we have not neglected God's ordinances. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: For, lo, our fathers have fallen by the sword, and our sons and daughters and wives are in captivity for this. Henry notes the consequences were visible: fathers fallen by the sword, sons, daughters and wives in captivity for this neglect of God's house. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: Now it is in mine heart to make a covenant with the Lord God of Israel, that his fierce wrath may turn away from us. Henry notes he declared his full purpose to revive religion and make it his business, fully resolved to make a covenant to worship God only in his appointed way, sure otherwise his fierce anger would not turn away, and he would bring his people into the bond. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: My sons, be not now negligent, for the Lord hath chosen you to stand before him, to serve him, and that ye should minister unto him, and burn incense. Henry notes he stirred them up: be not now negligent or remiss, for negligence in God's service deceives ourselves, and the quickening consideration is their office, for God chose them to stand before him, not to be idle and enjoy dignity leaving duty to others, but to serve and minister with double diligence. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: Then the Levites arose, Mahath son of Amasai, and Joel son of Azariah, of the Kohathites, and of Merari's sons, Kish son of Abdi, and Azariah son of Jehalelel, and of the Gershonites, Joah son of Zimmah, and Eden son of Joah. Henry notes the persons employed were priests and Levites, and several Levites are named, two of each principal house, Kohath, Gershon and Merari, not merely because chief in place, but because more zealous and active than the rest, for when God has work he raises leading men to preside. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: And of Elizaphan's sons, Shimri, and Jeiel, and of Asaph's sons, Zechariah, and Mattaniah. Henry notes two of each of the three families of singers, Asaph, Heman and Jeduthun, are also named among the zealous leaders, for it is not always the first in place who are most fit or forward for service. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: And of Heman's sons, Jehiel, and Shimei, and of Jeduthun's sons, Shemaiah, and Uzziel. Henry notes Heman's and Jeduthun's sons complete the named leaders, who not only bestirred themselves but gathered their brethren and quickened them to do according to the king's command by the Lord's word. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: And they gathered their brethren, and sanctified themselves, and came, according to the commandment of the king, by the words of the Lord, to cleanse the house of the Lord. Henry notes they did according to the king's command but with an eye to God's word: the king commanded what was already their duty by God's word, and in doing it they regarded God's word as rule and the king's command as spur. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: And the priests went into the inner part of the house of the Lord, to cleanse it, and brought out all the uncleanness found in the Lord's temple into the court of the Lord's house. And the Levites took it, to carry it out abroad into the brook Kidron. Henry notes the work was cleansing God's house from common dirt and from idols and idolatrous altars, a greater pollution than any sewer, and none but priests durst enter the inner part, while Levites, though leaders, did not disdain to be servitors, carrying to Kidron what priests brought to the court. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: Now they began on the first day of the first month to sanctify, and on the eighth day of the month came they to the porch of the Lord, so they sanctified the house of the Lord in eight days, and in the sixteenth day of the first month they made an end. Henry notes the expedition was remarkable: beginning on the first day of the first month, a happy new-year beginning, in eight days they cleansed the temple and in eight more its courts, for those doing good work should rid work and get it done quickly. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: Then they went in to Hezekiah the king, and said, We have cleansed all the house of the Lord, and the altar of burnt offering, with all its vessels, and the shewbread table, with all its vessels. Henry notes their report was agreeable: they gave account of what they had done, boasting not of their care nor coming to be paid, but letting the king know all profaned things were now sanctified according to law and ready for use, insisting most on the altar's readiness, for the good king had set his heart upon it. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: Moreover all the vessels, which king Ahaz in his reign did cast away in his transgression, have we prepared and sanctified, and behold, they are before the altar of the Lord. Henry notes the vessels Ahaz had cast away as vessels of no pleasure they gathered, sanctified and laid before the altar, for though sanctuary vessels may be profaned for a while, God will find a time and way to sanctify them, and neither his ordinances nor his people shall fail for ever. — Matthew Henry

20 COMMENTARY: Then Hezekiah the king rose early, and gathered the rulers of the city, and went up to the house of the Lord. Henry notes as soon as Hezekiah heard the temple was ready he lost no time, rising early to go up, earlier than other days, to show his heart was upon his work there, and a solemn assembly was called the very next day. — Matthew Henry

21 COMMENTARY: And they brought seven bullocks, and seven rams, and seven lambs, and seven he goats, for a sin offering for the kingdom, and for the sanctuary, and for Judah. And he commanded the priests Aaron's sons to offer them on the altar of the Lord. Henry notes atonement must be made for the last reign's sins: they thought it not enough to lament and forsake them but brought a sin-offering for kingdom, sanctuary and Judah, princes, priests and people having all corrupted their way, and they offered seven goats, because the congregation's sins had been very great and long continued, seven being perfection's number. — Matthew Henry

22 COMMENTARY: So they killed the bullocks, and the priests received the blood, and sprinkled it on the altar, likewise, when they had killed the rams, they sprinkled the blood upon the altar, they killed also the lambs, and they sprinkled the blood upon the altar. Henry notes burnt-offerings were offered with sin-offerings, seven bullocks, rams and lambs, to give glory to the God of Israel owned as the only true God, and the blood of those as well as sin-offerings was sprinkled on the altar. — Matthew Henry

23 COMMENTARY: And they brought forth the he goats for the sin offering before the king and the congregation, and they laid their hands upon them. Henry notes the king and congregation, as representatives, laid hands on the goats' heads, thereby owning themselves guilty before God and expressing desire that the sinner's guilt be transferred to the sacrifice, as by faith we lay hands on Christ and receive atonement. — Matthew Henry

24 COMMENTARY: And the priests killed them, and they made reconciliation with their blood upon the altar, to make an atonement for all Israel, for the king commanded that the burnt offering and the sin offering should be made for all Israel. Henry notes the blood made reconciliation for all Israel, not Judah only, for Christ is a propitiation not for Israel's sins only but the whole world's. — Matthew Henry

25 COMMENTARY: And he set the Levites in the house of the Lord with cymbals, with psalteries, and with harps, according to the commandment of David, and of Gad the king's seer, and Nathan the prophet, for so was the commandment of the Lord by his prophets. Henry notes while offerings burned, Levites sang the Lord's song with instruments God by his prophets had commanded, long neglected, for even sorrow for sin must not put us out of tune for praising God. — Matthew Henry

26 COMMENTARY: And the Levites stood with the instruments of David, and the priests with the trumpets. Henry notes Levites stood with David's instruments and priests with trumpets, the music ordered by David's appointment restored to the temple service. — Matthew Henry

27 COMMENTARY: And Hezekiah commanded to offer the burnt offering upon the altar. And when the burnt offering began, the song of the Lord began also with the trumpets, and with the instruments ordained by David king of Israel. Henry notes when the burnt-offering began, the Lord's song began also with trumpets and David's instruments, for prayers and praises must ascend with his offering to be accepted only in virtue of it. — Matthew Henry

28 COMMENTARY: And all the congregation worshipped, and the singers sang, and the trumpeters sounded, and all this continued until the burnt offering was finished. Henry notes the king and all the congregation testified consent and concurrence by bowing heads and worshipping, expressing awful veneration by postures of adoration, for it is not enough to be where God is worshipped if we do not ourselves worship him, and not with bodily exercise only but with the heart. — Matthew Henry

29 COMMENTARY: And when they had made an end of offering, the king and all that were present with him bowed themselves, and worshipped. Henry notes the king and all present bowed and worshipped when offering ended, expressing awful veneration of divine Majesty. — Matthew Henry

30 COMMENTARY: Moreover Hezekiah the king and the princes commanded the Levites to sing praise unto the Lord with the words of David, and of Asaph the seer. And they sang praises with gladness, and they bowed their heads and worshipped. Henry notes the king and princes commanded Levites to sing praise with David's and Asaph's words, and they sang praises with gladness and bowed and worshipped, rejoicing in Christ Jesus as righteousness. — Matthew Henry

31 COMMENTARY: Then Hezekiah answered and said, Now ye have consecrated yourselves unto the Lord, come near and bring sacrifices and thank offerings into the house of the Lord. And the congregation brought in sacrifices and thank offerings, and as many as were of a free heart burnt offerings. Henry notes the day's work looked forward: having consecrated themselves, made atonement and covenant by sacrifice, they were called to pursue communion, bringing sacrifices of prayer, praise and alms, and the people were free, bringing offerings according to what they had, as much as could be expected considering poverty and decayed piety. — Matthew Henry

32 COMMENTARY: And the number of the burnt offerings, which the congregation brought, was threescore and ten bullocks, an hundred rams, and two hundred lambs, all these were for a burnt offering to the Lord. Henry notes some were so generous as to bring burnt-offerings wholly consumed to God's honour, of which the offerer had no part: seventy bullocks, 100 rams and 200 lambs. — Matthew Henry

33 COMMENTARY: And the consecrated things were six hundred oxen and three thousand sheep. Henry notes others brought peace-offerings and thank-offerings, fat burnt on the altar and flesh divided between priests and offerers: 600 oxen and 3,000 sheep, perhaps because remembrance of sin in sacrificing on high places made them more willing to bring sacrifices now to God's altar. — Matthew Henry

34 COMMENTARY: But the priests were too few, so that they could not flay all the burnt offerings, wherefore their brethren the Levites did help them, till the work was ended, and until the other priests had sanctified themselves, for the Levites were more upright in heart to sanctify themselves than the priests. Henry notes priests were too few, many likely suspended as polluted for sacrificing to idols in the last reign, and the rest lacking zeal and taking no care to sanctify themselves, while Levites were more upright to sanctify themselves, better affected and prepared, and so were honoured to help flay offerings, a dispensation of necessity and just disgrace to careless priests. — Matthew Henry

35 COMMENTARY: And also the burnt offerings were in abundance, with the fat of the peace offerings, and the drink offerings for every burnt offering. So the service of the house of the Lord was set in order. Henry notes burnt-offerings were in abundance with fat of peace-offerings and drink-offerings for every burnt-offering, so the Lord's house service was set in order. — Matthew Henry

36 COMMENTARY: And Hezekiah rejoiced, and all the people, that God had prepared the people, for the thing was done suddenly. Henry notes king and people rejoiced in this blessed turn and new face of religion, pleased that it was done suddenly, with ease and without opposition, and that God's hand was plainly in it, for God had prepared the people by secret grace, changing minds that had doted on idolatrous altars to love God's altar, and this is the Lord's doing and marvellous. — Matthew Henry
