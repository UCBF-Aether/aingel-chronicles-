Study Notes — 2 Chronicles

Chapter 32

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: After these things, and the establishment thereof, Sennacherib king of Assyria came, and entered into Judah, and encamped against the fenced cities, and thought to win them for himself. Henry notes Sennacherib, terror and scourge of that part of the world, aimed at boundless monarchy on neighbours' ruins, and thought, as Shalmaneser had mastered Israel, to win Judah, invading immediately after the reformation, for God ordered it then that he might show himself strong for a returning reforming people, and we may be in duty's way yet meet trouble, ordered for trial of confidence and manifestation of God's care. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: And when Hezekiah saw that Sennacherib was come, and that he was purposed to fight against Jerusalem. Henry notes the little opposition Sennacherib met in entering Judah induced him to imagine all was his own, and he purposed to fight against Jerusalem. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: He took counsel with his princes and his mighty men to stop the waters of the fountains which were without the city, and they did help him. Henry notes Hezekiah prudently took counsel with princes, providing that the country should give the invader a cold reception, for he took care he should find no water, or at least scarcity, by which his army would be weakened, for a powerful army wanting water a few days will be but dry dust. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: So there was gathered much people together, who stopped all the fountains, and the brook that ran through the midst of the land, saying, Why should the kings of Assyria come, and find much water? Henry notes all hands were set to stop fountains and the brook running through the land, turning it probably into the city by underground pipes, as destroying forage before an invading army is now practised. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: Also he strengthened himself, and built up all the wall that was broken, and raised it up to the towers, and another wall without, and repaired Millo in the city of David, and made darts and shields in abundance. Henry notes that the city should give him a warm reception, he repaired the wall, raised towers, made darts and shields in abundance, and appointed captains, for those trusting God with safety must yet use proper means, otherwise they tempt him and do not trust him. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: And he set captains of war over the people, and gathered them together to him in the street of the gate of the city, and spake comfortably to them, saying. Henry notes he gathered the people in a broad open street and spoke comfortably to them, himself undaunted and confident the invasion would issue well, putting life into his people, especially captains, and speaking to their heart. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: Be strong and courageous, be not afraid nor dismayed for the king of Assyria, nor for all the multitude that is with him, for there be more with us than with him. Henry notes he endeavoured to keep down fears: be strong and courageous, do not think of surrendering or capitulating, but resolve to hold out to the last man, and keep up faith to silence fears, for Sennacherib has a multitude, yet more are with us than with him, for we have God with us. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: With him is an arm of flesh, but with us is the Lord our God to help us, and to fight our battles. And the people rested themselves upon the words of Hezekiah king of Judah. Henry notes with the enemy is an arm of flesh which he trusts to, but with us is the Lord, whose power is irresistible and promise inviolable, a God in covenant to help us and fight our battles, not only to help us fight them but to fight them for us, and the people rested not merely on his word but on the things he said concerning God's presence and power. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: After this did Sennacherib king of Assyria send his servants to Jerusalem, but he himself laid siege against Lachish, and all his power with him, unto Hezekiah king of Judah, and unto all Judah that were at Jerusalem, saying. Henry notes Sennacherib, hands full besieging Lachish, hearing Hezekiah fortified Jerusalem and encouraged his people to stand out, sent messengers to make speeches and wrote letters to frighten them into surrender before coming in person. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: Thus saith Sennacherib king of Assyria, Whereon do ye trust, that ye abide in the siege in Jerusalem? Henry notes his great malice was to withdraw subjects from allegiance, using mean artifices to terrify the common people and persuade them to desert Hezekiah, representing him as deceiving subjects into ruin. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: Doth not Hezekiah persuade you to give over yourselves to die by famine and by thirst, saying, The Lord our God shall deliver us out of the hand of the king of Assyria? Henry notes he represented Hezekiah as one who designed to deceive subjects into ruin and betray them to famine and thirst, and who held out against a force that would certainly be their ruin. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: Hath not the same Hezekiah taken away his high places and his altars, and commanded Judah and Jerusalem, saying, Ye shall worship before one altar, and burn incense upon it? Henry notes he represented Hezekiah as having done his people great wrong and exposed them to divine displeasure by taking away high places and altars, commanding worship before one altar only. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: Know ye not what I and my fathers have done unto all the people of other lands? were the gods of the nations of those lands any ways able to deliver their lands out of mine hand? Henry notes his great impiety was comparing Jehovah with dunghill gods of nations, boasting his triumphs over their gods, that none could protect their people, and thence inferring how shall your God deliver you. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: Who was there among all the gods of those nations that my fathers utterly destroyed, that could deliver his people out of mine hand, that your God should be able to deliver you out of mine hand? Henry notes he inferred not only how shall your God deliver you, but as if inferior to them all, how much less shall your God deliver you, as if an infinite eternal Spirit had no more wisdom and power than a stone or stock of a tree. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: Now therefore let not Hezekiah deceive you, nor persuade you on this manner, neither yet believe him, for no god of any nation or kingdom was able to deliver his people out of mine hand, and out of the hand of my fathers, how much less shall your God deliver you out of mine hand? Henry notes all this was intended to frighten the people from hope in God, as David's enemies said there is no help for him in God, hoping to take the city by weakening defenders' hands, for Satan aims to destroy faith in God's all-sufficiency. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: And his servants spake yet more against the Lord God, and against his servant Hezekiah. Henry notes Sennacherib's servants, learning insolence from their master, spoke yet more than he bade them against the Lord God and his servant Hezekiah, and God resents what is said against his servants and will reckon for it as well as against himself. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: He wrote also letters to rail on the Lord God of Israel, and to speak against him, saying, As the gods of the nations of other lands have not delivered their people out of mine hand, so shall not the God of Hezekiah deliver his people out of mine hand. Henry notes thus did they rail, rail in writing, which being more deliberate is so much the worse, on the Lord God of Israel, as if he were a cipher and empty name like all the rest. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: Then they cried with a loud voice in the Jews' speech unto the people of Jerusalem that were on the wall, to affright them, and to trouble them, that they might take the city. Henry notes they cried in the Jews' speech to those on the wall to affright and trouble them, that they might take the city by weakening defenders' hands. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: And they spake against the God of Jerusalem, as against the gods of the people of the earth, which were the work of the hands of man. Henry notes the God of Jerusalem, so called because that was the place he chose to put his name and which providence had under special protection, was compared with gods of the earth, the work of men's hands. — Matthew Henry

20 COMMENTARY: And for this cause Hezekiah the king, and the prophet Isaiah son of Amoz, prayed and cried to heaven. Henry notes the duty and interest of the church's friends in distress is to pray and cry to heaven, as Hezekiah and Isaiah joined in prayer, a happy time when king and prophet joined thus, for so we engage God for us and encourage ourselves in him. — Matthew Henry

21 COMMENTARY: And the Lord sent an angel, which cut off all the mighty men of valour, and the leaders and captains in the camp of Assyria's king. So he returned with shame of face to his own land. And when he was come into the house of his god, they that came forth of his own bowels slew him there with the sword. Henry notes the Assyrian army was cut off by an angel's sword, triumphing particularly in mighty men, leaders and captains who defied any man's sword, for God delights to abase the proud, and Sennacherib, having received this disgrace, was cut off by his own sons' sword, mortified first and then murdered, shamed first and then slain. — Matthew Henry

22 COMMENTARY: Thus the Lord saved Hezekiah and Jerusalem's inhabitants from the hand of Sennacherib king of Assyria, and from the hand of all other, and guided them on every side. Henry notes by this wonder God was glorified as his people's protector, saving Jerusalem not only from Sennacherib but from all others, an earnest of much mercy in store, and he guided them, that is, guarded them, on every side. — Matthew Henry

23 COMMENTARY: And many brought gifts unto the Lord to Jerusalem, and presents to Hezekiah king of Judah, so that he was magnified in the sight of all nations from thenceforth. Henry notes for this many brought gifts to the Lord, strangers induced to supplicate his favour and enemies to deprecate his wrath, and Hezekiah was magnified as Heaven's favourite and particular care, many bringing him presents to make interest in him. — Matthew Henry

24 COMMENTARY: In those days Hezekiah was sick to the death, and prayed unto the Lord, and he spake unto him, and he gave him a sign. Henry notes his sickness is briefly mentioned here, more largely in Kings: his disease seemed likely mortal, in extremity he prayed, God answered him and gave him a sign he should recover, the sun going back ten degrees. — Matthew Henry

25 COMMENTARY: But Hezekiah rendered not again according to the benefit done unto him, for his heart was lifted up, therefore there was wrath upon him, and upon Judah and Jerusalem. Henry notes his sin was that his heart was lifted up, proud of honour God put upon him and neighbours did him, and now Babylon's embassy caressing him, so that having destroyed other idolatries he began to idolize himself, and he made so bad a return for favours, making even favours the food and fuel of pride, for which wrath came upon him and his kingdom, though it was but a heart-sin. — Matthew Henry

26 COMMENTARY: Notwithstanding Hezekiah humbled himself for the pride of his heart, both he and Jerusalem's inhabitants, so that the wrath of the Lord came not upon them in the days of Hezekiah. Henry notes he humbled himself for his heart's pride, and Jerusalem's inhabitants humbled themselves with him, for people ought to mourn for rulers' sins, and the wrath came not in his days, so much does repentance avail to put by, or at least put off, God's anger's tokens. — Matthew Henry

27 COMMENTARY: And Hezekiah had exceeding much riches and honour, and he made himself treasuries for silver, and for gold, and for precious stones, and for spices, and for shields, and for all manner of pleasant jewels. Henry notes by providence while he lived he had exceeding riches and honour, replenishing stores, victualling his campus, fortifying his city, and doing all he wished, for God had given him very much substance. — Matthew Henry

28 COMMENTARY: Storehouses also for the increase of corn, and wine, and oil, and stalls for all manner of beasts, and cotes for flocks. Henry notes he had storehouses for corn, wine and oil, stalls for beasts and cotes for flocks, part of his exceeding riches and honour. — Matthew Henry

29 COMMENTARY: Moreover he provided him cities, and possessions of flocks and herds in abundance, for God had given him substance very much. Henry notes he provided cities and possessions of flocks and herds in abundance, for God had given him substance very much, and in general he prospered in all his works, for they were good works. — Matthew Henry

30 COMMENTARY: This same Hezekiah also stopped the upper watercourse of Gihon, and brought it straight down to the west side of the city of David. And Hezekiah prospered in all his works. Henry notes among his great performances, turning Gihon's watercourse is mentioned, done upon Sennacherib's invasion, gathering waters from the old and upper pools into a new place, the lower pool, for the city's greater convenience. — Matthew Henry

31 COMMENTARY: Howbeit in the business of the ambassadors of the princes of Babylon, who sent unto him to enquire of the wonder that was done in the land, God left him, to try him, that he might know all that was in his heart. Henry notes the occasion of his sin was Babylon's embassy to congratulate recovery and enquire of the wonder done in the land, either Assyria's destruction or the sun's going back, and God left him to himself to try him, permitting it for wise and holy ends, that by trial and weakness it might be known what was in his heart, that he was not so perfect in grace as he thought. — Matthew Henry

32 COMMENTARY: Now the rest of the acts of Hezekiah, and his goodness, behold, they are written in the vision of Isaiah the prophet, son of Amoz, and in the book of the kings of Judah and Israel. Henry notes Isaiah wrote his life and reign, his acts and goodness or piety, part of the honour to be recorded and remembered for examples to others. — Matthew Henry

33 COMMENTARY: And Hezekiah slept with his fathers, and they buried him in the chiefest of the sepulchres of David's sons, and all Judah and Jerusalem's inhabitants did him honour at his death. And Manasseh his son reigned in his stead. Henry notes the people did him honour at death, burying him in the chief sepulchres with great burning or great lamentation, as for Josiah, for the honour of serious godliness is manifested in men's consciences, and the memory of those kings was blessed among them. — Matthew Henry
