Study Notes — 2 Chronicles

Chapter 6

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Then said Solomon, The Lord hath said that he would dwell in the thick darkness. Henry notes the cloud of glory had filled the house, and Solomon improved the opportunity, addressing God as now peculiarly nigh at hand, with the house as the residence of that darkness in which God, who dwells in unapproachable light above, manifests himself below. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: But I have built an house of habitation for thee, and a place for thy dwelling for ever. Henry notes Solomon's highest end was God's glory and honour: the house was for the name of the Lord God of Israel, a house of habitation for him. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: And the king turned his face and blessed the whole congregation of Israel, while all the congregation stood. Henry's chapter outline frames this as Solomon's solemn declaration to the satisfaction of the people and the honour of God, both of whom he blessed. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: And he said, Blessed be the Lord God of Israel, who hath with his hands fulfilled what he spake with his mouth to my father David. Henry notes Solomon declared his inducements, which justify and magnify the undertaking, chiefly that he acted in performance of the word God had spoken. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: Since the day God brought Israel out of Egypt, he chose no city to build a house in for his name, nor any man to be ruler over Israel. Henry's declaration traces God's choice through history: no city and no ruler was chosen until Jerusalem and David, in God's own time. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: But I have chosen Jerusalem, that my name might be there, and have chosen David to be over my people Israel. Henry notes Solomon built in compliance with God's choice of Jerusalem as the city where he would record his name, fit to have a building excelling all the rest. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: Now it was in the heart of David my father to build a house for the name of the Lord God of Israel. Henry notes the project was David's, be it known to his honour, and it is no disparagement to pursue the good designs those before us laid and build upon their foundation. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: But the Lord said to David, Forasmuch as it was in thine heart to build a house for my name, thou didst well in that it was in thine heart. Henry notes God approved David's intention though he permitted him not to execute it: thou didst well that it was in thy heart. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: Notwithstanding thou shalt not build the house; but thy son which shall come forth out of thy loins, he shall build the house for my name. Henry notes the service was appointed to Solomon and the honour designed him by divine promise, so he did not act of his own head but was called of God. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: The Lord therefore hath performed his word: I am risen up in the room of David, set on the throne of Israel as the Lord promised, and have built the house for the name of the Lord God of Israel. Henry notes those who see their call clear may go on with great satisfaction, for he who appoints the work appoints the workmen. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: And in it have I put the ark, wherein is the covenant of the Lord, which he made with the children of Israel. Henry's dedication deed is sealed here: the temple was appropriated to God's honour and service, with the ark of the covenant at its heart. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: And he stood before the altar of the Lord in the presence of all the congregation, and spread forth his hands. Henry notes Solomon had signed and sealed the deed of dedication, and now prayed the consecration prayer, by which the temple became a figure of Christ, the great Mediator through whom we offer all prayers. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: For Solomon had made a bronze scaffold five cubits long, five broad and three high, set in the midst of the court; upon it he stood, kneeled down before all Israel, and spread forth his hands toward heaven. Henry's prayer posture is the same: standing before the altar, then kneeling with hands spread toward heaven, in the congregation's sight. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: And said, O Lord God of Israel, there is no God like thee in heaven nor in earth, who keepest covenant and showest mercy to thy servants who walk before thee with all their hearts. Henry lays this down as doctrine: the God of Israel is of incomparable perfection, with no peer, and those who walk before him with all their hearts shall find him faithful and kind. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: Thou which hast kept with thy servant David my father what thou promisedst him, speaking with thy mouth and fulfilling with thine hand, as it is this day. Henry's doctrine applies: God is true to every word spoken, and his servants in sincerity shall find him as good as his word and better. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: Now therefore, O Lord God of Israel, keep with David what thou promisedst, that there shall not fail a man to sit on Israel's throne, yet so that thy children take heed to walk in my law, as thou hast walked before me. Henry notes the prayer is warranted by the word: the prayer that will speed must be warranted by God's promise, here conditioned on walking in his law. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: Now then, O Lord God of Israel, let thy word be verified, which thou hast spoken unto thy servant David. Henry notes Solomon pleads the promise itself, that God's word be verified, as the ground of the whole dedication prayer. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: But will God in very deed dwell with men on the earth? Behold, heaven and the heaven of heavens cannot contain thee; how much less this house which I have built! Henry lays this down as doctrine: God is infinite and immense, whom heaven of heavens cannot contain, and to whose felicity nothing is added by our utmost service. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: Have respect therefore to the prayer of thy servant, and to his supplication, O Lord my God, to hearken unto the cry and prayer thy servant prayeth before thee. Henry notes Solomon asked not that God help them whether they prayed or not, but that he help them in answer to their prayers, even as Christ's intercessions encourage rather than supersede our supplications. — Matthew Henry

20 COMMENTARY: That thine eyes may be open upon this house day and night, upon the place whereof thou hast said thou wouldest put thy name there, to hearken unto the prayer thy servant prayeth toward this place. Henry notes this petition is pertinent because God himself had said he would put his name there; the prayer that will speed must be warranted by the word. — Matthew Henry

21 COMMENTARY: Hearken therefore unto the supplications of thy servant and thy people Israel, which they shall make toward this place: hear thou from thy dwelling place, even from heaven; and when thou hearest, forgive. Henry notes heaven is God's dwelling place still, not the temple, and forgiveness of sins makes way for all other answers to prayer. — Matthew Henry

22 COMMENTARY: If a man sin against his neighbour, and an oath be laid upon him to make him swear, and the oath come before thine altar in this house. Henry notes Solomon supposed controversies would be settled by appeal to God, laying an oath on the person whose testimony must decide, in reverence for an oath as old as conscience. — Matthew Henry

23 COMMENTARY: Then hear thou from heaven, and do, and judge thy servants, by requiting the wicked and justifying the righteous according to his righteousness. Henry notes this petition asks God to give judgment according to equity on all appeals made to him, for God sits on the throne judging right. — Matthew Henry

24 COMMENTARY: And if thy people Israel be put to the worse before the enemy, because they have sinned against thee, and shall return and confess thy name, and pray and make supplication before thee in this house. Henry notes Solomon expected troublesome times to come through sin, and supposed that in affliction those who had not called on God would seek him early and earnestly, confessing name and sin. — Matthew Henry

25 COMMENTARY: Then hear thou from the heavens, and forgive the sin of thy people Israel, and bring them again unto the land thou gavest to them and their fathers. Henry notes this petition asks God to return in mercy when his people repent, reform and seek him, building on God's repeated declarations of readiness to accept penitents. — Matthew Henry

26 COMMENTARY: When heaven is shut up and there is no rain, because they have sinned against thee, yet if they pray toward this place, and confess thy name, and turn from their sin when thou dost afflict them. Henry notes the same supposition: trouble drives to God those who said Depart, and in distress they will confess and turn. — Matthew Henry

27 COMMENTARY: Then hear thou from heaven, and forgive the sin of thy servants and thy people Israel, when thou hast taught them the good way wherein they should walk, and send rain upon thy land. Henry notes the petition pairs forgiveness with teaching the good way and restoring the land's rain, mercy following repentance. — Matthew Henry

28 COMMENTARY: If there be dearth, pestilence, blasting, mildew, locusts or caterpillars, if enemies besiege them, whatsoever sore or sickness there be. Henry notes Solomon supposed affliction in many forms would drive Israel to seek God, listing the troubles that would move a people to prayer. — Matthew Henry

29 COMMENTARY: Then what prayer or supplication soever shall be made of any man, or of all thy people Israel, when every one shall know his own sore and his own grief, and shall spread forth his hands in this house. Henry's affliction case applies: each person knowing his own sore spreads his hands in prayer, and trouble teaches the prayer that prosperity did not. — Matthew Henry

30 COMMENTARY: Then hear thou from heaven thy dwelling place, and forgive, and render unto every man according unto all his ways, whose heart thou knowest; for thou only knowest the hearts of the children of men. Henry lays this down as doctrine: God only knows hearts, all thoughts and affections naked before him, and he renders to every man according to his ways. — Matthew Henry

31 COMMENTARY: That they may fear thee, to walk in thy ways, so long as they live in the land thou gavest unto our fathers. Henry's purpose of forgiveness applies: mercy received should lead to fearing God and walking in his ways in the land of promise. — Matthew Henry

32 COMMENTARY: Moreover concerning the stranger, not of thy people Israel, but come from a far country for thy great name's sake and thy mighty hand and stretched out arm, if they come and pray in this house. Henry notes Solomon supposed strangers would come from afar to worship Israel's God, considering how worthless the nations' gods were and what proofs Israel's God had given of being Lord of the whole earth. — Matthew Henry

33 COMMENTARY: Then hear thou from the heavens, even from thy dwelling place, and do according to all the stranger calleth to thee for, that all people of the earth may know thy name and fear thee, as Israel does, and may know this house is called by thy name. Henry notes the petition bids strangers welcome, for if there be one law in duty, why not one law in privilege for stranger and native-born? — Matthew Henry

34 COMMENTARY: If thy people go out to war against their enemies by the way thou shalt send them, and they pray toward this city thou hast chosen and the house I have built for thy name. Henry notes Solomon prayed God would on all occasions own and plead Israel's cause against opposers, when they went out by his sending. — Matthew Henry

35 COMMENTARY: Then hear thou from the heavens their prayer and supplication, and maintain their cause. Henry notes this petition, repeated in verse 39, asks God to maintain his people's cause, for if they are the Israel of God, their cause is God's cause. — Matthew Henry

36 COMMENTARY: If they sin against thee, for there is no man which sinneth not, and thou be angry and deliver them over before their enemies, and they carry them away captives unto a land far off or near. Henry lays this down as doctrine: there is no sinless perfection in this life, no man who does good and sins not. — Matthew Henry

37 COMMENTARY: Yet if they bethink themselves in the land of captivity, and turn and pray unto thee there, saying, We have sinned, we have done amiss, and have dealt wickedly. Henry's repentance petition applies: in captivity itself, bethinking and confessing sin is the turning God waits for. — Matthew Henry

38 COMMENTARY: If they return to thee with all their heart and soul in the land of captivity, and pray toward their land, the city thou hast chosen, and the house I have built for thy name. Henry notes the petition asks God to return in mercy when his people return with all heart and soul and pray toward the chosen place. — Matthew Henry

39 COMMENTARY: Then hear thou from the heavens, even from thy dwelling place, their prayer and supplications, and maintain their cause, and forgive thy people which have sinned against thee. Henry notes this combines the petitions: maintain their cause and forgive, for forgiveness makes way for every other answer. — Matthew Henry

40 COMMENTARY: Now, my God, let thine eyes be open, and let thine ears be attent unto the prayer made in this place. Henry's opening petition returns as the prayer's close: God's eyes open on the house day and night, and his ears attentive to prayer made there. — Matthew Henry

41 COMMENTARY: Now therefore arise, O Lord God, into thy resting place, thou, and the ark of thy strength: let thy priests be clothed with salvation, and let thy saints rejoice in goodness. Henry notes Solomon borrowed these words from his father's psalm: that God take and keep possession of the temple as his resting place, that ministers be made public blessings clothed with salvation, and that saints rejoice in the goodness of God's house. — Matthew Henry

42 COMMENTARY: O Lord God, turn not away the face of thine anointed: remember the mercies of David thy servant. Henry notes Solomon pleads his relation as God's anointed and God's covenant mercies to David, and we may plead the same with an eye to Christ, God's anointed, whose face is never turned away, and whose mercies are the sure mercies of David. — Matthew Henry
