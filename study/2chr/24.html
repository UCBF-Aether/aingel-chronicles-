Study Notes — 2 Chronicles

Chapter 24

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Joash was seven years old when he began to reign, and he reigned forty years in Jerusalem. His mother's name also was Zibiah of Beersheba. Henry notes his reign began in the spirit but ended in the flesh, its progress and termination not of a piece with its beginning, though he was wonderfully preserved for the throne. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: And Joash did that which was right in the sight of the Lord all the days of Jehoiada the priest. Henry notes it is a happy thing for young people setting out in the world to be under wise, good, faithful direction, as Joash was under Jehoiada, during whose time he did right, and hearkening to such counsel is wisdom, not weakness. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: And Jehoiada took for him two wives, and he begat sons and daughters. Henry notes it was prudent for young people to take advice in marriages, as Joash left choosing his wives to his guardian, because Jezebel and Athaliah had been such plagues, and marriage often proves the making or marring of young people. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: And it came to pass after this, that Joash was minded to repair the house of the Lord. Henry notes the repairing of churches is a good work all in their places should promote, for the decency and convenience of religious assemblies, and Joash was more solicitous about it than Jehoiada himself. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: And he gathered the priests and Levites, and said to them, Go out unto Judah's cities, and gather of all Israel money to repair the house of your God from year to year, and see that ye hasten the matter. Howbeit the Levites hastened it not. Henry notes Joash reproved Jehoiada for remissness when money did not come in, for it is easier to build temples than to be temples to God. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: And the king called for Jehoiada the chief, and said unto him, Why hast thou not required of the Levites to bring in out of Judah and Jerusalem the collection, according to Moses' commandment and the congregation's, for the tabernacle of witness? Henry notes the king pressed the Moses collection for the tabernacle of witness, showing his zeal for the repair, though outward zeal may outstrip inward principle. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: For the sons of Athaliah, that wicked woman, had broken up the house of God, and also all the dedicated things of the house of the Lord did they bestow upon Baalim. Henry notes the temple had been broken up and its dedicated things given to Baalim under Athaliah's house, which is why repair was urgent. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: And at the king's commandment they made a chest, and set it without at the gate of the house of the Lord. Henry notes when one way of raising money failed Joash tried another: a chest with a hole in the lid, a way not used before, whose very novelty perhaps made it successful. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: And they made a proclamation through Judah and Jerusalem, to bring in to the Lord the collection that Moses the servant of God laid upon Israel in the wilderness. Henry notes many a good work lies undone for want of a few active men to stir it forward, and Joash's proclamation answered the intention. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: And all the princes and all the people rejoiced, and brought in, and cast into the chest, until they had made an end. Henry notes a great deal was thrown in with great cheerfulness, for they all rejoiced, and an invention to please people's humour may sometimes bring them to their duty. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: Now it came to pass, that at what time the chest was brought unto the king's office by the Levites, and when they saw much money, the king's scribe and the high priest's officer came and emptied the chest, and took it, and carried it to his place again. Thus they did day by day, and gathered money in abundance. Henry notes the king and Jehoiada faithfully paid money to the workmen, and faithfulness is the greatest praise and comfort of those entrusted with public treasure. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: And the king and Jehoiada gave it to such as did the work of the service of the house of the Lord, and hired masons and carpenters to repair the house of the Lord, and also such as wrought iron and brass to mend the house. Henry notes the king and Jehoiada faithfully paid the workmen, who faithfully did the work, masons, carpenters and smiths together. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: So the workmen wrought, and the work was perfected by them, and they set the house of God in his state, and strengthened it. Henry notes the workmen faithfully did the work, perfecting and strengthening God's house, a good work promoted by all in their places. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: And when they had finished it, they brought the rest of the money before the king and Jehoiada, whereof were made vessels for the house of the Lord, even vessels to minister, and to offer withal, and spoons, and vessels of gold and silver. And they offered burnt offerings in the house of the Lord continually all the days of Jehoiada. Henry notes with the rest, vessels for ministry and offering were made, and burnt offerings continued all Jehoiada's days, the temple service revived. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: But Jehoiada waxed old, and was full of days when he died, an hundred and thirty years old was he when he died. Henry notes it was a mercy that Jehoiada lived so long, 130 years, born in Solomon's time, living through six entire reigns before this, and his death was a great judgment to prince and people, for the little religion Joash had was buried in his grave. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: And they buried him in the city of David among the kings, because he had done good in Israel, both toward God, and toward his house. Henry notes he was buried among kings with the encomium that he had done good in Israel, toward God and toward his house in reviving temple service, and it is the greatest honour to do good in our generations. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: Now after the death of Jehoiada came the princes of Judah, and made obeisance to the king. Then the king hearkened unto them. Henry notes bad counsellors got about Joash: the princes of Judah wheedled and flattered him, congratulating him on release from his old tutor's discipline, promising his royal will should pass for law, and he hearkened to discourse more agreeable than Jehoiada's dictates. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: And they left the house of the Lord God of their fathers, and served groves and idols, and wrath came upon Judah and Jerusalem for this their trespass. Henry notes the king and princes, who lately repaired the temple, now forsook it and served groves and idols they had pulled down, for man is so inconstant and little confidence is to be put in him. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: Yet he sent prophets to them, to bring them again unto the Lord, and they testified against them, but they would not give ear. Henry notes God left not himself without witness even in degenerate times: he sent prophets to convince and instruct, assuring favour if they would return, for he would rather sinners turn and live, but they slighted all prophets and would not give ear. — Matthew Henry

20 COMMENTARY: And the Spirit of God came upon Zechariah son of Jehoiada the priest, which stood above the people, and said unto them, Thus saith God, Why transgress ye the commandments of the Lord, that ye cannot prosper? because ye have forsaken the Lord, he hath also forsaken you. Henry notes Zechariah, filled with the spirit of prophecy, stood up plainly but without provoking language, reminding them from the law of the precept they broke and the penalty incurred: they cannot prosper in evil, and because they forsook the Lord he hath forsaken them. — Matthew Henry

21 COMMENTARY: And they conspired against him, and stoned him with stones at the commandment of the king in the court of the house of the Lord. Henry notes by conspiracy of the princes and the king's command they stoned him in a popular tumult in the temple court, a horrid wickedness: person sacred, place sacred, message sacred, reproof just and delivery gentle, yet nothing less than the prophet's blood satisfied their indignation. — Matthew Henry

22 COMMENTARY: Thus Joash remembered not the kindness which Jehoiada his father had done to him, but slew his son. And when he died, he said, The Lord look upon it, and require it. Henry notes the aggravation: Zechariah was Jehoiada's son, who had been as a father to Joash, and Joash remembered not the father's kindness but slew the son for doing his duty, while the martyr's cry, the Lord look upon it and require it, came not from revenge but prophecy, and his blood was quickly reckoned for. — Matthew Henry

23 COMMENTARY: And it came to pass at the end of the year, that the host of Syria came up against him, and they came to Judah and Jerusalem, and destroyed all the princes of the people, and sent all the spoil unto the king of Damascus. Henry notes a small Syrian army mastered Jerusalem, destroyed the princes and plundered the city, sending spoil to Damascus, as instruments in God's hand executing judgment on Joash. — Matthew Henry

24 COMMENTARY: For the army of the Syrians came with a small company of men, and the Lord delivered a very great host into their hand, because they had forsaken the Lord God of their fathers. So they executed judgment against Joash. Henry notes while they kept in with God they had often conquered against greater numbers, but now a handful routed a great host because they had forsaken God, and God not only departed but turned to be their enemy and fought against them. — Matthew Henry

25 COMMENTARY: And when they were departed from him, for they left him in great diseases, his own servants conspired against him for the blood of the sons of Jehoiada the priest, and slew him on his bed, and he died, and they buried him in the city of David, but they buried him not in the sepulchres of the kings. Henry notes God smote him with great diseases, and when he thought to escape death by sickness he met it by the sword, his servants slaying him in his bed for the blood of Jehoiada's sons, and his people would not bury him among the kings because he had stained his honour. — Matthew Henry

26 COMMENTARY: And these are they that conspired against him: Zabad son of Shimeath an Ammonitess, and Jehozabad son of Shimrith a Moabitess. Henry notes the regicides are named, and both their mothers were foreigners, one an Ammonitess and one a Moabitess, showing how prohibited marriages countenanced by idolatrous kings resulted in their own destruction. — Matthew Henry

27 COMMENTARY: Now concerning his sons, and the greatness of the burdens laid upon him, and the repairing of the house of God, behold, they are written in the story of the book of the kings. And Amaziah his son reigned in his stead. Henry notes these judgments are called the burdens laid upon him, for God's wrath is a heavy burden, or the threatenings denounced by the prophets, and God sets special marks of displeasure upon apostates for warning to all. — Matthew Henry
