Study Notes — 2 Chronicles

Chapter 10

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: And Rehoboam went to Shechem, for to Shechem were all Israel come to make him king. Henry notes the chapter copies Kings almost verbatim, and frames Rehoboam's folly in treating with the tribes, the people's wickedness in forsaking him, and God's justice in fulfilling his counsel. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: And it came to pass, when Jeroboam son of Nebat, who was in Egypt whither he fled from Solomon, heard it, that Jeroboam returned out of Egypt. Henry's frame applies: the turbulent spirits who would complain of government gathered around Jeroboam, returned from exile, as the ferment began. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: And they sent and called him, so Jeroboam and all Israel came and spake to Rehoboam, saying. Henry notes turbulent and ungrateful spirits will find fault with government and complain of grievances when they have very little reason, though they had peace and plenty in Solomon's time. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: Thy father made our yoke grievous: now therefore ease thou somewhat the grievous servitude of thy father, and his heavy yoke that he put upon us, and we will serve thee. Henry notes they complained Solomon made their yoke grievous, though they were never plundered by invaders nor put in fear of war, and had meat and money enough. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: And he said unto them, Come again unto me after three days. And the people departed. Henry notes Rehoboam took three days to treat with them, a pause in which counsel, wise or foolish, would decide the kingdom. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: And King Rehoboam took counsel with the old men that had stood before Solomon his father while he yet lived, saying, What counsel give ye me to return answer to this people? Henry notes moderate counsels are generally wisest: the old experienced counsellors directed him to gentleness, which does what violence will not. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: And they spake unto him, saying, If thou be kind to this people, and please them, and speak good words to them, they will be thy servants for ever. Henry notes good words cost nothing but a little self-denial, yet purchase good things, and most people love to be accosted mildly. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: But he forsook the counsel the old men gave him, and took counsel with the young men brought up with him, that stood before him. Henry notes many ruin themselves by trampling on and provoking their inferiors: Rehoboam wore his father's crown but wanted his father's brains, and ought to have taken other measures. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: And he said unto them, What advice give ye that we may return answer to this people, which have spoken to me, saying, Ease somewhat the yoke that thy father did put upon us? Henry notes Rehoboam, having forsaken the old men's counsel, turned to young men brought up with him, whose advice would ruin him. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: And the young men brought up with him spake, saying, Thus shalt thou answer: My little finger shall be thicker than my father's loins. Henry notes God often fulfils his own counsel by infatuating men and giving them up to the counsels of their own folly, for no more is needed to ruin men than to leave them to pride and passion. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: For whereas my father put a heavy yoke upon you, I will put more to your yoke: my father chastised you with whips, but I will chastise you with scorpions. Henry notes Rehoboam paid dearly for threatening and talking big, thinking to carry matters with a high hand, though he lacked his father's skill to manage the people. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: So Jeroboam and all the people came to Rehoboam on the third day, as the king bade, saying, Come again to me on the third day. Henry's second section begins here: public affairs in a ferment, with the people's return to hear the answer that would split the kingdom. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: And the king answered them roughly, and King Rehoboam forsook the counsel of the old men. Henry notes rough answers stir up anger and bring oil to the flames, and violent proceedings in a ferment only make bad worse, for the pilot must steer steadily in a storm. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: And answered them after the advice of the young men, saying, My father made your yoke heavy, but I will add thereto: my father chastised you with whips, but I will chastise you with scorpions. Henry notes the rough answer followed the young men's counsel, and many have been driven to mischief they did not intend by being too severely dealt with. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: So the king hearkened not unto the people, for the cause was of God, that the Lord might perform his word, which he spake by Ahijah the Shilonite to Jeroboam. Henry notes whatever men's devices are, God is doing his own work and fulfilling his word, not one iota falling to the ground, yet this does not excuse Rehoboam's folly or lessen his guilt. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: And when all Israel saw the king would not hearken, the people answered, What portion have we in David? and we have none inheritance in the son of Jesse: every man to your tents, O Israel: and now, David, see to thine own house. So all Israel went to their tents. Henry notes worldly wealth, honour and dominion are uncertain: Solomon's good services were forgotten at once, and the government of Christ is likewise cast off by many despite all he has done. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: But as for the children of Israel that dwelt in the cities of Judah, Rehoboam reigned over them. Henry notes that while Solomon's iniquity was remembered and his son lost ten tribes, David's piety was not forgotten, nor the promise to him, and for his sake two tribes were preserved to his grandson. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: Then King Rehoboam sent Hadoram that was over the tribute, and Israel stoned him with stones, that he died. But Rehoboam made speed to get him up to his chariot, to flee to Jerusalem. Henry's revolt account applies: the people's wickedness in forsaking Rehoboam turned violent, and the king himself fled to Jerusalem. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: And Israel rebelled against the house of David unto this day. Henry notes God often visits fathers' iniquities upon children: Solomon forsook God, and therefore his son after him was forsaken by the greatest part of his people, a penal consequence lasting visibly after the sinner's death. — Matthew Henry
