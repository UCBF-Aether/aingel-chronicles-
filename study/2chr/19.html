Study Notes — 2 Chronicles

Chapter 19

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: And Jehoshaphat king of Judah returned to his house in peace to Jerusalem. Henry notes this was a great favour: he fared better than he expected after imminent peril, better than he deserved being out of duty's way, and better than Ahab, brought home slain, for God distinguishes the righteous from the ungodly, one taken and the other left. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: And Jehu son of Hanani the seer went out to meet him, and said, Shouldest thou help the ungodly, and love them that hate the Lord? Therefore is wrath upon thee from before the Lord. Henry notes it is great mercy to be told our faults in time: Jehu, son of Hanani who was stocked for plain dealing under Asa, was not afraid to reprove another king, and told Jehoshaphat plainly he had done very ill in joining Ahab, for God's people must not debase themselves by intimacy with haters of God. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: Nevertheless there are good things found in thee, in that thou hast taken away the groves out of the land, and hast prepared thine heart to seek God. Henry notes reproof should notice what is praiseworthy: his abolishing idolatry with a heart fixed to seek God was a good thing God accepted, so though displeased, God would not cast him off. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: And Jehoshaphat dwelt at Jerusalem, and he went out again through the people from Beersheba to Mount Ephraim, and brought them back unto the Lord God of their fathers. Henry notes he took reproof well, unlike his father with the seer, dwelt at home minding his own business, and made a pious visitation of his kingdom to atone for his visit to Ahab, reviving reformation and repairing damage his example may have done, for true repentance recovers those hardened by our example. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: And he set judges in the land throughout all the fenced cities of Judah, city by city. Henry notes having sent preachers to instruct, Jehoshaphat now sent judges to execute laws and be a terror to evil-doers, new-modelling courts whose judges had neglected business or been slighted, with new men and a new charge. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: And said to the judges, Take heed what ye do: for ye judge not for man, but for the Lord, who is with you in the judgment. Henry notes the charge prescribes caution, for judges above all must be cautious since so much depends on correctness, and piety, for the fear of God restrains from wrong and engages to duty, with motives from God: their commission is from him, his eye is upon them, and he is the example of justice. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: Wherefore now let the fear of the Lord be upon you; take heed and do it: for there is no iniquity with the Lord our God, nor respect of persons, nor taking of gifts. Henry notes magistrates are called gods and must resemble God, with whom there is no iniquity, bribery or respect of persons, and the fear of God will make them a terror to none but evil-doers. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: Moreover in Jerusalem did Jehoshaphat set of the Levites, and of the priests, and of the chief of the fathers of Israel, for the judgment of the Lord, and for controversies, when they returned to Jerusalem. Henry notes he erected a supreme court at Jerusalem, under the king's inspection, for pleas of the crown, the judgment of the Lord, and common pleas, controversies between party and party, with learned Levites and priests and chief fathers as judges. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: And he charged them, saying, Thus shall ye do in the fear of the Lord, faithfully, and with a perfect heart. Henry notes the supreme court's charge: act from a good principle, in the fear of the Lord always before them, then faithfully, conscientiously, and with a perfect upright heart. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: And what cause soever shall come to you of your brethren in your cities, between blood and blood, between law and commandment, statutes and judgments, ye shall even warn them that they trespass not against the Lord, and so wrath come upon you and your brethren: this do, and ye shall not trespass. Henry notes their great care must be to prevent sin, warning people not to trespass against the Lord, for wrath would come on people if they sinned and on magistrates if they did not punish it, and those with power contract guilt if they do not use it to restrain sin. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: And behold, Amariah the chief priest is over you in all matters of the Lord, and Zebadiah son of Ishmael, ruler of Judah, for all the king's matters, also Levites shall be officers before you. Deal courageously, and the Lord shall be with the good. Henry notes Amariah presided in ecclesiastical causes and Zebadiah in civil, Levites served as officers bringing causes and executing sentences, and the charge closed with resolution: deal courageously, fear not man's face, for the Lord shall be with the good. — Matthew Henry
