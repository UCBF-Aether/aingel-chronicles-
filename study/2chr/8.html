Study Notes — 2 Chronicles

Chapter 8

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: And it came to pass at the end of twenty years, wherein Solomon had built the house of the Lord and his own house. Henry notes Solomon spent his days not in contemplation but in action, in his country building cities, and having begun with God's house and made quick work of it, he prospered in all his undertakings. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: That the cities which Huram had restored to Solomon, Solomon built them, and caused Israel to dwell there. Henry's building account applies: Solomon, a man of business who did not consult his own ease, built and settled cities in a time of peace, preparing for war as in summer one provides food for winter. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: And Solomon went to Hamathzobah, and prevailed against it. Henry groups this with Solomon's building and fortifying work, the action of a king securing his dominion while at peace. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: And he built Tadmor in the wilderness, and all the store cities which he built in Hamath. Henry notes Solomon built all he desired to build, and divine providence gratified even his humour, giving success not only in what he needed but in what he had a mind to build. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: Also he built Bethhoron the upper and Bethhoron the nether, fenced cities, with walls, gates and bars. Henry notes Solomon fortified cities in peace against war, a man's business as much as providing food in summer for winter. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: And Baalath, and all the store cities, and all the chariot cities, and the cities of the horsemen, and all that Solomon desired to build in Jerusalem, in Lebanon, and throughout his dominion. Henry notes Solomon knew how to set bounds to his desires: he finished all he desired and then desired no more, unlike those who enlarge desires endlessly. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: As for all the people left of the Hittites, Amorites, Perizzites, Hivites and Jebusites, which were not of Israel. Henry notes there were a great many strangers in Israel, remnants of the Canaanites, welcome to live there but not to live and do nothing. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: But of their children, left after them in the land, whom Israel consumed not, them did Solomon make to pay tribute until this day. Henry notes it is a state's interest to promote industry and keep subjects from idleness, and these strangers were set to work and tribute. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: But of the children of Israel did Solomon make no servants for his work; but they were men of war, and chief of his captains, and captains of his chariots and horsemen. Henry's contrast applies: Israelites were not made servants for the work, but served as warriors and captains, while strangers supplied the labour. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: And these were the chief of King Solomon's officers, even two hundred and fifty, that bare rule over the people. Henry notes Solomon, a man of business himself, employed a great many hands and kept abundance of people to work, under officers ruling over them. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: And Solomon brought up the daughter of Pharaoh out of the city of David unto the house he had built for her, for he said, My wife shall not dwell in the house of David, because the places are holy, whereunto the ark of the Lord hath come. Henry notes Solomon consulted his father's memory: the place where the ark had been so long and publicly attended was too venerable for the gaiety, not to say iniquity, of Pharaoh's daughter's court, and the ancient landmarks between sacred and common ought to be kept up. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: Then Solomon offered burnt offerings unto the Lord on the altar he had built before the porch. Henry notes the building of the temple was in order to its service: in vain had the altar been built and fire come down, if sacrifices had not been constantly brought as food of the altar and fuel of that fire. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: Even after a certain rate every day, offering according to Moses' commandment, on sabbaths, new moons, and solemn feasts three times in the year, unleavened bread, weeks, and tabernacles. Henry notes daily sacrifices as duly as the day came, weekly double on the sabbath, monthly on new moons, and yearly at the three feasts, a settled method of devotion. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: And he appointed, according to David his father's order, the courses of the priests to their service, and Levites to their charges, to praise and minister before the priests, as every day required, and porters by courses at every gate, for so had David the man of God commanded. Henry notes Solomon, though builder of the temple, did not amend or add to what David the man of God commanded, but closely adhered to it and used his authority to have it duly observed. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: And they departed not from the commandment of the king unto the priests and Levites concerning any matter, or concerning the treasures. Henry notes Solomon observed God's laws, and then all obeyed his orders, none departing from the king's commandment. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: Now all the work of Solomon was prepared unto the day of the foundation of the house of the Lord, and until it was finished. So the house of the Lord was perfected. Henry notes the work was the main matter, not the place: the temple was unfinished till the service was put into good order, and then the house was perfected. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: Then went Solomon to Eziongeber and to Eloth, at the seaside in the land of Edom. Henry notes Solomon visited the seaport towns in person, for those who deal much in the world will find it their interest to inspect their affairs themselves and see with their own eyes. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: And Huram sent him by his servants ships, and servants that had knowledge of the sea, and they went with Solomon's servants to Ophir, and took thence four hundred and fifty talents of gold, and brought them to King Solomon. Henry notes Canaan, though rich, must send to Ophir for gold, and Israel, though wise, must be beholden to Tyre for seamen, teaching that grace, not gold, is the best riches, and acquaintance with God and his law the best knowledge. — Matthew Henry
