Study Notes — 2 Chronicles

Chapter 22

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: And the inhabitants of Jerusalem made Ahaziah his youngest son king in his stead, for the band with the Arabians had slain all the eldest. So Ahaziah son of Jehoram king of Judah reigned. Henry notes one son and one wife of Jehoram were left, Ahaziah and Athaliah, both reserved to be the shame and plague of his family, Ahaziah as partaker in Ahab's sin and destruction. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: Forty and two years old was Ahaziah when he began to reign, and he reigned one year in Jerusalem. His mother's name also was Athaliah daughter of Omri. Henry notes the forty-two cannot be his age, since his father died at forty, and Kings says twenty-two; some take it as his mother Athaliah's age, put for his in reproach because she managed him and in effect reigned, while many expositors allow a transcriber's mistake of forty-two for twenty-two. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: He also walked in the ways of the house of Ahab, for his mother was his counsellor to do wickedly. Henry notes his reign, summed up in two clauses, was that his mother and her relations counselled him to do wickedly, and it was to his destruction, for he walked in Ahab's way and worshipped Baalim and Ashtaroth. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: Wherefore he did evil in the sight of the Lord like the house of Ahab, for they were his counsellors after his father's death to his destruction. Henry notes after his father's death Ahab's house was industrious to keep him to idolatry, lest his father's miserable end deter him, making him seven times more a child of hell, and he gave himself up to be led by them, forsaking divine guidance. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: He walked also after their counsel, and went with Jehoram son of Ahab king of Israel to war against Hazael king of Syria at Ramothgilead, and the Syrians smote Joram. Henry notes those who counsel wickedly counsel to destruction: they exposed him to Syria's sword, drawing him into an expedition to Ramoth-Gilead where Joram was wounded, not for his honour. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: And he returned to be healed in Jezreel because of the wounds given him at Ramah, when he fought with Hazael king of Syria. And Azariah son of Jehoram king of Judah went down to see Jehoram son of Ahab at Jezreel, because he was sick. Henry notes Ahaziah came on a visit to Joram just at the time Jehu was executing God's judgment on Ahab's family, and so was cut off with them, for bad company brings not only infection but destruction. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: And the destruction of Ahaziah was of God by coming to Joram, for when he was come, he went out with Jehoram against Jehu son of Nimshi, whom the Lord had anointed to cut off the house of Ahab. Henry notes God's providence brought Ahaziah to Joram at this fatal juncture, that he might fall with him and be taken as in a snare, acknowledging God's justice in it. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: And it came to pass, when Jehu was executing judgment upon the house of Ahab, and found the princes of Judah, and the sons of Ahaziah's brethren, that ministered to Ahaziah, he slew them. Henry notes Jehu, executing judgment on Ahab's house, slew Judah's princes and Ahaziah's brethren's sons who ministered to him, part of the common ruin of Ahab's intimacy. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: And he sought Ahaziah, and they caught him, for he was hid in Samaria, and brought him to Jehu, and when they had slain him, they buried him, because, said they, he is the son of Jehoshaphat, who sought the Lord with all his heart. So Ahaziah's house had no power to keep still the kingdom. Henry notes he was decently buried, unlike Jehoram, because he was Jehoshaphat's grandson who sought the Lord with his heart, so the just are remembered with honour and respect shown even to degenerate seed for their sake. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: But when Athaliah mother of Ahaziah saw that her son was dead, she arose and destroyed all the seed royal of the house of Judah. Henry's chapter outline names Athaliah as the plague of Jehoram's family: she destroyed all the seed royal and usurped the throne. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: But Jehoshabeath, daughter of the king, took Joash son of Ahaziah, and stole him from among the king's sons that were slain, and put him and his nurse in a bedchamber. So Jehoshabeath, daughter of Jehoram, wife of Jehoiada the priest, sister of Ahaziah, hid him from Athaliah, so that she slew him not. Henry's Athaliah account applies: amid the destruction of the seed royal, Joash was stolen away and hidden by Jehoshabeath, preserving David's line. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: And he was with them hid in the house of God six years, and Athaliah reigned over the land. Henry notes Athaliah usurped the throne and reigned over the land, while Joash remained hidden in God's house for six years, the seed royal preserved in the temple. — Matthew Henry
