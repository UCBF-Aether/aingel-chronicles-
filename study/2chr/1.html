Study Notes — 2 Chronicles

Chapter 1

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Solomon was strengthened in his kingdom because the Lord his God was with him and magnified him exceedingly. Henry notes that though his title was contested, God's presence strengthened his heart, his hands and his interest in the people, and God's presence will be our strength. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: Solomon spoke to all Israel, to the captains, judges, governors and chief of the fathers, summoning them to worship with him. Henry notes that authority is well bestowed when used for God's glory, and that magistrates are likely to do well when they take God along with them at their setting out. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: Solomon and all the congregation went to the high place at Gibeon, where the tabernacle of the congregation was. Henry notes Solomon began his reign with this public, pious visit to God's altar, a very good omen, and that it is desirable to have many join in solemn worship, the more the better. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: The ark of God, however, David had brought up to Jerusalem, to the tent he had prepared for it. Henry notes the division: David kept mostly to the ark at Jerusalem, while Solomon honoured the tabernacle and its altars, and neither man's zeal should be censured for differing in its instance. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: The bronze altar Bezaleel had made stood before the tabernacle of the Lord at Gibeon. Henry notes Solomon brought his sacrifices thither because it is the altar that sanctifies every gift, and JFB adds that this was the legally appointed spot, on the time-honoured altar made in the wilderness. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: Solomon went up to the bronze altar and offered a thousand burnt offerings upon it. Henry notes the abundance: where God sows plentifully he expects to reap accordingly, and Solomon gave God his dues out of the flocks and herds David had left him. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: In that night God appeared to Solomon and said, Ask what I shall give thee. Henry notes God both put him in the way of receiving and tried him: men's characters appear in their choices, and asking what we would have tries a man as much as asking what he would do. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: Solomon answered by recalling God's great mercy to David his father, and that God had made him reign in his stead. Henry notes the plea: God who put him in David's place could in faith be asked for grace to do its duty, and the eminence of those before us should quicken prayer for wisdom. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: Solomon prayed that the promise to David his father be established, since he was made king over a people like the dust of the earth in multitude. Henry notes God's promises are our best pleas in prayer: wisdom was not itself promised, but was necessary for building the house and establishing the throne. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: Give me now wisdom and knowledge, that I may go out and come in before this people, Solomon prayed, for who can judge so great a people? Henry notes he chose spiritual blessings rather than temporal ones, owning wisdom a desirable gift and God its giver, to be sought for the work God calls us to. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: God answered that because this was in Solomon's heart, and he had not asked for riches, wealth, honour or the life of his enemies, his request would be granted. Henry notes wisdom is given freely to those who value it and resolve to use it, and God upbraids not petitioners with their folly. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: Wisdom and knowledge were granted, and with them riches, wealth and honour such as no king before or after had. Henry notes God gave the wealth and honour Solomon did not ask for precisely because he asked not for them: those who make the other world their end gain this world as well, in good measure. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: Then Solomon came from Gibeon to Jerusalem, from before the tabernacle, and reigned over Israel. Henry's concise summary frames the chapter's second movement: having honoured God by sacrifice and prayer, Solomon now honoured Israel by increasing its strength, wealth and trade. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

14 COMMENTARY: Solomon gathered fourteen hundred chariots and twelve thousand horsemen, stationed in chariot cities and with the king at Jerusalem. JFB notes his passion for horses exceeded any Israelite monarch's, a prohibited indulgence whether for luxury or power, and that the establishment served trade as well as show. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: The king made silver and gold at Jerusalem as plentiful as stones, and cedar trees as the sycamore trees of the valley. Henry's concise point covers the whole passage: Solomon's choice of wisdom was blessed with strength and wealth, and those who refer themselves to God's providence have the most comfort in what they have. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

16 COMMENTARY: Solomon had horses brought out of Egypt, and linen yarn, the king's merchants receiving the yarn at a price. JFB notes the Egyptian breed was highly valued, as fine as the Arabian but larger and more powerful, and that Solomon perceived the commercial advantage of controlling this trade through Judea. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: A chariot was brought up out of Egypt for six hundred shekels of silver, and a horse for a hundred and fifty, and so for the kings of the Hittites and of Syria. JFB notes the chariot cost four times a horse, and that Solomon's agents bought in Egyptian markets and sold onward, establishing a monopoly through his chariot cities. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
