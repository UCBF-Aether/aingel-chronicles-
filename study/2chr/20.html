Study Notes — 2 Chronicles

Chapter 20

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: It came to pass after this also, that Moab, Ammon, and others beside the Ammonites, came against Jehoshaphat to battle. Henry notes Jehoshaphat, well employed in reforming his kingdom, was surprised by a formidable invasion, with several nations joined, especially Lot's children, and the ten tribes beyond Jordan giving them passage, ungrateful after Jehoshaphat had helped them at Ramoth-Gilead. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: Then there came some that told Jehoshaphat, saying, There cometh a great multitude against thee from beyond the sea on this side Syria, and behold, they be in Hazazontamar, which is Engedi. Henry notes Jehoshaphat was surprised with intelligence when the enemy had already entered his country, coming from beyond the Dead Sea where Sodom had stood. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: And Jehoshaphat feared, and set himself to seek the Lord, and proclaimed a fast throughout all Judah. Henry notes holy fear spurred prayer: consciousness of guilt made him fear, yet he set himself to seek the Lord with fixed thought, sincerity and utmost resolution, and proclaimed a fast as a national act of humiliation, self-judging for sin and self-denial for the future. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: And Judah gathered themselves together, to ask help of the Lord, even out of all the cities of Judah they came to seek the Lord. Henry notes the people readily assembled from all Judah's cities in the temple court, standing before the Lord as beggars at his door, with wives and children, since families were in danger together. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: And Jehoshaphat stood in the congregation of Judah and Jerusalem, in the house of the Lord, before the new court. Henry notes Jehoshaphat himself was the mouth of the congregation, not devolving prayer on chaplains, standing in the house of the Lord before the new court, within reach of God's promise to attend prayer made in that place. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: And said, O Lord God of our fathers, art not thou God in heaven? and rulest not thou over all the kingdoms of the heathen? and in thine hand is there not power and might, so that none is able to withstand thee? Henry notes the prayer first acknowledges God's sovereign dominion: God in heaven, supreme over all kingdoms even of heathen who know him not, with power none can withstand, and Jehoshaphat asks him to glorify his omnipotence on their behalf. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: Art not thou our God, who didst drive out the inhabitants of this land before thy people Israel, and gavest it to the seed of Abraham thy friend for ever? Henry notes Jehoshaphat lays hold on covenant relation: God of their fathers and their God, and on title to the land, given to Abraham God's friend, whose seed they were, hoping to be beloved for the father's sake. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: And they dwelt therein, and have built thee a sanctuary therein for thy name, saying. Henry notes he mentions the sanctuary built for God's name, not as meriting anything, for of God's own they gave him, but as a token of God's favourable presence, with promise that he would hear and help when they cried before that house. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: If, when evil cometh upon us, as the sword, judgment, pestilence or famine, we stand before this house, and in thy presence, for thy name is in this house, and cry unto thee in our affliction, then thou wilt hear and help. Henry notes the temple was built for encouragement of faith at such a time: here thy name is, here we are, Lord help us for thy name's glory. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: And now, behold, Ammon and Moab and Mount Seir, whom thou wouldest not let Israel invade when they came out of Egypt, but they turned from them and destroyed them not. Henry notes he pleads the enemies' ingratitude: Israel was forbidden to meddle with Edom, Moab or Ammon, yet now they invade, rendering evil for good. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: Behold, I say, how they reward us, to come to cast us out of thy possession, which thou hast given us to inherit. Henry notes they break in upon ancient rights, coming to cast Judah out of God's own grant, and Jehoshaphat appeals to God as landlord to maintain his grant against unjust claims. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: O our God, wilt thou not judge them? for we have no might against this great company that cometh against us, neither know we what to do, but our eyes are upon thee. Henry notes the prayer professes entire dependence: though a great army was on foot, they had no might without God, knew not what to do, but their eyes were upon him, an eye of faith, desire, hope and patient expectation. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: And all Judah stood before the Lord, with their little ones, their wives, and their children. Henry notes the whole congregation stood before the Lord with families, pleading that little ones innocent should not perish in the storm, as Nineveh was spared for its little ones. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: Then upon Jahaziel son of Zechariah, son of Benaiah, son of Jeiel, son of Mattaniah, a Levite of Asaph's sons, came the Spirit of the Lord in the midst of the congregation. Henry notes God's answer was speedy, while he was yet speaking: the Spirit came upon a Levite in the midst of the congregation, not in eminency, for the Spirit blows where he lists, honouring a singer's office. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: And he said, Hearken ye, all Judah, and inhabitants of Jerusalem, and thou King Jehoshaphat, Thus saith the Lord unto you, Be not afraid nor dismayed by reason of this great multitude, for the battle is not yours, but God's. Henry notes Jahaziel encouraged trust: fear enough to bring them to God should not now drive them from him, for the battle is not theirs in their own strength or cause, but God's, who interests himself in it. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: To morrow go ye down against them: behold, they come up by the cliff of Ziz, and ye shall find them at the end of the brook, before the wilderness of Jeruel. Henry notes the prophet gave intelligence of the enemy's motions and orders to march, with time and place, for he who commands deliverance commands those for whom it is wrought. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: Ye shall not need to fight in this battle: set yourselves, stand ye still, and see the salvation of the Lord with you, O Judah and Jerusalem: fear not, nor be dismayed, to morrow go out against them, for the Lord will be with you. Henry notes they should be not glorious instruments but joyful spectators: they need not strike a stroke, only stand still and see, as Moses said at the Red Sea, for if the battle be God's, the victory shall be his too. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: And Jehoshaphat bowed his head with his face to the ground, and all Judah and Jerusalem's inhabitants fell before the Lord, worshipping the Lord. Henry notes they received assurances with faith, reverence and thankfulness, bowing first Jehoshaphat then all the people, saying with faith, Be it unto us according to thy word. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: And the Levites, of the Kohathites and Korhites, stood up to praise the Lord God of Israel with a loud voice on high. Henry notes they lifted voices in praise, for active faith can give thanks for a promise not yet performed, knowing God's bonds are as good as ready money. — Matthew Henry

20 COMMENTARY: And they rose early and went forth into the wilderness of Tekoa, and as they went forth, Jehoshaphat stood and said, Hear me, O Judah, and inhabitants of Jerusalem: Believe in the Lord your God, so shall ye be established; believe his prophets, so shall ye prosper. Henry notes faith was their armour: instead of calling them to handle arms and keep ranks, Jehoshaphat bade them believe God and his prophets, for true courage is what faith inspires, and a heart fixed in trust is kept in perfect peace. — Matthew Henry

21 COMMENTARY: And when he had consulted with the people, he appointed singers unto the Lord, that should praise the beauty of holiness, as they went out before the army, and to say, Praise the Lord, for his mercy endureth for ever. Henry notes praise was their vanguard: singers went before the army with nothing but to praise God's holiness, expressing reliance on God's word, animating soldiers, confounding the enemy, and engaging God, for praise pleases God better than burnt offering. — Matthew Henry

22 COMMENTARY: And when they began to sing and to praise, the Lord set ambushments against Ammon, Moab and Mount Seir, which were come against Judah, and they were smitten. Henry notes the enemy was destroyed unaccountably, not by thunder or angel's sword, but by the Lord setting ambushments, either angels or their own ambushments struck with confusion, and this God did when his people began to sing and praise. — Matthew Henry

23 COMMENTARY: For Ammon and Moab stood up against Seir's inhabitants, utterly to slay and destroy them, and when they had made an end of Seir, every one helped to destroy another. Henry notes God often makes wicked people instruments of destruction to one another: Ammon and Moab fell on Edom, then fell out with one another, for what alliances can hold together those God designs to dash in pieces? — Matthew Henry

24 COMMENTARY: And when Judah came toward the watch tower in the wilderness, they looked unto the multitude, and behold, they were dead bodies fallen to the earth, and none escaped. Henry notes Judah's army, expecting living men to fight, found only dead bodies spread as dung, and God not only delivered them from being spoiled but enriched them with the enemy's spoil. — Matthew Henry

25 COMMENTARY: And when Jehoshaphat and his people came to take the spoil, they found among them in abundance both riches with the dead bodies, and precious jewels, which they stripped off, more than they could carry away, and they were three days gathering the spoil, it was so much. Henry notes the plunder was very great and rich, precious jewels with bodies that could not save their owners, and the spoil took three days to gather, showing God's end in bringing the army was to humble and prove Judah, that he might do them good in their latter end. — Matthew Henry

26 COMMENTARY: And on the fourth day they assembled in the valley of Berachah, for there they blessed the Lord, therefore the place was called the valley of Berachah unto this day. Henry notes they kept a day of praise in the camp before leaving the field, blessing God with such zeal that the day's work named the place Berachah, blessing, perpetuating the wonder for succeeding generations to trust in God. — Matthew Henry

27 COMMENTARY: Then they returned, every man of Judah and Jerusalem, and Jehoshaphat in the forefront of them, to go again to Jerusalem with joy, for the Lord had made them to rejoice over their enemies. Henry notes they came in solemn procession, Jehoshaphat at their head, that the country might join their praises and thanks be given where prayer had obtained mercy, for praising God must not be a day's work only. — Matthew Henry

28 COMMENTARY: And they came to Jerusalem with psalteries and harps and trumpets unto the house of the Lord. Henry notes public mercies call for public acknowledgments in the courts of the Lord's house, and the procession came with music to the temple where prayer had been made. — Matthew Henry

29 COMMENTARY: And the fear of God was on all the kingdoms of those countries, when they had heard that the Lord fought against the enemies of Israel. Henry notes the victory made Judah look great abroad: neighbours, hearing God fought for Israel, reverenced God and feared injuring his people, for it is dangerous fighting those who have God with them. — Matthew Henry

30 COMMENTARY: So the realm of Jehoshaphat was quiet, for his God gave him rest round about. Henry notes they were quiet among themselves, objectors to image-destroying now satisfied that Israel's God alone delivers, and quiet from neighbours' insults, God having given rest round about, and if he give rest, who can give disturbance? — Matthew Henry

31 COMMENTARY: And Jehoshaphat reigned over Judah: he was thirty-five when he began to reign, and reigned twenty-five years in Jerusalem, and his mother's name was Azubah daughter of Shilhi. Henry notes the close of Jehoshaphat's reign is drawn toward, with his general character that he did right in the Lord's sight, keeping close to God's worship and keeping his people close to it. — Matthew Henry

32 COMMENTARY: And he walked in the way of Asa his father, and departed not from it, doing that which was right in the sight of the Lord. Henry notes Jehoshaphat kept close to the worship of God himself and did what he could to keep his people close to it, departing not from Asa's good way. — Matthew Henry

33 COMMENTARY: Howbeit the high places were not taken away, for as yet the people had not prepared their hearts unto the God of their fathers. Henry notes two things are lamented: the people retained partiality for high places, those for the true God being thought allowable, and Jehoshaphat was loth to disoblige them, for they had not prepared their hearts, complying for shame but not heartily, and the best magistrates cannot reform a cool people. — Matthew Henry

34 COMMENTARY: Now the rest of Jehoshaphat's acts, first and last, behold, they are written in the book of Jehu son of Hanani, who is mentioned in the book of the kings of Israel. Henry notes readers were referred to Jehu the prophet's authentic history, then extant, for a further account of Jehoshaphat's reign. — Matthew Henry

35 COMMENTARY: And after this did Jehoshaphat king of Judah join himself with Ahaziah king of Israel, who did very wickedly. Henry notes Jehoshaphat still retained partiality for Ahab's house, having married his son to its daughter, and after God had done such great things for him without scandalous confederacies, he joined himself with Ahaziah, who did very wickedly, an ungrateful return. — Matthew Henry

36 COMMENTARY: And he joined himself with him to make ships to go to Tarshish, and they made the ships in Eziongaber. Henry notes he joined Ahaziah not in war as with his father, but in trade, becoming partner in a fleet bound for Ophir, built at Eziongaber. — Matthew Henry

37 COMMENTARY: Then Eliezer son of Dodavah of Mareshah prophesied against Jehoshaphat, saying, Because thou hast joined thyself with Ahaziah, the Lord hath broken thy works. And the ships were broken, that they were not able to go to Tarshish. Henry notes God sent a prophet foretelling the blasting of the project, and a storm broke the ships in port before they sailed, warning him to break off the alliance, which he took, refusing Ahaziah's later pressing, for joining friendship with evil-doers is a pernicious snare hard to escape. — Matthew Henry
