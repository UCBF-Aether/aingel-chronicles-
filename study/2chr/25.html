Study Notes — 2 Chronicles

Chapter 25

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Amaziah was twenty-five years old when he began to reign, and he reigned twenty-nine years in Jerusalem. His mother's name was Jehoaddan of Jerusalem. Henry notes his reign was not one of the worse and yet far from good, covering the passages already related more briefly in Kings. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: And he did that which was right in the sight of the Lord, but not with a perfect heart. Henry notes he worshipped the true God and countenanced religion, but he was not a man of serious piety or devotion, nor zealous for religion's exercises, a cool and indifferent friend to it, doing good but not with the heart. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: Now it came to pass, when the kingdom was established to him, that he slew his servants that had killed the king his father. Henry notes Amaziah did what became him in calling his father's murderers to account, a necessary piece of justice upon the traitors. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: But he slew not their children, but did as written in the law in Moses' book, where the Lord commanded, the fathers shall not die for the children, neither the children for the fathers, but every man shall die for his own sin. Henry notes he forbade putting children to death for parents' sin, for even if the murderers claimed to avenge the prophet, they presumptuously took God's work out of his hands and their wickedness was not justified. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: Moreover Amaziah gathered Judah together, and made captains over thousands and hundreds, according to their fathers' houses, throughout Judah and Benjamin, and he numbered them from twenty years old and above, and found them three hundred thousand choice men, able to go forth to war, that could handle spear and shield. Henry notes he mustered and marshalled his forces, finding Judah and Benjamin but 300,000 fit for war, whereas in Jehoshaphat's time, fifty or sixty years before, they were four times as many, for sin weakens a people, diminishes and dispirits them. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: He hired also an hundred thousand mighty men of valour out of Israel for an hundred talents of silver. Henry notes finding his kingdom defective in men, he thought to make up the deficiency with money, taking 100,000 Israelites into pay, though had he advised with a prophet or considered how little his ancestors got by alliances with Israel, he would not have had this to undo, for rashness makes work for repentance. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: But there came a man of God to him, saying, O king, let not the army of Israel go with thee, for the Lord is not with Israel, to wit, with all the children of Ephraim. Henry notes God sent a prophet commanding him to dismiss Israel's forces: he would not have him call in any assistance at all, for it looked like distrust of God, and particularly not theirs, for the Lord is not with Ephraim because they are not with him but worship calves, so no good could be expected from those without God with them. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: But if thou wilt go, do it, be strong for the battle: God shall make thee fall before the enemy, for God hath power to help, and to cast down. Henry notes the prophet assured him that if he persisted in taking idolatrous Israelites, they would prove a dead weight, sink and betray his army, for God has power to help without them and to cast down though he has them. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: And Amaziah said to the man of God, But what shall we do for the hundred talents which I have given to the army of Israel? And the man of God answered, The Lord is able to give thee much more than this. Henry notes this is the objection men often make against duty, fearing loss, and the prophet's answer stands: the Lord is able to give much more, and will not see thee lose by him, for he is just, good and solvent, and a firm belief of God's all-sufficiency will make his yoke easy. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: Then Amaziah separated them, to wit, the army come out of Ephraim, to go home again, wherefore their anger was greatly kindled against Judah, and they returned home in great anger. Henry notes to his honour he would rather lose his money, disoblige allies and dismiss a fourth part of his army as they took the field, than offend God, while the dismissed men went home in great anger at the affront to profit and reputation. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: And Amaziah strengthened himself, and led forth his people, and went to the valley of salt, and smote of the children of Seir ten thousand. Henry notes his triumph over Edom: he left 10,000 dead on the field of battle in the valley of salt. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: And other ten thousand left alive did Judah's children carry away captive, and brought them unto the top of the rock, and cast them down from the top of the rock, that they all were broken in pieces. Henry notes 10,000 more were taken prisoners and barbarously killed by being thrown down a steep, craggy precipice, a cruelty certainly very severe, for which no provocation is told. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: But the soldiers of the army which Amaziah sent back, that they should not go with him to battle, fell upon Judah's cities, from Samaria even unto Bethhoron, and smote three thousand of them, and took much spoil. Henry notes the disbanded soldiers, enraged at being sent home, made a prey of Judah, plundering border cities and killing 3,000, and God doubtless intended hereby to chastise those cities for idolatries learned from Israel, who had corrupted them and now were made a plague to them. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: Now it came to pass, after Amaziah was come from the slaughter of the Edomites, that he brought the gods of the children of Seir, and set them up to be his gods, and bowed down himself before them, and burned incense unto them. Henry notes egregious folly: to worship the gods of those he had conquered, who could not protect their own worshippers, was the greatest absurdity, and if he had broken the idols instead of the prisoners he would have shown more piety and pity. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: Wherefore the anger of the Lord was kindled against Amaziah, and he sent unto him a prophet, which said unto him, Why hast thou sought after the gods of the people, which could not deliver their own people out of thine hand? Henry notes God, before sending to destroy him, sent to convince and reclaim him, and the prophet reasoned fairly and mildly: why seek the favour of gods that could not deliver their own people, for if men considered the inability of what they resort to when forsaking God, they would not be such enemies to themselves. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: And it came to pass, as he talked with him, that the king said unto him, Art thou made of the king's counsel? forbear, why shouldest thou be smitten? Then the prophet forbare, and said, I know that God hath determined to destroy thee, because thou hast done this, and hast not hearkened unto my counsel. Henry notes he could say nothing in excuse, but fell into passion, taunting the prophet as meddling, silencing him and threatening him, reminding him of Zechariah's fate, and so making himself guilty of both prophets' blood, while the prophet forbore, saying God hath determined to destroy thee, for those deaf to reproof ripen apace for destruction. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: Then Amaziah king of Judah took advice, and sent to Joash, son of Jehoahaz, son of Jehu, king of Israel, saying, Come, let us see one another in the face. Henry notes Amaziah took advice before challenging Israel, but not of the prophet, whom he had silenced, but of statesmen who would flatter him and bid him go up and prosper, for those who will not take advice from God's word are justly left to bad advisers. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: And Joash king of Israel sent to Amaziah king of Judah, saying, The thistle in Lebanon sent to the cedar in Lebanon, saying, Give thy daughter to my son to wife, and there passed by a wild beast in Lebanon, and trode down the thistle. Henry notes Joash answered with haughty scorn, the thistle and cedar parable, and friendly advice to sit still and know when he was well off. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: Thou sayest, Lo, thou hast smitten the Edomites, and thine heart lifteth thee up to boast: abide now at home, why shouldest thou meddle to thine hurt, that thou shouldest fall, even thou, and Judah with thee? Henry notes Joash's friendly advice was to abide at home and not meddle to his hurt, lest he fall with Judah, for a man's pride will bring him low and he that goes forth hastily to strive will not know what to do in the end. — Matthew Henry

20 COMMENTARY: But Amaziah would not hear, for it came of God, that he might deliver them into the hand of their enemies, because they sought after the gods of Edom. Henry notes Amaziah wilfully persisted, and his imprudence was made the punishment of his impiety, for it was of the Lord: he left him to himself to act foolishly, that he and his people might be delivered to enemies, because they had forsaken God for Edom's gods. — Matthew Henry

21 COMMENTARY: So Joash king of Israel went up, and they saw one another in the face, both he and Amaziah king of Judah, at Bethshemesh, which belongeth to Judah. Henry notes the wilful challenge came to battle at Beth-shemesh, where Amaziah's pride was thoroughly mortified. — Matthew Henry

22 COMMENTARY: And Judah was put to the worse before Israel, and they fled every man to his tent. Henry notes defeat was given him as Solomon's proverbs verify: pride goes before destruction and is often its immediate occasion, and he that exalteth himself shall be abased. — Matthew Henry

23 COMMENTARY: And Joash king of Israel took Amaziah king of Judah, son of Joash, son of Jehoahaz, at Bethshemesh, and brought him to Jerusalem, and brake down Jerusalem's wall from the gate of Ephraim to the corner gate, four hundred cubits. Henry notes the calamity he brought on himself and his city thereby: captured at Beth-shemesh, brought to Jerusalem, and the wall broken down four hundred cubits. — Matthew Henry

24 COMMENTARY: And he took all the gold and silver, and all the vessels found in God's house with Obededom, and the treasures of the king's house, the hostages also, and returned to Samaria. Henry notes Joash took the temple's gold and silver and vessels, the king's treasures and hostages, and returned to Samaria, the full price of Amaziah's rashness. — Matthew Henry

25 COMMENTARY: And Amaziah son of Joash king of Judah lived after the death of Joash son of Jehoahaz king of Israel fifteen years. Henry notes Amaziah lived fifteen years after Joash of Israel died, ending his days ingloriously, for his subjects' hearts had departed from him from the time he departed from the Lord. — Matthew Henry

26 COMMENTARY: Now the rest of the acts of Amaziah, first and last, behold, are they not written in the book of the kings of Judah and Israel? Henry notes the rest of his acts are referred to the book of the kings of Judah and Israel, as with other kings. — Matthew Henry

27 COMMENTARY: Now after the time that Amaziah did turn away from following the Lord they made a conspiracy against him in Jerusalem, and he fled to Lachish, but they sent to Lachish after him, and slew him there. Henry notes from the time he departed from the Lord his subjects' hearts departed from him and formed a design in Jerusalem, exasperated perhaps more by his rash war than his idolatry, and though he fled to Lachish as private or strong place, they sent after him and slew him there deliberately. — Matthew Henry

28 COMMENTARY: And they brought him upon horses, and buried him with his fathers in the city of Judah. Henry notes he was brought upon horses and buried with his fathers in the city of Judah, ending a reign not one of the worse yet far from good. — Matthew Henry
