Study Notes — 2 Chronicles

Chapter 11

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: And when Rehoboam was come to Jerusalem, he gathered of Judah and Benjamin a hundred and fourscore thousand chosen warriors, to fight against Israel, that he might bring the kingdom again to Rehoboam. Henry notes Rehoboam at length, like a bold man, raised an army to reduce the revolters, with Judah and Benjamin resolved to continue allegiance and ready to assist. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: But the word of the Lord came to Shemaiah the man of God, saying. Henry notes Rehoboam, like a conscientious man, let his design fall when God forbade it, reverencing divine authority and knowing he should not prosper contrary to God's command. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: Speak unto Rehoboam son of Solomon, king of Judah, and to all Israel in Judah and Benjamin, saying. Henry notes God called him Rehoboam son of Solomon, to intimate the division was determined for Solomon's sin, and opposing a decree gone forth would be to no purpose. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: Thus saith the Lord, Ye shall not go up, nor fight against your brethren: return every man to his house: for this thing is done of me. And they obeyed the words of the Lord, and returned from going against Jeroboam. Henry notes they obeyed though it looked mean and would turn to reproach among neighbours, because God would have it so, and it is dangerous to undertake anything, especially war, contrary to God's will. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: And Rehoboam dwelt in Jerusalem, and built cities for defence in Judah. Henry notes that, like a discreet man, he fortified his own country, making the best of what remained instead of chasing what was lost, and submitting to God's will concerning the lost tribes. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: He built even Bethlehem, and Etam, and Tekoa. Henry notes he fortified his frontiers and many principal cities of his kingdom, which in Solomon's peaceable reign had not been cared for in defence. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: And Bethzur, and Shoco, and Adullam. Henry's fortification account applies to each of these frontier cities, built for defence in Judah as Rehoboam made the best of the kingdom that remained. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: And Gath, and Mareshah, and Ziph. Henry notes the same discreet course: fortifying principal cities, since a few good words might have prevented defection, but now all his forces could not bring the revolters back. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: And Adoraim, and Lachish, and Azekah. Henry's fortification point covers these also: Rehoboam's wisdom was to keep what he had, fortifying the frontiers of Judah and Benjamin. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: And Zorah, and Aijalon, and Hebron, which are in Judah and Benjamin fenced cities. Henry notes these were fenced cities in Judah and Benjamin, the fortified strongholds by which Rehoboam preserved the two tribes that remained. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: And he fortified the strong holds, and put captains in them, and store of victual, and of oil and wine. Henry notes he furnished the cities with good stores of victuals and arms, and because God forbade him to fight, he did not sit down sullenly but prudently provided against attack, for those who may not be conquerors may yet be builders. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: And in every several city he put shields and spears, and made them exceeding strong, having Judah and Benjamin on his side. Henry notes the furnishing with arms made each city exceeding strong, preserving Judah and Benjamin on Rehoboam's side. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: And the priests and Levites that were in all Israel resorted to him out of all their coasts. Henry notes Rehoboam was strengthened by the accession of priests and Levites, and all devout Israelites true to God and religion, resorting to where God's altar was. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: For the Levites left their suburbs and their possession, and came to Judah and Jerusalem, for Jeroboam and his sons had cast them off from executing the priest's office unto the Lord. Henry notes Jeroboam set up worship they could not in conscience comply with, and would not let them go up to Jerusalem, so they left comfortable provision, loving their work better than maintenance, since the Lord was their inheritance. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: And he ordained him priests for the high places, and for the devils, and for the calves which he had made. Henry notes Jeroboam was willing they should turn out, to make room for mean and scandalous persons he ordained for high places, for he who cast off God cast off his ministers, who would not serve his gods or worship his golden image. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: And after them out of all tribes of Israel such as set their hearts to seek the Lord God of Israel came to Jerusalem, to sacrifice unto the Lord God of their fathers. Henry notes the devout of every tribe followed the priests, leaving fathers' inheritances for houses in Jerusalem, because what is best for our souls must take place of outward conveniences, and where God's faithful priests are, his faithful people should be. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: So they strengthened the kingdom of Judah, and made Rehoboam strong, three years, for three years they walked in the way of David and Solomon. Henry notes they strengthened Judah not only by added persons but by piety and prayers procuring blessing, and strength lasted while they cleaved to God and duty, and no longer. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: And Rehoboam took him Mahalath daughter of Jerimoth son of David to wife, and Abihail daughter of Eliab son of Jesse. Henry notes Rehoboam was more wise than his father in this, that his wives mentioned were not strange wives but daughters of Israel, of David's family. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: Which bare him children: Jeush, and Shamariah, and Zaham. Henry notes Rehoboam was more happy than his father in having many sons and daughters named as men of note, whereas only one son of Solomon is read of as worth mentioning. — Matthew Henry

20 COMMENTARY: And after her he took Maachah daughter of Absalom, which bare him Abijah, and Attai, and Ziza, and Shelomith. Henry notes Maachah was probably Absalom's daughter, of David's line, and her sons are named among Rehoboam's notable children. — Matthew Henry

21 COMMENTARY: And Rehoboam loved Maachah daughter of Absalom above all his wives and concubines, for he took eighteen wives and threescore concubines, and begat twenty-eight sons and threescore daughters. Henry notes Rehoboam was weakened by indulging himself in pleasures and desiring many wives, as his father did, though his wives were of Israel. — Matthew Henry

22 COMMENTARY: And Rehoboam made Abijah son of Maachah the chief, to be ruler among his brethren, for he thought to make him king. Henry notes Rehoboam designed Abijah for successor, and dispersed his other sons so they might not rival Abijah. — Matthew Henry

23 COMMENTARY: And he dealt wisely, and dispersed all his children throughout Judah and Benjamin, unto every fenced city, and gave them victual in abundance. And he desired many wives. Henry notes this dispersal was wisdom bought dearly: sons trusted with fenced cities, well victualled, to preserve public peace, and after-wisdom is better than none, though bought with the loss of a kingdom. — Matthew Henry
