Study Notes — 2 Chronicles

Chapter 13

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Now in the eighteenth year of King Jeroboam began Abijah to reign over Judah. Henry notes Chronicles gives a fuller account of Abijah than Kings, chiefly famous for a glorious victory over Jeroboam, though his character in Kings was no better than his father's. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: He reigned three years in Jerusalem. His mother's name also was Michaiah daughter of Uriel of Gibeah. And there was war between Abijah and Jeroboam. Henry notes his mother, called Maachah daughter of Absalom elsewhere, was probably Absalom's granddaughter by Tamar, her immediate father being Uriel, and Abijah's short reign was chiefly this war. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: And Abijah set battle in array with four hundred thousand chosen valiant men, and Jeroboam against him with eight hundred thousand chosen mighty men of valour. Henry notes Jeroboam's army was double Abijah's, ten tribes against two, yet the inferior number proved victorious, for the battle is not always to the strong nor the cause to the majority. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: And Abijah stood up upon Mount Zemaraim, in Mount Ephraim, and said, Hear me, thou Jeroboam, and all Israel. Henry notes Abijah reasoned before fighting, trying persuasion before force, for war must be the last resort of kings, and fair reasoning may prevent much mischief. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: Ought ye not to know that the Lord God of Israel gave the kingdom over Israel to David for ever, even to him and his sons by a covenant of salt? Henry notes Abijah claimed divine right: God gave the kingdom to David by a lasting covenant, a covenant of salt made by sacrifice, and all Israel had owned David a king of God's making. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: Yet Jeroboam son of Nebat, servant of Solomon son of David, is risen up and hath rebelled against his lord. Henry notes Jeroboam's dishonesty: he rebelled against his lord who had preferred him, and basely took advantage of Rehoboam's weakness, when in gratitude and justice he should have stood by him. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: And there are gathered unto him vain men, children of Belial, who strengthened themselves against Rehoboam son of Solomon, when Rehoboam was young and tenderhearted and could not withstand them. Henry notes Jeroboam's supporters were vain men, given to change without steady principle, and men of Belial, for shaking off government and setting over them those who would do as they pleased. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: And now ye think to withstand the kingdom of the Lord in the hand of the sons of David, and ye be a great multitude, and there are with you golden calves which Jeroboam made you for gods. Henry notes fighting the house of David was fighting the kingdom of the Lord, and Israel's multitude was no strength while calves for gods, unable to protect them, would ruin them like Achans troubling the camp. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: Have ye not cast out the priests of the Lord, sons of Aaron, and Levites, and made you priests after the manner of other lands, so that whosoever cometh with a young bullock and seven rams may be a priest of them that are no gods? Henry notes Israel cast off Levi and Aaron, and made any man a priest who would pay consecration, however scandalous, and such priests fit gods that were no gods, like to like, both pretenders and usurpers. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: But as for us, the Lord is our God, and we have not forsaken him, and the priests ministering unto the Lord are sons of Aaron, and Levites wait upon their business. Henry notes Judah's adherence: Jehovah is our God, God of our fathers, able to protect and give success, and he is with us because we are with him. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: And they burn unto the Lord every morning and evening burnt sacrifices and sweet incense, the showbread set in order upon the pure table, and the candlestick of gold with lamps to burn every evening, for we keep the charge of the Lord our God, but ye have forsaken him. Henry notes Judah kept God's charge, worshipping no images, with no priests but those he ordained and no rites but those he prescribed, neither adding nor diminishing. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: And behold, God himself is with us for our captain, and his priests with sounding trumpets to cry alarm against you. O children of Israel, fight ye not against the Lord God of your fathers, for ye shall not prosper. Henry notes God as captain, with priests sounding trumpets as testimony against Israel and assurance to Judah of remembrance before God, emboldened men more than anything, and Abijah warned them not to fight their fathers' God. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: But Jeroboam caused an ambushment to come about behind them, so they were before Judah, and the ambushment was behind them. Henry notes Jeroboam offered no answer to Abijah's speech, resolved not to heed it, and basely laid an ambush behind Judah while treating, against all laws of arms, trusting stratagem over cause. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: And when Judah looked back, behold, the battle was before and behind, and they cried unto the Lord, and the priests sounded with trumpets. Henry notes they were brought into a great strait and fright, yet no ambush can cut off communication with heaven, and in distress they renewed prayers, crying unto the Lord while priests sounded the sacred signal. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: Then the men of Judah gave a shout, and as they shouted, God smote Jeroboam and all Israel before Abijah and Judah. Henry notes to the cry of prayer they added the shout of faith, the day is ours for God is with us, and as they shouted God smote Israel with terror so they could not strike a stroke. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: And the children of Israel fled before Judah, and God delivered them into their hand. Henry notes the victory was God's: Jeroboam, trusting politics, was beaten, while Abijah's people, trusting God, came off conquerors despite disproportion of numbers. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: And Abijah and his people slew them with a great slaughter, so there fell down slain of Israel five hundred thousand chosen men. Henry notes the conquerors gave no quarter, putting 500,000 chosen men to the sword, more than ever read of in one battle, for the battle was the Lord's, chastising Israel's idolatry and owning David's house, though it was sad that Israelites shed Israelite blood while heathen neighbours rejoiced. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: Thus the children of Israel were brought under at that time, and the children of Judah prevailed, because they relied upon the Lord God of their fathers. Henry notes the prayer of faith is the prevailing prayer: they relied on the God of their fathers, depended on his power, and by faith overcame. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: And Abijah pursued after Jeroboam, and took cities from him, Bethel with its towns, Jeshanah with its towns, and Ephraim with its towns. Henry notes many cities were taken and remained in Judah's possession, Bethel particularly, though what became of its golden calf is not told, and Abijah, his heart not perfect, perhaps suffered it to stand. — Matthew Henry

20 COMMENTARY: Neither did Jeroboam recover strength again in the days of Abijah, and the Lord struck him, and he died. Henry notes Jeroboam never looked up after the defeat, though he survived two or three years, struck by God with disease or broken heart, for he escaped Abijah's sword but not God's. — Matthew Henry

21 COMMENTARY: But Abijah waxed mighty, and married fourteen wives, and begat twenty-two sons and sixteen daughters. Henry notes Abijah waxed mighty upon the victory, and now multiplied his wives to fourteen in all, by whom he had thirty-eight children, happy is the man that hath his quiver full of those arrows. — Matthew Henry

22 COMMENTARY: And the rest of the acts of Abijah, and his ways, and his sayings, are written in the story of the prophet Iddo. Henry notes Abijah had ways peculiar to himself, and sayings of his own, recorded with his acts in the history of those times, but his months were cut off in the midst, and soon after his triumphs death conquered the conqueror, perhaps because he was too much lifted up with his victories. — Matthew Henry
