Study Notes — 2 Chronicles

Chapter 26

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Then all the people of Judah took Uzziah, who was sixteen years old, and made him king in the room of his father Amaziah. Henry notes Uzziah, called Azariah in Kings, had a long and in some respects illustrious reign, related more fully here than in Kings. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: He built Eloth, and restored it to Judah, after that the king slept with his fathers. Henry's prosperity account applies: Uzziah restored and secured Judah's possessions, building and fortifying at home and abroad. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: Sixteen years old was Uzziah when he began to reign, and he reigned fifty and two years in Jerusalem. His mother's name also was Jecoliah of Jerusalem. Henry notes his reign was long, fifty-two years, and in some respects illustrious. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: And he did that which was right in the sight of the Lord, according to all that his father Amaziah did. Henry notes his piety was not very eminent or zealous, yet he did right in the Lord's sight, keeping up pure worship of the true God as his father did, and better than his father, inasmuch as we have no reason to think he ever worshipped idols, not even in his latter days. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: And he sought God in the days of Zechariah, who had understanding in the visions of God, and as long as he sought the Lord, God made him to prosper. Henry notes Zechariah, one with understanding in God's visions, well versed in prophecy and conversant with the upper world, had great influence with Uzziah, and as long as he sought the Lord and minded religion, God made him prosper, for religion and piety are very friendly to outward prosperity. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: And he went forth and warred against the Philistines, and brake down the wall of Gath, and the wall of Jabneh, and the wall of Ashdod, and built cities about Ashdod, and among the Philistines. Henry notes God helped him, and he triumphed over the Philistines, those old enemies, demolished their cities' fortifications and put his own garrisons among them. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: And God helped him against the Philistines, and against the Arabians that dwelt in Gurbaal, and the Mehunims. Henry notes God helped him against Philistines, Arabians and Mehunims, making all quiet about him and keeping them in awe. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: And the Ammonites gave gifts to Uzziah, and his name spread abroad even to the entering in of Egypt, for he strengthened himself exceedingly. Henry notes he obliged the Ammonites to pay tribute, and his name was celebrated through neighbouring countries, a good name for good things with God and good people, which is true fame. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: Moreover Uzziah built towers in Jerusalem at the corner gate, and at the valley gate, and at the turning of the wall, and fortified them. Henry notes while acting offensively abroad he did not neglect defence at home, building and fortifying towers in Jerusalem, much of whose wall had been broken down in his father's time, though his best fortification was close adherence to God's worship. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: Also he built towers in the desert, and digged many wells, for he had much cattle, both in the low country, and in the plains, husbandmen also, and vine dressers in the mountains, and in Carmel, for he loved husbandry. Henry notes he built desert towers to protect country people from plunderers, and dealt much in cattle and corn, employing many hands and getting much wealth, for he loved husbandry and probably inspected his country affairs himself, an honour to the husbandman's calling. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: Moreover Uzziah had an host of fighting men, that went out to war by bands, according to the number of their account by the hand of Jeiel the scribe and Maaseiah the ruler, under the hand of Hananiah, one of the king's captains. Henry notes he had two military establishments: a host of fighting men going out to war by bands, fetching spoil by reprisal for Judah's depredations, mustered by Jeiel and Maaseiah under Hananiah. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: The whole number of the chief of the fathers of the mighty men of valour were two thousand and six hundred. Henry notes the chief fathers of the mighty men numbered 2,600, heads of the host that made war with mighty power. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: And under their hand was an army, three hundred thousand and seven thousand and five hundred, that made war with mighty power, to help the king against the enemy. Henry notes under them was an army of 307,500 making war with mighty power, so great in number and valour that no enemy durst face them or could stand before them. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: And Uzziah prepared for them throughout all the host shields, and spears, and helmets, and habergeons, and bows, and slings to cast stones. Henry notes Uzziah furnished a great armoury, supplying soldiers with arms offensive and defensive, spears, bows, slings, shields, helmets and habergeons, swords not mentioned because every man likely wore his own constantly. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: And he made in Jerusalem engines, invented by cunning men, to be on the towers and upon the bulwarks, to shoot arrows and great stones withal. And his name spread far abroad, for he was marvellously helped, till he was strong. Henry notes engines were invented in his time for annoying besiegers with darts and stones from towers and bulwarks, a pity that lusts' wars made cunning men invent instruments of death, and he was marvellously helped till he was strong. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: But when he was strong, his heart was lifted up to his destruction, for he transgressed against the Lord his God, and went into the temple of the Lord to burn incense upon the altar of incense. Henry notes this is the only blot on Uzziah's name, and at its bottom was pride of heart, a lust that ruins more than any other: when strong and marvellously helped, instead of lifting up God's name in gratitude, his heart was lifted up to destruction, and he invaded the priest's office, intruding into the temple further than allowed, a transgression against the Lord his God. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: And Azariah the priest went in after him, and with him fourscore priests of the Lord, that were valiant men. Henry notes he was opposed by the chief priest and other priests attending him, valiant men, ready to burn incense for the king according to their duty, but plainly telling him he meddled with what did not belong to him. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: And they withstood Uzziah the king, and said unto him, It appertaineth not unto thee, Uzziah, to burn incense unto the Lord, but to the priests the sons of Aaron, that are consecrated to burn incense: go out of the sanctuary, for thou hast trespassed, neither shall it be for thine honour from the Lord God. Henry notes they reasoned with him, showing it was not lawful, for incense belonged to Aaron's consecrated sons alone, and it was not safe, for it shall not be for his honour but his disgrace and peril, the law running expressly against strangers coming nigh, and kingly and priestly offices being separated until united in the Messiah. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: Then Uzziah was wroth, and had a censer in his hand to burn incense, and while he was wroth with the priests, the leprosy even rose up in his forehead before the priests in the house of the Lord, from beside the incense altar. Henry notes he fell into passion with the priests, would not part with the censer, and while wroth, God smote him with leprosy rising in his forehead, for if presumptuous men will not see error by judgments of God's mouth, they shall see it by judgments of his hand. — Matthew Henry

20 COMMENTARY: And Azariah the chief priest, and all the priests, looked upon him, and behold, he was leprous in his forehead, and they thrust him out from thence, yea, himself hasted also to go out, because the Lord had smitten him. Henry notes the stroke ended the controversy: the priests were emboldened to thrust him out, and he himself hasted out because the Lord had smitten him with a disease secluding him from men, much more from God's altar, showing religious fear even in transgression, for though he strove with priests he would not strive with his Maker. — Matthew Henry

21 COMMENTARY: And Uzziah the king was a leper unto the day of his death, and dwelt in a several house, being a leper, for he was cut off from the house of the Lord, and Jotham his son was over the king's house, judging the people of the land. Henry notes leprosy remained a lasting punishment: he continued a leper to death, shut up and shut out from society, leaving his son to manage all business, an instance of God's resisting the proud and jealousy for his institutions' purity, and a loud call to repentance with long space to repent. — Matthew Henry

22 COMMENTARY: Now the rest of the acts of Uzziah, first and last, did Isaiah the prophet, son of Amoz, write. Henry notes the rest of Uzziah's acts were written by Isaiah son of Amoz, closing the account of a reign whose disgrace, though only one blot, stained all his other glory. — Matthew Henry

23 COMMENTARY: So Uzziah slept with his fathers, and they buried him with his fathers in the field of the burial which belonged to the kings, for they said, He is a leper, and Jotham his son reigned in his stead. Henry notes when dead they would not bury him in the kings' sepulchres because he was a leper, which stained all his other glory, and the punishment answered the sin as face does face: pride humbled, the invader of priests' office made subject to priests' inspection of leprosy, and the intruder into the temple thrust out of its very courts. — Matthew Henry
