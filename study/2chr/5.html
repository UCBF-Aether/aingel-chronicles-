Study Notes — 2 Chronicles

Chapter 5

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Thus all the work Solomon made for the house of the Lord was finished, and he brought in all the things David his father had dedicated, silver, gold and instruments, among the treasures of the house of God. Henry notes Solomon was just both to God and to his father: dedicated things must not be alienated, for it is sacrilege to do so, and the temple sanctified the gold, not the reverse. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: Then Solomon assembled the elders of Israel, all heads of tribes and chief of the fathers, to Jerusalem, to bring up the ark of the covenant from the city of David, which is Zion. Henry notes the ark was brought in attended by a very great assembly, to grace the solemnity with a sumptuous appearance. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: Wherefore all the men of Israel assembled to the king in the feast of the seventh month. Henry notes this was done with great joy, keeping a holy feast on the occasion, for the establishment of public worship with tokens of God's presence ought to be matter of great joy. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: And all the elders of Israel came, and the Levites took up the ark. Henry notes the ark was carried by the priests and Levites in solemn procession, attended by the elders who came to grace the occasion. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: And they brought up the ark, and the tabernacle of the congregation, and all the holy vessels in the tabernacle, which the priests and Levites brought up. Henry notes these were not alienated, altered or melted down, though there was no need of them, but carefully laid up as monuments of antiquity. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: Also King Solomon and all the congregation assembled before the ark sacrificed sheep and oxen which could not be told nor numbered for multitude. Henry notes that whatever comfort we have, we must by the sacrifice of praise give God the glory, and not be straitened therein, for with such sacrifices God is well pleased. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: And the priests brought the ark into its place, to the oracle of the house, into the most holy place, even under the wings of the cherubim. Henry notes the ark, with mercy-seat and cherubim, was the same as in the tabernacle, for God's presence and grace are the same in little assemblies as in large ones. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: For the cherubim spread forth their wings over the place of the ark, and covered the ark and its staves above. Henry notes the ark was put under the wings of the great cherubim Solomon had set up there, the token of God's presence overshadowed in the holy place. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: And they drew out the staves, so that their ends were seen from the ark before the oracle, but not seen without; and there it is unto this day. Henry notes this phrase marks the day of the source from which the story was transcribed, not the post-captivity day of the book's writing. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: There was nothing in the ark save the two tables Moses put therein at Horeb, when the Lord made a covenant with Israel coming out of Egypt. Henry's ark typology applies: the ark was a type of Christ and token of God's presence, and at its heart lay the covenant law, the promise that God is with his people. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: And it came to pass, when the priests were come out of the holy place, for all the priests present were sanctified and did not then wait by course. Henry notes the whole family of priests attended, not one course only, because there was work enough for all, and all should be eye-witnesses of the solemnity. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: Also the Levite singers, of Asaph, Heman and Jeduthun, with sons and brethren, arrayed in white linen, with cymbals, psalteries and harps, stood at the east end of the altar, with a hundred and twenty priests sounding trumpets. Henry notes all three families of singers and musicians were employed, with 120 trumpet priests completing the concert at the east end toward the people. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: It came to pass, as trumpeters and singers were as one, to make one sound praising and thanking the Lord, saying, For he is good, for his mercy endureth for ever, that the house was filled with a cloud. Henry notes it was not at the sacrifices but at the praises that God gave this token, for the sacrifice of praise pleases the Lord better than an ox, and it came when they were unanimous, fervent, and celebrating God's everlasting mercy. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: So the priests could not stand to minister by reason of the cloud, for the glory of the Lord had filled the house of God. Henry notes the cloud signified God's acceptance and his condescension to human weakness, and also intimated that the Levitical priesthood, made of men with infirmity, should cease when the Messiah came, in whom the fullness of God dwelt bodily. — Matthew Henry
