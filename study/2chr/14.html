Study Notes — 2 Chronicles

Chapter 14

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: So Abijah slept with his fathers, and they buried him in the city of David, and Asa his son reigned in his stead. In his days the land was quiet ten years. Henry notes Asa's peace was the reward of his piety and reformation, and Abijah's victory, owing under God to courage, laid the foundation for it, for amidst the din of arms the voice of law is unheard. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: And Asa did that which was good and right in the eyes of the Lord his God. Henry notes Asa aimed at pleasing God, studied to approve himself to him, saw God's eye always upon him, and God graciously accepted and approved his conduct as good and right. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: For he took away the altars of the strange gods, and the high places, and brake down the images, and cut down the groves. Henry notes Asa at once set reformation on foot, destroying idolatrous altars and images that provoked a jealous God and tempted a careless people, hoping by destroying idols to reform idolaters rather than ruin them. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: And commanded Judah to seek the Lord God of their fathers, and to do the law and the commandment. Henry notes Asa revived pure worship, obliging the people to do their part as priests did theirs, seeking the Lord God of their fathers and observing all divine institutions many had neglected. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: Also he took away out of all the cities of Judah the high places and the images, and the kingdom was quiet before him. Henry notes that in doing this the land was quiet before him: though people loved their idols and were loth to leave them, conscience sided with Asa's commands, and vice, a sneaking thing, could not stand against virtue's reason. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: And he built fenced cities in Judah, for the land had rest, and he had no war in those years, because the Lord had given him rest. Henry notes Asa prudently improved tranquillity: the land had rest because the Lord gave him rest, and those have rest indeed to whom God gives rest. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: Therefore he said unto Judah, Let us build these cities, and make about them walls, towers, gates and bars, while the land is yet before us, because we have sought the Lord our God, we have sought him, and he hath given us rest on every side. So they built and prospered. Henry notes Asa acknowledged rest as God's gift and reward of reformation, consulted his people, and concluded they must not be idle or secure, but build while opportunity lasted and prepare for trouble in peace. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: And Asa had an army of men that bare targets and spears, out of Judah three hundred thousand, and out of Benjamin, that bare shields and drew bows, two hundred and fourscore thousand, all mighty men of valour. Henry notes Judah and Benjamin were mustered severally, Benjamin almost equalling Judah, and differently armed, Judah with large targets and spears for close fighting, Benjamin with shields and bows for distance, both for the common good, neither able to say to the other, I have no need of thee. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: And there came out against them Zerah the Ethiopian with a host of a thousand thousand, and three hundred chariots, and came unto Mareshah. Henry notes this disturbance tried Asa's faith and gave God opportunity for great things: a million men against Asa's ready army, and provision thought needless soon proved of great advantage. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: Then Asa went out against him, and they set the battle in array in the valley of Zephathah at Mareshah. Henry's battle account applies: Asa went out to meet the Ethiopian host and set the battle in array at Mareshah, trusting not to his preparations alone but to God. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: And Asa cried unto the Lord his God, and said, Lord, it is nothing with thee to help, whether with many, or with them that have no power: help us, O Lord our God, for we rest on thee, and in thy name we go against this multitude. O Lord, thou art our God, let no man prevail against thee. Henry notes the short prayer has much in it: God's infinite power to help by many or few, covenant relation as our God, dependence on him in whose name they went, and interest of God's honour, for if man prevailed against them he would seem to prevail against God. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: So the Lord smote the Ethiopians before Asa and before Judah, and the Ethiopians fled. Henry notes God defeated the enemy with terror and unaccountable consternation, so they fled knowing neither why nor whither. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: And Asa and the people with him pursued them unto Gerar, and the Ethiopians were overthrown, that they could not recover themselves, for they were destroyed before the Lord and before his host, and they carried away very much spoil. Henry notes Asa took the advantage God gave: the enemy fell before the Lord and his host, and Judah carried away very much spoil from slain and baggage. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: And they smote all the cities round about Gerar, for the fear of the Lord came upon them, and they spoiled all the cities, for there was exceeding much spoil in them. Henry notes they smote the cities in league with the enemy, which could make no resistance, for the fear of the Lord came upon them and took away their heart to withstand. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: They smote also the tents of cattle, and carried away sheep and camels in abundance, and returned to Jerusalem. Henry notes they fetched away cattle in vast numbers from the enemy's country, so that the wealth of the sinner was laid up for the just, and they returned to Jerusalem. — Matthew Henry
