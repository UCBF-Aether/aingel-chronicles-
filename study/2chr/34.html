Study Notes — 2 Chronicles

Chapter 34

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Josiah was eight years old when he began to reign, and he reigned in Jerusalem one and thirty years. Henry notes Josiah came to the crown very young, only eight, yet his infancy did not debar him from his right, and he reigned thirty-one years, a considerable time, before which Judah and Jerusalem would yet see glorious years under a good king. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: And he did that which was right in the sight of the Lord, and walked in the ways of David his father, and declined neither to the right hand, nor to the left. Henry notes he reigned very well, approved himself to God, trod in David's steps, and declined neither to right nor left, for there are errors on both hands. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: For in the eighth year of his reign, while he was yet young, he began to seek after the God of David his father, and in the twelfth year he began to purge Judah and Jerusalem from the high places, and the groves, and the carved images, and the molten images. Henry notes while young, about sixteen, he began to seek after God, the duty and interest of young people and honour of young gentlemen, for those who seek him early shall find him, and in his twelfth year, when he likely took administration entirely into his own hands, he began to purge his kingdom from idolatry's remains. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: And they brake down the altars of Baalim in his presence, and the images, that were on high above them, he cut down, and the groves, and the carved images, and the molten images, he brake in pieces, and made dust of them, and strowed it upon the graves of them that had sacrificed unto them. Henry notes he destroyed high places, groves, images and altars, all idolatry's utensils, not only casting them out as Manasseh did but breaking them to pieces and making dust of them. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: And he burnt the bones of the priests upon their altars, and cleansed Judah and Jerusalem. Henry notes his purge cleansed Judah and Jerusalem, burning the idol priests' bones upon their altars as part of the thorough destruction. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: And so did he in the cities of Manasseh, and Ephraim, and Simeon, even unto Naphtali, with their mattocks round about. Henry notes his zeal carried him out to do this not only in Judah and Jerusalem but in Israel's cities too, as far as he had influence upon them. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: And when he had broken down the altars and the groves, and had beaten the graven images into powder, and cut down all the idols throughout all the land of Israel, he returned to Jerusalem. Henry notes the whole work, laid together briefly here and more largely in Kings, was probably begun in his twelfth year but not thoroughly done till the law book was found six years after, and when altars, groves and idols throughout Israel were broken down, he returned to Jerusalem. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: Now in the eighteenth year of his reign, when he had purged the land, and the house, he sent Shaphan son of Azaliah, and Maaseiah governor of the city, and Joah son of Joahaz the recorder, to repair the house of the Lord his God. Henry notes orders were given for the temple's repair: when he had purged the house of corruptions he began to fit it up for services to be performed in it, as the spiritual temple of the heart must first be cleansed from sin's pollutions and then renewed into God's image, and Josiah calls God the Lord his God, for those who truly love God will love his house's habitation. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: And when they came to Hilkiah the high priest, they delivered the money that was brought into the house of God, which the Levites that kept the doors had gathered of the hand of Manasseh and Ephraim, and of all the remnant of Israel, and of all Judah and Benjamin, and they returned to Jerusalem. Henry notes care was taken effectually: Levites went about the country gathering money, returned to the three trustees mentioned, and brought it to Hilkiah the high priest. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: And they put it in the hand of the workmen that had the oversight of the house of the Lord, and they gave it to the workmen that wrought in the house of the Lord, to repair and amend the house. Henry notes Hilkiah and the trustees put money into workmen's hands, overseers and labourers, who undertook to do it in the gross. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: Even to the artificers and builders gave they it, to buy hewn stone, and timber for couplings, and to floor the houses which the kings of Judah had destroyed. Henry notes the money went to artificers and builders to buy hewn stone and timber for couplings and flooring houses Judah's kings had destroyed. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: And the men did the work faithfully, and the overseers of them were Jahath and Obadiah, the Levites, of Merari's sons, and Zechariah and Meshullam, of the Kohathites, to set it forward, and other of the Levites, all that could skill of instruments of musick. Henry notes workmen were industrious and honest, doing the work faithfully, for workmen are not completely faithful unless both careful and diligent, and overseers were ingenious, for all employed to inspect were skilful in music's instruments, evidence of sense and ingenuity and a genius toward mathematics qualifying them for the trust. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: Also they were over the bearers of burdens, and were overseers of all that wrought the work in any manner of service, and of the Levites there were scribes, and officers, and porters. Henry notes God dispenses gifts variously: some were bearers of burdens, cut out for bodily labour, others of finer materials with skill in music were overseers, scribes and officers, hands and heads needing one another, and overseers should not despise burden-bearers nor workers grudge directors, but each esteem and serve the other in love. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: And when they brought out the money that was brought into the house of the Lord, Hilkiah the priest found a book of the law of the Lord given by Moses. Henry notes this whole paragraph was related in Kings, and we may hence bless God that we have plenty of Bibles, not rarities, for the fountain of life's waters is not sealed but flows for general use, and what shall we answer if God's law's great things, thus made common, are accounted strange. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: And Hilkiah answered and said to Shaphan the scribe, I have found the book of the law in the house of the Lord. And Hilkiah delivered the book to Shaphan. Henry notes Hilkiah found the law book in the Lord's house and delivered it to Shaphan, the discovery that would drive the reformation's second stage. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: And Shaphan carried the book to the king, and brought the king word back again, saying, All that was committed to thy servants, they do it. Henry notes Shaphan carried the book to the king and reported that all committed to his servants they do, the repair work faithfully in hand. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: And they have gathered together the money that was found in the house of the Lord, and have delivered it into the hand of the overseers, and to the hand of the workmen. Henry notes Shaphan reported the money found in the Lord's house gathered and delivered to overseers and workmen, showing the repair's faithful progress. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: Then Shaphan the scribe told the king, saying, Hilkiah the priest hath given me a book. And Shaphan read it before the king. Henry notes Shaphan told the king Hilkiah had given him a book and read it before the king, bringing God's word to the king's ears. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: And it came to pass, when the king had heard the words of the law, that he rent his clothes. Henry notes we are to learn, whenever we read or hear God's word, to affect our hearts with it and be possessed with holy fear of God's wrath revealed against ungodliness, as Josiah's tender heart was, for when he heard the law's words he rent his clothes, and God was well pleased, and we should rend the heart, not the garments. — Matthew Henry

20 COMMENTARY: And the king commanded Hilkiah, and Ahikam son of Shaphan, and Abdon son of Micah, and Shaphan the scribe, and Asaiah a servant of the king's, saying. Henry notes Josiah commanded Hilkiah, Ahikam, Abdon, Shaphan and Asaiah to enquire of the Lord, sending a delegation to seek God's word on the law's curses. — Matthew Henry

21 COMMENTARY: Go, enquire of the Lord for me, and for them that are left in Israel and in Judah, concerning the words of the book that is found, for great is the wrath of the Lord that is poured out upon us, because our fathers have not kept the word of the Lord, to do after all that is written in this book. Henry notes we are directed when under convictions of sin and apprehensions of divine wrath to enquire of the Lord, as Josiah did, asking what shall we do, for great is God's wrath poured out because fathers kept not the Lord's word to do all written in the book. — Matthew Henry

22 COMMENTARY: And Hilkiah, and they that the king had appointed, went to Huldah the prophetess, the wife of Shallum son of Tikvath, son of Hasrah, keeper of the wardrobe, now she dwelt in Jerusalem in the college, and they spake to her to that effect. Henry notes Hilkiah and the king's appointees went to Huldah the prophetess and spoke to her to that effect, seeking God's answer through his prophetess. — Matthew Henry

23 COMMENTARY: And she answered them, Thus saith the Lord God of Israel, Tell ye the man that sent you to me. Henry notes Huldah answered in the Lord God of Israel's name, telling the man that sent them God's word. — Matthew Henry

24 COMMENTARY: Thus saith the Lord, Behold, I will bring evil upon this place, and upon the inhabitants thereof, even all the curses that are written in the book which they have read before the king of Judah. Henry notes we are warned of the ruin sin brings on nations: those forsaking God bring evil on themselves and kindle a fire not to be quenched, for such will God's wrath's fire be when decree has gone forth against those obstinately persisting in wicked ways. — Matthew Henry

25 COMMENTARY: Because they have forsaken me, and have burned incense unto other gods, that they might provoke me to anger with all the works of their hands, therefore my wrath shall be poured out upon this place, and shall not be quenched. Henry notes because they forsook God and burned incense to other gods to provoke him with their hands' works, his wrath shall be poured out and not quenched. — Matthew Henry

26 COMMENTARY: And as for the king of Judah, who sent you to enquire of the Lord, so shall ye say unto him, Thus saith the Lord God of Israel concerning the words which thou hast heard. Henry notes as for the king who sent to enquire, God had a distinct word concerning the words he had heard, a personal answer to his humility. — Matthew Henry

27 COMMENTARY: Because thine heart was tender, and thou didst humble thyself before God, when thou heardest his words against this place, and against the inhabitants thereof, and humbledst thyself before me, and didst rend thy clothes, and weep before me, I have even heard thee also, saith the Lord. Henry notes we are encouraged to humble ourselves before God as Josiah did: if we cannot prevail to turn away God's wrath from our land, yet we shall deliver our own souls, for because his heart was tender and he humbled himself, rent clothes and wept before God, God heard him. — Matthew Henry

28 COMMENTARY: Behold, I will gather thee to thy fathers, and thou shalt be gathered to thy grave in peace, neither shall thine eyes see all the evil that I will bring upon this place, and upon the inhabitants of the same. So they brought the king word again. Henry notes good people are taught to be so far from fearing death as to welcome it when it takes them away from evil to come, for the promise alters death's property: thou shalt be gathered to thy grave in peace, housed in that ark as Noah when a deluge is coming. — Matthew Henry

29 COMMENTARY: Then the king sent and gathered together all the elders of Judah and Jerusalem. Henry notes upon hearing the law and receiving God's message by the prophetess, Josiah made further advances: he sent and gathered all Judah and Jerusalem's elders. — Matthew Henry

30 COMMENTARY: And the king went up into the house of the Lord, and all the men of Judah, and Jerusalem's inhabitants, and the priests, and the Levites, and all the people, great and small, and he read in their ears all the words of the book of the covenant that was found in the house of the Lord. Henry notes they were well taught: he did not force them to duty till he had first instructed them, calling all together, great and small, young and old, rich and poor, and the king himself read the book, in a manner showing he was himself affected, which would affect hearers. — Matthew Henry

31 COMMENTARY: And the king stood in his place, and made a covenant before the Lord, to walk after the Lord, and to keep his commandments, and his testimonies, and his statutes, with all his heart, and with all his soul, to perform the words of the covenant which are written in this book. Henry notes they were well fixed: the agreement's articles being read, king and people solemnly subscribed them, the king in his place covenanting to keep God's commandments with all heart and soul according to the book. — Matthew Henry

32 COMMENTARY: And he caused all that were present in Jerusalem and Benjamin to stand to it. And Jerusalem's inhabitants did according to the covenant of God, the God of their fathers. Henry notes he caused all present to stand to it and urged them to declare consent and solemnly promise faithful performance, and they could not for shame do otherwise, so Jerusalem's inhabitants did according to the covenant. — Matthew Henry

33 COMMENTARY: And Josiah took away all the abominations out of all the countries that pertained to Israel's children, and made all that were present in Israel to serve, even to serve the Lord their God. And all his days they departed not from following the Lord, the God of their fathers. Henry notes he made them all to serve, even to serve the Lord their God, the repetition denoting this was the only thing his heart was set on, and all his days they departed not from following the Lord, though kept with much ado from idolatry again, for there was in them a bent to backslide, and the reformation, though well designed by the prince, had little effect on the people, who were in heart still joined to idols. — Matthew Henry
