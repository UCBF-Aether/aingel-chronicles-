Study Notes — 2 Chronicles

Chapter 9

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: And when the queen of Sheba heard of Solomon's fame, she came to prove him with hard questions at Jerusalem, with a very great company, camels bearing spices, gold in abundance and precious stones, and communed with him of all in her heart. Henry notes those who know the worth of true wisdom grudge no pains nor cost to obtain it, and the queen, learning from Solomon to serve God, thought herself well paid for her pains. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: And Solomon told her all her questions, and there was nothing hid from Solomon which he told her not. Henry notes Solomon was communicative of his wisdom and willing to teach what he knew, having freely received and freely giving, as those rich in wisdom should be ready to distribute. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: And when the queen of Sheba had seen Solomon's wisdom, and the house he had built. Henry notes good order in a great family, especially in the things of God and regular worship, is highly expedient and much to be admired wherever found, and the queen was exceedingly affected by the propriety of Solomon's household and temple service. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: And the meat of his table, the sitting of his servants, the attendance of his ministers and their apparel, his cupbearers and their apparel, and his ascent to the house of the Lord, left no more spirit in her. Henry notes the queen was affected by the order with which Solomon's servants attended him and with which he and they attended in God's house. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: And she said to the king, It was a true report I heard in my own land of thine acts and thy wisdom. Henry's visit account applies: the report of Solomon's wisdom, consecrated to God in building and dedicating the temple, proved true on sight, for God made his wisdom and wealth redound to his reputation. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: Howbeit I believed not their words until I came and mine eyes had seen it, and behold, the one half of the greatness of thy wisdom was not told me, for thou exceedest the fame I heard. Henry notes the queen's experience as an example in enquiring after wisdom: the reality exceeded report, as heavenly wisdom is a pearl of great price well purchased at any cost. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: Happy are thy men, and happy are these thy servants, which stand continually before thee and hear thy wisdom. Henry notes those are happy who have constant converse with the knowing, wise and good, and the queen thought Solomon's servants happy who continually heard his wisdom. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: Blessed be the Lord thy God, which delighted in thee to set thee on his throne, to be king for the Lord thy God, because thy God loved Israel to establish them for ever, therefore made he thee king to do judgment and justice. Henry notes we ought to rejoice and give God thanks for the gifts and usefulness of others, tracing king and kingdom's happiness to divine favour, because God delighted in him and loved Israel. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: And she gave the king a hundred and twenty talents of gold, and spices in great abundance, and precious stones, and there was no such spice as the queen of Sheba gave. Henry notes it becomes the wise and good to be generous according to place and power, and queen and king confirmed their friendship by mutual presents, neither covetous of money. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: And the servants of Huram and of Solomon, which brought gold from Ophir, brought algum trees and precious stones. Henry's riches account applies: Solomon's trade brought gold and precious materials from afar, and no gold or silver mines were in his own kingdom, yet plenty came by export and exchange. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: And the king made of the algum trees terraces to the house of the Lord and to the king's palace, and harps and psalteries for singers, and there were none such seen before in Judah. Henry notes the temple service's music and order were part of Solomon's splendour, with instruments for singers in God's house. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: And King Solomon gave the queen of Sheba all her desire, whatsoever she asked, beside what she had brought, so she turned and went to her own land, she and her servants. Henry notes Solomon was generous to her as she to him, and both knew how to value wisdom, cultivating acquaintance by mutual presents. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: Now the weight of gold that came to Solomon in one year was six hundred and sixty-six talents of gold. Henry notes Solomon reigned in wealth and power, ease and fullness, such as could never since be paralleled, fulfilling the promise of riches, wealth and honour such as no kings had or shall have. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: Beside what chapmen and merchants brought, all the kings of Arabia and governors of the country brought gold and silver to Solomon. Henry notes presents came not as extorted tribute but as freewill offerings to procure his favour, or in exchange for husbandry produce, because neighbours coveted his acquaintance. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: And King Solomon made two hundred targets of beaten gold, six hundred shekels of beaten gold going to one target. Henry notes Solomon appeared in public with great splendour, with 200 targets and 300 shields of beaten gold carried before him, a lustre typical of the spiritual glory of Messiah's kingdom, though artificial beside the lilies' natural beauty. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: And three hundred shields made he of beaten gold, three hundred shekels to one shield, and the king put them in the house of the forest of Lebanon. Henry groups the shields with the targets as the public splendour carried before Solomon, recommending him to eyes that judge by sight. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: Moreover the king made a great throne of ivory, and overlaid it with pure gold. Henry notes Solomon sat upon a most stately throne, with no like in any kingdom, a faint representation of Messiah's throne above every throne. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: And there were six steps to the throne, with a footstool of gold fastened to it, and stays on each side of the sitting place, and two lions standing by the stays. Henry's throne description applies: the stately throne with its steps and lions was unmatched in any kingdom. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: And twelve lions stood on the one side and the other upon the six steps, and there was not the like made in any kingdom. Henry notes again there was not the like in any kingdom, Solomon's throne standing alone in splendour. — Matthew Henry

20 COMMENTARY: And all Solomon's drinking vessels were of gold, and all vessels of the house of the forest of Lebanon were of pure gold, none of silver, for silver was not anything accounted of in Solomon's days. Henry notes Solomon had greater plenty of gold and silver than any prince, though no mines were in his kingdom, fetched home by trade and export. — Matthew Henry

21 COMMENTARY: For the king's ships went to Tarshish with Huram's servants, and every three years came the ships of Tarshish bringing gold, silver, ivory, apes and peacocks. Henry notes Solomon's merchandise brought these riches by sea, with Tyrian seamen, showing that grace, not gold, is the best riches. — Matthew Henry

22 COMMENTARY: And King Solomon passed all the kings of the earth in riches and wisdom. Henry notes the promise was fulfilled: riches, wealth and honour such as no kings have had or shall have, with Solomon reigning forty years in profound peace, unlike princes famed for wars. — Matthew Henry

23 COMMENTARY: And all the kings of the earth sought Solomon's presence, to hear his wisdom that God had put in his heart. Henry notes no prince was so renowned for wisdom, so courted and consulted, whether in natural philosophy, physic, state policy, prudence, or religion, and their seeking Solomon will condemn men's contempt of Christ, in whom all treasures of wisdom are hidden. — Matthew Henry

24 COMMENTARY: And they brought every man his present, vessels of silver and gold, raiment, harness, spices, horses and mules, a rate year by year. Henry notes all kings of the earth in those parts brought presents, because they coveted his acquaintance and friendship, as a type of Christ, to whom wise men brought gold, frankincense and myrrh. — Matthew Henry

25 COMMENTARY: And Solomon had four thousand stalls for horses and chariots, and twelve thousand horsemen, whom he bestowed in the chariot cities and with the king at Jerusalem. Henry's account of Solomon's splendour and strength applies: his chariot cities and horsemen were part of the unrivalled ease and fullness of his reign. — Matthew Henry

26 COMMENTARY: And he reigned over all kings from the river even unto the Philistines' land, and to the border of Egypt. Henry notes Solomon's dominion and peace were part of the fulfilled promise, reigning over a wide empire without the wars that famed other princes. — Matthew Henry

27 COMMENTARY: And the king made silver in Jerusalem as stones, and cedar trees as the sycamore trees of the low plains in abundance. Henry notes the plenty of gold and silver in Solomon's days, when silver was not accounted of, and the kingdom's wealth was so abundant that common things became plentiful. — Matthew Henry

28 COMMENTARY: And they brought unto Solomon horses out of Egypt, and out of all lands. Henry notes all kings brought presents and trade to Solomon, and horses from Egypt and every land were part of the yearly traffic that made his court renowned. — Matthew Henry

29 COMMENTARY: Now the rest of Solomon's acts, first and last, are they not written in the book of Nathan the prophet, and in the prophecy of Ahijah the Shilonite, and in the visions of Iddo the seer against Jeroboam? Henry notes the close brings Solomon to the grave with unstained reputation, for Chronicles omits his latter defection, and his acts stand recorded by prophets and seers. — Matthew Henry

30 COMMENTARY: And Solomon reigned in Jerusalem over all Israel forty years. Henry notes Solomon reigned forty years in profound peace, a reign of wealth and wisdom unmatched, yet the throne could not secure him from the grave. — Matthew Henry

31 COMMENTARY: And Solomon slept with his fathers, and was buried in the city of David his father, and Rehoboam his son reigned in his stead. Henry notes Solomon dying, stripped of pomp, left all to a son he knew would be a fool, vanity and vexation of spirit, yet Scripture silence on his sin speaks pardon: when God pardons, he casts sin behind his back and remembers it no more. — Matthew Henry
