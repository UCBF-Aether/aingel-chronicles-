Study Notes — 2 Chronicles

Chapter 18

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Now Jehoshaphat had riches and honour in abundance, and joined affinity with Ahab. Henry notes this alliance is a great blemish in Jehoshaphat's reign: he grew greater in wealth and honour by piety and good management, but did not grow wiser, joining himself to Ahab, who had sold himself to work wickedness, by marrying his son Jehoram to Ahab's daughter Athaliah. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: And after certain years he went down to Ahab to Samaria, and Ahab killed sheep and oxen for him in abundance, and for his people, and persuaded him to go up with him to Ramothgilead. Henry notes the match drew Jehoshaphat into intimate familiarity: Ahab gave a splendid entertainment, plain meat in abundance, and the feast was designed only to wheedle him into the expedition, for the kisses of an enemy are deceitful. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: And Ahab said unto Jehoshaphat, Wilt thou go with me to Ramothgilead? And he answered, I am as thou art, and my people as thy people, and we will be with thee in the war. Henry notes Jehoshaphat, an easy man, yielded to a league against the Syrians for recovering Ramoth-Gilead, though Ahab's title was usurped and precarious, and the city belonged by right to David's heir. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: And Jehoshaphat said unto the king of Israel, Enquire, I pray thee, at the word of the Lord to day. Henry notes Jehoshaphat was not willing to proceed till he had enquired at God's word, the great duty of acknowledging God in all our ways, by prayer, scripture, conscience and providence. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: Therefore the king of Israel gathered four hundred prophets, and said, Shall we go to Ramothgilead to battle, or shall I forbear? And they said, Go up, for God will deliver it into the king's hand. Henry notes Ahab was surrounded with flatterers, especially flattering prophets who cried peace and prophesied only smooth things, and was cheated into ruin by hearkening to those who humoured him. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: But Jehoshaphat said, Is there not here a prophet of the Lord besides, that we might enquire of him? Henry notes Jehoshaphat, though in complaisance sitting among false prophets, still sought a true prophet of the Lord besides, showing the danger of bad company even to good men, who cannot converse familiarly with the wicked and get no hurt. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: And the king of Israel said, There is yet one man, Micaiah son of Imla, by whom we may enquire of the Lord, but I hate him, for he never prophesied good unto me, but always evil. And Jehoshaphat said, Let not the king say so. Henry notes Jehoshaphat could scarcely find heart to give even a mild reproof for hating a prophet of the Lord, and those who venture among the scornful cannot come off without guilt at least in omission of duty. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: And the king of Israel called for one of his officers, and said, Fetch quickly Micaiah son of Imla. Henry's account of the consultation applies: Micaiah was fetched quickly at Jehoshaphat's insistence, the faithful prophet summoned before the two kings. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: And the king of Israel and Jehoshaphat sat each on his throne, clothed in robes, in a void place at the gate of Samaria, and all the prophets prophesied before them. Henry notes Jehoshaphat sat in his robes patiently hearing false prophets speak lies in the name of the Lord, a mark of how bad company endangers even good men. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: And Zedekiah son of Chenaanah had made him horns of iron, and said, Thus saith the Lord, With these thou shalt push Syria until they be consumed. Henry notes the false prophets' smooth prophecies, acted out with iron horns, were the flattery that cheated Ahab, for counsel most pleasing is not always best. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: And all the prophets prophesied so, saying, Go up to Ramothgilead, and prosper, for the Lord shall deliver it into the king's hand. Henry notes the 400 prophets spoke with one assent of prosperity, the lying spirit's work, and Ahab preferred those who humoured him to a good prophet who warned him. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: And the messenger that went to call Micaiah spake to him, saying, Behold, the prophets declare good with one assent; let thy word therefore be like one of theirs, and speak thou good. Henry notes the pressure on faithful ministers to speak smooth things, which Micaiah resisted, as ministers must deal plainly and faithfully. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: And Micaiah said, As the Lord liveth, even what my God saith, that will I speak. Henry notes Micaiah's fidelity: for discharging a good conscience he was later buffeted and imprisoned, but he could appeal to the issue, as all persecuted for faithfulness may. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: And when he was come to the king, the king said, Micaiah, shall we go to Ramothgilead to battle, or shall I forbear? And he said, Go ye up, and prosper, and they shall be delivered into your hand. Henry's consultation account applies: Micaiah first answered ironically in the prophets' own smooth terms, before being adjured to speak truth. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: And the king said to him, How many times shall I adjure thee that thou say nothing but the truth to me in the name of the Lord? Henry notes Ahab, knowing the difference between flattery and truth, adjured Micaiah to speak only truth in the Lord's name, yet would not receive it when spoken. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: Then he said, I did see all Israel scattered upon the mountains, as sheep that have no shepherd, and the Lord said, These have no master; let them return every man to his house in peace. Henry notes Micaiah's true prophecy foretold Ahab's fall and Israel scattered without a shepherd, the fair warning Ahab preferred flatterers to. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: And the king of Israel said to Jehoshaphat, Did I not tell thee that he would not prophesy good unto me, but evil? Henry notes Ahab's complaint proves Henry's point: he hated the prophet for telling truth, preferring flatterers, and was justly cheated into ruin. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: Again he said, Therefore hear the word of the Lord; I saw the Lord sitting upon his throne, and all the host of heaven standing on his right hand and on his left. Henry notes Micaiah's vision of the heavenly court grounds the lying-spirit account: God, by divine permission, gave Ahab up to delusion for refusing the love of truth. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: And the Lord said, Who shall entice Ahab king of Israel, that he may go up and fall at Ramothgilead? And one spake after this manner, and another after that manner. Henry notes the heavenly council's question shows God's justice in giving those who rebel against truth up to strong delusions to believe a lie. — Matthew Henry

20 COMMENTARY: Then there came out a spirit, and stood before the Lord, and said, I will entice him. And the Lord said unto him, Wherewith? Henry notes the power of Satan, by divine permission, in the children of disobedience: one spirit offered to entice Ahab to his fall. — Matthew Henry

21 COMMENTARY: And he said, I will go out, and be a lying spirit in the mouth of all his prophets. And the Lord said, Thou shalt entice him, and thou shalt also prevail: go out, and do even so. Henry notes one lying spirit made 400 lying prophets and used them to deceive Ahab, for the devil becomes a murderer by being a liar and destroys men by deceiving them. — Matthew Henry

22 COMMENTARY: Now therefore, behold, the Lord hath put a lying spirit in the mouth of these thy prophets, and the Lord hath spoken evil against thee. Henry notes God's justice: those who will not receive the love of truth are given up to believe a lie, and the lying spirit prevails to entice to ruin those who will not be persuaded to duty. — Matthew Henry

23 COMMENTARY: Then Zedekiah son of Chenaanah came near, and smote Micaiah upon the cheek, and said, Which way went the Spirit of the Lord from me to speak unto thee? Henry notes Micaiah was buffeted for faithfulness, and Jehoshaphat dared not rebuke the false prophet who basely abused the faithful seer. — Matthew Henry

24 COMMENTARY: And Micaiah said, Behold, thou shalt see on that day when thou shalt go into an inner chamber to hide thyself. Henry notes the day will declare who is right: persecuted faithful people will be vindicated when Christ appears, to their consolation and their persecutors' confusion, when they will see what they now refuse to believe. — Matthew Henry

25 COMMENTARY: Then the king of Israel said, Take ye Micaiah, and carry him back to Amon governor of the city, and to Joash the king's son. Henry notes Ahab committed the faithful seer to prison, as faithful ministers' hard lot has often been, to be hated and ill-treated for being true to God and kind to souls. — Matthew Henry

26 COMMENTARY: And say, Thus saith the king, Put this fellow in the prison, and feed him with bread of affliction and water of affliction, until I return in peace. Henry notes Micaiah was condemned to the bread and water of affliction, yet could appeal to the issue with assurance. — Matthew Henry

27 COMMENTARY: And Micaiah said, If thou certainly return in peace, then hath not the Lord spoken by me. And he said, Hearken, all ye people. Henry notes Micaiah appealed to the event as the test of true prophecy, and all persecuted for faithfulness may do the same, for the day will declare the right. — Matthew Henry

28 COMMENTARY: So the king of Israel and Jehoshaphat king of Judah went up to Ramothgilead. Henry notes the expedition went forward despite Micaiah's warning, with Jehoshaphat consenting to join Ahab, to his own endangering. — Matthew Henry

29 COMMENTARY: And the king of Israel said unto Jehoshaphat, I will disguise myself, and go to the battle, but put thou on thy robes. So Israel disguised himself, and they went to battle. Henry notes Ahab, pretending friendship, really aimed at Jehoshaphat's life, advising him to wear robes to make himself an easy mark, a treachery justly repaid when Ahab was taken in the pit he dug. — Matthew Henry

30 COMMENTARY: Now the king of Syria had commanded his chariot captains, saying, Fight ye not with small or great, save only with the king of Israel. Henry notes the enemy's captains had their eye on the robes and orders to strike only Israel's king, setting the trap for Jehoshaphat. — Matthew Henry

31 COMMENTARY: And it came to pass, when the chariot captains saw Jehoshaphat, they said, It is the king of Israel. Therefore they compassed about him to fight, but Jehoshaphat cried out, and the Lord helped him, and God moved them to depart from him. Henry notes Jehoshaphat cried out, to friends or enemies or, most likely, to God for succour, and found it not in vain, for God has men's hearts in hand and turns them contrary to first intentions to serve his purposes. — Matthew Henry

32 COMMENTARY: For it came to pass, when the chariot captains perceived it was not the king of Israel, they turned back from pursuing him. Henry notes the Lord helped Jehoshaphat out of distress by moving the captains to depart, an invisible power moving men unaccountably to themselves and others. — Matthew Henry

33 COMMENTARY: And a certain man drew a bow at a venture, and smote the king of Israel between the joints of the harness, therefore he said to his chariot man, Turn thine hand, that thou mayest carry me out of the host, for I am wounded. Henry notes wicked Ahab disguising himself thought himself secured, yet was slain, for no art or arms can save those God has appointed to ruin, while Jehoshaphat is safe in his robes and Ahab killed in armour. — Matthew Henry

34 COMMENTARY: And the battle increased that day, howbeit the king of Israel stayed himself up in his chariot against the Syrians until the even, and about sunset he died. Henry notes the race is not to the swift nor the battle to the strong: Ahab, who would not heed Micaiah, died at sunset, fulfilling the word he had despised. — Matthew Henry
