Study Notes — 2 Chronicles

Chapter 27

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Jotham was twenty-five years old when he began to reign, and he reigned sixteen years in Jerusalem. His mother's name also was Jerushah, daughter of Zadok. Henry notes this is a very short account of a pious prosperous prince, of whom one would wish to have known more, and the brevity is welcome because his story needs no melancholy conclusion of degeneracy like the last three kings. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: And he did that which was right in the sight of the Lord, according to all that his father Uzziah did, howbeit he entered not into the temple of the Lord. And the people did yet corruptly. Henry notes what was amiss in his father he amended in himself: he did all his father did well and wisely, but would not imitate his presumption in entering the temple to burn incense, taking warning by his fate, though what was amiss in his people he could not prevail to amend, for they did yet corruptly. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: He built the high gate of the house of the Lord, and on the wall of Ophel he built much. Henry notes he prospered and became reputable: he began with the Lord's house gate, repairing, beautifying and raising it, then fortified Ophel's wall. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: Moreover he built cities in the mountains of Judah, and in the forests he built castles and towers. Henry notes he took all possible care for fortifying his country and replenishing it, building cities in Judah's mountains and castles and towers in the forests. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: He fought also with the king of the Ammonites, and prevailed against them. And the children of Ammon gave him the same year an hundred talents of silver, and ten thousand measures of wheat, and ten thousand of barley. So much did Ammon's children pay unto him, both the second year, and the third. Henry notes he conquered: he prevailed against the Ammonites, who had invaded Judah in Jehoshaphat's time, triumphed over them and exacted great contributions, 100 talents of silver and 10,000 measures each of wheat and barley for three years. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: So Jotham became mighty, because he prepared his ways before the Lord his God. Henry notes he became mighty in wealth, power and influence on neighbouring nations, and this he got by preparing his ways before the Lord his God, walking circumspectly, steadily and constantly in duty, unlike predecessors who ran well but were hindered by inconstancy. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: Now the rest of the acts of Jotham, and all his wars, and his ways, lo, they are written in the book of the kings of Israel and Judah. Henry notes his wars abroad and ways at home were so glorious they were recorded in the book of the kings of Israel as well as Judah. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: He was five and twenty years old when he began to reign, and reigned sixteen years in Jerusalem. Henry notes he finished his course too soon but with honour, dying at forty-one, yet not outliving his reputation as the last three predecessors did. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: And Jotham slept with his fathers, and they buried him in the city of David, and Ahaz his son reigned in his stead. Henry notes the chapter's last words are the most melancholy: Ahaz his son, whose character in all respects was the reverse of his, reigned in his stead, and when wealth and power with which wise men did good devolve upon fools who will do hurt, it is a lamentation. — Matthew Henry
