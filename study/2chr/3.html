Study Notes — 2 Chronicles

Chapter 3

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Solomon began to build the house of the Lord at Jerusalem on Mount Moriah, where the Lord appeared to David, in the place David prepared on Ornan's threshingfloor. Henry notes Solomon was neither at liberty to choose nor at a loss to fix the place: it was determined before, at the place of atonement by fire, fitly where Abraham's offering typified Christ's sacrifice. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: He began on the second day of the second month, in the fourth year of his reign. Henry notes the first three years were not trifled away but spent in necessary preparations, gathering so many hands, and possibly this was a sabbatical year when the people had leisure for the work. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: Solomon was instructed for the building: the length by the first measure was sixty cubits, and the breadth twenty cubits. Henry notes these dimensions, given by divine wisdom and taught him by his father, were the rule he went by, and what God does shall be for ever, nothing to be added or taken away. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: The porch in front was twenty cubits long, according to the breadth of the house, and a hundred and twenty high, overlaid within with pure gold. Henry groups the porch with the temple's rich ornaments: fine timber within overlaid with pure gold, substantially rich beyond any modern house's paint. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: The greater house he ceiled with fir tree, overlaid with fine gold, and set thereon palm trees and chains. Henry notes the gold was embossed with palm trees and chains, the timber-work very fine yet within overlaid with fine gold. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: He garnished the house with precious stones for beauty, and the gold was gold of Parvaim, the best gold. Henry notes the precious stones dedicated to God were set where they would show to best advantage, as a type of the new Jerusalem whose walls and foundations are of precious stones. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: He overlaid the house, its beams, posts, walls and doors, with gold, and graved cherubim on the walls. Henry notes beams, posts, walls and doors alike were overlaid, so that the whole interior shone with gold. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: He made the most holy house twenty cubits long and twenty broad, overlaid with fine gold amounting to six hundred talents. Henry notes the most holy place, ten yards square, was overlaid top, bottom and sides with fine gold. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: The weight of the nails was fifty shekels of gold, and he overlaid the upper chambers with gold. Henry notes every nail, screw or pin fastening the golden plates weighed fifty shekels, workmanship and all, and even the upper chambers were overlaid. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: In the most holy house he made two cherubim of image work and overlaid them with gold. Henry notes these large cherubim were added, doubtless by divine appointment, to fill the enlarged holy place, which otherwise would have looked bare like an unfurnished room. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: The wings of the cherubim were twenty cubits long: one wing of one cherub, five cubits, reached to the wall, and the other wing likewise five cubits reached to the wing of the other cherub. Henry notes each wing extended five cubits, so the whole was twenty cubits, just the breadth of the most holy place. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: One wing of the other cherub, five cubits, reached to the wall, and its other wing, five cubits, joined to the wing of the other cherub. Henry's point is the same span: wing met wing across the whole breadth, filling the holy place from wall to wall. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: The wings spread forth twenty cubits, and the cherubim stood on their feet, their faces inward. Henry notes they stood as servants, faces toward the ark, not to be adored but as attendants on the invisible God: we must not worship angels, but worship with angels. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: He made the veil of blue, purple, crimson and fine linen, and wrought cherubim thereon. Henry notes the veil denoted the darkness of that dispensation and the distance worshippers were kept at, but at Christ's death it was rent, so that through him we have boldness to enter the holiest. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: Also he made before the house two pillars thirty-five cubits high, with a chapiter of five cubits on each. Henry notes both together were somewhat above thirty-five cubits, about eighteen cubits high apiece. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: He made chains, as in the oracle, and put them on the heads of the pillars, and made a hundred pomegranates and put them on the chains. Henry groups these ornaments with the pillars Jachin and Boaz, whose names signify establishment and strength in temple-work and by it. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: He reared the pillars before the temple, one on the right hand and the other on the left, calling the right Jachin and the left Boaz. Henry notes the names mean establishment and strength, a standing witness that temple-work stands firm by God's strength. — Matthew Henry
