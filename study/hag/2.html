Study Notes — Haggai

Chapter 2

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: In the seventh month, on the twenty-first day, in the second year of Darius, God's word came again by Haggai. This was not quite a month after the people had begun the work, and shortly before Zechariah began to prophesy. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: The word is addressed again to Zerubbabel, Joshua, and the residue of the people, the same leaders and remnant as in chapter 1. Henry groups this whole opening section as encouragement to a discouraged people to proceed. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: Who is left that saw this house in its first glory, and how do you see it now, as nothing in comparison? Elders who remembered Solomon's temple wept at the contrast, and the Jews counted five lacks: sacred fire, Shekinah, ark, Urim and Thummim, and prophecy. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: Yet now be strong, Zerubbabel, Joshua, and all the people, and work, for I am with you, says the Lord of hosts. The greatest strength is to have the Lord with us as our strength, not man's might but God's Spirit. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: God is with them according to the word he covenanted when they came out of Egypt, and his Spirit remains among them, so they need not fear. The covenant promise at Sinai and the present inspiration of Haggai are both motives to persevere. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: Yet once, in a little while, God will shake heavens, earth, sea, and dry land. JFB reads this as a series of shakings of nations and world powers, beginning before Messiah's first coming and finished at his second, clearing the way for his unshakeable kingdom. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: He will shake all nations, and the Desire of all nations shall come, and fill this house with glory. Henry identifies this Desire as Christ, desirable to all nations, in whom all the earth is blessed; JFB adds that he alone satisfies the yearning all nations felt unconsciously for a Saviour. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: The silver is mine and the gold is mine, says the Lord of hosts. If he pleased he could adorn the temple with precious metals, but he will adorn it with a far more precious glory, the presence of his Son. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: The glory of this latter house shall be greater than the former, and in this place God will give peace. That greater glory is Messiah himself present in the temple, and the peace is first peace with God through his blood, then peace in the heart, then peace on earth. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: On the twenty-fourth day of the ninth month, in the second year of Darius, God's word came by Haggai a third time. This was a little over two months after the second prophecy, and the people had made considerable progress in the work meanwhile. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: Haggai is told to ask the priests concerning the law, putting a test question to them. The priests were the authorized expounders of the law, so their ruling would settle the principle Haggai was about to apply. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: If one carries holy flesh in his garment's skirt and the skirt touches bread, stew, wine, oil, or any food, shall it be holy? The priests answered, No: holiness in the skirt cannot be passed onward to something else. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: But if one unclean by a dead body touches any of these, shall it be unclean? The priests answered, It shall be unclean. Legal impurity spreads readily, while legal holiness is not so easily communicated. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: So is this people and this nation before me, and so is every work of their hands, and what they offer there is unclean. Their sacrifices could not sanctify disobedient offerers; instead their disobedience defiled their very offerings, a warning against falling back. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: Now consider from this day backward, from before a stone was laid upon a stone in the temple. They are to compare the evils of their disobedient past with the present, when obedience has resumed, and perceive blessing following obedience. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: Since those days, one coming to a heap expected to be twenty measures found but ten, and one coming to the press for fifty vessels found but twenty. The shortfall was the visible fruit of the curse on neglected duty. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: God smote them with blasting, mildew, and hail in all the labours of their hands, yet they did not turn to him. Affliction hardens the heart when it is not referred to God as its author. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

18 COMMENTARY: Consider now from this day onward, from the twenty-fourth day of the ninth month, from the day the foundation of the Lord's temple was laid. The blessing promised in verse 19 is set in deliberate contrast with the curse of verses 16-17. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

19 COMMENTARY: Is the seed yet in the barn? No: vine, fig, pomegranate, and olive have not yet brought forth, yet from this day God will bless them. They may trust his promise before any visible sign appears, so the blessing will be evidently his, not nature's. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

20 COMMENTARY: Again God's word came to Haggai on the twenty-fourth day of the month, the same date as the third prophecy. This fourth prophecy turns from the people at large to Zerubbabel their civil leader and representative. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

21 COMMENTARY: Speak to Zerubbabel: I will shake the heavens and the earth. The Jews feared these convulsions would destroy their national existence, so the promise is addressed to their representative to assure them of safety in God's electing love. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

22 COMMENTARY: God will overthrow the throne of kingdoms, destroy the strength of heathen kingdoms, and overthrow chariots, horses, and riders, each by his brother's sword. All world kingdoms fall to make way for Christ's universal kingdom of peace. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

23 COMMENTARY: In that day God will take Zerubbabel his servant and make him as a signet, for he has chosen him. A signet is the king's seal of delegated authority, constantly in his regard; Zerubbabel is the type, and Messiah his descendant the antitype, in whom God's people are sealed and safe. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
