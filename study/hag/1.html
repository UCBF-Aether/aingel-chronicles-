Study Notes — Haggai

Chapter 1

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: In the second year of Darius the Persian king, God's word came by Haggai to Zerubbabel the governor and Joshua the high priest. JFB notes the Jews, having no king of their own, dated by the reigning world king, and Haggai addresses civil and religious leaders together. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: The people said the time had not come to build the Lord's house, pleading delay as if past interruption proved God's timing. JFB calls this ingenuity in excuses: their real motive was selfish dislike of trouble, expense, and danger. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: God's word came again by Haggai to answer that excuse directly. Henry groups this verse with the whole reproof of verses 1-11: the people did not refuse outright, they only said, Not yet. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: Is it time for you to dwell in panelled, luxurious houses while God's house lies waste? The shameful contrast, JFB notes, is between their comfort and a temple whose walls were not even raised above the foundations. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: Therefore the Lord of hosts says, Consider your ways: set your heart on what you have done and what you have suffered. Pondering earnestly whether seeking self at God's expense has actually gained anything is the first step back. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: They sowed much and brought in little, ate without being satisfied, drank without being filled, clothed themselves without warmth, and wages vanished like money put into a bag with holes. The punishment answered the sin: the poverty they hoped to avoid by neglecting the temple came upon them for neglecting it. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: Again the Lord says, Consider your ways, repeating the call to self-examination. Henry urges every reader to examine his own spiritual state rather than prying into other people's ways while careless of his own. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: Go up to the mountain, bring wood, and build the house, and God will take pleasure in it and be glorified. Wood is named as the first necessity from the wooded hills around, not to the exclusion of stone, and building honours God, whose presence the temple mirrors. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: They looked for much and it came to little, and even the little brought home God blew upon and scattered. The reason is given plainly: his house lay waste while every man ran eagerly to his own house. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: Therefore heaven was stayed from dew and earth from her fruit, as inanimate nature obeys God's will. JFB pictures the sky withholding its goods, shocked, as it were, at the people's disobedience. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: God himself called for drought on land, mountains, corn, wine, oil, all the ground's produce, men, cattle, and all the labour of their hands. The wordplay is pointed: they left his house waste, so a wasting drought came on all that was theirs. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: Then Zerubbabel, Joshua, and all the remnant obeyed the voice of the Lord and Haggai's words, and the people feared before the Lord. Their purpose of obedience counted as obedience in God's eyes even before the work itself began. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: Haggai, the Lord's messenger speaking in the Lord's message, answered them: I am with you, says the Lord. On the mere disposition to obey, God at once passes from reproof to tenderness, and his presence is the best of blessings, guaranteeing success. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: The Lord stirred up the spirit of Zerubbabel, of Joshua, and of all the remnant, and they came and did work in the house of the Lord. Every good impulse and revival, JFB notes, is God's direct work by his Spirit, giving slothful people alacrity and perseverance. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: This began on the twenty-fourth day of the sixth month, in the second year of Darius, twenty-three days after Haggai's first message. Those who have lost time, Henry urges, need to redeem it, and the longer they loitered, the more haste they should make. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
