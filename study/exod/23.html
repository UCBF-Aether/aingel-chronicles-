Study Notes — Exodus

Chapter 23

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Thou shalt not raise a false report, and put not thine hand with the wicked to be an unrighteous witness. Henry notes the law of Moses bears plain marks of sound moral feeling: neither parties, friends, witnesses, nor common opinions must move us to misrepresent anything. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: Thou shalt not follow a multitude to do evil, neither shalt thou speak in a cause to decline after many to wrest judgment. Henry observes numbers do not make wrong right: a crowd following evil is still a crowd doing evil, and a cause must not be bent to please the many. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: Neither shalt thou countenance a poor man in his cause. Henry notes justice must not favour even the poor: pity for poverty must not lessen great faults or excuse offenders, for the law is no respecter of persons. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: If thou meet thine enemy's ox or his ass going astray, thou shalt surely bring it back to him again. Henry observes love of neighbour began with an enemy's beast: enmity did not cancel the duty of return. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: If thou see the ass of him that hateth thee lying under his burden, and wouldest forbear to help him, thou shalt surely help with him. Henry notes the harder case: not only returning the strayed beast, but lifting the burden of a hater, for mercy must overcome enmity in act, not only in word. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: Thou shalt not wrest the judgment of thy poor in his cause. Henry observes the poor man's cause was guarded twice over: neither favoured in verse 3 nor wrested here, for justice must be straight for the weak as for the strong. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: Keep thee far from a false matter, and the innocent and righteous slay thou not, for I will not justify the wicked. Henry notes the distance commanded: not merely avoid falsehood, but keep far from it, for God will not justify the wicked even when courts do. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: Thou shalt take no gift, for the gift blindeth the wise and perverteth the words of the righteous. Henry observes bribes blind even the wise: the gift does its work before the judge knows he is bought. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: Also thou shalt not oppress a stranger, for ye know the heart of a stranger, seeing ye were strangers in the land of Egypt. Henry notes the reason was experience: Israel knew the stranger's heart from Egypt, and that knowledge was to become mercy. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: Six years thou shalt sow thy land and gather in the fruits thereof. Henry observes the six years of labour were the ordinary course, that the seventh year's rest might stand out as God's appointment, not as idleness. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: But the seventh year thou shalt let it rest and lie still, that the poor of thy people may eat, and what they leave the beasts of the field shall eat, and in like manner thou shalt deal with thy vineyard and oliveyard. Henry notes the rest taught dependence on providence and God's faithfulness in sending the larger increase while they kept his appointments, and it was typical of the heavenly rest when all earthly labours shall cease. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: Six days thou shalt do thy work, and on the seventh day thou shalt rest, that thine ox and thine ass may rest, and the son of thy handmaid and the stranger may be refreshed. Henry observes the weekly rest reached beast and servant and stranger alike, for rest was a gift to all who laboured. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: In all things that I have said unto you be circumspect, and make no mention of the name of other gods, neither let it be heard out of thy mouth. Henry notes all respect to the heathen gods was strictly forbidden, and since Israel leaned toward idolatry, they must blot out even the remembrance of those gods. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: Three times thou shalt keep a feast unto me in the year. Henry observes solemn religious attendance on God, in the place he should choose, was strictly required: they must come together before the Lord, and what a good Master we serve, who has made it our duty to rejoice before him. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: Thou shalt keep the feast of unleavened bread, eating unleavened bread seven days as commanded, in the time appointed of the month Abib, for in it thou camest out from Egypt, and none shall appear before me empty. Henry notes they were not to come empty-handed, and so now we must not come to worship God empty-hearted, but filled with holy desires and dedications of ourselves. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: And the feast of harvest, the firstfruits of thy labours which thou hast sown in the field, and the feast of ingathering, at the end of the year, when thou hast gathered in thy labours out of the field. Henry observes the feasts marked the year's rhythm: firstfruits in the spring and ingathering at the end, that every harvest might be returned to God in thanks. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: Three times in the year all thy males shall appear before the LORD God. Henry notes the thrice-yearly appearing gathered the nation before God, that worship might be national as well as household. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: Thou shalt not offer the blood of my sacrifice with leavened bread, neither shall the fat of my sacrifice remain until the morning. Henry observes the sacrifices were guarded from mixture and delay: leaven had no place beside blood, and the fat was not to be kept overnight. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: The first of the firstfruits of thy land thou shalt bring into the house of the LORD thy God, and thou shalt not seethe a kid in his mother's milk. Henry notes the firstfruits belonged to God's house, and the strange prohibition guarded against a cruel and idolatrous practice of the nations. — Matthew Henry

20 COMMENTARY: Behold, I send an Angel before thee, to keep thee in the way, and to bring thee into the place which I have prepared. Henry notes the promise of guidance through the wilderness to the land of promise, and the Angel is Christ, the Angel of Jehovah, sent before his people. — Matthew Henry

21 COMMENTARY: Beware of him, and obey his voice, provoke him not, for he will not pardon your transgressions, for my name is in him. Henry observes the precept joined with the promise: obedience to this Angel, in whom God's name dwelt, was the condition of guidance. — Matthew Henry

22 COMMENTARY: But if thou shalt indeed obey his voice, and do all that I speak, then I will be an enemy unto thine enemies, and an adversary unto thine adversaries. Henry notes how reasonable the conditions were: serve the only true God, not the gods of the nations which are no gods at all, and God himself would fight their battles. — Matthew Henry

23 COMMENTARY: For mine Angel shall go before thee, and bring thee in unto the Amorites, Hittites, Perizzites, Canaanites, Hivites, and Jebusites, and I will cut them off. Henry observes the Angel went before as well as with: the conquest was led, not merely assisted, by God. — Matthew Henry

24 COMMENTARY: Thou shalt not bow down to their gods, nor serve them, nor do after their works, but thou shalt utterly overthrow them, and quite break down their images. Henry notes the command was total: no bowing, no serving, no imitating, and the images themselves were to be broken down. — Matthew Henry

25 COMMENTARY: And ye shall serve the LORD your God, and he shall bless thy bread and thy water, and I will take sickness away from the midst of thee. Henry notes how rich the particulars of the promise were: the comfort of food, the continuance of health, the increase of wealth, and the prolonging of life to old age. — Matthew Henry

26 COMMENTARY: There shall nothing cast their young, nor be barren, in thy land, and the number of thy days I will fulfil. Henry observes godliness has the promise of the life that now is, as well as of that which is to come, in fruitfulness and full years. — Matthew Henry

27 COMMENTARY: I will send my fear before thee, and will destroy all the people to whom thou shalt come, and I will make all thine enemies turn their backs unto thee. Henry notes the fear sent before was itself a weapon: enemies would flee before Israel ever struck a blow. — Matthew Henry

28 COMMENTARY: And I will send hornets before thee, which shall drive out the Hivite, the Canaanite, and the Hittite. Henry observes hosts of hornets made way for the hosts of Israel, for such mean creatures can God use for chastising his people's enemies. — Matthew Henry

29 COMMENTARY: I will not drive them out from before thee in one year, lest the land become desolate, and the beast of the field multiply against thee. Henry notes the kindness in the delay: a land emptied at once would become a wilderness of beasts, so God restrained his own conquest. — Matthew Henry

30 COMMENTARY: By little and little I will drive them out from before thee, until thou be increased, and inherit the land. Henry observes that in real kindness to the church, its enemies are subdued by little and little, keeping us on guard and in continual dependence on God, as corruptions are driven out of hearts not all at once but gradually. — Matthew Henry

31 COMMENTARY: And I will set thy bounds from the Red Sea even unto the sea of the Philistines, and from the desert unto the river, for I will deliver the inhabitants of the land into thine hand, and thou shalt drive them out before thee. Henry notes the bounds were set by God himself, from sea to sea and desert to river, a large inheritance for an obedient people. — Matthew Henry

32 COMMENTARY: Thou shalt make no covenant with them, nor with their gods. Henry notes the precept with the promise: no friendship with idolaters, for those who would keep from bad courses must keep from bad company. — Matthew Henry

33 COMMENTARY: They shall not dwell in thy land, lest they make thee sin against me, for if thou serve their gods, it will surely be a snare unto thee. Henry closes on the danger: it is dangerous to live in a bad neighbourhood, for others' sins will be our snares, and our greatest danger is from those who would make us sin against God. — Matthew Henry
