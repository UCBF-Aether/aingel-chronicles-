Study Notes — Exodus

Chapter 10

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: The LORD told Moses to go in to Pharaoh, for he had hardened Pharaoh's heart and his servants' hearts, that he might show his signs before him. Henry observes the hardening is stated before the plague, lest any think the plague caused what the heart had already chosen. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: The plagues were given that Israel might tell their sons and grandsons what God had wrought in Egypt, and know that he is the LORD. Henry notes the judgments were also lessons for generations: deliverance must be told, not merely enjoyed. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: Moses and Aaron asked Pharaoh, How long wilt thou refuse to humble thyself before the LORD God of the Hebrews? Let my people go, that they may serve me. Henry observes the question exposed the root: not ignorance but refusal to humble himself. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: Else, if Pharaoh refused, tomorrow God would bring the locusts into his coast. Henry notes the eighth plague was aimed at what the hail had spared, finishing the ruin of Egypt's crops. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: The locusts would cover the face of the earth so one could not see the earth, and eat the residue escaped from the hail and every tree growing. Henry observes the plague left nothing green: it consumed the remnant, not the abundance. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: The locusts would fill Pharaoh's houses, his servants' houses, and all Egyptians' houses, such as their fathers had never seen. Henry notes the invasion was domestic as well as agricultural: the houses that held Egypt's wealth would hold its plague. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: Pharaoh's servants asked, How long shall this man be a snare unto us? Let the men go and serve the LORD; knowest thou not that Egypt is destroyed? Henry observes the court at last spoke against the king, for ruin had taught them what wonders could not. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: Moses and Aaron were brought again to Pharaoh, who said, Go, serve the LORD your God, but who are they that shall go? Henry notes the concession hid a trap: Pharaoh would grant worship while keeping hostages. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: Moses answered, We will go with our young and old, our sons and daughters, our flocks and herds, for we must hold a feast unto the LORD. Henry observes the demand was total: no part of Israel could be left behind, for the whole people were God's. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: Pharaoh retorted, Let the LORD be so with you as I will let you go with your little ones; look to it, for evil is before you. Henry reads the sneer: Pharaoh mocked God's presence while threatening evil, as if he, not God, held their fate. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: Not so, said Pharaoh: go now ye that are men and serve the LORD, for that ye did desire, and they were driven out from his presence. Henry notes the compromise was refused and the messengers expelled, for a divided Israel was no Israel at all. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: The LORD told Moses to stretch out his hand over Egypt for the locusts, that they might come up and eat every herb the hail had left. Henry observes the command followed the expulsion at once: rejected mercy gave way to judgment. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: Moses stretched his rod over Egypt, and the LORD brought an east wind all that day and night, and in the morning the east wind brought the locusts. Henry notes the natural means were God's own: wind from the desert carried the army of insects. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: The locusts went up over all Egypt and rested in all its coasts, very grievous, such as neither before nor after were like them. Henry observes the plague was unmatched in scale, as the hail had been unmatched in kind. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: They covered the face of the whole earth, so that the land was darkened, and they ate every herb and all the fruit the hail had left, so that no green thing remained. Henry notes the darkness was twofold: the sky darkened by swarms, the land stripped bare beneath. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: Pharaoh called for Moses and Aaron in haste, confessing, I have sinned against the LORD your God and against you. Henry observes the confession now named both God and his messengers, yet it was spoken in haste, under pressure. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: Forgive my sin only this once, said Pharaoh, and intreat the LORD your God, that he may take away from me this death only. Henry notes he called the locusts death, rightly, for they devoured the life of the land, yet he sought pardon only for this once. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: Moses went out from Pharaoh and intreated the LORD. Henry observes Moses again interceded for a king who had confessed and would soon deny, showing prayer is not conditioned on the sinner's sincerity. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: The LORD turned a mighty strong west wind, which took away the locusts and cast them into the Red Sea, so that not one remained in all Egypt's coasts. Henry notes the removal was as miraculous as the coming: a wind reversed, a sea swallowed an army. — Matthew Henry

20 COMMENTARY: But the LORD hardened Pharaoh's heart, so that he would not let Israel go. Henry observes the removal of the plague removed the repentance with it, the now-familiar pattern of a heart hardened by mercy. — Matthew Henry

21 COMMENTARY: The LORD told Moses to stretch his hand toward heaven, that there might be darkness over Egypt, even darkness which may be felt. Henry observes the ninth plague struck the sun itself, Egypt's chief god, with a darkness thick enough to touch. — Matthew Henry

22 COMMENTARY: Moses stretched his hand toward heaven, and there was thick darkness in all the land of Egypt three days. Henry notes the duration: three days of night without dawn, a figure of the darkness of a soul forsaken by God. — Matthew Henry

23 COMMENTARY: They saw not one another, neither rose any from his place for three days, but all Israel had light in their dwellings. Henry observes the division was now absolute: Egypt sat in palpable night while Goshen shone, neighbours separated by light itself. — Matthew Henry

24 COMMENTARY: Pharaoh called Moses and said, Go ye, serve the LORD; only let your flocks and herds be stayed, but let your little ones go with you. Henry notes the new bargain: the people might go, but their wealth must stay, a chain of property instead of persons. — Matthew Henry

25 COMMENTARY: Moses answered, Thou must give us also sacrifices and burnt offerings, that we may sacrifice unto the LORD our God. Henry observes the reply turned the bargain upside down: Pharaoh must supply, not withhold, the means of worship. — Matthew Henry

26 COMMENTARY: Our cattle also shall go with us; there shall not a hoof be left behind, for thereof must we take to serve the LORD, and we know not with what we shall serve him until we come thither. Henry notes the principle: worship requires its substance, and God's demands cannot be met with what is left behind. — Matthew Henry

27 COMMENTARY: But the LORD hardened Pharaoh's heart, and he would not let them go. Henry observes the ninth plague ended as the eighth had: light restored to Egypt, darkness restored to Pharaoh's heart. — Matthew Henry

28 COMMENTARY: Pharaoh said, Get thee from me, take heed to thyself, see my face no more; for in the day thou seest my face thou shalt die. Henry notes the threat was the last word of a defeated king: barred from the court, the messenger was threatened with death for returning. — Matthew Henry

29 COMMENTARY: Moses answered, Thou hast spoken well, I will see thy face again no more. Henry observes Moses accepted the dismissal calmly, for his work before Pharaoh was nearly done, and the next meeting would be on God's terms, not the king's. — Matthew Henry
