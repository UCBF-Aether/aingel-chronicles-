Study Notes — Exodus

Chapter 6

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: The LORD answered Moses's complaint: Now shalt thou see what I will do to Pharaoh, for with a strong hand he would both let Israel go and drive them out. Henry notes the answer to increased affliction was not explanation but a nearer promise of deliverance. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: God spake to Moses, saying, I am the LORD. Henry notes the name stands at the head of every promise in this chapter, grounding each one in God's unchanging being. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: God had appeared to Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob as God Almighty, but by his name JEHOVAH he was not known to them. Henry explains the patriarchs knew God by promise, while Israel would know him by performance, in the great works of deliverance. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: God had established his covenant to give the patriarchs Canaan, the land of their pilgrimage. Henry notes the covenant was made long before and had waited through centuries, yet stood unbroken. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: God had also heard Israel's groaning under Egyptian bondage and remembered his covenant. Henry observes the two motives joined: God's compassion for present misery and his faithfulness to ancient promise. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: Wherefore Moses was to tell Israel: I am the LORD, I will bring you out from under Egypt's burdens, deliver you from bondage, and redeem you with a stretched-out arm and great judgments. Henry notes redemption here is by power and judgment together. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: I will take you to me for a people, and I will be to you a God, and ye shall know I am the LORD who brought you out. Henry calls this the heart of the covenant: deliverance ends in belonging, and knowledge of God comes by experience of his acts. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: God would bring Israel into the land he swore to give Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob, and give it them for a heritage, for he is the LORD. Henry notes the promise reaches past Egypt to Canaan: deliverance is not an end in itself but the road to inheritance. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: Moses spake so to Israel, but they hearkened not for anguish of spirit and cruel bondage. Henry observes affliction had dulled their ears: they could not take comfort from a promise while the brick-kilns still smoked. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: The LORD spake again to Moses, renewing the command after the people's refusal. Henry notes God did not withdraw the commission because the first hearing failed. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: Moses was told to go in and speak to Pharaoh, that he let Israel go out of his land. Henry observes the command was repeated after disappointment, for God's purposes do not change with man's reception. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: Moses answered that Israel had not hearkened to him, so how should Pharaoh hearken, seeing he was of uncircumcised lips? Henry notes Moses argued from the lesser to the greater, and made his own weakness the reason to shrink. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: The LORD gave Moses and Aaron a charge unto Israel and unto Pharaoh, to bring Israel out of Egypt. Henry notes the charge was laid on both brothers together, so that neither could plead the other's duty. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: Here begins the record of the heads of the fathers' houses, starting with Reuben, the firstborn of Israel. Henry notes the genealogy is set here to show the stock from which the deliverers came, beginning in birth order. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: These are the names of Levi's sons according to their generations: Gershon, Kohath, and Merari, and Levi lived a hundred and thirty-seven years. Henry notes the long lives spanning the bondage, a quiet witness that God preserved the tribe through the oppression. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: The sons of Kohath were Amram, Izhar, Hebron, and Uzziel, and Kohath lived a hundred and thirty-three years. Henry notes the generations are counted to bring the line steadily down to Moses and Aaron. — Matthew Henry

20 COMMENTARY: Amram took Jochebed his father's sister to wife, and she bare him Aaron and Moses, and Amram lived a hundred and thirty-seven years. Henry notes this is the hinge of the genealogy: from this marriage came the prophet and the priest of the Exodus. — Matthew Henry

23 COMMENTARY: Aaron took Elisheba, daughter of Amminadab and sister of Naashon, to wife, and she bare him Nadab, Abihu, Eleazar, and Ithamar. Henry notes the priestly house is thus recorded, the family from which Israel's priesthood would come. — Matthew Henry

25 COMMENTARY: Eleazar, Aaron's son, took a daughter of Putiel to wife, and she bare him Phinehas. Henry notes the priestly line continues here, carrying the house of Aaron into the next generation. — Matthew Henry

26 COMMENTARY: These are that Aaron and Moses to whom the LORD said, Bring out Israel from Egypt according to their armies. Henry notes the genealogy ends where it began, with the two men charged to lead a people ordered like an army. — Matthew Henry

27 COMMENTARY: These are they which spake to Pharaoh to bring out Israel from Egypt, even that Moses and Aaron. Henry observes the repetition seals their identity: the men of the pedigree are the men of the commission. — Matthew Henry

28 COMMENTARY: On the day the LORD spake to Moses in Egypt, the narrative resumes after the genealogy. Henry notes the break was purposeful: the reader now knows who speaks to Pharaoh and by what right. — Matthew Henry

29 COMMENTARY: The LORD spake to Moses, saying, I am the LORD: speak thou unto Pharaoh all that I say unto thee. Henry notes the command is total: Moses must deliver all, not a softened part, of God's word. — Matthew Henry

30 COMMENTARY: Moses said, Behold, I am of uncircumcised lips, and how shall Pharaoh hearken unto me? Henry notes the old excuse returned at the very threshold, and God's answer would again be his own presence, not Moses's eloquence. — Matthew Henry

No note for verse(s) 15, 17, 19, 21, 22, 24 — the public-domain sources consulted give no distinct explanation for these verses beyond the list itself, so nothing was invented for them.
