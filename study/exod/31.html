Study Notes — Exodus

Chapter 31

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: And the LORD spake unto Moses, giving the workmen for the work he had commanded. Henry notes the honour which comes from God is always attended with a work to be done, and to be employed for God is high honour. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: See, I have called by name Bezaleel the son of Uri, the son of Hur, of the tribe of Judah. Henry observes the call was by name: God chose a particular man from Judah, and named him, for the work of his house was personal, not anonymous. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: And I have filled him with the spirit of God, in wisdom, and in understanding, and in knowledge, and in all manner of workmanship. Henry notes the Israelites, who had been masons and bricklayers in Egypt, were not qualified for curious workmanship, but the Spirit who gave the apostles utterance miraculously gave Bezaleel the skill that was wanting. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: To devise cunning works, to work in gold, and in silver, and in brass. Henry observes the Spirit's gifts covered design as well as execution: to devise cunning works, and then to work them in gold, silver, and brass. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: And in cutting of stones, to set them, and in carving of timber, to work in all manner of workmanship. Henry notes the skill extended to stone and timber alike: the craftsman was fitted for every material the tabernacle required. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: And I, behold, I have given with him Aholiab, the son of Ahisamach, of the tribe of Dan, and in the hearts of all that are wise hearted I have put wisdom, that they may make all that I have commanded thee. Henry observes those whom God calls to any service, he will find or make fit for it, and the Lord gives different gifts to different persons, so let each mind his proper work, remembering that whatever wisdom any possesses, the Lord put it in the heart. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: The tabernacle of the congregation, and the ark of the testimony, and the mercy seat that is thereupon, and all the furniture of the tabernacle. Henry notes the work list began with the house and its throne: tabernacle, ark, and mercy seat, with all their furniture. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: And the table and his furniture, and the pure candlestick with all his furniture, and the altar of incense. Henry observes the holy place furniture followed: table, candlestick, and incense altar, the bread, the light, and the prayer of the house. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: And the altar of burnt offering with all his furniture, and the laver and his foot. Henry notes the court furniture followed: the altar of sacrifice and the laver of washing, the outer service of the house. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: And the cloths of service, and the holy garments for Aaron the priest, and the garments of his sons, to minister in the priest's office. Henry observes the garments were part of the same commission: the priests' clothing was made by the same Spirit-filled hands as the house. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: And the anointing oil, and sweet incense for the holy place, according to all that I have commanded thee shall they do. Henry notes the oil and incense completed the list, and the rule governed all: according to all that God had commanded, nothing added and nothing left out. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: And the LORD spake unto Moses, giving the sabbath law after the tabernacle law. Henry notes orders were now given that a tabernacle should be set up, but they must not think the nature of the work, or the haste required, would justify working at it on sabbath days. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: Speak thou also unto the children of Israel, saying, Verily my sabbaths ye shall keep, for it is a sign between me and you throughout your generations, that ye may know that I am the LORD that doth sanctify you. Henry observes the sabbath was a sign between God and Israel, and the Hebrew word shabath signifies rest, or ceasing from labour, and the rest signified is that rest in glory which remains for the people of God. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: Ye shall keep the sabbath therefore, for it is holy unto you, and every one that defileth it shall surely be put to death, and whosoever doeth any work therein, that soul shall be cut off from among his people. Henry notes the penalty matched the holiness: defiling the sabbath was capital, and working on it cut the worker off from the people. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: Six days may work be done, but in the seventh is the sabbath of rest, holy to the LORD, and whosoever doeth any work in the sabbath day, he shall surely be put to death. Henry observes the six days of work were granted, but the seventh was holy to the LORD, and no work, not even tabernacle work, might invade it. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: Wherefore the children of Israel shall keep the sabbath, to observe the sabbath throughout their generations, for a perpetual covenant. Henry notes the sabbath was a perpetual covenant, observed throughout generations, for the moral obligation of the sabbath must continue till time is swallowed up in eternity. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: It is a sign between me and the children of Israel forever, for in six days the LORD made heaven and earth, and on the seventh day he rested, and was refreshed. Henry observes the sign rested on creation itself: six days of making, and a seventh of rest, a pattern older than Sinai. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: And he gave unto Moses, when he had made an end of communing with him upon mount Sinai, two tables of testimony, tables of stone, written with the finger of God. Henry notes the law was written in tables of stone to show how lasting it is, and to denote the hardness of our hearts, for one might more easily write on stone than write anything good on corrupt natural hearts, and it was written with the finger of God, by his will and power, for God only can write his law in the heart. — Matthew Henry
