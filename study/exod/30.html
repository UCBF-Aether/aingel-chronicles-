Study Notes — Exodus

Chapter 30

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Thou shalt make an altar to burn incense upon, of shittim wood shalt thou make it. Henry notes the altar of incense represented the Son of God in his human nature, and the incense burned thereon typified his pleading for his people. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: A cubit shall be the length thereof, and a cubit the breadth thereof, foursquare shall it be, and two cubits shall be the height thereof, and the horns thereof shall be of the same. Henry observes the incense altar was small and square, a cubit each way, for the place of intercession was not large, but it was of the same piece as its horns. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: Thou shalt overlay it with pure gold, the top thereof, and the sides thereof round about, and the horns thereof, and thou shalt make unto it a crown of gold round about. Henry notes the pure gold overlay marked it as the golden altar, distinct from the brazen altar of sacrifice, for intercession is nearer the throne than sacrifice. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: And two golden rings shalt thou make to it under the crown of it, by the two corners thereof, upon the two sides of it shall they be, for places for the staves to bear it withal. Henry observes the rings under the crown bore the altar, that the place of prayer might travel with the camp. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: And thou shalt make the staves of shittim wood, and overlay them with gold. Henry notes the staves were wood overlaid with gold like the altar itself, common wood made glorious by its service. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: And thou shalt put it before the vail that is by the ark of the testimony, before the mercy seat that is over the testimony, where I will meet with thee. Henry observes the altar stood before the vail, nearest to the mercy seat, for intercession stands closest to the throne. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: And Aaron shall burn thereon sweet incense every morning, when he dresseth the lamps, he shall burn incense upon it. Henry notes the continual intercession of Christ was represented by the daily burning of incense, morning and evening, joined to the dressing of the lamps. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: And when Aaron lighteth the lamps at even, he shall burn incense upon it, a perpetual incense before the LORD throughout your generations. Henry observes the evening incense matched the morning, a perpetual incense, for Christ's intercession never ceases. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: Ye shall offer no strange incense thereon, nor burnt sacrifice, nor meat offering, neither shall ye pour drink offering thereon. Henry notes the altar admitted only its own incense: no strange fire, no sacrifice, no offering beside the appointed sweet incense. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: And Aaron shall make an atonement upon the horns of it once in a year with the blood of the sin offering of atonements, and once in the year shall he make atonement upon it throughout your generations, for it is most holy unto the LORD. Henry observes once every year the blood of atonement was applied to it, denoting that Christ's intercession has all its virtue from his sufferings on earth, and we need no other sacrifice or intercessor but Christ alone. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: And the LORD spake unto Moses, giving the law of the ransom. Henry notes the numbering of Israel was joined to a ransom, that counting God's people might not become presumption. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: When thou takest the sum of the children of Israel after their number, then shall they give every man a ransom for his soul unto the LORD, when thou numberest them, that there be no plague among them, when thou numberest them. Henry observes the ransom guarded the census: every soul counted must be ransomed, lest numbering breed a plague. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: This they shall give, every one that passeth among them that are numbered, half a shekel after the shekel of the sanctuary, a shekel is twenty gerahs, and a half shekel shall be the offering of the LORD. Henry notes the tribute was half a shekel, about fifteen pence, a small sum for a great truth: every soul owed the same ransom. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: Every one that passeth among them that are numbered, from twenty years old and above, shall give an offering unto the LORD. Henry observes the age of twenty marked the counted: those of military age, who could bear arms, bore the ransom. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: The rich shall not give more, and the poor shall not give less than half a shekel, when they give an offering unto the LORD, to make an atonement for their souls. Henry notes the rich were not to give more, nor the poor less, for the souls of rich and poor are alike precious, and God is no respecter of persons, and in other offerings men gave according to ability, but this, the ransom of the soul, must be alike for all. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: And thou shalt take the atonement money of the children of Israel, and shalt appoint it for the service of the tabernacle of the congregation, that it may be a memorial unto the children of Israel before the LORD, to make an atonement for their souls. Henry observes the money raised was used in the service of the tabernacle, for those who have the benefit must not grudge the necessary charges of God's public worship, and money cannot make atonement for the soul, but it may be used for the honour of him who has made the atonement. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: And the LORD spake unto Moses, giving the law of the laver. Henry notes the laver followed the ransom: counted and ransomed, the priests must now be washed for service. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: Thou shalt also make a laver of brass, and his foot also of brass, to wash withal, and thou shalt put it between the tabernacle of the congregation and the altar, and thou shalt put water therein. Henry observes a large vessel of brass holding water was set near the door of the tabernacle, between the altar and the house, for washing stood between sacrifice and service. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: For Aaron and his sons shall wash their hands and their feet thereat. Henry notes Aaron and his sons must wash hands and feet at this laver every time they went in to minister, to teach them purity in all their services and to dread the pollution of sin. — Matthew Henry

20 COMMENTARY: When they go into the tabernacle of the congregation, they shall wash with water, that they die not, and when they come near to the altar to minister, to burn offering made by fire unto the LORD. Henry observes they must not only wash and be made clean when first made priests, but must wash and be kept clean whenever they went to minister. — Matthew Henry

21 COMMENTARY: So they shall wash their hands and their feet, that they die not, and it shall be a statute forever unto him and to his seed throughout their generations. Henry notes the statute was forever, teaching us daily to attend upon God, daily to renew repentance for sin and looking to the blood of Christ for remission, for in many things we daily offend. — Matthew Henry

22 COMMENTARY: Moreover the LORD spake unto Moses, giving the recipe of the holy oil. Henry notes directions are here given for making the holy anointing oil and the incense, to show the excellency of holiness, for there was this spiced oil in the tabernacle, grateful to sight and smell. — Matthew Henry

23 COMMENTARY: Take thou also unto thee principal spices, of pure myrrh five hundred shekels, and of sweet cinnamon half so much, even two hundred and fifty shekels, and of sweet calamus two hundred and fifty shekels. Henry observes the principal spices were measured by weight: myrrh five hundred, cinnamon and calamus two hundred and fifty each, precious spices for a precious oil. — Matthew Henry

24 COMMENTARY: And of cassia five hundred shekels, after the shekel of the sanctuary, and of oil olive a hin. Henry notes cassia matched the myrrh at five hundred, and a hin of olive oil carried them all, for the oil was the base of the ointment. — Matthew Henry

25 COMMENTARY: And thou shalt make it an oil of holy ointment, an ointment compound after the art of the apothecary, and it shall be an holy anointing oil. Henry observes the compound was made after the apothecary's art, a skilled mixture, for holiness itself was to be compounded with care. — Matthew Henry

26 COMMENTARY: And thou shalt anoint the tabernacle of the congregation therewith, and the ark of the testimony. Henry notes the oil anointed first the house and then the ark, for Christ's name is as ointment poured forth, and the good name of Christians is like precious ointment. — Matthew Henry

27 COMMENTARY: And the table and all his vessels, and the candlestick and his vessels, and the altar of incense. Henry observes the oil reached table, candlestick, and incense altar in turn, sanctifying every vessel of the holy place. — Matthew Henry

28 COMMENTARY: And the altar of burnt offering with all his vessels, and the laver and his foot. Henry notes the oil reached even the outer court, altar and laver alike, for the whole service, inner and outer, was anointed. — Matthew Henry

29 COMMENTARY: And thou shalt sanctify them, that they may be most holy, and whatsoever toucheth them shall be holy. Henry observes the anointing sanctified the vessels to be most holy, so that whatever touched them was holy, for holiness spread from the anointed things. — Matthew Henry

30 COMMENTARY: And thou shalt anoint Aaron and his sons, and consecrate them, that they may minister unto me in the priest's office. Henry notes the priests were anointed with the same oil as the vessels, for men and things were consecrated together to one service. — Matthew Henry

31 COMMENTARY: And thou shalt speak unto the children of Israel, saying, This shall be an holy anointing oil unto me throughout your generations. Henry observes the oil was holy throughout generations, not for one anointing only, for the consecration it signified was perpetual. — Matthew Henry

32 COMMENTARY: Upon man's flesh shall it not be poured, neither shall ye make any other like it, after the composition of it, for it is holy, and it shall be holy unto you. Henry notes the oil was guarded from common use: it must not be poured on common flesh, nor copied, for God would keep reverence for his own services. — Matthew Henry

33 COMMENTARY: Whosoever compoundeth any like it, or whosoever putteth any of it upon a stranger, shall even be cut off from his people. Henry observes the penalty was cutting off, for to counterfeit the holy oil was to profane what God had made holy. — Matthew Henry

34 COMMENTARY: And the LORD said unto Moses, Take unto thee sweet spices, stacte, and onycha, and galbanum, and these sweet spices with pure frankincense, of each shall there be a like weight. Henry notes the incense was prepared of sweet spices, each of like weight, for the fragrance before God was compounded in equal measure. — Matthew Henry

35 COMMENTARY: And thou shalt make it a perfume, a confection after the art of the apothecary, tempered together, pure and holy. Henry observes the perfume was tempered together, pure and holy, a confection of the apothecary's art, for prayer itself must be compounded with purity. — Matthew Henry

36 COMMENTARY: And thou shalt beat some of it very small, and put of it before the testimony in the tabernacle of the congregation, where I will meet with thee, and it shall be unto you most holy. Henry notes the incense was beaten very small, and thus it pleased the Lord to bruise the Redeemer, when he offered himself for a sacrifice of a sweet-smelling savour. — Matthew Henry

37 COMMENTARY: And as for the perfume which thou shalt make, ye shall not make to yourselves according to the composition thereof, for it shall be unto you holy for the LORD. Henry notes the perfume, like the oil, was not to be made for private use, for God would keep his services from becoming common. — Matthew Henry

38 COMMENTARY: Whosoever shall make like unto that, to smell thereto, shall even be cut off from his people. Henry closes on the warning: it is a great affront to God to jest with sacred things, and most dangerous to use professions of the gospel to forward worldly interests. — Matthew Henry
