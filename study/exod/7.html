Study Notes — Exodus

Chapter 7

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: God said, I have made thee a god to Pharaoh, and Aaron thy brother shall be thy prophet. Henry explains the strange title: Moses would speak with divine authority, and Aaron would utter what Moses received, as a prophet utters God's word. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: Moses was to speak all God commanded, and Aaron was to speak to Pharaoh, that he send Israel out. Henry notes the division of labour was God's own appointment, answering Moses's plea of uncircumcised lips. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: God would harden Pharaoh's heart and multiply his signs and wonders in Egypt. Henry explains the hardening as judicial: Pharaoh's repeated refusals were given over to their own course, that God's wonders might be multiplied. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: Pharaoh would not hearken, that God might lay his hand upon Egypt and bring forth his armies by great judgments. Henry notes the plagues were not accidents but judgments, each aimed at Egypt's gods and pride. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: The Egyptians would know that God is the LORD when he stretched out his hand upon Egypt and brought out Israel. Henry observes the plagues were also revelation: Egypt would learn the name Pharaoh had scorned. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: Moses and Aaron did as the LORD commanded them, so did they. Henry notes the simple obedience recorded here contrasts with all their earlier excuses, and it is the pattern of the whole deliverance. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: Moses was eighty years old and Aaron eighty-three when they spoke to Pharaoh. Henry observes God called old men to this work, that the power might plainly be his and not theirs. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: The LORD spake to Moses and Aaron, directing them before they entered Pharaoh's court again. Henry notes every step was directed: the messengers did not improvise before the king. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: When Pharaoh demanded a miracle, Aaron was to cast his rod before him, and it would become a serpent. Henry notes the sign was fitted to the demand: Pharaoh asked for proof, and proof was given at once. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: Aaron cast down his rod before Pharaoh and his servants, and it became a serpent. Henry observes the wonder was done openly in court, where it could neither be hidden nor denied. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: Pharaoh called the wise men and sorcerers, and the magicians of Egypt did in like manner with their enchantments. Henry notes Satan's counterfeits appear wherever God's true wonders are wrought, to keep unbelief supplied with excuses. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: Each magician's rod became a serpent, but Aaron's rod swallowed up their rods. Henry reads the swallowing as the verdict on the contest: God's power devoured the imitation, leaving Pharaoh without a rival wonder. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: Pharaoh's heart was hardened, and he hearkened not, as the LORD had said. Henry notes the miracle convinced no one who had resolved not to be convinced; the heart, not the eye, was the seat of the refusal. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: The LORD told Moses Pharaoh's heart was hardened and he refused to let the people go. Henry observes the diagnosis preceded the first plague: the disease was named before the cure was applied. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: Moses was to meet Pharaoh in the morning as he went out to the water, standing by the river's brink with the rod in his hand. Henry notes the place was chosen: Pharaoh at his river-god, confronted where his pride and worship met. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: Moses was to say, The LORD God of the Hebrews hath sent me, saying, Let my people go, that they may serve me in the wilderness, but hitherto thou wouldest not hear. Henry observes the demand was now backed by warning: past refusal would be answered by judgment. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: In this thou shalt know that I am the LORD: Moses would smite the river's waters with the rod, and they would be turned to blood. Henry notes the sign answered Pharaoh's question, Who is the LORD? with a deed, not a debate. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: The fish would die, the river would stink, and the Egyptians would loathe to drink of it. Henry observes the plague struck Egypt's life-source and its god at once: the Nile that fed them became undrinkable. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: Aaron was to stretch his hand over all Egypt's waters, rivers, streams, and pools, and over vessels of wood and stone, that all might become blood. Henry notes the universality left no refuge: stored water as well as flowing water turned. — Matthew Henry

20 COMMENTARY: Moses and Aaron did so, and Aaron lifted the rod and smote the waters in the sight of Pharaoh and his servants, and all the waters became blood. Henry observes the wonder was wrought before witnesses, that none could call it a tale. — Matthew Henry

21 COMMENTARY: The fish died, the river stank, the Egyptians could not drink, and there was blood throughout all the land of Egypt. Henry notes the completeness of the stroke: a nation's drink became its horror for seven days. — Matthew Henry

22 COMMENTARY: The magicians did so with their enchantments, and Pharaoh's heart was hardened, neither did he hearken. Henry observes the counterfeit only deepened the plague, for it added blood without removing any, yet Pharaoh took it as excuse. — Matthew Henry

23 COMMENTARY: Pharaoh turned and went into his house, neither did he set his heart to this also. Henry notes the most damning response of all: not rage, but indifference, walking away from a judgment as if it were nothing. — Matthew Henry

24 COMMENTARY: All the Egyptians digged round about the river for water to drink, for they could not drink of the river. Henry observes the people felt what their king ignored, digging for life beside a river of death. — Matthew Henry

25 COMMENTARY: Seven days were fulfilled after the LORD had smitten the river. Henry notes the plague was given its full week, that Egypt might feel the duration as well as the stroke, before the next demand came. — Matthew Henry
