Study Notes — Exodus

Chapter 19

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: In the third month after Israel went forth from Egypt, the same day they came into the wilderness of Sinai. Henry notes the timing: three months from bondage to the mountain where the covenant would be given. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: They had departed from Rephidim and come to the desert of Sinai, and pitched in the wilderness, and Israel camped before the mount. Henry observes the camp was set facing the mountain, for the whole people were about to meet God there. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: Moses went up unto God, and the LORD called unto him out of the mountain, saying, Thus shalt thou say to the house of Jacob, and tell the children of Israel. Henry notes Moses was called up as messenger of the covenant, whose Maker and first Mover is God himself. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: Ye have seen what I did unto the Egyptians, and how I bare you on eagles' wings, and brought you unto myself. Henry observes the covenant was granted out of God's own free grace, and the eagle's wings picture a deliverance wholly God's work, carrying a helpless people to himself. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: Now therefore, if ye will obey my voice indeed, and keep my covenant, then ye shall be a peculiar treasure unto me above all people, for all the earth is mine. Henry notes this was the national covenant, by which Israel became a people under Jehovah's government, a type and shadow of the new covenant in Christ. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: Ye shall be unto me a kingdom of priests, and an holy nation. These are the words which thou shalt speak unto the children of Israel. Henry observes the dignity and the duty were joined: a priestly kingdom must be holy, set apart to God among all nations. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: Moses came and called for the elders of the people, and laid before their faces all these words which the LORD commanded him. Henry notes the covenant was laid before the elders first, that the people's answer might be deliberate and representative. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: All the people answered together, saying, All that the LORD hath spoken we will do, and Moses returned the words of the people unto the LORD. Henry observes Israel consented as one man, and adds, Oh that there had been such a heart in them! For Moses as mediator returned their words to God, as Christ the Mediator reveals God's will to us and offers our resolutions to God. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: The LORD said unto Moses, Lo, I come unto thee in a thick cloud, that the people may hear when I speak with thee, and believe thee forever, and Moses told the words of the people unto the LORD. Henry notes the thick cloud served the people's faith: they would hear God speak with Moses and believe him forever after. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: The LORD told Moses to go unto the people and sanctify them today and tomorrow, and let them wash their clothes. Henry observes the solemn preparation was meant to impress the people with a right sense of the Divine majesty, and to convince them of their own guilt. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: They were to be ready against the third day, for on the third day the LORD would come down in the sight of all the people upon mount Sinai. Henry notes the set day taught reverence: God would come publicly, before all eyes, not in secret. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: Moses was to set bounds unto the people round about, saying, Take heed to yourselves, that ye go not up into the mount, or touch the border of it, for whosoever toucheth the mount shall surely be put to death. Henry observes the bounds preached holiness and distance: in drawing near to God we must never forget his greatness nor our own meanness and pollution. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: There was to be no hand touching the offender, but he would surely be stoned or shot through, whether beast or man, and when the trumpet sounded long they might come up to the mount. Henry notes even the punishment kept its distance: no hand was to touch what had touched the holy mount. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: Moses went down from the mount unto the people, and sanctified the people, and they washed their clothes. Henry observes the mediator carried the command down at once, and the people obeyed in outward preparation for the inward law. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: Moses said unto the people, Be ready against the third day, and come not at your wives. Henry notes the readiness was total, touching even household life, that nothing might distract from meeting God. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: On the third day in the morning there were thunders and lightnings, and a thick cloud upon the mount, and the voice of the trumpet exceeding loud, so that all the people in the camp trembled. Henry observes never was there such a sermon preached as this to the church in the wilderness, yet terrors alone could not check presumption in an unawakened heart. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: Moses brought forth the people out of the camp to meet with God, and they stood at the nether part of the mount. Henry notes the meeting was real: the people were brought out to stand before God, not merely to hear about him. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: Mount Sinai was altogether on a smoke, because the LORD descended upon it in fire, and the smoke ascended as the smoke of a furnace, and the whole mount quaked greatly. Henry observes the fire and quaking displayed the majesty before which no sinner can stand in judgment by his own obedience. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: When the voice of the trumpet sounded long and waxed louder and louder, Moses spake, and God answered him by a voice. Henry notes the dialogue itself was the wonder: a man spoke, and God answered by a voice, in the hearing of the camp. — Matthew Henry

20 COMMENTARY: The LORD came down upon mount Sinai, on the top of the mount, and called Moses up to the top, and Moses went up. Henry observes the descent and the ascent met: God came down, and Moses went up, the pattern of mediation. — Matthew Henry

21 COMMENTARY: The LORD told Moses to go down and charge the people, lest they break through unto the LORD to gaze, and many of them perish. Henry notes the warning guarded against curiosity: even after thunder and fire, sinners would trifle with the most terrible threatenings. — Matthew Henry

22 COMMENTARY: Let the priests also, which come near to the LORD, sanctify themselves, lest the LORD break forth upon them. Henry observes nearness increased the danger, not lessened it: priests who came near without sanctification would be broken forth upon. — Matthew Henry

23 COMMENTARY: Moses answered that the people could not come up, for God had charged them to set bounds about the mount and sanctify it. Henry notes Moses pleaded the bounds already set, as if the warning were needless. — Matthew Henry

24 COMMENTARY: The LORD said, Away, get thee down, and thou shalt come up, thou, and Aaron with thee, but let not the priests and the people break through to come up unto the LORD, lest he break forth upon them. Henry observes the command was repeated with Aaron added: mediation was to be shared, but the bounds stood. — Matthew Henry

25 COMMENTARY: So Moses went down unto the people, and spake unto them. Henry observes the chapter ends with the mediator descending to speak, for the law was about to be given to a people who had been warned, prepared, and set in their place. — Matthew Henry
