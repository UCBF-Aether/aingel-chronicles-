Study Notes — Exodus

Chapter 1

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Exodus opens by naming those who went down into Egypt with Jacob, continuing the Genesis story into the forming of Israel as a church and a nation. JFB sends the reader back to the fuller list in Genesis 46. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: After Joseph's generation died, Israel grew fruitful and exceedingly mighty in Egypt. JFB adds the natural circumstance of Egypt's famed fruitfulness to the fulfilment of the promise made to Abraham. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: A new king arose who knew not Joseph, after a revolution some sixty years after Joseph's death united upper and lower Egypt. JFB notes the new dynasty would have known nothing of the Hebrews and regarded them, as foreigners and shepherds, with dislike. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: The new king told his people Israel had become more and mightier than they, fearing they would join Egypt's enemies. Henry observes that the offence of Israel was simply that he prospered, a sight hateful to a wicked man. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: Pharaoh proposed dealing wisely with Israel lest they multiply further and escape in war. Henry calls this human wisdom foolish and sinful, since counsel against the Lord only creates greater vexation to those who take it. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: Taskmasters were set over Israel to afflict them, and they built the treasure cities Pithom and Raamses. JFB describes serf labour driven in bands on public works, and places both fortified store-cities in Goshen near the invasion route from Asia. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: The more Israel was afflicted, the more they multiplied and grew. Henry draws the church's comfort from it: Christianity spread most when persecuted, and the blood of the martyrs was the seed of the church. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: The Egyptians made Israel serve with rigour, embittering their lives with mortar and brick. JFB notes crude sun-baked brick was universal in Egypt, and monuments still depict brickmakers watched by taskmasters with uplifted sticks. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: Every kind of field service was added to the brickwork, all of it imposed with rigour. Henry stresses the burden was meant not only for Pharaoh's profit but to afflict, so that their very lives became bitter. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: The king spoke to the Hebrew midwives Shiphrah and Puah, evidently the heads of their company. JFB suggests that by tampering with these two, Pharaoh designed to terrify the rest into secret compliance. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: The midwives were ordered to kill every Hebrew son at birth and spare the daughters. JFB records divided opinion on the method, whether the birth-stools were low seats or stone troughs by the riverside. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: The midwives feared God and saved the male children alive, disobeying a cruel tyrant at the risk of their lives. JFB adds that their faith was blended with weakness, shrinking from speaking the whole truth. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

18 COMMENTARY: Pharaoh demanded why the midwives had saved the children alive. Their answer, that Hebrew women were lively and delivered before the midwives arrived, was the plea by which they covered their disobedience. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: The midwives told Pharaoh Hebrew women were not like Egyptian women but vigorous, giving birth before help arrived. Henry reads the whole episode as the serpent's seed showing enmity against the Seed of the woman, forgetting all pity. — Matthew Henry

20 COMMENTARY: God dealt well with the midwives and the people multiplied and waxed very mighty. JFB explains the Hebrew idiom: to build a house means to have a numerous progeny, so God protected the midwives and Israel prospered. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

21 COMMENTARY: Because the midwives feared God, he made them houses, that is, gave them families of their own. Henry notes services done for God's Israel are often repaid in kind. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

22 COMMENTARY: Pharaoh then charged all his people to cast every Hebrew son into the river and save every daughter alive. Henry sees the same enemy who tried to destroy the church in its infancy still busy stifling godly reflection in the heart of man. — Matthew Henry

No note for verse(s) 2, 3, 4, 5, 6 — the public-domain sources consulted give no distinct explanation for these verses beyond the list itself, so nothing was invented for them.
