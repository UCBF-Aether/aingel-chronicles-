Study Notes — Exodus

Chapter 35

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: And Moses gathered all the congregation of Israel together, and said unto them, These are the words which the LORD hath commanded, that ye should do them. Henry notes the gathering was of all the congregation, for the sabbath and the offering concerned the whole people, not the priests alone. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: Six days shall work be done, but on the seventh day there shall be to you a holy day, a sabbath of rest to the LORD, and whosoever doeth work therein shall be put to death. Henry observes the mild and easy yoke of Christ has made our sabbath duties more delightful and our restraints less irksome than those of the Jews, but we are the more guilty by neglecting them, for God's wisdom in giving us the sabbath is sinfully disregarded. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: Ye shall kindle no fire throughout your habitations upon the sabbath day. Henry notes the prohibition reached the hearth itself: no fire kindled in any habitation, for the sabbath rest was household rest, not temple rest only. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: And Moses spake unto all the congregation of Israel, saying, This is the thing which the LORD commanded, saying. Henry observes the offering command came after the sabbath command, that the people might learn that even holy work must keep holy time. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: Take ye from among you an offering unto the LORD, and whosoever is of a willing heart, let him bring it, an offering of the LORD, gold, and silver, and brass. Henry notes the tabernacle was to be dedicated to God's honour and used in his service, and therefore what was brought for it was an offering to the Lord, and the rule is, Whosoever is of a willing heart, let him bring. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: And blue, and purple, and scarlet, and fine linen, and goats' hair. Henry observes the coloured cloths and hair were the first gifts named, for the curtains of the house required them before all else. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: And rams' skins dyed red, and badgers' skins, and shittim wood. Henry notes the skins and wood were the durable gifts: coverings dyed red and the hard wood of the frame. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: And oil for the light, and spices for anointing oil, and for the sweet incense. Henry observes the oil and spices served light and fragrance: the house must shine and smell sweet, and both required gifts. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: And onyx stones, and stones to be set for the ephod, and for the breastplate. Henry notes the precious stones were for the priest's garments, that the mediator might bear the tribes before God. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: And every wise hearted among you shall come, and make all that the LORD hath commanded. Henry observes all that were skilful must work, for God dispenses his gifts, and as every man hath received, so he must minister, and those with materials to work on and those with skill must serve together, as they needed one another. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: The tabernacle, his tent, and his covering, his taches, and his boards, his bars, his pillars, and his sockets. Henry notes the work list began with the house itself: tent, covering, clasps, boards, bars, pillars, and sockets, the whole frame. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: The ark, and the staves thereof, with the mercy seat, and the vail of the covering. Henry observes the ark with its staves, mercy seat, and vail stood next, for the throne within the house was the heart of the work. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: The table, and his staves, and all his vessels, and the shewbread. Henry notes the table with its staves, vessels, and shewbread followed, for the bread of the house must be furnished always. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: The candlestick also for the light, and his furniture, and his lamps, with the oil for the light. Henry observes the candlestick with its furniture, lamps, and oil followed, for the light of the house must be tended. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: And the incense altar, and his staves, and the anointing oil, and the sweet incense, and the hanging for the door at the entering in of the tabernacle. Henry notes the incense altar with its staves, oil, incense, and door hanging followed, for prayer and anointing belonged to the holy place. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: The altar of burnt offering, with his brasen grate, his staves, and all his vessels, the laver and his foot. Henry observes the court furniture followed: the altar of sacrifice with its grate and vessels, and the laver with its foot, the outer service. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: The hangings of the court, his pillars, and their sockets, and the hanging for the door of the court. Henry notes the court hangings, pillars, sockets, and gate hanging enclosed the whole, for the house stood within its court. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: The pins of the tabernacle, and the pins of the court, and their cords. Henry observes even the pins and cords were named, for no part of God's house was too small to be commanded. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: The cloths of service, to do service in the holy place, the holy garments for Aaron the priest, and the garments of his sons, to minister in the priest's office. Henry notes the cloths of service and the priestly garments closed the list, for the ministers' clothing was part of the house's furnishing. — Matthew Henry

20 COMMENTARY: And all the congregation of Israel departed from the presence of Moses. Henry observes the congregation departed to do what they had heard, for the command was not for hearing only but for bringing. — Matthew Henry

21 COMMENTARY: And they came, every one whose heart stirred him up, and every one whom his spirit made willing, and they brought the LORD's offering to the work of the tabernacle of the congregation, and for all his service, and for the holy garments. Henry notes without a willing mind, costly offerings would be abhorred, and with it, the smallest will be accepted, for our hearts are willing when we cheerfully assist in promoting the cause of God. — Matthew Henry

22 COMMENTARY: And they came, both men and women, as many as were willing hearted, and brought bracelets, and earrings, and rings, and tablets, all jewels of gold, and every man that offered offered an offering of gold unto the LORD. Henry observes men and women came together, bringing their own jewels, for the gold that had made the calf was now given to make the tabernacle. — Matthew Henry

23 COMMENTARY: And every man, with whom was found blue, and purple, and scarlet, and fine linen, and goats' hair, and red skins of rams, and badgers' skins, brought them. Henry notes each brought what he had: the man with blue brought blue, and the man with skins brought skins, for the offering matched the giver's store. — Matthew Henry

24 COMMENTARY: Every one that did offer an offering of silver and brass brought the LORD's offering, and every man, with whom was found shittim wood for any work of the service, brought it. Henry observes silver and brass and wood were offered as readily as gold, for the house needed every metal and every timber. — Matthew Henry

25 COMMENTARY: And all the women that were wise hearted did spin with their hands, and brought that which they had spun, both of blue, and of purple, and of scarlet, and of fine linen. Henry notes the women who spun were wise-hearted, because they did it heartily to the Lord, and those diligent and contented in employments considered mean are as much accepted of God as those engaged in splendid services. — Matthew Henry

26 COMMENTARY: And all the women whose heart stirred them up in wisdom spun goats' hair. Henry observes the goats' hair spinners are named apart, for their work was coarser, yet their wisdom was the same, and the labourer who attends to his work in the faith and fear of God may be as wise for his place as the most useful minister. — Matthew Henry

27 COMMENTARY: And the rulers brought onyx stones, and stones to be set, for the ephod, and for the breastplate. Henry notes the rulers brought the precious stones, for the costliest gifts befitted those with most, and the stones for the ephod and breastplate were the rulers' part. — Matthew Henry

28 COMMENTARY: And spice, and oil for the light, and for the anointing oil, and for the sweet incense. Henry observes spice and oil came last from the rulers, for the light and the fragrance of the house were their charge. — Matthew Henry

29 COMMENTARY: The children of Israel brought a willing offering unto the LORD, every man and woman, whose heart made them willing to bring for all manner of work, which the LORD had commanded to be made by the hand of Moses. Henry notes the summary: every man and woman whose heart made them willing brought, for our wisdom and duty consist in giving God the glory and use of our talents, be they many or few. — Matthew Henry

30 COMMENTARY: And Moses said unto the children of Israel, See, the LORD hath called by name Bezaleel the son of Uri, the son of Hur, of the tribe of Judah. Henry notes here is the divine appointment of the master-workmen, that there might be no strife for the office, and that all employed might take direction from, and give account to, them. — Matthew Henry

31 COMMENTARY: And he hath filled him with the spirit of God, in wisdom, in understanding, and in knowledge, and in all manner of workmanship. Henry observes those whom God called by name to his service, he filled with the Spirit of God, for skill, even in worldly employments, is God's gift and comes from above. — Matthew Henry

32 COMMENTARY: And to devise curious works, to work in gold, and in silver, and in brass. Henry notes Bezaleel was filled to devise as well as to work, for the master-workman must design before others can execute. — Matthew Henry

33 COMMENTARY: And in the cutting of stones, to set them, and in carving of wood, to make any manner of cunning work. Henry observes the skill covered stone and wood alike, for the master-workman must judge every material. — Matthew Henry

34 COMMENTARY: And he hath put in his heart that he may teach, both he, and Aholiab, the son of Ahisamach, of the tribe of Dan. Henry notes these men were not only to devise and work themselves, but to teach others, for those that rule should teach, and those to whom God has given knowledge should be willing to make it known for the benefit of others. — Matthew Henry

35 COMMENTARY: Them hath he filled with wisdom of heart, to work all manner of work, of the engraver, and of the cunning workman, and of the embroiderer, in blue, and in purple, in scarlet, and in fine linen, and of the weaver, even of them that do any work, and of those that devise cunning work. Henry closes on the breadth: engraver, cunning workman, embroiderer, weaver, and deviser, all filled with wisdom of heart, for the house required every craft, and God supplied every craftsman. — Matthew Henry
