Study Notes — Exodus

Chapter 32

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: When the people saw that Moses delayed to come down out of the mount, they gathered themselves together unto Aaron, and said, Up, make us gods, which shall go before us, for as for this Moses, the man that brought us up out of Egypt, we wot not what is become of him. Henry notes while Moses was in the mount receiving the law, the people made a tumultuous address to Aaron, for this giddy multitude were weary of waiting, and weariness in waiting betrays to many temptations. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: Aaron said unto them, Break off the golden earrings, which are in the ears of your wives, of your sons, and of your daughters, and bring them unto me. Henry notes their readiness to part with their earrings to make an idol should shame our niggardliness in the service of the true God, for they did not draw back on account of the cost of their idolatry. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: And all the people brake off the golden earrings which were in their ears, and brought them unto Aaron. Henry observes the people obeyed at once, stripping themselves of gold for a god they could see. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: And he received them at their hand, and fashioned it with a graving tool, after he had made it a molten calf, and they said, These be thy gods, O Israel, which brought thee up out of the land of Egypt. Henry notes Aaron produced the shape of an ox or calf, giving it some finish with a graving tool, and they changed the truth of God into a lie, calling the calf the god that had brought them out. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: And when Aaron saw it, he built an altar before it, and Aaron made proclamation, and said, To morrow is a feast to the LORD. Henry observes Aaron built an altar to the idol and proclaimed a feast to the LORD, mingling the true God's name with false worship, which made the sin worse, not better. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: And they rose up early on the morrow, and offered burnt offerings, and brought peace offerings, and the people sat down to eat and to drink, and rose up to play. Henry notes they offered sacrifice to this idol, and having set up an image, their sacrifices were abomination, for they had, only days before, heard the voice of God speaking out of the fire, Thou shalt not make to thyself any graven image. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: And the LORD said unto Moses, Go, get thee down, for thy people, which thou broughtest out of the land of Egypt, have corrupted themselves. Henry notes God says the Israelites had corrupted themselves, for sin is the corruption of the sinner, and it is a self-corruption, every man being tempted when drawn aside of his own lust. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: They have turned aside quickly out of the way which I commanded them, and they have made them a molten calf, and have worshipped it, and have sacrificed thereunto, and said, These be thy gods, O Israel, which have brought thee up out of the land of Egypt. Henry observes they turned aside out of the way, for sin is a departing from the way of duty into a by-path, and they soon forgot God's works, though they had so lately covenanted to do all he had said. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: And the LORD said unto Moses, I have seen this people, and, behold, it is a stiffnecked people. Henry notes God sees what men cannot discover, nor is any wickedness of the world hid from him, and we could not bear to see the thousandth part of the evil which God sees every day. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: Now therefore let me alone, that my wrath may wax hot against them, and that I may consume them, and that I may make of thee a great nation. Henry observes God expressed his just displeasure after the manner of men, as if Moses's prayer could save them from ruin, and thus Moses was a type of Christ, by whose mediation alone God would reconcile the world to himself. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: And Moses besought the LORD his God, and said, LORD, why doth thy wrath wax hot against thy people, which thou hast brought forth out of the land of Egypt with great power, and with a mighty hand? Henry notes Moses pleaded God's glory, for the glorifying of God's name, as it ought to be our first petition, so it ought to be our great plea. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: Wherefore should the Egyptians speak, and say, For mischief did he bring them out, to slay them in the mountains, and to consume them from the face of the earth? Turn from thy fierce wrath, and repent of this evil against thy people. Henry observes Moses pleaded that Egypt would blaspheme God's name if Israel perished, for God's honour among the nations was at stake. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: Remember Abraham, Isaac, and Israel, thy servants, to whom thou swarest by thine own self, and saidst unto them, I will multiply your seed as the stars of heaven, and all this land that I have spoken of will I give unto your seed, and they shall inherit it forever. Henry notes God's promises are to be our pleas in prayer, for what he has promised he is able to perform. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: And the LORD repented of the evil which he thought to do unto his people. Henry observes that in answer to Moses's prayers, God showed his purpose of sparing the people, as he had before seemed determined on their destruction, which change of the outward discovery of his purpose is called repenting of the evil. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: And Moses turned, and went down from the mount, and the two tables of the testimony were in his hand, the tables were written on both their sides, on the one side and on the other were they written. Henry notes what a change it is to come down from the mount of communion with God to converse with a wicked world: in God we see nothing but what is pure and pleasing, in the world nothing but what is sinful and provoking. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: And the tables were the work of God, and the writing was the writing of God, graven upon the tables. Henry observes the tables were God's work and God's writing, graven, not painted, for the law was meant to last. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: And when Joshua heard the noise of the people as they shouted, he said unto Moses, There is a noise of war in the camp. Henry notes Joshua, waiting below, mistook the noise for war, for he had not seen the camp's revelry. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: And he said, It is not the voice of them that shout for mastery, neither is it the voice of them that cry for being overcome, but the noise of them that sing do I hear. Henry observes Moses distinguished the sound at once: not war, not defeat, but singing, the noise of idolatrous feasting. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: And it came to pass, as soon as he came nigh unto the camp, that he saw the calf, and the dancing, and Moses's anger waxed hot, and he cast the tables out of his hands, and brake them beneath the mount. Henry notes the sight broke the tables: the law was broken in the camp before it was broken beneath the mount, for Israel had already broken the covenant the tables contained. — Matthew Henry

20 COMMENTARY: And he took the calf which they had made, and burnt it in the fire, and ground it to powder, and strewed it upon the water, and made the children of Israel drink of it. Henry notes that it might appear an idol is nothing in the world, Moses ground the calf to dust, and mixing the powder with their drink signified that the backslider in heart should be filled with his own ways. — Matthew Henry

21 COMMENTARY: And Moses said unto Aaron, What did this people unto thee, that thou hast brought so great a sin upon them? Henry observes the question put the blame where it belonged: Aaron, set apart by divine appointment to the priesthood, had shamed himself so far as to build an altar to a golden calf, and must own himself unworthy of attending at God's altar, indebted to free grace alone. — Matthew Henry

22 COMMENTARY: And Aaron said, Let not the anger of my lord wax hot, for thou knowest the people, that they are set on mischief. Henry notes Aaron began with the people's mischief, as if their pressure excused his compliance. — Matthew Henry

23 COMMENTARY: For they said unto me, Make us gods, which shall go before us, for as for this Moses, the man that brought us up out of the land of Egypt, we wot not what is become of him. Henry observes Aaron repeated the people's demand as his defence, but men can only tempt us to sin, they cannot force us, and we must never be drawn into sin by anything man can say or do. — Matthew Henry

24 COMMENTARY: And I said unto them, Whosoever hath any gold, let them break it off. So they gave it me, then I cast it into the fire, and there came out this calf. Henry notes never did any wise man make a more frivolous and foolish excuse than Aaron's, for the calf did not come out of the fire by itself, but by his graving tool. — Matthew Henry

25 COMMENTARY: And when Moses saw that the people were naked, for Aaron had made them naked unto their shame among their enemies, the approach of Moses turned their dancing into trembling. Henry notes they were exposed to shame by their sin, and Moses's course to roll away the reproach was not by concealing the sin or putting a false colour upon it, but by punishing it. — Matthew Henry

26 COMMENTARY: Then Moses stood in the gate of the camp, and said, Who is on the LORD's side? Let him come unto me. And all the sons of Levi gathered themselves together unto him. Henry observes the call divided the camp: those on the LORD's side gathered to Moses, and the Levites answered first. — Matthew Henry

27 COMMENTARY: And he said unto them, Thus saith the LORD God of Israel, Put every man his sword by his side, and go in and out from gate to gate throughout the camp, and slay every man his brother, and every man his companion, and every man his neighbour. Henry notes the Levites were to slay the ringleaders in this wickedness, yet none were executed but those who openly stood forth. — Matthew Henry

28 COMMENTARY: And the children of Levi did according to the word of Moses, and there fell of the people that day about three thousand men. Henry observes those who in the morning were shouting and dancing, before night were dying, for such sudden changes do the Lord's judgments sometimes make with sinners secure and jovial in their sin. — Matthew Henry

29 COMMENTARY: For Moses had said, Consecrate yourselves today to the LORD, even every man upon his son, and upon his brother, that he may bestow upon you a blessing this day. Henry notes the Levites' consecration came through judgment on their own kin, for loyalty to God outranked blood. — Matthew Henry

30 COMMENTARY: And it came to pass on the morrow, that Moses said unto the people, Ye have sinned a great sin, and now I will go up unto the LORD, peradventure I shall make an atonement for your sin. Henry notes Moses called it a great sin, for the work of ministers is to show people the greatness of their sins, and the great evil of sin appears in the price of pardon. — Matthew Henry

31 COMMENTARY: And Moses returned unto the LORD, and said, Oh, this people have sinned a great sin, and have made them gods of gold. Henry observes Moses came not to make excuses but to make atonement, confessing the sin in full before he pleaded for it. — Matthew Henry

32 COMMENTARY: Yet now, if thou wilt forgive their sin, and if not, blot me, I pray thee, out of thy book which thou hast written. Henry notes we are not to suppose Moses meant he would be willing to perish forever for the people's sake, for we are to love our neighbour as ourselves, and not more than ourselves, but having that mind which was in Christ, he was willing to lay down his life in the most painful manner, if he might thereby preserve the people. — Matthew Henry

33 COMMENTARY: And the LORD said unto Moses, Whosoever hath sinned against me, him will I blot out of my book. Henry observes the answer guarded substitution: each sinner stood in his own name, and Moses could not wholly turn away God's wrath, which shows the law of Moses was not able to reconcile men to God and perfect our peace with him. — Matthew Henry

34 COMMENTARY: Therefore now go, lead the people unto the place of which I have spoken unto thee, and behold, mine Angel shall go before thee, and nevertheless in the day when I visit I will visit their sin upon them. Henry notes the march would resume, but with a reservation: the sin was not forgotten, and visitation would come in its day. — Matthew Henry

35 COMMENTARY: And the LORD plagued the people, because they made the calf, which Aaron made. Henry closes on the sober end: no unhumbled, carnal heart can long endure the holy precepts and spiritual worship of God, but a god, a priest, a worship, and a doctrine suited to the carnal mind will ever meet with abundance of worshippers, and well is it for us that the Prophet like unto Moses has made atonement for our souls and now intercedes in our behalf. — Matthew Henry
