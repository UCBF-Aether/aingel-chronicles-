Study Notes — Exodus

Chapter 16

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: They journeyed from Elim and came to the wilderness of Sin, between Elim and Sinai, on the fifteenth day of the second month after departing Egypt. Henry notes the provisions brought from Egypt were spent by the middle of the second month, and want became the occasion of murmuring. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: The whole congregation murmured against Moses and Aaron in the wilderness. Henry observes it is no new thing for the greatest kindness to be basely represented as the greatest injury, and none talk more absurdly than murmurers. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: They said, Would to God we had died by the hand of the LORD in Egypt, when we sat by the flesh pots and ate bread to the full, for ye have brought us out to kill this assembly with hunger. Henry notes the absurdity: they had not plenty in Egypt, nor could they fear dying for want while they had flocks and herds. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: The LORD said to Moses, Behold, I will rain bread from heaven for you, and the people shall go out and gather a certain rate every day, that I may prove them whether they will walk in my law. Henry observes God promised a speedy and constant supply, and tried whether they would trust him and rest satisfied with the bread of the day in its day. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: On the sixth day they would prepare what they brought in, and it would be twice as much as they gathered daily. Henry notes the double portion was itself a provision for the sabbath, teaching them to prepare beforehand for holy rest. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: Moses and Aaron told all Israel, At even ye shall know that the LORD hath brought you out of Egypt. Henry observes the evening meal would settle the question the murmuring had raised: their deliverer was the LORD, not Moses. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: In the morning they would see the glory of the LORD, for he heard their murmurings against the LORD, and what were Moses and Aaron that they murmured against them? Henry notes the rebuke: when we begin to fret, we ought to consider that God hears all our murmurings. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: Moses said the LORD would give them flesh in the evening and bread in the morning to the full, and their murmurings were not against Moses and Aaron but against the LORD. Henry observes the distinction mattered: to murmur against God's servants for God's providence is to murmur against God himself. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: Moses told Aaron to say to the congregation, Come near before the LORD, for he hath heard your murmurings. Henry notes the summons was gracious even in rebuke: the murmurers were called near, not cast off. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: As Aaron spoke to the whole congregation, they looked toward the wilderness, and behold, the glory of the LORD appeared in the cloud. Henry observes the glory answered the summons at once, that Israel might know their God had heard. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: The LORD spake unto Moses, renewing the promise after the glory appeared. Henry notes God spoke again to confirm what the cloud had shown, that the people might have both sight and word. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: I have heard the murmurings of Israel, said the LORD: at even ye shall eat flesh, and in the morning ye shall be filled with bread, and ye shall know that I am the LORD your God. Henry observes the twofold lesson: when God plagued Egypt it was to make them know he was their Lord, and when he provided for Israel it was to make them know he was their God. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: At even the quails came up and covered the camp, and in the morning the dew lay round about the camp. Henry notes the quails came at evening and were caught with ease, as many as they needed, a sudden supply for a sudden want. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: When the dew was gone up, there lay upon the wilderness a small round thing, as small as the hoar frost. Henry observes the manna appeared when the dew was gone, small and round like coriander seed, in colour like pearls. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: When Israel saw it they said one to another, It is manna, for they wist not what it was, and Moses said, This is the bread which the LORD hath given you to eat. Henry notes they called it Manhu, What is this? and took it as the portion God had allotted them, with thankfulness. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: This is the thing the LORD commanded: Gather of it every man according to his eating, an omer for every man, according to the number of persons, every man for them which are in his tent. Henry observes God's bounty left room for man's duty: they must not eat till they have gathered. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: Israel did so and gathered, some more, some less. Henry notes the gathering itself taught diligence: with quietness working, and eating their own bread, not the bread of idleness. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: When they meted it with an omer, he that gathered much had nothing over, and he that gathered little had no lack, every man according to his eating. Henry observes the lesson of contentment: he who gathers much has nothing over, and he who gathers little has no lack, for there is less disproportion in enjoyment than in possession. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: Moses said, Let no man leave of it till the morning. Henry notes the command taught dependence on providence: they were to sleep quietly with no bread in their tents, trusting God to bring daily bread with the day. — Matthew Henry

20 COMMENTARY: Notwithstanding, some hearkened not and left of it until the morning, and it bred worms and stank, and Moses was wroth with them. Henry observes the folly of hoarding: manna laid up distrustfully bred worms, and what is covetously spared proves most wasted. — Matthew Henry

21 COMMENTARY: They gathered it every morning, every man according to his eating, and when the sun waxed hot, it melted. Henry notes the morning discipline: the gift had its hour, and those who let slip the morning of opportunity might find it too late to gather. — Matthew Henry

22 COMMENTARY: On the sixth day they gathered twice as much bread, two omers for one, and all the rulers of the congregation came and told Moses. Henry observes the double quantity on the sixth day was the sign of the sabbath provision, and the rulers reported what they did not yet understand. — Matthew Henry

23 COMMENTARY: Moses said, This is what the LORD hath said: Tomorrow is the rest of the holy sabbath unto the LORD; bake what ye will bake, seethe what ye will seethe, and lay up what remaineth until the morning. Henry notes the sabbath was known before Sinai, from the beginning, and God took care that his people should be no losers by keeping it. — Matthew Henry

24 COMMENTARY: They laid it up till the morning as Moses bade, and it did not stink, neither was there any worm therein. Henry observes the contrast: kept against a command it stank, kept by a command it was sweet and good, for everything is sanctified by the word of God and prayer. — Matthew Henry

25 COMMENTARY: Moses said, Eat that today, for today is a sabbath unto the LORD, and today ye shall not find it in the field. Henry notes the sabbath meal came from yesterday's gathering: rest was provided for, not improvised. — Matthew Henry

26 COMMENTARY: Six days ye shall gather it, but on the seventh day, which is the sabbath, in it there shall be none. Henry observes the rhythm was fixed: six days of gathering, one of rest, and the manna itself preached the week. — Matthew Henry

27 COMMENTARY: Some of the people went out on the seventh day to gather, and found none. Henry notes the disobedience proved the miracle: on the seventh day God did not send the manna, therefore they must not expect it nor go out to gather. — Matthew Henry

28 COMMENTARY: The LORD said unto Moses, How long refuse ye to keep my commandments and my laws? Henry observes the question rebuked not ignorance but refusal: the sabbath had been shown by double portions and empty fields, yet some still went out. — Matthew Henry

29 COMMENTARY: See, for the LORD hath given you the sabbath, therefore he giveth you on the sixth day the bread of two days; abide every man in his place, let no man go out of his place on the seventh day. Henry notes the sabbath was a gift, not a burden, and the double bread was the proof that none would lose by serving God. — Matthew Henry

30 COMMENTARY: So the people rested on the seventh day. Henry observes the rest finally came, after rebuke and demonstration, and the camp learned to keep the day. — Matthew Henry

31 COMMENTARY: The house of Israel called the name thereof Manna, and it was like coriander seed, white, and the taste of it was like wafers made with honey. Henry notes it was pleasant and wholesome food, unlike anything found before or since, ground or beaten and made into cakes and baked. — Matthew Henry

32 COMMENTARY: Moses said, This is what the LORD commandeth: Fill an omer of it to be kept for your generations, that they may see the bread wherewith I fed you in the wilderness, when I brought you out of Egypt. Henry observes eaten bread must not be forgotten: God's miracles and mercies are to be had in remembrance. — Matthew Henry

33 COMMENTARY: Moses told Aaron to take a pot and put an omer full of manna therein, and lay it up before the LORD to be kept for their generations. Henry notes the word of God is the manna by which souls are nourished, and the hidden manna is the comfort of the Spirit. — Matthew Henry

34 COMMENTARY: As the LORD commanded Moses, so Aaron laid it up before the Testimony, to be kept. Henry observes the memorial was placed before the Testimony itself, that the bread of the wilderness might stand beside the law of the covenant. — Matthew Henry

35 COMMENTARY: Israel did eat manna forty years, until they came to a land inhabited; they did eat manna until they came unto the borders of Canaan. Henry notes it continued wherever they went for forty years, and ceased when they arrived in Canaan, a provision exactly fitted to the pilgrimage. — Matthew Henry

36 COMMENTARY: Now an omer is the tenth part of an ephah. Henry observes the measure is defined for the reader, that the daily portion might be known: enough for each, and none had too much. — Matthew Henry
