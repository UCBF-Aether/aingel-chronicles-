Study Notes — Exodus

Chapter 11

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: The LORD told Moses he would bring yet one plague more upon Pharaoh and Egypt, after which Pharaoh would let them go, and thrust them out altogether. Henry notes the last plague would finish what nine had begun: not a reluctant permission but an expulsion. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: Moses was to speak in the people's ears, that every man borrow of his neighbour, and every woman of her neighbour, jewels of silver and jewels of gold. Henry observes Israel was to ask openly, not plunder secretly, and the asking itself was part of the judgment on Egypt. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: The LORD gave the people favour in the sight of the Egyptians, and Moses was very great in the land of Egypt, in the sight of Pharaoh's servants and of the people. Henry notes the reversal: the despised shepherd stood great in the land that had enslaved him, and even Egypt's court honoured him. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: Moses said, Thus saith the LORD, About midnight will I go out into the midst of Egypt. Henry observes the time was named: midnight, when Egypt slept in security, would be the hour of its waking to death. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: All the firstborn in Egypt would die, from Pharaoh's firstborn upon his throne to the firstborn of the maidservant behind the mill, and all the firstborn of beasts. Henry notes the stroke levelled every rank: throne and mill shared one sentence. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: There would be a great cry throughout all Egypt, such as there was none like it before, nor shall be like it again. Henry observes the cry would be universal because the loss was universal: no house without its firstborn dead. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: But against any of Israel not a dog would move its tongue, against man or beast, that Egypt might know the LORD put a difference between Egyptians and Israel. Henry notes the contrast was total: Egypt wailed while Israel slept in silence, and even the dogs kept still. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: All Pharaoh's servants would come down and bow down to Moses, saying, Get thee out, and all the people that follow thee, and after that Moses would go out, and he went out from Pharaoh in great anger. Henry observes the proudest court in the world would beg the deliverer to leave, and Moses's anger was righteous grief at a king who would only learn by death. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: The LORD told Moses Pharaoh would not hearken, that his wonders might be multiplied in Egypt. Henry notes the final refusal was foreknown, yet the warning was still given, for God's justice is never without witness. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: Moses and Aaron did all these wonders before Pharaoh, and the LORD hardened Pharaoh's heart, so that he would not let Israel go. Henry closes the nine plagues on this summary: all wonders done, all refused, and the tenth now stood at the door. — Matthew Henry
