Study Notes — Exodus

Chapter 14

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: The LORD spake to Moses, directing the march after the departure. Henry notes the direction itself was part of the design: God ordered the route that would become the trap for Pharaoh. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: Israel was to turn and encamp before Pihahiroth, between Migdol and the sea, over against Baalzephon, encamping by the sea. Henry observes the place seemed a dead end, sea before them and wilderness behind, chosen precisely that Pharaoh might think them entangled. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: Pharaoh would say of Israel, They are entangled in the land, the wilderness hath shut them in. Henry notes what seems to tend to the church's ruin is often overruled to the ruin of the church's enemies. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: God would harden Pharaoh's heart to follow after them, and be honoured upon Pharaoh, his chariots, and his horsemen, that the Egyptians might know he is the LORD, and Israel did so. Henry observes all men are made for their Maker's honour: those whom he is not honoured by, he will be honoured upon. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: It was told the king of Egypt that the people fled, and the heart of Pharaoh and his servants was turned, asking why they had let Israel go from serving them. Henry notes that though with the greatest reason he had let them go, Pharaoh was now angry with himself for it, for envy and rage torment those who harbour them. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: Pharaoh made ready his chariot and took his people with him. Henry observes the king who had begged Israel to leave now armed himself to fetch them back: he would not tamely part with any out of his service. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: He took six hundred chosen chariots, and all the chariots of Egypt, and captains over every one of them. Henry notes the whole military strength of Egypt was mustered against an unarmed people on foot. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: The LORD hardened Pharaoh's heart, and he pursued after Israel, while Israel went out with a high hand. Henry observes the two motions together: Pharaoh driven by his own rage, Israel marching out boldly under God's hand. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: The Egyptians pursued with all Pharaoh's horses, chariots, and horsemen, and his army, and overtook Israel encamping by the sea beside Pihahiroth before Baalzephon. Henry notes the trap was now sprung from Pharaoh's side, exactly as God had said. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: When Pharaoh drew nigh, Israel lifted up their eyes and saw the Egyptians marching after them, and they were sore afraid and cried unto the LORD. Henry observes some cried out to the Lord, and their fear led them to pray, which was well: God brings us into straits that he may bring us to our knees. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: They said to Moses, Because there were no graves in Egypt, hast thou taken us away to die in the wilderness? Henry notes the bitter absurdity of unbelief: they quarrelled with Moses for bringing them out, and so were angry with God for the greatest kindness ever done them. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: Is not this the word we told thee in Egypt, saying, Let us alone, that we may serve the Egyptians? For it had been better to serve the Egyptians than to die in the wilderness. Henry observes fear rewrote their history: they now claimed they had never wanted deliverance at all. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: Moses said, Fear ye not, stand still, and see the salvation of the LORD, which he will show you today, for the Egyptians ye have seen today ye shall see again no more forever. Henry notes the counsel: think not to save yourselves by fighting or flying; wait God's orders and observe them. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: The LORD shall fight for you, and ye shall hold your peace. Henry observes it is always our duty and interest, when we cannot get out of troubles, yet to get above our fears, and let them quicken prayer without silencing faith and hope. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: The LORD said to Moses, Wherefore criest thou unto me? Speak unto Israel, that they go forward. Henry notes Moses's silent prayers of faith prevailed more with God than Israel's loud outcries of fear, and the command now was not to stand but to march. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: Moses was to lift up his rod and stretch out his hand over the sea and divide it, and Israel would go on dry ground through the midst of the sea. Henry observes God can force a way where he does not find one, and open a passage through waters some miles over. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: God would harden the Egyptians' hearts, and they would follow Israel, and he would get honour upon Pharaoh, his chariots, and his horsemen. Henry notes the ruin of sinners is brought on by their own rage and presumption: they might have let Israel alone, and would not. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: The Egyptians would know that God is the LORD when he had gotten honour upon Pharaoh, his chariots, and his horsemen. Henry observes the sea would teach Egypt what the plagues had not: the name Pharaoh had scorned, written in the drowning of his army. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: The angel of God, which went before Israel's camp, removed and went behind them, and the pillar of cloud moved from before their face to behind them. Henry notes the guard came where it was needed: the guide became the rearguard. — Matthew Henry

20 COMMENTARY: The pillar came between the camp of the Egyptians and the camp of Israel, and was a cloud and darkness to the Egyptians, but gave light by night to Israel, so that the one came not near the other all night. Henry observes the word and providence of God have a dark side toward sinners and a bright side toward his people, as he who divided light from darkness allotted darkness to Egypt and light to Israel. — Matthew Henry

21 COMMENTARY: Moses stretched out his hand over the sea, and the LORD caused the sea to go back by a strong east wind all that night, and made the sea dry land, and the waters were divided. Henry notes the means were natural, a wind all night, yet the result was wholly miraculous: a sea parted. — Matthew Henry

22 COMMENTARY: Israel went into the midst of the sea upon dry ground, and the waters were a wall unto them on their right hand and on their left. Henry observes they walked upon dry land in the midst of the sea, an encouragement to God's people in all ages to trust him in the greatest straits. — Matthew Henry

23 COMMENTARY: The Egyptians pursued and went in after them to the midst of the sea, even all Pharaoh's horses, his chariots, and his horsemen. Henry notes they followed where they had no right to follow, presuming upon a path opened for others. — Matthew Henry

24 COMMENTARY: In the morning watch the LORD looked unto the host of the Egyptians through the pillar of fire and of the cloud, and troubled the host. Henry observes the look of God was itself a weapon: the same pillar that lit Israel now troubled Egypt. — Matthew Henry

25 COMMENTARY: He took off their chariot wheels, so that they drave heavily, and the Egyptians said, Let us flee from the face of Israel, for the LORD fighteth for them against the Egyptians. Henry notes the confession came too late: they would flee from Israel's face, and could not, for the sea held them. — Matthew Henry

26 COMMENTARY: The LORD told Moses to stretch out his hand over the sea, that the waters might come again upon the Egyptians, their chariots, and their horsemen. Henry observes the same hand that divided the sea was commanded to close it. — Matthew Henry

27 COMMENTARY: Moses stretched forth his hand, and the sea returned to his strength when the morning appeared, and the Egyptians fled against it, and the LORD overthrew the Egyptians in the midst of the sea. Henry notes the timing: at dawn, when flight seemed possible, the waters returned. — Matthew Henry

28 COMMENTARY: The waters returned and covered the chariots and the horsemen and all the host of Pharaoh that came into the sea after them, so that there remained not so much as one of them. Henry observes Pharaoh and his servants, who had hardened one another in sin, now fell together, not one escaping. — Matthew Henry

29 COMMENTARY: But Israel walked upon dry land in the midst of the sea, and the waters were a wall unto them on their right hand and on their left. Henry notes the contrast is the point: the same waters were a wall of safety to one people and a grave to the other. — Matthew Henry

30 COMMENTARY: Thus the LORD saved Israel that day out of the hand of the Egyptians, and Israel saw the Egyptians dead upon the sea shore. Henry observes the sight very much affected them: while men see God's works and feel the benefit, they fear him and trust in him. — Matthew Henry

31 COMMENTARY: Israel saw that great work which the LORD did upon the Egyptians, and the people feared the LORD and believed the LORD and his servant Moses. Henry closes on the frame Israel was in: how well were it for us if we were always in as good a frame as sometimes, holding fast by God while our enemies rage. — Matthew Henry
