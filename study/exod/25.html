Study Notes — Exodus

Chapter 25

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: The LORD spake unto Moses, giving the pattern of the sanctuary. Henry notes God chose Israel to be a peculiar people, and he himself would be their King, ordering a royal palace to be set up among them for himself. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: Moses was to speak unto Israel, that they bring an offering, of every man that giveth it willingly with his heart. Henry observes the people were to furnish the materials by their own free will, and the best use of worldly wealth is to honour God with it in works of piety and charity, cheerfully, not grudgingly. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: This is the offering to be taken: gold, and silver, and brass. Henry notes the metals themselves preached the palace's dignity: the King's house was to be furnished with the best the people had. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: And blue, and purple, and scarlet, and fine linen, and goats' hair. Henry observes the colours and cloths were the stuff of a royal tent: blue, purple, and scarlet for glory, fine linen for purity, and goats' hair for the outer covering. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: And rams' skins dyed red, and badgers' skins, and shittim wood. Henry notes the skins and wood were the wilderness materials of the palace: durable coverings and a hard wood, fitted to a tabernacle that must move with the people. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: Oil for the light, spices for anointing oil, and for sweet incense. Henry observes the oil and spices served the light and the fragrance of the house: the sanctuary was to shine and to smell sweet before God. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: Onyx stones, and stones to be set in the ephod and in the breastplate. Henry notes the precious stones were for the priest's garments, that the mediator might bear the tribes upon his heart and shoulders before God. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: And let them make me a sanctuary, that I may dwell among them. Henry observes the purpose of all the materials: because Israel dwelt in tents in the wilderness, God's royal palace was ordered to be a tabernacle, that it might move with them and show his presence among them. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: According to all that I show thee, after the pattern of the tabernacle and the pattern of all the instruments thereof, even so shall ye make it. Henry notes nothing was left to Moses's fancy, or to the workmen's, or the people's: the will of God must be observed in every particular, and whatsoever is done in God's service must be done by his direction. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: They were to make an ark of shittim wood, two cubits and a half long, a cubit and a half broad, and a cubit and a half high. Henry notes the ark was a chest overlaid with gold, in which the two tables of the law were to be kept, the testimony by which God testified his will. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: Thou shalt overlay it with pure gold, within and without, and shalt make upon it a crown of gold round about. Henry observes the gold within and without figured a sinless nature in personal union with the Divine, for the ark was a type of Christ, who saw no corruption. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: Thou shalt cast four rings of gold for it, and put them in the four corners thereof, two rings on one side and two on the other. Henry notes the rings were the ark's provision for journeying: the throne of God among his people was to travel with them. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: Thou shalt make staves of shittim wood, and overlay them with gold. Henry observes the staves were wood overlaid with gold like the ark itself, common material made glorious by its service. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: Thou shalt put the staves into the rings by the sides of the ark, that the ark may be borne with them. Henry notes the ark was to be borne, not dragged or carried by hand: God's presence moved by appointed means, not by casual handling. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: The staves shall be in the rings of the ark, and they shall not be taken from it. Henry observes the perpetual staves taught readiness: the ark was always prepared to move, for Israel was still on pilgrimage. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: Thou shalt put into the ark the testimony which I shall give thee. Henry notes the tables were called the testimony because God in them testified his will, to direct Israel in duty, and to testify against them if they transgressed. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: Thou shalt make a mercy seat of pure gold, two cubits and a half long and a cubit and a half broad. Henry notes it was covered with a covering of gold called the mercy-seat, where God is said to dwell, or sit between the cherubim, as a prince on his throne. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: Thou shalt make two cherubims of gold, of beaten work shalt thou make them, in the two ends of the mercy seat. Henry observes the cherubim denoted the angels' attendance on the Redeemer, their readiness to do his will, and their desire to look into the mysteries of the gospel. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: Make one cherub on the one end, and the other cherub on the other end, even of the mercy seat shall ye make the cherubims on the two ends thereof. Henry notes the cherubim were of one piece with the mercy seat, not added ornaments, for the angels' ministry belongs to the throne of mercy itself. — Matthew Henry

20 COMMENTARY: The cherubims shall stretch forth their wings on high, covering the mercy seat with their wings, and their faces shall look one to another, and toward the mercy seat. Henry observes the cherubim looked one toward another, and both looked downward toward the ark, attending on the Redeemer and present in the assemblies of saints. — Matthew Henry

21 COMMENTARY: Thou shalt put the mercy seat above upon the ark, and in the ark thou shalt put the testimony that I shall give thee. Henry notes the order: the law within, and mercy above it, for the blood of sacrifices was sprinkled and the incense burned before it, and above it appeared the visible glory, the symbol of the Divine presence. — Matthew Henry

22 COMMENTARY: And there I will meet with thee, and I will commune with thee from above the mercy seat, from between the two cherubims which are upon the ark of the testimony, of all things which I will give thee in commandment unto Israel. Henry observes there God would give his law and hear supplicants, as a prince on his throne, meeting his mediator at the place of mercy. — Matthew Henry

23 COMMENTARY: Thou shalt also make a table of shittim wood, two cubits long, a cubit broad, and a cubit and a half high. Henry notes the table was to stand in the outer tabernacle, always furnished with the shew-bread, typifying the communion the Lord holds with his redeemed people in his ordinances. — Matthew Henry

24 COMMENTARY: Thou shalt overlay it with pure gold, and make thereto a crown of gold round about. Henry observes the gold overlay and crown marked the table as royal furniture: the provisions of God's house are a king's feast, not a servant's ration. — Matthew Henry

25 COMMENTARY: Thou shalt make unto it a border of a hand breadth round about, and thou shalt make a golden crown to the border thereof round about. Henry notes the border kept the vessels in place, a small detail of order in the house where God dwelt. — Matthew Henry

26 COMMENTARY: Thou shalt make for it four rings of gold, and put the rings in the four corners that are on the four feet thereof. Henry observes the table, like the ark, was made to travel, with rings for bearing, for the feast must move with the pilgrims. — Matthew Henry

27 COMMENTARY: Over against the border shall the rings be for places of the staves to bear the table. Henry notes the rings stood by the border, fitted to the table's frame, that it might be borne steadily and without tipping its holy bread. — Matthew Henry

28 COMMENTARY: Thou shalt make the staves of shittim wood, and overlay them with gold, that the table may be borne with them. Henry observes the same wood and gold as the ark: the table of communion was borne with the same honour as the throne of mercy. — Matthew Henry

29 COMMENTARY: Thou shalt make the dishes thereof, and spoons thereof, and covers thereof, and bowls thereof, to cover withal, of pure gold shalt thou make them. Henry notes the vessels were all pure gold, for the food for souls which believers always find when they hunger after it is served in vessels worthy of the feast. — Matthew Henry

30 COMMENTARY: And thou shalt set upon the table shewbread before me alway. Henry observes the bread was always before God, a perpetual sign of the provisions of his house and the delight he takes in his people's persons and services, as presented before him in Christ. — Matthew Henry

31 COMMENTARY: Thou shalt make a candlestick of pure gold, of beaten work shall the candlestick be made, his shaft, and his branches, his bowls, his knops, and his flowers, shall be of the same. Henry notes the candlestick represents the light of God's word and Spirit, in and through Christ Jesus, afforded in this dark world to his believing people. — Matthew Henry

32 COMMENTARY: And six branches shall come out of the sides of it, three branches out of the one side, and three branches out of the other side. Henry observes the six branches spread the light: one lamp was not enough for the house, for the word must shine in every direction. — Matthew Henry

33 COMMENTARY: Three bowls made like unto almonds, with a knop and a flower in one branch, and three bowls made like unto almonds in the other branch, so in the six branches. Henry notes the almond blossoms figured life and fruitfulness: the light of God's house is a living light, budding like Aaron's rod. — Matthew Henry

34 COMMENTARY: And in the candlestick shall be four bowls made like unto almonds, with their knops and their flowers. Henry observes the central shaft bore four bowls, more than any branch, for the shaft carried the whole and its light was the chief light. — Matthew Henry

35 COMMENTARY: And there shall be a knop under two branches of the same, and a knop under two branches of the same, and a knop under the two branches of the same, according to the six branches. Henry notes the knops joined each pair of branches to the shaft, for all the lights were one candlestick, not six separate lamps. — Matthew Henry

36 COMMENTARY: Their knops and their branches shall be of the same, all it shall be one beaten work of pure gold. Henry observes the candlestick was one beaten work, hammered from a single talent of gold, for the light of God's word is one, though it shines through many branches. — Matthew Henry

37 COMMENTARY: Thou shalt make the seven lamps thereof, and they shall light the lamps thereof, that they may give light over against the candlestick. Henry notes the seven lamps gave light toward the table, for the word must shine upon the bread, that worship and communion might be seen together. — Matthew Henry

38 COMMENTARY: And the tongs thereof, and the snuffdishes thereof, shall be of pure gold. Henry observes even the tending tools were pure gold, for nothing that touched the light of God's house was to be common. — Matthew Henry

39 COMMENTARY: Of a talent of pure gold shall he make it, with all these vessels. Henry notes the whole candlestick, with all its vessels, came from a single talent, a great weight of gold for a single light, for the light of the world is costly. — Matthew Henry

40 COMMENTARY: And look that thou make them after their pattern, which was showed thee in the mount. Henry closes with the express caution: nothing was left to fancy, and Christ's instruction to his disciples is like this, Observe all things whatsoever I have commanded you. — Matthew Henry
