Study Notes — Exodus

Chapter 20

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: And God spake all these words, saying. Henry observes God speaks many ways to men, by conscience, by providences, by his voice, but he never spake at any time as he spake the Ten Commandments, for this law had been written in man's heart and sin had defaced it, so that its knowledge must be revived. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: I am the LORD thy God, which have brought thee out of the land of Egypt, out of the house of bondage. Henry notes the preface grounds the commands in deliverance: the God who redeemed claims obedience, and his law is spiritual, taking knowledge of secret thoughts and desires. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: Thou shalt have no other gods before me. Henry notes the first commandment concerns the object of worship, Jehovah and him only, and whatever comes short of perfect love, gratitude, reverence, or worship breaks it. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: Thou shalt not make unto thee any graven image, or any likeness of anything in heaven above, or in the earth beneath, or in the water under the earth. Henry notes it is forbidden to make any image or picture of the Deity in any form or for any purpose, or to worship any creature, image, or picture. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: Thou shalt not bow down thyself to them, nor serve them, for I the LORD thy God am a jealous God, visiting the iniquity of the fathers upon the children unto the third and fourth generation of them that hate me. Henry observes the spiritual import extends further: all superstition and all mere human inventions in worship are forbidden, and the jealousy of God guards his sole honour. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: And showing mercy unto thousands of them that love me, and keep my commandments. Henry notes the mercy outruns the judgment: iniquity is visited to the third and fourth generation, but mercy runs to thousands who love God and keep his commands. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: Thou shalt not take the name of the LORD thy God in vain, for the LORD will not hold him guiltless that taketh his name in vain. Henry notes the third commandment concerns the manner of worship, with all possible reverence: all false oaths, all light appealing to God, and all profane cursing are a horrid breach of it. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: Remember the sabbath day, to keep it holy. Henry notes the form, Remember, shows the command was not now first given but was known before, and one day in seven is to be kept holy. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: Six days shalt thou labour, and do all thy work. Henry observes six days are allotted to worldly business, but not so as to neglect the service of God and the care of our souls, and on those days we must do all our work and leave none for the sabbath. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: But the seventh day is the sabbath of the LORD thy God, in which thou shalt not do any work, thou, nor thy son, nor thy daughter, thy manservant, nor thy maidservant, nor thy cattle, nor thy stranger that is within thy gates. Henry notes Christ allowed works of necessity, charity, and piety, for the sabbath was made for man, but all works of luxury, vanity, or self-indulgence are forbidden, and sloth is a carnal, not a holy, rest. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: For in six days the LORD made heaven and earth, the sea, and all that in them is, and rested the seventh day, wherefore the LORD blessed the sabbath day and hallowed it. Henry observes the day is blessed and men are blessed by it and in it, and the blessing and direction to keep holy are spoken of the sabbath day itself. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: Honour thy father and thy mother, that thy days may be long upon the land which the LORD thy God giveth thee. Henry notes the fifth commandment concerns duties to relations: esteem shown in conduct, obedience to lawful commands, submission to counsels and corrections, and care to comfort parents and make their old age easy, and observers have noted a peculiar blessing in temporal things on obedient children. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: Thou shalt not kill. Henry notes the sixth commandment requires regarding the life and safety of others as our own, and much which is not deemed murder by the laws of man is such before God, including envy, malice, hatred, and provoking language, while it requires a spirit of kindness, longsuffering, and forgiveness. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: Thou shalt not commit adultery. Henry notes the seventh commandment concerns chastity, and we should be as much afraid of that which defiles the body as of that which destroys it, for whatever pollutes the imagination or raises the passions falls under this law. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: Thou shalt not steal. Henry notes the eighth commandment is the law of love as it respects property, requiring industry, frugality, and content, and forbidding fraud, evasion of just debts, and squeezing the poor in their wages. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: Thou shalt not bear false witness against thy neighbour. Henry notes the ninth commandment forbids speaking falsely on any matter, lying, equivocating, slandering, backbiting, and tale-bearing, and endeavouring to raise our reputation upon the ruin of our neighbour's. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: Thou shalt not covet thy neighbour's house, wife, manservant, maidservant, ox, ass, nor anything that is thy neighbour's. Henry notes the tenth commandment strikes at the root: the others forbid the desire of doing injury, and this forbids all wrong desire of having what will gratify ourselves. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: All the people saw the thunderings, and the lightnings, and the noise of the trumpet, and the mountain smoking, and when they saw it, they removed and stood afar off. Henry observes this law is so extensive we cannot measure it, so spiritual we cannot evade it, and so reasonable we cannot find fault with it, and it will be the rule of the future judgment. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: They said unto Moses, Speak thou with us, and we will hear, but let not God speak with us, lest we die. Henry notes the people's fear was well placed: with this holy law and an awful judgment before us, who can despise the gospel of Christ, and the knowledge of the law shows our need of repentance. — Matthew Henry

20 COMMENTARY: Moses said unto the people, Fear not, for God is come to prove you, and that his fear may be before your faces, that ye sin not. Henry observes the terror had a purpose: to prove them and to plant God's fear before them as a guard against sin. — Matthew Henry

21 COMMENTARY: And the people stood afar off, and Moses drew near unto the thick darkness where God was. Henry notes the contrast: the people stood afar, while the mediator drew near into the darkness, the pattern of all mediation between God and sinners. — Matthew Henry

22 COMMENTARY: The LORD said unto Moses, Thus thou shalt say unto the children of Israel, Ye have seen that I have talked with you from heaven. Henry notes Moses, having entered the thick darkness, heard there all that follows to the end of chapter 23, mostly an exposition of the Ten Commandments. — Matthew Henry

23 COMMENTARY: Ye shall not make with me gods of silver, neither shall ye make unto you gods of gold. Henry notes idolatry is forbidden again, in its costliest forms: silver and gold gods are no better than gods of wood and stone. — Matthew Henry

24 COMMENTARY: An altar of earth thou shalt make unto me, and shalt sacrifice thereon thy burnt offerings and thy peace offerings, thy sheep and thine oxen, and in all places where I record my name I will come unto thee and bless thee. Henry notes the Israelites are assured of God's gracious acceptance of their devotions, and under the gospel men are encouraged to pray everywhere, for wherever God's people meet in his name, he will be in the midst of them. — Matthew Henry

25 COMMENTARY: And if thou wilt make me an altar of stone, thou shalt not build it of hewn stone, for if thou lift up thy tool upon it, thou hast polluted it. Henry observes the altar must be unhewn: human art upon it would pollute it, for God's worship must not be dressed by man's invention. — Matthew Henry

26 COMMENTARY: Neither shalt thou go up by steps unto mine altar, that thy nakedness be not discovered thereon. Henry notes even the approach to the altar was guarded, that nothing unseemly might appear in worship. — Matthew Henry
