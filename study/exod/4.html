Study Notes — Exodus

Chapter 4

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Moses objected that Israel would not believe him or hearken to his voice, saying the LORD had not appeared to him. Henry notes this distrust followed hard upon God's promise, showing how weak faith clings to difficulties even after clear assurances. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: The LORD asked what was in Moses's hand, and he answered, A rod, the shepherd's staff of his ordinary calling. Henry observes God commonly works through the mean things in our hands rather than through what we do not have. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: Cast on the ground, the rod became a serpent, and Moses fled from before it. Henry reads the sign as proof that God could turn Moses's own staff into an instrument of power, and that even the chosen messenger feared his first wonder. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: Bidden to take the serpent by the tail, Moses caught it and it became a rod again in his hand. Henry notes obedience in the face of fear was the condition of the wonder, and the restored rod showed God's power to govern what he had made fearful. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: The sign was given that Israel might believe the LORD God of their fathers had appeared unto Moses. Henry stresses the signs were not for display but to confirm the messenger's commission to a doubting people. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: Put into his bosom, Moses's hand came out leprous as snow. Henry reads the leprosy as an emblem of sin's defilement and of the power that would both smite Egypt and heal Israel. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: Put into his bosom again, the hand came out restored as his other flesh. Henry notes the second half of the sign: the same hand that smites can heal, and God's judgments carry mercy within them. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: If Israel would not believe the first sign, they would believe the voice of the latter sign. Henry observes God gives line upon line to unbelief, multiplying evidence rather than abandoning his people to their doubts. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: If neither sign persuaded them, Moses was to take Nile water and pour it on dry land, where it would become blood. Henry notes the third sign struck at Egypt's river-god in advance, a warning of the plagues to come. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: Moses pleaded he was not eloquent, but slow of speech and of a slow tongue, neither before nor since God spoke to him. Henry calls this a modest but unbelieving excuse: God had not promised eloquence, but his presence. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: The LORD answered, Who hath made man's mouth, or maketh the dumb, the deaf, the seeing, or the blind? Henry notes the Creator of speech can supply speech, and infirmity is no bar to a divine commission. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: Go, said God, and I will be with thy mouth and teach thee what thou shalt say. Henry observes this promise answers every excuse at once: God's teaching, not man's readiness, makes the messenger sufficient. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: Moses still prayed, Send by the hand of him whom thou wilt send, in effect declining the work. Henry marks this as the excuse that crossed from humility into unbelief, and it kindled the LORD's anger. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: The LORD's anger was kindled, yet he gave Aaron the Levite as spokesman, who was coming to meet Moses and would be glad in his heart. Henry notes mercy still mingled with rebuke: Moses lost some honour, but gained a helper. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: Moses was to speak to Aaron and put words in his mouth, while God would be with both their mouths and teach them what to do. Henry observes the partnership: Moses received the word, Aaron uttered it, and God owned them both. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: Aaron would be Moses's spokesman to the people, instead of a mouth, while Moses would be to him instead of God. Henry notes the order preserved: the prophet hears from God, and the people hear from the prophet. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: Moses was to take the rod in his hand, wherewith he would do signs. Henry observes the shepherd's staff, once a sign of his lowly calling, was now consecrated as the instrument of wonders. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: Moses returned to Jethro and asked leave to go back to his brethren in Egypt to see whether they were yet alive. Henry notes he sought his father-in-law's consent with courtesy, and concealed nothing by fraud, though he did not yet declare the whole commission. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: The LORD told Moses in Midian to return to Egypt, for all the men who sought his life were dead. Henry observes providence had cleared the way: the old king and his court were gone before the deliverer set out. — Matthew Henry

20 COMMENTARY: Moses took his wife and sons upon an ass and returned to Egypt, carrying the rod of God in his hand. Henry notes he went in the same humble manner as he had fled, but now bearing the token of divine authority. — Matthew Henry

21 COMMENTARY: God warned Moses to do all the wonders before Pharaoh, yet said he would harden Pharaoh's heart so he would not let the people go. Henry explains the hardening as God leaving Pharaoh to the hardness he had chosen, and overruling it for greater glory. — Matthew Henry

22 COMMENTARY: Moses was to tell Pharaoh, Israel is my son, even my firstborn. Henry notes the claim grounded the demand: Israel was God's adopted firstborn, dear to him above other nations. — Matthew Henry

23 COMMENTARY: Let my son go that he may serve me, said the LORD, and if Pharaoh refused, God would slay his firstborn son. Henry observes the warning matched the crime: Pharaoh who slew Israel's sons would lose his own. — Matthew Henry

24 COMMENTARY: By the way at the inn, the LORD met Moses and sought to kill him, because his son was uncircumcised. Henry reads this as a sharp rebuke: the lawgiver himself must keep the covenant sign before he could carry God's message. — Matthew Henry

25 COMMENTARY: Zipporah took a sharp stone, circumcised her son, and cast the foreskin at Moses's feet, saying, A bloody husband thou art to me. Henry notes her reluctant act removed the offence, and the danger passed. — Matthew Henry

26 COMMENTARY: Then the LORD let him go, and Zipporah repeated, A bloody husband thou art, because of the circumcision. Henry observes the sharp saying marked her distaste for the rite, though she had performed it to save her husband's life. — Matthew Henry

27 COMMENTARY: The LORD told Aaron to go into the wilderness to meet Moses, and he met him in the mount of God and kissed him. Henry notes the brothers' meeting was itself a providence, joining the prophet and the spokesman at the very place of the call. — Matthew Henry

28 COMMENTARY: Moses told Aaron all the words of the LORD and all the signs he had commanded. Henry observes the commission was shared in full, so Aaron spoke not his own words but the words given to Moses. — Matthew Henry

29 COMMENTARY: Moses and Aaron gathered all the elders of Israel together. Henry notes the orderly course: the elders first, as representatives, before the whole congregation was addressed. — Matthew Henry

30 COMMENTARY: Aaron spoke all the words the LORD had spoken to Moses and did the signs in the sight of the people. Henry observes the signs did their appointed work, confirming the word to those who heard it. — Matthew Henry

31 COMMENTARY: The people believed, and when they heard the LORD had visited Israel and looked upon their affliction, they bowed their heads and worshipped. Henry closes on the happy answer to Moses's fear: the people who he thought would not believe, believed and worshipped. — Matthew Henry
