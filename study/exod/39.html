Study Notes — Exodus

Chapter 39

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: And of the blue, and purple, and scarlet, they made cloths of service, to do service in the holy place, and made the holy garments for Aaron, as the LORD commanded Moses. Henry notes the holy garments not only distinguished the priests from the people, but were emblems of that holy conduct which should ever be the glory and beauty of ministers of religion. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: And he made the ephod of gold, blue, and purple, and scarlet, and fine twined linen. Henry notes this richly-wrought ephod was the outmost garment of the high priest, a short coat without sleeves, fastened close to the body with a girdle. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: And they did beat the gold into thin plates, and cut it into wires, to work it in the blue, and in the purple, and in the scarlet, and in the fine linen, with cunning work. Henry observes the gold was beaten into plates and cut into wires, that it might be woven into the colours, for the ephod's gold was thread, not plate. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: They made shoulderpieces for it, to couple it together, and by the two edges was it coupled together. Henry notes the shoulderpieces were buttoned together, joining front and back into one garment. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: And the curious girdle of his ephod, that was upon it, was of the same, according to the work thereof, of gold, blue, and purple, and scarlet, and fine twined linen, as the LORD commanded Moses. Henry observes the girdle was of the same work as the ephod, binding it close for service. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: And they wrought onyx stones inclosed in ouches of gold, graven, as signets are graven, with the names of the children of Israel. Henry notes the shoulder stones bore the tribes' names engraven like a signet, deep and durable, that Christ our High Priest might present his people before the Lord for a memorial. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: And he put them on the shoulders of the ephod, that they should be stones for a memorial to the children of Israel, as the LORD commanded Moses. Henry observes the stones on the shoulders reminded us of the power with which Christ pleads for his own, bearing them up with almighty strength. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: And he made the breastplate of cunning work, like the work of the ephod, of gold, blue, and purple, and scarlet, and fine twined linen. Henry notes the breastplate was the chief ornament of the high priest, a rich piece of cloth curiously worked, called the breastplate of judgment. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: It was foursquare, and they made the breastplate double, a span was the length thereof, and a span was the breadth thereof, being doubled. Henry observes the breastplate was doubled and foursquare, a span each way, folded to hold the stones and the oracle. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: And they set in it four rows of stones: the first row was a sardius, a topaz, and a carbuncle. Henry notes each tribe's name was graven in a precious stone, to signify how precious in God's sight believers are, and how honourable, however small and poor the tribe. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: And the second row, an emerald, a sapphire, and a diamond. Henry observes the second row continued the pattern: every tribe, great or small, stood as a precious stone in the breastplate. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: And the third row, a ligure, an agate, and an amethyst. Henry notes the third row added three more stones, that the twelve might be complete. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: And the fourth row, a beryl, an onyx, and a jasper, and they were inclosed in ouches of gold in their inclosings. Henry observes the fourth row completed the four rows of three, each stone set in gold. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: And the stones were according to the names of the children of Israel, twelve, according to their names, like the engravings of a signet, every one with his name, according to the twelve tribes. Henry notes the signet engraving made each stone personal: every tribe by name, graven deep. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: And they made upon the breastplate chains at the ends, of wreathen work of pure gold. Henry observes the wreathen chains of pure gold linked the breastplate to the ephod, that judgment and memorial might hang together. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: And they made two ouches of gold, and two gold rings, and put the two rings in the two ends of the breastplate. Henry notes the rings at the ends received the chains, small fittings holding the oracle close to the priest's heart. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: And they put the two wreathen chains of gold in the two rings on the ends of the breastplate. Henry observes the chains were put through the rings, fastening the breastplate to the shoulders. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: And the two ends of the two wreathen chains they fastened in the two ouches, and put them on the shoulderpieces of the ephod, on the foreside thereof. Henry notes the chains ran from breastplate to shoulder ouches, joining heart and shoulders: love and power together. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: And they made two rings of gold, and put them on the two ends of the breastplate, upon the border thereof, which was on the side of the ephod inward. Henry observes the lower rings, on the inward border, received the lace that bound the breastplate below. — Matthew Henry

20 COMMENTARY: And they made two other golden rings, and put them on the two sides of the ephod underneath, toward the forepart thereof, over against the other coupling thereof, above the curious girdle of the ephod. Henry notes the matching rings on the ephod, above the girdle, answered the breastplate's rings. — Matthew Henry

21 COMMENTARY: And they did bind the breastplate by his rings unto the rings of the ephod with a lace of blue, that it might be above the curious girdle of the ephod, and that the breastplate might not be loosed from the ephod, as the LORD commanded Moses. Henry observes the blue lace bound breastplate to ephod above the girdle, that the breastplate might not be loosed. — Matthew Henry

22 COMMENTARY: And he made the robe of the ephod of woven work, all of blue. Henry notes the robe of the ephod was under the ephod and reached down to the knees, without sleeves, all of blue, the colour of heaven. — Matthew Henry

23 COMMENTARY: And there was a hole in the midst of the robe, as the hole of an habergeon, with a band round about the hole, that it should not rend. Henry observes the reinforced hole kept the robe from tearing, for the priest's garment must not be rent in ministry. — Matthew Henry

24 COMMENTARY: And they made upon the hems of the robe pomegranates of blue, and purple, and scarlet, and twined linen. Henry notes the pomegranates on the hem represented the fruit the saints bring forth. — Matthew Henry

25 COMMENTARY: And they made bells of pure gold, and put the bells between the pomegranates upon the hem of the robe, round about between the pomegranates. Henry notes the golden bells, by their preciousness and pleasant sound, well represent the good profession that the saints make. — Matthew Henry

26 COMMENTARY: A bell and a pomegranate, a bell and a pomegranate, round about the hem of the robe to minister in, as the LORD commanded Moses. Henry observes the alternation was exact: bell and fruit, bell and fruit, sound and fruit together. — Matthew Henry

27 COMMENTARY: And they made coats of fine linen of woven work for Aaron, and for his sons. Henry notes the linen coats were the undergarments of white, the priest's purity beneath all the colour and gold. — Matthew Henry

28 COMMENTARY: And a mitre of fine linen, and goodly bonnets of fine linen, and linen breeches of fine twined linen. Henry observes the mitre, bonnets, and breeches were all fine linen, for the priests' covering was white from head to foot beneath the coloured robes. — Matthew Henry

29 COMMENTARY: And a girdle of fine twined linen, and blue, and purple, and scarlet, of needlework, as the LORD commanded Moses. Henry notes the girdle of needlework bound the linen coats, coloured like the ephod, that the undergarments might share the house's glory. — Matthew Henry

30 COMMENTARY: And they made the plate of the holy crown of pure gold, and wrote upon it a writing, like to the engravings of a signet, Holiness to the LORD. Henry notes a golden plate was fixed on Aaron's forehead, engraven with Holiness to the Lord, reminding him that God is holy and his priests must be holy, devoted to the Lord, and the engraving was deep and durable, not painted to be washed off. — Matthew Henry

31 COMMENTARY: And they tied unto it a lace of blue, to fasten it on high upon the mitre, as the LORD commanded Moses. Henry observes the plate stood on the forefront of the mitre, tied with blue lace, in open profession of relation to God. — Matthew Henry

32 COMMENTARY: Thus was all the work of the tabernacle of the tent of the congregation finished, and the children of Israel did according to all that the LORD commanded Moses, so did they. Henry notes the finishing itself was the testimony: all the work was done according to all that the LORD commanded, nothing added and nothing left out. — Matthew Henry

33 COMMENTARY: And they brought the tabernacle unto Moses, the tent, and all his furniture, his taches, his boards, his bars, his pillars, and his sockets. Henry observes the whole house was brought to Moses for inspection, tent and furniture, clasps and boards, bars and pillars and sockets, that the mediator might see it all. — Matthew Henry

34 COMMENTARY: And the covering of rams' skins dyed red, and the covering of badgers' skins, and the vail of the covering. Henry notes the coverings and the vail were brought with the house, the outer skins and the inner partition together. — Matthew Henry

35 COMMENTARY: The ark of the testimony, and the staves thereof, and the mercy seat. Henry observes the ark with its staves and mercy seat was brought, the throne of the house, that Moses might see the heart of the work. — Matthew Henry

36 COMMENTARY: The table, and all the vessels thereof, and the shewbread. Henry notes the table with its vessels and shewbread was brought, for the bread of the house must be furnished always. — Matthew Henry

37 COMMENTARY: The pure candlestick, with the lamps thereof, even with the lamps to be set in order, and all the vessels thereof, and the oil for light. Henry observes the pure candlestick with its lamps in order, vessels, and oil was brought, for the light of the house must be tended. — Matthew Henry

38 COMMENTARY: And the golden altar, and the anointing oil, and the sweet incense, and the hanging for the tabernacle door. Henry notes the golden altar with oil, incense, and door hanging was brought, for prayer and anointing belonged to the holy place. — Matthew Henry

39 COMMENTARY: The brasen altar, and his grate of brass, his staves, and all his vessels, the laver and his foot. Henry observes the brasen altar with grate, staves, vessels, and laver was brought, the outer service complete. — Matthew Henry

40 COMMENTARY: The hangings of the court, his pillars, and his sockets, and the hanging for the court gate, his cords, and his pins, and all the vessels of the service of the tabernacle, for the tent of the congregation. Henry notes the court hangings, pillars, sockets, gate, cords, and pins were brought, the whole enclosure with the house. — Matthew Henry

41 COMMENTARY: The cloths of service to do service in the holy place, and the holy garments for Aaron the priest, and his sons' garments, to minister in the priest's office. Henry notes the cloths of service and the priestly garments were brought last, for the ministers' clothing completed the house's furnishing. — Matthew Henry

42 COMMENTARY: According to all that the LORD commanded Moses, so the children of Israel made all the work. Henry observes the refrain sealed the delivery: according to all the LORD commanded, so they made all the work, a second witness to exact obedience. — Matthew Henry

43 COMMENTARY: And Moses did look upon all the work, and, behold, they had done it as the LORD had commanded, even so had they done it, and Moses blessed them. Henry closes on the blessing: the mediator looked upon all the work, found it done as commanded, and blessed the workers, for exact obedience to God's pattern is itself blessed. — Matthew Henry
