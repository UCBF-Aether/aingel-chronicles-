Study Notes — Exodus

Chapter 29

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: And this is the thing that thou shalt do unto them to hallow them, to minister unto me in the priest's office: take one young bullock, and two rams without blemish. Henry notes the consecration began with unblemished victims, for the priests who would offer for sin must themselves be set apart by sacrifice. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: And unleavened bread, and cakes unleavened tempered with oil, and wafers unleavened anointed with oil, of wheaten flour shalt thou make them. Henry observes the unleavened bread of consecration matched the bread of the Exodus: no leaven of malice or hypocrisy in those set apart to God. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: And thou shalt put them into one basket, and bring them in the basket, with the bullock and the two rams. Henry notes the bread and the beasts were brought together, for the priests' consecration joined sacrifice and sustenance in one basket. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: And Aaron and his sons thou shalt bring unto the door of the tabernacle of the congregation, and shalt wash them with water. Henry observes the washing came first: the priests were washed before they were clothed, for cleansing precedes clothing in consecration. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: And thou shalt take the garments, and put upon Aaron the coat, and the robe of the ephod, and the ephod, and the breastplate, and gird him with the curious girdle of the ephod. Henry notes the clothing followed the washing: the priest was arrayed in the garments of glory and beauty before he was anointed. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: And thou shalt put the mitre upon his head, and put the holy crown upon the mitre. Henry observes the mitre and the holy crown completed the array, with Holiness to the LORD upon his forehead, that all might see to whom he belonged. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: Then shalt thou take the anointing oil, and pour it upon his head, and anoint him. Henry notes the anointing poured upon the head signified the Spirit's gifts for office: the priest was not merely dressed but anointed for his work. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: And thou shalt bring his sons, and put coats upon them. Henry observes the sons were clothed in coats, simpler than Aaron's, yet still holy garments, for every priest shared the consecration. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: And thou shalt gird them with girdles, Aaron and his sons, and put the bonnets on them, and the priest's office shall be theirs for a perpetual statute, and thou shalt consecrate Aaron and his sons. Henry notes the girdles and bonnets completed the sons' array, and the office was perpetual: the priesthood would pass from father to son by statute. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: And thou shalt cause a bullock to be brought before the tabernacle of the congregation, and Aaron and his sons shall put their hands upon the head of the bullock. Henry observes the laying on of hands transferred the priests' sins to the victim, for even consecrated priests needed atonement. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: And thou shalt kill the bullock before the LORD, by the door of the tabernacle of the congregation. Henry notes the killing was before the LORD at the door, in the sight of all, for the priests' sin offering was public, not private. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: And thou shalt take of the blood of the bullock, and put it upon the horns of the altar with thy finger, and pour all the blood beside the bottom of the altar. Henry observes the blood on the horns and at the base consecrated the altar itself: the place of sacrifice was sanctified by the sacrifice. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: And thou shalt take all the fat that covereth the inwards, and the caul that is above the liver, and the two kidneys, and the fat that is upon them, and burn them upon the altar. Henry notes the fat, the best part, was burned to God, for the inward parts belonged wholly to him. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: But the flesh of the bullock, and his skin, and his dung, shalt thou burn with fire without the camp, for it is a sin offering. Henry observes the sin offering was burned outside the camp, for sin was to be carried away from the holy place, a figure of Christ suffering outside the gate. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: Thou shalt also take one ram, and Aaron and his sons shall put their hands upon the head of the ram. Henry notes the first ram was the burnt offering of consecration, and the hands laid on it again confessed the priests' need of atonement. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: And thou shalt slay the ram, and thou shalt take his blood, and sprinkle it round about upon the altar. Henry observes the blood sprinkled round about the altar covered every side: the consecration was total, leaving no side unsprinkled. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: And thou shalt cut the ram in pieces, and wash the inwards of him, and his legs, and put them unto his pieces, and unto his head. Henry notes the washing of inwards and legs showed the inward and outward cleansing required: the sacrifice must be clean within and without. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: And thou shalt burn the whole ram upon the altar, for it is a burnt offering unto the LORD, a sweet savour, an offering made by fire unto the LORD. Henry observes the whole ram was burned, nothing kept back, for the burnt offering was whole surrender, a sweet savour to God. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: And thou shalt take the other ram, and Aaron and his sons shall put their hands upon the head of the ram. Henry notes the second ram was the ram of consecration itself, distinct from the burnt offering, for the priests' own portion was yet to come. — Matthew Henry

20 COMMENTARY: Then shalt thou kill the ram, and take of his blood, and put it upon the tip of the right ear of Aaron, and upon the tip of the right ear of his sons, and upon the thumb of their right hand, and upon the great toe of their right foot, and sprinkle the blood upon the altar round about. Henry observes the blood marked ear, hand, and foot: the priest must hear God's word, do God's work, and walk God's way, all under blood. — Matthew Henry

21 COMMENTARY: And thou shalt take of the blood that is upon the altar, and of the anointing oil, and sprinkle it upon Aaron, and upon his garments, and upon his sons, and upon their garments with him, and he shall be hallowed, and his garments, and his sons, and his sons' garments with him. Henry notes blood and oil together hallowed priest and garments: atonement and anointing joined, for no service is holy without both. — Matthew Henry

22 COMMENTARY: Also thou shalt take of the ram the fat and the rump, and the fat that covereth the inwards, and the caul above the liver, and the two kidneys, and the fat that is upon them, and the right shoulder, for it is a ram of consecration. Henry observes the choice parts, with the right shoulder, were taken for the wave offering, for the best belonged to God and the priest together. — Matthew Henry

23 COMMENTARY: And one loaf of bread, and one cake of oiled bread, and one wafer out of the basket of the unleavened bread that is before the LORD. Henry notes the three kinds of bread joined the ram: loaf, cake, and wafer, the priests' bread of consecration. — Matthew Henry

24 COMMENTARY: And thou shalt put all in the hands of Aaron, and in the hands of his sons, and shalt wave them for a wave offering before the LORD. Henry observes the wave offering was put into the priests' own hands and waved before God, for they offered what they had received. — Matthew Henry

25 COMMENTARY: And thou shalt receive them of their hands, and burn them upon the altar for a burnt offering, for a sweet savour before the LORD, for it is an offering made by fire unto the LORD. Henry notes the waved portions were then burned, a sweet savour, for the priests' consecration ended in fire, as all service must. — Matthew Henry

26 COMMENTARY: And thou shalt take the breast of the ram of Aaron's consecration, and wave it for a wave offering before the LORD, and it shall be thy part. Henry observes the breast was Moses's part as the consecrator, for the wave breast belonged to the one who waved it. — Matthew Henry

27 COMMENTARY: And thou shalt sanctify the breast of the wave offering, and the shoulder of the heave offering, which is waved, and which is heaved up, of the ram of the consecration, even of that which is for Aaron, and of that which is for his sons. Henry notes the breast and shoulder were sanctified as the priests' perpetual portion: the breast of love and the shoulder of strength, waved and heaved before God. — Matthew Henry

28 COMMENTARY: And it shall be Aaron's and his sons' by a statute forever from the children of Israel, for it is a heave offering, and it shall be a heave offering from the children of Israel of the sacrifice of their peace offerings, even their heave offering unto the LORD. Henry observes the statute was forever: the priests' portion was not a gift of the moment but a law for generations. — Matthew Henry

29 COMMENTARY: And the holy garments of Aaron shall be his sons' after him, to be anointed therein, and to be consecrated therein. Henry notes the garments passed down: the sons were anointed in their father's robes, for the office outlived the man. — Matthew Henry

30 COMMENTARY: And that son that is priest in his stead shall put them on seven days, when he cometh into the tabernacle of the congregation to minister in the holy place. Henry observes the successor wore the garments seven days, a full week of consecration before he ministered, for the new priest must be fully set apart. — Matthew Henry

31 COMMENTARY: And thou shalt take the ram of the consecration, and seethe his flesh in the holy place. Henry notes the ram's flesh was boiled in the holy place, for the priests' meal was itself holy, eaten where God dwelt. — Matthew Henry

32 COMMENTARY: And Aaron and his sons shall eat the flesh of the ram, and the bread that is in the basket by the door of the tabernacle of the congregation. Henry observes the priests ate at the door, between the camp and the holy place, for their consecration meal stood at the threshold of service. — Matthew Henry

33 COMMENTARY: And they shall eat those things wherewith the atonement was made, to consecrate and to sanctify them, but a stranger shall not eat thereof, because they are holy. Henry notes the meal was atonement eaten: the priests fed on the very things that had made atonement, and no stranger might share, for the food was holy. — Matthew Henry

34 COMMENTARY: And if ought of the flesh of the consecrations, or of the bread, remain unto the morning, then thou shalt burn the remainder with fire, and it shall not be eaten, because it is holy. Henry observes the remainder was burned, not kept, for holy food must not grow common by delay. — Matthew Henry

35 COMMENTARY: And thus shalt thou do unto Aaron, and to his sons, according to all things which I have commanded thee, and seven days shalt thou consecrate them. Henry notes the consecration lasted seven days, a complete week, for the priests were set apart fully, not hastily. — Matthew Henry

36 COMMENTARY: And thou shalt offer every day a bullock for a sin offering for atonement, and thou shalt cleanse the altar, when thou hast made an atonement for it, and thou shalt anoint it, to sanctify it. Henry observes the daily bullock atoned for the altar itself during the seven days, for even the place of sacrifice needed cleansing. — Matthew Henry

37 COMMENTARY: Seven days thou shalt make an atonement for the altar, and sanctify it, and it shall be an altar most holy, and whatsoever toucheth the altar shall be holy. Henry notes the altar, after seven days of atonement, became most holy, so that whatever touched it was holy, for holiness spread from the altar to all it touched. — Matthew Henry

38 COMMENTARY: Now this is that which thou shalt offer upon the altar: two lambs of the first year day by day continually. Henry observes the daily offering began here: two lambs a day, continually, the perpetual worship of the house. — Matthew Henry

39 COMMENTARY: The one lamb thou shalt offer in the morning, and the other lamb thou shalt offer at even. Henry notes the morning and evening lambs framed the day: worship opened and closed the day, that Israel might live between two sacrifices. — Matthew Henry

40 COMMENTARY: And with the one lamb a tenth deal of flour mingled with the fourth part of a hin of beaten oil, and the fourth part of a hin of wine for a drink offering. Henry observes the meat and drink offerings joined the lamb: flour and oil and wine, the fruits of the field, offered with the flock. — Matthew Henry

41 COMMENTARY: And the other lamb thou shalt offer at even, and shalt do thereto according to the meat offering of the morning, and according to the drink offering thereof, for a sweet savour, an offering made by fire unto the LORD. Henry notes the evening lamb matched the morning exactly, for the perpetual offering admitted no variation. — Matthew Henry

42 COMMENTARY: This shall be a continual burnt offering throughout your generations at the door of the tabernacle of the congregation before the LORD, where I will meet you, to speak there unto thee. Henry observes the continual offering stood at the door, where God would meet his people, for the place of sacrifice was the place of meeting. — Matthew Henry

43 COMMENTARY: And there I will meet with the children of Israel, and the tabernacle shall be sanctified by my glory. Henry notes the tabernacle was sanctified not by its gold but by God's glory dwelling in it, for his presence made it holy. — Matthew Henry

44 COMMENTARY: And I will sanctify the tabernacle of the congregation, and the altar, and I will sanctify also both Aaron and his sons, to minister unto me in the priest's office. Henry observes God himself sanctified house, altar, and priests together, for consecration is God's work, not man's. — Matthew Henry

45 COMMENTARY: And I will dwell among the children of Israel, and will be their God. Henry notes the promise that crowns the chapter: God would dwell among them and be their God, the end of all the tabernacle's gold and all the priests' array. — Matthew Henry

46 COMMENTARY: And they shall know that I am the LORD their God, that brought them forth out of the land of Egypt, that I may dwell among them, and I am the LORD their God. Henry closes on the reason: the Exodus itself was for this dwelling, that God might live among the people he had redeemed. — Matthew Henry
