Study Notes — Exodus

Chapter 34

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: And the LORD said unto Moses, Hew thee two tables of stone like unto the first, and I will write upon these tables the words that were in the first tables, which thou brakest. Henry notes when God was reconciled to Israel, he ordered the tables to be renewed and wrote his law in them, for even under the gospel of peace by Christ, the moral law continues to bind believers. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: And be ready in the morning, and come up in the morning unto mount Sinai, and present thyself there to me in the top of the mount. Henry observes the second ascent was ordered like the first: ready in the morning, alone on the top, for the renewed law came by the same solemn way. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: And no man shall come up with thee, neither let any man be seen throughout all the mount, neither let the flocks nor herds feed before that mount. Henry notes the solitude was total: no man on the mount, no flock before it, for the renewal of the covenant was between God and Moses alone. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: And he hewed two tables of stone like unto the first, and Moses rose up early in the morning, and went up unto mount Sinai, as the LORD had commanded him, and took in his hand the two tables of stone. Henry observes Moses hewed the tables himself this time, for man now bore a part in the renewal, though God alone would write. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: And the LORD descended in the cloud, and stood with him there, and proclaimed the name of the LORD. Henry notes the Lord descended by some open token of his presence and manifestation of his glory in a cloud, and thence proclaimed his name, that is, the perfections and character denoted by the name Jehovah. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: And the LORD passed by before him, and proclaimed, The LORD, The LORD God, merciful and gracious, longsuffering, and abundant in goodness and truth. Henry observes the proclamation unfolded the name: merciful, ready to forgive the sinner and relieve the needy; gracious, kind and ready to bestow undeserved benefits; longsuffering, slow to anger, giving time for repentance; abundant in goodness and truth, for all he reveals is infallible truth and all he promises is faithfulness. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: Keeping mercy for thousands, forgiving iniquity and transgression and sin, and that will by no means clear the guilty, visiting the iniquity of the fathers upon the children, and upon the children's children, unto the third and to the fourth generation. Henry notes he continually shows mercy to sinners and has treasures which cannot be exhausted, and his mercy reaches to full and free forgiveness, yet he will by no means clear the guilty, for the holiness and justice of God are part of his goodness and love, and in Christ's sufferings that holiness and justice are fully shown. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: And Moses made haste, and bowed his head toward the earth, and worshipped. Henry observes Moses bowed down and worshipped reverently, for every perfection in the name of God the believer may plead for forgiveness, for holiness of heart, and for the enlargement of the Redeemer's kingdom. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: And he said, If now I have found grace in thy sight, O LORD, let my LORD, I pray thee, go among us, for it is a stiffnecked people, and pardon our iniquity and our sin, and take us for thine inheritance. Henry notes the plea turned the people's stiff neck into an argument for presence, not against it: a stiffnecked people needed God among them most of all. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: And he said, Behold, I make a covenant, before all thy people I will do marvels, such as have not been done in all the earth, nor in any nation, and all the people among which thou art shall see the work of the LORD, for it is a terrible thing that I will do with thee. Henry notes the covenant was renewed with marvels promised before all the people, that the work of the LORD might be seen by all. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: Observe thou that which I command thee this day, and behold, I drive out before thee the Amorite, and the Canaanite, and the Hittite, and the Perizzite, and the Hivite, and the Jebusite. Henry observes the command began with attention: observe what I command, for the driving out of the nations depended on Israel's observing. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: Take heed to thyself, lest thou make a covenant with the inhabitants of the land whither thou goest, lest it be for a snare in the midst of thee. Henry notes the warning against alliance: a covenant with idolaters would become a snare in Israel's midst. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: But ye shall destroy their altars, break their images, and cut down their groves. Henry observes the Israelites were commanded to destroy every monument of idolatry, however curious or costly, for no monument of false worship might remain. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: For thou shalt worship no other god, for the LORD, whose name is Jealous, is a jealous God. Henry notes jealousy is called the rage of a man, but in God it is holy and just displeasure, and those cannot worship God aright who do not worship him only. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: Lest thou make a covenant with the inhabitants of the land, and they go a whoring after their gods, and do sacrifice unto their gods, and one call thee, and thou eat of his sacrifice. Henry observes the danger was social before it was theological: a shared feast would lead to shared worship. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: And thou take of their daughters unto thy sons, and their daughters go a whoring after their gods, and make thy sons go a whoring after their gods. Henry notes marriage was the deepest snare: daughters taken in marriage would draw sons after foreign gods. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: Thou shalt make thee no molten gods. Henry observes the prohibition came last and pointed: no molten gods, the very crime of the calf, must never be repeated. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: The feast of unleavened bread shalt thou keep. Seven days thou shalt eat unleavened bread, as I commanded thee, in the time of the month Abib, for in the month Abib thou camest out from Egypt. Henry notes the feasts were rehearsed with the covenant: the Passover first, in remembrance of the deliverance out of Egypt. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: All that openeth the matrix is mine, and every firstling among thy cattle, whether ox or sheep, that is male. Henry observes the firstborn law was renewed with the covenant: all that opened the womb belonged to God, man and beast alike. — Matthew Henry

20 COMMENTARY: But the firstling of an ass thou shalt redeem with a lamb, and if thou redeem him not, then shalt thou break his neck. All the firstborn of thy sons thou shalt redeem. And none shall appear before me empty. Henry notes the redemption of the unclean beast and of sons stood together, and none might appear before God empty-handed. — Matthew Henry

21 COMMENTARY: Six days thou shalt work, but on the seventh day thou shalt rest, and in earing time and in harvest thou shalt rest. Henry observes once a week they must rest, even in ploughing time and in harvest, and all worldly business must give way to that holy rest, for even harvest work will prosper the better for the religious observance of the sabbath in harvest time. — Matthew Henry

22 COMMENTARY: And thou shalt observe the feast of weeks, of the firstfruits of wheat harvest, and the feast of ingathering at the year's end. Henry notes three feasts are mentioned: the Passover, the feast of weeks or Pentecost with the law of firstfruits, and the feast of ingathering or Tabernacles. — Matthew Henry

23 COMMENTARY: Thrice in the year shall all your menchildren appear before the LORD God, the God of Israel. Henry observes thrice a year they must appear before the Lord God, the God of Israel, and the way of duty is the way of safety, for those who venture for him never lose by him. — Matthew Henry

24 COMMENTARY: For I will cast out the nations before thee, and enlarge thy borders, and neither shall any man desire thy land, when thou shalt go up to appear before the LORD thy God thrice in the year. Henry notes Canaan was a desirable land and the neighbours were greedy, yet God said they would not desire it while Israel went up to appear, for God checks sinful desires in others' hearts when we check them in our own. — Matthew Henry

25 COMMENTARY: Thou shalt not offer the blood of my sacrifice with leaven, neither shall the sacrifice of the feast of the passover be left unto the morning. Henry observes the sacrifice was guarded from leaven and from delay: blood with leaven was forbidden, and the passover sacrifice must not remain till morning. — Matthew Henry

26 COMMENTARY: The first of the firstfruits of thy land thou shalt bring unto the house of the LORD thy God. Thou shalt not seethe a kid in his mother's milk. Henry notes the firstfruits belonged to God's house, and the kid in its mother's milk was forbidden, a guard against cruelty and idolatry. — Matthew Henry

27 COMMENTARY: And the LORD said unto Moses, Write thou these words, for after the tenor of these words I have made a covenant with thee and with Israel. Henry observes Moses was to write these words, that the people might know them better, for we can never be enough thankful to God for the written word, and God made a covenant with Israel in Moses as mediator, as the covenant of grace is made with believers through Christ. — Matthew Henry

28 COMMENTARY: And he was there with the LORD forty days and forty nights, and he did neither eat bread, nor drink water. And he wrote upon the tables the words of the covenant, the ten commandments. Henry notes the forty days without bread or water marked the second communion, and the words written were the covenant itself, the ten commandments. — Matthew Henry

29 COMMENTARY: And it came to pass, when Moses came down from mount Sinai with the two tables of testimony in Moses's hand, when he came down from the mount, that Moses wist not that the skin of his face shone while he talked with him. Henry observes near and spiritual communion with God improves the graces of a renewed and holy character, and serious godliness puts a lustre upon a man's countenance. — Matthew Henry

30 COMMENTARY: And when Aaron and all the children of Israel saw Moses, behold, the skin of his face shone, and they were afraid to come nigh him. Henry notes the shine commanded esteem and affection, yet also fear, for glory, even reflected, is terrible to sinners. — Matthew Henry

31 COMMENTARY: And Moses called unto them, and Aaron and all the rulers of the congregation returned unto him, and Moses talked with them. Henry observes Moses called them back, and the rulers returned first, that the people might follow their leaders to the shining face. — Matthew Henry

32 COMMENTARY: And afterward all the children of Israel came nigh, and he gave them in commandment all that the LORD had spoken with him in mount Sinai. Henry notes the whole people then came nigh, and Moses gave them all the LORD had spoken, for the shining face carried the whole law. — Matthew Henry

33 COMMENTARY: And till Moses had done speaking with them, he put a vail on his face. Henry notes the vail marked the obscurity of that dispensation compared with the gospel, and was an emblem of the natural vail on men's hearts respecting spiritual things. — Matthew Henry

34 COMMENTARY: But when Moses went in before the LORD to speak with him, he took the vail off, until he came out. Henry observes the vail was removed before God: Moses unveiled when he spoke with the LORD, for fear and unbelief would put the vail before us and hinder our free approach to the mercy-seat. — Matthew Henry

35 COMMENTARY: And the children of Israel saw the face of Moses, that the skin of Moses's face shone, and Moses put the vail upon his face again, until he went in to speak with him. Henry closes on the rhythm: unveiled before God, veiled before men, until the Spirit of the Lord should take away the vail by showing Christ, the end of the law for righteousness to every one that believeth. — Matthew Henry
