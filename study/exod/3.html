Study Notes — Exodus

Chapter 3

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Moses kept the flock of Jethro to the backside of the desert, to Horeb, the mountain of God. Henry divides Moses's life into three forties, and notes that God found him employed as a shepherd, learning the meekness for which he is most noted. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: The angel of the LORD appeared in a flame of fire from a bush that burned yet was not consumed. Henry reads the bush as an emblem of the church in Egypt: under severest persecution, kept by God's presence from being destroyed. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: Moses turned aside to see why the bush was not burnt. Henry notes that those who would have communion with God must attend upon him even in so lowly a manifestation as a bush. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: When the LORD saw Moses turn aside, God called to him out of the bush, and Moses answered, Here am I. Henry observes God gave a gracious call and Moses returned a ready answer. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: God bade Moses not draw nigh, but loose his shoes, for the place was holy ground. Henry explains putting off the shoe as a token of respect and submission, and a call to solemn preparation before God. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: God said, I am the God of thy father, Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob, and Moses hid his face in fear. Henry stresses God says I am, not I was: the patriarchs still live, and no length of time separates the souls of the just from their Maker. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: The LORD said he had surely seen Israel's affliction and heard their cry by reason of their taskmasters. Henry notes even the secret sorrows of God's people are known to him, and their highest oppressors are not above him. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: God was come down to deliver Israel from Egypt and bring them to a good land flowing with milk and honey, the place of the Canaanites and other nations. Henry reads the earthly Canaan as the figure of the heavenly Canaan to which grace brings its redeemed. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: The cry of Israel was come unto God, and he had also seen the oppression wherewith the Egyptians oppressed them. Henry joins the two: God both hears the cry and sees the wrong. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: God said, Come now, I will send thee unto Pharaoh, that thou mayest bring forth my people Israel. Henry notes the commission came only after Moses had learned his own weakness in forty years of obscurity. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: Moses asked, Who am I, that I should go unto Pharaoh and bring forth Israel? Henry contrasts this with his earlier haste: former self-confidence has given way to a sense of weakness, though sinful distrust can creep in under the garb of humility. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: God answered, Certainly I will be with thee, and gave the token that Israel would serve God upon this mountain. Henry says all objections are answered in that one promise: God's presence is enough. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: Moses asked what he should say when Israel asked the name of the God who sent him. Henry notes Moses must revive among them the religion of their fathers, which was almost lost. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: God said, I AM THAT I AM, and bade Moses tell Israel, I AM hath sent me. Henry expounds the name: God is self-existent, eternal and unchangeable, incomprehensible, and faithful and true to all his promises. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: God gave his memorial name, the LORD God of Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob, to be remembered unto all generations. Henry distinguishes the two names: I AM denotes what God is in himself, and the God of your fathers what he is to his people. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: Moses was to gather the elders and tell them the LORD God of their fathers had appeared and had surely visited them and seen what was done to them in Egypt. Henry notes God could promise beforehand that they would hearken, for he would make them willing in the day of power. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: God promised to bring Israel up out of Egyptian affliction unto the land of Canaan, flowing with milk and honey. Henry reads this as the pattern of redemption: those delivered from a spiritual Egypt are brought to a heavenly Canaan. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: The elders would hearken, and Moses and they were to ask Pharaoh for three days' journey into the wilderness to sacrifice. Henry observes that petitions and humble complaints would not prevail with Pharaoh, but God's mighty hand would break him. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: God told Moses he was sure Pharaoh would not let them go, no, not by a mighty hand. Henry notes those who will not bow to the power of God's word will certainly be broken by the power of his hand. — Matthew Henry

20 COMMENTARY: God would stretch out his hand and smite Egypt with wonders, and after that Pharaoh would let them go. Henry stresses deliverance would come by methods out of the common ways of providence. — Matthew Henry

21 COMMENTARY: God would give the people favour in the sight of the Egyptians, so they would not go out empty. Henry sees in this the reversal of tyranny: the oppressors would furnish the oppressed with riches at their departure. — Matthew Henry

22 COMMENTARY: Every woman would borrow jewels of silver and gold and raiment of her neighbour, put them on her children, and spoil the Egyptians. Henry notes the redeemed come forth with all they have, applying all to the glory of God and the service of his church. — Matthew Henry
