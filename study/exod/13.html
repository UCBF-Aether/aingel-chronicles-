Study Notes — Exodus

Chapter 13

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: The LORD spake to Moses, giving the law of the firstborn before the people marched. Henry notes the law followed the judgment at once: the firstborn spared were to be claimed by the God who spared them. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: Sanctify unto me all the firstborn, whatsoever openeth the womb among Israel, both of man and beast, for it is mine. Henry observes the claim rested on redemption: their lives were preserved through a ransom, and ransomed lives are to be consecrated to God's service. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: Moses told the people, Remember this day, in which ye came out from Egypt, out of the house of bondage, for by strength of hand the LORD brought you out, and no leavened bread was to be eaten. Henry notes the remembrance was commanded, not left to feeling: the day of deliverance must be kept up every year. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: This day came ye out in the month Abib. Henry notes the month itself was named for the season of new life, that the calendar might preach the Exodus. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: When the LORD brought them into the land of the Canaanites, Hittites, Amorites, Hivites, and Jebusites, a land flowing with milk and honey, they were to keep this service in this month. Henry observes the ordinance was given for the land of rest, not only for the night of flight. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: Seven days they were to eat unleavened bread, and on the seventh day there would be a feast to the LORD. Henry notes the week of unleavened bread taught sincerity, and under the gospel we must not only remember Christ but observe his holy supper. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: Unleavened bread was to be eaten seven days, and no leavened bread was to be seen with them, nor leaven in all their quarters. Henry observes the thoroughness: leaven was not merely uneaten but unseen, for hypocrisy hidden at home spoils the feast. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: They were to show their son in that day, saying, This is done because of that which the LORD did unto me when I came forth out of Egypt. Henry calls this an old law for catechising: children must be acquainted betimes with the histories of the Bible, and parents must have the story on their lips. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: It would be for a sign upon the hand and a memorial between the eyes, that the LORD's law might be in their mouth, for with a strong hand the LORD brought them out. Henry notes those who have God's law in their heart should have it in their mouth, speaking of it often to affect themselves and teach others. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: They were therefore to keep this ordinance in its season from year to year. Henry observes the yearly return kept the memory from fading: old mercies must not be forgotten, that God may be praised and faith encouraged. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: When the LORD brought them into the land of the Canaanites, as he swore to them and their fathers, and gave it to them, the law of the firstborn would take effect. Henry notes the promise and the duty arrived together: the land given was the land where the firstborn were claimed. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: They were to set apart unto the LORD all that openeth the matrix, and every firstling that cometh of a beast which is male, for it would be the LORD's. Henry observes the claim covered the whole firstborn order, man and beast alike. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: Every firstling of an ass was to be redeemed with a lamb, and if not redeemed, its neck was to be broken; and all the firstborn of man among their children were to be redeemed. Henry notes the unclean beast could not be sacrificed, so it was ransomed or destroyed, a figure of souls forfeited to justice unless ransomed by Christ. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: When a son asked in time to come, What is this? the father was to answer by the story of the Exodus. Henry observes the question was expected and the answer commanded: the institutions existed to make children ask, and parents answer. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: When Pharaoh would hardly let them go, the LORD slew all the firstborn in Egypt, both man and beast, therefore Israel sacrificed the males that opened the matrix and redeemed all their firstborn sons. Henry notes the reason was recited with the rite, lest the custom harden into empty form. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: It would be for a token upon the hand and for frontlets between the eyes, for by strength of hand the LORD brought them out of Egypt. Henry observes the repetition seals the point: redemption must mark both work and sight, hand and forehead. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: When Pharaoh had let the people go, God led them not through the way of the Philistines, although that was near, lest seeing war they should repent and return to Egypt. Henry notes God's way is the right way though it seems about, and he proportions his people's trials to their strength. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: God led the people about, through the way of the wilderness of the Red Sea, and they went up harnessed out of Egypt. Henry notes they went up in good order, in ranks, in expectation of Canaan, carrying Joseph's bones with them through the desert. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: Moses took the bones of Joseph with him, for Joseph had straitly sworn Israel that God would surely visit them, and they were to carry his bones up hence. Henry observes the bones were a pledge carried through the wilderness: Joseph's faith expected the promise, and Israel carried its proof. — Matthew Henry

20 COMMENTARY: They took their journey from Succoth and encamped in Etham, in the edge of the wilderness. Henry notes the first stages were orderly marches, each camp a step from bondage toward the sea. — Matthew Henry

21 COMMENTARY: The LORD went before them by day in a pillar of cloud, to lead them the way, and by night in a pillar of fire, to give them light, that they might go by day and night. Henry notes the pillar was the appearance of the Divine Majesty itself: it sheltered by day from heat and gave light by night, and the Bible is to us what the pillar was to Israel. — Matthew Henry

22 COMMENTARY: He took not away the pillar of cloud by day, nor the pillar of fire by night, from before the people. Henry closes on the constancy: the guide that led them out never left them, and Christ will not leave nor lose his church in the wilderness. — Matthew Henry
