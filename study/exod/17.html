Study Notes — Exodus

Chapter 17

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: All Israel journeyed from the wilderness of Sin, after their journeys, according to the commandment of the LORD, and pitched in Rephidim, where there was no water to drink. Henry observes we may be in the way of duty, yet meet troubles which providence brings for the trial of faith, that God may be glorified in our relief. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: The people chid with Moses, saying, Give us water that we may drink, and Moses answered, Why chide ye with me? Wherefore do ye tempt the LORD? Henry notes it is folly to answer passion with passion, and Moses answered mildly, naming their demand for what it was: tempting God. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: The people thirsted there and murmured, saying, Wherefore hast thou brought us up out of Egypt, to kill us and our children and our cattle with thirst? Henry observes they began to question whether God was with them at all, which is tempting God: distrust after such proofs of his power and goodness. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: Moses cried unto the LORD, saying, What shall I do unto this people? They be almost ready to stone me. Henry notes the leader's extremity: the people he had delivered were ready to kill him for thirst, and he carried the strait straight to God. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: The LORD told Moses to go on before the people, take with him of the elders of Israel, and take in his hand the rod wherewith he smote the river. Henry observes the elders were called as witnesses, and the rod of judgment was now to become the rod of supply. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: Behold, said God, I will stand before thee there upon the rock in Horeb, and thou shalt smite the rock, and water shall come out of it, that the people may drink, and Moses did so in the sight of the elders. Henry notes how wonderful God's patience is toward provoking sinners, giving water out of a rock, and making it a miracle of mercy as well as power. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: He called the name of the place Massah, and Meribah, because of the chiding of Israel and because they tempted the LORD, saying, Is the LORD among us, or not? Henry observes the new name remembered not the mercy of supply but the sin of murmuring: Massah, Temptation, and Meribah, Strife, for sin leaves a blot upon the name. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: Then came Amalek, and fought with Israel in Rephidim. Henry notes Israel now faced war as well as thirst, and engaged in their own necessary defence, for God makes his people able and calls them to various services for the good of his church. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: Moses told Joshua to choose out men and go out and fight with Amalek, and tomorrow he would stand on the top of the hill with the rod of God in his hand. Henry observes the division of service: Joshua fights, Moses prays, and both minister to Israel. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: So Joshua did as Moses had said and fought with Amalek, and Moses, Aaron, and Hur went up to the top of the hill. Henry notes it was great encouragement to the people to see Joshua before them in the field and Moses above them on the hill. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: When Moses held up his hand, Israel prevailed, and when he let down his hand, Amalek prevailed. Henry observes the rod was held up as a banner to encourage the soldiers and as an appeal to God, and success rose and fell with it, to convince Israel that Moses's hand did more for their safety than their own. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: Moses's hands were heavy, so they took a stone and put it under him, and he sat thereon, and Aaron and Hur stayed up his hands, one on one side and the other on the other side, and his hands were steady until the going down of the sun. Henry notes the strongest arm fails with being long held out, and Moses was glad of help: we should not be shy either of asking help or giving it. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: Joshua discomfited Amalek and his people with the edge of the sword. Henry observes the victory was won by sword below and prayer above together, and weapons formed against God's Israel cannot prosper long. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: The LORD told Moses to write this for a memorial in a book and rehearse it in the ears of Joshua, for he would utterly put out the remembrance of Amalek from under heaven. Henry notes Moses must write both what Amalek had done and what God would do, that neither their hatred nor God's salvation be forgotten. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: Moses built an altar and called the name of it Jehovahnissi. Henry observes the altar named the victory's author: the LORD is my banner, for the rod held up on the hill had been Israel's standard. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: For he said, Because the LORD hath sworn that the LORD will have war with Amalek from generation to generation. Henry observes Amalek's destruction was typical of the destruction of all the enemies of Christ and his kingdom, a war that lasts until the last enemy falls. — Matthew Henry
