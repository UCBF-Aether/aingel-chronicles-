Study Notes — Zechariah

Chapter 13

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: In that day there shall be a fountain opened to the house of David and to the inhabitants of Jerusalem for sin and for uncleanness, because the day of mourning is followed by a fountain opened for sin and uncleanness, cleansing the very house and city that mourned. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: And it shall come to pass in that day, says the Lord of hosts, that I will cut off the names of the idols out of the land, and they shall no more be remembered, and also I will cause the prophets and the unclean spirit to pass out of the land, because idols' names are cut off from remembrance, and false prophets with the unclean spirit pass out of the land together. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: And it shall come to pass, that when any shall yet prophesy, then his father and his mother that begat him shall say unto him, thou shalt not live, for thou speakest lies in the name of the Lord, and his father and his mother that begat him shall thrust him through when he prophesies, because zeal against false prophecy will be so great that parents themselves will execute the liar who speaks in the Lord's name. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: And it shall come to pass in that day, that the prophets shall be ashamed every one of his vision, when he has prophesied, neither shall they wear a rough garment to deceive, because false prophets will be ashamed of their visions and will lay aside the rough garment worn to deceive, abandoning the prophet's dress. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: But he shall say, I am no prophet, I am a husbandman, for man taught me to keep cattle from my youth, because the exposed prophet will deny his calling altogether, claiming to be only a husbandman taught to keep cattle from youth. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: And one shall say unto him, what are these wounds in thine hands, then he shall answer, those with which I was wounded in the house of my friends, because the wounds in the hands, the marks of idolatrous cutting, are explained away as wounds received among friends, which Henry notes as the false prophet's excuse, distinct from the wounds of the true Shepherd. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: Awake, O sword, against my shepherd, and against the man that is my fellow, says the Lord of hosts, smite the shepherd, and the sheep shall be scattered, and I will turn mine hand upon the little ones, because the sword is called to awake against God's own Shepherd, his fellow, and the smiting scatters the sheep, while God's hand turns upon the little ones. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: And it shall come to pass, that in all the land, says the Lord, two parts therein shall be cut off and die, but the third shall be left therein, because two parts of the land are cut off and die, while a third part is left, which is the remnant through whom the promise continues. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: And I will bring the third part through the fire, and will refine them as silver is refined, and will try them as gold is tried, they shall call on my name, and I will hear them, I will say, it is my people, and they shall say, the Lord is my God, because the third part is brought through fire and refined like silver and tried like gold, until calling and hearing meet, and the covenant words are spoken on both sides. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
