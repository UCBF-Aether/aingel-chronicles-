Study Notes — Zechariah

Chapter 5

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Then I turned, and lifted up mine eyes, and looked, and behold a flying roll, because the sixth vision shows a scroll flying, a curse in motion, not yet resting, over the whole land. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: And he said unto me, what seest thou, and I answered, I see a flying roll, the length thereof is twenty cubits, and the breadth thereof ten cubits, because the roll's size, twenty by ten cubits, matches the porch of the temple, as if the curse were measured by the sanctuary it avenges. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: Then said he unto me, this is the curse that goes forth over the face of the whole earth, for every one that steals shall be cut off as on this side according to it, and every one that swears shall be cut off as on that side according to it, because the roll is written on both sides, with theft on one side and false swearing on the other, the two sins that break both tables of the law. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: I will bring it forth, says the Lord of hosts, and it shall enter into the house of the thief, and into the house of him that swears falsely by my name, and it shall remain in the midst of his house, and shall consume it with the timber thereof and the stones thereof, because the curse enters the sinner's house and stays there, consuming timber and stones together, until nothing remains. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: Then the angel that talked with me went forth, and said unto me, lift up now thine eyes, and see what is this that goes forth, because a second sight in the same chapter follows, and the prophet is told to look again at what goes forth. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: And I said, what is it, and he said, this is an ephah that goes forth, he said moreover, this is their resemblance through all the earth, because the ephah, the measure of corn, is the measure of the people's sins, seen alike through all the earth. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: And, behold, there was lifted up a talent of lead, and this is a woman that sits in the midst of the ephah, because under the leaden lid sits a woman, the figure of wickedness itself, shut inside the measure. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: And he said, this is wickedness, and he cast it into the midst of the ephah, and he cast the weight of lead upon the mouth thereof, because the angel names the woman wickedness, casts her back into the ephah, and seals the mouth with lead, so that wickedness is shut in. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: Then lifted I up mine eyes, and looked, and, behold, there came out two women, and the wind was in their wings, for they had wings like the wings of a stork, and they lifted up the ephah between the earth and the heaven, because two winged women, swift as storks, lift the sealed ephah between earth and heaven, carrying wickedness away. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: Then said I to the angel that talked with me, whither do these bear the ephah, because the prophet asks where wickedness is being carried, which is the question the vision exists to answer. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: And he said unto me, to build it a house in the land of Shinar, and it shall be established, and set there upon her own base, because wickedness is carried to Shinar, to Babylon, its own home, where it will be settled upon its own base, removed from the holy land. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
