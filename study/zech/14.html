Study Notes — Zechariah

Chapter 14

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Behold, the day of the Lord comes, and thy spoil shall be divided in the midst of thee, because the day of the Lord opens with Jerusalem's spoil divided in her very midst, as if the enemy had already won. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: For I will gather all nations against Jerusalem to battle, and the city shall be taken, and the houses rifled, and the women ravished, and half of the city shall go forth into captivity, and the residue of the people shall not be cut off from the city, because God himself gathers all nations, and the city is taken and rifled, yet half goes captive while a residue is not cut off, which is the remnant preserved in the worst hour. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: Then shall the Lord go forth, and fight against those nations, as when he fought in the day of battle, because after the gathering, the Lord himself goes forth to fight, as he fought in former days of battle. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: And his feet shall stand in that day upon the mount of Olives, which is before Jerusalem on the east, and the mount of Olives shall cleave in the midst thereof toward the east and toward the west, and there shall be a very great valley, and half of the mountain shall remove toward the north, and half of it toward the south, because the Lord's feet stand on Olivet, and the mountain splits east and west, making a great valley, half moving north and half south. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: And ye shall flee to the valley of the mountains, for the valley of the mountains shall reach unto Azal, yea, ye shall flee, like as ye fled from before the earthquake in the days of Uzziah king of Judah, and the Lord my God shall come, and all the saints with thee, because the people flee through the new valley to Azal, as they fled the earthquake in Uzziah's days, and the Lord comes with all his saints. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: And it shall come to pass in that day, that the light shall not be clear, nor dark, because the day of the Lord has a light neither clear nor dark, a mingled light, unlike any ordinary day. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: But it shall be one day which shall be known to the Lord, not day, nor night, but it shall come to pass, that at evening time it shall be light, because the day is one day known to the Lord alone, neither day nor night, yet at evening time it shall be light, reversing the order of darkness. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: And it shall be in that day, that living waters shall go out from Jerusalem, half of them toward the former sea, and half of them toward the hinder sea, in summer and in winter shall it be, because living waters flow from Jerusalem to both seas, former and hinder, in summer and winter alike, never failing. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: And the Lord shall be king over all the earth, in that day shall there be one Lord, and his name one, because the Lord's kingship covers all the earth, and in that day there is one Lord and one name, with no rival worship beside him. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: All the land shall be turned as a plain from Geba to Rimmon south of Jerusalem, and it shall be lifted up, and inhabited in her place, from Benjamin's gate unto the place of the first gate, unto the corner gate, and from the tower of Hananeel unto the king's winepresses, because the land is levelled from Geba to Rimmon, while Jerusalem is lifted up and inhabited in her place, from gate to gate and tower to winepress. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: And men shall dwell in it, and there shall be no more utter destruction, but Jerusalem shall be safely inhabited, because the end of the levelling is safe dwelling, with no more utter destruction, and Jerusalem safely inhabited. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: And this shall be the plague wherewith the Lord will smite all the people that have fought against Jerusalem, their flesh shall consume away while they stand upon their feet, and their eyes shall consume away in their holes, and their tongue shall consume away in their mouth, because the plague consumes flesh, eyes and tongue while the enemies still stand, a wasting that strikes every member. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: And it shall come to pass in that day, that a great tumult from the Lord shall be among them, and they shall lay hold every one on the hand of his neighbour, and his hand shall rise up against the hand of his neighbour, because a great tumult from the Lord turns every man's hand against his neighbour's hand, so the confederacy destroys itself. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: And Judah also shall fight at Jerusalem, and the wealth of all the heathen round about shall be gathered together, gold, and silver, and apparel, in great abundance, because Judah fights at Jerusalem, and the wealth of the surrounding nations, gold, silver and apparel, is gathered in great abundance. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: And so shall be the plague of the horse, of the mule, of the camel, and of the ass, and of all the beasts that shall be in these tents, as this plague, because the plague reaches the beasts as well as men, horse, mule, camel and ass, in the tents of the besiegers. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: And it shall come to pass, that every one that is left of all the nations which came against Jerusalem shall even go up from year to year to worship the King, the Lord of hosts, and to keep the feast of tabernacles, because the survivors of the very nations that came against Jerusalem go up year by year to worship the King and keep tabernacles. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: And it shall be, that whoso will not come up of all the families of the earth unto Jerusalem to worship the King, the Lord of hosts, even upon them shall be no rain, because the families that refuse the pilgrimage lose the rain, since worship and rain are joined. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

18 COMMENTARY: And if the family of Egypt go not up, and come not, that have no rain, there shall be the plague, wherewith the Lord will smite the heathen that come not up to keep the feast of tabernacles, because Egypt, which needs no rain, is not excepted, but receives the plague appointed for all who refuse the feast. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

19 COMMENTARY: This shall be the punishment of Egypt, and the punishment of all nations that come not up to keep the feast of tabernacles, because the punishment is stated once for Egypt and once for all nations, so that no nation claims exemption. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

20 COMMENTARY: In that day shall there be upon the bells of the horses, HOLINESS UNTO THE LORD, and the pots in the Lord's house shall be like the bowls before the altar, because even horses' bells bear the high priest's inscription, and common pots become like the altar bowls, since holiness spreads to everything. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

21 COMMENTARY: Yea, every pot in Jerusalem and in Judah shall be holiness unto the Lord of hosts, and all they that sacrifice shall come and take of them, and seethe therein, and in that day there shall be no more the Canaanite in the house of the Lord of hosts, because every pot in Jerusalem and Judah is holy, all who sacrifice use them, and no more is there a trafficker, a Canaanite, in the Lord's house. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
