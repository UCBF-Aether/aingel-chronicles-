Study Notes — Zechariah

Chapter 12

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: The burden of the word of the Lord for Israel, says the Lord, which stretches forth the heavens, and lays the foundation of the earth, and forms the spirit of man within him, because the burden opens with God's power as Creator of heaven, earth and the spirit of man, which grounds all that follows. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: Behold, I will make Jerusalem a cup of trembling unto all the people round about, when they shall be in the siege both against Judah and against Jerusalem, because Jerusalem will be a cup that makes every besieger reel, when the siege closes on Judah and Jerusalem together. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: And in that day will I make Jerusalem a burdensome stone for all people, all that burden themselves with it shall be cut in pieces, though all the people of the earth be gathered together against it, because Jerusalem is a stone too heavy to lift, cutting all who burden themselves with it, even if all the earth gathers against it. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: In that day, says the Lord, I will smite every horse with astonishment, and his rider with madness, and I will open mine eyes upon the house of Judah, and will smite every horse of the people with blindness, because the besiegers' horses are struck with astonishment and their riders with madness and blindness, while God's eyes are opened upon Judah. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: And the governors of Judah shall say in their heart, the inhabitants of Jerusalem shall be my strength in the Lord of hosts their God, because Judah's governors find their strength in Jerusalem's inhabitants, through the Lord of hosts their God. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: In that day will I make the governors of Judah like a hearth of fire among the wood, and like a torch of fire in a sheaf, and they shall devour all the people round about, on the right hand and on the left, and Jerusalem shall be inhabited again in her own place, even in Jerusalem, because Judah's governors become fire among wood and a torch in a sheaf, devouring round about, while Jerusalem is inhabited again in her own place. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: The Lord also shall save the tents of Judah first, that the glory of the house of David and the glory of the inhabitants of Jerusalem do not magnify themselves against Judah, because the tents of Judah are saved first, so that David's house and Jerusalem cannot boast over Judah. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: In that day shall the Lord defend the inhabitants of Jerusalem, and he that is feeble among them at that day shall be as David, and the house of David shall be as God, as the angel of the Lord before them, because defence lifts the feeble to David's stature, and David's house to godlike strength, like the angel of the Lord before them. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: And it shall come to pass in that day, that I will seek to destroy all the nations that come against Jerusalem, because God himself seeks the destruction of the nations that come against Jerusalem, which is the purpose of the defence. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: And I will pour upon the house of David, and upon the inhabitants of Jerusalem, the spirit of grace and of supplications, and they shall look upon me whom they have pierced, and they shall mourn for him, as one mourns for his only son, and shall be in bitterness for him, as one that is in bitterness for his firstborn, because the spirit of grace and supplication produces a look upon the Pierced One, and mourning like that for an only son and a firstborn, which Henry reads as mourning for the Messiah pierced. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: In that day shall there be a great mourning in Jerusalem, as the mourning of Hadadrimmon in the valley of Megiddon, because the mourning is as great as that for Josiah at Hadad-rimmon in the valley of Megiddo, the national mourning for a slain king. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: And the land shall mourn, every family apart, the family of the house of David apart, and their wives apart, the family of the house of Nathan apart, and their wives apart, because mourning is by families apart, David's house and Nathan's house each apart, with wives apart, since true repentance is personal even within the household. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: The family of the house of Levi apart, and their wives apart, the family of Shimei apart, and their wives apart, because Levi's house and Shimei's house mourn apart likewise, priestly and common families each in their own grief. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: All the families that remain, every family apart, and their wives apart, because the mourning is completed by all remaining families, every family apart, with wives apart, so that no family is excepted. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
