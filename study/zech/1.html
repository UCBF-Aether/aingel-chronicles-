Study Notes — Zechariah

Chapter 1

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: In the eighth month, in the second year of Darius, came the word of the Lord unto Zechariah, the son of Berechiah, the son of Iddo the prophet, because the prophecy is dated to the rebuilding period, when the returned exiles needed a word to resume the temple work. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: The Lord has been sore displeased with your fathers, because the present generation is reminded that their fathers' ruin came from a displeasure they provoked, not from an accident that befell them. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: Therefore say thou unto them, thus says the Lord of hosts, turn ye unto me, says the Lord of hosts, and I will turn unto you, says the Lord of hosts, because the threefold name of the Lord of hosts frames the promise, that man's turning is answered by God's turning. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: Be ye not as your fathers, unto whom the former prophets have cried, saying, thus says the Lord of hosts, turn ye now from your evil ways, and from your evil doings, but they did not hear, nor hearken unto me, says the Lord, because the fathers were warned by prophet after prophet and refused to hear, and the sons are warned not to repeat the refusal. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: Your fathers, where are they, and the prophets, do they live for ever, because both fathers and prophets are gone, which proves that the warnings were not idle, since the fathers perished as warned. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: But my words and my statutes, which I commanded my servants the prophets, did they not take hold of your fathers, and they returned and said, like as the Lord of hosts thought to do unto us, according to our ways, and according to our doings, so has he dealt with us, because the words outlived the speakers and took hold at last, and the fathers confessed that God had done as he said. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: Upon the four and twentieth day of the eleventh month, which is the month Sebat, in the second year of Darius, came the word of the Lord unto Zechariah, because a second, precisely dated vision follows, given in the winter of the rebuilding, to encourage the work. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: I saw by night, and behold a man riding upon a red horse, and he stood among the myrtle trees that were in the bottom, and behind him were there red horses, speckled, and white, because the rider among the lowly myrtles pictures God's watchful presence with his afflicted people, and the horses of different colours are his patrols through the earth. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: Then said I, O my lord, what are these, and the angel that talked with me said unto me, I will shew thee what these be, because the prophet asks, and the interpreting angel answers, which is the pattern of all the visions that follow. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: And the man that stood among the myrtle trees answered and said, these are they whom the Lord has sent to walk to and fro through the earth, because the horses are God's messengers, patrolling the whole earth and reporting its state. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: And they answered the angel of the Lord that stood among the myrtle trees, and said, we have walked to and fro through the earth, and, behold, all the earth sits still, and is at rest, because the report is that the nations are at peace, which makes Jerusalem's continued affliction seem strange. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: Then the angel of the Lord answered and said, O Lord of hosts, how long wilt thou not have mercy on Jerusalem and on the cities of Judah, against which thou hast had indignation these threescore and ten years, because the angel pleads the seventy years of indignation, asking how long mercy will wait while the nations rest. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: And the Lord answered the angel that talked with me with good words and comfortable words, because the answer to the plea is not rebuke but comfort, good words spoken for Jerusalem. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: So the angel that communed with me said unto me, cry thou, saying, thus says the Lord of hosts, I am jealous for Jerusalem and for Zion with a great jealousy, because God's jealousy is the jealousy of a husband for his own, and it is great against those who afflict her. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: And I am very sore displeased with the heathen that are at ease, for I was but a little displeased, and they helped forward the affliction, because the nations, at ease themselves, went beyond God's measured displeasure and added their own cruelty to the affliction. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: Therefore thus says the Lord, I am returned to Jerusalem with mercies, my house shall be built in it, says the Lord of hosts, and a line shall be stretched forth upon Jerusalem, because return with mercies means the temple rebuilt and the city measured out anew for building. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: Cry yet, saying, thus says the Lord of hosts, my cities through prosperity shall yet be spread abroad, and the Lord shall yet comfort Zion, and shall yet choose Jerusalem, because prosperity, comfort and election are all promised again, each with a yet, as things still to come. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

18 COMMENTARY: Then lifted I up mine eyes, and saw, and behold four horns, because the second vision shows four horns, the powers that scattered Judah, Israel and Jerusalem. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

19 COMMENTARY: And I said unto the angel that talked with me, what be these, and he answered me, these are the horns which have scattered Judah, Israel, and Jerusalem, because the horns are named by their work, scattering all three names of God's people. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

20 COMMENTARY: And the Lord shewed me four carpenters, because against four horns God shows four workmen, the powers raised to break the powers that scattered. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

21 COMMENTARY: Then said I, what come these to do, and he spoke, saying, these are the horns which have scattered Judah, so that no man did lift up his head, but these are come to fray them, to cast out the horns of the Gentiles, which lifted up their horn over the land of Judah to scatter it, because the carpenters come to terrify and cast down the horns that lifted themselves over Judah, so that no man could lift his head. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
