Study Notes — 1 Chronicles

Chapter 14

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Hiram king of Tyre sent messengers to David, with cedar trees, masons, and carpenters, to build him a house. Henry notes no man has such sufficiency that he needs no neighbours: great David still needed little Hiram's workmen and materials. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: David perceived the Lord had confirmed him king over Israel, for his kingdom was lifted up on high because of his people Israel. Henry notes a wise man is satisfied to be settled only when he sees God's special providence in it, and advancements are for usefulness, not self-glory. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: David took more wives at Jerusalem, and begat more sons and daughters. Henry calls this David's infirmity in settling, yet notes the numerous issue added to his honour and strength, for children are a heritage of the Lord. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: These are the names of his children born in Jerusalem: Shammua, Shobab, Nathan, and Solomon. Henry notes David's children are recorded again here, as before, because it was their honour to have such a father. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: When the Philistines heard David was anointed king over all Israel, they sought him, and David heard and went out against them. Henry warns against security in any settlement: when Christ's kingdom advances, the serpent's seed insults it, and molestation should be expected. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: The Philistines came and spread themselves in the valley of Rephaim. Henry sets this invasion as the occasion for David's repeated enquiry of God. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: David enquired of God, saying, shall I go up against the Philistines, and wilt thou deliver them into my hand? And the Lord said, go up, for I will deliver them. Henry directs readers in all ways to acknowledge God: in distress fly to him, when wronged appeal to him, and when unsure ask counsel at his oracles. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: They came up to Baal-perazim, and David smote them there, saying, God hath broken in upon my enemies by my hand like the breaking forth of waters, therefore they called the place Baal-perazim. Henry encourages resisting spiritual enemies in observance of divine directions and dependence on divine strength, as Philistines fled before David. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: When the Philistines left their gods there, David gave commandment and they were burned with fire. Henry teaches holy indignation against idolatry and all its remains from this burning. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: The Philistines yet again spread themselves in the valley. Henry notes David enquired a second time, modelling repeated dependence rather than presuming on one victory. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: God said, go not up after them, but turn away and come upon them over against the mulberry trees. Henry presents the second, different direction as proof that guidance must be sought afresh, not assumed from past success. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: When thou shalt hear a sound of going in the tops of the mulberry trees, then go out to battle, for God is gone forth before thee to smite the Philistines. Henry urges attending God's motions in providence and Spirit: when God goes before us, gird up and follow. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: David did as God commanded him, and they smote the Philistine host from Gibeon even to Gazer. Henry makes obedience to the new direction the pattern of success. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: The fame of David went out into all lands, and the Lord brought the fear of him upon all nations. Henry reads this reputation as a type of the Son of David, exalted and given a name above every name, formidable to enemies and desirable as an ally. — Matthew Henry

No note for verse(s) 5, 6, 7 — the public-domain sources consulted give no distinct explanation for these verses beyond the list itself, so nothing was invented for them.
