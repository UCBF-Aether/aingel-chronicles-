Study Notes — 1 Chronicles

Chapter 10

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: The Philistines fought against Israel, and Israel fled and fell slain on Mount Gilboa. Henry observes princes sin and the people suffer: Israel quitted its cities and the Philistines dwelt in them, chiefly for Saul's sin. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: The Philistines followed hard after Saul and his sons, and slew Jonathan, Abinadab, and Malchi-shua. Henry notes parents sin and children suffer: Jonathan, gracious and generous, fell with his father as part of the condemned house. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: The battle went sore against Saul, and the archers hit and wounded him. Henry sees a fitting answer to his sin: he had thrown a javelin at David and missed, but the archers hit him. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: Saul told his armour-bearer to thrust him through, lest the uncircumcised abuse him, but the bearer refused in fear, so Saul fell on his own sword. Henry notes he who commanded Doeg to slay the priests is now left to be his own murderer. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: When his armour-bearer saw Saul dead, he likewise fell on his sword and died. Henry treats this as the close of Saul's household falling together, sentence long reprieved at last executed. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: So Saul died, and his three sons, and all his house died together. Henry stresses the visitation on the house: the measure of Saul's iniquity was full, and his day to fall had come. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: When Israel in the valley saw the flight and Saul's death, they forsook their cities and fled, and the Philistines dwelt in them. Henry notes the people suffered for their prince's sin, even though Samuel had reformed them from idolatry. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: On the morrow the Philistines came to strip the slain and found Saul and his sons fallen on Gilboa. Henry draws the lesson that greater dignity brings greater danger of disgrace: Saul's body, because he was king, was abused more than any other. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: They stripped him, took his head and armour, and sent tidings to their idols and people throughout Philistia. Henry mocks the idols that knew nothing till tidings were brought, and warns that if we give God no glory in success, even Philistines will rise in judgment. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: They put his armour in the house of their gods and fastened his head in Dagon's temple. Henry asks whether Dagon should have so honourable a share in their triumph while the true God is forgotten in ours. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: When all Jabesh-gilead heard what the Philistines had done to Saul, they acted. Henry introduces their rescue as the respect due to the remains of the deceased, especially of princes, whose bodies were once united to immortal souls. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: All the valiant men arose, took Saul's body and his sons' bodies, brought them to Jabesh, buried their bones under the oak, and fasted seven days. Henry honours this rescue and burial as due respect, leaving the eternal state to God. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: Saul died for his transgression against the Lord's word, which he kept not, and for asking counsel of one with a familiar spirit. Henry lists the causes: disobedience in not destroying the Amalekites, and consulting a witch, a sin that fills the measure of iniquity. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: He enquired not of the Lord, therefore the Lord slew him and turned the kingdom to David son of Jesse. Henry reconciles this with Saul's late enquiry: he did not enquire till the last extremity, too late, and what wicked hands did was yet by God's determinate counsel. — Matthew Henry
