Study Notes — 1 Chronicles

Chapter 13

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: David consulted with the captains of thousands and hundreds, and with every leader. Henry notes that though David had authority to command this good work, he chose consultation to honour the great men, gain advice on manner and timing, and make it a national act. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: David said to all Israel's assembly, if it seem good and it be of the Lord our God, let us send to our brethren left in Israel and to the priests and Levites in their cities, to gather unto us. Henry notes he calls the people brethren in humility, remembers them as a remnant after scattering wars, and insists priests and Levites be summoned, since the ark was their province. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: Let us bring again the ark of our God to us, for we enquired not at it in the days of Saul. Henry notes David's first care on the throne was to honour God with his power and enjoy the sacred oracle, and he humbly shares the guilt of past neglect rather than blaming Saul. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: The thing was right in the eyes of all the people, and all the congregation said they would do so. Henry notes unanimous agreement: a prudently proposed good work may find readier concurrence than expected. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: David gathered all Israel from Shihor of Egypt even to Hamath, to bring the ark from Kirjath-jearim. Henry notes it was worth travelling far, from south to north, to attend God's ark and grace the solemnity. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: They went to Baalah, Kirjath-jearim of Judah, to bring up the ark of God, whose name is called by the name of the Lord that dwelleth between the cherubims. Henry frames this as the revival of a neglected ordinance and return of God's presence token, worthy of all joy. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: They carried the ark on a new cart out of Abinadab's house, and Uzza and Ahio drove the cart. Henry warns even wise and good men err when ordinances are revived: David copied the Philistines' cart, but we must walk by rule, not by example varying from the rule. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: David and all Israel played before God with all their might, with singing, harps, psalteries, timbrels, cymbals, and trumpets. Henry reads this joy as fitting for religion shining out of obscurity, openly professed and countenanced by princes. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: When they came to Chidon's threshingfloor, the oxen stumbled, and Uzza put forth his hand to hold the ark. Henry warns Uzza's sin teaches against presumption and irreverence about holy things: a good intention does not justify a bad action, nor does familiarity excuse contempt. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: The anger of the Lord was kindled against Uzza, and he smote him because he put his hand to the ark, and he died there before God. Henry compares Uzza to Nadab and Abihu: God will be sanctified in those who come near him, and the nearer they are, the more he resents presumption. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: David was displeased because the Lord had made a breach upon Uzza, and he called the place Perez-uzza to this day. Henry cautions against quarrelling with God under rebuke: if God is angry with us, shall we dare be angry with him? — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: David was afraid of God that day, saying, how shall I bring the ark of God home to me? Henry notes the damp on Israel's joy should teach rejoicing with trembling and serving the Lord with fear even amid gladness. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: So David brought not the ark home to himself to the city of David, but carried it aside into the house of Obed-edom the Gittite. Henry warns the stop should not drive David from duty, only from sin: he should have gone on, and the breach might have been made up. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: The ark remained with Obed-edom's family three months, and the Lord blessed his house and all that he had. Henry urges welcoming God's ordinances into our houses: the ark is a guest nobody loses by, life to those who receive it in love. — Matthew Henry
