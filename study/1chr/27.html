Study Notes — 1 Chronicles

Chapter 27

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Now the children of Israel after their number, to wit, the chief fathers and captains of thousands and hundreds, and their officers that served the king in any matter of the courses, which came in and went out month by month throughout all the months of the year, of every course were twenty and four thousand. Henry praises David's prudent model: not a standing army burdening the country, nor disbandment leaving the kingdom naked, but 24,000 constantly in arms, changed monthly, totalling 288,000 trained militia. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: Over the first course for the first month was Jashobeam the son of Zabdiel, and in his course were twenty and four thousand. Henry notes the twelve commanders were David's worthies, first signalised by great actions, then advanced: honour attending merit. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: And over the course of the second month was Dodai an Ahohite, and of his course was Mikloth also the ruler. Henry notes Dodai had Mikloth as substitute when absent or infirm, or successor when dead. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: The third captain of the host for the third month was Benaiah the son of Jehoiada, a chief priest, and in his course were twenty and four thousand. Henry notes cohen can mean priest or prince, and here better chief ruler or principal officer. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: This is that Benaiah, who was mighty among the thirty, and above the thirty, and in his course was Ammizabad his son. Henry notes Benaiah had his son under him in his course. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: The fourth captain for the fourth month was Asahel the brother of Joab, and Zebadiah his son after him, and in his course were twenty and four thousand. Henry notes Asahel's son succeeding him shows the militia plan was laid in David's early reign, since Asahel was killed while David reigned in Hebron, then revived after the wars. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: Furthermore over the tribes of Israel: the ruler of the Reubenites was Eliezer, and of the Simeonites, Shephatiah. Henry notes Moses's ancient order continued, every tribe with its prince, whether by election or succession. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: Of Judah, Elihu, one of the brethren of David. Henry notes Elihu, or Eliab, prince of Judah, was Jesse's eldest son, descended from Nahshon and Salmon, princes in Moses's time. — Matthew Henry

21 COMMENTARY: Of Benjamin, Jaasiel the son of Abner. Henry notes though Abner opposed David's throne, David did not oppose his son's preferment, perhaps nominating him himself, teaching us to render good for evil. — Matthew Henry

23 COMMENTARY: But David took not the number of them from twenty years old and under, because the Lord had said he would increase Israel like to the stars of the heavens. Henry notes David forbade numbering under twenty, thinking to save reflection on the promise of innumerability, yet it was a poor salvo, since under-twenty were never customarily numbered. — Matthew Henry

24 COMMENTARY: Joab the son of Zeruiah began to number, but he finished not, because there fell wrath for it against Israel, neither was the number put in the account of the chronicles of king David. Henry notes the prideful numbering turned to no good account: never perfected, never exact, never recorded as authentic, for Joab did it by halves and David was ashamed of it. — Matthew Henry

25 COMMENTARY: And over the king's treasures was Azmaveth the son of Adiel, and over the storehouses in the fields, cities, villages, and castles, was Jehonathan the son of Uzziah. Henry introduces the court officers as rulers of the king's substance, all for service, not state or sport, in the simplicity of those times. — Matthew Henry

31 COMMENTARY: And over the flocks was Jaziz the Hagerite. All these were the rulers of the substance which was king David's. Henry sums up the substance officers: David, great soldier, scholar, and prince, was yet a great husband of his estate, stocked for profit not pleasure, since the king himself is served of the field. — Matthew Henry

32 COMMENTARY: Also Jonathan David's uncle was a counsellor, a wise man, and a scribe, and Jehiel the son of Hachmoni was with the king's sons. Henry notes David's attendants were eminent for wisdom: his uncle, wise and scripturally read, was counsellor, and another excelled in learning as tutor to his children. — Matthew Henry

33 COMMENTARY: And Ahithophel was the king's counsellor, and Hushai the Archite was the king's companion. Henry distinguishes Ahithophel, very cunning, as counsellor, from Hushai, an honest man, as companion and confidant. — Matthew Henry

34 COMMENTARY: And after Ahithophel was Jehoiada the son of Benaiah, and Abiathar, and the general of the king's army was Joab. Henry closes the court list with Jehoiada and Abiathar succeeding Ahithophel, and Joab as general, noting much of a prince's wisdom is seen in choosing his ministry. — Matthew Henry

No note for verse(s) 3, 8, 9, 10, 11, 12, 13, 14, 15, 17, 19, 20, 22, 26, 27, 28, 29, 30 — the public-domain sources consulted give no distinct explanation for these verses beyond the list itself, so nothing was invented for them.
