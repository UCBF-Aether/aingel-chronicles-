Study Notes — 1 Chronicles

Chapter 12

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: These are they that came to David to Ziklag, while he yet kept himself close because of Saul. Henry notes David's kingdom ripened slowly, and God inclined helpers to come even while David did not invite them, foreseeing Saul's death was near. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: They were of Saul's brethren of Benjamin, armed with bows, using right and left hand in hurling stones and shooting arrows. Henry suggests generous indignation at Saul's base treatment of David moved his own tribesmen to act vigorously for him, and their ambidextrous dexterity made a few of great service. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: Of the Gadites there separated themselves unto David into the hold to the wilderness men of might, fit for battle, with shield and buckler, faces like lions, swift as roes. Henry calls this a laudable separation: though few, eleven, they were brave, disciplined, and daring, unlike David's earlier broken-fortune followers. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: These Gadites were captains of the host: the least was over a hundred, the greatest over a thousand. Henry notes they were officers of their tribe's militia, so though they brought no soldiers, they had hundreds and thousands at command. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: These swam over Jordan when it overflowed its banks, and put to flight all in the valleys east and west. Henry praises men fit for God's cause who venture in dependence on divine protection and go through with their business, following their blow both ways. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: There came of Benjamin and Judah to the hold unto David. Henry introduces Amasai's company as Judah and Benjamin men coming to David, whose leader's speech follows. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: David went out to meet them and said, if ye come peaceably to help me, my heart shall be knit unto you, but if to betray me, the God of our fathers look and rebuke it. Henry praises David's prudent, fair treaty: he accepts cordial goodwill thankfully, and leaves treachery to God, refusing to judge or avenge his own cause. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: Then the Spirit came upon Amasai, chief of the captains, and he said, thine are we, David, and on thy side, thou son of Jesse; peace be unto thee, and peace to thy helpers, for thy God helpeth thee. Henry notes the Spirit here is wisdom and resolution, and the speech professes adherence, wishes peace, and assures help from heaven. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: Of Manasseh also there fell some to David when he went with the Philistines against Saul. Henry notes providence gave them the opportunity as David marched through their country, and though not many, they were mighty men who helped against the Amalekites who plundered Ziklag. — Matthew Henry

22 COMMENTARY: For at that time day by day there came to David to help him, until it was a great host, like the host of God. Henry marvels how David's interest grew strangely just when he needed it: when the promise comes to birth, God finds strength to bring it forth. — Matthew Henry

23 COMMENTARY: These are the numbers of the bands ready armed to the war that came to David to Hebron, to turn Saul's kingdom to him according to the Lord's word. Henry frames the Hebron muster as the tribes perfecting David's settlement after Ishbosheth's death, each tribe bringing its armed quota. — Matthew Henry

24 COMMENTARY: The children of Judah, bearing shield and spear, were 6,800 ready armed to the war. Henry notes near tribes brought fewest, not from coldness but prudence, since the rest lay near within call, while Judah entertained those from afar. — Matthew Henry

25 COMMENTARY: Of Simeon, mighty men of valour for the war, 7,100. Henry counts Simeon among the near tribes bringing few, yet still mighty men of valour. — Matthew Henry

26 COMMENTARY: Of the Levites 4,600. Henry notes Levites and priests appeared hearty for David, ready to fight for him as well as pray, because they knew he was called of God. — Matthew Henry

27 COMMENTARY: Jehoiada, leader of the Aaronites, and with him 3,700. Henry includes the Aaronite leader in the priestly support, hearty in a cause they knew was God's calling. — Matthew Henry

28 COMMENTARY: Zadok, a young man mighty of valour, and of his father's house twenty-two captains. Henry names Zadok among the mighty men of valour who came armed and governable. — Matthew Henry

29 COMMENTARY: Of Benjamin, Saul's brethren, 3,000, for hitherto the greatest part of them had kept Saul's ward. Henry notes even Saul's kindred came over, though not many, because foolish tribal affection kept many long in Saul's sinking interest, and kindred must never overrule conscience. — Matthew Henry

30 COMMENTARY: Of Ephraim 20,800, mighty men of valour, famous throughout their fathers' house. Henry cites Ephraim among the mighty men of valour in the Hebron host. — Matthew Henry

32 COMMENTARY: Of Issachar, men that had understanding of the times, to know what Israel ought to do, 200 chiefs, and all their brethren at their commandment. Henry calls them the fewest yet as serviceable as the greatest numbers: statesmen who knew the political times and judged Israel ought to make David king. — Matthew Henry

33 COMMENTARY: Of Zebulun, such as went forth to battle, expert in war, with all instruments of war, 50,000, who could keep rank, and were not of double heart. Henry singles out Zebulun for singleness: Israelites indeed, in whom was no guile, of one heart for the public good. — Matthew Henry

35 COMMENTARY: Of Dan expert in war 28,600. Henry cites Dan among those expert in war, bringing arms and instruments, since David could not furnish them all. — Matthew Henry

36 COMMENTARY: Of Asher, such as went forth to battle, expert in war, 40,000. Henry pairs Asher with Dan as expert in war, from the remote tribes bringing large numbers out of zeal. — Matthew Henry

37 COMMENTARY: Of the other side Jordan, Reuben, Gad, and half Manasseh, with all manner of instruments of war, 120,000. Henry notes the trans-Jordan tribes brought the largest host, showing zeal matching their distance. — Matthew Henry

38 COMMENTARY: All these men of war, who could keep rank, came with a perfect heart to Hebron to make David king over all Israel, and all the rest of Israel were of one heart. Henry stresses their perfection was unity: none had separate interests, all for the public good, warm hearts but cool heads. — Matthew Henry

39 COMMENTARY: There they were with David three days, eating and drinking, for their brethren had prepared for them. Henry notes adjacent tribes, at least pains in travelling, bore the charge of victualling the camps, that there might be equality. — Matthew Henry

40 COMMENTARY: Moreover they that were nigh brought bread, meat, meal, cakes, wine, oil, oxen, and sheep abundantly, for there was joy in Israel. Henry closes with the noble feast: when the righteous bear rule the city rejoices, a foretaste of the joy when Christ's throne is set up in a soul. — Matthew Henry

No note for verse(s) 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 9, 10, 11, 12, 13, 20, 21, 31, 34 — the public-domain sources consulted give no distinct explanation for these verses beyond the list itself, so nothing was invented for them.
