Study Notes — 1 Chronicles

Chapter 18

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: After this David smote the Philistines, subdued them, and took Gath and her towns out of their hand. Henry notes after sweet communion with God in the last chapter, David went on with extraordinary vigour, conquering and to conquer, and long enemies of Israel are brought down at last. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: He smote Moab, and the Moabites became David's servants and brought gifts. Henry lists Moab among the conquests abroad that followed David's piety and prayer. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: David smote Hadarezer king of Zobah unto Hamath, as he went to stablish his dominion by the river Euphrates. Henry notes the uncertainty of the world: Hadarezer was smitten as he went to establish his dominion. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: David took from him a thousand chariots, seven thousand horsemen, and twenty thousand footmen, and houghed all the chariot horses but reserved a hundred chariots. Henry notes David believed a horse is a vain thing for safety, and resolved not to trust in chariots. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: When the Syrians of Damascus came to help Hadarezer, David slew twenty-two thousand Syrians. Henry notes enemies of God's church often ruin themselves by helping one another, gathered as sheaves into the floor. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: Then David put garrisons in Syria-damascus, and the Syrians became his servants and brought gifts, and the Lord preserved David whithersoever he went. Henry notes the wealth of the sinner laid up for the just: Syrian gifts, gold shields, and brass came to Jerusalem, as the temple would be built from Gentile spoils. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: David took the shields of gold on Hadarezer's servants and brought them to Jerusalem. Henry counts the gold shields among the spoils dedicated to the temple's enriching. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: From Tibhath and Chun, cities of Hadarezer, David took very much brass, wherewith Solomon made the brasen sea, pillars, and vessels of brass. Henry notes the temple itself was built of Gentile spoils, a presage of Gentile interest in the gospel church. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: When Tou king of Hamath heard how David had smitten all Hadarezer's host, he sent to David. Henry notes it is our interest to make friends of those who have God's presence: Tou courted David's favour rather than contending with the Son of David. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: He sent Hadoram his son to enquire of David's welfare and congratulate him, because he had fought Hadarezer, with vessels of gold, silver, and brass. Henry notes the noble present, and that the presents we bring Christ are not gold vessels but hearts and whole selves. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: Them also David dedicated unto the Lord, with silver and gold from all nations: Edom, Moab, Ammon, Philistines, and Amalek. Henry notes what God blesses us with we must honour him with: friends' presents and enemies' spoils alike were laid up for the temple. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: Moreover Abishai son of Zeruiah slew eighteen thousand Edomites in the valley of salt. Henry lists the Edomite conquest among David's prosperity abroad. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: He put garrisons in Edom, and all Edomites became David's servants, and the Lord preserved David whithersoever he went. Henry repeats the refrain: those who take God along whithersoever they go may expect to prosper and be preserved whithersoever they go. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: So David reigned over all Israel, and executed judgment and justice among all his people. Henry notes God gives power not for looking great but for doing good: David was not so intent on conquests abroad as to neglect justice at home, an eminent type of the Messiah whose sceptre is right. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: Joab son of Zeruiah was over the host, and Jehoshaphat son of Ahilud, recorder. Henry lists the flourishing court: Joab commanding the army, Jehoshaphat recording. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: Zadok son of Ahitub and Abimelech son of Abiathar were priests, and Shavsha was scribe. Henry notes the priests and scribe among the court officers in David's settled kingdom. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: Benaiah son of Jehoiada was over the Cherethites and Pelethites, and David's sons were chief about the king. Henry closes the court list with the bodyguard captain and the king's sons as chief about him. — Matthew Henry
