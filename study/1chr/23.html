Study Notes — 1 Chronicles

Chapter 23

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: When David was old and full of days, he made Solomon his son king over Israel. Henry notes David, only seventy yet full of days, settled the crown in solemn assembly, making Adonijah's later attempt impudent, and securing church and state together. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: David gathered together all the princes of Israel, with the priests and the Levites. Henry notes the numbering and distribution were done in this solemn assembly for the temple service's order. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: Now the Levites were numbered from thirty years old and upward, and their number by their polls, man by man, was thirty and eight thousand. Henry notes Moses's Levites by this rule were 8,580, but now serviceable men were 38,000, above fourfold, and David numbered them not in pride but for good purpose. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: Of which, twenty and four thousand were to set forward the work of the house of the Lord. Henry notes almost two-thirds were appointed to attend priests in sacrifices, keeping vessels clean and service exact, serving 1,000 a week in twenty-four courses. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: And six thousand were officers and judges; moreover four thousand were porters, and four thousand praised the Lord with instruments. Henry notes officers and judges administered justice in the country, porters guarded the temple as its life-guards, and singers kept up the new office of praise. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: David divided them into courses among the sons of Levi, namely, Gershon, Kohath, and Merari. Henry notes families were mustered and disposed by kindred, so duty could be checked by roll call and families employed together would love and assist one another. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: The sons of Merari's line are joined in one reckoning where sons were few. Henry notes two families were joined in one because they had not many sons: those weak separately may appear considerable together. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: The sons of Amram: Aaron and Moses. Aaron was separated, that he should sanctify the most holy things, he and his sons for ever, to burn incense before the Lord, to minister unto him, and to bless in his name for ever. Henry notes Aaron's family was advanced to the priesthood as recompense for his self-denial in submitting to younger Moses, while Moses' own family stood level with common Levites. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: Now concerning Moses the man of God, his sons were named of the tribe of Levi. Henry calls Moses' levelling an evidence of self-denial: with his interest with God and man he could have raised his family, but left his children no marks of distinction. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: And the sons of Eliezer were, Rehabiah the chief. And Eliezer had none other sons, but the sons of Rehabiah were very many. Henry notes Rehabiah's sons were highly multiplied, a recompense for Moses generously refusing a great nation from himself in place of Israel. — Matthew Henry

24 COMMENTARY: These were the sons of Levi after the house of their fathers, even the chief of the fathers, as they were counted by number of names by their polls, that did the work for the service of the house of the Lord, from the age of twenty years and upward. Henry notes David, by God's direction, lowered enlistment from thirty to twenty, confirmed in his last words. — Matthew Henry

25 COMMENTARY: For David said, the Lord God of Israel hath given rest unto his people, that they may dwell in Jerusalem for ever. Henry gives the reason: with rest and Jerusalem as everlasting dwelling, there was no more carrying of tabernacle burdens, so service was easier and twenty-year-olds could bear it. — Matthew Henry

26 COMMENTARY: And also unto the Levites, they shall no more carry the tabernacle, nor any vessels of it for the service thereof. Henry notes God considers our frame and lays no more than we can bear: heavy burdens required full strength, but settled service did not. — Matthew Henry

27 COMMENTARY: For by the last words of David the Levites were numbered from twenty years old and above. Henry notes David's last hand to the establishment expressly appointed twenty years forever after, to prevent idleness and pleasure habits in young Levites waiting till twenty-five. — Matthew Henry

28 COMMENTARY: Because their office was to wait on the sons of Aaron for the service of the house of the Lord, in the courts, and in the chambers, and in the purifying of all holy things, and the work of the service of the house of God. Henry notes Levites did the drudgery-work of God's house: keeping courts and chambers clean, setting things in place, ready for use. — Matthew Henry

29 COMMENTARY: Both for the shewbread, and for the fine flour for meat offering, and for the unleavened cakes, and for that which is baked in the pan, and for that which is fried, and for all manner of measure and size. Henry notes Levites prepared showbread and offerings so priests had all ready, and kept the sanctuary standards of weights and measures exact. — Matthew Henry

30 COMMENTARY: And to stand every morning to thank and praise the Lord, and likewise at even. Henry notes singers thanked and praised at morning and evening sacrifices, a reasonable, articulate service beyond mere trumpet sound. — Matthew Henry

31 COMMENTARY: And to offer all burnt sacrifices unto the Lord in the sabbaths, in the new moons, and on the set feasts, by number, according to the order commanded unto them, continually before the Lord. Henry notes praise accompanied oblations on sabbaths, new moons, and feasts, as the Jewish church grew more intelligent in devotion. — Matthew Henry

32 COMMENTARY: And that they should keep the charge of the tabernacle of the congregation, and the charge of the holy place, and the charge of the sons of Aaron their brethren, in the service of the house of the Lord. Henry notes porters kept charge of tabernacle and holy place, admitting none beyond what was allowed, and served Aaron's sons as brethren, hewn from the same rock. — Matthew Henry

No note for verse(s) 7, 8, 9, 10, 12, 15, 16, 18, 19, 20, 21, 22, 23 — the public-domain sources consulted give no distinct explanation for these verses beyond the list itself, so nothing was invented for them.
