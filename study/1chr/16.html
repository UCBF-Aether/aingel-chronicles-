Study Notes — 1 Chronicles

Chapter 16

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: They brought the ark of God and set it in the midst of the tent David pitched for it, and offered burnt sacrifices and peace offerings before God. Henry calls it a glorious day: the ark, once obscure in a country town and borrowed in a private house, now had its own habitation in the royal city. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: When David had made an end of offering, he blessed the people in the name of the Lord. Henry notes David gave the people both a royal treat and, far better, a blessing in the Lord's name, as father and prophet, praying and commending them to grace. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: He dealt to every one of Israel, man and woman, a loaf of bread, a good piece of flesh, and a flagon of wine. Henry notes David's open-handed generosity, enlarged by holy joy, giving all what was worth coming for. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: He appointed certain Levites to minister before the ark, to record, thank, and praise the Lord God of Israel. Henry notes David gave God glory by sacrifices and by songs, appointing Levites to record the story and celebrate it. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: Asaph the chief, and next to him Zechariah, Jeiel, Shemiramoth, Jehiel, Mattithiah, Eliab, Benaiah, Obed-edom, and Jeiel with psalteries and harps, but Asaph made a sound with cymbals. Henry names Asaph as chief musician to whom the thanksgiving psalm was delivered. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: Benaiah and Jahaziel the priests with trumpets continually before the ark of the covenant of God. Henry includes priestly trumpets in the settled ministry before the ark. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: On that day David delivered first this psalm to thank the Lord into Asaph's hand. Henry notes this thanksgiving psalm, gathered from several psalms, was now first delivered to Asaph for the church's use. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: Give thanks unto the Lord, call upon his name, make known his deeds among the people. Henry notes praise must glorify God and edify others: thanksgiving, prayer, song, and discourse, with deeds made known among the people. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: Sing unto him, sing psalms unto him, talk of all his wondrous works. Henry includes singing and discourse among the ways to glorify God by praise. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: Glory ye in his holy name: let the heart of them rejoice that seek the Lord. Henry notes those who give glory to God's name may glory in it, valuing their relation to God and venturing on his promise. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: Seek the Lord and his strength, seek his face continually. Henry explains seeking him by the ark of his strength, in which he manifests himself. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: Remember his marvellous works that he hath done, his wonders, and the judgments of his mouth. Henry urges commemorating God's former mercies to ancestors with thankfulness. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: O ye seed of Israel his servant, ye children of Jacob, his chosen ones. Henry frames the psalm as addressed to the covenant people, the spiritual seed of the patriarchs. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: He is the Lord our God; his judgments are in all the earth. Henry glorifies God as creator and ruler of the whole creation, and as ours. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: Be ye mindful always of his covenant, the word he commanded to a thousand generations. Henry calls the everlasting covenant the great matter of joy: God never forgets it, so we never must. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: Even of the covenant he made with Abraham, and his oath unto Isaac. Henry notes the covenant was ancient, made with Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob long dead, yet still sure. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: And hath confirmed the same to Jacob for a law, and to Israel for an everlasting covenant. Henry stresses the covenant was commanded, God having authority to promise and ability to perform. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: Saying, unto thee will I give the land of Canaan, the lot of your inheritance. Henry notes the promise of Canaan to the patriarchs as the covenant's concrete gift. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: When ye were but few, even a few, and strangers in it. Henry recalls the patriarchs as strangers and sojourners in Canaan, few and easily swallowed up, yet protected. — Matthew Henry

20 COMMENTARY: And when they went from nation to nation, and from one kingdom to another people. Henry notes they were continually on the remove and exposed, yet no man was suffered to do them wrong. — Matthew Henry

21 COMMENTARY: He suffered no man to do them wrong: yea, he reproved kings for their sakes. Henry cites Pharaoh and Abimelech reproved and plagued for the patriarchs' sakes. — Matthew Henry

22 COMMENTARY: Saying, touch not mine anointed, and do my prophets no harm. Henry explains the patriarchs were God's anointed, sanctified by grace and glory, and his prophets, instructed and commissioning others; to touch them was to touch the apple of God's eye. — Matthew Henry

23 COMMENTARY: Sing unto the Lord, all the earth; shew forth from day to day his salvation. Henry calls the great salvation, the promised salvation by Christ, a subject never exhausted, to be celebrated daily. — Matthew Henry

24 COMMENTARY: Declare his glory among the heathen, his marvellous works among all nations. Henry urges declaring God's glory among the heathen, that strangers may be led to acquaintance, allegiance, and adoration. — Matthew Henry

25 COMMENTARY: For great is the Lord, and greatly to be praised: he also is to be feared above all gods. Henry glorifies God as great, supreme, and sole God, greatly to be praised. — Matthew Henry

26 COMMENTARY: For all the gods of the people are idols: but the Lord made the heavens. Henry contrasts idols with the creator, who made the heavens. — Matthew Henry

27 COMMENTARY: Glory and honour are in his presence; strength and gladness are in his place. Henry describes God as most bright and blessed in himself, with glory and honour in his presence. — Matthew Henry

28 COMMENTARY: Give unto the Lord, ye kindreds of the people, give unto the Lord glory and strength. Henry calls for giving the Lord the glory due to his name, and owning even more his due. — Matthew Henry

29 COMMENTARY: Give unto the Lord the glory due unto his name: bring an offering, and come before him: worship the Lord in the beauty of holiness. Henry notes holiness is the beauty of the Lord and of all sanctified performances, and offerings now are the fruit of lips and heart. — Matthew Henry

30 COMMENTARY: Fear before him, all the earth: the world also shall be stable, that it be not moved. Henry urges reverence for God's universal monarchy, which establishes the world so it cannot be removed. — Matthew Henry

31 COMMENTARY: Let the heavens be glad, and let the earth rejoice: and let men say among the nations, the Lord reigneth. Henry joins rejoicing to reverence: because the Lord reigns, heavens and earth may be glad. — Matthew Henry

32 COMMENTARY: Let the sea roar, and the fulness thereof: let the fields rejoice, and all that is therein. Henry extends rejoicing to sea, fields, and woods, even looking to the day of judgment. — Matthew Henry

33 COMMENTARY: Then shall the trees of the wood sing out at the presence of the Lord, because he cometh to judge the earth. Henry notes the prospect of judgment inspires awful pleasure: creation rejoices that he comes to judge. — Matthew Henry

34 COMMENTARY: O give thanks unto the Lord; for he is good; for his mercy endureth for ever. Henry makes this the burden of the Gibeon singers' songs, and the psalm's repeated refrain. — Matthew Henry

35 COMMENTARY: And say ye, save us, O God of our salvation, and gather us together, and deliver us from the heathen, that we may give thanks to thy holy name, and glory in thy praise. Henry insists that amid praise we must pray for afflicted brethren, scattered and oppressed, as our own. — Matthew Henry

36 COMMENTARY: Blessed be the Lord God of Israel for ever and ever. And all the people said, Amen, and praised the Lord. Henry notes David begins with give thanks and concludes with blessed be the Lord, and the people signified consent with Amen, praising the Lord. — Matthew Henry

37 COMMENTARY: So he left there before the ark Asaph and his brethren, to minister before the ark continually, as every day's work required. Henry stresses worship is not only for solemn days but every day, and Asaph's ministry at Jerusalem was continual song, without sacrifices, since the altars were at Gibeon. — Matthew Henry

38 COMMENTARY: And Obed-edom with their brethren, threescore and eight; Obed-edom also the son of Jeduthun and Hosah to be porters. Henry notes the porters and attendants settled in their posts as part of the daily method. — Matthew Henry

39 COMMENTARY: And Zadok the priest, and his brethren the priests, before the tabernacle of the Lord in the high place at Gibeon. Henry explains the separation since Eli's time: ark at Jerusalem, altar at Gibeon, and David took care neither should be neglected. — Matthew Henry

40 COMMENTARY: To offer burnt offerings unto the Lord upon the altar continually morning and evening, according to all written in the law of Moses. Henry notes ceremonial worship, though inferior to prayer and praise, was kept up as a type of Christ's mediation, morning and evening by law. — Matthew Henry

41 COMMENTARY: And with them Heman and Jeduthun, and the rest chosen, who were expressed by name, to give thanks to the Lord, because his mercy endureth for ever. Henry notes Gibeon's singers also sang, with the burden that his mercy endures forever, on instruments appointed for holy service. — Matthew Henry

42 COMMENTARY: And with them Heman and Jeduthun with trumpets and cymbals for those that should make a sound, and with musical instruments of God. And the sons of Jeduthun were porters. Henry distinguishes common mirth from holy joy, with limits carefully kept in instruments and service. — Matthew Henry

43 COMMENTARY: And all the people departed every man to his house: and David returned to bless his house. Henry closes with satisfaction: the people went home pleased, and David returned to keep up family worship, which public worship must not supersede. — Matthew Henry
