Study Notes — 1 Chronicles

Chapter 22

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Then David said, this is the house of the Lord God, and this is the altar of burnt offering for Israel. Henry notes God chose the ground, a Jebusite threshingfloor, a presage of the gospel temple among Gentiles, and the temple was built for the altar, since altars long preceded temples. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: David commanded to gather the strangers in Israel, and set masons to hew wrought stones for God's house. Henry notes strangers, proselytes but not of Israel's dignity, were employed as artists, sparing free-born Israelites servile stone-hewing after Egypt's brick bondage. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: David prepared iron in abundance for nails for doors and gates and joinings, and brass without weight. Henry lists iron and brass among the abundant preparations, with cedar and stones. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: Also cedar trees in abundance, for the Zidonians and Tyrians brought much cedar wood to David. Henry notes cedar came from Tyre and Zidon, the daughter of Tyre bringing her gift. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: David said, Solomon my son is young and tender, and the house to be builded must be exceeding magnifical, of fame and glory throughout all countries: I will therefore now make preparation for it. So David prepared abundantly before his death. Henry notes aged David, quickened by approaching death, prepared for young Solomon, lest he lose time, and designed a magnificent house to inspire holy awe and invite strangers to know the true God. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: Then he called for Solomon his son, and charged him to build an house for the Lord God of Israel. Henry notes Solomon, though young, was capable of instruction, and had only one charge: to build God's house. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: David said to Solomon, my son, as for me, it was in my mind to build an house unto the name of the Lord my God. Henry notes David tells Solomon why he himself did not build, though it was in his mind. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: But the word of the Lord came to me, saying, thou hast shed blood abundantly, and hast made great wars: thou shalt not build an house unto my name, because thou hast shed much blood upon the earth in my sight. Henry explains this as war blood, justly shed yet making David less fit, showing how precious human life is, and typing Christ who builds by saving lives, not destroying them. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: Behold, a son shall be born to thee, who shall be a man of rest, and I will give him rest from all his enemies, for his name shall be Solomon, and I will give peace and quietness unto Israel in his days. He shall build an house for my name. Henry notes Solomon was nominated by God, and would have leisure, rest abroad and peace at home, and therefore should build; where God gives rest he expects work. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: He shall build an house for my name, and he shall be my son, and I will be his father, and I will establish the throne of his kingdom over Israel for ever. Henry notes God's promise to establish Solomon's kingdom should encourage him to honour God. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: Now, my son, the Lord be with thee, and prosper thee, and build the house of the Lord thy God, as he hath said of thee. Henry concludes the charge with presence and prosperity, and the call to rise and be doing. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: Only the Lord give thee wisdom and understanding, and give thee charge concerning Israel, that thou mayest keep the law of the Lord thy God. Henry notes David's prayer for Solomon: if God gives the charge, he will give wisdom for its discharge, a prayer Solomon later echoed for an understanding heart. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: Then shalt thou prosper, if thou takest heed to fulfil the statutes and judgments which the Lord charged Moses with: be strong, and of good courage; dread not, nor be dismayed. Henry warns building the temple cannot purchase a dispensation to sin: the king remains subject to Israel's God, and needs courage for a vast undertaking. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: Now, behold, in my trouble I have prepared for the house of the Lord an hundred thousand talents of gold, and a thousand thousand talents of silver, and brass and iron without weight, and timber and stone have I prepared, and thou mayest add thereto. Henry notes David speaks of it humbly, in my poverty, as encouragement, and explains the vast sums as including all dedicated things, with numbers expressing very much. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: Moreover there are workmen with thee in abundance, hewers and workers of stone and timber, and all manner of cunning men for every manner of work. Henry notes Solomon must not rest in preparations but add thereto, improving on others' labours. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: Of the gold, silver, brass, and iron, there is no number. Arise therefore, and be doing, and the Lord be with thee. Henry notes hope of God's presence must not slacken endeavour: though the Lord be with us, we must rise and be doing. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: David also commanded all the princes of Israel to help Solomon his son, saying. Henry notes those about the throne must set in with the king, advising and quickening Solomon and making the work easy. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: Is not the Lord your God with you? And hath he not given you rest on every side? For he hath given the inhabitants of the land into mine hand, and the land is subdued before the Lord and before his people. Henry presses gratitude: God had given victory, rest, and a good land, so the more he has done, the more we should do for him. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: Now set your heart and your soul to seek the Lord your God; arise therefore, and build ye the sanctuary of the Lord God, to bring the ark of the covenant and the holy vessels into the house built for the Lord's name. Henry urges setting heart and soul on seeking God as chief good, so head, hand, estate, and interest will be cheerfully employed for his sanctuary. — Matthew Henry
