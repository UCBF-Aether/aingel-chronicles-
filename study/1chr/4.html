Study Notes — 1 Chronicles

Chapter 4

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: The sons of Judah here are descendants, not immediate sons, for with the exception of Pharez none were Judah's immediate sons. JFB notes the others are mentioned solely to introduce Shobal, whose genealogy the historian intended to trace. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: Jabez was more honourable than his brethren, and his mother named him for sorrow. JFB eulogises him for sincere and fervent piety and perhaps public and patriotic works, and notes Jewish writers made him an eminent doctor of the law whose scribes gave a town his name. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: Jabez called on the God of Israel to bless him, enlarge his coast, keep his hand with him, and keep him from evil so it might not grieve him, and God granted his request. JFB reads the prayer as a vow like Jacob's, made on entering a critical service, probably expelling Canaanites, and notes the plea plays on his name, grief: let me have no more sorrow. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: The sons of Kenaz were Othniel and Seraiah. JFB notes Kenaz was grandfather of Caleb, who from that relationship is called a Kenezite. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: Seraiah begat Joab, father of the valley of Charashim, for they were craftsmen. JFB renders it the valley of craftsmen, where persons of one trade dwelt together from father to son, probably carpenters near Jerusalem. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: She bare Miriam among the sons of Ezra, a verse JFB finds obscure as it stands and clears by rearrangement: Bithiah, Pharaoh's daughter, whom Mered took, bore Miriam, while his wife Jehudijah bore the others. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

18 COMMENTARY: His wife Jehudijah bore Jered, Heber, and Jekuthiel, and these are the sons of Bithiah, Pharaoh's daughter, whom Mered took. JFB calls Jehudijah the Jewess, to distinguish her from the Egyptian wife, and notes the Egyptian princess must have renounced idolatry and converted to Israel's God before such a marriage. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

21 COMMENTARY: The sons of Shelah include Laadah, father of Mareshah, and the families of fine-linen workers of Ashbea's house. JFB sees here hereditary trades among the Hebrews, with linen knowledge most probably acquired in Egypt. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

22 COMMENTARY: Jokim, the men of Chozeba, Joash, and Saraph had dominion in Moab, and Jashubi-lehem, and these are ancient things. JFB improves the rendering to sojourned in Moab but returned to Beth-lehem, identifying the potters of the next verse as king's workmen at Netaim and Gedera on Judah's border. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

23 COMMENTARY: These were the potters, and those that dwelt among plants and hedges, dwelling with the king for his work. JFB identifies them as potters employed by the king in his own work at Netaim and Gedera. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

24 COMMENTARY: The sons of Simeon are classed along with Judah, because their possession was partly taken out of Judah's extensive territory. JFB notes differences from other genealogies arise because some persons bore more than one name. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

27 COMMENTARY: Shimei had sixteen sons and six daughters, but his brethren had not many children, nor did their family multiply like Judah's. JFB cross-refers the census figures showing Simeon's smaller increase. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

31 COMMENTARY: These were their cities unto the reign of David. JFB explains that through Simeonite sloth or cowardice some allotted cities were only nominally theirs, never taken from the Philistines until David, when he assigned them to Judah. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

38 COMMENTARY: These mentioned by name were princes in their families, and their fathers' house increased greatly. JFB links their increase to the migration that follows, as Simeon, holding only part of Judah's land, was forced to seek accommodation elsewhere. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

39 COMMENTARY: They went to the entrance of Gedor, to the east side of the valley, to seek pasture for their flocks. JFB describes their settlement in the fertile pastures of Gederah, soon broken up by nomad plunderers driving them from place to place toward Mount Seir. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

41 COMMENTARY: These written by name came in Hezekiah's days, smote the tents and habitations found there, destroyed them utterly, and dwelt in their rooms for pasture. JFB places this Simeonite expansion in Hezekiah's reign, after their earlier displacement. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

42 COMMENTARY: Some Simeonites, five hundred men under Pelatiah, Neariah, Rephaiah, and Uzziel, sons of Ishi, went to Mount Seir. JFB notes this as the final push of the displaced Simeonites seeking a settlement by force. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

43 COMMENTARY: They smote the rest of the Amalekites that escaped, and dwelt there unto this day. JFB closes the Simeonite migration with this conquest of Mount Seir, completing their search for pasture begun at Gedor. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

No note for verse(s) 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 11, 12, 15, 16, 19, 20, 25, 26, 28, 29, 30, 32, 33, 34, 35, 36, 37, 40 — the public-domain sources consulted give no distinct explanation for these verses beyond the list itself, so nothing was invented for them.
