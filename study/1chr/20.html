Study Notes — 1 Chronicles

Chapter 20

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: After the year expired, at the time kings go out to battle, Joab led the army, wasted Ammon, besieged Rabbah, but David tarried at Jerusalem, and Joab smote Rabbah and destroyed it. Henry notes the parenthesis, David tarried, hints at the sin in Uriah's matter, which Chronicles deliberately does not repeat: we should not delight in repeating others' faults. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: David took their king's crown, weighing a talent of gold with precious stones, set it on his own head, and brought exceeding much spoil out of the city. Henry lists the crown and spoil among the fruits of Rabbah's destruction. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: He brought out the people and cut them with saws, harrows of iron, and axes, and so dealt with all Ammon's cities, then David and all the people returned to Jerusalem. Henry notes the great severity used toward Rabbah's people and all Ammon's cities. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: After this war arose at Gezer with the Philistines, where Sibbechai the Hushathite slew Sippai, of the giant's children, and they were subdued. Henry notes giants of Gath were last subdued, as Anak's sons were last in Joshua's conquest: some sins, like giants, keep ground long but judgment comes to victory at last. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: There was war again with the Philistines, and Elhanan son of Jair slew Lahmi brother of Goliath the Gittite, whose spear staff was like a weaver's beam. Henry notes David's servants, men of ordinary stature, were too hard for Gath's giants because they had God on their side. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: Yet again there was war at Gath, where was a man of great stature, with twenty-four fingers and toes, six on each hand and foot, also son of the giant. Henry asks what a finger more on each hand can do in contest with Omnipotence, and notes God's plants grow in usefulness, not bulk. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: When he defied Israel, Jonathan son of Shimea, David's brother, slew him. Henry notes giants who defied Israel paid for insolence: none are more visibly marked for ruin than those who reproach God and his Israel. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: These were born unto the giant in Gath, and fell by the hand of David and his servants. Henry closes the giant wars with gradual victory: as David's victories were gradual, so the Son of David's, until death itself, the last enemy, is triumphed over. — Matthew Henry
