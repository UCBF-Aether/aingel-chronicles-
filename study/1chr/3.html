Study Notes — 1 Chronicles

Chapter 3

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: These were David's sons born in Hebron, and JFB stresses place and time of birth matter: the oldest son born after his father's accession was, by Eastern notions, the proper heir, which explains Amnon's ambition when a younger brother was placed above him. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: The sixth son in Hebron was Ithream by Eglah his wife. JFB supposes Eglah another name of Michal, who may have had a son before her mockery of David, and notes she is called wife because she was his proper wife, restored after being taken away. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: These six were born in Hebron, where David reigned seven years and six months, and in Jerusalem thirty-three years. JFB's section on David's sons frames the Hebron list against the Jerusalem list that follows. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: Four sons were born in Jerusalem of Bath-shua, daughter of Ammiel, that is Bath-sheba, whose father is called Eliam elsewhere. JFB notes Solomon, though named last, was not her only son but is called so from the distinguished affection of which he was the object. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: Elishama and Eliphelet appear twice among David's Jerusalem sons. JFB explains the sons of the same name were children of different mothers, distinguished by some title, or the first two died and their names were given to later sons to preserve their memories. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: Elishama, Eliada, and Eliphelet make nine Jerusalem sons in this list, though Samuel counts eleven. JFB explains two are omitted, either for early deaths or because they left no issue. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: Solomon's line is drawn down to the captivity through a succession of good and bad but influential monarchs. JFB marvels that a crown passed father to son in lineal descent for seventeen reigns, the promised reward of David's piety, with vacillation only near the close. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: Josiah's sons were Johanan, Jehoiakim, Zedekiah, and Shallum. JFB notes Zedekiah is called Josiah's son but is elsewhere his brother's son in effect, since Hebrew words of relationship are used with great latitude, and Shallum is generally supposed to be Jehoahaz. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: The sons of Jeconiah begin with Assir, rather Jeconiah the prisoner or captive. JFB explains the record of his condition shows Salathiel was born during the Babylonian captivity, and that written childless means no son succeeding him on the throne. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

18 COMMENTARY: Malchiram, Pedaiah, Shenazar, and the rest follow in a text JFB suspects is dislocated after Jeconiah. He gives Davidson's rearrangement tracing the royal stem through Salathiel to Zerubbabel, the historian's object being to trace the line through Zerubbabel. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

19 COMMENTARY: The sons of Pedaiah were Zerubbabel and Shimei, and Zerubbabel's sons Meshullam, Hananiah, and their sister Shelomith. JFB's rearrangement places Zerubbabel as son of Salathiel, making him the link by which the royal line continues after the captivity. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

No note for verse(s) 2, 7, 9, 11, 12, 13, 14, 16, 20, 21, 22, 23, 24 — the public-domain sources consulted give no distinct explanation for these verses beyond the list itself, so nothing was invented for them.
