Study Notes — 1 Chronicles

Chapter 21

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Satan stood up against Israel and provoked David to number Israel. Henry notes the tempter's aim was to weaken Israel by tempting its best friend to pride, the sin most offensive to God, and even the best saints are not out of Satan's reach. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: David said to Joab and the rulers, go, number Israel from Beer-sheba to Dan, and bring the number to me that I may know it. Henry notes numbering seemed innocent, as a shepherd knowing his flock, but done in pride of heart it was a great provocation. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: Joab answered, the Lord make his people a hundred times so many more, but why doth my lord require this thing? Why will he be a cause of trespass to Israel? Henry notes Joab's remonstrance: it was needless, since God promised multiplication, and dangerous, since it might provoke God against Israel. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: Nevertheless the king's word prevailed against Joab, so Joab departed, went throughout Israel, and came to Jerusalem. Henry notes Joab obeyed only because forced, doing the work with the greatest reluctance. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: Joab gave the sum of the number to David: all Israel were 1,100,000 men that drew sword, and Judah 470,000. Henry notes the muster was really very great, yet Joab had no heart to perfect it. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: But Levi and Benjamin counted he not among them, for the king's word was abominable to Joab. Henry notes Joab left two considerable tribes unnumbered, and was perhaps inexact elsewhere, which may explain differences from Samuel's sums. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: God was displeased with this thing, therefore he smote Israel. Henry notes God's dearest children must smart for wrongdoing, and no sin is more displeasing than pride of heart. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: David said unto God, I have sinned greatly, because I have done this thing, but now take away the iniquity of thy servant, for I have done very foolishly. Henry notes David's penitent confession: he owned he had sinned greatly and foolishly, and prayed the iniquity be done away. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: The word of the Lord came unto Gad, David's seer, saying. Henry introduces Gad as the prophet through whom David is put to his choice of punishment. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: Go and tell David, thus saith the Lord, I offer thee three things, choose thee one of them, that I may do it unto thee. Henry notes David must be punished by one of three judgments, for his further humiliation. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: So Gad came to David and said, thus saith the Lord, choose thee. Henry presents Gad delivering the trilemma to David. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: Either three years' famine, or three months to be destroyed before thy foes, or else three days the sword of the Lord, even pestilence in the land, and the angel destroying throughout Israel. Henry lists the three rods: war, famine, or pestilence, with the terror of all three impressed on David. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: David said, I am in a great strait: let me fall now into the hand of the Lord, for very great are his mercies, but let me not fall into the hand of man. Henry praises David casting himself on God's mercy even while God frowned: though he slay me, yet will I trust him. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: So the Lord sent pestilence upon Israel, and there fell of Israel seventy thousand men. Henry notes divine justice made the people fewer, whom David was proud of numbering: God numbered them to the sword, a black bill of mortality against his muster-roll. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: God sent an angel unto Jerusalem to destroy it, and as he was destroying, the Lord beheld, and repented him of the evil, and said to the angel, it is enough, stay now thine hand. And the angel stood by the threshingfloor of Ornan the Jebusite. Henry notes when David repented, God repented of the judgment and stayed the angel's hand. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: David lifted up his eyes and saw the angel of the Lord stand between earth and heaven, having a drawn sword stretched out over Jerusalem, and David and the elders fell upon their faces. Henry notes the terrible sight: while we lie under God's wrath, holy angels are armed against us, though unseen. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: David said, is it not I that commanded the people to be numbered? Even I it is that have sinned and done evil indeed; but as for these sheep, what have they done? Let thine hand be upon me and my father's house, but not on thy people, that they should be plagued. Henry notes David accepted punishment, asked to be the sufferer as the sinner, and showed tender concern for the people plagued for his transgression. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: Then the angel of the Lord commanded Gad to say to David that he should go up and set up an altar unto the Lord in the threshingfloor of Ornan the Jebusite. Henry notes the same angel who carried on the war set on foot the treaty of peace, commanding the altar through the seer to honour the prophetic office. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: David went up at the saying of Gad, which he spake in the name of the Lord. Henry notes David immediately obeyed the direction to build the altar. — Matthew Henry

20 COMMENTARY: Ornan turned back and saw the angel, and his four sons with him hid themselves. Henry notes Ornan had himself seen the angel and was terrified by its brightness and drawn sword, so he hid with his sons. — Matthew Henry

21 COMMENTARY: As David came to Ornan, Ornan looked and saw David, and went out of the threshingfloor and bowed himself to David with his face to the ground. Henry presents Ornan's reverent meeting of the king on the threshingfloor. — Matthew Henry

22 COMMENTARY: David said to Ornan, grant me the place of this threshingfloor, that I may build an altar unto the Lord, for the full price, that the plague may be stayed from the people. Henry notes David's bargain: he would not serve God at other people's charge. — Matthew Henry

23 COMMENTARY: Ornan said unto David, take it to thee, and let my lord the king do that which is good in his eyes: lo, I give thee the oxen also for burnt offerings, and the threshing instruments for wood, and the wheat for the meat offering; I give it all. Henry notes Ornan generously offered it gratis, willing to do anything toward atonement after seeing the angel. — Matthew Henry

24 COMMENTARY: King David said to Ornan, nay, but I will verily buy it for the full price, for I will not take that which is thine for the Lord, nor offer burnt offerings without cost. Henry stresses David's principle: he would not serve God at another's charge. — Matthew Henry

25 COMMENTARY: So David gave to Ornan for the place six hundred shekels of gold by weight. Henry records the full-price purchase of the threshingfloor, the future temple site. — Matthew Henry

26 COMMENTARY: David built there an altar unto the Lord, offered burnt offerings and peace offerings, and called upon the Lord, and he answered him from heaven by fire upon the altar of burnt offering. Henry notes God's acceptance: the fire that might have fastened on the sinner fastened on the sacrifice, signifying anger turned away. — Matthew Henry

27 COMMENTARY: And the Lord commanded the angel, and he put up his sword again into the sheath thereof. Henry notes the destroying sword returned to its sheath once atonement was accepted. — Matthew Henry

28 COMMENTARY: At that time when David saw that the Lord had answered him in the threshingfloor of Ornan, he sacrificed there. Henry notes David continued sacrificing where God had graciously met him, expecting to meet him there still. — Matthew Henry

29 COMMENTARY: For the tabernacle of the Lord, which Moses made in the wilderness, and the altar of burnt offering, were at that season in the high place at Gibeon. Henry notes the Mosaic altar remained at Gibeon, while David's new altar stood at Ornan's floor. — Matthew Henry

30 COMMENTARY: But David could not go before it to enquire of God, for he was afraid because of the sword of the angel of the Lord. Henry notes David dared not leave the angel with drawn sword over Jerusalem, and the business required haste, so God dispensed with one altar in the present distress. — Matthew Henry
