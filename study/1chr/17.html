Study Notes — 1 Chronicles

Chapter 17

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: David dwelt in his house and said to Nathan, lo, I dwell in a house of cedars, but the ark of the covenant remains under curtains. Henry notes good people should be solicitous for God's kingdom: David could not be easy in cedar while the ark lodged in curtains, asking not what to do for his children or coffers, but what to do for God. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: Nathan said to David, do all that is in thine heart, for God is with thee. Henry notes prophets should be ready to encourage every good purpose, and Nathan saw no reason to doubt God was with David in it. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: The same night the word of God came to Nathan, saying. Henry frames the night word as God's gracious acceptance of David's purpose, with a promise in place of permission. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: Go and tell David my servant, thus saith the Lord, thou shalt not build me an house to dwell in. Henry notes David must prepare for the house but not build it, as Moses brought Israel in sight of Canaan but left Joshua to possess it. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: I have not dwelt in an house since I brought up Israel unto this day, but have gone from tent to tent, and from one tabernacle to another. Henry notes God affects little external pomp: his ark was content with a tabernacle, and we may be content awhile with mean accommodations. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: Wheresoever I have walked with all Israel, spake I a word to any of the judges, saying, why have ye not built me an house of cedars? Henry notes God commanded judges to feed his people but never bade them build him a house. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: Thus saith the Lord of hosts, I took thee from the sheepcote, even from following the sheep, that thou shouldest be ruler over my people Israel. Henry notes David must not think preferment vain because he may not build: to be ruler over Israel is honour enough, and something must be left for successors. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: I have been with thee whithersoever thou hast walked, cut off all thine enemies from before thee, and made thee a name like the great men in the earth. Henry notes God had given David victories and a name, and intended by him to establish Israel. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: I will ordain a place for my people Israel, and plant them, that they may dwell in their place and be moved no more, nor the children of wickedness waste them any more. Henry notes establishing and securing Israel against enemies was David's fit work as a man of war, while building churches belonged to one never cut out for a soldier. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: And since the time that I commanded judges over my people Israel, I will subdue all thine enemies, and the Lord telleth thee that he will build thee an house. Henry notes the reward for a willing mind prevented by God's act: the Lord will build David a house and annex Israel's crown to it. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: When thy days are expired to go with thy fathers, I will raise up thy seed after thee, and establish his kingdom. Henry notes the work will not fail because David may not do it: God will raise up his seed, and the temple shall be built in its appointed time. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: He shall build me an house, and I will stablish his throne for ever. Henry notes the son's house-building is the temporal shadow of a greater settlement to come. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: I will be his father, and he shall be my son, and I will not take my mercy away from him, as I took it from him that was before thee. Henry contrasts this abiding mercy with Saul, from whom mercy was taken. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: But I will settle him in mine house and in my kingdom for ever, and his throne shall be established for evermore. Henry notes David must look beyond Solomon's unsettled family to the Messiah, whom God will settle in his house and kingdom, priest upon his throne with all power in heaven and earth. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: According to all these words, and according to all this vision, so did Nathan speak unto David. Henry notes Nathan faithfully delivered the whole vision, promise and restraint together. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: Then David went in and sat before the Lord, and said, who am I, O Lord God, and what is mine house, that thou hast brought me hitherto? Henry notes David's humble, believing, fervent prayer: he abases himself, advances God's name, and builds on the promise by faith. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: And yet this was a small thing in thine eyes, O God, for thou hast also spoken of thy servant's house for a great while to come, and hast regarded me according to the estate of a man of high degree. Henry notes God regards believers according to high degree, treating them as persons of distinction, and reads here a glimpse of the Messiah, the Word made flesh seen by faith. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: What can David say more unto thee for the honour of thy servant? For thou knowest thy servant. Henry notes the honour of covenant and communion is so great that servants need not and cannot desire to be more highly honoured. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: O Lord, for thy servant's sake, and according to thine own heart, hast thou done all this greatness, in making known all these great things. Henry notes what Samuel gives as for thy word's sake is here for thy servant's sake: Jesus is both Word and Servant, and for his sake promises are made and made good. — Matthew Henry

20 COMMENTARY: O Lord, there is none like thee, neither is there any God beside thee, according to all that we have heard with our ears. Henry notes David magnifies the God of Israel, advancing his name above all. — Matthew Henry

21 COMMENTARY: And what one nation in the earth is like thy people Israel, whom God went to redeem to be his own people, to make thee a name of greatness and terribleness? Henry notes Israel's redemption as the pattern of God's great deeds for his people. — Matthew Henry

22 COMMENTARY: For thy people Israel didst thou make thine own people for ever, and thou, Lord, becamest their God. Henry notes Israel is God's own people forever, a people whose God answers to the name. — Matthew Henry

23 COMMENTARY: Therefore now, Lord, let the thing that thou hast spoken concerning thy servant and concerning his house be established for ever, and do as thou hast said. Henry notes promises are meant to direct and excite prayer: has God said, I will bless? Let hearts answer, Lord, bless me. — Matthew Henry

24 COMMENTARY: Let it even be established, that thy name may be magnified for ever, saying, the Lord of hosts is the God of Israel, even a God to Israel: and let the house of David be established before thee. Henry notes the God of Israel is not a mere name like Assyria's gods, but a God to Israel, filling the relation and benefiting his people. — Matthew Henry

25 COMMENTARY: For thou, O my God, hast told thy servant that thou wilt build him an house: therefore thy servant hath found in his heart to pray before thee. Henry notes God's revelation of blessing encouraged David to pray, since promises direct prayer. — Matthew Henry

26 COMMENTARY: And now, Lord, thou art God, and hast promised this goodness unto thy servant. Henry notes David builds on God's character: he is God, and what he promises he performs. — Matthew Henry

27 COMMENTARY: Now therefore let it please thee to bless the house of thy servant, that it may be before thee for ever: for thou blessest, O Lord, and it shall be blessed for ever. Henry closes on holy faith, not mere desire: thou blessest, and it shall be blessed forever, for God's blessings cannot be revoked or opposed. — Matthew Henry
