Study Notes — 1 Chronicles

Chapter 26

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Concerning the divisions of the porters: of the Korhites was Meshelemiah the son of Kore, of the sons of Asaph. Henry notes porters guarded all temple avenues, opened and shut gates, directed worshippers in decorum, encouraged the timorous, sent back strangers and unclean, and guarded against thieves. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: For God blessed him. Henry notes Obed-edom had eight sons because God blessed him, and a great blessing it is to have many children able for and eminent in God's service. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: Also unto Shemaiah his son were sons born, that ruled throughout the house of their father: for they were mighty men of valour. Henry notes several porters were mighty men of valour, qualified by strength, courage, and resolution for their post. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: The sons of Shemaiah: Othni, Rephael, Obed, and Elzabad, whose brethren were strong men, Elihu, and Semachiah. Henry notes these brethren were strong men, fit for porter service. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: All these of the sons of Obed-edom: they and their sons and their brethren, able men for strength for the service, were threescore and two of Obed-edom. Henry notes Obed-edom's sixty-two were able men, rewarded for faithfully attending the ark in his own house by attending it in God's house. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: Also Hosah, of the children of Merari, had sons: Simri the chief, for though he was not the firstborn, his father made him the chief. Henry notes Simri was made chief though not firstborn, for excellence or the elder's weakness, in service requiring personal qualifications, not inheritance. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: And they cast lots, as well the small as the great, according to the house of their fathers, for every gate. Henry notes porters, like singers, had posts assigned by lot, so each knew his post and made it good, distributed into about twenty-four companies. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: And the lot eastward fell to Shelemiah. Then for Zechariah his son, a wise counsellor, they cast lots, and his lot came out northward. Henry notes Zechariah was a wise counsellor, who having used the deacon's office well was preferred from gate to council-board. — Matthew Henry

20 COMMENTARY: And of the Levites, Ahijah was over the treasures of the house of God, and over the treasures of the dedicated things. Henry notes a great house needs stores: flour, wine, oil, salt, fuel, lamps, vestments, and utensils, typifying plenty in the Father's house. — Matthew Henry

26 COMMENTARY: Which Shelomith and his brethren were over all the treasures of the dedicated things, which David the king, and the chief fathers, the captains over thousands and hundreds, and the captains of the host, had dedicated. Henry notes treasurers kept treasures from moth, rust, thieves, waste, and embezzlement, and kept accounts of receipts and outlay. — Matthew Henry

27 COMMENTARY: Out of the spoils won in battles did they dedicate to maintain the house of the Lord. Henry notes dedicated treasures came mostly from battle spoils, a grateful acknowledgment of divine protection, reviving the custom of Abraham, Moses's officers, Samuel, and David. — Matthew Henry

28 COMMENTARY: And all that Samuel the seer, and Saul the son of Kish, and Abner the son of Ner, and Joab the son of Zeruiah, had dedicated; and whosoever had dedicated any thing, it was under the hand of Shelomith, and of his brethren. Henry notes Saul, Abner, and Joab also dedicated spoils, and the more God bestows, the more he expects in piety and charity. — Matthew Henry

29 COMMENTARY: Of the Izharites, Chenaniah and his sons were for the outward business over Israel, for officers and judges. Henry notes outward business must not be neglected for the temple: magistracy is God's ordinance for the church's good as truly as ministry, and Levites administered justice with tribal princes. — Matthew Henry

30 COMMENTARY: And of the Hebronites, Hashabiah and his brethren, men of valour, a thousand and seven hundred, were officers among them of Israel on this side Jordan westward in all the business of the Lord, and in the service of the king. Henry notes their charge covered both God's business and the king's: tithes and taxes, offences against God and against public peace, idolatry and injustice. — Matthew Henry

31 COMMENTARY: Among the Hebronites was Jerijah the chief, even among the Hebronites, according to the generations of his fathers. In the fortieth year of the reign of David they were sought for, and there were found among them mighty men of valour at Jazer of Gilead. Henry notes this settlement was made in David's fortieth, last year, urging industry as the day approaches. — Matthew Henry

32 COMMENTARY: And his brethren, men of valour, were two thousand and seven hundred chief fathers, whom king David made rulers over the Reubenites, the Gadites, and the half tribe of Manasseh, for every matter pertaining to God, and affairs of the king. Henry notes more Levite judges served the trans-Jordan tribes, 2,700 against 1,700 west, because frontiers furthest from Jerusalem most needed guarding against idolatry. — Matthew Henry

No note for verse(s) 2, 3, 4, 9, 11, 12, 15, 16, 17, 18, 19, 21, 22, 23, 24, 25 — the public-domain sources consulted give no distinct explanation for these verses beyond the list itself, so nothing was invented for them.
