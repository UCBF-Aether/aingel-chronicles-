Study Notes — 1 Chronicles

Chapter 15

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: David made houses for himself in Jerusalem, and prepared a place for the ark, pitching a tent for it. Henry notes the correction of the first attempt: wherever we build for ourselves, we must make room for God's ark, a church in the house. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: None ought to carry the ark but the Levites, for the Lord chose them to carry it and minister forever. Henry notes David now remembered the express rule: Kohathites bore it on shoulders, with no wagons, and even knowing men may overlook a rule until corrected. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: David gathered all Israel to Jerusalem to bring up the ark to its place. Henry notes he not only summoned Levites as before, but saw that they assembled. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: David assembled the children of Aaron and the Levites. Henry stresses the chiefs especially were summoned, since those advanced in dignity should go before others in duty. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: David called Zadok and Abiathar the priests, and the Levite chiefs Uriel, Asaiah, Joel, Shemaiah, Eliel, and Amminadab. Henry notes the sons of Aaron were called with the chiefs to receive the solemn charge. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: Ye are chief of the fathers of the Levites: sanctify yourselves, that ye may bring up the ark of the Lord God of Israel. Henry notes chiefs bear more expectation, both in service and influence, and their first neglect had cost a breach. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: For because ye did it not at the first, the Lord made a breach upon us, for we sought him not after the due order. Henry makes this the key confession: it is not enough to do good, we must do it well, seeking God in due order, and learn regularity from chastisement. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: The priests and Levites sanctified themselves to bring up the ark. Henry notes the breach on Uzza made them careful to cleanse from ceremonial pollution and compose themselves, so as to strike reverence on the people. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: The Levites bore the ark on their shoulders with staves, as Moses commanded according to the Lord's word. Henry notes many remiss in duty will reform when faithfully told, and the Levites now carried it by law. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: David spake to the chief Levites to appoint their brethren singers with psalteries, harps, and cymbals, sounding aloud with joy. Henry notes officers were appointed to welcome the ark with every expression of joy, proficients nominated for the service. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: The Levites appointed Heman son of Joel, Asaph son of Berechiah, and Ethan son of Kushaiah. Henry notes Heman, Asaph, and Ethan were now first appointed as the three chief singers. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: With them their brethren of the second degree, Zechariah and others, and Obed-edom and Jeiel the porters. Henry notes some were appointed porters, and Obed-edom, the ark's former housekeeper, counted doorkeeping an honour and recompense. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: The singers Heman, Asaph, and Ethan were appointed to sound with cymbals of brass. Henry notes they undertook to sound with symbols, the chief musicians leading the welcome. — Matthew Henry

20 COMMENTARY: Zechariah and others were with psalteries on Alamoth. Henry notes others sang with psalteries, in the concert's order. — Matthew Henry

21 COMMENTARY: Mattithiah and others were with harps on the Sheminith to excel. Henry notes harps on the eighth, eight notes higher or lower, according to the rules of the concert. — Matthew Henry

22 COMMENTARY: Chenaniah, chief of the Levites, was for song, because he was skilful. Henry notes one was appointed for song who could sing well and instruct others: those excelling in a gift should teach others, not grudge making them as wise. — Matthew Henry

23 COMMENTARY: Berechiah and Elkanah were doorkeepers for the ark. Henry lists door-keepers for the ark among the appointed offices. — Matthew Henry

24 COMMENTARY: Shebaniah and other priests blew trumpets before the ark, and Obed-edom and Jehiah were doorkeepers. Henry notes priests blew trumpets as usual at the ark's removal and solemn feasts. — Matthew Henry

25 COMMENTARY: David, Israel's elders, and captains went to bring up the ark from Obed-edom's house with joy. Henry marks this second conveyance as done more successfully: no mistake in the work, and no damp on the joy. — Matthew Henry

26 COMMENTARY: It came to pass, when God helped the Levites that bare the ark, that they offered seven bullocks and seven rams. Henry notes God helped trembling Levites, encouraging them and silencing fears, and they offered sacrifices in atonement for the former error and acknowledgment of help. — Matthew Henry

27 COMMENTARY: David was clothed with a robe of fine linen, and all Levites that bare the ark, and singers, and Chenaniah master of song, and David had a linen ephod. Henry notes great expressions of rejoicing: sacred music, David dancing, singers singing, people shouting. — Matthew Henry

28 COMMENTARY: All Israel brought up the ark with shouting, sound of cornet, trumpets, cymbals, psalteries, and harps. Henry calls times of public reformation times of public rejoicing: those unworthy of the ark are not glad of it. — Matthew Henry

29 COMMENTARY: As the ark came into David's city, Michal daughter of Saul looked out a window, saw David dancing and playing, and despised him in her heart. Henry notes her despising did not make David despicable, nor did the wise and good think worse of him for zealous devotion. — Matthew Henry

No note for verse(s) 5, 6, 7, 8, 9, 10 — the public-domain sources consulted give no distinct explanation for these verses beyond the list itself, so nothing was invented for them.
