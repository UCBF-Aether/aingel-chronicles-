Study Notes — Amos

Chapter 2

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Thus says the Lord: for three transgressions of Moab, and for four, he will not turn away punishment, because he burned Edom's king's bones into lime, cruelty extending even to the dead in hatred and contempt. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: God will send fire upon Moab, devouring Kerioth's palaces, and Moab shall die with tumult, shouting, and trumpet sound, cruelly dealt with because it dealt cruelly. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: He will cut off the judge from Moab's midst and slay all its princes with him, says the Lord, government itself removed. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: Thus says the Lord: for three transgressions of Judah, and for four, he will not turn away punishment, because they despised the Lord's law, kept not his commandments, and their lies, their idols, caused them to err after their fathers' way, other nations being reckoned with for injuries to men, Judah for dishonor done to God. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: God will send fire upon Judah, devouring Jerusalem's palaces, the holy city not spared for its privileges. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: Thus says the Lord: for three transgressions of Israel, and for four, he will not turn away punishment, because they sold the righteous for silver and the poor for a pair of shoes, justice sold for the smallest bribe. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: They pant after dust on the poor's head and turn aside the way of the meek, while father and son go in to the same maid to profane God's holy name, oppression joined to shameless lust. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: They lie down on clothes taken in pledge beside every altar and drink the wine of the condemned in their god's house, worship itself turned into a cover for extortion. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: Yet God destroyed the Amorite before them, tall as cedars and strong as oaks, destroying fruit above and roots beneath, the gift of the land showing God's power on their behalf. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: Also God brought them up from Egypt and led them forty years through the wilderness to possess the Amorite's land, mercies received adding much to the evil of sins committed. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: God raised up their sons for prophets and young men for Nazarites, is it not so, O children of Israel, faithful ministers being great blessings and God himself raising them up. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: But they gave Nazarites wine to drink and commanded prophets, saying, prophesy not, doing what they could to lead believers aside and silence God's messengers. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: Behold, God is pressed under them as a cart pressed full of sheaves, the Lord complaining of his professing people's sins as a burden to him. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: Therefore flight shall perish from the swift, the strong shall not strengthen his force, and the mighty shall not deliver himself, boasted power and resources failing when a people rejects God's word with obstinacy. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: Neither shall the bowman stand, nor the swift of foot deliver himself, nor the horseman deliver himself, every military advantage proving useless. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: And the courageous among the mighty shall flee away naked in that day, says the Lord, so that sinners should humble themselves for ingratitude and unfaithfulness. — Matthew Henry
