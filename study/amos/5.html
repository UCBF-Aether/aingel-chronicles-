Study Notes — Amos

Chapter 5

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Hear this word which the prophet takes up against them, even a lamentation, O house of Israel, the convincing, awakening word needing to be heard as well as words of comfort, since whether heard or not, God's word shall take effect. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: The virgin of Israel is fallen, she shall no more rise, forsaken upon her land with none to raise her up, the nation pictured as a fallen virgin abandoned. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: For thus says the Lord God: the city that went out by a thousand shall leave a hundred, and that by a hundred shall leave ten, to the house of Israel, a remnant of a remnant surviving. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: For thus says the Lord to Israel: seek me, and you shall live, the Lord still proclaiming mercy, though men often expect deliverance from self-invented forms that make condemnation sure. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: But seek not Bethel, nor enter Gilgal, nor pass to Beersheba, for Gilgal shall surely go into captivity and Bethel shall come to nought, the idolatrous sanctuaries themselves doomed, unable to protect their worshippers. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: Seek the Lord, and you shall live, lest he break out like fire in Joseph's house and devour it, with none to quench it in Bethel, since while they refuse to come to Christ for mercy, divine wrath breaks forth. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: You who turn judgment to wormwood and leave off righteousness in the earth are addressed next, justice itself made bitter by corruption. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: Seek him who makes the seven stars and Orion, turns death's shadow into morning, darkens day with night, calls the sea's waters and pours them on the earth: the Lord is his name, the same almighty power able to turn affliction into joy for the repentant. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: He strengthens the spoiled against the strong, so the spoiled shall come against the fortress, God's power reversing human odds. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: They hate him who rebukes in the gate and abhor him who speaks uprightly, evil times not bearing plain dealing. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: Forasmuch as their treading is upon the poor and they take from him burdens of wheat, though they built houses of hewn stone they shall not dwell in them, and though they planted pleasant vineyards they shall not drink their wine, gains from oppression not enjoyed. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: For God knows their manifold transgressions and mighty sins: they afflict the just, take a bribe, and turn aside the poor in the gate from his right, so that judgments that often passed by them shall now pass through them. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: Therefore the prudent shall keep silence in that time, for it is an evil time, wise and good men seeing it vain even to speak to such evil men. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: Seek good and not evil, that you may live, and so the Lord God of hosts shall be with you as you have spoken, those who seek and love good helping to save a land from ruin. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: Hate the evil, love the good, and establish judgment in the gate, that it may be the Lord God of hosts will be gracious to Joseph's remnant, piety and duty attended to by souls that seek God, who is ever ready to be gracious. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: Therefore the Lord God of hosts says: wailing shall be in all streets, and in highways they shall say, alas, alas, calling the husbandman to mourning and skillful lamenters to wailing. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: And in all vineyards shall be wailing, for God will pass through them, says the Lord, judgment reaching every place of work and joy. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: Woe to you who desire the day of the Lord, to what end is it for you, the day of the Lord being darkness and not light, woe to those who long for war and confusion hoping to rise on their country's ruins, since the desolation will profit nobody. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: As if a man fled from a lion and a bear met him, or went into a house, leaned his hand on the wall, and a serpent bit him, so judgments pursue those not reformed by them, escaping one only to meet another. — Matthew Henry

20 COMMENTARY: Shall not the day of the Lord be darkness and not light, even very dark with no brightness in it, since when God makes a day dark, all the world cannot make it light. — Matthew Henry

21 COMMENTARY: God hates and despises their feast days and will not smell their solemn assemblies, a pretense of piety being double iniquity. — Matthew Henry

22 COMMENTARY: Though they offer burnt offerings and meat offerings, God will not accept them, nor regard the peace offerings of fat beasts, sacrifices without communion with God thriving little. — Matthew Henry

23 COMMENTARY: Take away from God the noise of their songs, for he will not hear the melody of their viols, worship's music rejected when the heart is idolatrous. — Matthew Henry

24 COMMENTARY: But let judgment run down as waters and righteousness as a mighty stream, the demand being justice and righteousness in life, not ritual noise. — Matthew Henry

25 COMMENTARY: Have they offered God sacrifices and offerings in the wilderness forty years, O house of Israel, the question exposing that their worship was never truly his alone. — Matthew Henry

26 COMMENTARY: But they bore the tabernacle of Moloch and Chiun their images, the star of their god, which they made for themselves, copying their forefathers' crimes and led captive by Satan into idolatry. — Matthew Henry

27 COMMENTARY: Therefore God will cause them to go into captivity beyond Damascus, says the Lord, whose name is the God of hosts, captivity among idolaters being the fit end of idolatry. — Matthew Henry
