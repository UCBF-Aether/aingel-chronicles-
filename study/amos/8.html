Study Notes — Amos

Chapter 8

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Thus the Lord God showed Amos: behold, a basket of summer fruit, fruit gathered and ready to be eaten, signifying the people ripe for destruction, the year of God's patience drawing to conclusion. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: And God said, Amos, what seest thou, and he said, a basket of summer fruit, then the Lord said, the end is come upon my people Israel, I will not again pass by them any more, summer fruits not keeping till winter but needing use at once, so judgment can no longer be deferred. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: And the temple's songs shall be howlings in that day, says the Lord God, with many dead bodies in every place, cast forth with silence, worship turned to wailing and the dead too many to lament. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: Hear this, you who swallow up the needy, even to make the poor of the land fail, the rich and powerful being most guilty of oppression as well as foremost in idolatry. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: Saying, when will the new moon be gone, that we may sell corn, and the sabbath, that we may set forth wheat, making the ephah small, the shekel great, and falsifying balances by deceit, weary of holy restraints because no common work might be done therein, thinking of marketings even during worship. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: That we may buy the poor for silver and the needy for a pair of shoes, yea, and sell the refuse of the wheat, the poor themselves becoming merchandise and even spoiled grain sold at full price. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: The Lord has sworn by Jacob's excellency, surely I will never forget any of their works, God remembering sin against unjust men, though sinners put off repentance thinking the Lord delays judgments. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: Shall not the land tremble for this, and everyone mourning that dwells therein, rising wholly as a flood, cast out and drowned as by Egypt's flood, terror and desolation coming when little thought of, creature comforts proving uncertain, in the midst of life we are in death. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: And in that day, says the Lord God, I will cause the sun to go down at noon and darken the earth in the clear day, judgment as a noonday eclipse. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: And I will turn their feasts into mourning, all songs into lamentation, bring sackcloth on all loins and baldness on every head, making it as mourning for an only son, the end as a bitter day, wailing following sinful and sensual pleasures. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: Behold, days come, says the Lord God, when I will send a famine in the land, not of bread nor thirst for water, but of hearing the Lord's words, a token of God's highest displeasure and the heaviest judgment, especially in trouble. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: And they shall wander from sea to sea, and from north even to east, running to and fro to seek the Lord's word, and shall not find it, their own plans for salvation standing them in no stead when God visits a backsliding church. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: In that day fair virgins and young men shall faint for thirst, even the most amiable and zealous perishing for want of the water of life which Christ only can bestow. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: They who swear by Samaria's sin, saying, thy god, O Dan, lives, and, the manner of Beersheba lives, even they shall fall and never rise again, idolatrous oaths ending in final fall, a warning to value advantages, profit by them, and fear sinning them away. — Matthew Henry
