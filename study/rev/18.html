Study Notes — Revelation

Chapter 18

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: After these things John saw another angel come down from heaven, having great power, and the earth was lightened with his glory. JFB notes the angel's power is authority, and the earth was illumined owing to it. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: He cried mightily with a strong voice, saying, "Babylon the great is fallen, is fallen, and is become the habitation of devils, and the hold of every foul spirit, and a cage of every unclean and hateful bird." JFB notes the phrase is prophetical of her fall, still future, as the call to come out in verse 4 proves. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: For all nations have drunk of the wine of the wrath of her fornication, and the kings of the earth have committed fornication with her, and the merchants of the earth are waxed rich through the abundance of her delicacies. JFB notes the reference is not to earthly merchandise but to spiritual wares, indulgences, idolatries, and superstitions, wherewith the harlot, the apostate church, has made merchandise of men. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: John heard another voice from heaven, saying, "Come out of her, my people, that ye be not partakers of her sins, and that ye receive not of her plagues." JFB notes even in the Romish church God has a people, but their only safety is coming out at once, as Lot was warned out of Sodom and the first Christians out of Jerusalem before judgment. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: For her sins have reached unto heaven, and God hath remembered her iniquities. JFB notes her sins stand as a great heap, reaching so far as to come into close contact with heaven itself. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: Reward her even as she rewarded you, and double unto her double according to her works: in the cup which she hath filled fill to her double. JFB notes this is addressed to the executioners of God's wrath, and that the double is of sorrow, contrasted with the double of joy Jerusalem shall receive for past suffering. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: How much she hath glorified herself, and lived deliciously, so much torment and sorrow give her: for she saith in her heart, "I sit a queen, and am no widow, and shall see no sorrow." JFB notes her complete unconcerned security as to past, present, and future, since the world power is her husband and supporter, and Rome, like Babylon queen of the East, is queen of the West. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: Therefore shall her plagues come in one day, death, and mourning, and famine; and she shall be utterly burned with fire: for strong is the Lord God who judgeth her. JFB notes death instead of her security, mourning instead of feasting, famine instead of luxurious delicacies, and fire, literal or mystical, symbolizing utter destruction. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: The kings of the earth, who have committed fornication and lived deliciously with her, shall bewail her and lament for her when they see the smoke of her burning. JFB contrasts this with the world's rejoicing over the two witnesses: men relish a religion that gives an opiate to conscience while leaving license to indulge lusts. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: Standing afar off for the fear of her torment, saying, "Alas, alas, that great city Babylon, that mighty city! for in one hour is thy judgment come." JFB notes God's judgments inspire fear even in the worldly, but it is short-lived, for the kings soon attach themselves to the beast in its last and worst shape. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: The merchants of the earth shall weep and mourn over her, for no man buyeth their merchandise any more. JFB notes the merchandise must be spiritual, since Rome was not a commercial city: the harlot did not witness against carnal luxury, the source of the merchants' gains, but conformed to it, caring for the wool, not the sheep. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: The merchandise of gold, silver, precious stones, pearls, fine linen, purple, silk, scarlet, thyine wood, ivory vessels, precious wood, brass, iron, and marble. JFB notes the fine linen manufacture was Egypt's, the type of the apostate church, in contrast to the fine linen God puts on Israel and on the New Testament church, the Bride. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: Cinnamon, odours, ointments, frankincense, wine, oil, fine flour, wheat, beasts, sheep, horses, chariots, slaves, and souls of men. JFB notes cinnamon was designed for the holy anointing oil but desecrated to vile uses, and that the harlot, especially Rome, has enslaved both bodies and souls of men, deriving her greatest gains from masses and indulgences. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: The fruits that thy soul lusted after are departed from thee, and all things which were dainty and goodly are departed from thee, and thou shalt find them no more at all. JFB notes this is a direct address to Babylon, whose autumn-ripe fruits of the soul's eager desire have perished. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: The merchants of these things, which were made rich by her, shall stand afar off for the fear of her torment, weeping and wailing. JFB notes they derived their riches from her, and stand afar off as the kings did, for fear of sharing her torment. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: They say, "Alas, alas, that great city, that was clothed in fine linen, and purple, and scarlet, and decked with gold, and precious stones, and pearls! For in one hour so great riches is come to nought." JFB notes the merchants' lament echoes the harlot's array in chapter 17, now desolated in a single hour. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: Every shipmaster, and all the company in ships, and sailors, and as many as trade by sea, stood afar off. JFB notes vessels were freighted with pilgrims to various shrines, a source of gain to the papal see and to shipmasters and pilots, but mainly the passage refers in the mystical sense to all who share in the spiritual traffic of apostate Christendom. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

18 COMMENTARY: They cried when they saw the smoke of her burning, saying, "What city is like unto this great city!" JFB notes the praise closely resembles that given to the beast in chapter 13, whereas such praise is due to God alone. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

19 COMMENTARY: They cast dust on their heads, and cried, weeping and wailing, saying, "Alas, alas, that great city, wherein were made rich all that had ships in the sea by reason of her costliness! for in one hour is she made desolate." JFB notes the costly treasures, abstract for concrete, enriched all who traded by sea. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

20 COMMENTARY: Rejoice over her, thou heaven, and ye holy apostles and prophets; for God hath avenged you on her. JFB notes there is more joy in heaven at the harlot's downfall than at that of the two beasts, since the most heinous sin is that of those who know God's word of grace and keep it not. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

21 COMMENTARY: A mighty angel took up a stone like a great millstone, and cast it into the sea, saying, "Thus with violence shall that great city Babylon be thrown down, and shall be found no more at all." JFB compares the judgment on Egypt's hosts at the Red Sea and the foretold doom of Babylon in Jeremiah, noting the verse shows the prophecy is still to be fulfilled. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

22 COMMENTARY: The voice of harpers, musicians, pipers, and trumpeters shall be heard no more at all in thee, and no craftsman of whatsoever craft shall be found any more in thee, and the sound of a millstone shall be heard no more at all in thee. JFB notes musicians, painters, and sculptors desecrated their art to lend fascination to the sensuous worship of corrupt Christendom. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

23 COMMENTARY: The light of a candle shall shine no more at all in thee, and the voice of the bridegroom and of the bride shall be heard no more at all in thee: for thy merchants were the great men of the earth; for by thy sorceries were all nations deceived. JFB contrasts this with the city of God, where they need no candle because the Lord God gives them light, and the Bridegroom and Bride are present at the marriage supper. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

24 COMMENTARY: In her was found the blood of prophets, and of saints, and of all that were slain upon the earth. JFB notes this proves Babylon means not merely literal Rome but the whole faithless church of both testaments, and gives three reasons for her downfall: the worldly greatness of her merchants, her sorceries, and her persecution of prophets and saints. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
