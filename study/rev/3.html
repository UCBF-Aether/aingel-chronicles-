Study Notes — Revelation

Chapter 3

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: The message to Sardis comes from him who has the seven Spirits of God and the seven stars. Henry notes Christ holds the Spirit without measure for a languishing church to apply to for revival, and holds ministers accountable to himself, since the Spirit usually works by the ministry and the ministry is nothing without the Spirit. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: Sardis is told to be watchful and strengthen the things that remain and are ready to die, for Christ has not found their works perfect before God. Henry traces their deadness to a dropped watch, and their works as shells without kernels: prayers unfilled with holy desire, alms without true charity, sabbaths without devotion of soul. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: They must remember how they received and heard, hold fast, and repent, or Christ will come on them as a thief in an hour they do not know. Henry stresses remembering not only what they received but how, with what first affections, and warns that a dead people's security keeps them from discerning Christ's judicial visit until it strips their remaining mercies. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: Yet Sardis has a few names who have not defiled their garments, and they shall walk with Christ in white, for they are worthy. Henry notes God marks the smallest faithful remnant, and the white robes are those of justification, adoption, and glory, a suitable reward for keeping unspotted from the world. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: The overcomer shall be clothed in white raiment, and Christ will not blot his name out of the book of life, but confess his name before his Father and his angels. Henry explains the book as the register of eternal election and remembrance, and notes church rolls may lose mere names, but the faithful overcome shall never be blotted out. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: He who has an ear is to hear what the Spirit says to the churches. Henry notes every word from God deserves attention, and what seems directed to one body instructs all. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: The message to Philadelphia comes from him who is holy, who is true, and who has the key of David, opening and no man shutting, shutting and no man opening. Henry notes holiness makes Christ true to his word, and the key is absolute sovereignty over the church's government: doors of opportunity, utterance, entrance, and heaven itself. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: Christ has set before Philadelphia an open door no man can shut, and commends them: though they have a little strength, they have kept his word and not denied his name. Henry notes the open door is Christ's own gift, kept open despite adversaries, and that weak true grace, though it calls for growth, still keeps Christ's word and confesses his name. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: Christ will make those of the synagogue of Satan, who say they are Jews and are not, come and worship before Philadelphia's feet, and know that Christ has loved her. Henry explains the subjection is not divine honour paid to the church but conviction: enemies forced to acknowledge the church is right and beloved, and desiring communion with her. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: Because Philadelphia kept the word of Christ's patience, he will keep them from the hour of temptation coming on all the world to try earth's dwellers. Henry notes the gospel is the word of God's patience and Christ's exemplary patience, and that those who keep it in peace are prepared by the same grace to be faithful in persecution. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: Christ comes quickly, so they must hold fast what they have, that no man take their crown. Henry urges perseverance: the crown once hoped for can be lost by backsliding professors, while the persevering Christian wins the prize. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: The overcomer shall be made a pillar in the temple of God, never to go out, with the name of God, the name of the city of God, the new Jerusalem, and Christ's new name written upon him. Henry explains the pillar is a monument of free and powerful grace, never defaced, inscribed with the believer's service to the church and the Captain under whose banner he fought. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: He who has an ear is to hear what the Spirit says to the churches, how Christ loves, values, commends, and will crown his faithful people. Henry closes the Philadelphia letter on Christ's love for fidelity. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: The message to Laodicea comes from the Amen, the faithful and true witness, the beginning of the creation of God. Henry notes Christ is steady and unchangeable in purposes and promises, a swift true witness against lukewarm professors, and the first cause of the first creation and head of the second, the church. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: Christ knows Laodicea's works: they are neither cold nor hot, and he wishes they were cold or hot. Henry calls lukewarmness the worst temper in the world: if religion is real it deserves all earnestness, and if not, earnest opposition, leaving no room for neutrality between God and Baal. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: Because they are lukewarm, Christ will spue them out of his mouth. Henry compares lukewarm water turning the stomach: lukewarm professors turn Christ's heart against them, and what they call charity or moderation is nauseous to him, ending in final rejection. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: Laodicea says, "I am rich, and increased with goods, and have need of nothing," yet Christ says they are wretched, miserable, poor, blind, and naked. Henry notes the self-deception: learning taken for religion, gifts for grace, wit for wisdom, ordinances for the God of ordinances, while their souls starved in abundance, blind to their state and naked of justification and sanctification. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: Christ counsels them to buy from him gold tried in the fire to be rich, white raiment to cover their nakedness, and eye-salve to see. Henry explains the purchase is without money and without price, parting only with sin and self-sufficiency, receiving Christ's imputed righteousness and holiness, and resigning blind human wisdom to his word and Spirit. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: As many as Christ loves, he rebukes and chastens, so Laodicea is to be zealous and repent. Henry notes rebuke is a token of love: had Christ hated them he would have left them to ruin, and sinners should take the wounds of a friend over the flattering smiles of an enemy. — Matthew Henry

20 COMMENTARY: Behold, Christ stands at the door and knocks; if anyone hears his voice and opens, he will come in and sup with him, and he with Christ. Henry pictures Christ coming by word and Spirit to a heart shut by ignorance, unbelief, and prejudice, waiting patiently, and bringing the best part of the entertainment himself, so communion is his gift as well as theirs. — Matthew Henry

21 COMMENTARY: To the overcomer Christ will grant to sit with him on his throne, as he overcame and sat down with his Father on his throne. Henry notes Christ met temptations and overcame them all, reassuming the glory he had veiled on earth, and that those conformed to him in trials and victories shall be conformed to him in glory and judgment. — Matthew Henry

22 COMMENTARY: He who has an ear is to hear what the Spirit says to the churches. Henry closes the seven letters by noting they are patterns to all ages: succeeding churches resembling these in graces or sins may expect God to deal with them as he dealt with these. — Matthew Henry
