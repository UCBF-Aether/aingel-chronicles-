Study Notes — Revelation

Chapter 16

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: John heard a great voice out of the temple saying to the seven angels, "Go your ways, and pour out the vials of the wrath of God upon the earth." Henry notes nothing was executed without an immediate positive order from God, answering his people's prayers and avenging their quarrel, and that the angels obeyed at once with no delay or objection. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: The first angel poured out his vial upon the earth, and there fell a noisome and grievous sore upon the men who had the mark of the beast and worshipped his image. Henry notes they had marked themselves by their sin, and now God marks them out by his judgments, the sore discovering their inward distemper as a token of further evil. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: The second angel poured out his vial upon the sea, and it became as the blood of a dead man, and every living soul died in the sea. Henry notes the sea figures the papacy's jurisdiction and whole system of false doctrines, corrupt glosses, superstitious rites, and idolatrous worship, whose pretended means of salvation poisoned men's souls. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: The third angel poured out his vial upon the rivers and fountains of waters, and they became blood. Henry notes the rivers figure the antichristian emissaries, especially the Jesuits, who like streams conveyed the venom of errors and idolatries from the spring-head through the earth. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: The angel of the waters said, "Thou art righteous, O Lord, which art, and wast, and shalt be, because thou hast judged thus." Henry notes the angel extols God's righteousness in this retaliation, as the instrument God used in the work. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: For they have shed the blood of saints and prophets, and thou hast given them blood to drink; for they are worthy. Henry notes the following doxology favours reading this as Christian princes taking just revenge upon those who had occasioned the shedding of the blood of armies and martyrs. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: Another angel out of the altar said, "Even so, Lord God Almighty, true and righteous are thy judgments." Henry notes the second angel answers the angel of the waters by full consent. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: The fourth angel poured out his vial upon the sun, and power was given unto him to scorch men with fire. Henry notes the sun figures some eminent prince of the popish communion who should renounce the false religion shortly before its downfall, so that the sun which once cherished them now grows hot against the idolaters. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: Men were scorched with great heat, and blasphemed the name of God, who has power over these plagues, and they repented not to give him glory. Henry notes princes using their power to suppress the idolaters would so far fail to bring them to repentance that they would curse God and their king, hardened to their ruin. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: The fifth angel poured out his vial upon the seat of the beast, and his kingdom was full of darkness, and they gnawed their tongues for pain. Henry notes Rome itself, the mystical Babylon and head of the antichristian empire, once the seat of policy, learning, pomp, and pleasure, now becomes a source of darkness, pain, and anguish. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: They blasphemed the God of heaven because of their pains and their sores, and repented not of their deeds. Henry notes darkness forebodes the contempt, confusion, and anguish to which the antichristian interest should be exposed. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: The sixth angel poured out his vial upon the great river Euphrates, and its water was dried up, that the way of the kings of the east might be prepared. Henry notes the drying river, which furnished the city with wealth and provisions, removes the hindrance Rome's idolatry posed to the conversion of Jews and Gentiles. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: John saw three unclean spirits like frogs come out of the mouth of the dragon, out of the mouth of the beast, and out of the mouth of the false prophet. Henry notes hell, the secular power of antichrist, and the ecclesiastical power combine to send instruments furnished with hellish malice, worldly policy, and religious falsehood. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: For they are the spirits of devils, working miracles, which go forth unto the kings of the earth and of the whole world, to gather them to the battle of that great day of God Almighty. Henry notes the old stratagem of lying wonders, revived before antichrist's fall, would amuse and deceive the world into mustering the devil's forces for a decisive battle. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: Behold, I come as a thief. Blessed is he that watches and keeps his garments, lest he walk naked and they see his shame. Henry notes the warning: the trial would be sudden and unexpected, so Christians should be clothed, armed, and ready, that they might not be surprised and ashamed. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: He gathered them together into a place called in the Hebrew tongue Armageddon. Henry notes the mount of Megiddo, famous both for Barak's victory over Sisera and for Josiah's death, shall now be the field of the last battle in which the church shall be engaged, and she shall be victorious. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: The seventh angel poured out his vial into the air, and there came a great voice out of the temple of heaven, from the throne, saying, "It is done." Henry notes the plague fell on the prince of the power of the air: his powers restrained, his policies confounded, and the church triumphant rejoiced that the work was done. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: There were voices, and thunders, and lightnings, and a great earthquake, such as was not since men were upon the earth, so mighty an earthquake and so great. Henry notes the mighty commotion shook the very centre, ushered in by thunder and lightnings, as the finishing stroke of Babylon's downfall. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: The great city was divided into three parts, the cities of the nations fell, and great Babylon came in remembrance before God, to give unto her the cup of the wine of the fierceness of his wrath. Henry notes Babylon, having taken in something of Jewish, pagan, and Christian religion, was as three cities in one, and now God, who seemed to have forgotten her idolatry and cruelty, gives her the cup of his wrath. — Matthew Henry

20 COMMENTARY: Every island fled away, and the mountains were not found. Henry notes the downfall reached from the centre to the circumference, carrying away even the places that seemed by nature and situation most secured. — Matthew Henry

21 COMMENTARY: There fell upon men a great hail out of heaven, every stone about the weight of a talent, and men blasphemed God because of the plague of the hail, for the plague thereof was exceeding great. Henry notes the dreadful plague of the heart: the greatest calamities will not bring men to repentance without God's grace, and those not made better by his judgments are always the worse for them. — Matthew Henry
