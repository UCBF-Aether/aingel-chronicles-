Study Notes — Revelation

Chapter 11

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: John was given a reed like a rod and told, "Rise, and measure the temple of God, and the altar, and them that worship therein." Henry notes the measuring, after Ezekiel's pattern, may be for preservation in danger, for trial against the true model, or for reformation of what is redundant, deficient, or changed. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: The court outside the temple was to be left out and not measured, for it was given to the Gentiles, and the holy city would be trodden under foot forty-two months. Henry explains the outer court was no part of the temple by Solomon's or Zerubbabel's model, so God abandoned it to pagan ceremonies, while the desolations of the church are limited in time and she shall be delivered. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: Christ will give power to his two witnesses, who shall prophesy a thousand two hundred and threescore days, clothed in sackcloth. Henry notes the number is small yet sufficient, since every cause is established by two witnesses, and that they are God's eminent faithful ministers preaching in the worst of times, deeply affected by the church's distress. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: These are the two olive trees and the two candlesticks standing before the God of the earth. Henry compares them to Zerubbabel and Joshua in Zechariah's vision: God gave them the oil of holy zeal, courage, strength, and comfort, so their lamps of profession burned by inward gracious principles. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: If anyone would hurt them, fire proceeds out of their mouth and devours their enemies, and whoever would hurt them must in this manner be killed. Henry notes God's words in the prophets' mouths are fire devouring enemies as wood, and that persecutors are galled and self-condemned by faithful preaching, praying, and suffering. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: They have power to shut heaven so it does not rain in the days of their prophecy, power over waters to turn them to blood, and power to smite the earth with all plagues as often as they will. Henry compares the shut heaven to Elijah's prayer and the bloodied waters to Pharaoh's plagues, showing God inflicts judgments on enemies at his witnesses' prayers. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: When they have finished their testimony, the beast ascending out of the bottomless pit shall make war against them, overcome them, and kill them. Henry notes they are immortal and invulnerable until their work is done, and that antichrist would prevail against them for a time by subtle learning and chiefly by open force. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: Their dead bodies shall lie in the street of the great city, spiritually called Sodom and Egypt, where also our Lord was crucified. Henry notes the malice pursued even their corpses, denied a quiet grave in Babylon's high street, where Christ in his mystical body suffered more than anywhere. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: People, kindreds, tongues, and nations shall see their dead bodies three days and a half, and not suffer their bodies to be put in graves. Henry treats the short exposure as part of the enemies' insult, contrasting with the long prophecy that preceded it. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: Those dwelling on the earth shall rejoice over them, make merry, and send gifts to one another, because these two prophets tormented those who dwelt on the earth. Henry notes the world's gladness at being rid of witnesses whose doctrine and example teased, terrified, and tormented guilty consciences. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: After three days and a half the Spirit of life from God entered into them, and they stood on their feet, and great fear fell on those who saw them. Henry notes God's witnesses may be slain but shall rise again in their successors, as God revives his work when it seems dead, putting life and courage into them. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: They heard a great voice from heaven saying, "Come up hither," and ascended up to heaven in a cloud, while their enemies beheld them. Henry notes the witnesses did not grasp at advancement until God called them, and that persecutors seeing the faithful honoured is itself part of their punishment. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: In the same hour there was a great earthquake, a tenth part of the city fell, seven thousand were slain, and the remnant were affrighted and gave glory to the God of heaven. Henry refers this to the Reformation, when princes and states fell from Rome, many were slain by war, and others, convicted by the Spirit's sword, repented and glorified God. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: The second woe is past, and behold, the third woe comes quickly. Henry notes the seventh trumpet had been suspended until John was acquainted with these intervening occurrences of great moment. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: The seventh angel sounded, and great voices in heaven said, "The kingdoms of this world are become the kingdoms of our Lord, and of his Christ; and he shall reign for ever and ever." Henry notes the kingdoms were always his by creation and purchase in title, and now title turns into possession, a reign that shall never end. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: The four and twenty elders who sat before God on their seats fell on their faces and worshipped God. Henry notes the manner of their adoration: they rose from their seats and fell on their faces with reverence and humility. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: They said, "We give thee thanks, O Lord God Almighty, which art, and wast, and art to come; because thou hast taken to thee thy great power, and hast reigned." Henry notes they thank God for asserting his rights and exerting his power, turning title into actual possession. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: The nations were angry, and God's wrath is come, with the time of the dead to be judged, of rewarding his servants the prophets, saints, and those who fear his name, small and great, and of destroying those who destroy the earth. Henry notes the nations met God's wrath with their own anger, fretting against the just rewarding of his people and increasing their guilt. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: The temple of God was opened in heaven, and the ark of his testament was seen in his temple, with lightnings, voices, thunderings, an earthquake, and great hail. Henry notes the opened temple means freer communication between heaven and earth, and the ark, long locked away under antichrist, now brought to view as a token of God's returned presence and favour in Christ the propitiation. — Matthew Henry
