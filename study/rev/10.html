Study Notes — Revelation

Chapter 10

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: John saw another mighty angel come down from heaven, clothed with a cloud, a rainbow on his head, his face as the sun, and his feet as pillars of fire. Henry notes the figure seems none other than Christ: veiled in cloud, mindful of his covenant by the rainbow, bright as the sun, with ways pure and steady as pillars of fire. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: He had in his hand a little book open, and set his right foot on the sea and his left foot on the earth. Henry reads the stance as absolute dominion over the world, and the open little book as the formerly sealed book now opened and gradually fulfilled by him. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: He cried with a loud voice, as when a lion roars, and when he cried, seven thunders uttered their voices. Henry notes the awful voice was echoed by seven solemn and terrible ways of discovering God's mind. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: When the seven thunders had uttered their voices, John was about to write, but a voice from heaven said, "Seal up those things which the seven thunders uttered, and write them not." Henry notes the apostle would have published everything he saw and heard, but the time had not yet come. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: The angel who stood on the sea and on the earth lifted up his hand to heaven. Henry notes the manner of the oath: lifted hand, sworn by him who lives for ever, by God as God, to whom the angel as Lord, Redeemer, and ruler appeals. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: He swore by him who lives for ever and ever, who created heaven and its contents, earth and its contents, and the sea and its contents, that there should be time no longer. Henry gives the oath two senses: no longer delay in fulfilling the predictions once the last angel sounds, and, when the mystery is finished, time itself swallowed up in eternity. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: But in the days of the voice of the seventh angel, when he shall begin to sound, the mystery of God should be finished, as he declared to his servants the prophets. Henry notes this is the matter of the oath: speedy execution of everything, until the whole mystery of God's counsel is finished in the world. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: The voice from heaven told John to go and take the little book open in the hand of the angel standing on the sea and on the earth. Henry notes the charge came from the same heavenly voice that had forbidden writing the thunders, showing John's commission was directed step by step. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: John went to the angel and said, "Give me the little book," and the angel said, "Take it, and eat it up; and it shall make thy belly bitter, but it shall be in thy mouth sweet as honey." Henry explains the charge: before publishing, John must thoroughly digest the predictions and be suitably affected by them himself. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: John took the little book from the angel's hand and ate it up, and it was sweet as honey in his mouth, but his belly became bitter. Henry notes the twofold relish: pleasure in receiving a word from God and foresight of the future, then bitterness as the awful persecutions and desolations were digested, as with Ezekiel's roll. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: The angel said to John, "Thou must prophesy again before many peoples, and nations, and tongues, and kings." Henry notes the book was not given merely to gratify curiosity or to please or pain him, but to be communicated: his prophetical commission was renewed for an embassy to the world and its greatest men. — Matthew Henry
