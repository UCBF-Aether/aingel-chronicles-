Study Notes — Revelation

Chapter 19

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: After these things John heard a great voice of much people in heaven, saying, "Alleluia; Salvation, and glory, and honour, and power, unto the Lord our God." JFB notes this song of praise at the great closing event, the judgment on the harlot, contrasts with the lamentations of chapter 18, and that Alleluia, "Praise ye JAH," implies God experienced as a present help. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: For true and righteous are his judgments: for he hath judged the great whore, which did corrupt the earth with her fornication, and hath avenged the blood of his servants at her hand. JFB notes she continually corrupted the earth, promoting its sinful decay by her own earthliness, and that God exacted in retribution the blood shed by the apostate churches. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: And again they said, "Alleluia. And her smoke rose up for ever and ever." JFB notes the second Alleluia, with smoke going up to the ages of the ages, seals the permanence of Babylon's judgment. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: The four and twenty elders and the four beasts fell down and worshipped God that sat on the throne, saying, "Amen; Alleluia." JFB notes the living creatures and elders join the multitude's thanksgiving at the harlot's judgment. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: A voice came out of the throne, saying, "Praise our God, all ye his servants, and ye that fear him, both small and great." JFB compares this solemn act of praise to the Levites' praise when the house of God was filled with divine glory. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: John heard as it were the voice of a great multitude, and as the voice of many waters, and as the voice of mighty thunderings, saying, "Alleluia: for the Lord God omnipotent reigneth." JFB notes the many waters here contrast with the many waters on which the whore sat, and that her overthrow clears the way for his advent to reign. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: Let us be glad and rejoice, and give honour to him: for the marriage of the Lamb is come, and his wife hath made herself ready. JFB notes the marriage symbol includes perfect union with him personally and participation in his holiness, joy, glory, and kingdom, and that the bride gives her affections exclusively to Christ, unlike the harlot who divided hers among many lovers. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: To her was granted that she should be arrayed in fine linen, clean and white: for the fine linen is the righteousness of saints. JFB notes it is her Lord who makes her ready by granting the array, sanctifying her with the washing of water by the word, and that the righteousness is imputed, each saint's robes made white in the blood of the Lamb. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: The angel said to John, "Write, Blessed are they which are called unto the marriage supper of the Lamb. And he saith unto me, These are the true sayings of God." JFB notes the called are effectually called into partaking, not merely invited, and that the sayings are genuine and shall surely be fulfilled. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: John fell at the angel's feet to worship him, but he said, "See thou do it not: I am thy fellowservant, and of thy brethren that have the testimony of Jesus: worship God: for the testimony of Jesus is the spirit of prophecy." JFB notes John's impulse was adoring joy at the blessed prospect, yet angels and apostles alike bear testimony concerning Jesus by one Spirit, so the angel is a fellow servant, not a lord to be worshipped. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: John saw heaven opened, and a white horse, and he that sat upon him was called Faithful and True, and in righteousness he doth judge and make war. JFB notes he is identical with the rider of chapter 6, coming forth conquering and to conquer, and that at the highest point of the world power's security and God's people's distress, the Lord shall appear visibly to end the world's course and establish his kingdom of glory. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: His eyes were as a flame of fire, and on his head were many crowns, and he had a name written, that no man knew, but he himself. JFB notes the many diadems are royal crowns as King of Kings, comprising all earthly and heavenly diadems, and that his unknown name can only be understood when the saints' union with him and joint triumph are perfectly manifested. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: He was clothed with a vesture dipped in blood, and his name is called The Word of God. JFB notes the blood is not his own but his foes', a premonition of their blood shed in righteous retribution, and that "the Word of God" indicates his incommunicable Godhead joined to his manhood, manifested in glory. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: The armies which were in heaven followed him upon white horses, clothed in fine linen, white and clean. JFB notes these are the glorified saints whom God will bring with Christ at his advent, the called, chosen, and faithful of chapter 17, together with his mighty angels. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: Out of his mouth goes a sharp sword, that with it he should smite the nations, and he shall rule them with a rod of iron, and he treads the winepress of the fierceness and wrath of Almighty God. JFB notes the sword acts here in avenging power, consuming with the Spirit of his mouth, and that he who would have shepherded rebels with pastoral love shall dash them in pieces with a rod of iron. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: He hath on his vesture and on his thigh a name written, KING OF KINGS, AND LORD OF LORDS. JFB notes the name was written partly on the vesture, partly on the thigh, where the robe drops in an equestrian figure, the thigh symbolizing his humanity from the loins of David, now appearing as the glorified Son of man. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: John saw an angel standing in the sun, who cried with a loud voice to all the fowls that fly in the midst of heaven, "Come and gather yourselves together unto the supper of the great God." JFB notes the angel stands in the sun so as to be conspicuous in sight of the whole world. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

18 COMMENTARY: That ye may eat the flesh of kings, and the flesh of captains, and the flesh of mighty men, and the flesh of horses, and of them that sit on them, and the flesh of all men, both free and bond, both small and great. JFB contrasts this supper with the marriage supper of the Lamb, and notes giving their flesh to the fowls is righteous retribution for not suffering the dead bodies of Christ's witnesses to be put in graves. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

19 COMMENTARY: John saw the beast, and the kings of the earth, and their armies, gathered together to make war against him that sat on the horse, and against his army. JFB notes they gather at Armageddon under the sixth vial, for the war foretold in chapters 16 and 17. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

20 COMMENTARY: The beast was taken, and with him the false prophet that wrought miracles before him, with which he deceived them that had received the mark of the beast and worshipped his image. These both were cast alive into a lake of fire burning with brimstone. JFB notes the second beast is identical with the false prophet, the spiritual power of ideas serving the worldly antichristian power, and that their living death is no mere annihilation. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

21 COMMENTARY: The remnant were slain with the sword of him that sat upon the horse, which sword proceeded out of his mouth, and all the fowls were filled with their flesh. JFB notes the remnant is the kings and their armies classed together in one indiscriminate mass, a solemn confirmation of the warning in Psalm 2. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
