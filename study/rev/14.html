Study Notes — Revelation

Chapter 14

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: John looked, and the Lamb stood on Mount Sion with a hundred forty-four thousand, having his Father's name written in their foreheads. JFB notes the Lamb has left his position in the midst of the throne to stand on Sion, and that God's and Christ's name answers to the seal upon their foreheads in chapter 7, the 144,000 being the first-fruits of Israel. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: John heard a voice from heaven as the voice of many waters and as great thunder, and the voice of harpers harping with their harps. JFB notes the voice of his people is like his own voice, and that the harpers and singers evidently include the 144,000. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: They sang as it were a new song before the throne, before the four beasts and the elders, which no man could learn but the hundred forty-four thousand redeemed from the earth. JFB notes the song is new because such a conflict with the dragon, beast, and false prophet had never been fought before, and that not even angels can learn it, since they have not come out of great tribulation washed white in the Lamb's blood. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: These are they who were not defiled with women, for they are virgins, who follow the Lamb wherever he goes, redeemed from among men as first-fruits to God and to the Lamb. JFB reads their virginity spiritually, in contrast to apostate Babylon the harlot, and their following as the fitting reward of following him fully on earth. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: In their mouth was found no guile, for they are without fault before the throne of God. JFB notes they are blameless in respect to the sincerity of their fidelity to him, regarded as such on the ground of his righteousness, not absolutely blameless in themselves. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: John saw another angel fly in the midst of heaven, having the everlasting gospel to preach to those dwelling on the earth, to every nation, kindred, tongue, and people. JFB notes this portion concerns the Gentile world: before the end the gospel is preached for a witness to all nations, giving them a last season of grace to decide for or against Christ before judgment descends. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: The angel said with a loud voice, "Fear God, and give glory to him; for the hour of his judgment is come: and worship him that made heaven, and earth, and the sea, and the fountains of waters." JFB notes fear of God is the forerunner to embracing his love, and that the judgment here is not the general judgment but that upon Babylon, the beast, and his worshippers. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: Another angel followed, saying, "Babylon is fallen, is fallen, that great city, because she made all nations drink of the wine of the wrath of her fornication." JFB notes Babylon, first mentioned here, is the harlot, the apostate church, distinct from the beast and judged separately, and that as she made nations drunk with her fornication, so she shall be made drunk with the wine of God's wrath. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: A third angel followed, warning that if any man worship the beast and his image and receive his mark in his forehead or in his hand, he faces the doom the following verses describe. JFB heads this section the doom of the beast worshippers, proclaimed by the third angel, in contrast to the gospel and Babylon's fall announced by the first two. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: The same shall drink of the wine of the wrath of God, poured out without mixture into the cup of his indignation, and shall be tormented with fire and brimstone in the presence of the holy angels and of the Lamb. JFB notes the wine is undiluted, with no drop of water to cool its heat and no grace or hope blended with it. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: The smoke of their torment ascends up for ever and ever, and they have no rest day nor night, who worship the beast and his image and receive the mark of his name. JFB contrasts this with the living creatures, who rest not day and night in praise yet rest from sin, sorrow, weariness, and temptation, while the lost have no rest from sin, Satan, terror, torment, and remorse. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: Here is the patience of the saints: here are they that keep the commandments of God and the faith of Jesus. JFB notes that in the fiery ordeal awaiting all who will not worship the beast, the faith and patient, persevering endurance of God's followers shall be put to the test and proved. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: A voice from heaven said, "Write, Blessed are the dead which die in the Lord from henceforth: Yea, saith the Spirit, that they may rest from their labours; and their works do follow them." JFB notes their full blessedness is now from this time, since the judgment on the beast and the harvest gatherings are imminent, and their works follow with them as proof that they are Christ's. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: John looked, and a white cloud, and upon the cloud one sat like unto the Son of man, having on his head a golden crown and in his hand a sharp sickle. JFB notes the crown is a garland of victory, not a kingly diadem, and that the victory is described in detail in chapter 19. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: Another angel out of the temple cried to him on the cloud, "Thrust in thy sickle, and reap: for the time is come for thee to reap; for the harvest of the earth is ripe." JFB notes the angel does not command the Son of man but merely announces the Father's will, in whose hands the times and seasons are kept, and that by the harvest the elect righteous are gathered out. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: He that sat on the cloud thrust in his sickle on the earth, and the earth was reaped. JFB notes the Son himself, with a golden crown, gathers the elect in the harvest, while a mere angel conducts the vintage that follows. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: Another angel came out of the temple which is in heaven, he also having a sharp sickle. JFB connects this temple with the temple opened in heaven in chapter 11, marking the vintage as a distinct act of judgment after the harvest. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

18 COMMENTARY: Another angel came out from the altar, having power over fire, and cried to him with the sharp sickle, "Thrust in thy sharp sickle, and gather the clusters of the vine of the earth; for her grapes are fully ripe." JFB notes the angel comes from the altar of the saints' incense-accompanied prayers, which bring down God's fiery judgment, and that the grapes are ripe for punishment. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

19 COMMENTARY: The angel thrust in his sickle into the earth, gathered the vine of the earth, and cast it into the great winepress of the wrath of God. JFB notes the vine is judged because its grapes are wild grapes, not what God looked for after careful culture, and that the object is the apostate world of Christendom, not the heathen world that has not heard of Christ. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

20 COMMENTARY: The winepress was trodden without the city, and blood came out of the winepress, even unto the horse bridles, by the space of a thousand and six hundred furlongs. JFB notes the scene of Christ's and his people's blood-shedding shall also be the scene of God's vengeance, and that the sixteen hundred furlongs signify the completeness and universality of the destruction. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
