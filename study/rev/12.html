Study Notes — Revelation

Chapter 12

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: There appeared a great wonder in heaven: a woman clothed with the sun, with the moon under her feet, and on her head a crown of twelve stars. Henry reads the woman as the church, spouse of Christ and mother of saints, clothed in Christ's imputed righteousness as the Sun of righteousness, standing above the world, and crowned with the gospel doctrine preached by the twelve apostles. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: Being with child, she cried, travailing in birth, and pained to be delivered. Henry notes the church's travail: desiring that conviction begun in sinners would end in conversion, and that when children were brought to the birth there might be strength to bring forth. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: Another wonder appeared: a great red dragon, having seven heads and ten horns, and seven crowns upon his heads. Henry reads the dragon as Satan in strength and cruel fierceness, the seven heads as Rome on seven hills, the ten horns as the empire's ten provinces, and the seven crowns as seven kings. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: His tail drew the third part of the stars of heaven and cast them to the earth, and the dragon stood before the woman ready to devour her child as soon as it was born. Henry notes the dragon turned ministers and professors out of their places and stood vigilant to crush Christianity in its birth and prevent its growth. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: She brought forth a man child, who was to rule all nations with a rod of iron, and her child was caught up unto God and to his throne. Henry takes the child as a race of true believers, strong and united, resembling Christ and ruling by doctrine and life now and as assessors with Christ at the great day, taken into God's special, powerful protection. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: The woman fled into the wilderness, where she has a place prepared of God, that they should feed her there a thousand two hundred and threescore days. Henry notes the church's obscure, dispersed state proved her security through divine providence, though only for a limited time. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: There was war in heaven: Michael and his angels fought against the dragon, and the dragon fought and his angels. Henry notes heaven espouses the church's quarrel: Christ, the great Angel of the covenant, with his faithful followers, against Satan and all his instruments, superior in outward strength but not in captain. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: The dragon and his angels prevailed not, neither was their place found any more in heaven. Henry notes the victory fell to Christ and his church, and pagan idolatry, a worshipping of devils, was extirpated from the empire in Constantine's time. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: The great dragon was cast out, that old serpent, called the Devil and Satan, who deceives the whole world, cast out into the earth, and his angels cast out with him. Henry notes the cast-out is total: conquered and expelled from heaven's church, with his angels. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: A loud voice in heaven said, "Now is come salvation, and strength, and the kingdom of our God, and the power of his Christ: for the accuser of our brethren is cast down, which accused them before our God day and night." Henry notes the accuser, like against Job and Joshua, brought indictments continually, but now the indictments are quashed and the accuser turned out of court. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: They overcame him by the blood of the Lamb, and by the word of their testimony, and they loved not their lives unto the death. Henry notes the threefold victory: the Lamb's blood as meritorious cause, the gospel testimony as the Spirit's sword, and courage in suffering, since love of life was overcome by loyalty to Christ. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: Therefore rejoice, you heavens and you who dwell in them, but woe to the inhabiters of the earth and of the sea, for the devil is come down to you, having great wrath, because he knows he has but a short time. Henry notes Satan's rage grows greater as he is limited in place and time, and being defeated against the church, he disturbs the world in general. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: When the dragon saw he was cast to the earth, he persecuted the woman who brought forth the man child. Henry notes the church's obscurity could not altogether protect her, as the old serpent followed her into the wilderness. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: To the woman were given two wings of a great eagle, that she might fly into the wilderness, into her place, where she is nourished for a time, and times, and half a time, from the face of the serpent. Henry notes God conveyed her as on eagles' wings to a place of safety for a certain space, in prophetic characters from Daniel. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: The serpent cast out of his mouth water as a flood after the woman, that he might cause her to be carried away by the flood. Henry reads the flood as error and heresy breathed by Arius, Nestorius, Pelagius, and many more, by which the church was in danger of being overwhelmed. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: The earth helped the woman, opened her mouth, and swallowed up the flood which the dragon cast out of his mouth. Henry suggests the Gothic and Vandal invasions gave the Arian rulers other work, so the flood was swallowed up and the church enjoyed respite, since God often sends the sword to avenge his covenant's quarrel. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: The dragon was wroth with the woman, and went to make war with the remnant of her seed, who keep the commandments of God and have the testimony of Jesus Christ. Henry notes that, defeated against the universal church, the devil turns against particular persons, and that fidelity in doctrine, worship, and practice is what exposes believers to his rage to the end. — Matthew Henry
