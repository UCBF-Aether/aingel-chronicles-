Study Notes — Revelation

Chapter 5

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: John saw in the right hand of him who sat on the throne a book written within and on the backside, sealed with seven seals. Henry explains the book as God's decrees: the design is laid, every part adjusted, and passed into record, yet known to none but God until he allows it to be opened, with each of seven parts sealed until its turn. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: A strong angel proclaimed with a loud voice, "Who is worthy to open the book, and to loose the seals thereof?" Henry notes the angel comes as a champion, challenging any creature to try its wisdom on the counsels of God, loudly enough for every creature to hear. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: No man in heaven, nor in earth, neither under the earth, was able to open the book or even to look thereon. Henry stresses the point: angels before the throne, the wisest and best of men, prophets beyond what God reveals, fallen angels, and departed spirits all alike cannot dive into God's decrees; God only can open them. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: John wept much because no man was found worthy to open and read the book, nor to look thereon. Henry notes those who have seen most of God desire to see more, and that eager desires not presently answered turn to grief, since hope deferred makes the heart sick. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: One of the elders told John not to weep: behold, the Lion of the tribe of Judah, the Root of David, has prevailed to open the book and loose its seven seals. Henry explains the titles: Lion by his human nature after Jacob's prophecy, Root of David by his divine nature, and as God and man, Mediator, he alone is fit and worthy to open and execute God's counsels. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: In the midst of the throne, the living creatures, and the elders stood a Lamb as it had been slain, having seven horns and seven eyes, which are the seven Spirits of God sent forth into all the earth. Henry notes the paradox: a lion to conquer Satan, a lamb to satisfy God's justice, bearing his sufferings' marks, with perfect power and wisdom from the Spirit received without measure. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: The Lamb came and took the book out of the right hand of him who sat on the throne. Henry stresses he took it not by violence or fraud but by merit and worthiness, by authority and the Father's appointment, and that God willingly put his eternal counsels into Christ's hand, who gladly took them to reveal and do his Father's will. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: When he took the book, the four living creatures and twenty-four elders fell down before the Lamb, each with harps and golden vials full of odours, which are the prayers of saints. Henry notes the church leads the worship, with profound adoration, and that prayer and praise should always go together. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: They sang a new song: "Thou art worthy to take the book, and to open the seals thereof: for thou wast slain, and hast redeemed us to God by thy blood out of every kindred, and tongue, and people, and nation." Henry notes the song suits the gospel state, grounding Christ's worthiness chiefly in his sufferings and their fruit: redemption from sin, guilt, and Satan to God. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: Christ has made the redeemed kings and priests to God, and they shall reign on the earth. Henry notes the height of the preferment: ransomed slaves are not merely freed but made kings to rule their spirits and overcome the world, and priests with access to offer spiritual sacrifices, judging the world with Christ at the great day. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: John heard the voice of many angels round the throne, the living creatures, and the elders, numbering ten thousand times ten thousand, and thousands of thousands. Henry notes the angels, though needing no Saviour themselves, rejoice in sinners' redemption and join the church's doxology. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: The angels said with a loud voice, "Worthy is the Lamb that was slain to receive power, and riches, and wisdom, and strength, and honour, and glory, and blessing." Henry notes Christ is worthy both of the office requiring the greatest power and wisdom and of all honour for being sufficient and faithful in it. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: Every creature in heaven, on earth, under the earth, in the sea, and all in them said, "Blessing, and honour, and glory, and power, be unto him that sitteth upon the throne, and unto the Lamb for ever and ever." Henry notes the same honour is ascribed in the same words to the Lamb and to him on the throne, since their essence is one, though their parts in salvation are distinct. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: The four living creatures said, Amen, and the twenty-four elders fell down and worshipped him who lives for ever and ever. Henry closes the scene as it began: the church that started the anthem ends it with low prostration, as the sealed book passes with great solemnity from the Creator's hand into the Redeemer's. — Matthew Henry
