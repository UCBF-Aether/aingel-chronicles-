Study Notes — Revelation

Chapter 20

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: John saw an angel come down from heaven, having the key of the bottomless pit and a great chain in his hand. Henry notes the angel is very probably none other than the Lord Jesus Christ, who alone has power to bind the strong man armed, cast him out, and spoil his goods, having the powers of heaven and the keys of hell. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: He laid hold on the dragon, that old serpent, which is the Devil, and Satan, and bound him a thousand years. Henry notes neither the dragon's strength nor the serpent's subtlety could rescue him from Christ's hands: he caught hold and kept his hold. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: He cast him into the bottomless pit, shut him up, and set a seal upon him, that he should deceive the nations no more, till the thousand years should be fulfilled: and after that he must be loosed a little season. Henry notes Christ shuts and none can open, seals by his authority, and even devils cannot break his lock and seal, though the church's trials were not yet over. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: John saw thrones, and judgment was given to those who sat on them, and the souls of those beheaded for the witness of Jesus and for the word of God, who had not worshipped the beast or his image nor received his mark, lived and reigned with Christ a thousand years. Henry notes the honoured are all who suffered for Christ and kept clear of pagan and papal idolatry, raised and restored to life, given thrones and judgment, reigning with Christ in glorious conformity to him. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: But the rest of the dead lived not again until the thousand years were finished. This is the first resurrection. Henry notes this first resurrection belongs only to those who served and suffered for Christ, while the wicked shall not be raised and restored to power again until Satan is let loose. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: Blessed and holy is he that hath part in the first resurrection: on such the second death hath no power, but they shall be priests of God and of Christ, and shall reign with him a thousand years. Henry notes none can be blessed but the holy, and all the holy shall be blessed, secured from the second death, the death of the soul, eternal separation from God. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: When the thousand years are expired, Satan shall be loosed out of his prison. Henry notes the restraints laid for a long time are at length taken off, for while this world lasts Satan's power will not be wholly destroyed, only limited and lessened. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: He shall go out to deceive the nations which are in the four quarters of the earth, Gog and Magog, to gather them together to battle: the number of whom is as the sand of the sea. Henry notes no sooner is Satan loose than he falls to his old work, deceiving nations as to both the cause and the issue, and raising a mighty army from all parts of the world. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: They went up on the breadth of the earth, and compassed the camp of the saints about, and the beloved city: and fire came down from God out of heaven, and devoured them. Henry notes the saints' army lay encamped about the spiritual Jerusalem, compassed by a superior enemy, until God in an extraordinary and immediate manner fought this last and decisive battle for his people. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: The devil that deceived them was cast into the lake of fire and brimstone, where the beast and the false prophet are, and shall be tormented day and night for ever and ever. Henry notes the grand enemy is now cast into hell with his two great officers, tyranny and idolatry, not for any term of time but for ever. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: John saw a great white throne, and him that sat on it, from whose face the earth and the heaven fled away, and there was found no place for them. Henry notes the throne is very glorious and perfectly just, and the Judge is the Lord Jesus Christ, putting on such majesty and terror that the whole frame of nature dissolves. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: John saw the dead, small and great, stand before God, and the books were opened, and another book was opened, which is the book of life: and the dead were judged out of those things which were written in the books, according to their works. Henry notes the books are God's omniscience, the sinner's conscience, and the scriptures, the statute-book of heaven, the touchstone by which hearts and lives are tried. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: The sea gave up the dead which were in it, and death and hell delivered up the dead which were in them: and they were judged every man according to their works. Henry notes none are so mean but they have talents to account for, and none so great as to avoid this court's jurisdiction, since the grave, hell, and sea all surrender their dead. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: Death and hell were cast into the lake of fire. This is the second death. Henry notes those who made a covenant with death and an agreement with hell shall be condemned with their infernal confederates, cast into the lake of fire as not entitled to eternal life. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: Whosoever was not found written in the book of life was cast into the lake of fire. Henry notes those justified and acquitted by the gospel shall then be justified by the Judge and enter eternal life, having nothing more to fear from death, hell, or wicked men, for these are all destroyed together. — Matthew Henry
