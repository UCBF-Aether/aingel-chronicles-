Study Notes — Revelation

Chapter 1

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: This is the revelation of Jesus Christ, which God gave him to show his servants things which must shortly come to pass, sent and signified by his angel to his servant John. Henry traces the order: God gave it to Christ, Christ employed an angel, and the angel signified it to John, who was to deliver it to all the church's servants, not ministers only. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: John bore record of the word of God, the testimony of Jesus Christ, and all things that he saw. Henry notes John, like the prophets, prefixes his name and office, ready to vouch for the truth: he was an eye-witness who added nothing and kept back no part of God's counsels. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: Blessed is he who reads, and they who hear the words of this prophecy and keep what is written, for the time is at hand. Henry urges study of this book despite its obscurity, since it repays careful readers, but insists reading and hearing are not enough: the words must be kept in memory, mind, affection, and practice. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: John writes to the seven churches in Asia, wishing grace and peace from him who is, was, and is to come, and from the seven Spirits before his throne. Henry explains the blessing as holiness and comfort, grace going before and peace following, and the seven Spirits as the one infinite perfect Spirit in diversity of gifts and operations. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: The blessing also comes from Jesus Christ, the faithful witness, the first begotten of the dead, and the prince of the kings of the earth, who loved us and washed us from our sins in his own blood. Henry notes Christ cannot be deceived and cannot deceive us, raised himself by his own power, and limits and overrules the powers of earthly kings. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: Christ has made us kings and priests to God and his Father, to whom be glory and dominion for ever and ever. Henry explains the double honour: as kings they govern their spirits, conquer Satan, and prevail in prayer, and as priests they have access into the holiest to offer spiritual sacrifices. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: Behold, he comes with clouds, and every eye shall see him, including those who pierced him, and all kindreds of the earth shall wail because of him. Henry notes the book begins and ends with the second coming, and that Christ comes publicly, to the terror of the impenitent and the astonishment of the pagan world. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: Christ himself ratifies the account: "I am Alpha and Omega, the beginning and the ending, saith the Lord, which is, and which was, and which is to come, the Almighty." Henry notes he justly claims the same honour and power ascribed to the Father, being the eternal, unchangeable one from whom and for whom all things are. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: John describes himself as the churches' brother and companion in tribulation, in the kingdom and patience of Jesus Christ, who was on the isle of Patmos for the word of God and the testimony of Jesus. Henry notes he values his relation to the church above his apostolic authority, and that he suffered not as an evil-doer but for bearing witness to Christ. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: John was in the Spirit on the Lord's day and heard behind him a great voice, as of a trumpet. Henry identifies the Lord's day as the Christian sabbath, the first day of the week kept in remembrance of the resurrection, and notes God prepares souls for uncommon manifestations by the Spirit's quickening influence. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: The voice said, "I am Alpha and Omega, the first and the last," and commanded John to write what he saw in a book and send it to the seven churches: Ephesus, Smyrna, Pergamos, Thyatira, Sardis, Philadelphia, and Laodicea. Henry notes Christ, the captain of salvation, gave notice of his appearance as with a trumpet blast. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: John turned to see the voice that spoke with him, and saw seven golden candlesticks. Henry explains the emblem: churches hold forth the light of the gospel, receiving their light from Christ and his gospel, and as golden candlesticks they should be precious and pure. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: In the midst of the candlesticks he saw one like the Son of man, clothed with a garment down to the foot and girt about the paps with a golden girdle. Henry reads the princely and priestly robe as righteousness and honour, and the golden girdle as the high priest's breastplate bearing his people's names, ready girt for the Redeemer's work. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: His head and hairs were white like wool, as white as snow, and his eyes were as a flame of fire. Henry notes the hoary head is no sign of decay but a crown of glory, marking him the Ancient of days, while the fiery eyes pierce into hearts and scatter terror among adversaries. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: His feet were like fine brass, as if they burned in a furnace, and his voice was as the sound of many waters. Henry reads the burning brass as strength and steadfastness, supporting his own interest and treading enemies to powder, and the voice as a mighty gospel stream heard by those afar off as well as near. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: He had in his right hand seven stars, and out of his mouth went a sharp two-edged sword, and his countenance was as the sun shining in his strength. Henry explains the stars as the churches' ministers, held, lit, and preserved by Christ, and the sword as his word, wounding and healing, striking at sin on either hand. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: When John saw him, he fell at his feet as dead, and Christ laid his right hand on him, saying, "Fear not; I am the first and the last." Henry notes the apostle was overpowered by the lustre and glory, though he had been familiar with Christ before, and draws the comfort that God speaks to us by men like ourselves, whose terrors do not make us afraid, while Christ's touch put strength into his disciple. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: Christ said, "I am he that liveth, and was dead; and, behold, I am alive for evermore, Amen; and have the keys of hell and of death." Henry notes the two claims together: by resurrection he conquered death, opened the grave, and partakes of an endless life, and by office he holds sovereign dominion over the invisible world, opening the gates of death and of the eternal world of happiness or misery as Judge of all, from whose sentence there lies no appeal. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: Christ commanded John to write the things he had seen, the things which are, and the things which shall be hereafter. Henry notes this threefold commission covers the vision itself, the present state of the churches, and the prophecy of things to come. — Matthew Henry

20 COMMENTARY: The mystery of the seven stars is that they are the angels of the seven churches, and the seven golden candlesticks are the seven churches. Henry closes the chapter with this key: Christ stands in the midst of his churches, filling them with light, life, and love, as their animating soul. — Matthew Henry
