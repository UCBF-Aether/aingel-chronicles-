Study Notes — Revelation

Chapter 7

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: John saw four angels standing on the four corners of the earth, holding the four winds, so wind would not blow on earth, sea, or any tree. Henry reads the winds as errors and corruptions that trouble the church, restrained by angels, since the spirit of error cannot go forth until God permits it. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: Another angel ascended from the east, having the seal of the living God, and cried loudly to the four angels given power to hurt the earth and sea. Henry notes that while some angels restrain Satan and his agents, another is employed to mark out and distinguish God's faithful servants. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: The angel charged, "Hurt not the earth, neither the sea, nor the trees, till we have sealed the servants of our God in their foreheads." Henry notes the restraint lasted only until the sealing: God first establishes his servants, then tries them, holding the timing of their trials in his own hand. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: John heard the number of those sealed: a hundred and forty-four thousand of all the tribes of Israel. Henry explains the definite number stands for an indefinite remnant, and that God knows who are his and will keep them safe in dangerous temptation. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: Of Judah, Reuben, and Gad, twelve thousand were sealed from each tribe. Henry notes the list omits Dan, perhaps because that tribe was greatly addicted to idolatry, and alters the tribes' order, perhaps according to their faithfulness to God. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: Of Asher, Naphtali, and Manasses, twelve thousand were sealed from each tribe. Henry takes this sealed remnant either as Jews reserved for mercy at Jerusalem's destruction or, more generally, as God's chosen remnant in the world according to the election of grace. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: Of Simeon, Levi, and Issachar, twelve thousand were sealed from each tribe. Henry notes the sealing sets God's servants apart for mercy and safety in the worst of times, with a seal known to God and plain as if it appeared on their foreheads. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: Of Zebulon, Joseph, and Benjamin, twelve thousand were sealed from each tribe, completing the hundred and forty-four thousand. Henry observes that though the church is a little flock compared with the wicked world, it is no contemptible society, but really large and to be still more enlarged. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: After this John saw a great multitude no man could number, of all nations, kindreds, peoples, and tongues, standing before the throne and the Lamb, clothed in white robes, with palms in their hands. Henry notes God will have a greater harvest among the Gentiles than among the Jews, and that the white robes are justification, holiness, and victory, with palms as in a conqueror's triumph. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: They cried with a loud voice, "Salvation to our God which sitteth upon the throne, and unto the Lamb." Henry reads this as both hosannah and hallelujah: the Father contrived salvation, the Son purchased it, and those who enjoy it bless Lord and Lamb publicly and fervently. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: All the angels round the throne, the elders, and the four living creatures fell on their faces and worshipped God. Henry marvels at the humility: the most excellent creatures, who never sinned and stand continually before God, fall on their faces, teaching frail creatures reverence in approaching him. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: The angels said, "Amen, Blessing, and glory, and wisdom, and thanksgiving, and honour, and power, and might, be unto our God for ever and ever. Amen." Henry notes they consented to the saints' praises in perfect harmony, then added their own acknowledgement of God's wisdom, power, and might. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: One of the elders asked John, "What are these which are arrayed in white robes? and whence came they?" Henry notes the question was asked for John's instruction, not the elder's information, and that faithful Christians deserve notice, since the lowest saint in heaven knows more than the greatest apostle on earth. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: John answered, "Sir, thou knowest," and was told these are they who came out of great tribulation and washed their robes white in the blood of the Lamb. Henry stresses it is not the martyrs' own blood but the Lamb's that washes away sin, and that tribulation, however great, cannot separate us from God's love. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: Therefore they are before the throne of God, serving him day and night in his temple, and he who sits on the throne shall dwell among them. Henry notes their happiness in station, in the presence where there is fulness of joy, and in employment: heaven is service without weariness, rest without sloth, a praising delightful rest. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: They shall hunger no more, neither thirst any more, neither shall the sun light on them, nor any heat. Henry notes their freedom from all want and from all sickness and pain, with every uneasiness of this present life removed. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: The Lamb in the midst of the throne shall feed them and lead them to living fountains of waters, and God shall wipe away all tears from their eyes. Henry closes with the tender Father image: God himself, with his own gentle hand, wipes away tears shed over sin and affliction, turning sorrow into rejoicing, so that those who sow in tears reap in joy. — Matthew Henry
