Study Notes — Revelation

Chapter 21

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: John saw a new heaven and a new earth, for the first heaven and the first earth were passed away, and there was no more sea. Henry notes a new universe opens to view: a new state for men's bodies as well as a heaven for their souls, the old world with all its troubles and commotions having passed away to make way for it. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: John saw the holy city, new Jerusalem, coming down from God out of heaven, prepared as a bride adorned for her husband. Henry notes this is the church of God in its new and perfect state, beautified with all perfection of wisdom and holiness, meet for the full fruition of the Lord Jesus Christ in glory. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: A great voice out of heaven said, "Behold, the tabernacle of God is with men, and he will dwell with them, and they shall be his people, and God himself shall be with them, and be their God." Henry notes God's presence is the glory of the church, a wonder that a holy God should dwell with men, and in heaven it will not be interrupted but continual, the covenant relation filled up and perfected. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: God shall wipe away all tears from their eyes, and there shall be no more death, neither sorrow, nor crying, neither shall there be any more pain: for the former things are passed away. Henry notes all effects of former trouble shall be done away, God himself wiping tears with his own kind hand, and all causes of future sorrow removed for ever. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: He that sat upon the throne said, "Behold, I make all things new. And he said unto me, Write: for these words are true and faithful." Henry notes the vision's truth is ratified and ordered into writing for perpetual memory, since many ages and trials must intervene before its accomplishment. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: He said unto me, "It is done. I am Alpha and Omega, the beginning and the end. I will give unto him that is athirst of the fountain of the water of life freely." Henry notes "It is done" is as sure as if done already, God's promise taken as present payment, and that he who gave rise to the world and church will finish the work begun, not leave it imperfect. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: He that overcometh shall inherit all things; and I will be his God, and he shall be my son. Henry notes the felicity's freeness, fulness, and tenure: a free gift, enjoyed at the fountain-head of all blessedness, by right of inheritance as sons of God, the most honourable and indefeasible title. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: But the fearful, and unbelieving, and the abominable, and murderers, and whoremongers, and sorcerers, and idolaters, and all liars, shall have their part in the lake which burneth with fire and brimstone: which is the second death. Henry notes the fearful lead the black list, their slavish fear proceeding from unbelief, yet so desperate as to run into abominable wickedness, and their misery, to die and be always dying, illustrates the saints' blessedness. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: One of the seven angels which had the seven vials full of the seven last plagues said to John, "Come hither, I will shew thee the bride, the Lamb's wife." Henry notes God employs his angels variously: to warn, to pour out vials, and to discover heavenly things to the heirs of salvation, each commission readily executed. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: He carried John away in the spirit to a great and high mountain, and shewed him that great city, the holy Jerusalem, descending out of heaven from God. Henry notes those who would have clear views of heaven must get as near heaven as they can, into the mount of meditation and faith, as from Pisgah beholding the heavenly Canaan. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: Having the glory of God: and her light was like unto a stone most precious, even like a jasper stone, clear as crystal. Henry notes the bride is comely through the comeliness put on her by her husband, glorious in her relation to Christ, in his image now perfected in her, and in his favour shining upon her. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: The city had a wall great and high, and had twelve gates, and at the gates twelve angels, and names written thereon, which are the names of the twelve tribes of the children of Israel. Henry notes heaven is a safe state, enclosed by a wall separating and securing from all evils, and that the twelve gates answer to the twelve tribes, so all the true Israel of God shall have entrance. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: On the east three gates; on the north three gates; on the south three gates; and on the west three gates. Henry notes the four equal sides answer to the four quarters of the world, signifying that from all quarters some shall get safely to heaven, with as free entrance from one part of the world as another, for in Christ there is neither Jew nor Greek. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: The wall of the city had twelve foundations, and in them the names of the twelve apostles of the Lamb. Henry notes the foundations are the promise and power of God and the purchase of Christ, their number alluding to the apostles whose gospel doctrines are the foundations, Christ himself being the chief corner-stone. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: He that talked with John had a golden reed to measure the city, and the gates thereof, and the wall thereof. Henry notes the measure shows room sufficient for all the people of God, many mansions in their Father's house. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: The city lies foursquare, and the length is as large as the breadth: and he measured the city with the reed, twelve thousand furlongs. The length and the breadth and the height of it are equal. Henry notes the regular, uniform form: in the new Jerusalem all shall be equal in purity and perfection, an absolute uniformity wanted on earth but not to be expected until heaven. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: He measured the wall thereof, an hundred and forty and four cubits, according to the measure of a man, that is, of the angel. Henry notes the wall's height, seventy yards, is sufficient both for ornament and security, and the city is impregnable as well as precious. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: The building of the wall of it was of jasper: and the city was pure gold, like unto clear glass. Henry notes the wall is all built of the most precious stones, for firmness and lustre, and the city itself pure gold. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: The foundations of the wall of the city were garnished with all manner of precious stones. The first foundation was jasper; the second, sapphire; the third, a chalcedony; the fourth, an emerald. Henry notes the twelve sorts of precious stones denote the variety and excellency of the gospel's doctrines, the graces of the Spirit, or the personal excellencies of Christ. — Matthew Henry

20 COMMENTARY: The fifth, sardonyx; the sixth, sardius; the seventh, chrysolite; the eighth, beryl; the ninth, a topaz; the tenth, a chrysoprasus; the eleventh, a jacinth; the twelfth, an amethyst. Henry notes the foundations' varied and precious matter sets forth the variety and excellency of the gospel, as in the preceding verse. — Matthew Henry

21 COMMENTARY: The twelve gates were twelve pearls; every several gate was of one pearl: and the street of the city was pure gold, as it were transparent glass. Henry notes the gates were all of pearls, Christ being the pearl of great price and our way to God, and that the saints in heaven tread upon gold in a state of delightful motion and converse, not sleep and inactivity. — Matthew Henry

22 COMMENTARY: John saw no temple therein: for the Lord God Almighty and the Lamb are the temple of it. Henry notes the temple is altogether spiritual and divine, not made with hands: the saints are above the need of ordinances, which were means of preparation for heaven, and perfect, immediate communion with God more than supplies their place. — Matthew Henry

23 COMMENTARY: The city had no need of the sun, neither of the moon, to shine in it: for the glory of God did lighten it, and the Lamb is the light thereof. Henry notes God in Christ will be an everlasting fountain of knowledge and joy to the saints, so there is no need of sun or moon, any more than candles at noonday. — Matthew Henry

24 COMMENTARY: The nations of them which are saved shall walk in the light of it: and the kings of the earth do bring their glory and honour into it. Henry notes the inhabitants are whole nations of saved souls, including kings and princes, whose former honour and glory will be swallowed up by the heavenly glory that so much excels. — Matthew Henry

25 COMMENTARY: The gates of it shall not be shut at all by day: for there shall be no night there. Henry notes there is no night and therefore no need of shutting the gates: someone is coming in every hour, and the sanctified always find the gates open, with abundant entrance into the kingdom. — Matthew Henry

26 COMMENTARY: They shall bring the glory and honour of the nations into it. Henry notes whatever is excellent and valuable in this world shall be enjoyed there in a more refined kind and far greater degree: brighter crowns, a better and more enduring substance, and more glorious honour. — Matthew Henry

27 COMMENTARY: There shall in no wise enter into it any thing that defileth, neither whatsoever worketh abomination, or maketh a lie: but they which are written in the Lamb's book of life. Henry notes the saints shall have no impure thing remaining in them, cleansed at death from everything defiling, and no impure persons admitted among them, neither the openly profane nor hypocrites, since the city is reserved for the called, chosen, and faithful. — Matthew Henry
