Study Notes — Revelation

Chapter 2

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: The message to Ephesus comes from him who holds the seven stars in his right hand and walks among the seven golden candlesticks. Henry notes ministers are in Christ's hand, directed, filled with light, and supported, while Christ himself is intimately present in his churches, observing what is amiss and what they lack. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: Christ knows Ephesus' works, labour, and patience, and that they cannot bear evil men, having tried those who claimed to be apostles and found them liars. Henry commends their diligence, their bearing and waiting patience, and their discreet zeal, which tried pretences before casting anyone off. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: They have borne up and have patience, labouring for Christ's name without fainting. Henry notes Christ keeps an account of every day's and hour's work his servants do for him, and their labour shall not be in vain in the Lord. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: Nevertheless Christ has somewhat against them: they have left their first love. Henry explains they had not forsaken its object but lost its fervent degree, and that lively first affections cool without constant care, grieving Christ, who will faithfully reprove what he sees amiss. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: They are told to remember from where they have fallen, repent, and do the first works, or Christ will come quickly and remove their candlestick unless they repent. Henry urges them to compare present with former state, grieve inwardly, and begin again step by step, reviving first zeal, tenderness, and seriousness. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: Yet Ephesus retains this: they hate the deeds of the Nicolaitans, which Christ also hates. Henry notes the Nicolaitans were a loose sect sheltering under Christianity with hateful doctrines and deeds, and praises Ephesus' just abhorrence, since indifference between truth and error is not pleasing to Christ. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: He who has an ear is to hear what the Spirit says to the churches, and to the overcomer Christ will give to eat of the tree of life in the midst of the paradise of God. Henry notes what is said to one church concerns all, and that persevering victors receive from Christ the perfection and confirmation in holiness Adam would have had. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: The message to Smyrna comes from the first and the last, who was dead and is alive. Henry notes this title suits a suffering church: Christ is from everlasting to everlasting, died to purchase salvation, and ever lives to apply it and make intercession. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: Christ knows Smyrna's works, tribulation, and poverty, yet says, "but thou art rich," and knows the blasphemy of those who say they are Jews and are not, but are the synagogue of Satan. Henry contrasts outward poverty with spiritual riches in faith, works, privileges, and hope, and warns that assemblies opposing gospel truth and worship are Satan's synagogue, not God's church. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: They are told to fear none of the coming sufferings: the devil will cast some of them into prison to be tried, with tribulation ten days, but they must be faithful unto death to receive a crown of life. Henry notes the trial is limited, not universal and not perpetual, meant to prove faith, patience, and courage, and the crown suits those who part with life itself in fidelity. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: He who has an ear is to hear what the Spirit says, and the overcomer shall not be hurt of the second death. Henry explains the second death as eternal, the dying of the soul without mixture of support, from which Christ saves all his faithful servants. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: The message to Pergamos comes from him who has the sharp sword with two edges. Henry notes the title suits a church infested with corrupt teachers: Christ will fight them with the sword of his word, which cuts every way, with the edge of law and the edge of gospel, wounding and then opening wounds to heal. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: Christ knows Pergamos' works and where they dwell, even where Satan's seat is, yet they hold fast his name and have not denied his faith, even when Antipas, his faithful martyr, was slain among them. Henry notes Christ makes gracious allowance for hard places, and that their steadfastness shone brighter for Antipas' martyrdom in Satan's very seat. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: Yet Christ has a few things against them: they have those who hold the doctrine of Balaam, who taught Balak to put a stumblingblock before Israel, to eat things sacrificed to idols and commit fornication. Henry observes corrupt doctrine and corrupt worship lead to corrupt conduct, as Balaam drew Israel by impure worship into impure practice. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: So they also have those who hold the doctrine of the Nicolaitans, which Christ hates. Henry warns that continuing in communion with corrupt principles displeases God and draws guilt on the whole society, which must exclude such persons from communion or face Christ's displeasure. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: They must repent, or Christ will come quickly and fight against them with the sword of his mouth. Henry notes repentance is the duty of churches as well as persons, including repentance for others' sins so far as they were accessory by connivance, and that no sword cuts so deep as the sword of Christ's mouth. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: To the overcomer Christ will give hidden manna, a white stone, and a new name written on it, known only to the receiver. Henry explains the manna as the Spirit's comforts in communion with Christ, hidden from the world, and the white stone as absolution, after the ancient custom of acquittal, with the new name as the name of adoption. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: The message to Thyatira comes from the Son of God, whose eyes are like a flame of fire and whose feet are like fine brass. Henry notes the eyes signify piercing, perfect knowledge that searches hearts and reins, and the brass feet signify providence steady, awful, pure, and holy in judgment and action. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: Christ knows Thyatira's works, charity, service, faith, patience, and works, the last more than the first. Henry praises their growing fruitfulness: while others left their first love, these grew wiser and better, and Christians should ambition that their last works be their best. — Matthew Henry

20 COMMENTARY: Yet Christ has a few things against them: they suffer the woman Jezebel, who calls herself a prophetess, to teach and seduce his servants to commit fornication and eat things sacrificed to idols. Henry compares her to Jezebel, persecutor of prophets and patroness of idolatry, and charges the church with connivance, since it neglected its ministerial power to censure and excommunicate. — Matthew Henry

21 COMMENTARY: Christ gave her space to repent of her fornication, and she repented not. Henry notes repentance requires time and is a great work, and that where God gives space he expects fruits meet for repentance, while lost space brings double destruction. — Matthew Henry

22 COMMENTARY: Behold, Christ will cast her into a bed, and those who commit adultery with her into great tribulation, unless they repent of their deeds. Henry reads the bed as a bed of pain and flames, not pleasure, yet still escapable by repentance for her partners. — Matthew Henry

23 COMMENTARY: He will kill her children with death, and all the churches shall know that he searches the reins and hearts, giving to each according to his works. Henry notes this judgment teaches Christ's infallible knowledge of secret inclinations and his impartial justice, since neither Christian name nor church membership shelters sin. — Matthew Henry

24 COMMENTARY: To the rest in Thyatira who have not this doctrine nor known the depths of Satan, Christ will put no other burden. Henry contrasts the seducers' boasted "depths," profound mysteries, with Christ's name for them, depths of Satan, and stresses Christ's tenderness: he will not overburden faith with new mysteries or consciences with new laws. — Matthew Henry

25 COMMENTARY: But what they already have, they are to hold fast till Christ comes. Henry notes Christ is coming to end all his people's temptations, and holding fast faith and a good conscience until then leaves all difficulty and danger over. — Matthew Henry

26 COMMENTARY: To the overcomer who keeps Christ's works to the end, he will give power over the nations. Henry reads this as dominion either when the empire turned Christian or in the world to come, when believers sit with Christ on his throne of judgment over his enemies. — Matthew Henry

27 COMMENTARY: He shall rule them with a rod of iron, breaking them to shivers as a potter's vessels, even as Christ received of his Father. Henry pairs this dominion with the next verse's gift, as power requiring suitable wisdom. — Matthew Henry

28 COMMENTARY: Christ will give the overcomer the morning star. Henry identifies the star as Christ himself, bringing day into the soul, with the perfection of light and wisdom requisite for the dignity believers will hold in the morning of the resurrection. — Matthew Henry

29 COMMENTARY: He who has an ear is to hear what the Spirit says to the churches. Henry notes that in this and the following epistles the call to attention comes after the promise, teaching us to attend to promises as well as precepts. — Matthew Henry
