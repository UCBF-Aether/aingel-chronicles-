Study Notes — Ecclesiastes

Chapter 8

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Who is as the wise man, and who knows the interpretation of a thing, for a man's wisdom makes his face to shine, and the boldness of his face is changed, because wisdom gives a man both understanding and a gracious bearing. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: Keep the king's commandment, and that in regard of the oath of God, because obedience to the ruler is part of obedience to God, by whose ordinance the ruler stands. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: Be not hasty to go out of his sight, and stand not in an evil thing, for he does whatsoever pleases him, because open defiance of a king who can do as he pleases only invites ruin. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: Where the word of a king is, there is power, and who may say unto him, what doest thou, because a king's command carries authority that a subject cannot call to account. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: Whoso keeps the commandment shall feel no evil thing, and a wise man's heart discerns both time and judgment, because obedience keeps a man safe, and wisdom knows when and how to act. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: Because to every purpose there is time and judgment, therefore the misery of man is great upon him, since man knows there is a right season and judgment, yet cannot master either. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: For he knows not that which shall be, and who can tell him when it shall be, because the future is hidden, and no man can be told the hour of what is coming. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: There is no man that has power over the spirit to retain the spirit, neither has he power in the day of death, and there is no discharge in that war, neither shall wickedness deliver those given to it, because death is a war from which no man is discharged. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: All this he saw, and applied his heart to every work done under the sun, where one man rules over another to his own hurt, because dominion misused harms the ruler as well as the ruled. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: So he saw the wicked buried, who had come and gone from the place of the holy, and they were forgotten in the city where they had done so, which is also vanity, because even a splendid funeral cannot preserve a wicked memory. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: Because sentence against an evil work is not executed speedily, therefore the heart of the sons of men is fully set in them to do evil, because delay in punishment is mistaken for impunity. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: Though a sinner do evil a hundred times and his days be prolonged, yet surely he knows it shall be well with them that fear God, which fear before him, because God's patience is not God's approval. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: But it shall not be well with the wicked, neither shall he prolong his days, which are as a shadow, because he fears not before God, and a life lived without that fear has no true length. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: There is a vanity done upon the earth, that there are just men unto whom it happens according to the work of the wicked, and wicked men unto whom it happens according to the work of the righteous, which he says is vanity, because present providence does not sort men visibly by desert. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: Then he commended mirth, because a man has no better thing under the sun than to eat and drink and be merry, for that shall abide with him of his labour all the days of his life which God gives him under the sun. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: When he applied his heart to know wisdom and to see the business done upon the earth, even one who neither day nor night sees sleep with his eyes, he found the labour of knowledge without rest. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: Then he beheld all the work of God, that a man cannot find out the work done under the sun, because though a man labour to seek it out, yet he shall not find it, and though a wise man think to know it, yet shall he not be able to find it. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
