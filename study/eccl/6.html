Study Notes — Ecclesiastes

Chapter 6

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: There is an evil seen under the sun, and it is common among men, namely the case of a man given everything yet given no power to enjoy it, which Henry makes the next proof of the vanity of riches. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: A man to whom God has given riches, wealth and honour, so that he wants nothing, yet God gives him not power to eat thereof, but a stranger eats it, which is vanity and an evil disease, because possession without enjoyment is possession in name only. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: If a man beget a hundred children and live many years, yet his soul be not filled with good and he have no burial, an untimely birth is better than he, because a life unfilled and unhonoured at the end is worse than a life never begun. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: The untimely birth comes in with vanity and departs in darkness, and its name is covered with darkness, yet it has known neither the toil nor the disappointment that embitter the long life. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: Moreover it has not seen the sun nor known anything, and this has more rest than the other, because rest from trouble, even in oblivion, is better than a restless life of unsatisfied desire. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: Though a man live a thousand years twice told, yet has he seen no good, and do not all go to one place, so that length of days without goodness adds nothing at the end, since all end in the same grave. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: All the labour of man is for his mouth, and yet the appetite is not filled, because the labour that feeds the body can never feed the soul, which craves beyond bread. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: What has the wise more than the fool, and what has the poor, that knows to walk before the living, but the same unsatisfied appetite, since wisdom and poverty alike end at the grave's door. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: Better is the sight of the eyes than the wandering of the desire, because contentment with what is present is worth more than a roaming appetite that is never at home, which is also vanity and vexation of spirit. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: That which has been is named already, and it is known that it is man, and he cannot contend with him that is mightier than he, because man's nature and limits were fixed long ago, and he cannot dispute with God. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: Seeing there are many things that increase vanity, what is man the better, since multiplying words and schemes about earthly things only multiplies the emptiness. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: Who knows what is good for man in this life, all the days of his vain life which he spends as a shadow, and who can tell him what shall be after him under the sun, since man cannot see either his true good or his own future. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
