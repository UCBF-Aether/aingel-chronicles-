Study Notes — Ecclesiastes

Chapter 5

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Keep thy foot when thou goest to the house of God, and be more ready to hear than to give the sacrifice of fools, because worship must begin with reverent attention, not with careless offerings. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: Be not rash with thy mouth, nor hasty to utter anything before God, for God is in heaven and thou upon earth, therefore let thy words be few, since reverence suits the distance between God and man. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: A dream comes through the multitude of business, and a fool's voice is known by multitude of words, because a mind crowded with affairs and a mouth crowded with words both betray a lack of government. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: When thou vowest a vow unto God, defer not to pay it, for he has no pleasure in fools, so pay what thou hast vowed, since a promise to God is not a thing to be trifled with. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: Better it is that thou shouldest not vow than that thou shouldest vow and not pay, because an unmade vow leaves no debt, while a broken vow adds guilt to rashness. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: Suffer not thy mouth to cause thy flesh to sin, nor say before the angel that it was an error, because an excuse offered after a rash vow cannot undo it, and God will be angry at the voice that made it. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: In the multitude of dreams and many words there are also vanities, but fear thou God, because dreams and talk are empty, while the fear of God is the solid ground of worship. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: If thou seest the oppression of the poor and violent perverting of judgment in a province, marvel not at the matter, for he that is higher than the highest regards it, and there are higher than they, so that no wrong escapes a higher court. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: Moreover the profit of the earth is for all, and the king himself is served by the field, because even the highest rank depends on the tillage of the ground, which humbles human pride. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: He that loves silver shall not be satisfied with silver, nor he that loves abundance with increase, because desire grows with possession, and this also is vanity. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: When goods increase, they are increased that eat them, so what good is there to the owners but the beholding of them with their eyes, since wealth multiplies dependents faster than enjoyment. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: The sleep of a labouring man is sweet, whether he eat little or much, but the abundance of the rich will not suffer him to sleep, because a quiet conscience rests while a full estate watches. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: There is a sore evil under the sun, riches kept for the owners thereof to their hurt, because wealth hoarded becomes a snare to the very man who hoards it. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: Those riches perish by evil travail, and the man begets a son and there is nothing in his hand, because one misadventure can strip a lifetime's gathering in a day. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: As he came forth of his mother's womb, naked shall he return to go as he came, and he shall take nothing of his labour to carry in his hand, because death strips every man to the state in which he was born. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: This also is a sore evil, that in all points as he came so shall he go, and what profit has he that has laboured for the wind, since all his toil leaves him exactly where he began. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: All his days also he eats in darkness, and has much sorrow and wrath with his sickness, because the life spent grasping at wealth ends in gloom even before it ends in death. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

18 COMMENTARY: Behold what he has seen to be good and comely, to eat and drink and enjoy the good of all his labour all the days of his life which God gives him, for it is his portion. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

19 COMMENTARY: Every man to whom God has given riches and wealth, and power to eat thereof and take his portion and rejoice in his labour, has received the gift of God, and enjoyment itself is God's grant, not man's purchase. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

20 COMMENTARY: He shall not much remember the days of his life, because God answers him in the joy of his heart, so that a heart contented with its portion is kept from brooding on the brevity of its days. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
