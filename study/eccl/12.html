Study Notes — Ecclesiastes

Chapter 12

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Remember now thy Creator in the days of thy youth, while the evil days come not, nor the years draw nigh when thou shalt say, I have no pleasure in them, because youth is the season for choosing God, before age makes obedience hard. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: While the sun, or the light, or the moon, or the stars be not darkened, nor the clouds return after the rain, because the decay of age is pictured as a darkening sky, in which brightness fails and troubles return in succession. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: In the day when the keepers of the house shall tremble, and the strong men shall bow themselves, and the grinders cease because they are few, and those that look out of the windows be darkened, because the body itself is the house, and its keepers, strength, teeth and eyes all fail together. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: And the doors shall be shut in the streets, when the sound of the grinding is low, and he shall rise up at the voice of the bird, and all the daughters of music shall be brought low, because age shuts the senses, breaks sleep, and dulls delight in sound. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: When they shall be afraid of that which is high, and fears shall be in the way, and the almond tree shall flourish, and the grasshopper shall be a burden, and desire shall fail, because the aged fear heights and stumbling, grow grey like almond blossom, and lose strength and appetite. Because man goes to his long home, and the mourners go about the streets, since death is a homecoming to the grave, and the street is filled with those who lament the departure. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: Or ever the silver cord be loosed, or the golden bowl be broken, or the pitcher be broken at the fountain, or the wheel broken at the cistern, because life is pictured as a lamp hung by a cord and water drawn by a wheel, and death breaks the whole apparatus at once. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: Then shall the dust return to the earth as it was, and the spirit shall return unto God who gave it, because the body and the soul part at death, each to its own origin, which grounds the whole book's call to fear God. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: Vanity of vanities, says the Preacher, all is vanity, because the closing sentence repeats the opening text, now proved by the whole survey of life under the sun. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: Because the Preacher was wise, he still taught the people knowledge, and gave good heed, and sought out, and set in order many proverbs, because wisdom is not kept for oneself but ordered for the teaching of others. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: The Preacher sought to find out acceptable words, and that which was written was upright, even words of truth, because true teaching must be both pleasing in form and upright in matter. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: The words of the wise are as goads, and as nails fastened by the masters of assemblies, which are given from one shepherd, because wise words prick the conscience into action and fasten truth firmly, all from one Shepherd. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: And further, by these, my son, be admonished, for of making many books there is no end, and much study is a weariness of the flesh, because human learning without limit wearies, and the reader is warned to rest in the words already given. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: Let us hear the conclusion of the whole matter: fear God and keep his commandments, for this is the whole duty of man, because the entire inquiry into vanity ends in reverence and obedience, which alone give life its duty. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: For God shall bring every work into judgment, with every secret thing, whether it be good or whether it be evil, because the final judgment reaches what no human court can reach, and this is the book's last answer to vanity. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
