Study Notes — Ecclesiastes

Chapter 11

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Cast thy bread upon the waters, for thou shalt find it after many days, because liberality, like seed cast on uncertain waters, returns after long waiting, and Henry applies this to charity and to every good work sown in faith. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: Give a portion to seven, and also to eight, for thou knowest not what evil shall be upon the earth, because dividing gifts among many guards against a calamity that may fall on any one part. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: If the clouds be full of rain, they empty themselves upon the earth, and if the tree fall toward the south or toward the north, in the place where the tree falls, there it shall be, because both blessing and judgment fall where they are appointed, and man's lot is fixed by more than his planning. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: He that observes the wind shall not sow, and he that regards the clouds shall not reap, because waiting for perfect weather forfeits every season, and diligence must act despite uncertainty. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: As thou knowest not what is the way of the spirit, nor how the bones do grow in the womb of her that is with child, even so thou knowest not the works of God who makes all, because the growth of life is hidden, and so is the whole work of providence. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: In the morning sow thy seed, and in the evening withhold not thy hand, for thou knowest not whether shall prosper, either this or that, or whether they both shall be alike good, because diligence at all hours covers the ignorance of outcomes. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: Truly the light is sweet, and a pleasant thing it is for the eyes to behold the sun, because life itself, with its light, is a gift to be enjoyed, not merely endured. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: If a man live many years and rejoice in them all, yet let him remember the days of darkness, for they shall be many, and all that comes is vanity, because long life must still reckon with darkness, and rejoicing must be sober. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: Rejoice, O young man, in thy youth, and let thy heart cheer thee in the days of thy youth, and walk in the ways of thine heart and in the sight of thine eyes, but know thou that for all these things God will bring thee into judgment, because youthful joy is allowed, but never unaccountable. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: Therefore remove sorrow from thy heart, and put away evil from thy flesh, for childhood and youth are vanity, because youth passes quickly, and the sorrows and sins nursed in youth follow a man out of it. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
