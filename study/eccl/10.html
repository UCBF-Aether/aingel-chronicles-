Study Notes — Ecclesiastes

Chapter 10

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Dead flies cause the ointment of the apothecary to send forth a stinking savour, so does a little folly him that is in reputation for wisdom and honour, because one small folly spoils a great reputation. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: A wise man's heart is at his right hand, but a fool's heart at his left, because wisdom directs a man aright in his course, while folly leads him crosswise to everything. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: When he that is a fool walks by the way, his wisdom fails him, and he says to everyone that he is a fool, because folly betrays itself in a man's very carriage and speech. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: If the spirit of the ruler rise up against thee, leave not thy place, for yielding pacifies great offences, because a calm refusal to quit one's post disarms anger that resistance would inflame. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: There is an evil seen under the sun, as an error which proceeds from the ruler, namely folly set in great dignity while the rich sit in low place, because promotion often follows favour rather than merit. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: Folly is set in great dignity, and the rich sit in low place, which is the world turned upside down, where worth is debased and emptiness exalted. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: He has seen servants upon horses, and princes walking as servants upon the earth, because the order of ranks is often inverted by the caprice of rulers. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: He that digs a pit shall fall into it, and whoso breaks a hedge, a serpent shall bite him, because mischief plotted against others recoils upon the plotter. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: Whoso removes stones shall be hurt therewith, and he that cleaves wood shall be endangered thereby, because even lawful labour carries hazards that wisdom must foresee. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: If the iron be blunt and he do not whet the edge, then must he put to more strength, but wisdom is profitable to direct, because skill saves the strength that mere force wastes. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: Surely the serpent will bite without enchantment, and a babbler is no better, because a tongue left uncharmed does the same harm as a serpent left uncharmed. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: The words of a wise man's mouth are gracious, but the lips of a fool will swallow up himself, because wise speech wins favour while foolish speech destroys the speaker. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: The beginning of the words of his mouth is foolishness, and the end of his talk is mischievous madness, because folly grows worse as it goes, from idle talk to dangerous talk. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: A fool also is full of words, yet a man cannot tell what shall be, and what shall be after him, who can tell him, because the fool multiplies words precisely where knowledge fails. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: The labour of the foolish wearies every one of them, because he knows not how to go to the city, because folly cannot manage even the plainest road, and wears itself out on what wisdom does easily. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: Woe to thee, O land, when thy king is a child, and thy princes eat in the morning, because a realm ruled by appetite and immaturity is given to excess from the start of the day. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: Blessed art thou, O land, when thy king is the son of nobles, and thy princes eat in due season, for strength and not for drunkenness, because rulers who eat for strength govern, while rulers who eat for pleasure only consume. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

18 COMMENTARY: By much slothfulness the building decays, and through idleness of the hands the house drops through, because a house, like a kingdom, falls when its keepers will not work. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

19 COMMENTARY: A feast is made for laughter, and wine makes merry, but money answers all things, because in the world's reckoning wealth is treated as the universal provision, though it cannot answer the soul. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

20 COMMENTARY: Curse not the king, no not in thy thought, and curse not the rich in thy bedchamber, for a bird of the air shall carry the voice, and that which has wings shall tell the matter, because secret disloyalty finds a way to be reported. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
