Study Notes — Ecclesiastes

Chapter 3

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: To everything there is a season and a time to every purpose under heaven, because human life is not governed by man's will but by appointments he does not control. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: There is a time to be born and to die, to plant and to pluck up, pairing the beginnings and endings of life and labour, neither of which man can fix at his pleasure. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: There is a time to kill and to heal, to break down and to build up, because destruction and repair each have their appointed hour in a world God orders. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: There is a time to weep and to laugh, to mourn and to dance, because sorrow and joy succeed one another in seasons, and neither lasts unbroken. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: There is a time to cast away stones and to gather them, to embrace and to refrain from embracing, because even gathering and affection must wait their season. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: There is a time to get and to lose, to keep and to cast away, because possession itself is seasonal, and what is kept in one season is thrown away in another. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: There is a time to rend and to sew, to keep silence and to speak, because mourning and repair, restraint and utterance, each belong to their own hour. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: There is a time to love and to hate, a time of war and a time of peace, because the affections and the affairs of nations alike turn in seasons appointed over them. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: What profit has he that works in that wherein he labours, if every work is bounded by its season and no season can be held beyond its time. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: He has seen the travail God has given to the sons of men to be exercised in it, a labour that humbles man by showing him how little of life he commands. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: God has made everything beautiful in his time, and has set the world in man's heart, so that man longs for eternity yet cannot find out the work of God from beginning to end. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: He knows there is no good in earthly things but for a man to rejoice and do good in his life, receiving present comfort as the good that is actually within his reach. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: Every man should eat and drink and enjoy the good of all his labour, because this enjoyment is the gift of God, not a right earned by toil. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: Whatever God does shall be for ever, nothing can be put to it or taken from it, and God does it that men should fear before him, since his work alone is unchangeable. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: That which has been is now, and that which is to be has already been, and God requires that which is past, because history repeats itself under his unchanging government. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: He saw under the sun, in the place of judgment, wickedness, and in the place of righteousness, iniquity, because even the seats meant for justice are corrupted by men. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: He said in his heart that God shall judge the righteous and the wicked, for there is a time for every purpose and for every work, and corrupt judgment on earth does not cancel judgment in heaven. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

18 COMMENTARY: Concerning the sons of men, God manifests them that they may see they themselves are beasts, because death, which they share with beasts, strips away the pride of life. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

19 COMMENTARY: As the one dies so dies the other, both have one breath, and man has no preeminence above a beast in this, for all is vanity when life is measured only by its breath. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

20 COMMENTARY: All go unto one place, all are of the dust and all turn to dust again, because the body, whether of man or beast, returns to the earth from which it came. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

21 COMMENTARY: Who knows whether the spirit of man goes upward and the spirit of the beast goes downward to the earth, a question Henry reads as man's ignorance, not as denial, since the difference of souls is known to God. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

22 COMMENTARY: Therefore he perceives there is nothing better than that a man should rejoice in his own works, for that is his portion, and who shall bring him to see what shall be after him. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
