Study Notes — Ecclesiastes

Chapter 9

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: He considered all this in his heart, that the righteous and the wise and their works are in the hand of God, and no man knows either love or hatred by all that is before them, because outward fortune does not reveal God's favour. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: All things come alike to all, one event to the righteous and to the wicked, to the good and to the clean and to the unclean, to him that sacrifices and to him that sacrifices not, as is the good, so is the sinner, and he that swears as he that fears an oath, because death and fortune fall without visible distinction. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: This is an evil among all things done under the sun, that there is one event unto all, and the heart of the sons of men is full of evil, and madness is in their heart while they live, and after that they go to the dead, because a common end does not restrain a common corruption. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: To him that is joined to all the living there is hope, for a living dog is better than a dead lion, because life, however low, still holds opportunity that death has closed. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: The living know that they shall die, but the dead know not anything, neither have they any more a reward, for the memory of them is forgotten, because death ends all present agency and remembrance in this world. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: Also their love and their hatred and their envy is now perished, neither have they any more a portion for ever in anything done under the sun, because the dead take no further part in earthly affairs. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: Go thy way, eat thy bread with joy and drink thy wine with a merry heart, for God now accepts thy works, because the present enjoyment of God's gifts, received thankfully, is itself accepted. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: Let thy garments be always white, and let thy head lack no ointment, because cheerful, decent enjoyment of life is commended, not a sour refusal of God's gifts. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: Live joyfully with the wife whom thou lovest all the days of the life of thy vanity which he has given thee under the sun, for that is thy portion in this life and in thy labour which thou takest under the sun. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: Whatsoever thy hand finds to do, do it with thy might, for there is no work, nor device, nor knowledge, nor wisdom in the grave whither thou goest, because present duty is the only field in which diligence can act. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: He returned and saw under the sun that the race is not to the swift, nor the battle to the strong, neither yet bread to the wise, nor riches to men of understanding, nor favour to men of skill, but time and chance happen to them all, because human excellences do not command success. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: For man also knows not his time, as the fishes taken in an evil net and as birds caught in the snare, so are the sons of men snared in an evil time when it falls suddenly upon them. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: This wisdom also he saw under the sun, and it seemed great to him, the case of a little city besieged and saved by wisdom, which Henry makes the next proof that wisdom is truly excellent though poorly rewarded. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: There was a little city and few men within it, and a great king came against it and besieged it and built great bulwarks against it, so that its danger was out of all proportion to its strength. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: Now there was found in it a poor wise man, and he by his wisdom delivered the city, yet no man remembered that same poor man, because the world forgets its deliverers once the danger is past. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: Then he said, wisdom is better than strength, nevertheless the poor man's wisdom is despised and his words are not heard, because poverty silences even the wisdom that saves a city. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: The words of wise men are heard in quiet more than the cry of him that rules among fools, because quiet counsel prevails where clamour only rules. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

18 COMMENTARY: Wisdom is better than weapons of war, but one sinner destroys much good, because a single folly can undo what much wisdom has built. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
