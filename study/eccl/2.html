Study Notes — Ecclesiastes

Chapter 2

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Having tried wisdom and found it vanity, the Preacher next tries mirth, resolving to prove himself with pleasure, and finds that this also is vanity. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: Laughter he judges madness, because it promises joy and gives none that lasts, and mirth he asks what it accomplishes, since it leaves the heart as empty as before. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: He sought to give himself to wine while still acquainting his heart with wisdom, testing whether sensual delight, guided rather than abandoned, could show what good men might find under heaven. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: He made great works, building houses and planting vineyards, trying whether lasting monuments and estates could give the satisfaction that pleasure had not. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: He made gardens and orchards and planted trees of every fruit, surrounding himself with ordered beauty, yet beauty kept in the hand proved as unsatisfying as mirth. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: He made pools of water to irrigate the wood that brings forth trees, a work of royal provision and foresight, which still left the soul unwatered. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: He gathered servants and handmaids, with servants born in his house, and great possessions of herds above all before him in Jerusalem, multiplying the apparatus of a great estate. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: He gathered silver and gold and the peculiar treasure of kings and provinces, with singers and musical instruments, the delights of the sons of men, and found even abundance attended by emptiness. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: He became great and increased above all before him in Jerusalem, and his wisdom remained with him through the experiment, so that the trial was fair and its failure decisive. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: Whatever his eyes desired he kept not from them, and withheld his heart from no joy, taking the portion of his labour, yet even joy taken without restraint could not fill him. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: Looking back on all his works and labour, he saw only vanity and vexation of spirit, with no profit under the sun, because nothing made by hands outlasts the hand that made it. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: He turned to behold wisdom, madness and folly, asking what the man can do who comes after the king, since the next king can only repeat what has already been tried. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: He saw that wisdom excels folly as light excels darkness, so wisdom is genuinely better, even though it cannot by itself make a man happy. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: The wise man's eyes are in his head and he sees where he goes, while the fool walks in darkness, yet one event happens to them both, which bounds the advantage of wisdom in this life. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: He said in his heart that as it happens to the fool, so it happens to him, and asked why he was then more wise, concluding that this also is vanity, since death levels the distinction. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: There is no remembrance of the wise more than of the fool for ever, because both are forgotten in days to come, and the wise man dies just as the fool does. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: Therefore he hated life, because the work done under the sun was grievous to him, all of it vanity and vexation of spirit, and the hatred is of life as it is lived apart from God. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

18 COMMENTARY: He hated all his labour because he must leave it to the man after him, having no security that the heir will be wise or will keep what wisdom built. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

19 COMMENTARY: Who knows whether the heir shall be wise or a fool, yet he shall rule over all the labour wherein wisdom was shown, which the Preacher counts vanity and a sore grief. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

20 COMMENTARY: Therefore he caused his heart to despair of all the labour he had taken under the sun, seeing that toil spent on an uncertain inheritance ends in despair. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

21 COMMENTARY: There is a man whose labour is in wisdom, knowledge and equity, yet he must leave it as his portion to a man who has not laboured in it, which is vanity and a great evil. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

22 COMMENTARY: What has a man of all his labour and of the vexation of his heart under the sun, but days of sorrows and grief, with nights that take no rest, which is also vanity. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

23 COMMENTARY: All his days are sorrows and his travail grief, and even at night his heart takes no rest, so that labour pursued as an end becomes its own punishment. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

24 COMMENTARY: There is nothing better for a man than to eat and drink and make his soul enjoy good in his labour, which the Preacher now sees as from the hand of God, to be received as a gift rather than seized as a possession. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

25 COMMENTARY: Who can eat or hasten to enjoy more than the Preacher himself, who had tried every delight, yet enjoyment itself proved to be God's gift and not man's achievement. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

26 COMMENTARY: To the man good before God, God gives wisdom, knowledge and joy, while to the sinner he gives travail to gather and heap up, that he may give it to him who is good before God, which is also vanity and vexation of spirit. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
