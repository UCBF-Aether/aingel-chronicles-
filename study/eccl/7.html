Study Notes — Ecclesiastes

Chapter 7

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: A good name is better than precious ointment, and the day of death than the day of one's birth, because reputation outlasts perfume, and death ends the trial that birth began. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: It is better to go to the house of mourning than to the house of feasting, for that is the end of all men, and the living will lay it to his heart, since mourning teaches what feasting hides. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: Sorrow is better than laughter, for by the sadness of the countenance the heart is made better, because grief sobers and reforms the heart while laughter only flatters it. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: The heart of the wise is in the house of mourning, but the heart of fools is in the house of mirth, because the wise choose the place that instructs, and fools the place that distracts. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: It is better to hear the rebuke of the wise than for a man to hear the song of fools, because a wounding rebuke heals, while flattering song only pleases. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: As the crackling of thorns under a pot, so is the laughter of the fool, loud and brief, giving heat without substance, and this also is vanity. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: Surely oppression makes a wise man mad, and a gift destroys the heart, because even the wise are unsettled by wrong, and a bribe corrupts the judgment of the best. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: Better is the end of a thing than the beginning thereof, and the patient in spirit is better than the proud in spirit, because endings reveal what beginnings only promise, and patience outlasts pride. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: Be not hasty in thy spirit to be angry, for anger rests in the bosom of fools, because a quick temper is a fool's lodging, while wisdom is slow to take offence. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: Say not thou, what is the cause that the former days were better than these, for thou dost not enquire wisely concerning this, because fond memory of the past is no wise judgment on the present. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: Wisdom is good with an inheritance, and by it there is profit to them that see the sun, because wisdom joined to an estate makes the estate useful, while wealth alone is wasted. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: Wisdom is a defence, and money is a defence, but the excellency of knowledge is that wisdom gives life to them that have it, while money can only guard the life it cannot give. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: Consider the work of God, for who can make that straight which he has made crooked, because the disorders of providence are beyond man's mending, and must be submitted to rather than fought. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: In the day of prosperity be joyful, but in the day of adversity consider, for God has set the one over against the other, to the end that man should find nothing after him, since neither state lasts. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: All things he has seen in the days of his vanity, a just man that perishes in his righteousness, and a wicked man that prolongs his life in his wickedness, because in this life providence does not visibly repay each according to his deeds. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: Be not righteous over much, neither make thyself over wise, for why shouldest thou destroy thyself, because a self-righteous severity and a pretended wisdom ruin the very man who boasts them. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: Be not over much wicked, neither be thou foolish, for why shouldest thou die before thy time, because open wickedness and folly cut short the life they abuse. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

18 COMMENTARY: It is good that thou shouldest take hold of this, and also from that not withdraw thy hand, for he that fears God shall come forth of them all, because the fear of God keeps a man from both extremes. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

19 COMMENTARY: Wisdom strengthens the wise more than ten mighty men which are in the city, because one wise counsel is worth more than the force of many captains. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

20 COMMENTARY: There is not a just man upon earth that does good and sins not, because even the best fall short, and no man's record can bear a perfect reckoning. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

21 COMMENTARY: Also take no heed unto all words that are spoken, lest thou hear thy servant curse thee, because listening to every report invites offences that a wise man would pass over. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

22 COMMENTARY: For oftentimes also thine own heart knows that thou thyself likewise hast cursed others, because a man who remembers his own sharp words will forgive sharp words spoken against him. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

23 COMMENTARY: All this he proved by wisdom, saying he would be wise, but it was far from him, because the more wisdom was pursued, the farther its perfection seemed. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

24 COMMENTARY: That which is far off and exceeding deep, who can find it out, because the whole scheme of providence lies beyond man's sounding. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

25 COMMENTARY: He applied his heart to know and to search and to seek out wisdom and the reason of things, and to know the wickedness of folly, even of foolishness and madness, because he would trace folly to its root. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

26 COMMENTARY: He found more bitter than death the woman whose heart is snares and nets and whose hands are bands, because enticement to sin is a deadlier snare than death itself, while he who pleases God escapes her and the sinner is taken by her. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

27 COMMENTARY: Behold, this he found, counting one by one to find out the account, seeking a true reckoning of human character by particular trial, not by general complaint. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

28 COMMENTARY: Which yet his soul seeks but finds not: one man among a thousand he found, but a woman among all those he found not, because upright persons are rare, and he speaks of his own court experience, where corruption had spread widely. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

29 COMMENTARY: Lo, this only he found, that God has made man upright, but they have sought out many inventions, because the fault lies not in man's making but in man's own devices. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
