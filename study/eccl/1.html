Study Notes — Ecclesiastes

Chapter 1

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: The speaker is the Preacher, a son of David and king in Jerusalem, whom Henry identifies as Solomon writing late in life, as one made sensible of his own folly and recording his experience for the benefit of others. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: Vanity of vanities is the text of the whole sermon: everything in this world, considered apart from God, is empty, unable to satisfy the soul or to answer its true end. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: Labour under the sun yields no lasting profit, because all it gains cannot supply the wants of the soul, atone for its sins, or stand it in stead at death and judgment. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: One generation passes and another comes, while the earth abides, so that man is brief against a world that outlasts him and keeps its course without him. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: The sun rises and sets and hastens back to its place, a picture of ceaseless motion that never arrives at rest, like man's own restless pursuits. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: The wind blows in shifting circuits and returns again, constant only in its inconstancy, as all earthly things are in perpetual flux and reflux. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: All rivers run into the sea, yet the sea is never full, because the waters return again to their sources; so the soul, like the sea, is never filled by outward streams of prosperity. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: All things are full of labour beyond what words can utter, and the senses are soon tired yet still craving, the eye never satisfied with seeing nor the ear filled with hearing. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: What has been shall be again, and there is nothing new under the sun, because men's hearts, corruptions, desires and complaints remain the same in every age. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: When something is called new, it has already existed in ages before, and only seems new because the memory of former times has perished. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: There is no remembrance of former things, nor will there be of things to come, so that even things and persons once thought very great are forgotten, a warning against expecting happiness from fame. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: The Preacher speaks from his own station, having been king over Israel in Jerusalem, so his verdict on earthly things rests on trial made from the highest place, not on hearsay. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: He set his heart to seek out by wisdom all that is done under heaven, and found the search itself a sore travail given to men, weariness to flesh and mind alike. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: Having surveyed all the works done under the sun, he judges them all vanity and vexation of spirit, feeding the mind without feeding the soul. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: What is crooked cannot be made straight, and what is wanting cannot be numbered, because human wisdom can neither redress the disorders of the world nor reckon up its defects. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: He communed with his heart that he had attained greatness and wisdom beyond all before him in Jerusalem, and that his heart had great experience of wisdom and knowledge. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: He gave his heart to know wisdom and also madness and folly, and perceived that even this pursuit was vexation of spirit, since it exposed more of man's wickedness and misery. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

18 COMMENTARY: In much wisdom is much grief, and he that increases knowledge increases sorrow, because clearer sight of the world brings clearer sight of its vanity and of sin, the cause of it all. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
