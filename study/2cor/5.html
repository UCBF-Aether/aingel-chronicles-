Study Notes — 2 Corinthians

Chapter 5

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: For we know that if our earthly house of this tabernacle were dissolved, we have a building of God, an house not made with hands, eternal in the heavens. Henry notes the tent is the body, easily taken down, and the building is God's own work, not made with hands, eternal in heaven. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: For in this we groan, earnestly desiring to be clothed upon with our house which is from heaven. JFB notes the groan is not for death but for the heavenly clothing: the believer longs to be clothed upon, not stripped. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: If so be that being clothed we shall not be found naked. Henry notes the condition guards the hope: the resurrection body clothes, so that the soul is not left naked, unhoused between death and glory. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: For we that are in this tabernacle do groan, being burdened: not for that we would be unclothed, but clothed upon, that mortality might be swallowed up of life. JFB notes the groan is a burdened groan, and its desire is precise: not to be unclothed by death, but clothed upon, so that mortality is swallowed by life. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: Now he that has wrought us for the selfsame thing is God, who also has given unto us the earnest of the Spirit. Henry notes God himself fashions believers for this heavenly house, and the Spirit's earnest is his pledge that the house will follow. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: Therefore we are always confident, knowing that, whilst we are at home in the body, we are absent from the Lord. JFB notes confidence and absence stand together: confidence about the end does not cancel the present absence from the Lord. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: (For we walk by faith, not by sight:) Henry notes the parenthesis is the rule of the present life: faith, not sight, is the way believers walk while absent. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: We are confident, I say, and willing rather to be absent from the body, and to be present with the Lord. JFB notes the preference is stated without fear: absence from the body is presence with the Lord, and the apostle is willing for the exchange. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: Wherefore we labour, that, whether present or absent, we may be accepted of him. Henry notes the labour has one aim in both states: acceptance with the Lord, whether in the body or out of it. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: For we must all appear before the judgment seat of Christ; that every one may receive the things done in his body, according to that he has done, whether it be good or bad. JFB notes the judgment seat is universal and personal: each receives according to deeds done in the body, good or bad. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: Knowing therefore the terror of the Lord, we persuade men; but we are made manifest unto God; and I trust also are made manifest in your consciences. Henry notes the terror of the Lord drives persuasion, not terrorising: Paul persuades men, while God already sees him plainly, and he hopes Corinth's conscience does too. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: For we commend not ourselves again unto you, but give you occasion to glory on our behalf, that ye may have somewhat to answer them which glory in appearance, and not in heart. JFB notes Paul arms Corinth against the false teachers: they may glory in appearance, but Paul gives his people a true ground to answer them. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: For whether we be beside ourselves, it is to God: or whether we be sober, it is for your cause. Henry notes both states are accounted for: ecstasy, if any, is God's, and soberness is Corinth's, so that neither can be charged against him. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: For the love of Christ constrains us; because we thus judge, that if one died for all, then were all dead. JFB notes love is the constraint, and the judgment is its ground: one died for all, therefore all were dead, and love leaves no room for self-serving ministry. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: And that he died for all, that they which live should not henceforth live unto themselves, but unto him which died for them, and rose again. Henry notes the purpose of the death: the living no longer live to themselves, but to the one who died and rose for them. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: Wherefore henceforth know we no man after the flesh: yea, though we have known Christ after the flesh, yet now henceforth know we him no more. JFB notes the new knowledge: men are no longer judged by fleshly measures, and even Christ is no longer known merely after the flesh, but in his risen glory. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: Therefore if any man be in Christ, he is a new creature: old things are passed away; behold, all things are become new. Henry notes the new creation is total: in Christ, the old passes and all becomes new, not patched but recreated. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

18 COMMENTARY: And all things are of God, who has reconciled us to himself by Jesus Christ, and has given to us the ministry of reconciliation. JFB notes reconciliation is wholly God's work, and the ministry of it is his gift to those he has reconciled. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

19 COMMENTARY: To wit, that God was in Christ, reconciling the world unto himself, not imputing their trespasses unto them; and has committed unto us the word of reconciliation. Henry notes the word of reconciliation has two parts: God in Christ, and trespasses not imputed, and the word itself is entrusted to ministers. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

20 COMMENTARY: Now then we are ambassadors for Christ, as though God did beseech you by us: we pray you in Christ's stead, be ye reconciled to God. JFB notes the ambassador's dignity and urgency: God beseeches through his ministers, and the plea is in Christ's stead, be reconciled. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

21 COMMENTARY: For he has made him to be sin for us, who knew no sin; that we might be made the righteousness of God in him. Henry notes the great exchange: the sinless one made sin, that sinners might be made God's righteousness in him. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
