Study Notes — 2 Corinthians

Chapter 9

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: For as touching the ministering to the saints, it is superfluous for me to write to you. Henry notes Paul begins as if the subject needed no writing, because Corinth's readiness is already known, and the writing that follows is to secure the doing. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: For I know the forwardness of your mind, for which I boast of you to them of Macedonia, that Achaia was ready a year ago; and your zeal has provoked very many. JFB notes the boasting is reciprocal: Paul boasted of Corinth to Macedonia, and Macedonia's zeal now provokes Corinth in return. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: Yet have I sent the brethren, lest our boasting of you should be in vain in this behalf; that, as I said, ye may be ready. Henry notes the brethren are sent to make the boast true: readiness a year ago must still be readiness when the collectors arrive. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: Lest haply if they of Macedonia come with me, and find you unprepared, we (that we say not, ye) should be ashamed in this same confident boasting. JFB notes the shame would fall on Paul first, and then on Corinth: a boast unfulfilled shames both boaster and boasted-of. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: Therefore I thought it necessary to exhort the brethren, that they would go before unto you, and make up beforehand your bounty, whereof ye had notice before, that the same might be ready, as a matter of bounty, and not as of covetousness. Henry notes the bounty must be ready beforehand, so that it looks like bounty, not like money wrung out by pressure at the last moment. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: But this I say, He which sows sparingly shall reap also sparingly; and he which sows bountifully shall reap also bountifully. JFB notes the harvest rule: sowing and reaping keep proportion, sparing with sparing, bountiful with bountiful. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: Every man according as he purposes in his heart, so let him give; not grudgingly, or of necessity: for God loves a cheerful giver. Henry notes the giving rule: purposed in the heart, not grudging, not of necessity, because God loves the cheerful giver, and cheerfulness is part of the gift. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: And God is able to make all grace abound toward you; that ye, always having all sufficiency in all things, may abound to every good work. JFB notes the ability is God's: all grace abounding produces all sufficiency in all things, and sufficiency is for abounding in every good work, not for hoarding. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: (As it is written, He has dispersed abroad; he has given to the poor: his righteousness remains for ever.) Henry notes the psalm's liberal man disperses and gives, and his righteousness remains: giving does not diminish righteousness, it proves its permanence. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: Now he that ministers seed to the sower both minister bread for your food, and multiply your seed sown, and increase the fruits of your righteousness. JFB notes God supplies both seed and bread: he gives what is sown and what is eaten, and multiplies the seed while increasing righteousness's fruits. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: Being enriched in every thing to all bountifulness, which causes through us thanksgiving to God. Henry notes enrichment aims at bountifulness, and bountifulness aims at thanksgiving: the gift's end is praise to God through the givers' hands. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: For the administration of this service not only supplies the want of the saints, but is abundant also by many thanksgivings unto God. JFB notes the service has two abundances: it fills the saints' want, and it overflows in many thanksgivings to God. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: Whiles by the experiment of this ministration they glorify God for your professed subjection unto the gospel of Christ, and for your liberal distribution unto them, and unto all men. Henry notes the Jerusalem saints read the gift as proof: Corinth's subjection to the gospel is proved by liberal distribution, to them and to all. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: And by their prayer for you, which long after you for the exceeding grace of God in you. JFB notes the gift returns as prayer: the saints pray for Corinth and long after them, because they see God's exceeding grace in the givers. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: Thanks be unto God for his unspeakable gift. Henry notes the chapter ends beyond words: after all the reasoning about giving, Paul thanks God for the gift that cannot be spoken, Christ himself, the pattern and giver of all giving. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
