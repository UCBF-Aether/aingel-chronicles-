Study Notes — 2 Corinthians

Chapter 12

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: It is not expedient for me doubtless to glory. I will come to visions and revelations of the Lord. JFB notes glorying is not expedient, yet Paul will come to visions and revelations, because the rivals force him to speak of what he would rather hide. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: I knew a man in Christ above fourteen years ago, (whether in the body, I cannot tell; or whether out of the body, I cannot tell: God knows;) such an one caught up to the third heaven. Henry notes the man is Paul himself, spoken of in the third person, caught up fourteen years before to the third heaven, and even he cannot tell whether in body or out of it. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: And I knew such a man, (whether in the body, or out of the body, I cannot tell: God knows;). JFB notes the repetition stresses the ignorance: twice Paul confesses he does not know the manner, and leaves it to God who knows. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: How that he was caught up into paradise, and heard unspeakable words, which it is not lawful for a man to utter. Henry notes paradise is named alongside the third heaven, and the words heard there are unspeakable and unlawful to utter, so the vision gives Paul nothing to boast with in detail. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: Of such an one will I glory: yet of myself I will not glory, but in mine infirmities. JFB notes the distinction: Paul will glory of the man caught up, but of himself, his present self, he will glory only in infirmities. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: For though I would desire to glory, I shall not be a fool; for I will say the truth: but now I forbear, lest any man should think of me above that which he sees me to be, or that he hears of me. Henry notes Paul forbears for the hearers' sake: he will not let men think him above what they see and hear of him, though the truth would allow more. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: And lest I should be exalted above measure through the abundance of the revelations, there was given to me a thorn in the flesh, the messenger of Satan to buffet me, lest I should be exalted above measure. JFB notes the thorn is given against exaltation: a messenger of Satan to buffet, permitted by God, to keep the man of revelations low. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: For this thing I besought the Lord thrice, that it might depart from me. Henry notes the prayer is thrice repeated, like the Lord's own prayer in Gethsemane, and its answer is not removal but sufficiency. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: And he said unto me, My grace is sufficient for thee: for my strength is made perfect in weakness. Most gladly therefore will I rather glory in my infirmities, that the power of Christ may rest upon me. JFB notes the answer turns weakness into the place of strength: grace sufficient, strength perfected in weakness, so Paul glories gladly in infirmities that Christ's power may rest on him. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: Therefore I take pleasure in infirmities, in reproaches, in necessities, in persecutions, in distresses for Christ's sake: for when I am weak, then am I strong. Henry notes pleasure in such a list is the paradox proved: when weak, then strong, because Christ's strength fills the weakness. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: I am become a fool in glorying; ye have compelled me: for I ought to have been commended of you: for in nothing am I behind the very chiefest apostles, though I be nothing. JFB notes the compulsion is Corinth's: they ought to have commended Paul, and their silence compelled his foolish glorying, in which he is nothing, yet behind no apostle. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: Truly the signs of an apostle were wrought among you in all patience, in signs, and wonders, and mighty deeds. Henry notes the signs are the apostle's true credentials, wrought among Corinth itself in patience, signs, wonders and mighty deeds. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: For what is it wherein ye were inferior to other churches, except it be that I myself was not burdensome to you? forgive me this wrong. JFB notes the irony returns: Corinth's only inferiority is that Paul was not burdensome, and he asks forgiveness for that 'wrong.' — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: Behold, the third time I am ready to come to you; and I will not be burdensome to you: for I seek not yours but you: for the children ought not to lay up for the parents, but the parents for the children. Henry notes the third coming is announced with the same policy: not burdensome, seeking not theirs but them, as parents lay up for children, not children for parents. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: And I will very gladly spend and be spent for you; though the more abundantly I love you, the less I be loved. JFB notes the spending is total: spend and be spent, gladly, even though greater love is met with less love in return. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: But be it so, I did not burden you: nevertheless, being crafty, I caught you with guile. Henry notes Paul quotes the slander: granted he did not burden, yet, say the critics, being crafty he caught them with guile, through Titus and the collection. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: Did I make a gain of you by any of them whom I sent unto you? JFB notes the question invites audit: name any emissary, and ask whether Paul gained by him. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

18 COMMENTARY: Henry notes Titus is the test case: if he made no gain and walked in the same spirit and steps as Paul, then the guile charge against Paul falls with it. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

19 COMMENTARY: Again, think ye that we excuse ourselves unto you? we speak before God in Christ: but we do all things, dearly beloved, for your edifying. JFB notes the defence is not an excuse: spoken before God in Christ, and done for Corinth's edifying, not for Paul's reputation. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

20 COMMENTARY: For I fear, lest, when I come, I shall not find you such as I would, and that I shall be found unto you such as ye would not: lest there be debates, envyings, wraths, strifes, backbitings, whisperings, swellings, tumults. Henry notes the fear is mutual disappointment: Paul may find Corinth unlike his hope, and Corinth may find Paul unlike its wish, with eight sins of discord named as the danger. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

21 COMMENTARY: And lest, when I come again, my God will humble me among you, and that I shall bewail many which have sinned already, and have not repented of the uncleanness and fornication and lasciviousness which they have committed. JFB notes the humbling is pastoral: Paul would be humbled to bewail unrepented sinners, whose uncleanness, fornication and lasciviousness remain unrepented. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
