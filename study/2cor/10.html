Study Notes — 2 Corinthians

Chapter 10

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Now I Paul myself beseech you by the meekness and gentleness of Christ, who in presence am base among you, but being absent am bold toward you. Henry notes Paul opens the defence in Christ's own temper, meekness and gentleness, and quotes his critics' charge: base in presence, bold in absence. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: But I beseech you, that I may not be bold when I am present with that confidence, wherewith I think to be bold against some, which think of us as if we walked according to the flesh. JFB notes Paul hopes not to need boldness in person: it is reserved for those who charge him with walking after the flesh, by worldly policy. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: For though we walk in the flesh, we do not war after the flesh. Henry notes the distinction: living in flesh is unavoidable, warring after the flesh is a choice, and Paul disowns the choice. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: (For the weapons of our warfare are not carnal, but mighty through God to the pulling down of strong holds;) JFB notes the weapons are mighty through God, not through carnal force, and their work is demolition: strongholds pulled down, not merely battered. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: Casting down imaginations, and every high thing that exalts itself against the knowledge of God, and bringing into captivity every thought to the obedience of Christ. Henry notes the campaign is inward: imaginations and high things are cast down, and every thought is taken captive to Christ's obedience. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: And having in a readiness to revenge all disobedience, when your obedience is fulfilled. JFB notes discipline waits on obedience: Paul is ready to punish disobedience, but only when the church's obedience is fulfilled, so that the innocent are not swept up with the guilty. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: Do ye look on things after the outward appearance? If any man trust to himself that he is Christ's, let him of himself think this again, that, as he is Christ's, even so are we Christ's. Henry notes the test is outward appearance, and it cuts both ways: if a critic is Christ's on his own trust, Paul is Christ's on the same ground. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: For though I should boast somewhat more of our authority, which the Lord has given us for edification, and not for your destruction, I should not be ashamed. JFB notes authority's purpose bounds its boasting: given for edification, not destruction, Paul need not be ashamed to claim it. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: That I may not seem as if I would terrify you by letters. Henry notes Paul guards against the charge that his letters are threats: he writes to edify, not to terrify from a distance. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: For his letters, say they, are weighty and powerful; but his bodily presence is weak, and his speech contemptible. JFB notes the critics' taunt is quoted verbatim: weighty letters, weak presence, contemptible speech, the standard charge against a man whose pen outruns his person. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: Let such an one think this, that, such as we are in word by letters when we are absent, such will we be also in deed when we are present. Henry notes the answer is a promise: absence in word will match presence in deed, and the critics will find the man equal to his letters. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: For we dare not make ourselves of the number, or compare ourselves with some that commend themselves: but they measuring themselves by themselves, and comparing themselves among themselves, are not wise. JFB notes self-measurement is folly: the false teachers measure by themselves and compare among themselves, a circle that always flatters. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: But we will not boast of things without our measure, but according to the measure of the rule which God has distributed to us, a measure to reach even unto you. Henry notes Paul's boast has a measure: the rule God distributed, which reaches to Corinth, and no farther is claimed. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: For we stretch not ourselves beyond our measure, as though we reached not unto you: for we are come as far as to you also in preaching the gospel of Christ. JFB notes Corinth is within Paul's measure because he came first with the gospel there: he did not stretch into another man's field. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: Not boasting of things without our measure, that is, of other men's labours; but having hope, when your faith is increased, that we shall be enlarged by you according to our rule abundantly. Henry notes the hope is orderly: as Corinth's faith grows, Paul's rule enlarges through them, without boasting in others' labours. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: To preach the gospel in the regions beyond you, and not to boast in another man's line of things made ready to our hand. JFB notes the aim beyond Corinth: regions beyond, not another man's ready-made line, and the gospel carried where it has not yet been preached. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: But he that glories, let him glory in the Lord. Henry notes the rule that ends all boasting: glory, if there must be glory, is in the Lord alone. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

18 COMMENTARY: For not he that commends himself is approved, but whom the Lord commends. JFB notes approval is the Lord's to give: self-commendation proves nothing, and the Lord's commendation proves everything. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
