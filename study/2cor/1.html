Study Notes — 2 Corinthians

Chapter 1

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Paul, an apostle of Jesus Christ by the will of God, and Timothy our brother, write to the church of God at Corinth, with all the saints in all Achaia. Henry notes the authority is not Paul's own choosing but God's will, and the address widens from one church to a whole province. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: Grace be to you and peace from God our Father, and from the Lord Jesus Christ. JFB notes the greeting joins the two gifts the whole epistle will unfold: grace as the source, peace as the fruit, from Father and Son together. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: Blessed be God, even the Father of our Lord Jesus Christ, the Father of mercies, and the God of all comfort. Henry notes Paul blesses God before he mentions his trouble, and the titles name God as the fountain of both mercy and comfort. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: Who comforts us in all our tribulation, that we may be able to comfort them in any trouble, by the comfort wherewith we ourselves are comforted of God. JFB notes comfort is not kept: it is received in order to be passed on, and experience of it qualifies the comforter. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: For as the sufferings of Christ abound in us, so our consolation also abounds by Christ. Henry notes the proportion: suffering and consolation rise together, and both come through Christ, so neither is borne alone. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: And whether we be afflicted, it is for your consolation and salvation, which is effectual in enduring the same sufferings which we also suffer: or whether we be comforted, it is for your consolation and salvation. JFB notes both Paul's affliction and his comfort serve Corinth: his endurance teaches theirs, and his comfort encourages theirs. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: And our hope of you is steadfast, knowing, that as ye are partakers of the sufferings, so shall ye be also of the consolation. Henry notes the hope rests on a partnership: sharers in suffering will be sharers in comfort, by the same rule. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: For we would not have you ignorant of our trouble in Asia, that we were pressed out of measure, above strength, insomuch that we despaired even of life. JFB notes Paul tells Corinth of a trouble that passed human strength, so that the deliverance that follows is plainly God's. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: But we had the sentence of death in ourselves, that we should not trust in ourselves, but in God which raises the dead. Henry notes the sentence of death was the cure for self-trust: despair of life drove Paul to the God who raises the dead. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: Who delivered us from so great a death, and does deliver: in whom we trust that he will yet deliver us. JFB notes three tenses of deliverance: past, present and future, all resting on the same God. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: Ye also helping together by prayer for us, that for the gift bestowed upon us by the means of many persons thanks may be given by many on our behalf. Henry notes Corinth's prayers are part of the deliverance, and many prayers will mean many thanksgivings. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: For our rejoicing is this, the testimony of our conscience, that in simplicity and godly sincerity, not with fleshly wisdom, but by the grace of God, we have had our conversation in the world, and more abundantly to you-ward. JFB notes Paul's boast is his conscience: a life lived in sincerity by grace, not by worldly craft, especially toward Corinth. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: For we write none other things unto you, than what ye read or acknowledge; and I trust ye shall acknowledge even to the end. Henry notes Paul writes plainly: there is no hidden meaning behind his words for Corinth to suspect. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: As also ye have acknowledged us in part, that we are your rejoicing, even as ye also are ours in the day of the Lord Jesus. JFB notes the mutual rejoicing is deferred to the Lord's day, when minister and people will rejoice in each other before Christ. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: And in this confidence I was minded to come unto you before, that ye might have a second benefit. Henry notes the planned first visit was meant as a double blessing: coming and going through Corinth on the way to Macedonia and back. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: And to pass by you into Macedonia, and to come again out of Macedonia unto you, and of you to be brought on my way toward Judaea. JFB notes the itinerary shows Paul intended Corinth as his base twice over, not as a place to avoid. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: When I therefore was thus minded, did I use lightness? or the things that I purpose, do I purpose according to the flesh, that with me there should be yea yea, and nay nay? Henry notes Paul meets the charge of fickleness head-on: changing a plan is not the same as breaking a promise. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

18 COMMENTARY: But as God is true, our word toward you was not yea and nay. JFB notes Paul appeals to God's truthfulness for his own: his preaching, like God's character, does not contradict itself. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

19 COMMENTARY: For the Son of God, Jesus Christ, who was preached among you by us, even by me and Silvanus and Timotheus, was not yea and nay, but in him was yea. Henry notes the preached Christ is the proof: a gospel that is 'yea' in Christ cannot come from a double-tongued apostle. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

20 COMMENTARY: For all the promises of God in him are yea, and in him Amen, unto the glory of God by us. JFB notes every promise finds its yes in Christ and its amen in the church's assent, to God's glory. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

21 COMMENTARY: Now he which establishes us with you in Christ, and has anointed us, is God. Henry notes establishment and anointing are God's acts, covering both apostle and people in the same Christ. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

22 COMMENTARY: Who has also sealed us, and given the earnest of the Spirit in our hearts. JFB notes seal and earnest together: the Spirit marks believers as God's own and gives a first instalment of the inheritance to come. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

23 COMMENTARY: Moreover I call God for a record upon my soul, that to spare you I came not as yet unto Corinth. Henry notes the real reason for the delayed visit is mercy: Paul stayed away to spare Corinth a painful visit of discipline. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

24 COMMENTARY: Not for that we have dominion over your faith, but are helpers of your joy: for by faith ye stand. JFB notes apostolic authority is bounded: Paul helps joy and serves faith, he does not lord over it, for faith stands by itself before God. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
