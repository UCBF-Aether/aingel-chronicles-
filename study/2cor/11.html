Study Notes — 2 Corinthians

Chapter 11

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Would to God ye could bear with me a little in my folly: and indeed bear with me. Henry notes Paul asks leave to be foolish for a little, because the false teachers have forced him to a boasting he despises, and Corinth already bears with him. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: For I am jealous over you with godly jealousy: for I have espoused you to one husband, that I may present you as a chaste virgin to Christ. JFB notes the jealousy is godly, like a father's for a betrothed daughter: Corinth is espoused to one husband, Christ, and must be presented chaste to him. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: But I fear, lest by any means, as the serpent beguiled Eve through his subtilty, so your minds should be corrupted from the simplicity that is in Christ. Henry notes the fear is Eve's pattern: subtilty corrupting minds from simplicity, so that a church, like Eve, is beguiled away from single devotion to Christ. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: For if he that comes preaches another Jesus, whom we have not preached, or if ye receive another spirit, which ye have not received, or another gospel, which ye have not accepted, ye might well bear with him. JFB notes the irony: Corinth bears gladly with a preacher of another Jesus, another spirit, another gospel, while questioning the apostle who preached the true ones. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: For I suppose I was not a whit behind the very chiefest apostles. Henry notes the claim is measured: not above the chiefest apostles, but not a whit behind them, a defence against those who ranked Paul below his rivals. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: But though I be rude in speech, yet not in knowledge; but we have been throughly made manifest among you in all things. JFB notes Paul concedes rude speech, the critics' charge, but denies lack of knowledge, and appeals to full manifestation among Corinth itself. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: Have I committed an offence in abasing myself that ye might be exalted, because I have preached to you the gospel of God freely? Henry notes the offence charged is generosity: Paul abased himself and preached freely, that Corinth might be exalted, and this is turned against him. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: I robbed other churches, taking wages of them, to do you service. JFB notes the irony is sharp: Paul 'robbed' other churches, taking their wages, so that Corinth could have the gospel free. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: And when I was present with you, and wanted, I was chargeable to no man: for that which was lacking to me the brethren which came from Macedonia supplied: and in all things I have kept myself from being burdensome unto you, and so will I keep myself. Henry notes the record is complete: present and wanting, yet chargeable to none, supplied by Macedonia, and resolved to remain unburdensome. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: As the truth of Christ is in me, no man shall stop me of this boasting in the regions of Achaia. JFB notes the oath is by Christ's truth in him: the boasting of free preaching in Achaia will not be stopped by any man. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: Henry notes Paul anticipates the slander that refusal of support means lack of love, and appeals to God, who knows the contrary, as his witness. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: But what I do, that I will do, that I may cut off occasion from them which desire occasion; that wherein they glory, they may be found even as we. JFB notes the policy: by preaching free, Paul cuts off the occasion the false teachers seek, so that if they glory in free preaching they must be found doing as he does. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: For such are false apostles, deceitful workers, transforming themselves into the apostles of Christ. Henry notes the naming is plain at last: false apostles, deceitful workers, wearing an apostolic shape they do not possess. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: And no marvel; for Satan himself is transformed into an angel of light. JFB notes the transformation has a master: Satan himself appears as an angel of light, so his servants' disguise is no wonder. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: Therefore it is no great thing if his ministers also be transformed as the ministers of righteousness; whose end shall be according to their works. Henry notes the end follows the works: ministers of a disguised righteousness will end according to what they have done, not what they seemed. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: I say again, Let no man think me a fool; if otherwise, yet as a fool receive me, that I may boast myself a little. JFB notes Paul repeats the plea: if he must seem a fool to boast, then receive him as a fool, for a little boasting, to answer the false teachers. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: That which I speak, I speak it not after the Lord, but as it were foolishly, in this confidence of boasting. Henry notes the disclaimer is honest: boasting is not after the Lord's pattern, and Paul speaks it as folly, forced by the occasion. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

18 COMMENTARY: Seeing that many glory after the flesh, I will glory also. JFB notes the concession: since many glory after the flesh, Paul will glory too, lest silence be read as defeat. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

19 COMMENTARY: For ye suffer fools gladly, seeing ye yourselves are wise. Henry notes the irony bites: Corinth, wise in its own eyes, suffers fools gladly, and Paul asks to be suffered as one. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

20 COMMENTARY: For ye suffer, if a man bring you into bondage, if a man devour you, if a man take of you, if a man exalt himself, if a man smite you on the face. JFB notes the five sufferings name the false teachers' tyranny: bondage, devouring, taking, exalting, smiting, all borne by Corinth from men it calls apostles. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

21 COMMENTARY: I speak as concerning reproach, as though we had been weak. Howbeit whereinsoever any is bold, (I speak foolishly,) I am bold also. Henry notes Paul speaks as if reproached for weakness, and then takes up the boldness challenge on the rivals' own ground. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

22 COMMENTARY: JFB notes the pedigree match is exact, Hebrew, Israelite and Abraham's seed in turn, so Paul matches every claim of birth the rivals make. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

23 COMMENTARY: Are they ministers of Christ? (I speak as a fool) I am more; in labours more abundant, in stripes above measure, in prisons more frequent, in deaths oft. Henry notes the claim passes pedigree to service: more labours, more stripes, more prisons, more deaths, and the 'fool' disclaimer shows Paul knows how this sounds. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

24 COMMENTARY: Of the Jews five times received I forty stripes save one. JFB notes the Jewish scourging, thirty-nine stripes, five times, a punishment Paul bore as a Jew from his own people, for Christ's sake. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

25 COMMENTARY: Thrice was I beaten with rods, once was I stoned, thrice I suffered shipwreck, a night and a day I have been in the deep. Henry notes the Roman rods, the stoning, the three shipwrecks and the night and day adrift are counted without complaint, as a minister's ledger. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

26 COMMENTARY: In journeyings often, in perils of waters, in perils of robbers, in perils by mine own countrymen, in perils by the heathen, in perils in the city, in perils in the wilderness, in perils in the sea, in perils among false brethren. JFB notes the eight perils cover every road and every company: waters, robbers, countrymen, heathen, city, wilderness, sea, and worst, false brethren. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

27 COMMENTARY: In weariness and painfulness, in watchings often, in hunger and thirst, in fastings often, in cold and nakedness. Henry notes the bodily catalogue closes the sufferings: weariness, pain, sleeplessness, hunger, thirst, fasts, cold and nakedness, the daily wear of the ministry. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

28 COMMENTARY: Beside those things that are without, that which comes upon me daily, the care of all the churches. JFB notes the inward burden outweighs the outward list: besides all these, the daily care of all the churches presses on Paul. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

29 COMMENTARY: Who is weak, and I am not weak? who is offended, and I burn not? Henry notes the care is sympathetic: another's weakness is Paul's weakness, another's offence is Paul's burning, because the churches live in his heart. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

30 COMMENTARY: If I must needs glory, I will glory of the things which concern mine infirmities. JFB notes the boast is redirected: if glory there must be, it will be in infirmities, the very things the world counts shameful. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

31 COMMENTARY: The God and Father of our Lord Jesus Christ, which is blessed for evermore, knows that I lie not. Henry notes the oath before the Damascus escape: God, blessed for ever, is witness that the strange story that follows is true. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

32 COMMENTARY: In Damascus the governor under Aretas the king kept the city of the Damascenes with a garrison, desirous to apprehend me. JFB notes the governor of a king's city, with a garrison, is set to seize one man, showing how far Paul's preaching had alarmed power. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

33 COMMENTARY: And through a window in a basket was I let down by the wall, and escaped his hands. Henry notes the escape is humiliating and undignified, a basket through a window, and Paul tells it as glory, because it is glory in infirmity. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
