Study Notes — 2 Corinthians

Chapter 13

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: This is the third time I am coming to you. In the mouth of two or three witnesses shall every word be established. Henry notes the third coming is announced as a judicial visit: every word established by two or three witnesses, as the law requires for judgment. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: I told you before, and foretell you, as if I were present, the second time; and being absent now I write to them which heretofore have sinned, and to all other, that, if I come again, I will not spare. JFB notes the warning is given twice, present and absent, and its content is plain: coming again, he will not spare the impenitent. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: Since ye seek a proof of Christ speaking in me, which to you-ward is not weak, but is mighty in you. Henry notes the proof sought will be found: Christ speaking in Paul is not weak toward Corinth, but mighty in Corinth itself, in the very church that doubts him. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: For though he was crucified through weakness, yet he lives by the power of God. For we also are weak in him, but we shall live with him by the power of God toward you. JFB notes the parallel: Christ crucified in weakness lives by God's power, and Paul, weak in him, will live with him by God's power toward Corinth. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: Examine yourselves, whether ye be in the faith; prove your own selves. Know ye not your own selves, how that Jesus Christ is in you, except ye be reprobates? Henry notes the examination is turned back: before seeking proof of Christ in Paul, Corinth must prove Christ in itself, and the test is whether Christ is in them. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: But I trust that ye shall know that we are not reprobates. JFB notes the trust is gentle after the sharp test: Paul trusts Corinth will know he is not reprobate, once it has examined itself. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: Now I pray to God that ye do no evil; not that we should appear approved, but that ye should do that which is honest, though we be as reprobates. Henry notes the prayer prefers their honesty to his approval: Paul would rather be thought reprobate himself than see Corinth do evil to prove him approved. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: For we can do nothing against the truth, but for the truth. JFB notes apostolic power is bounded by truth: it cannot act against the truth, only for it, so discipline, if it comes, will be truth's servant. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: For we are glad, when we are weak, and ye are strong: and this also we wish, even your perfection. Henry notes the gladness is self-emptying: Paul is glad to be weak if Corinth is strong, and his wish is their perfection, not his vindication. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: Therefore I write these things being absent, lest being present I should use sharpness, according to the power which the Lord has given me to edification, and not to destruction. JFB notes the writing is meant to prevent sharpness: absent words now, so that present power need not be sharp, and the power itself is for edification, not destruction. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: Finally, brethren, farewell. Be perfect, be of good comfort, be of one mind, live in peace; and the God of love and peace shall be with you. Henry notes the farewell is a cluster of commands: perfection, comfort, one mind, peace, and the promise that the God of love and peace will be with a church that lives so. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: Greet one another with an holy kiss. JFB notes the holy kiss is the greeting of a reconciled church, sealing the epistle's plea for peace with a sign of affection. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: All the saints salute you. Henry notes the salutation widens again: all the saints, not only Paul, greet Corinth, as the epistle began with all the saints of Achaia. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: The grace of the Lord Jesus Christ, and the love of God, and the communion of the Holy Ghost, be with you all. Amen. JFB notes the benediction names the Trinity in order: Christ's grace, God's love, the Spirit's communion, with all, and the amen seals the whole epistle. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
