Study Notes — 2 Corinthians

Chapter 4

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Therefore seeing we have this ministry, as we have received mercy, we faint not. Henry notes the ministry is received as mercy, not earned, and that is why its ministers do not faint under its weight. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: But have renounced the hidden things of dishonesty, not walking in craftiness, nor handling the word of God deceitfully; but by manifestation of the truth commending ourselves to every man's conscience in the sight of God. JFB notes the contrast with the false teachers: no hidden craft, no deceitful handling of Scripture, only truth set before every conscience in God's sight. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: But if our gospel be hid, it is hid to them that are lost. Henry notes the hiding is not in the preaching, which is plain, but in the hearers: the gospel is hidden to those already perishing. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: In whom the god of this world has blinded the minds of them which believe not, lest the light of the glorious gospel of Christ, who is the image of God, should shine unto them. JFB notes Satan, as god of this world, blinds minds against the light, and Christ is named as the image of God, the very light they are kept from seeing. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: For we preach not ourselves, but Christ Jesus the Lord; and ourselves your servants for Jesus' sake. Henry notes the preacher's subject and the preacher's place: Christ is Lord, Paul is servant, and the service is for Jesus' sake, not for his own. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: For God, who commanded the light to shine out of darkness, has shined in our hearts, to give the light of the knowledge of the glory of God in the face of Jesus Christ. JFB notes creation's first light is the pattern: as God said 'let light shine out of darkness' at creation, so he shines in hearts, and the light is God's glory seen in Christ's face. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: But we have this treasure in earthen vessels, that the excellency of the power may be of God, and not of us. Henry notes the vessel is deliberately fragile: the treasure's power must be God's, because the carrier is only clay. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: We are troubled on every side, yet not distressed; we are perplexed, but not in despair. JFB notes the pairs hold tension without collapse: trouble without distress, perplexity without despair, because the power is not the vessel's. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: Persecuted, but not forsaken; cast down, but not destroyed. Henry notes the second pair: enemies may pursue and strike down, but they cannot forsake or destroy, because God stands with the persecuted. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: Always bearing about in the body the dying of the Lord Jesus, that the life also of Jesus might be made manifest in our body. JFB notes the apostle's body carries Jesus' dying daily, so that Jesus' life is displayed in the same body that suffers. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: For we which live are always delivered unto death for Jesus' sake, that the life also of Jesus might be made manifest in our mortal flesh. Henry notes the paradox of ministry: living men are daily given over to death, so that life reaches others through their dying. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: So then death works in us, but life in you. JFB notes the exchange: Paul's dying is Corinth's living, and the ministry's cost is the church's gain. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: We having the same spirit of faith, according as it is written, I believed, and therefore have I spoken; we also believe, and therefore speak. Henry notes the quotation from the psalmist joins faith and speech: belief that does not speak is not the apostles' faith. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: Knowing that he which raised up the Lord Jesus shall raise up us also by Jesus, and shall present us with you. JFB notes resurrection is the ground of speech: the God who raised Jesus will raise his ministers and present them together with their people. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: For all things are for your sakes, that the abundant grace might through the thanksgiving of many redound to the glory of God. Henry notes grace multiplies thanksgiving, and thanksgiving multiplies glory: the ministry's sufferings end in many mouths praising God. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: For which cause we faint not; but though our outward man perish, yet the inward man is renewed day by day. JFB notes the outward man wastes under affliction, but the inward man is renewed daily, so fainting has no ground. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: For our light affliction, which is but for a moment, works for us a far more exceeding and eternal weight of glory. Henry notes the scales: a light, momentary affliction against an exceeding, eternal weight of glory, and the affliction itself works the glory. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

18 COMMENTARY: While we look not at the things which are seen, but at the things which are not seen: for the things which are seen are temporal; but the things which are not seen are eternal. JFB notes the gaze decides the weight: seen things are temporary, unseen things eternal, and faith looks at the unseen. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
