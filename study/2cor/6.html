Study Notes — 2 Corinthians

Chapter 6

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: We then, as workers together with him, beseech you also that ye receive not the grace of God in vain. JFB notes ministers are fellow-workers with God, and the beseeching is that grace received be not received in vain, without fruit. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: (For he says, I have heard thee in a time accepted, and in the day of salvation have I succoured thee: behold, now is the accepted time; behold, now is the day of salvation.) Henry notes the quotation from Isaiah is applied to the present: the accepted time is not future, it is now, and the day of salvation is today. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: Giving no offence in any thing, that the ministry be not blamed. JFB notes the minister's care extends to offence: nothing in his conduct must give the ministry a bad name. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: But in all things approving ourselves as the ministers of God, in much patience, in afflictions, in necessities, in distresses. Henry notes the approval is proved in endurance: patience under affliction, necessity and distress is the minister's credential. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: In stripes, in imprisonments, in tumults, in labours, in watchings, in fastings. JFB notes the list moves from sufferings inflicted by others to disciplines imposed by himself: stripes and prisons, tumults, labours, sleepless nights and fasts. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: By pureness, by knowledge, by longsuffering, by kindness, by the Holy Ghost, by love unfeigned. Henry notes the inner furniture matches the outer endurance: purity, knowledge, patience, kindness, the Spirit's power and unfeigned love. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: By the word of truth, by the power of God, by the armour of righteousness on the right hand and on the left. JFB notes the weapons are truth and God's power, and the armour of righteousness covers both hands, for offence and defence alike. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: By honour and dishonour, by evil report and good report: as deceivers, and yet true. Henry notes the minister passes through both honours and dishonours, and is called a deceiver while being true, the world's misreading of a faithful life. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: As unknown, and yet well known; as dying, and, behold, we live; as chastened, and not killed. JFB notes the paradoxes continue: unknown to the world yet known to God, dying yet living, chastened yet not killed. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: As sorrowful, yet always rejoicing; as poor, yet making many rich; as having nothing, and yet possessing all things. Henry notes the final paradoxes are the ministry's summary: sorrowful yet rejoicing, poor yet enriching many, having nothing yet possessing all. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: O ye Corinthians, our mouth is open unto you, our heart is enlarged. JFB notes the open mouth and enlarged heart are the fruit of the preceding catalogue: Paul speaks freely because his heart is wide toward Corinth. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: Ye are not straitened in us, but ye are straitened in your own bowels. Henry notes the narrowness is not Paul's: his heart is enlarged, and if Corinth feels cramped, the cramping is in its own affections. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: Now for a recompense in the same, (I speak as unto my children,) be ye also enlarged. JFB notes Paul asks a recompense in kind: as a father to children, he asks for enlarged hearts to match his own. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: Be ye not unequally yoked together with unbelievers: for what fellowship has righteousness with unrighteousness? and what communion has light with darkness? Henry notes the yoke image forbids partnerships that pull in opposite directions: righteousness and unrighteousness cannot share a yoke, nor light with darkness. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: And what concord has Christ with Belial? or what part has he that believes with an infidel? JFB notes the pairs sharpen: Christ against Belial, believer against unbeliever, with no part in common. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: And what agreement has the temple of God with idols? for ye are the temple of the living God; as God has said, I will dwell in them, and walk in them; and I will be their God, and they shall be my people. Henry notes the temple argument is the ground: believers are God's dwelling, so agreement with idols is unthinkable. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: Wherefore come out from among them, and be ye separate, says the Lord, and touch not the unclean thing; and I will receive you. JFB notes separation is commanded with a promise: come out, touch nothing unclean, and the Lord himself will receive you. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

18 COMMENTARY: And will be a Father unto you, and ye shall be my sons and daughters, says the Lord Almighty. Henry notes the promise crowns the command: separation from unbelievers ends in adoption, sons and daughters of the Almighty. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
