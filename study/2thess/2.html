Study Notes — 2 Thessalonians

Chapter 2

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Paul beseeches them by Christ's coming and by the gathering of believers to him, so that the hope itself steadies them rather than unsettles them. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: They must not be soon shaken or troubled by spirit, word, or forged letter claiming the day of Christ is at hand, for error spreads fastest by alarm. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: No one should deceive them, because that day will not come until a falling away comes first and the man of sin is revealed. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: The man of sin opposes and exalts himself above all called God or worshipped, usurping divine honours in the very place of God's worship. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: Paul reminds them he told them these things while still with them, so this warning is no new doctrine but a repetition of his oral teaching. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: They already know what withholds the man of sin, so that he can only be revealed in his own appointed time, not before. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: The mystery of iniquity already works in secret, while the restrainer holds it back until he is taken out of the way. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: Then the Wicked will be revealed, only to be consumed by the spirit of Christ's mouth and destroyed by the brightness of his coming. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: His coming works after Satan, with all power, signs, and lying wonders, so that his miracles are wonders of deceit, not proofs of truth. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: His deceit works in those who perish because they received not the love of the truth, and a heart that rejects truth is left open to lies. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: For this cause God sends strong delusion that they believe a lie, not as author of sin but in righteous judgment withdrawing grace from wilful unbelief. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: All are thus damned who believed not the truth but had pleasure in unrighteousness, since an erroneous mind and a vicious life go together. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: Against that terror Paul gives thanks for them, beloved of the Lord, chosen from the beginning to salvation through sanctification and belief of the truth. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: God called them by the gospel to obtain the glory of the Lord Jesus Christ, so their hope rests on his call, not on their own strength. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: Therefore they must stand fast and hold the traditions taught by word or epistle, since steadfastness is the guard against both error and apostasy. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: Christ himself and God the Father have loved them and given everlasting consolation and good hope through grace, a comfort no trouble can cancel. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: Paul prays that God would comfort their hearts and establish them in every good word and work, for comfort is itself a means of establishment. — Matthew Henry
