Study Notes — 2 Thessalonians

Chapter 3

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Paul asks their prayers that the word of the Lord may have free course and be glorified among others as it was among them. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: He also asks deliverance from unreasonable and wicked men, for all men have not faith, and unbelief often turns restless and malicious. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: But the Lord is faithful, and will establish them and keep them from evil, so their safety rests on his faithfulness, not their own. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: Paul's confidence that they do and will do what he commands is founded on his confidence in God, for otherwise there is no confidence in man. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: He prays the Lord to direct their hearts into the love of God and into the patient waiting for Christ, the two affections that keep duty steady. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: In Christ's name he commands them to withdraw from every brother who walks disorderly and not after the tradition received from the apostles. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: They know how they ought to follow the apostles, who did not behave disorderly among them but lived by the pattern they preached. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: Paul ate no man's bread for nothing but worked night and day, so as not to be chargeable to any of them. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: This was not for lack of right to support, but to make himself an example for them to follow in honest labour. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: Even while with them he commanded that if any would not work, neither should he eat, since the gospel does not countenance sloth. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: He hears that some walk disorderly, working not at all but acting as busybodies, idleness breeding meddling in other people's affairs. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: Such persons he commands and exhorts by Christ to work quietly and eat their own bread, restoring industry to its proper dignity. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: The rest must not grow weary in well doing because others are disorderly, for the fault of a few does not cancel the duty of all. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: If any man obeys not the word of this epistle, they are to note him and have no company with him, that he may be ashamed. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: Yet he must not be counted as an enemy but admonished as a brother, since the aim of withdrawing is shame leading to reform, not hatred. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: The Lord of peace himself is asked to give them peace always by all means, and to be with them all, since peace is his own gift. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: Paul's own handwritten salutation is the token in every epistle, a guard against counterfeit letters, which he knew would be dangerous. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: The epistle closes with grace, for it is through the grace of the Lord Jesus Christ that believers may hope for peace with God and his presence. — Matthew Henry
