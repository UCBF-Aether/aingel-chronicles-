Study Notes — Song of Songs

Chapter 1

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: This is the Song of songs, excellent above any others, Henry says, for it is wholly taken up with describing the excellences of Christ and the love between him and his redeemed people. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

2 COMMENTARY: Let him kiss me with the kisses of his mouth, for thy love is better than wine. The church, or rather the believer, speaks here as the spouse of the King, the Messiah, and the kisses of his mouth mean those assurances of pardon with which believers are favoured, filling them with peace and joy in believing. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

3 COMMENTARY: Because of the savour of thy good ointments, thy name is as ointment poured forth, therefore do the virgins love thee. The name of Christ is not like ointment sealed up, Henry says, but like ointment poured forth, denoting the freeness and fulness of his grace set forth by the gospel. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

4 COMMENTARY: Draw me, we will run after thee; the king has brought me into his chambers. Believers entreat Christ to draw them by the quickening influences of his Spirit, and the more clearly we discern his glory, the more sensible we are that we cannot follow him suitably, yet the more desirous of doing it. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

5 COMMENTARY: I am black, but comely, O ye daughters of Jerusalem, as the tents of Kedar, as the curtains of Solomon. The believer is black, as defiled and sinful by nature, but comely, as renewed by divine grace to the holy image of God, Henry explains. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

6 COMMENTARY: Look not upon me because I am black, because the sun has looked upon me; my mother's children were angry with me and made me keeper of the vineyards, but mine own vineyard have I not kept. The blackness was owing to hard usage suffered, Henry notes, which caused her to neglect the care of her soul. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

7 COMMENTARY: Tell me, O thou whom my soul loveth, where thou feedest, where thou makest thy flock to rest at noon. Observe the title given to Christ, Henry says: those whose souls love Jesus may come to him boldly and humbly plead with him, and Christ has rest for them even at a noon-time of troubles. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

8 COMMENTARY: If thou know not, O thou fairest among women, go forth by the footsteps of the flock and feed thy kids beside the shepherds' tents. God is ready to answer prayer, Henry notes: follow the track, ask for the good old way, and sit under the direction of good ministers. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

9 COMMENTARY: I have compared thee, O my love, to a company of horses in Pharaoh's chariots. The Bridegroom gives high praises of his spouse, Henry says: in the sight of Christ believers are the excellent of the earth, fitted to be instruments for promoting his glory. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

10 COMMENTARY: Thy cheeks are comely with rows of jewels, thy neck with chains of gold. The spiritual gifts and graces Christ bestows on every true believer are described by the ornaments then in use, Henry explains. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

11 COMMENTARY: We will make thee borders of gold with studs of silver. The graces of the saints are many but depend upon each other, Henry notes, and he who is the Author will be the Finisher of the good work. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

12 COMMENTARY: While the king sits at his table, my spikenard sends forth its smell. The grace received from Christ's fulness springs forth into lively exercises of faith, affection, and gratitude, Henry says, yet Christ, not his gifts, is most precious to them. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

13 COMMENTARY: A bundle of myrrh is my wellbeloved unto me; he shall lie all night betwixt my breasts. Believers resolve to lodge Christ in their hearts all the night, Henry says, during the continuance of the troubles of life. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

14 COMMENTARY: My beloved is unto me as a cluster of camphire in the vineyards of Engedi. The word translated camphire signifies atonement or propitiation, Henry notes: Christ is dear to all believers because he is the propitiation for their sins. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

15 COMMENTARY: Behold, thou art fair, my love; thou hast doves' eyes. Christ takes delight in the good work his grace has wrought on believers' souls, Henry says, and the spouse has a humble, modest eye, discovering simplicity and godly sincerity, eyes enlightened by the Holy Spirit, that blessed Dove. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

16 COMMENTARY: Behold, thou art fair, my beloved, yea, pleasant: also our bed is green. The church expresses her value for Christ, Henry notes: thou art the great Original, but I am but a faint and imperfect copy, and Christ is fair yet pleasant. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

17 COMMENTARY: The beams of our house are cedar, and our rafters of fir. Whether the believer is in the courts of the Lord or in retirement, at daily labours or confined on a bed of sickness, Henry says, a sense of the divine presence will turn the place into a paradise. — Matthew Henry (Concise)
