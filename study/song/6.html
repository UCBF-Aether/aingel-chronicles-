Study Notes — Song of Songs

Chapter 6

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Whither is thy beloved gone, O thou fairest among women? Whither is thy beloved turned aside, that we may seek him with thee? Those made acquainted with the excellences of Christ desire to know where they may meet him, Henry says, and those who would find Christ must seek him early and diligently. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

2 COMMENTARY: My beloved is gone down into his garden, to the beds of spices, to feed in the gardens, and to gather lilies. Christ's church is a garden, enclosed and separated from the world, Henry says; he takes care of it, delights in it, and visits it, and those who would find Christ must attend him in his ordinances: the word, sacraments, and prayer. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

3 COMMENTARY: I am my beloved's, and my beloved is mine; he feeds among the lilies. When Christ comes to his church, Henry notes, it is to entertain his friends and to take believers to himself; he picks the lilies one by one, and at the great day he will send his angels to gather all his lilies. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

4 COMMENTARY: Thou art beautiful, O my love, as Tirzah, comely as Jerusalem, terrible as an army with banners. All the real excellence and holiness on earth centre in the church, Henry says: Christ goes forth subduing his enemies, while his followers gain victories over the world, the flesh, and the devil. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

5 COMMENTARY: Turn away thine eyes from me, for they have overcome me; thy hair is as a flock of goats that appear from Gilead. Here Christ shows the tenderness of a Redeemer, Henry says, and the delight he takes in his redeemed people, and the workings of his own grace in them. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

6 COMMENTARY: Thy teeth are as a flock of sheep which go up from the washing, whereof every one bears twins, and there is not one barren among them. True believers alone can possess the beauty of holiness, Henry notes, and when their real character is known, it will be commended. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

7 COMMENTARY: As a piece of a pomegranate are thy temples within thy locks. This image belongs to the same portrait of the church's beauty of holiness, Henry says, which centres all real excellence on earth in the church. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

8 COMMENTARY: There are threescore queens, and fourscore concubines, and virgins without number. Against this multitude, Henry sets the one dove of the next verse: the church's singularity among many pretenders. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

9 COMMENTARY: My dove, my undefiled is but one; she is the only one of her mother, the choice one of her that bare her; the daughters saw her and blessed her, yea, the queens and the concubines, and they praised her. The church is one, Henry says, and when her real character is known it will be commended even by those around her. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

10 COMMENTARY: Who is she that looks forth as the morning, fair as the moon, clear as the sun, and terrible as an army with banners? At first conversion, Henry explains, the church and believers look forth as the morning, their light small but increasing; as to sanctification they are fair as the moon, deriving all light and grace from Christ; as to justification, clear as the sun, clothed with Christ, the Sun of righteousness. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

11 COMMENTARY: I went down into the garden of nuts to see the fruits of the valley, and to see whether the vine flourished and the pomegranates budded. In retirement and meditation the Christian character is formed and perfected, Henry says, but not in the retirement of the idle or self-indulgent; the Christian's prayer is that all things belonging to the Spirit may live and grow within him and around him. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

12 COMMENTARY: Or ever I was aware, my soul made me like the chariots of Amminadib. In humility and self-abasement, Henry notes, the humble Christian would turn away from the sight of all, but the Lord delights to honour him. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

13 COMMENTARY: Return, return, O Shulamite; return, return, that we may look upon thee. What will ye see in the Shulamite? As it were the company of two armies. The church is called the Shulamite, Henry explains: the word signifies perfection and peace, not in herself, but in Christ, in whom she is complete through his righteousness, and has peace which he made through his blood. — Matthew Henry (Concise)
