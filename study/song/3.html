Study Notes — Song of Songs

Chapter 3

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: By night on my bed I sought him whom my soul loveth; I sought him, but I found him not. The night is a time of coldness, darkness, and drowsiness, Henry says, and at first, when uneasy, only feeble efforts are made to obtain the comfort of communion with Christ. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

2 COMMENTARY: I will rise now and go about the city in the streets and broad ways to seek him whom my soul loveth; I sought him, but I found him not. The believer is then roused to increased diligence, Henry notes, and the streets and broad-ways imply the means of grace in which the Lord is to be sought. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

3 COMMENTARY: The watchmen that go about the city found me, and I asked, saw ye him whom my soul loveth? Application is made to those who watch for men's souls, Henry says, but immediate satisfaction is not found, for we must not rest in any means but by faith apply directly to Christ. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

4 COMMENTARY: It was but a little that I passed from them, but I found him whom my soul loveth; I held him and would not let him go until I had brought him into my mother's house. The holding of Christ and not letting him go denotes earnest cleaving to him, Henry says, and what prevails is humble, ardent suing by prayer with lively faith on his promises. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

5 COMMENTARY: I charge you, O ye daughters of Jerusalem, that ye stir not up nor awake my love till he please. Wherever we find Christ, Henry says, we must take him home with us to our houses, especially to our hearts, and beware of grieving our holy Comforter and provoking the departure of the Beloved. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

6 COMMENTARY: Who is this that comes out of the wilderness like pillars of smoke, perfumed with myrrh and frankincense? A wilderness is an emblem of the world, Henry explains; the believer comes out of it when delivered from the love of its sinful pleasures and pursuits, to seek happiness in communion with the Saviour. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

7 COMMENTARY: Behold his bed, which is Solomon's; threescore valiant men are about it, of the valiant of Israel. He who is the Peace of his people, the King of the heavenly Zion, has provided for the safe conveyance of his redeemed through the wilderness of this world, Henry says. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

8 COMMENTARY: They all hold swords, being expert in war, every man with his sword upon his thigh because of fear in the night. The church is well guarded, Henry notes; more are with her than are against her, and believers reposing in Christ, though they have fears in the night, are yet safe. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

9 COMMENTARY: King Solomon made himself a chariot of the wood of Lebanon. The chariot denotes the covenant of redemption, the way of our salvation, Henry says: the work of Christ which makes him loved and admired in the eyes of believers. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

10 COMMENTARY: He made its pillars of silver, its bottom of gold, its covering of purple, the midst thereof being paved with love, for the daughters of Jerusalem. The blood of the covenant, that rich purple, is the cover of this chariot, Henry explains, sheltering believers from divine wrath and the troubles of this world, while its midst is the love of Christ which passes knowledge. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

11 COMMENTARY: Go forth, O ye daughters of Zion, and behold King Solomon with the crown wherewith his mother crowned him in the day of his espousals, and in the day of the gladness of his heart. Take special notice of his crown, Henry urges: applied to Christ, it speaks the honour put upon him and his power and dominion. — Matthew Henry (Concise)
