Study Notes — Song of Songs

Chapter 7

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: How beautiful are thy feet with shoes, O prince's daughter! The joints of thy thighs are like jewels, the work of a cunning workman. The similitudes here refer in the original to glorious and splendid clothing, Henry says: having put on Christ, the saints are distinguished by their beautiful and glorious apparel. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

2 COMMENTARY: Thy navel is like a round goblet which wants not liquor; thy belly is like a heap of wheat set about with lilies. Such honour have all his saints, Henry notes: they adorn the doctrine of God their Saviour in all things, and consistent believers honour Christ, recommend the gospel, and awaken sinners. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

3 COMMENTARY: Thy two breasts are like two young roes that are twins. This image belongs to the same portrait of the church's beauty of holiness, Henry says, in which believers shall be presented to their heavenly Bridegroom. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

4 COMMENTARY: Thy neck is as a tower of ivory; thine eyes like the fishpools in Heshbon, by the gate of Bathrabbim; thy nose is as the tower of Lebanon which looks toward Damascus. The similitudes denote the beauty of holiness, Henry explains, and the church resembles the stately and spreading palm. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

5 COMMENTARY: Thine head upon thee is like Carmel, and the hair of thine head like purple; the king is held in the galleries. The King is held in the galleries, Henry says: Christ takes delight in the assemblies and ordinances of his people, and admires the fruit of his grace in them. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

6 COMMENTARY: How fair and how pleasant art thou, O love, for delights! When applied to the church and to each faithful Christian, Henry says, all this denotes that beauty of holiness in which they shall be presented to their heavenly Bridegroom. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

7 COMMENTARY: This thy stature is like to a palm tree, and thy breasts to clusters of grapes. The church resembles the stately and spreading palm, Henry notes, while her love for Christ and the obedience resulting therefrom are precious fruit of the true Vine. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

8 COMMENTARY: I said, I will go up to the palm tree, I will take hold of the boughs thereof; now also thy breasts shall be as clusters of the vine, and the smell of thy nose like apples. Christ takes delight in the fruit of his grace in his people, Henry says, and admires it in them. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

9 COMMENTARY: And the roof of thy mouth like the best wine for my beloved, that goes down sweetly, causing the lips of those that are asleep to speak. The best wine here is for the Beloved, Henry notes, as all the fruit of grace is for his honour and glory. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

10 COMMENTARY: I am my beloved's, and his desire is toward me. The church, the believing soul, triumphs in its relation to Christ and interest in him, Henry says, and humbly desires communion with him. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

11 COMMENTARY: Come, my beloved, let us go forth into the field; let us lodge in the villages. Let us walk together, Henry paraphrases, that I may receive counsel, instruction, and comfort from thee, and make known my wants and grievances with freedom; those who would converse with Christ must go forth from the world. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

12 COMMENTARY: Let us get up early to the vineyards; let us see if the vine flourish, whether the tender grape appear, and the pomegranates bud forth: there will I give thee my loves. Those who would go abroad with Christ must begin early, Henry says, seek him early and diligently, and our own souls are our vineyards, which we should often search to see whether we are fruitful in righteousness. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

13 COMMENTARY: The mandrakes give a smell, and at our gates are all manner of pleasant fruits, new and old, which I have laid up for thee, O my beloved. The fruits and exercises of graces are pleasant to the Lord Jesus, Henry says; these must be laid up and always ready, that by bringing forth much fruit he may be glorified, for it is all from him, and therefore fit it should be all for him. — Matthew Henry (Concise)
