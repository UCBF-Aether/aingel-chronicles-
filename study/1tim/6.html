Study Notes — 1 Timothy

Chapter 6

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Servants under the yoke must count their masters worthy of all honour, lest God's name and doctrine be blasphemed, and Henry warns that religious knowledge gives no right to despise masters, disobey lawful commands, or expose their faults. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: Servants with believing masters must not despise them because they are brethren, but rather serve them, since they are faithful and beloved partakers of the benefit, and Henry notes they must serve with double diligence and cheerfulness because of their shared faith. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: Whoever teaches otherwise and consents not to the wholesome words of Christ and the doctrine of godliness has departed from the only words to which unfeigned consent is due. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: Such a man is proud, knowing nothing, doting about questions and strifes of words, and Henry observes that commonly those are most proud who know least, for they do not know themselves. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: From such disputes come envy, strife, railings, evil surmisings, and perverse disputings of corrupt minds destitute of the truth, supposing gain is godliness, and Henry calls these disputes all subtlety and no solidity, pursued for worldly advantage. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: Godliness with contentment is great gain, and Henry contrasts those who make a trade of Christianity for this world, who will be disappointed, with those who mind it as their calling and find its promise for this life and the next. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: We brought nothing into this world, and can carry nothing out, and Henry notes that in the greatest straits we cannot be poorer than when we came in, and that a shroud, a coffin, and a grave are all the richest man can have from his wealth. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: Having food and raiment, let us be therewith content, since nature should be content with a little and grace with less, and the necessaries of life bound a true Christian's desires. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: Those who will be rich fall into temptation and a snare and many foolish and hurtful lusts that drown men in destruction, and Henry stresses it is not the rich but those determined to be rich who place their happiness in wealth and give Satan opportunity to tempt them. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: The love of money is the root of all evil, and some, coveting after it, have erred from the faith and pierced themselves with many sorrows, and Henry says every sort of wickedness grows from the love of money, as daily experience proves. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: But thou, O man of God, flee these things, and follow righteousness, godliness, faith, love, patience, meekness, and Henry notes it ill becomes any man, but especially a man of God, to set his heart on the things of this world. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: Fight the good fight of faith and lay hold on eternal life, to which Timothy was called and professed a good profession before many witnesses, and Henry sets eternal life as the crown proposed for encouragement in the conflict with corruption and the powers of darkness. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: Timothy is charged in the sight of God who quickens all things and of Christ who witnessed a good confession before Pontius Pilate to keep the commandment without spot until Christ's appearing. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: That appearing God will show in his own time, he who is the blessed and only Potentate, King of kings and Lord of lords, and Henry notes the appearing is certain though its time is not for us to know. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: He alone has immortality, dwelling in unapproachable light, whom no man has seen nor can see, and Henry notes that mortal eyes cannot bear the brightness of divine glory, and God is known to sinners only in and through Christ. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: To him be honour and power everlasting, and Henry adores the Godhead here without distinction of Persons, since all these things are properly spoken of Father, Son, or Holy Ghost. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: The rich in this world must not be highminded nor trust in uncertain riches, but in the living God who gives richly all things to enjoy, and Henry notes that being rich toward God is wholly different from being rich in this world, and that wealth is among the most uncertain of things. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: They must do good, be rich in good works, ready to distribute and willing to communicate, since what is an estate worth except as it gives opportunity to do the more good. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: So they lay up a good foundation against the time to come, that they may lay hold on eternal life, showing faith by fruits of love while the self-indulgent around them lift up their eyes in torment. — Matthew Henry

20 COMMENTARY: O Timothy, keep that which is committed to thy trust, avoiding profane and vain babblings and oppositions of science falsely so called, and Henry warns that learning which opposes the gospel is not true science, and that advancing reason above faith endangers faith itself. — Matthew Henry

21 COMMENTARY: Some professing that false knowledge have erred concerning the faith, and the epistle closes with grace be with thee, and Henry closes likewise: grace includes all that is good and is an earnest and beginning of glory, and where God gives grace he will give glory. — Matthew Henry
