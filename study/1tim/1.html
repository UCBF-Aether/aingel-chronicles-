Study Notes — 1 Timothy

Chapter 1

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Paul writes as an apostle of Jesus Christ by the commandment of God our Saviour, and Henry notes his credentials were unquestionable, since he held not only a commission but a commandment from God and from Christ. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: Timothy is addressed as Paul's own son in the faith, with grace, mercy, and peace wished him, and Henry notes Paul had been the means of Timothy's conversion and Timothy had served with him as a dutiful son with a loving father. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: Paul had besought Timothy to abide at Ephesus when he went to Macedonia, charging some to teach no other doctrine, since the epistle's design is to guard the church against false teachers who corrupt the gospel by subtle distinctions and endless disputes. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: Timothy must oppose fables and endless genealogies, which raise questions rather than edifying, and Henry warns that what gives occasion for doubtful disputes pulls the church down rather than building it up. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: The end of the commandment is charity out of a pure heart, a good conscience, and faith unfeigned, and Henry notes that whatever weakens love to God or to brethren defeats the commandment's very end. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: Some, having swerved from that end, turned aside to vain jangling, substituting empty talk for the love the commandment was meant to produce. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: These men desired to be teachers of the law yet understood neither what they said nor what they affirmed, claiming authority over a law whose meaning they had not grasped. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: The law is good if used lawfully, and Henry explains that for believers made righteous in God's appointed way the law is not against them, since its design agrees with the gospel. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: The law is made not for a righteous man but for the lawless and disobedient, for the ungodly and sinners, so that its proper work is to expose and restrain sin rather than to condemn those already righteous by faith. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: The catalogue continues through the unholy and profane, murderers, whoremongers, men-stealers, liars, and perjurers, all contrary to sound doctrine, showing the law's reach over every form of ungodliness. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: All of this stands according to the glorious gospel of the blessed God, and Henry notes that unless we are made righteous by faith in Christ, really repenting and forsaking sin, we remain under the curse of the law even according to that gospel. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: Paul thanks Christ who enabled him, counting him faithful and putting him into the ministry, and Henry observes that Paul knew he would justly have perished had the Lord been extreme to mark what was amiss. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: Paul had been a blasphemer, persecutor, and injurious man, yet he obtained mercy because he did it ignorantly in unbelief, and Henry traces his whole turn to grace abundant when he was dead in sin, working faith and love in his heart. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: The grace of the Lord was exceeding abundant with faith and love in Christ Jesus, so that the chief persecutor became the chief exhibit of grace's power. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: This is a faithful saying worthy of all acceptation, that Christ Jesus came into the world to save sinners, of whom Paul is chief, and Henry insists no man with Paul's example before him can question Christ's love and power to save him if he truly trusts him. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: Paul obtained mercy so that in him first Jesus Christ might show all longsuffering, as a pattern to those who should afterward believe to life everlasting, making his own case the template of hope for later sinners. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: To the King eternal, immortal, invisible, the only wise God, Paul ascribes honour and glory for ever, and Henry turns the doxology into a call to admire and praise the grace of God our Saviour and ascribe glory to Father, Son, and Holy Ghost. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: Paul renews his charge to Timothy according to the prophecies that went before, that he might war a good warfare, and Henry notes the ministry is a warfare against sin and Satan under Christ, the Captain of salvation, and that others' good hopes of us should stir us to duty. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: Timothy must hold faith and a good conscience, which some put away and made shipwreck of their faith, and Henry warns that putting away conscience is the way to make shipwreck of faith also. — Matthew Henry

20 COMMENTARY: Hymenaeus and Alexander are named as those delivered to Satan that they might learn not to blaspheme, and Henry explains the highest censure's design: to prevent further sin and reclaim the sinner, not merely to punish. — Matthew Henry
