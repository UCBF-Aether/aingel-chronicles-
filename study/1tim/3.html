Study Notes — 1 Timothy

Chapter 3

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: It is a true saying that desiring the office of a bishop is desiring a good work, and Henry approves the desire when it springs from love to Christ and to souls, ready to deny self and undergo hardship. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: A bishop must be blameless, the husband of one wife, vigilant, sober, of good behaviour, given to hospitality, and apt to teach, and Henry notes that sobriety and watchfulness assist one another, and that a minister must give as little occasion for blame as can be. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: He must be not given to wine, no striker, not greedy of filthy lucre, but patient, not a brawler, not covetous, so that temperance in appetite, temper, and money all mark the office together. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: He must rule his own house well, having his children in subjection with all gravity, and Henry notes that ministers' families ought to be examples of good to all other families. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: For if a man know not how to rule his own house, how shall he take care of the church of God, and the household becomes the visible proof of fitness for the larger household. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: He must not be a novice, lest being lifted up with pride he fall into the condemnation of the devil, and Henry warns that pride is the sin that turned angels into devils. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: He must also have a good report of them that are without, lest he fall into reproach and the snare of the devil, so that his former life must stand examination by neighbours as well as by the church. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: Deacons likewise must be grave, not doubletongued, not given to much wine, not greedy of filthy lucre, and Henry notes that deacons were first appointed to distribute the church's charity and manage its concerns, a great trust not to be lodged in unfit hands. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: They must hold the mystery of the faith in a pure conscience, joining sound belief to a clean conscience, since doctrine held in an impure conscience soon corrupts. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: They must first be proved, and then, if found blameless, serve, and Henry stresses that public trusts should not be given until men are found fit for the business entrusted to them. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: Their wives likewise must be grave, not slanderers, sober, faithful in all things, and Henry extends the warning to all related to ministers, who must walk as becomes the gospel. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: Deacons must be husbands of one wife, ruling their children and houses well, the same household test applied to the second office as to the first. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: Those who use the office of deacon well purchase a good degree and great boldness in the faith, so that faithful service in a lower trust prepares for greater confidence in the gospel. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: Paul writes these things hoping to come shortly, yet providing instruction in case he tarries, so that the church is not left without order while its apostle is absent. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: The aim is that Timothy know how to behave in the house of God, which is the church of the living God, and Henry notes that God dwells there, and that when a church ceases to be the pillar and ground of truth, regard for truth must come first. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: Great is the mystery of godliness: God manifest in the flesh, justified in the Spirit, seen of angels, preached to the Gentiles, believed on in the world, received up into glory, and Henry sums it up: the mystery is Christ himself, come to take away sins and purify a peculiar people zealous of good works. — Matthew Henry
