Study Notes — 1 Timothy

Chapter 5

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: An elder must not be rebuked sharply but entreated as a father, and Henry notes that respect must be paid to the dignity of years and place, with much meekness and care in reproving those who deserve reproof. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: Younger men are to be treated as brethren, elder women as mothers, and younger women as sisters with all purity, so that every correction keeps the manners of a family, not of a court. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: Widows that are widows indeed are to be honoured, that is, relieved and maintained, and Henry notes that widowhood is a desolate state in which widows must trust in the Lord and continue in prayer. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: If a widow has children or nephews, they must first learn to show piety at home and requite their parents, and Henry warns that those who do not maintain their poor relations in effect deny the faith. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: The widow indeed, desolate and trusting in God, continues in supplications and prayers night and day, making prayer the work of her solitude. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: She that lives in pleasure is dead while she lives, spiritually dead in trespasses and sins, and Henry laments how many nominal Christians answer this description even to the latest period of life. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: These things Timothy must command, so that the widows and their families may be blameless, and the church's care does not become a cover for disorder. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: Whoever provides not for his own, especially his own house, has denied the faith and is worse than an infidel, and Henry applies it sharply: spending on lusts and pleasures what should maintain a family is a denial of the gospel professed. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: A widow is not to be taken into the church's number under threescore years, having been the wife of one man, since office in the church requires freedom from just censure, though many proper objects of charity ought not to be employed in public services. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: She must be well reported of for good works: bringing up children, lodging strangers, washing saints' feet, relieving the afflicted, and following every good work, so that a life of mercy is the qualification for the church's trust. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: Younger widows are to be refused for that number, since when they wax wanton against Christ they will marry, and Henry notes that those who would find mercy in distress must show mercy in prosperity, while idleness seldom stays idle but makes mischief among neighbours. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: Having damnation because they cast off their first faith, they fall under judgment for breaking the pledge their enrolment implied. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: With idleness they learn to wander from house to house, becoming tattlers and busybodies speaking things they ought not, and Henry warns that the idle very seldom are only idle, but sow discord among brethren. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: Paul wills therefore that younger women marry, bear children, guide the house, and give the adversary no occasion to speak reproachfully, turning domestic faithfulness into the answer to gossip. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: For some are already turned aside after Satan, showing that the danger Paul guards against is not hypothetical but already at work. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: If any believing man or woman has widows, they must relieve them and not burden the church, so that the church may relieve those who are widows indeed, entirely destitute and friendless. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: Elders that rule well are worthy of double honour, especially those who labour in word and doctrine, and Henry notes that care must be taken that ministers are maintained, since it is their just due as much as the labourer's reward. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: Scripture says, Thou shalt not muzzle the ox that treadeth out the corn, and, The labourer is worthy of his reward, grounding ministerial maintenance in both Testaments. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: An accusation against an elder is not to be received except before two or three witnesses, guarding a public office against a single malicious tongue. — Matthew Henry

20 COMMENTARY: Those who sin are to be rebuked before all, that others also may fear, so that public sin receives public correction for the sake of the whole church. — Matthew Henry

21 COMMENTARY: Timothy is charged before God, Christ, and the elect angels to observe these things without preferring one before another and doing nothing by partiality, and Henry notes the solemn need to watch at all times against partaking of other men's sins. — Matthew Henry

22 COMMENTARY: Hands must not be laid suddenly on any man, nor must Timothy partake of other men's sins, but keep himself pure, and Henry explains purity here as refusing not only to do the like himself but to countenance or help it in others. — Matthew Henry

23 COMMENTARY: Timothy is told to drink no longer water only, but use a little wine for his stomach's sake and often infirmities, and Henry notes the balance: bodies are to be neither masters nor slaves, but used so as to be most helpful in God's service. — Matthew Henry

24 COMMENTARY: Some men's sins are open beforehand, going before to judgment, and some follow after, and Henry reminds us that God will bring to light the hidden things of darkness and make known the counsels of hearts. — Matthew Henry

25 COMMENTARY: Likewise good works are manifest beforehand, and those that are otherwise cannot be hid, so that both sin and goodness come to light at the judgment, and Timothy must act with that day in view. — Matthew Henry
