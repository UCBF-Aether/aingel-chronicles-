Study Notes — 1 Timothy

Chapter 4

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: The Spirit speaks expressly that in the latter times some shall depart from the faith, giving heed to seducing spirits and doctrines of devils, and Henry notes this turning was foretold in both Testaments for the whole Christian dispensation. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: They speak lies in hypocrisy, having their conscience seared with a hot iron, so that false teaching is carried by men whose moral sense has been cauterised rather than merely mistaken. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: They forbid marriage and command abstinence from meats God created to be received with thanksgiving, and Henry names the pattern: forbidding as evil what God allowed, and commanding as duty what he left indifferent. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: Every creature of God is good, and nothing is to be refused if received with thanksgiving, so that the creation itself answers the ascetic error. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: It is sanctified by the word of God and prayer, and Henry adds that nothing will be good to us unless we seek the Lord's blessing upon it by prayer, while nothing justifies an intemperate or improper use of things. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: Timothy, putting the brethren in remembrance of these things, will be a good minister of Jesus Christ, nourished in the words of faith and good doctrine, and Henry notes that outward acts of self-denial profit little if we do not mortify sin itself. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: He must refuse profane and old wives' fables and exercise himself unto godliness, trading idle tales for disciplined training in the faith. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: Bodily exercise profits little, but godliness is profitable for all things, having promise of the life that now is and of that which is to come, and Henry observes that the gain of godliness lies much in the promise, especially for the life to come, so that though we lose for Christ we shall not lose by him. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: This is a faithful saying worthy of all acceptation, and Henry treats the promise of godliness as words to be depended on, not as pious exaggeration. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: Therefore believers labour and suffer reproach because they trust in the living God, the Saviour of all men, especially of those that believe, and Henry notes that if Christ is thus the Saviour of all, much more will he be the Rewarder of those who seek and serve him. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: These things Timothy must command and teach, with authority, since the directions carry apostolic weight and are not offered as mere suggestions. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: No man may despise Timothy's youth, but he must be an example in word, conversation, charity, spirit, faith, and purity, and Henry notes that youth will not be despised if kept from vanities and follies, and that those who teach by doctrine must teach by life. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: Until Paul comes, Timothy must give attendance to reading, exhortation, and doctrine, making public Scripture and its application his principal occupation. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: He must not neglect the gift given by prophecy with the laying on of the elders' hands, since ordination gifts are to be used, not buried. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: He must meditate on these things and give himself wholly to them, so that his profiting may appear to all, and Henry notes that a minister's doctrine must be scriptural, clear, evangelical, and practical, leaving no leisure for trifling visits or idle conversation. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: He must take heed to himself and to the doctrine, continuing in them, for in doing so he will save both himself and his hearers, and Henry closes by praying that every believer's profiting may appear, experiencing the gospel's power in his own soul and its fruits in his life. — Matthew Henry
