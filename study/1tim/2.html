Study Notes — 1 Timothy

Chapter 2

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Paul exhorts that supplications, prayers, intercessions, and thanksgivings be made for all men, and Henry notes that Christ's disciples must be praying people without distinction of nation, sect, rank, or party. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: Prayer is made especially for kings and all in authority, so that believers may lead a quiet and peaceable life in godliness and honesty, which Henry sums up as the whole of Christian duty: right worship of God and good conduct toward men. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: Such prayer is good and acceptable in the sight of God our Saviour, and Henry urges us to abound in whatever is acceptable to him rather than treating it as optional. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: God will have all men to be saved and to come to the knowledge of the truth, and Henry insists that knowledge of the truth is God's appointed way to save sinners, since we cannot be ruled by a truth we do not know. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: There is one God and one Mediator between God and men, the man Christ Jesus, and Henry notes that sin made the quarrel between us and God, and Christ the Mediator makes the peace. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: Christ gave himself a ransom for all, to be testified in due time, and Henry explains that in Old Testament times his sufferings and following glory were spoken of as things to be revealed in the last times. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: Paul was ordained a preacher, apostle, and teacher of the Gentiles in faith and truth, and Henry notes this appointment was for Jews and Gentiles of every nation, that all who are willing may come to the mercy-seat of a pardoning God. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: Men must pray everywhere, lifting up holy hands without wrath and doubting, and Henry applies this widely: in closets, families, at meals, on journeys, and in solemn assemblies, always in charity and in faith. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: Women likewise are to adorn themselves in modest apparel with shamefacedness and sobriety, not with braided hair, gold, pearls, or costly array, and Henry notes that modesty and neatness are more to be consulted than elegance and fashion. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: Their proper ornament is good works, as becomes women professing godliness, and Henry calls good works the best ornament, of great price in God's sight, worth more time and money than decorating selves and children. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: Women are to learn in silence with all subjection, and Henry explains that learning itself is not excused: they must learn what is necessary to salvation, though not usurp authority. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: Paul does not permit a woman to teach or usurp authority over the man, but to be in silence, and Henry reasons that teaching is an office of authority, while good women may and ought to teach their children at home. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: The reason given is creation order: Adam was first formed, then Eve, and Henry notes that as woman was last in creation, which is one reason for her subjection, so she was first in transgression. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: Adam was not deceived, but the woman being deceived was in the transgression, and Henry keeps both halves of Paul's argument together: order in creation and priority in the fall. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: Yet there is comfort: she shall be saved in childbearing if they continue in faith, charity, holiness, and sobriety, and Henry reads this as salvation with childbearing, by the Messiah who was born of a woman, with the sorrows of that sex calling men to exercise authority with gentleness and affection. — Matthew Henry
