Study Notes — Isaiah

Chapter 42

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Behold my servant, whom I uphold, my elect, in whom my soul delights; I have put my spirit upon him, and he will bring forth judgment to the Gentiles. This chapter treats Messiah the antitype of Cyrus: God's description of His character in verses 1 to 4, God addressing Him directly in verses 5 to 7, an address to the people to attend in verses 8 and 9, and a call to all, especially the exile Jews, to rejoice in the coming deliverance in verses 10 to 25. My servant means the law of prophetic suggestion leads Isaiah from Cyrus to the far greater Deliverer, behind whom the former is lost sight of; the express quotation in Matthew and the description can apply to Messiah alone. Israel also, in its highest ideal, is called the servant of God, but this ideal is realised only in the antitypical Israel, its representative-man and Head, Messiah. Servant was the position assumed by the Son of God throughout His humiliation. Elect means chosen by God before the foundation of the world for an atonement; redemption was no afterthought to remedy an unforeseen evil. In Matthew it is rendered My beloved, the only beloved Son, beloved in a sense distinct from all others; election and the love of God are inseparably joined. Soul is a human phrase applied to God, because of the intended union of humanity with the Divinity: I Myself. Delighteth means is well pleased with, and accepts, as a propitiation; God could have delighted in no created being as a mediator. Spirit upon him recalls chapters 11 and 61, Luke, and John. Judgment is the gospel dispensation, founded on justice, the canon of the divine rule and principle of judgment called the law; the Gospel has a discriminating judicial effect, saving to penitents, condemnatory to Satan the enemy and the wilfully impenitent. Matthew has, He shall show, for He shall bring forth, or cause to go forth; Christ both produced and announced His judgment, the Hebrew dwelling most on His producing it, Matthew on His announcement of it, and the two are joined in Him. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: He will not cry, nor lift up, nor cause his voice to be heard in the street. Matthew marks the kind of cry as that of altercation by quoting it, He shall not strive. Street is translated by the Septuagint, outside: an image from an altercation in a house, loud enough to be heard in the street outside, appropriate of Him who withdrew Himself from the public fame created by His miracles to privacy, though Matthew also shows another and sterner aspect of His character, which is also implied in the term judgment. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: A bruised reed he will not break, and the smoking flax he will not quench; he will bring forth judgment unto truth. Bruised recalls, It pleased the Lord to bruise Him, so He can feel for the bruised; as verse 2 described His unturbulent spirit towards His violent enemies and His utter freedom from love of notoriety, so verse 3 describes His tenderness in cherishing the first spark of grace in the penitent. Reed is fragile, easily shaken with the wind: those who are at best feeble, and who besides are oppressed by calamity or by the sense of sin. Break means entirely crush or condemn, compare bind up the broken-hearted. Flax is put for the lamp-wick, formed of flax; the believer is the lamp, his conscience enlightened by the Holy Ghost is the wick. Smoking means dimly burning, smouldering, the flame not quite extinct; this expresses the positive side of the penitent's religion, as bruised reed the negative. Broken-hearted in himself, but not without some spark of flame, literally from above; Christ will supply such a one with grace as with oil. Also the light of nature smouldering in the Gentiles amidst the hurtful fumes of error: He not only did not quench, but cleared away the mists and superadded the light of revelation. Truth is, Matthew quotes it, send forth judgment unto victory; Matthew, under the Spirit, gives the virtual sense, but varies the word, in order to bring out a fresh aspect of the same thing. Truth has in itself the elements of victory over all opposing forces; truth is the victory of Him who is the truth. The gospel judicial sifting of believers and unbelievers, begun already in part, will be consummated victoriously in truth only at His second coming; verses 13 and 14 here, and Matthew, show reference to the judicial aspect of the Gospel, especially finally, besides the mild triumph of Jesus coming in mercy to the penitent now in verse 2, there shall be finally the judgment on His enemies, when the truth shall be perfectly developed. Compare chapter 61, where the two comings are similarly joined. On judgment, see verse 1. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: He will not fail nor be discouraged, till he has set judgment in the earth, and the isles will wait for his law. Fail means faint; man in religion may become as the almost expiring flax-wick in verse 3, but not so He in His purposes of grace. Discouraged is literally broken, that is, checked in zeal by discouragements. Judgment is His true religion, the canon of His judgments and righteous reign. Isles wait means the distant lands beyond sea shall put their trust in His gospel way of salvation; Matthew virtually gives the sense, with the inspired addition of another aspect of the same thing, In his name shall the Gentiles trust, as wait for here means. His law is not something distinct from Himself, but is indeed Himself, the manifestation of God's character, name, in Christ, who is the embodiment of the law. Isles here, and in verse 12, may refer to the fact that the populations of which the church was primarily formed were Gentiles of the countries bordering on the Mediterranean. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: Thus says God the Lord, he that created the heavens, and stretched them out, he that spread forth the earth, and that which comes out of it, he that gives breath unto the people upon it, and spirit to them that walk therein. Previously God had spoken of Messiah; now in verses 5 to 7 He speaks to Him. To show to all that He is able to sustain the Messiah in His appointed work, and that all might accept Messiah as commissioned by such a mighty God, He commences by announcing Himself as the Almighty Creator and Preserver of all things. Spread forth the earth recalls Psalm 136. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: I the Lord have called you in righteousness, and will hold your hand, and will keep you, and give you for a covenant of the people, for a light of the Gentiles. In righteousness is rather, for a righteous purpose; God set forth His Son to be a propitiation so as to declare His, God's, righteousness, that God might be just, and yet the justifier of him which believeth in Jesus. Hold hand is as to Israel, the type of Messiah, in Hosea. Covenant is the medium of the covenant, originally made between God and Abraham; the mediator of a better covenant than the law. So the abstract peace, for peace-maker. The people is Israel, as chapter 49 compared with this verse proves. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: To open the blind eyes, to bring out the prisoners from the prison, and them that sit in darkness out of the prison house. Blind is spiritually, as in verses 16, 18, and 19, chapter 35, and John 9. Prison recalls chapter 61. Darkness is opposed to light in verse 6, Ephesians, and 1 Peter. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: I am the Lord, that is my name, and my glory will I not give to another, neither my praise to graven images. God turns from addressing Messiah to the people. Lord is Jehovah, God's distinguishing and incommunicable name, indicating essential being and immutable faithfulness. My means that is due to Me, and to Me alone. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: Behold, the former things are come to pass, and new things do I declare; before they spring forth I tell you of them. Former things are former predictions of God, now fulfilled, adduced as proof that they ought to trust in Him alone as God, namely the predictions as to Israel's restoration from Babylon. New means namely predictions as to Messiah, who is to bring all nations to the worship of Jehovah. Spring forth is the same image from plants just beginning to germinate as in chapters 43 and 58; before there is the slightest indication to enable a sagacious observer to infer the coming event, God foretells it. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: Sing unto the Lord a new song, and his praise from the end of the earth, you that go down to the sea, and all that is therein, the isles, and the inhabitants thereof. New song is such as has never before been sung, called for by a new manifestation of God's grace, to express which no hymn for former mercies would be appropriate; the new song shall be sung when the Lord shall reign in Jerusalem, and all nations shall flow unto it. Ye that go down to the sea means whose conversion will be the means of diffusing the Gospel to distant lands. All therein means all the living creatures that fill the sea, or all sailors and voyagers; but these were already mentioned in the previous clause, there he called on all who go upon the sea, in this clause all animals in the sea, so in verse 11 he calls on the inanimate wilderness to lift up its voice. External nature shall be so renovated as to be in unison with the moral renovation. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: Let the wilderness and the cities thereof lift up their voice, the villages that Kedar inhabits; let the inhabitants of the rock sing, let them shout from the top of the mountains. Cities are in a region not wholly waste, but mainly so, with an oasis here and there. Kedar is in Arabia-Deserta; the Kedarenians led a nomadic, wandering life, so Kedar is here put in general for that class of men. Rock is Sela, that is, Petra, the metropolis of Idumea and the Nabathoean Ishmaelites, or it may refer in general to those in Arabia-Petraea, who had their dwellings cut out of the rock. The mountains are namely of Paran, south of Sinai, in Arabia-Petraea. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: Let them give glory unto the Lord, and declare his praise in the islands. Islands recalls chapter 24. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: The Lord will go forth as a mighty man, he will stir up jealousy like a man of war; he will cry, yes, roar; he will prevail against his enemies. Jehovah will no longer restrain His wrath: He will go forth as a mighty warrior to destroy His people's enemies, and to deliver Israel. Stir up jealousy means rouse His indignation. Roar is an image from the battle cry of a warrior. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: I have long time held my peace; I have been still, and refrained myself; now will I cry like a travailing woman; I will destroy and devour at once. Long time means namely during the desolation of Israel in chapter 32. Holden my peace recalls Psalm 50 and Habakkuk. Cry like a travailing woman means like a woman in parturition, who, after having restrained her breathing for a time, at last, overcome with labour pain, lets out her voice with a panting sigh; so Jehovah will give full vent to His long pent-up wrath. Translate, instead of destroy and devour, I will at once breathe hard and pant, namely, giving loose to My wrath. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: I will make waste mountains and hills, and dry up all their herbs, and I will make the rivers islands, and I will dry up the pools. This means I will destroy all My foes. Mountains in Palestine are usually planted with vines and olives in terraces, up to their tops. Islands are rather dry lands; God will destroy His foes, the heathen, and their idols, and dry up the fountains of their oracles, their doctrines and institutions, the symbol of which is water, and their schools which promoted idolatry. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: I will bring the blind by a way that they knew not; I will lead them in paths that they have not known; I will make darkness light before them, and crooked things straight. These things will I do unto them, and not forsake them. They knew not refers to the various means employed by Providence for the establishment of the church in the world, such as would never have occurred to the mind of mere man. Blind, they are called, as not having heretofore seen God's ways in ordering His church. Make darkness light, etc., implies that the glorious issue would only be known by the event itself; the same holds good of the individual believer. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: They will be turned back, they will be greatly ashamed, that trust in graven images, that say to the molten images: You are our gods. Ashamed means disappointed in their trust, the same phrase as in Psalm 35. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

18 COMMENTARY: Hear, you deaf, and look, you blind, that you may see. Deaf means namely to the voice of God. Blind means to your duty and interest, wilfully so, as in verse 20; in this they differ from the blind in verse 16. The Jews are referred to: He had said God would destroy the heathen idolatry, here he remembers that even Israel, His servant in verse 19, from whom better things might have been expected, is tainted with this sin. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

19 COMMENTARY: Who is blind, but my servant? Or deaf, as my messenger that I sent? Who is blind as he that is perfect, and blind as the Lord's servant? My servant is namely Israel; who of the heathen is so blind? Considering Israel's high privileges, the heathen's blindness was as nothing compared with that of Israelite idolaters. My messenger sent means Israel was designed by God to be the herald of His truth to other nations. Perfect means furnished with institutions, civil and religious, suited to their perfect well-being, compare the title Jeshurun, the perfect one, applied to Israel, as the type of Messiah; or translate, the friend of God, which Israel was by virtue of descent from Abraham, who was so called in chapter 41. The language, my servant, messenger, perfect, can, in the full antitypical sense, only apply to Christ, so verse 21 plainly refers to Him. Blind and deaf in His case refer to His endurance of suffering and reproach, as though He neither saw nor heard. Thus there is a transition by contrast from the moral blindness of Israel in verse 18 to the patient blindness and deafness of Messiah. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

20 COMMENTARY: Seeing many things, but you observe not; opening the ears, but he hears not. Observest means Thou dost not keep them; the many things are the many proofs which all along from the first God had given Israel of His goodness and His power. He means transition from the second to the third person; opening ears means, that is, though he, Israel, hath his ears open, as in chapter 6. This language too, as in verse 19, applies to Messiah as Jehovah's servant. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

21 COMMENTARY: The Lord is well pleased for his righteousness' sake; he will magnify the law, and make it honourable. His righteousness is not His people's, but His own; chapter 42 verse 24 shows they had no righteousness. God is well pleased with His Son, in whom My soul delighteth in verse 1, who fulfils all righteousness for them, and with them for His sake. Perhaps in God's righteousness here is included His faithfulness to His promises given to Israel's forefathers, because of this He is well pleased with Israel, even though displeased with their sin, which He here reproves; but that promise could only be based on the righteousness of Messiah, the promised seed, which is God's righteousness. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

22 COMMENTARY: But this is a people robbed and spoiled; they are all of them snared in holes, and they are hid in prison houses; they are for a prey, and none delivers; for a spoil, and none says: Restore. Holes means caught by their foes in the caverns where they had sought refuge, or bound in subterranean dungeons. Prison-houses are either literal prisons, or their own houses, whence they dare not go forth for fear of the enemy. The connection is: notwithstanding God's favour to His people for His righteousness' sake in verse 21, they have fallen into misery, the Babylonish and Romish captivities and their present dispersion, owing to their disregard of the divine law; spiritual imprisonment is included in verse 7. None saith, Restore means there is no deliverer. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

23 COMMENTARY: Who among you will give ear to this? Who will hearken and hear for the time to come? This is a call that they should be warned by the past judgments of God to obey Him for the time to come. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

24 COMMENTARY: Who gave Jacob for a spoil, and Israel to the robbers? Did not the Lord, he against whom we have sinned? For they would not walk in his ways, neither were they obedient unto his law. Who means their calamity was not the work of chance, but God's immediate act for their sins. Jacob, Israel, we means change from the third to the first person; Isaiah first speaking to them as a prophet, distinct from them, then identifying himself with them, and acknowledging his share in the nation's sins. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

25 COMMENTARY: Therefore he has poured upon him the fury of his anger, and the strength of battle, and it has set him on fire round about, yet he knew not, and it burned him, yet he laid it not to heart. Him is Israel in verse 24. Strength of battle means violence of war. It means the battle or war. Knew not means knew not the lesson of repentance which the judgment was intended to teach. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
