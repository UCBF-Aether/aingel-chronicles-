Study Notes — Isaiah

Chapter 55

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Ho, every one that thirsts, come to the waters, and he that has no money; come, buy, and eat, yes, come, buy wine and milk without money and without price. This chapter treats the call of the Gentile world to faith, the result of God's grace to the Jews first. Every one means after the special privileges of Israel in chapter 54 there follow, as the consequence, the universal invitation to the Gentiles, as in Luke and Romans. Ho means calls the most earnest attention. Thirsteth means has a keen sense of need, as in Matthew. Waters, wine and milk mean a gradation: not merely water, which is needed to maintain life at all, but wine and milk to strengthen, cheer, and nourish; the spiritual blessings of the Gospel are meant, as in chapters 25 and Song of Solomon and John. Waters, plural, to denote abundance, as in chapters 43 and 44. No money means yet, in verse 2, it is said, ye spend money. A seeming paradox: ye are really spiritual bankrupts, but thinking yourselves to have money, namely, a devotion of your own making, ye lavish it on that which is not bread, that is, on idols, whether literal or spiritual. Buy without money is another paradox: we are bought, but not with a price paid by ourselves, as in Corinthians and Peter. In a different sense we are to buy salvation, namely, by parting with everything which comes between us and Christ who has bought it for us and by making it our own, as in Matthew, Luke, and Revelation. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: Wherefore do you spend money for that which is not bread? And your labour for that which satisfies not? Hearken diligently unto me, and eat that which is good, and let your soul delight itself in fatness. Not bread recalls Habakkuk: Bread of deceit, as in Proverbs. Contrast this with the bread of life, as in John, also Luke. Satisfieth not recalls Ecclesiastes. Hearken and eat means when two imperatives are joined, the second expresses the consequence of obeying the command in the first, as in Genesis: By hearkening ye shall eat. So in verse 1, buy and eat: By buying, and so making it your own, ye shall eat, that is, experimentally enjoy it, as in John. Compare the invitation in Proverbs and Matthew. Fatness recalls Psalms. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: Incline your ear, and come unto me; hear, and your soul will live, and I will make an everlasting covenant with you, even the sure mercies of David. Live means by coming to me ye shall live, for I am the life, as in John. Everlasting covenant recalls Jeremiah and 2 Samuel. With you, David means God's covenant is with the antitypical David, Messiah, as in Ezekiel, and so with us by our identification with Him. Sure means answering to everlasting, irrevocable, unfailing, to be relied on, as in Psalms, Jeremiah, 2 Samuel, and 2 Corinthians. Mercies of David means the mercies of grace, as in chapters 63 and John, which I covenanted to give to David, and especially to Messiah, his antitype, as quoted in Acts. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: Behold, I have given him for a witness to the people, a leader and commander to the people. Him means the mystical David, as in Ezekiel, Jeremiah, and Hosea, given by God, as in chapter 49. Witness means He bore witness even unto death for God, to His law, claims, and plan of redeeming love, as in John and Revelation. Revelation is a testimony, because it is propounded to be received on the authority of the Giver, and not merely because it can be proved by arguments. Commander means preceptor, lawgiver. To the people is rather, peoples. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: Behold, you will call a nation that you know not, and nations that knew not you will run unto you because of the Lord your God, and for the Holy One of Israel, for he has glorified you. Thou means Jehovah addresses Messiah. Call, run means God must call, before man can, or will, run, as in Song of Solomon and John; not merely come, but run eagerly. Thou knowest not means now as thy people, as in Matthew. Nation, nations means gradation; from Israel, one nation, the Gospel spread to many nations, and will do so more fully on Israel's conversion. Knew not thee recalls chapters 52 and Ephesians. Because of thy God, glorified thee recalls chapters 60 and Zechariah, where similar language is directed to Israel, because of the identification of Israel with Messiah, who is the ideal Israel, as in Matthew with Hosea and Acts. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: Seek the Lord while he may be found, call upon him while he is near. This is the condition and limit in the obtaining of the spiritual benefits in verses 1 to 3: first, Seek the Lord; second, Seek Him while He is to be found, as in chapters 65 and Psalm 32 and Matthew and John and Corinthians and Hebrews. Call means casting yourselves wholly on His mercy, as in Romans; stronger than seek, so near is more positive than while He may be found, as in Romans. Near means propitious, as in Psalms. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: Let the wicked forsake his way, and the unrighteous man his thoughts, and let him return unto the Lord, and he will have mercy upon him, and to our God, for he will abundantly pardon. Unrighteous is Hebrew, man of iniquity, true of all men. The wicked sins more openly in his way; the unrighteous refers to the more subtle workings of sin in the thoughts. All are guilty in the latter respect, though many fancy themselves safe, because not openly wicked in ways, as in Psalm 94. The parallelism is that of gradation. The progress of the penitent is to be from negative reformation, forsaking his way, and a farther step, his thoughts, to positive repentance, returning to the Lord, the only true repentance, as in Zechariah, and making God his God, along with the other children of God, the crowning point, appropriation of God to ourselves: to our God. Return implies that man originally walked with God, but has apostatised. Isaiah saith, our God, the God of the believing Israelites; those themselves redeemed desire others to come to their God, as in Psalm 34 and Revelation. Abundantly pardon is literally, multiply to pardon, still more than have mercy; God's graciousness is felt more and more the longer one knows Him, as in Psalm 130. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: For my thoughts are not your thoughts, neither are your ways my ways, says the Lord. For refers to verse 7: you need not doubt His willingness abundantly to pardon, compare verse 12; for, though the wicked man's ways, and the unrighteous man's thoughts, are so aggravated as to seem unpardonable, God's thoughts and ways in pardoning are not regulated by the proportion of the former, as man's would be towards his fellow man who offended him; compare the for in Psalm 25 and Romans. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: For as the heavens are higher than the earth, so are my ways higher than your ways, and my thoughts than your thoughts. This recalls Psalms 57, 89, and 103. For is repeated from verse 8. But some, after the negation, translate, but. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: For as the rain comes down, and the snow from heaven, and returns not thither, but waters the earth, and makes it bring forth and bud, that it may give seed to the sower, and bread to the eater. The hearts of men, once barren of spirituality, shall be made, by the outpouring of the Spirit under Messiah, to bear fruits of righteousness, as in chapters 5 and Deuteronomy and 2 Samuel and Psalm 72. Snow means which covers plants from frost in winter, and, when melted in spring, waters the earth. Returneth not means void, as in verse 11; it returns not in the same shape, or without accomplishing the desired end. Bud means germinate. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: So will my word be that goes forth out of my mouth; it will not return unto me void, but it will accomplish that which I please, and it will prosper in the thing whereto I sent it. This recalls Matthew: rain may to us seem lost when it falls on a desert, but it fulfils some purpose of God. So the gospel word falling on the hard heart; it sometimes works a change at last; and even if so, it leaves men without excuse. The full accomplishment of this verse, and verses 12 and 13, is, however, to be at the Jews' final restoration and conversion of the world, as in chapters 11 and 60. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: For you will go out with joy, and be led forth with peace; the mountains and the hills will break forth before you into singing, and all the trees of the field will clap their hands. Go out means from the various countries in which ye, the Jews, are scattered, to your own land, as in Ezekiel. Led means by Messiah, your Leader, as in verses 4 and chapter 52 and Micah. Mountains, trees, etc., are images justly used to express the seeming sympathy of nature with the joy of God's people. For, when sin is removed, the natural world shall be delivered from vanity, and be renewed, so as to be in unison with the regenerated moral world, as in chapters 44 and Psalm 98 and Romans. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: Instead of the thorn will come up the fir tree, and instead of the brier will come up the myrtle tree, and it will be to the Lord for a name, for an everlasting sign that will not be cut off. Thorn means emblem of the wicked, as in 2 Samuel and Micah. Fir tree means the godly, as in chapters 60 and Psalm 92. Compare as to the change wrought, Romans. Brier means emblem of uncultivation, as in chapter 5. Myrtle is Hebrew, Hedes, from which comes Hedassah, the original name of Esther. Type of the Christian church; for it is a lowly, though beautiful, fragrant, and evergreen shrub, as in Psalm 92. For a name, everlasting sign means a perpetual memorial to the glory of Jehovah, as in Jeremiah. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
