Study Notes — Isaiah

Chapter 31

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Woe to those who go down to Egypt for help, and stay on horses, and trust in chariots because they are many, and in horsemen because they are very strong, but do not look unto the Holy One of Israel, neither seek the Lord! The chief strength of the Egyptian armies lay in their cavalry: in Egypt's level and fertile plains horses could easily be used and fed, whereas in hilly Palestine horses were not so easily had or available, so the Jews were the more eager to get Egyptian chariots as allies against Assyrian cavalry, whose chariots are represented drawn by three horses with three men in them. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: Yet he also is wise, and will bring evil, and will not call back his words, but will arise against the house of the evildoers, and against the help of those who work iniquity. He also is wise, as well as the Egyptian priests so famed for wisdom, who are fools before Him; He not only devises but executes what He devises without calling back His words. The house is the whole race of evildoers, and the help is the Egyptian succour sought by the Jews. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: Now the Egyptians are men, and not God, and their horses flesh, and not spirit. When the Lord stretches out his hand, both he that helps will fall, and he that is helped will fall down, and they all will fail together. The Egyptians are men, not God, and their horses flesh, not of divine power; he that helps is Egypt, and he that is helped is Judah, and both fall together when the Lord stretches out His hand. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: For thus has the Lord spoken to Isaiah: Like as the lion and the young lion roaring on his prey, when a multitude of shepherds is called forth against him, he will not be afraid of their voice, nor abase himself for the noise of them, so will the Lord of hosts come down to fight for Mount Zion, and for the hill thereof. The lion roars, or growls, over his prey, and will not be disheartened or frightened by the shepherds' multitude, and so the Lord of hosts will come down to fight for Mount Zion with fearless might. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: As birds flying, so will the Lord of hosts defend Jerusalem, defending also he will deliver it, and passing over he will preserve it. As in the lion image the point is Jehovah's fearless might, so in the birds it is His solicitous affection; flying is rather defending their young with their wings, hovering over to protect them, and passing over is as the destroying angel passing over to spare the blood-marked houses at the first passover, leaping forward to destroy the enemy and spare His people. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: Turn unto him from whom the children of Israel have deeply revolted. The power and love of Jehovah just mentioned are the strongest incentives for returning to Him, and the change of person marks that when they return to the Lord He will address them in more direct terms of communion in the second person, whereas so long as they were revolters God speaks of them at a distance in the third person. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: For in that day every man will cast away his idols of silver, and his idols of gold, which your own hands have made unto you for a sin. In the day of trial the idols will be found to render no help and will therefore be cast away, as at the future restoration and conversion of Israel simultaneously with Jehovah's interposition in its defence; the idols are those whereby especially they contracted guilt. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: Then the Assyrian will fall with the sword, not of a mighty man, and the sword, not of a mean man, will devour him, and he will flee from the sword, and his young men will be discomfited. The Assyrian is Sennacherib, representative of some powerful head of the ungodly in the latter ages, and he will fall by the unseen sword of God, not of mighty or mean man; he alone fled homewards after his army was destroyed, and his young men, the flower of his army, will be subject to slavery, liable to tribute and personal service. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: He will pass over to his stronghold for fear, and his princes will be afraid of the ensign, says the Lord, whose fire is in Zion, and his furnace in Jerusalem. He will rather pass beyond his strongholds, not stopping to take refuge in them through fear, and his princes will be afraid of the ensign, the banner of Jehovah protecting the Jews; the fire and furnace are the light and fire of Jehovah's altar at Jerusalem, or His dwelling-place, His hearth, there, or else the fiery furnace awaiting all enemies who attack Jerusalem. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
