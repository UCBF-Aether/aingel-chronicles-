Study Notes — Isaiah

Chapter 64

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Oh that you would rend the heavens, that you would come down, that the mountains might flow down at your presence. This chapter treats the transition from complaint to prayer. Rend heavens means bursting forth to execute vengeance, suddenly descending on Thy people's foe, as in Psalms and Habakkuk. Flow down recalls Judges and Micah. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: As when the melting fire burns, the fire causes the waters to boil, to make your name known to your adversaries, that the nations may tremble at your presence! Oh, that Thy wrath would consume Thy foes as the fire. Rather, as the fire burneth the dry brushwood. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: When you did terrible things which we looked not for, you came down, the mountains flowed down at your presence. When means supply from verse 2, As when. Terrible things recalls Psalm 65. We looked not for means far exceeding the expectation of any of our nation; unparalleled before, as in Exodus and Psalm 68. Camest down means on Mount Sinai. Mountains flowed means repeated from verse 1; they pray God to do the very same things for Israel now as in former ages. Some, instead of flowed here, and flow in verse 1, translate from a different Hebrew root, quake, quaked; but fire melts and causes to flow, rather than to quake, as in verse 2. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: For since the beginning of the world men have not heard, nor perceived by the ear, neither has the eye seen, O God, besides you, what he has prepared for him that waits for him. Perceived by the ear means Paul in 1 Corinthians has for this, nor have entered into the heart of man; the virtual sense, sanctioned by his inspired authority; men might hear with the outward ear, but they could only by the Spirit perceive with the heart the spiritual significancy of God's acts, both those in relation to Israel, primarily referred to here, and those relating to the Gospel secondarily, which Paul refers to. O God, what he prepared is rather, nor hath eye seen a god beside thee who doeth such things. They refer to God's past marvellous acts in behalf of Israel as a plea for His now interposing for His people; but the Spirit, as Paul by inspiration shows, contemplated further God's revelation in the Gospel, which abounds in marvellous paradoxes never before heard of by carnal ear, not to be understood by mere human sagacity, and when foretold by the prophets not fully perceived or credited; and even after the manifestation of Christ not to be understood save through the inward teaching of the Holy Ghost. These are partly past and present, and partly future; therefore Paul substitutes prepared for doeth, though his context shows he includes all three. For waiteth he has love Him; godly waiting on Him must flow from love, and not mere fear. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: You meet him that rejoices and works righteousness, those that remember you in your ways; behold, you are wroth, for we have sinned; in those is continuance, and we will be saved. Meetest means that is, Thou makest peace, or enterest into covenant with him, as in chapter 47. Rejoiceth and worketh means that is, who with joyful willingness worketh, as in Acts and John. Those means Thou meetest those, in apposition to him who represents a class whose characteristics those that, etc., more fully describes. Remember thee in thy ways recalls chapter 26. Sinned is literally, tripped, carrying on the figure in ways. In those is continuance means a plea to deprecate the continuance of God's wrath; it is not in Thy wrath that there is continuance, as in chapters 54 and Psalms, but in Thy ways, those, namely, of covenant mercy to Thy people, as in Micah and Malachi; on the strength of the everlasting continuance of His covenant they infer by faith, we shall be saved. God remembered for them His covenant, as in Psalm 106, though they often remembered not Him, as in Psalm 78. Some translate, we have sinned for long in them, thy ways, and could we then be saved? But they hardly would use such a plea when their very object was to be saved. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: But we are all as an unclean thing, and all our righteousnesses are as filthy rags, and we all do fade as a leaf, and our iniquities, like the wind, have taken us away. Unclean thing means legally unclean, as a leper. True of Israel, everywhere now cut off by unbelief and by God's judgments from the congregation of the saints. Righteousness means plural, uncleanness extended to every particular act of theirs, even to their prayers and praises. True of the best doings of the unregenerate, as in Philippians, Titus, and Hebrews. Filthy rags is literally, a menstruous rag, as in Leviticus and Lamentations. Fade, leaf recalls Psalm 90. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: There is none that calls upon your name, that stirs up himself to take hold of you, for you have hid your face from us, and have consumed us, because of our iniquities. Stirreth means rouseth himself from spiritual drowsiness. Take hold recalls chapter 27. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: But now, O Lord, you are our father; we are the clay, and you our potter, and we all are the work of your hand. Father recalls chapter 63 verse 16. Clay, potter recalls chapters 29 and 45. Unable to mould themselves aright, they beg the sovereign will of God to mould them unto salvation, even as He made them at the first, and is their Father. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: Be not wroth very sore, O Lord, neither remember iniquity for ever; behold, see, we beseech you, we are all your people. This recalls Psalm 74. We are thy people recalls Jeremiah. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: Your holy cities are a wilderness, Zion is a wilderness, Jerusalem a desolation. Holy cities means no city but Jerusalem is called the holy city, as in chapters 48 and 52; the plural, therefore, refers to the upper and the lower parts of the same city Jerusalem; or all Judea was holy to God, so its cities were deemed holy. But the parallelism favours the upper and lower parts reading. Zion and Jerusalem, the one city, answering to holy cities. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: Our holy and our beautiful house, where our fathers praised you, is burned up with fire, and all our pleasant things are laid waste. House means the temple. Beautiful means includes the idea of glorious, as in Mark and Acts. Burned recalls Psalm 74, Lamentations, and 2 Chronicles. Its destruction under Nebuchadnezzar prefigured that under Titus. Pleasant things is Hebrew, objects of desire; our homes, our city, and all its dear associations. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: Will you refrain yourself for these things, O Lord? will you hold your peace, and afflict us very sore? For these things means wilt Thou, notwithstanding these calamities of Thy people, still refuse Thy aid, as in chapter 42? — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
