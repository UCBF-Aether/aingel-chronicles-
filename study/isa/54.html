Study Notes — Isaiah

Chapter 54

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Sing, O barren, you that did not bear; break forth into singing, and cry aloud, you that did not travail with child, for more are the children of the desolate than the children of the married wife, says the Lord. This chapter treats the fruit of Messiah's sufferings, and of Israel's final penitence at her past unbelief in chapter 53 verse 6: her joyful restoration and enlargement by Jehovah, whose wrath was momentary, but His kindness everlasting. Israel converted is compared to a wife in verse 5 and chapter 62, put away for unfaithfulness, but now forgiven and taken home again. The converted Gentiles are represented as a new progeny of the long-forsaken but now restored wife. The pre-eminence of the Hebrew church as the mother church of Christendom is the leading idea; the conversion of the Gentiles is mentioned only as part of her felicity. Sing means for joy, as in Zephaniah. Barren means the Jewish church once forsaken by God, and therefore during that time destitute of spiritual children, as in verse 6. Didst not bear means during the Babylonian exile primarily; secondarily, and chiefly, during Israel's present dispersion. The children means the Gentiles adopted by special grace into the original church, as in verse 3 and chapter 49. Than married wife means than were her spiritual children, when Israel was still a married wife, under the law, before the Babylonian exile, before God put her away. So Paul contrasts the universal church of the New Testament with the church of the Old Testament legal dispensation, quoting this very passage in Galatians. But the full accomplishment of it is yet future. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: Enlarge the place of your tent, and let them stretch forth the curtains of your habitations; spare not, lengthen your cords, and strengthen your stakes. This recalls chapters 49 and Jeremiah: thy children shall be so many that thy borders must be extended to contain them. Curtains means the cloth forming the covering of the tent. Spare not means give abundantly the means for the enlargement of the church, as in 2 Corinthians. Cords and stakes means the more the tent is enlarged by lengthening the cords by which the cloth covering is fastened to the ground, the more the stakes supporting the tent need to be strengthened; the church is not merely to seek new converts, but to strengthen those she has in the faith. The image is appropriate, as the tabernacle was the symbol of the old Israelitish church, as in chapter 33. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: For you will break forth on the right hand and on the left, and your seed will inherit the Gentiles, and make the desolate cities to be inhabited. Break forth is rather, burst forth with increase; thy offspring shall grow, answering to thy seed in the parallel clause. Thy seed means Israel and her children, as distinguished from the Gentiles. Desolate cities means of Israel, as in chapter 44. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: Fear not, for you will not be ashamed, neither be confounded, for you will not be put to shame, for you will forget the shame of your youth, and will not remember the reproach of your widowhood any more. This recalls chapters 41. Shame of thy youth means Israel's unfaithfulness as wife of Jehovah, almost from her earliest history. Reproach of widowhood means Israel's punishment in her consequent dismissal from God and barrenness of spiritual children in Babylon and her present dispersion, as in verses 1 and chapter 49 and Jeremiah and Hosea. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: For your Maker is your husband; The Lord of hosts is his name, and your Redeemer the Holy One of Israel; The God of the whole earth will he be called. Husbands is plural for singular, to denote excellency. Of Israel, whole earth means not until He manifests Himself as God of Israel shall He appear as God of the whole earth, as in Psalm 102 and Zechariah. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: For the Lord has called you as a woman forsaken and grieved in spirit, and a wife of youth, when you were refused, says your God. Called means that is, recalled: the prophetic past for the future. Forsaken means that had been forsaken. When thou means or, when she was rejected; one who had been a wife of youth, as in Ezekiel and Jeremiah, at the time when thou, or she, was rejected for infidelity. A wife of youth but afterwards rejected. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: For a small moment have I forsaken you, but with great mercies will I gather you. Small moment means as compared with Israel's coming long prosperity, as in chapters 26 and 60. So the spiritual Israel, as in Psalm 30 and 2 Corinthians. Gather thee means to Myself from thy dispersions. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: In a little wrath I hid my face from you for a moment, but with everlasting kindness will I have mercy on you, says the Lord your Redeemer. In a little wrath is rather, In the overflowing of wrath, as in Proverbs, margin. The wrath, though but for a moment, was overflowing while it lasted. Hid face recalls chapters 8 and Psalm 30. Everlasting means in contrast to for a moment. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: For this is as the waters of Noah unto me, for as I have sworn that the waters of Noah should no more go over the earth, so have I sworn that I would not be angry with you, nor rebuke you. I am about to do the same in this instance as in Noah's flood. As I swore then that it should not return, as in Genesis, and I kept that promise, so I swear now to My people, and will perform My promise, that there shall be no return of the deluge of My wrath upon them. Some, on insufficient authority, read, the same will I do now as, in the days of Noah. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: For the mountains will depart, and the hills be removed, but my kindness will not depart from you, neither will the covenant of my peace be removed, says the Lord that has mercy on you. This recalls chapters 51 and Psalm 89 and Romans. Covenant of my peace recalls 2 Samuel: the covenant whereby I have made thee at peace with Me. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: O you afflicted, tossed with tempest, and not comforted, behold, I will lay your stones with fair colours, and lay your foundations with sapphires. Not comforted means by anyone; none gave her help or comfort. Lay with fair colours is rather, lay in cement of vermilion. The Hebrew for fair colours means stibium, the paint with which Eastern women painted their eyelids and eyelashes, as in 2 Kings. The very cement shall be of the most beautiful colour, as in Revelation. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: I will make your windows of agates, and your gates of carbuncles, and all your borders of pleasant stones. Windows is rather, battlements; literally, suns, applied to battlements from their radiated appearance. Agates is rather, rubies. Carbuncles is literally, sparkling gems; the carbuncle when held to the sun becomes like a burning coal. All thy borders is rather, thy whole circuit, consisting of precious stones. The glory of the church on earth, when the Hebrew church, according to the original design, shall be the metropolis of Christendom. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: All your children will be taught of the Lord, and great will be the peace of your children. This is quoted by the Saviour in John, to prove that in order to come to Him, men must be drawn by the Father, so Jeremiah, Micah, Corinthians, Hebrews, and 1 John. Great peace means generally, as in Psalm 119. Specially referring to the peaceful prosperity which shall prevail under Messiah in the latter days, as in chapters 2 and 9. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: In righteousness you will be established; you will be far from oppression, for you will not fear, and from terror, for it will not come near you. Righteousness means the characteristic of the reign of Messiah, as in chapters 11 and Psalm 72 and Revelation. Far from oppression, etc., means far from suffering oppression; for thou shalt have nothing to fear. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: Behold, they will surely gather together, but not by me; whosoever will gather together against you will fall for your sake. Gather together, etc., means that is, If it should happen that enemies gather together against thee, as in Psalm 2, they will not have been sent by Me, as in Hosea, as instruments of My wrath, nay, it will be with My disapproval; for whosoever shall gather together, etc., as in Psalm 59. Fall for thy sake is rather, shall come over to thy side. Literally, fall to thee, as in Jeremiah. To be fully fulfilled to Jerusalem hereafter, as in Zechariah. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: Behold, I have created the smith that blows the coals in the fire, and that brings forth an instrument for his work, and I have created the waster to destroy. The workman that forms weapons against thee in verse 17 is wholly in My power, therefore thou needest not fear, having Me on thy side. For his work is rather, by his labour. According to the exigencies of his work. Waster to destroy recalls chapters 10, 37, and 45: desolating conquerors who use the instruments framed by the smith. The repetition of the I implies, however, something in the latter half of the verse contrasted with the former; understand it, therefore, thus: I have in My power both him who frames arms and him who destroys them, arms. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: No weapon that is formed against you will prosper, and every tongue that will rise against you in judgment you will condemn. This is the heritage of the servants of the Lord, and their righteousness is of me, says the Lord. Condemn is an image from a court of justice. Those who desire to condemn thee thou shalt condemn, as in Exodus, Joshua, Psalm 64, and Romans. Righteousness of me recalls chapters 45 and 46; rather, this is their justification from Me. Their enemies would condemn them, but I justify and vindicate them, and so they condemn their enemies. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
