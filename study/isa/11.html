Study Notes — Isaiah

Chapter 11

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: There will come forth a rod out of the stem of Jesse, and a Branch will grow out of his roots. When the proud boughs of Lebanon, the Assyrians, are lopped and the forests cut down, a seemingly humble rod will come from Jesse to retrieve the injuries done by the Assyrian rod; the stem is literally the stump of a tree cut close by the roots, expressing the depressed state of David's royal house when Messiah arises from it to more than pristine glory, and he is both Branch and Root. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: The Spirit of the Lord will rest upon him, the spirit of wisdom and understanding, of counsel and might, of knowledge and of the fear of the Lord. Seven gifts are specified to imply their perfection in him, as the seven Spirits signify the Holy Ghost in perfect fullness; the Spirit rests permanently on him, not merely coming upon him, and the prophets had only a portion out of the fullness that is in the Son. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: He will be of quick understanding in the fear of the Lord — literally quick-scented in the fear of Jehovah, endowed with singular sagacity in discerning genuine religious fear even dormant in the unawakened sinner — and he will not judge after the sight of his eyes or reprove after the hearing of his ears. He judges not by mere external appearances or plausible hearsays but by the true merits of each case, as a just Judge and Ruler. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: With righteousness he will judge the poor and reprove with equity for the meek of the earth, and he will smite the earth with the rod of his mouth and slay the wicked with the breath of his lips. The rod of his mouth is his condemning sentences against the wicked, and the breath of his lips his judicial decisions; as the Word of God he comes to strike the blow deciding his claim to the kingdom usurped by Satan and the beast. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: Righteousness will be the girdle of his loins, and faithfulness the girdle of his reins. As the girdle secures firmly the rest of the garments, so truth gives firm consistency to his whole character, and he is the antitypical High Priest. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: The wolf will dwell with the lamb, the leopard lie down with the kid, the calf and young lion and fatling together, and a little child will lead them. Each animal is coupled with its natural prey, a fit state under the Prince of Peace; the animals may figure men of corresponding characters, yet a literal change restoring the state in Eden, with man's lost dominion over animals restored in the Son of Man, is the more likely interpretation. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: The cow and the bear will feed together and their young ones lie down together, and the lion will eat straw like the ox. The feeding together completes the picture of peace among former enemies, with predators no longer living on flesh and blood. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: The sucking child will play on the hole of the asp, and the weaned child will put his hand on the cockatrice's den. The child delights himself in sport where danger once reigned, and the cockatrice is a kind of adder more venomous than the asp, a fabulous serpent supposed hatched from a cock's egg. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: They will not hurt nor destroy in all God's holy mountain, for the earth will be full of the knowledge of the Lord as the waters cover the sea. The holy mountain, Zion and Jerusalem, seat of Messiah's throne, stands for the whole earth, and as waters find their way into every cavern of the sea's depths, so the knowledge of the Lord will pervade every recess of the earth. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: In that day there will be a root of Jesse, standing for an ensign of the people, to which the Gentiles will seek, and his rest will be glorious. The root is rather a shoot from the root, standing permanently and prominently as a banner and rallying point, to which the peoples give their allegiance and resort as to an oracle, and his resting-place is glorious like the sanctuary where the ark rested. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: In that day the Lord will set his hand again the second time to recover the remnant of his people from Assyria, Egypt, Pathros, Cush, Elam, Shinar, Hamath, and the islands of the sea. This coming restoration is distinct from that after Babylon yet resembles it, and like the first literal restoration so the second will be literal, only much more universal, gathering the remnant from Upper Egypt, Ethiopia, Persia, Babylonian Mesopotamia, and the far western regions beyond the sea. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: He will set up an ensign for the nations and assemble the outcasts of Israel and gather together the dispersed of Judah from the four corners of the earth. In the first restoration Judah alone was restored with a few of Israel, but in the future restoration both are expressly specified: outcasts are ascribed to Israel, dispersed to Judah, the masculine and feminine conjoined expressing the universality of the restoration, from the wings of the earth. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: The envy of Ephraim will depart and the adversaries of Judah will be cut off: Ephraim will not envy Judah, and Judah will not vex Ephraim. The jealousy began as early as Joshua's time, increased through Saul and David, and ended in disruption under Jeroboam; in the restoration the old envy and the adversaries from Judah hostile to Ephraim both cease. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: With united forces they will fly upon the shoulders of the Philistines toward the west and spoil them of the east together, laying their hand upon Edom and Moab, while the children of Ammon obey them. As a bird of prey they will make a sudden victorious descent upon their southwestern borders, and take possession of Edom south of Judah, Moab east of Jordan, and Ammon north of Moab, while the Arabs of the east are only despoiled, not reduced to regular government. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: The Lord will utterly destroy the tongue of the Egyptian sea and shake his hand over the river with his mighty wind, smiting it into seven streams so men may go over dryshod. There will be a second exodus eclipsing even the former one from Egypt: the Red Sea's tongue will be dried up as God dooms it, and the Euphrates, the obstruction to return from Assyria, will be divided into many streams, as Cyrus divided the Gyndes, so that even in sandals the people cross unwetted. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: There will be a highway for the remnant of his people left from Assyria, as there was for Israel in the day he came up out of Egypt. The highway will be clear of obstructions, like the way through the sea, for the remnant returning from Assyria. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
