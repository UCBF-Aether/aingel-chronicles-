Study Notes — Isaiah

Chapter 17

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: The burden of Damascus: Behold, Damascus is taken away from being a city, and it will be a ruinous heap. This prophecy concerns Damascus and its ally Samaria, Syria and Israel leagued together in chapters 7–8; Tiglath-pileser had already carried Damascus's people to Kir, but now in Hezekiah's reign a further overthrow is foretold, and Damascus is named before Israel because it was the prevailing power in the league, with Ephraim standing or falling with it. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: The cities of Aroer are forsaken, and they will be for flocks, which will lie down, and none will make them afraid. The cities of Aroer are the cities round Aroer and under its jurisdiction, near the river of Gad and founded by the Gadites, and the forsaken cities will become pasture like the waste places of chapter 5. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: The fortress also will cease from Ephraim, and the kingdom from Damascus, and the remnant of Syria will be as the glory of the children of Israel, says the Lord of hosts. The strongholds will be pulled down, Samaria especially, and the remnant of Syria left after Tiglath-pileser's overthrow will meet the same fate as Israel, its ally. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: In that day the glory of Jacob will be made thin, and the fatness of his flesh will wax lean. The glory of Jacob is the kingdom of Ephraim and all they rely on, and its fatness will waste like the fat ones of Assyria in chapter 10, a figure for destruction. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: It will be as when the harvestman gathers the corn and reaps the ears with his arm, and it will be as he that gathers ears in the valley of Rephaim. The inhabitants and wealth of Israel will be swept away with only a few left behind, as the husbandman gathers corn and fruit and leaves only a few gleaning ears, collecting the standing grain with one arm to cut it with the sickle in the other, in the fertile valley of Rephaim southwest of Jerusalem. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: Yet gleaning grapes will be left in it, as the shaking of an olive tree, two or three berries in the top of the uppermost bough, four or five in the outmost fruitful branches, says the Lord God of Israel. A few poor inhabitants will be left in Israel, like the two or three olive berries left on the topmost boughs, not worth the trouble of reaching. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: At that day a man will look to his Maker, and his eyes will have respect to the Holy One of Israel. Instead of trusting in fortresses, the remnant will look to the God who made them, turning from the strongholds that failed them. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: He will not look to the altars, the work of his hands, neither will he respect that which his fingers have made, either the groves or the images. The groves are symbolical trees representing the hosts of heaven, Saba, answering to Astarte queen of heaven as Baal is king, and the images are literally images to the sun, to Baal, who answers to the sun as Astarte answers to the hosts of heaven. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: In that day his strong cities will be as a forsaken bough and an uppermost branch, which they left because of the children of Israel, and there will be desolation. The forsaken bough is rather the leavings of woods, what the axeman leaves when he cuts down the grove, and only a few cities out of many will be left to Israel by God's purpose, executed by the Assyrian. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: Because you have forgotten the God of your salvation and have not been mindful of the rock of your strength, therefore you will plant pleasant plants and set them with strange slips. The God of salvation and rock of strength are the titles of Deuteronomy 32, and the pleasant plants are rather nursery grounds or pleasure-grounds, set with strange slips, cuttings of plants from far and therefore valuable — the idolatrous plantings of a people who forgot God. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: In the day you will make your plant to grow, and in the morning you will make your seed to flourish, but the harvest will be a heap in the day of grief and of desperate sorrow. In the day of planting, as soon as you plant it grows, and in the morning, immediately after, the seed flourishes, but promising as the prospect is, the harvest is gone in the day of expected possession, and the sorrow is desperate and irremediable. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: Woe to the multitude of many people, who make a noise like the noise of the seas, and to the rushing of nations, that make a rushing like the rushing of mighty waters! This begins a fragment on the sudden destruction of Sennacherib's great army in Judea: the prophet in vision perceives the vast and mixed Assyrian hosts on the hills of Judah, roaring like the seas, yet the connection is that notwithstanding calamities on Israel, God's people will not be utterly destroyed, and the Assyrian spoilers will perish. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: The nations will rush like the rushing of many waters, but God will rebuke them, and they will flee far off, and will be chased as the chaff of the mountains before the wind, and like a rolling thing before the whirlwind. God rebukes them and they flee, chased as chaff from threshing floors set on elevated places to catch the wind that separates chaff from wheat, and like stubble rolling before the whirlwind. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: And behold, at eveningtide trouble, and before the morning he is not: this is the portion of them that spoil us, and the lot of them that rob us. The destruction before morning was fulfilled to the letter in the host that at eveningtide was such a terror to Judah, and this is the general declaration of the doom awaiting the foes of God's people. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
