Study Notes — Isaiah

Chapter 45

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Thus says the Lord to his anointed, to Cyrus, whose right hand I have holden, to subdue nations before him, and I will loose the loins of kings, to open before him the two leaved gates, and the gates will not be shut. This chapter follows up the subject of the deliverance by Cyrus: verses 1 to 7 should have been appended to the previous chapter, and the new chapter should begin with verse 8, Drop down, etc. Reference to the deliverance by Messiah often breaks out from amidst the local and temporary details of the deliverance from Babylon, as the great ultimate end of the prophecy. His anointed means Cyrus is so called as being set apart as king, by God's providence, to fulfil His special purpose; though kings were not anointed in Persia, the expression is applied to him in reference to the Jewish custom of setting apart kings to the regal office by anointing. Right hand holden is an image from sustaining a feeble person by holding his right hand. Subdue nations means namely, the Cilicians, Syrians, Babylonians, Lydians, Bactrians, etc.; his empire extended from Egypt and the Mediterranean to the Indian Ocean, and from Ethiopia to the Euxine Sea. Loose the girdle of loins means that is, the girdle off the loins, and so enfeeble them; the loose outer robe of the Orientals, when girt fast round the loins, was the emblem of strength and preparedness for action, ungirt, was indicative of feebleness; weakeneth the strength of the mighty, looseth the girdle of the strong. The joints of Belshazzar's loins, we read in Daniel, were loosed during the siege by Cyrus, at the sight of the mysterious handwriting on the palace walls; his being taken by surprise, unaccoutred, is here foretold. To open gates means in the revelry in Babylon on the night of its capture, the inner gates, leading from the streets to the river, were left open, for there were walls along each side of the Euphrates with gates, which, had they been kept shut, would have hemmed the invading hosts in the bed of the river, where the Babylonians could have easily destroyed them. Also, the gates of the palace were left open, so that there was access to every part of the city, and such was its extent, that they who lived in the extremities were taken prisoners before the alarm reached the centre of the palace. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: I will go before you, and make the crooked places straight; I will break in pieces the gates of brass, and cut in sunder the bars of iron. Straight is, as in chapter 40, rather, maketh mountains plain, that is, clear out of thy way all opposing persons and things. The Keri reads as in verse 13, make straight. Gates of brass recalls Psalm 107; Herodotus says Babylon had a hundred massive gates, twenty-five on each of the four sides of the city, all, as well as their posts, of brass. Bars of iron are with which the gates were fastened. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: I will give you the treasures of darkness, and hidden riches of secret places, that you may know that I, the Lord, which call you by your name, am the God of Israel. Treasures of darkness means that is, hidden in subterranean places, a common Oriental practice. Sorcerers pretended to be able to show where such treasures were to be found; in opposition to their pretensions, God says, He will really give hidden treasures to Cyrus. Pliny says Cyrus obtained from the conquest of Asia thirty-four thousand pounds weight of gold, besides golden vases, and five hundred thousand talents of silver, and the goblet of Semiramis, weighing fifteen talents. That thou mayest know means namely, not merely that He was the God of Israel, but that He was Jehovah, the true God; Ezra shows the correspondence of the event with the prediction had the desired effect on Cyrus. Which call thy name means so long before designate thee by name, as in chapter 43 verse 1. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: For Jacob my servant's sake, and Israel my elect, I have even called you by your name; I have surnamed you, though you have not known me. This recalls chapters 41 verse 8 and 43 verse 14. Surnamed means that is, designated to carry out My design of restoring Judah, as in chapters 44 and 45 verse 1; some translate, I have addressed thee by an honourable name. Hast not known me means previous to My calling thee to this office; after God's call, Cyrus did know Him in some degree, as in Ezra. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: I am the Lord, and there is none else, there is no God beside me; I girded you, though you have not known me. This recalls chapters 42 verse 8, 43 verses 3 and 11, 44 verse 8, and 46 verse 9. Girded thee means whereas I will loose the girdle off the loins of kings in verse 1, strengthening thee, but enfeebling them before thee. Though not known me recalls verse 4; God knows His elect before they are made to know Him. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: That they may know from the rising of the sun, and from the west, that there is none beside me. I am the Lord, and there is none else. From the rising to the setting of the sun means that is, from east to west, the whole habitable world. It is not said, from north to south, for that would not imply the habitable world, as, from east to west, does. The conquest of Jerusalem by Babylon, the capital of the world, and the overthrow of Babylon and restoration of the Jews by Cyrus, who expressly acknowledged himself to be but the instrument in God's hands, were admirably suited to secure, throughout the world, the acknowledgment of Jehovah as the only true God. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: I form the light, and create darkness; I make peace, and create evil; I the Lord do all these things. Create means, yatzar, to give form to previously existing matter, while bara means to create from nothing the chaotic dark material. Light and darkness are literally, as in Genesis, emblematical also, prosperity to Cyrus, calamity to Babylon and the nations to be vanquished. Isaiah refers also to the Oriental belief in two coexistent, eternal principles, ever struggling with each other, light or good, and darkness or evil, Oromasden and Ahrimanen; God, here, in opposition, asserts His sovereignty over both. Create evil means not moral evil, but in contrast to peace in the parallel clause, war, disaster, as in chapters 65 and Amos. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: Drop down, you heavens, from above, and let the skies pour down righteousness; let the earth open, and let them bring forth salvation, and let righteousness spring up together; I the Lord have created it. Drop means namely, the fertilising rain. Skies means clouds, lower than the heavens. Righteousness means that is, the dews of the Holy Spirit, whereby righteousness shall spring up, as at the latter end of the verse. Earth means figuratively for the hearts of men on it, opened for receiving the truth by the Holy Ghost. Them means the earth and the heavens; some prefer: Let the earth open, and let salvation and justice grow forth, let it bring them forth together, I the Lord have created him, as in verse 13. Some translate, Let all kinds of salvation, prosperity, be fruitful. The revival of religion after the return from Babylon suggests to the prophet the diffusion of Messiah's Gospel, especially in days still future; hence the elevation of the language to a pitch above what is applicable to the state of religion after the return. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: Woe unto him that strives with his Maker! Let the potsherd strive with the potsherds of the earth. Will the clay say to him that fashions it: What do you make? Or your work: He has no hands? This anticipates the objections which the Jews might raise as to why God permitted their captivity, and when He did restore them, why He did so by a foreign prince, Cyrus, not a Jew, as in chapter 40, but mainly and ultimately, the objections about to be raised by the Jews against God's sovereign act in adopting the whole Gentile world as His spiritual Israel in verse 8, referring to this catholic diffusion of the Gospel, as if it were an infringement of their nation's privileges, so Paul expressly quotes it in Romans 9. Let strive is not in the Hebrew; rather, in apposition with him, A potsherd among the potsherds of the earth! A creature fragile and worthless as the fragment of an earthen vessel, among others equally so, and yet presuming to strive with his Maker! The English version implies, it is appropriate for man to strive with man, in opposition to 2 Timothy. Thy, He means shall thy work say of thee, He? — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: Woe unto him that says unto his father: What do you beget? Or to the woman: What have you brought forth? If it be wrong for a child, born in less favourable circumstances, to upbraid his parents with having given him birth, a fortiori, it is, to upbraid God for His dealings with us. Rather translate, a father, a woman. The Jews considered themselves exclusively God's children and were angry that God should adopt the Gentiles besides. Woe to him who says to one already a father, Why dost thou beget other children? — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: Thus says the Lord, the Holy One of Israel, and his Maker: Ask me of things to come concerning my sons, and concerning the work of my hands command me. Command means instead of striving with Me in regard to My purposes, your wisdom is in prayer to ask, and even command Me, in so far as it is for My glory, and for your real good. Sons recalls chapters 54 and Galatians. Work of my hands is spiritually, as in Ephesians, also literal Israel, as in chapter 60. Some translate, instead of command, Leave it to Me, in My dealings concerning My sons and concerning the work of My hands, to do what I will with My own. Some read it interrogatively, Do ye presume to question Me and dictate to Me, as in verses 9 and 10? The same sense is given, if the words be taken in irony. But the English version is best. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: I have made the earth, and created man upon it; I, even my hands, have stretched out the heavens, and all their host have I commanded. The same argument for prayer, drawn from God's omnipotence and consequent power, to grant any request, occurs in chapter 40 verses 26 to 31. I, even my hands is so Hebrew, Thou, thy hand, both nominatives, in apposition. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: I have raised him up in righteousness, and I will direct all his ways; he will build my city, and he will let go my captives, not for price nor reward, says the Lord of hosts. Him is Cyrus, type of Messiah, who redeems the captives of Satan without money and without price, freely, gratuitously, as in chapters 55, 52, and 61, Zechariah, and Romans. In righteousness means to fulfil My righteous purpose, as in chapters 41 and 42 and Jeremiah. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: Thus says the Lord: The labour of Egypt, and merchandise of Ethiopia and of the Sabeans, men of stature, will come over unto you, and they will be yours; they will come after you; in chains they will come over, and they will fall down unto you, they will make supplication unto you, saying: Surely God is in you, and there is none else, there is no God. The language but cursorily alludes to Egypt, Ethiopia, and Seba, being given to Cyrus as a ransom in lieu of Israel whom he restored in chapter 43 verse 3, but mainly and fully describes the gathering in of the Gentiles to Israel, especially at Israel's future restoration, as in chapters 2, 14, 19, 60, and 49, Psalms 68 and 72. Labour means wealth acquired by labour. Sabeans of stature are the men of Meroe, in Upper Egypt; Herodotus calls the Ethiopians the tallest of men, as in chapter 18 and 1 Chronicles. Thee means Jerusalem, my city, in verse 13. In chains recalls Psalm 149: The saints shall judge the world and rule the nations with a rod of iron. The chains, in the case of the obedient, shall be the easy yoke of Messiah; as the sword of the Spirit also is saving to the believer, condemnatory to the unbeliever. God is in thee recalls Jeremiah. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: Verily you are a God that hides yourself, O God of Israel, the Saviour. God that hidest thyself is, some, after Jerome, explain this as the confession of Egypt, etc., that God is concealed in human form in the person of Jesus. Rather, connected with verses 9 and 10, the prophet, contemplating the wonderful issue of the seemingly dark counsels of God, implies a censure on those who presume to question God's dealings, as in chapters 55 and Deuteronomy 29. Faith still discerns, even under the veil, the covenant-keeping God of Israel, the Saviour, as in chapter 8. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: They will be ashamed, and also confounded, all of them; they will go to confusion together that are makers of idols. Ashamed means disappointed in their expectation of help from their idols, as in chapter 42 verse 17 and Psalm 97. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: But Israel will be saved in the Lord with an everlasting salvation; you will not be ashamed nor confounded world without end. In the Lord recalls verses 24 and 25, contrasted with the idols which cannot give even temporary help in verse 16; in Jehovah there is everlasting salvation, as in chapter 26. Not ashamed is opposed to the doom of the idolaters, who, in the hour of need, shall be ashamed, as in verse 16. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

18 COMMENTARY: For thus says the Lord that created the heavens; God himself that formed the earth and made it; he has established it, he created it not in vain, he formed it to be inhabited; I am the Lord, and there is none else. This recalls verse 12. Not in vain, he formed it to be inhabited means therefore, Judah, lying waste during the Babylonish captivity, shall be peopled again by the exiles. The Jews, from this passage, infer that, after the resurrection, the earth shall be inhabited, for there can be no reason why the earth should then exist in vain any more than now. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

19 COMMENTARY: I have not spoken in secret, in a dark place of the earth; I said not unto the seed of Jacob: Seek me in vain; I the Lord speak righteousness, I declare things that are right. Secret means not like the heathen oracles which gave their responses from dark caverns, with studied obscurity, as in chapter 48. Christ plainly quotes these words, thereby identifying Himself with Jehovah. I said not, Seek in vain means when I commanded you to seek Me, Jehovah did so in verse 11, Ask Me, etc., it was not in order that ye might be sent empty away. Especially in Israel's time of trial, God's interposition, in behalf of Zion hereafter, is expressly stated as about to be the answer to prayer, as in chapter 62 and Psalm 102. So in the case of all believers, the spiritual Israel. Righteousness means that which is veracious: not in the equivocal terms of heathen responses, fitly symbolised by the dark places from which they were uttered. Right means true, as in chapter 41 verse 26. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

20 COMMENTARY: Assemble yourselves and come; draw near together, you that are escaped of the nations; they have no knowledge that set up the wood of their graven image, and pray unto a god that cannot save. Escaped of the nations means those of the nations who shall have escaped the slaughter inflicted by Cyrus. Now, at last, ye shall see the folly of praying to a god that cannot save, as in verse 16. Ultimately, those that shall be left of all the nations which shall come against Jerusalem are meant, as in Zechariah; they shall then all be converted to the Lord, as in chapters 66 and Jeremiah and Zechariah. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

21 COMMENTARY: Tell, and bring them near, yes, let them take counsel together; who has declared this from ancient time? Who has told it from that time? Have not I the Lord? And there is no God else beside me; a just God and a Saviour; there is none beside me. This is a challenge to the worshippers of idols, as in chapter 41 verse 1. Take counsel together means as to the best arguments wherewith to defend the cause of idolatry. Who from that time recalls chapters 41 and 44 verse 8: Which of the idols has done what God hath, namely, foretold, primarily as to Cyrus, ultimately as to the final restoration of Israel hereafter? The idolatry of Israel before Cyrus' time will have its counterpart in the Antichrist and the apostasy, which shall precede Christ's manifestation. Just and Saviour means righteous in keeping His promises, and therefore a Saviour to His people. Not only is it not inconsistent with, but it is the result of, His righteousness, or justice, that He should save His redeemed, as in chapters 42 and Psalm 85 and Romans. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

22 COMMENTARY: Look unto me, and be saved, all the ends of the earth, for I am God, and there is none else. And be ye saved means the second imperative expresses the result which will follow obedience to the first, ye shall be saved, as in John. Numbers: If a serpent had bitten any man, when he beheld the serpent of brass he lived. What so simple as a look? Not do something, but look to the Saviour. Believers look by faith, the eye of the soul. The look is that of one turning to God, as at once Just and the Saviour in verse 21, that is, the look of conversion. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

23 COMMENTARY: I have sworn by myself, the word is gone out of my mouth in righteousness, and will not return, that unto me every knee will bow, every tongue will swear. Sworn by myself is equivalent to, As I live, as Romans quotes it, so Numbers. God could swear by no greater, therefore He swears by Himself. Word in righteousness is rather, the truth, as in verse 19, is gone forth from My mouth, the word of promise, and it shall not return, that is, which shall not be revoked. But the accents favour the English version. Tongue swear means namely, an oath of allegiance to God as their true King, as in chapters 19 and 65. Yet to be fulfilled, as in Zechariah. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

24 COMMENTARY: Surely, will one say: In the Lord have I righteousness and strength; even unto him will men come, and all that are incensed against him will be ashamed. Rather, Only in Jehovah shall men say of me, this clause is parenthetical, is there righteousness, which includes salvation, as in verse 21, a just God and a Saviour, and chapter 46. Strength means namely, to save. Shall men come means those who have set themselves up against God shall come to Him in penitence for the past, as in chapter 19. Ashamed recalls verses 16 and 54 and chapter 41. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

25 COMMENTARY: In the Lord will all the seed of Israel be justified, and will glory. Israel means the spiritual Israel and the literal Israel, that is, the final remnant which shall all be saved, as in verse 17 and Romans. Justified means treated as if they were just, through Christ's righteousness and death. Glory means literally, sing in His praise. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
