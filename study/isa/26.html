Study Notes — Isaiah

Chapter 26

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: In that day this song will be sung in the land of Judah: We have a strong city; salvation will God appoint for walls and bulwarks. This is the song of praise of Israel after being restored to their own land, and as the overthrow of the apostate faction is described in chapter 25, so the peace of the faithful is described under the image of a well-fortified city. The strong city is Jerusalem, strong in Jehovah's protection and type of the new Jerusalem, contrasted with the overthrow of the ungodly foe, and salvation itself serves as walls, with the trench and antemural earthworks exterior to the wall as bulwarks. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: Open the gates, that the righteous nation which keeps the truth may enter in. This is the address of the returning people to the gates of Jerusalem, type of the heavenly city, and the righteous nation is that which had not apostatized during the captivity, or the nation of the Just One, the Jews. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: You will keep him in perfect peace, whose mind is stayed on you, because he trusts in you. Stayed recalls the Psalms, and Jesus can create perfect peace within the mind though storms of trial rage without, as a city kept securely by a strong garrison within though besieged without; keep literally means guard as with a garrison, and the mind is rather a thing formed, God's workmanship, constantly supported and preserved in perpetual peace. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: Trust in the Lord forever, for in the Lord Jehovah is everlasting strength. Lord Jehovah is in Hebrew Jah, Jehovah, and the union of the two names expresses in the highest degree God's unchanging love and power; this is one of the four passages where the English version retains Jehovah, and Jah, the eternal unchangeable One, is Jehovah, the rock of ages. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: For he brings down those who dwell on high, and the lofty city he lays low, he lays it low even to the ground, and brings it even to the dust. The lofty city is Babylon, representative of the stronghold of God's people's foes in all ages, as in chapters 25 and 13. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: The foot will tread it down, even the feet of the poor, and the steps of the needy. The poor are the once-afflicted Jewish captives, and the foot treading is figurative for exulting in the fall of God's enemies. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: The way of the just is uprightness: you, most upright, do weigh the path of the just. Uprightness is rather direct, directed by God to a prosperous issue however many afflictions intervene, as with the Jewish exiles, so the way means God's dealings with the righteous; the most upright is God himself, who weighs — rather, makes plain and level — the path, removing all obstacles. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: Yes, in the way of your judgments, O Lord, we have waited for you; the desire of our soul is to your name, and to the remembrance of you. The way of judgments is waiting for God's proceeding to punish the enemy, and the name and remembrance are the manifested character of God by which he would be remembered. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: With my soul I have desired you in the night, yes, with my spirit within me I will seek you early, for when your judgments are in the earth, the inhabitants of the world will learn righteousness. The faithful Jews speak individually, and the overthrow of the foe and restoration of the Jews follow upon prayer on their part and on all God's people's part; in the night recalls the Psalms and Song, and when God's judgments are in the earth, the remnant left after judgments will learn righteousness. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: Let favour be shown to the wicked, yet he will not learn righteousness; in the land of uprightness he will deal unjustly, and will not behold the majesty of the Lord. Uprightness here is rather prosperity, answering to favour and in antithesis to judgments in the earth: where prosperity attends the wicked as well as the just, he will not learn righteousness, therefore judgments must be sent that he may learn it. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: Lord, when your hand is lifted up, they will not see, but they will see and be ashamed for their envy at the people; yes, the fire of your enemies will devour them. God's hand is lifted up to punish his people's foes, and they who will not see will be made to see to their cost; their envy at the people is God's zeal for his people, seen with confusion, and the fire of his enemies is the fire to which his enemies are doomed. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: Lord, you will ordain peace for us, for you also have wrought all our works in us. Peace is God's favour, including all blessings temporal and spiritual, opposed to their previous trials, and God himself has wrought all their works in them. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: O Lord our God, other lords besides you have had dominion over us, but by you only will we make mention of your name. The other lords are temporal heathen kings such as Nebuchadnezzar, and spiritually idols and lusts, and it is due to God alone that his people again worship him as their Lord — they are his only, and will celebrate his name — showing the sanctifying effect of affliction. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: They are dead, they will not live; they are deceased, they will not rise; therefore you have visited and destroyed them, and made all their memory to perish. They are the other lords or tyrants, who will not live again; the deceased are in Hebrew Rephaim, powerless in the land of shades, and therefore — inasmuch as — God has visited and destroyed them and made their memory perish. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: You have increased the nation, O Lord, you have increased the nation, and are glorified; you had removed it far unto all the ends of the earth. Hast is a prophetical preterite, and to have removed far unto the ends of the earth is rather to have extended far all the borders of the land. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: Lord, in trouble they have visited you, they poured out a prayer when your chastening was upon them. Visited means sought, and poured out is as a vessel emptying all its contents; the prayer is literally a whispered prayer, a secret sighing to God for help in chastening. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: Like as a woman with child, that draws near the time of her delivery, is in pain and cries out in her pangs, so we have been in your sight, O Lord. This is an image of anguish accompanied with expectation, to be followed by joy that will cause the anguish utterly to be forgotten: Zion, looking for deliverance seemingly in vain but really about to be gloriously saved. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

18 COMMENTARY: We have been with child, we have been in pain, we have as it were brought forth wind, we have not wrought any deliverance in the earth, neither have the inhabitants of the world fallen. Wind is a figure for an abortive effort, and the "we" stands against "thy" and "my" in verse 19: what we vainly attempt, God will accomplish. The land of Judea is not made a place of security from enemies, and the inhabitants of the world — Israel, not the world in general — are not yet born, for the country still lies desolate and unpopulated. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

19 COMMENTARY: Your dead men will live, together with my dead body they will arise. Awake and sing, you that dwell in dust, for your dew is as the dew of herbs, and the earth will cast out the dead. In antithesis to verse 14, where Israel's foes will not live, Jehovah's dead men, the Jews, will live, primarily restored spiritually, civilly, and nationally, and ultimately and in the fullest scope restored to life literally. Together with my dead body is rather my dead body or bodies, the Jewish nation personified as spiritually and civilly dead, or the nation as a parent speaking of her children's bodies, and as Jesus is the antitype to Israel, Christ's dead body raised again is the source of his people's raising. Awake is spiritual, those in dust are prostrate and dead spiritually and nationally and literally, the dew falls copiously in the East and supplies the lack of rain, and the earth will bring the dead forth to life again. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

20 COMMENTARY: Come, my people, enter into your chambers, and shut your doors about you; hide yourself as it were for a little moment, until the indignation be overpast. The chambers are a place of safety where God shuts in the saints when he is about to take vengeance on the ungodly, as Noah and his family were shut in during the flood and as Israel was commanded not to go out on the night of the first-born's slaying; the saints are calmly and confidently to await the issue. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

21 COMMENTARY: For behold, the Lord comes out of his place to punish the inhabitants of the earth for their iniquity, and the earth will disclose her blood, and will no more cover her slain. The Lord comes out of his place as in Micah, and the earth disclosing her blood means all innocent blood shed and all wrongs done, so long seemingly with impunity, will then be avenged. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
