Study Notes — Isaiah

Chapter 25

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: O Lord, you are my God; I will exalt you, I will praise your name, for you have done wonderful things; your counsels of old are faithfulness and truth. This thanksgiving for the overthrow of the apostate faction and the setting up of Jehovah's throne on Zion continues chapter 24, with the restoration from Babylon as type and pledge; wonderful recalls chapter 9, the counsels of old are purposes planned long ago for his people's deliverance, and truth is in Hebrew Amen, covenant-keeping faithfulness to promises, the peculiar characteristic of Jesus. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: For you have made of a city a heap, of a defenced city a ruin, and a palace of strangers to be no city, and it will never be built. The heap is Babylon, type of Antichrist's seat to be destroyed in the last days, pictured as heaps as Babylon and Nineveh now are; the palace is Babylon regarded for its splendour as a vast palace, or a citadel, of strangers, foreigners whose capital pre-eminently Babylon was, aliens from the commonwealth of Israel and strangers from the covenants of promise, and it will never be built, as in chapter 13. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: Therefore the strong people will glorify you, and the city of the terrible nations will fear you. The strong people cannot be the Jews but other nations on which Babylon exercised cruelty, who will worship Jehovah, awed by the judgment on Babylon, and the city is collectively the cities of the surrounding nations, not Babylon, which will then be destroyed. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: For you have been a strength to the poor, a strength to the needy in his distress, a refuge from the storm, and a shadow from the heat, when the blast of the terrible ones is as a storm against the wall. The needy are the Jews, exiles from their country, the heat is calamity, the blast is wrath, and the storm is a tempest of rain, a winter flood rushing against and overthrowing a house's wall. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: You will bring down the noise of strangers, as the heat in a dry place, even the heat with the shadow of a cloud, and the branch of the terrible ones will be brought low. As heat in a dry land is brought down by a cloud's shadow, so God brings down the tumult, the shout of triumph, of strangers, and as heat is brought low by the cloud's shadow, so the branch, the offspring, of the terrible ones is brought low; or, as heat burning under a cloud makes the branch wither, the wicked are brought to ruin not for want of natural prosperity but by God's immediate act. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: In this mountain the Lord of hosts will make unto all people a feast of fat things, a feast of wines on the lees, of fat things full of marrow, of wines on the lees well refined. This mountain is Zion, where Messiah's kingdom was to begin and is to have its central seat hereafter at Jerusalem, as the common country of all nations; the feast is an image of felicity, the fat things are delicacies, the rich mercies of God in Christ, the wines on the lees are the oldest and most generous wine long kept on its lees, the marrow is the choicest dainties, and well refined is cleared of all dregs. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: He will destroy in this mountain the face of the covering cast over all people, and the veil spread over all nations. The covering is an image from mourning, in which the face was covered with a veil, and the veil is the mist of ignorance as to a future state and the way to eternal life, enveloping the nations and the unbelieving Jew; the Jew is first to be converted before all nations, for it is in this mountain, Zion, that the veil is taken off. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: He will swallow up death in victory, and the Lord God will wipe away tears from off all faces, and the rebuke of his people he will take away from off all the earth, for the Lord has spoken it. Quoted in 1 Corinthians in support of the resurrection, to swallow up in victory is completely and permanently to abolish death, and the rebuke taken away is the reproach his people bore, removed from the whole earth. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: In that day it will be said: Lo, this is our God; we have waited for him, and he will save us; this is the Lord; we have waited for him, we will be glad and rejoice in his salvation. After death is swallowed up forever, God's delivered people will say to the Lord, Lo, this is our God, whom unbelievers regarded as only a man; the words are moulded to point specially to the Son of God who saves, as he vouchsafed Israel temporal saving so to his elect he appears to confer eternal salvation, and the Jews have a special share in saying, This is our God. Waited is characteristic of God's people in all ages. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: For in this mountain the hand of the Lord will rest, and Moab will be trodden down under him, even as straw is trodden down for the dunghill. The hand rests as a permanent protector, while Israel is protected the foe is destroyed, and Moab is the representative of all foes of God's people, trodden down in his own place or country, in the water of the dung heap in which straw was trodden to make manure, or as the threshing-floor is trampled by the corn-drag. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: He will spread forth his hands in the midst of them, as he that swims spreads forth his hands to swim, and he will bring down their pride together with the spoils of their hands. Jehovah will spread his hands to strike the foe on this side and on that, with as little effort as a swimmer spreads his arms to cleave a passage through water; or Moab, sinking, strains every nerve to save himself, but Jehovah brings down his pride together with the craftily acquired spoils of his hands, or the joints of his hands, though Moab struggle hand and foot. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: The fortress of the high fort of your walls he will bring down, lay low, and bring to the ground, even to the dust. The fortress is the strongholds of Moab, representative of God's people's foes, or Babylon, or the society of infidels represented as a city, brought down to the dust. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
