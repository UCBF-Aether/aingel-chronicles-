Study Notes — Isaiah

Chapter 56

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Thus says the Lord: Keep judgment, and do justice, for my salvation is near to come, and my righteousness to be revealed. This chapter treats the preparation needed on the part of those who wish to be admitted to the kingdom of God. Judgment means equity. John the Baptist preached similarly a return to righteousness, as needed to prepare men for Messiah's first coming, as in Luke. So it shall be before the second coming, as in Malachi. Near to come recalls Matthew, also as to the second coming, as in chapters 62 and Luke and Romans and Hebrews. Righteousness, answering to salvation in the parallel clause, therefore means righteousness which bringeth salvation, as in chapters 46 and Romans. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: Blessed is the man that does this, and the son of man that lays hold on it, that keeps the sabbath from polluting it, and keeps his hand from doing any evil. This recalls Luke. The man is Hebrew, enosh, a man in humble life, in contradistinction to Hebrew, ish, one of high rank. Even the humblest, as the stranger and the eunuch in verses 4 and 6, are admissible to these privileges. This, it means what follows: keeping the Sabbath, etc., as in chapters 58 and Ezekiel. A proof that the Sabbath, in the spirit of its obligation, was to be binding under the Gospel, as in chapter 66. That gospel times are referred to is plain, from the blessing not being pronounced on the man who observed the sacrificial ritual of the Jewish law. Layeth hold is an image from one grasping firmly some precious object which he is afraid of having forcibly snatched from him. The Sabbath here includes all the ordinances of divine worship under the new gospel law. Keepeth hand from evil means the observance of the second table of the law; as the Sabbath referred to the first table. Together, they form the whole duty of man, the worship of God and a holy life. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: Neither let the son of the stranger, that has joined himself to the Lord, speak, saying: The Lord has utterly separated me from his people, neither let the eunuch say: Behold, I am a dry tree. God welcomes all believers, without distinction of persons, under the new economy, as in Acts. Joined to the Lord recalls Numbers: Proselytes. Separated means proselytes from the Gentiles were not admitted to the same privileges as native Israelites. This barrier between Jews and Gentiles was to be broken down, as in Ephesians. Eunuch recalls Acts: eunuchs were chamberlains over harems, or court ministers in general. Dry tree means barren, as in Luke; not admissible into the congregation of Israel, as in Deuteronomy. Under the Gospel the eunuch and stranger should be released from religious and civil disabilities. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: For thus says the Lord unto the eunuchs that keep my sabbaths, and choose the things that please me, and take hold of my covenant. Please me means sacrifice their own pleasure to mine. Take hold means so layeth hold, as in verse 2. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: Even unto them will I give in my house and within my walls a place and a name better than of sons and of daughters; I will give them an everlasting name, that will not be cut off. In mine house means the temple, the emblem of the church, as in 1 Timothy. They shall no longer be confined as proselytes were, to the outer court, but shall be admitted into the holiest, as in Hebrews. A place is literally, a hand. Than of sons means though the eunuch is barren of children in verse 3, I will give him a more lasting name than that of being father of sons and daughters, regarded as a high honour among the Hebrews, as in John and 1 John and Revelation. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: Also the sons of the stranger, that join themselves to the Lord, to serve him, and to love the name of the Lord, to be his servants, every one that keeps the sabbath from polluting it, and takes hold of my covenant. Lord recalls Jeremiah. Conditions of admission to the privileges of adoption. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: Even them will I bring to my holy mountain, and make them joyful in my house of prayer; their burnt offerings and their sacrifices will be accepted upon my altar, for my house will be called a house of prayer for all people. Even them recalls Ephesians. To my holy mountain means Jerusalem, the seat of the Lord's throne in His coming kingdom, as in chapters 2 and Jeremiah. Joyful recalls Romans. Burnt offerings, sacrifices means spiritual, of which the literal were types, as in Romans, Hebrews, and 1 Peter. Accepted recalls Ephesians. Altar recalls Hebrews, spiritually, the Cross of Christ, which sanctifies our sacrifices of prayer and praise. House for all people is or rather, peoples. No longer restricted to one favoured people, as in Malachi, John, and 1 Timothy. To be fully realised at the second coming, as in chapter 2. No longer literal, but spiritual sacrifice, namely, prayer shall be offered, as in Psalms, Malachi, and Matthew. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: The Lord God which gathers the outcasts of Israel says: Yet will I gather others to him, beside those that are gathered unto him. Jehovah will not only restore the scattered outcasts of Israel, as in chapters 11 and Psalm 147, to their own land, but will gather others, strangers, to him, Israel, besides those gathered, margin, to his gathered, that is, in addition to the Israelites collected from their dispersion, as in John and Ephesians. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: All you beasts of the field, come to devour, yes, all you beasts in the forest. Beasts means Gentile idolatrous nations hostile to the Jews, summoned by God to chastise them, as in Jeremiah and Ezekiel: the Chaldeans and subsequently the Romans. The mention of the outcasts of Israel in verse 8 brings in view the outcasting, caused by the sins of their rulers in verses 10 to 12. To devour means namely, Israel. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: His watchmen are blind; they are all ignorant, they are all dumb dogs, they cannot bark; sleeping, lying down, loving to slumber. His watchmen means Israel's spiritual leaders, as in chapters 62 and Ezekiel. Dumb dogs is an image from bad shepherds' watchdogs, which fail to give notice, by barking, of the approach of wild beasts. Blind recalls Matthew. Sleeping, lying down is rather, dreamers, sluggards. Not merely sleeping inactive, but under visionary delusions. Loving to slumber means not merely slumbering involuntarily, but loving it. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: Yes, they are greedy dogs which can never have enough, and they are shepherds that cannot understand; they all look to their own way, every one for his gain, from his quarter. Greedy is literally, strong, that is, insatiable, in appetite, as in Ezekiel and Micah. Cannot understand means unable to comprehend the wants of the people, spiritually: so verse 10, cannot bark. Look to own way means that is, their own selfish interests; not to the spiritual welfare of the people, as in Jeremiah and Ezekiel. From his quarter is rather, from the highest to the lowest. From his quarter, that is, from one end to the other of them, one and all, as in Genesis. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: Come, say they, I will fetch wine, and we will fill ourselves with strong drink, and to-morrow will be as this day, and much more abundant. Fetch wine is language of the national teachers challenging one another to drink. Some translate, I will take another cup, as in chapter 5. To-morrow, etc., means their self-indulgence was habitual and intentional: not merely they drink, but they mean to continue so. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
