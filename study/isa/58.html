Study Notes — Isaiah

Chapter 58

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Cry aloud, spare not, lift up your voice like a trumpet, and show my people their transgression, and the house of Jacob their sins. This chapter is a reproof of the Jews for their dependence on mere outward forms of worship. Aloud is Hebrew, with the throat, that is, with full voice, not merely from the lips, as in 1 Samuel. Speak loud enough to arrest attention. My people means the Jews in Isaiah's time, and again in the time of our Lord, more zealous for externals than for inward holiness. Some think the reference to be to the Jews in the captivity practising their rites to gain God's favour and a release; and that hence, sacrifices are not mentioned, but only fasting and Sabbath observance, which they could keep though far away from the temple in Jerusalem. The same also applies to their present dispersion, in which they cannot offer sacrifices, but can only show their zeal in fastings, etc. Compare as to our Lord's time, Matthew and Luke. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: Yet they seek me daily, and delight to know my ways, as a nation that did righteousness, and forsook not the ordinance of their God; they ask of me the ordinances of justice; they take delight in approaching to God. Put the stop at ways; and connect as a nation that, etc., with what follows; As a nation that did righteousness, thus answers to, they ask of Me just judgments, that is, as a matter of justice due to them, salvation to themselves, and destruction to their enemies; and forsook not the ordinance of their God, answers to they desire the drawing near of God, that God would draw near to exercise those just judgments in behalf of them, and against their enemies. So Jerome, In the confidence, as it were, of a good conscience, they demand a just judgment, in the language of the saints: Judge me, O Lord, for I have walked in mine integrity. So in Malachi, they affect to be scandalised at the impunity of the wicked, and impugn God's justice. Thus, seek Me daily, and desire, English version not so well, delight, to know My ways, refers to their requiring to know why God delayed so long in helping them. The English version gives a good, though different sense; namely, dispelling the delusion that God would be satisfied with outward observances, while the spirit of the law, was violated and the heart unchanged, as in verses 3 to 14 and Ezekiel, compare John, scrupulosity side by side with murder. The prophets were the commentators on the law, as their Magna Charta, in its inward spirit and not the mere letter. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: Wherefore have we fasted, say they, and you see not? Wherefore have we afflicted our soul, and you take no knowledge? Behold, in the day of your fast you find pleasure, and exact all your labours. Wherefore means the words of the Jews: Why is it that, when we fast, Thou dost not notice it, by delivering us? They think to lay God under obligation to their fasting, as in Psalm 73 and Malachi. Afflicted soul recalls Leviticus. Behold means God's reply. Pleasure means in antithesis to their boast of having afflicted their soul; it was only in outward show they really enjoyed themselves. Some not so well translate, business. Exact labours is rather, oppressive labours. Some, with the Vulgate, translate, Exact the whole upon your debtors; those who owe you labour, as in Nehemiah. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: Behold, you fast for strife and debate, and to smite with the fist of wickedness; you will not fast as you do this day, to make your voice to be heard on high. Ye shall not fast is rather, ye do not fast at this time, so as to make your voice to be heard on high, that is, in heaven; your aim in fasting is strife, not to gain the ear of God, as in 1 Kings. In the English version the sense is, If you wish acceptance with God, ye must not fast as ye now do, to make your voice heard high in strife. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: Is it such a fast that I have chosen? A day for a man to afflict his soul? Is it to bow down his head as a bulrush, and to spread sackcloth and ashes under him? Will you call this a fast, and an acceptable day to the Lord? For a man to afflict his soul means the pain felt by abstinence is not the end to be sought, as if it were meritorious; it is of value only in so far as it leads us to amend our ways, as in verses 6 and 7. Bow head, sackcloth means to affect the outward tokens, so as to appear to men to fast, as in Matthew, 1 Kings, and Esther. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: Is not this the fast that I have chosen? To loose the bands of wickedness, to undo the heavy burdens, and to let the oppressed go free, and that you break every yoke? Bands of wickedness means that is, to dissolve every tie wherewith one has unjustly bound his fellow men, as in Leviticus. Servitude, a fraudulent contract, etc. Undo heavy burdens is Hebrew, loose the bands of the yoke. Oppressed is literally, the broken. The expression, to let go free, implies that those broken with the yoke of slavery, are meant, as in Nehemiah and Jeremiah. Jerome interprets it, broken with poverty; bankrupt. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: Is it not to deal your bread to the hungry, and that you bring the poor that are cast out to your house? When you see the naked, that you cover him, and that you hide not yourself from your own flesh? Deal means distribute, as in Job. Cast out is rather, reduced. Naked, cover him recalls Matthew. Hide thyself means to be strange towards them, and not to relieve them in their poverty, as in Matthew. Flesh means kindred, as in Genesis. Also brethren in common descent from Adam, and brethren in Christ, as in James. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: Then will your light break forth as the morning, and your health will spring forth speedily, and your righteousness will go before you; the glory of the Lord will be your rereward. Light means emblem of prosperity, as in verse 10 and Job. Health is literally, a long bandage, applied by surgeons to heal a wound, as in chapter 1. Hence restoration from all past calamities. Go before thee means thy conformity to the divine covenant acts as a leader, conducting thee to peace and prosperity. Glory, reward means like the pillar of cloud and fire, the symbol of God's glory, which went behind Israel, separating them from their Egyptian pursuers, as in chapter 52 and Exodus. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: Then will you call, and the Lord will answer; you will cry, and he will say: Here I am. If you take away from the midst of you the yoke, the putting forth of the finger, and speaking vanity. Call, answer means when sin is renounced, as in chapter 65. When the Lord's call is not hearkened to, He will not hear our call, as in Psalms and Proverbs. Putting forth of finger means the finger of scorn pointed at simple-minded godly men. The middle finger was so used by the Romans. Speaking vanity means every injurious speech. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: And if you draw out your soul to the hungry, and satisfy the afflicted soul, then will your light rise in obscurity, and your darkness be as the noonday. Draw out thy soul means impart of thine own subsistence, or sustenance. Soul is figurative for that wherewith thou sustainest thy soul, or life. Light in obscurity means calamities shall be suddenly succeeded by prosperity, as in Psalm 112. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: The Lord will guide you continually, and satisfy your soul in drought, and make fat your bones, and you will be like a watered garden, and like a spring of water, whose waters fail not. In drought recalls chapters 41. Literally, drought, that is, parched places. Make fat is rather, strengthen. Give thee the free use of thy bones, or, of thy strength. Watered garden means an Oriental picture of happiness. Fail not is Hebrew, deceive not; as streams that disappoint the caravan which had expected to find water, as formerly, but find it dried up, as in Job. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: They that will be of you will build the old waste places; you will raise up the foundations of many generations, and you will be called: The repairer of the breach, The restorer of paths to dwell in. Of thee means thy people, the Israelites. Old waste places means the old ruins of Jerusalem, as in chapters 61 and Ezekiel. Foundations of many generations means that is, the buildings which had lain in ruins, even to their foundations, for many ages; called in the parallel passage in chapter 61, the former desolations; and in the preceding clause here, the old waste places. The literal and spiritual restoration of Israel is meant, which shall produce like blessed results on the Gentile world, as in Amos and Acts. Be called means appropriately: the name truly designating what thou shalt do. Breach means the calamity wherewith God visited Israel for their sin, as in chapters 30 and 1 Chronicles. Paths to dwell in means not that the paths were to be dwelt in, but the paths leading to their dwellings were to be restored; paths, so as to dwell in the land. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: If you turn away your foot from the sabbath, from doing your pleasure on my holy day, and call the sabbath a delight, the holy of the Lord, honourable, and will honour him, not doing your own ways, nor finding your own pleasure, nor speaking your own words. This recalls chapters 56 and Nehemiah. The Sabbath, even under the new dispensation, was to be obligatory, as in chapter 66. Foot means the instrument of motion, as in Proverbs; men are not to travel for mere pleasure on the Sabbath, as in Acts. The Jews were forbidden to travel on it farther than the tabernacle or temple. If thou keep thy foot from going on thy own ways and doing thy pleasure, etc., as in Exodus. My holy day means God claims it as His day; to take it for our pleasure is to rob Him of His own. This is the very way in which the Sabbath is mostly broken; it is made a day of carnal pleasure instead of spiritual delight. Holy of the Lord means not the predicate, but the subject; if thou call the holy day of Jehovah honourable; if thou treat it as a day to be honoured. Him means or else, it, the Sabbath. Not doing own way means answering to, turn away thy foot from the Sabbath. Nor finding pleasure means answering to, doing thy pleasure. To keep the Sabbath in an idle manner is the sabbath of oxen and asses; to pass it in a jovial manner is the sabbath of the golden calf, when the people sat down to eat and drink, and rose again to play; to keep it in surfeiting and wantonness is the sabbath of Satan, the devil's holiday, as Bishop Andrewes. Nor speaking words means answering to, call Sabbath a delight, honourable. Man's own words would call it a weariness; it is the spiritual nature given from above which calls it a delight, as in Amos and Malachi. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: Then will you delight yourself in the Lord, and I will cause you to ride upon the high places of the earth, and feed you with the heritage of Jacob your father, for the mouth of the Lord has spoken it. In the Lord means God rewards in kind, as He punishes in kind. As we delight in keeping God's Sabbath, so God will give us delight in Himself, as in Genesis, Job, and Psalm 37. Ride upon high places means I will make thee supreme lord of the land; the phrase is taken from a conqueror riding in his chariot, and occupying the hills and fastnesses of a country, as in Deuteronomy, Micah, and Habakkuk. Judea was a land of hills; the idea thus is, I will restore thee to thine own land, as Calvin. The parallel words, heritage of Jacob, confirm this, as in Genesis. Mouth of the Lord spoken it means a formula to assure men of the fulfilment of any solemn promise which God has made, as in chapter 40. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
