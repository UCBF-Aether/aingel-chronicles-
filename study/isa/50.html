Study Notes — Isaiah

Chapter 50

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Thus says the Lord: Where is the bill of your mother's divorcement, whom I have put away? Or which of my creditors is it to whom I have sold you? Behold, for your iniquities have you sold yourselves, and for your transgressions is your mother put away. This chapter treats how the judgments on Israel were provoked by their crimes, yet they are not finally cast off by God. Where, mothers divorcement means Zion is the mother, the Jews are the children, and God the Husband and Father, as in chapters 54, 62, and Jeremiah. Some think God means by the question to deny that He had given a bill of divorcement to her, as was often done on slight pretexts by a husband, or that He had sold His and her children, as a poor parent sometimes did under pressure of his creditors; that it was they who sold themselves through their own sins. Others explain, Show the bill of your mother's divorcement, whom, produce the creditors to whom ye have been sold; so it will be seen that it was not from any caprice of Mine, but through your own fault, your mother has been put away, and you sold, as in chapter 52. Others best explain, as the antithesis between I and yourselves shows, I have never given your mother a regular bill of divorcement; I have merely put her away for a time, and can, therefore, by right as her husband still take her back on her submission; I have not made you, the children, over to any creditor to satisfy a debt; I therefore still have the right of a father over you, and can take you back on repentance, though as rebellious children you have sold yourselves to sin and its penalty. Bill, whom is rather, the bill with which I have put her away. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: Wherefore, when I came, was there no man? When I called, was there none to answer? Is my hand shortened at all, that it cannot redeem? Or have I no power to deliver? Behold, at my rebuke I dry up the sea, I make the rivers a wilderness; their fish stinks, because there is no water, and dies for thirst. I means Messiah. No man means willing to believe in and obey Me, as in chapter 52. The same Divine Person had come by His prophets in the Old Testament, appealing to them, but in vain, as in Jeremiah, who was about to come under the New Testament. Hand shortened is the Oriental emblem of weakness, as the long stretched-out hand is of power, as in chapter 59; notwithstanding your sins, I can still redeem you from your bondage and dispersion. Dry up sea recalls Exodus; the second exodus shall exceed, while it resembles in wonders, the first, as in chapters 11 and 51. Make rivers wilderness means turn the prosperity of Israel's foes into adversity. Fish stinketh means the very judgment inflicted on their Egyptian enemies at the first exodus. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: I clothe the heavens with blackness, and I make sackcloth their covering. Blackness is another of the judgments on Egypt to be repeated hereafter on the last enemy of God's people, as in Exodus. Sackcloth recalls Revelation. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: The Lord God has given me the tongue of the learned, that I should know how to speak a word in season to him that is weary; he wakens morning by morning, he wakens my ear to hear as the learned. Messiah, as the servant of Jehovah in chapter 42, declares that the office has been assigned to Him of encouraging the weary exiles of Israel by words in season suited to their case, and that, whatever suffering it is to cost Himself, He does not shrink from it, in verses 5 and 6, for that He knows His cause will triumph at last, in verses 7 and 8. Learned means not in mere human learning, but in divinely taught modes of instruction and eloquence, as in chapters 49 and Exodus and Matthew. Speak a word in season recalls Proverbs; literally, to succour by words, namely, in their season of need, the weary dispersed ones of Israel, as in Deuteronomy; also, the spiritual weary, as in chapters 42 and Matthew. Wakeneth morning by morning, etc., means compare daily rising up early, as in Jeremiah and Mark; the image is drawn from a master wakening his pupils early for instruction. Wakeneth ear means prepares me for receiving His divine instructions. As the learned means as one taught by Him; He learned obedience, experimentally, by the things which He suffered, thus gaining that practical learning which adapted Him for speaking a word in season to suffering men, as in Hebrews. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: The Lord God has opened my ear, and I was not rebellious, neither turned away back. Ear recalls chapters 42 and 48; that is, hath made me obediently attentive, but some, hath informed me of my duty, as a servant to his master, as in Psalm 40, with Philippians, and chapters 42, 49, 52, 53, and Matthew and Luke. Not rebellious means but, on the contrary, most willing to do the Father's will in proclaiming and procuring salvation for man, at the cost of His own sufferings, as in Hebrews. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: I gave my back to the smiters, and my cheeks to them that plucked off the hair; I hid not my face from shame and spitting. Smiters means with scourges and with the open hand, as in chapter 52 and Mark; literally fulfilled in Matthew and Luke. To pluck the hair is the highest insult that can be offered an Oriental, as in 2 Samuel and Lamentations. I gave implies the voluntary nature of His sufferings; His example corresponds to His precept in Matthew. Spitting means to spit in another's presence is an insult in the East, much more on one, most of all in the face, as in Job and Matthew and Luke. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: For the Lord God will help me; therefore will I not be confounded; therefore have I set my face like a flint, and I know that I will not be ashamed. This is a sample of His not being discouraged, as in chapters 42 and 49. Set face like flint means set Myself resolutely, not to be daunted from My work of love by shame or suffering, as in Ezekiel. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: He is near that justifies me; who will contend with me? Let us stand together; who is my adversary? Let him come near to me. This recalls chapter 49 verse 4. The believer, by virtue of his oneness with Christ, uses the same language, as in Psalm 138 and Romans. But justify in His case, is God's judicial acceptance and vindication of Him on the ground of His own righteousness, as in Luke, Romans, 1 Timothy, with 1 Peter; in their case, on the ground of His righteousness and meritorious death imputed to them, as in Romans. Stand together means in judgment, to try the issue. Adversary is literally, master of my cause, that is, who has real ground of accusation against me, so that he can demand judgment to be given in his favour, as in Zechariah and Revelation. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: Behold, the Lord God will help me; who is he that will condemn me? Lo, they all will wax old as a garment; the moth will eat them up. This compares deal, or proper, in chapter 52, chapter 53, Psalm 118, and Jeremiah. As a garment recalls chapters 51 and Psalm 102: a leading constituent of wealth in the East is change of raiment, which is always liable to the inroads of the moth, hence the frequency of the image in Scripture. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: Who is among you that fears the Lord, that obeys the voice of his servant, that walks in darkness, and has no light? Let him trust in the name of the Lord, and stay upon his God. Messiah exhorts the godly after His example in chapters 49 and 42, when in circumstances of trial, darkness, as in chapter 47, to trust in the arm of Jehovah alone. Who is, etc., means that is, Whosoever, as in Judges. Obeyeth servant means namely, Messiah; the godly honour the Son, even as they honour the Father, as in John. Darkness recalls Micah: God never had a son who was not sometimes in the dark. For even Christ, His only Son, cried out, My God, My God, why hast Thou forsaken Me? Light is rather, splendour, bright sunshine, for the servant of God is never wholly without light; a godly man's way may be dark, but his end shall be peace and light. A wicked man's way may be bright, but his end shall be utter darkness, as in Psalms. Let him trust in the name of the Lord means as Messiah did in verses 8 and 9. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: Behold, all you that kindle a fire, that compass yourselves about with sparks; walk in the light of your fire, and in the sparks that you have kindled. This will you have of my hand; you will lie down in sorrow. In contrast to the godly in verse 10, the wicked, in times of darkness, instead of trusting in God, trust in themselves, kindle a light for themselves to walk by, as in Ecclesiastes. The image is continued from verse 10, darkness; human devices for salvation, as in Proverbs, are like the spark that goes out in an instant in darkness, as in Job, with Psalm 18. Sparks means not a steady light, but blazing sparks extinguished in a moment. Walk means not a command, but implying that as surely as they would do so, they should lie down in sorrow, as in Jeremiah. In exact proportion to mystic Babylon's previous glorifying of herself shall be her sorrow, as in Matthew and Revelation. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
