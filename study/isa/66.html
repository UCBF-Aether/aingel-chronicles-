Study Notes — Isaiah

Chapter 66

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Thus says the Lord, The heaven is my throne, and the earth is my footstool; where is the house that you build unto me? and where is the place of my rest? This chapter treats the humble comforted, the ungodly condemned, at the Lord's appearing: Jerusalem made a joy on earth. This closing chapter is the summary of Isaiah's prophecies as to the last days, hence the similarity of its sentiments with what went before. Heaven, throne, where is house, ye build means the same sentiment is expressed, as a precautionary proviso for the majesty of God in deigning to own any earthly temple as His, as if He could be circumscribed by space, as in 1 Kings, in inaugurating the temple of stone; next, as to the temple of the Holy Ghost, as in Acts; lastly here, as to the tabernacle of God with men, as in chapters 2 and Ezekiel and Revelation. Where is rather, what is this house that ye are building, etc., what place is this for My rest? — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: For all those things has my hand made, and all those things have been, says the Lord, but to this man will I look, even to him that is poor and of a contrite spirit, and trembles at my word. Have been means namely, made by Me. Or, absolutely, were things made; and therefore belong to Me, the Creator. Look means have regard. Poor means humble, as in chapter 57. Trembleth at word recalls 2 Kings and Ezra. The spiritual temple of the heart, though not superseding the outward place of worship, is God's favourite dwelling, as in John. In the final state in heaven there shall be no temple, but the Lord God Himself, as in Revelation. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: He that kills an ox is as if he slew a man; he that sacrifices a lamb, as if he cut off a dog's neck; he that offers an oblation, as if he offered swine's blood; he that burns incense, as if he blessed an idol. Yes, they have chosen their own ways, and their soul delights in their abominations. God loathes even the sacrifices of the wicked, as in chapters 1 and Proverbs. Is as if means Lowth not so well omits these words: He that killeth an ox, presently after, murders a man, as in Ezekiel. But the omission in the Hebrew of is as if increases the force of the comparison. Human victims were often offered by the heathen. Dog's neck means an abomination according to the Jewish law, as in Deuteronomy; perhaps made so, because dogs were venerated in Egypt. He does not honour this abomination by using the word sacrifice, but uses the degrading term, cut off a dog's neck, as in Exodus. Dogs as unclean are associated with swine, as in Matthew and 2 Peter. Oblation means unbloody: in antithesis to swine's blood, chapter 65 verse 4. Burneth is Hebrew, he who offereth as a memorial oblation, as in Leviticus. They have chosen means opposed to the two first clauses of verse 4: as they have chosen their own ways, etc., so I will choose their delusions. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: I also will choose their delusions, and will bring their fears upon them, because when I called, none did answer; when I spoke, they did not hear, but they did evil before my eyes, and chose that in which I delighted not. Delusions recalls 2 Thessalonians, answering to their own ways, verse 3, as in Proverbs. However, the Hebrew means rather vexations, calamities, which also the parallelism to fears requires; choose their calamities means, choose the calamities which they thought to escape by their own ways. Their fears means the things they feared, to avert which their idolatrous abominations, verse 3, were practised. I called, none, answer recalls chapters 65 and Jeremiah. Did, chose means not only did the evil deed, but did it deliberately as a matter of choice, as in Romans. They chose that in which I delighted not; therefore, I will choose that in which they delight not, the calamities and fears which they were most anxious to avert. Before mine eyes recalls chapter 65. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: Hear the word of the Lord, you that tremble at his word: Your brethren that hated you, that cast you out for my name's sake, said, Let the Lord be glorified, but he will appear to your joy, and they will be ashamed. Word means the same persons as in verse 2, the believing few among the Jews. Cast you out for my name's sake means excommunicate, as if too polluted to worship with them, as in chapter 65. So in Christ's first sojourn on earth, as in Matthew and John. So it shall be again in the last times, when the believing shall be few, as in Luke. Let the Lord be glorified means the mocking challenge of the persecutors, as if their violence towards you was from zeal for God. Let the Lord show Himself glorious, namely, by manifesting Himself in your behalf; as the parallelism to, He shall appear to your joy, requires, as in chapters 5, 28, and 57. So again Christ on the cross, as in Matthew. Appear to your joy means giving you joy instead of your rebuke, as in chapter 25. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: A voice of noise from the city, a voice from the temple, a voice of the Lord that renders recompense to his enemies. God, from Jerusalem and His temple, shall take vengeance on the enemy, as in Ezekiel and Zechariah. The abrupt language of this verse marks the suddenness with which God destroys the hostile Gentile host outside: as verse 5 refers to the confounding of the unbelieving Jews. Voice of noise means that is, the Lord's loud-sounding voice, as in Psalms and 1 Thessalonians. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: Before she travailed, she brought forth; before her pain came, she was delivered of a man child. She means Zion. Before travailed, brought forth means the accession of numbers, and of prosperity to her, shall be sudden beyond all expectation and unattended with painful effort, as in chapter 54. Contrast with this case of the future Jewish church the travail-pains of the Christian church in bringing forth a man child, as in Revelation. A man child's birth is in the East a matter of special joy, while that of a female is not so; therefore, it here means the manly sons of the restored Jewish church, the singular being used collectively for the plural: or the many sons being regarded as one under Messiah, who shall then be manifested as their one representative Head. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: Who has heard such a thing? who has seen such things? Shall the earth be made to bring forth in one day? or shall a nation be born at once? for as soon as Zion travailed, she brought forth her children. Earth is rather, to suit the parallelism, is a country, put for the people in it, brought forth in one day? In English Version it means, the earth brings forth its productions gradually, not in one day, as in Mark. At once means in this case, contrary to the usual growth of the nations by degrees, Israel starts into maturity at once. For is rather, is a nation born at once, that Zion has, so soon as she travailed, brought forth? — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: Shall I bring to the birth, and not cause to bring forth? says the Lord; shall I cause to bring forth, and shut the womb? says your God. Cause to bring forth, and shut is rather, Shall I who beget, restrain the birth?, as in chapters 37 and Hosea; that is, Shall I who have begun, not finish My work of restoring Israel?, as in 1 Samuel, Romans, and Philemon. Shut recalls Revelation. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: Rejoice with Jerusalem, and be glad with her, all you that love her; rejoice for joy with her, all you that mourn for her. Mourn for her recalls Psalms. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: That you may suck, and be satisfied with the breasts of her consolations; that you may milk out, and be delighted with the abundance of her glory. Suck recalls chapters 60, 61, and 49. Abundance is Hebrew, the ray-like flow of her opulence, that is, with the milk spouting out from her full breasts, answering to the parallel, breast of her consolations, in ray-like streams. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: For thus says the Lord, Behold, I will extend peace to her like a river, and the glory of the Gentiles like a flowing stream; then will you suck, you will be borne upon her sides, and be dandled upon her knees. Extend means I will turn peace, prosperity, upon her, like a river turned in its course. Or, I will spread peace over her as an overflowing river, as in chapter 48. Flowing stream means as the Nile by its overflow fertilises the whole of Egypt. Borne upon sides recalls chapter 60. Her, her means if ye refers to the Jews, translate, ye shall be borne upon their sides, their knees, namely, those of the Gentiles, as in chapter 49; and as suck, chapter 60, refers to the Jews sucking the Gentile wealth. However, English Version gives a good sense: The Jews, and all who love Jehovah, verse 10, shall suck, and be borne by her as a mother. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: As one whom his mother comforts, so will I comfort you, and you will be comforted in Jerusalem. Mother recalls chapter 49. Comforteth recalls chapter 40. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: When you see this, your heart will rejoice, and your bones will flourish like an herb, and the hand of the Lord will be known toward his servants, and his indignation toward his enemies. Bones means which once were dried up by the fire of God's wrath, as in Lamentations, shall live again, as in Proverbs and Ezekiel. Flourish, herb recalls Romans. Known toward means manifested in behalf of. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: For, behold, the Lord will come with fire, and with his chariots like a whirlwind, to render his anger with fury, and his rebuke with flames of fire. This recalls chapters 9 and Psalm 50 and Habakkuk and 2 Thessalonians and 2 Peter. Chariots, whirlwind recalls Jeremiah. Render means as the Hebrew elsewhere, as in Job and Psalm 78, means to allay or stay wrath. Some translate it so here: He stays His anger with nothing but fury, etc.; nothing short of pouring out all His fiery fury will satisfy His wrath. Fury means burning heat, to which the parallel, flames of fire, answers. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: For by fire and by his sword will the Lord plead with all flesh, and the slain of the Lord will be many. Rather, With fire will Jehovah judge, and with His sword, He will judge, all flesh. The parallelism and collocation of the Hebrew words favour this, as in chapter 65. All flesh means that is, all who are the objects of His wrath. The godly shall be hidden by the Lord in a place of safety away from the scene of judgment, as in chapters 26 and Psalm 31 and 1 Thessalonians. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: They that sanctify themselves, and purify themselves in the gardens behind one tree in the midst, eating swine's flesh, and the abomination, and the mouse, will be consumed together, says the Lord. Gardens means Hebrew and the Septuagint rather require, for, entering into, gardens, namely, to sacrifice there. Behind one tree is rather, following one, that is, some idol or other, which, from contempt, he does not name. Some think the Hebrew for one, Ahhadh, to be the name of the god; called Adad, meaning One, in Syria, as in Acts. The idol's power was represented by inclined rays, as of the sun shining on the earth. Some translate, following one, namely, Hierophant, priest, who led the rest in performing the sacred rites. In midst means namely, of the garden, as in chapter 65. Mouse means legally unclean, as in Leviticus, because it was an idol to the heathen, as in chapter 37 and 1 Samuel. Translate, the field mouse, or dormouse. The Pharisees with their self-righteous purifications, and all mere formalists, are included in the same condemnation, described in language taken from the idolatries prevalent in Isaiah's times. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

18 COMMENTARY: For I know their works and their thoughts; it will come, that I will gather all nations and tongues, and they will come, and see my glory. Their works and thoughts, etc.; namely, will punish. It shall come means the time is come that I will, etc. Gather nations means against Jerusalem, where the ungodly Jews shall perish; and then the Lord at last shall fight for Jerusalem against those nations: and the survivors, verse 19, shall see God's glory, as in Zechariah. Tongues means which have been many owing to sin, being confounded at Babel, but which shall again be one in Christ, as in Daniel, Zephaniah, and Revelation. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

19 COMMENTARY: I will set a sign among them, and I will send those that escape of them unto the nations, to Tarshish, Pul, and Lud, that draw the bow, to Tubal, and Javan, to the isles afar off, that have not heard my fame, neither have seen my glory, and they will declare my glory among the Gentiles. Sign means a banner on a high place, to indicate the place of meeting for the dispersed Jewish exiles, preparatory to their return to their land, as in chapters 5, 11, and 62. Those that escape of them means the Gentile survivors spared by God, as in verse 18 and Zechariah. Chapters 2 and Micah and Zechariah represent it, not that the Jews go as missionaries to the Gentiles, but that the Gentiles come up to Jerusalem to learn the Lord's ways there. Tarshish means Tartessus in Spain, in the west. Pul means east and north of Africa: probably the same as Philoe, an island in the Nile, called by the Egyptians Pilak, that is, the border country, being between Egypt and Ethiopia. Lud means the Libyans of Africa, as in Genesis, Ludim being son of Mizraim, Egypt: an Ethiopian people famous as bowmen, as in Jeremiah: employed as mercenaries by Tyre and Egypt, as in Ezekiel. Tubal means Tibarenians, in Asia Minor, south of the Caucasus, between the Black Sea and Araxes. Or, the Iberians, as Josephus. Italy, as Jerome. Javan means the Greeks; called Ionians, including all the descendants of Javan, both in Greece and in Asia Minor, as in Genesis. My glory, Gentiles recalls Malachi. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

20 COMMENTARY: They will bring all your brethren for an offering unto the Lord out of all nations upon horses, and in chariots, and in litters, and upon mules, and upon swift beasts, to my holy mountain Jerusalem, says the Lord, as the children of Israel bring an offering in a clean vessel into the house of the Lord. They means the Gentiles, verse 19. Bring your brethren means the Jews, back to the Holy Land, as in chapter 49. It cannot mean the mere entrance of the Jews into the Christian church; for such an entrance would be by faith, not upon horses, litters, and mules. Offering is metaphorical, as in Romans. Horses means not much used by the Jews. The Gentiles are here represented as using their modes of conveyance to bring the Jews to Jerusalem. Chariots means as these are not found in Oriental caravans, translate, vehicles, namely, borne, not drawn on wheels. Litters means covered sedans for the rich. Upon swift beasts means dromedaries: from Hebrew root, to dance, from their bounding motion, often accelerated by music. Panniers were thrown across the dromedaries' back for poorer women. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

21 COMMENTARY: I will also take of them for priests and for Levites, says the Lord. Of them means the Gentiles. Priests, Levites means for spiritual worship: enjoying the direct access to God which was formerly enjoyed by the ministers of the temple alone, as in 1 Peter and Revelation. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

22 COMMENTARY: For as the new heavens and the new earth, which I will make, will remain before me, says the Lord, so will your seed and your name remain. This recalls chapters 65 and 2 Peter and Revelation. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

23 COMMENTARY: It will come to pass, that from one new moon to another, and from one sabbath to another, will all flesh come to worship before me, says the Lord. Literally, As often as the new moon, shall be, in its own new moon, that is, every month, as in Zechariah. Sabbath means which is therefore perpetually obligatory on earth. All flesh recalls Psalms. Before me means at Jerusalem, as in Jeremiah. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

24 COMMENTARY: They will go forth, and look upon the carcasses of the men that have transgressed against me, for their worm will not die, neither will their fire be quenched, and they will be an abhorring unto all flesh. Go forth, and look means as the Israelites looked at the carcasses of the Egyptians destroyed at the Red Sea, as in Exodus, compare chapters 26 and Psalms and Malachi. Carcasses, etc. recalls verse 16, those slain by the Lord in the last great battle near Jerusalem, as in Zechariah; type of the final destruction of all sinners. Worm, not die recalls Mark. Image of hell, from bodies left unburied in the valley of Hinnom, whence comes Gehenna, or hell, south of Jerusalem, where a perpetual fire was kept to consume the refuse thrown there, as in chapter 30. It shall not be inconsistent with true love for the godly to look with satisfaction on God's vengeance on the wicked, as in Revelation. May God bless this Commentary, and especially its solemn close, to His glory, and to the edification of the writer and the readers of it, for Jesus' sake! — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
