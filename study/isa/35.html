Study Notes — Isaiah

Chapter 35

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: The wilderness and the solitary place will be glad for them, and the desert will rejoice, and blossom as the rose. This chapter continues the prophecy of chapter 34: the solitary place is literally a dry place without springs of water, and a moral wilderness is meant. For them means on account of the punishment inflicted on the enemy in the preceding prophecy, and the blessings here are probably included among the causes for joy. The rose is rather the meadow-saffron, an autumnal flower with bulbous roots. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: It will blossom abundantly, and rejoice even with joy and singing; the glory of Lebanon will be given unto it, the excellency of Carmel and Sharon, and they will see the glory of the Lord, and the excellency of our God. The glory of Lebanon is its ornament, namely its cedars; the excellency of Carmel is its beauty; Sharon is famed for its fertility. They will see the glory of the Lord and the excellency of God: while the wilderness which had neither glory nor excellency will have both given to it, the Lord will have all the glory and excellency ascribed to Him, not to the transformed wilderness. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: Strengthen the weak hands, and confirm the feeble knees. The Hebrew for strengthen refers to the strength residing in the hand for grasping and holding a thing manfully, and confirm to the firmness with which one keeps his ground so as not to be dislodged by another. The charge is to encourage the Jews, now desponding, by the assurance of the blessings promised. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: Say to them that are of a fearful heart: Be strong, fear not; behold, your God will come with vengeance, even God with a recompense; he will come and save you. Fearful is hasty, that is, with a heart fluttered with agitation, and with is more forcible in Hebrew: God will come, vengeance! even God, a recompense! The sense is the same — God will come and save. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: Then the eyes of the blind will be opened, and the ears of the deaf will be unstopped. This language is figuratively descriptive of the joy felt at deliverance from Assyria and Babylon, and literally true of the antitypical times of Messiah and His miracles, as in Matthew 11 and Luke 7. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: Then the lame man will leap as a hart, and the tongue of the dumb sing, for in the wilderness waters will break out, and streams in the desert. This language is figuratively descriptive of the joy felt at deliverance from Assyria and Babylon, and literally true of the antitypical times of Messiah and His miracles; leap is literally fulfilled in Acts 3 and 14, sing is joyful thanksgiving, and in the wilderness waters recalls chapter 41. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: The parched ground will become a pool, and the thirsty land springs of water; in the habitation of dragons, where each lay, will be grass with reeds and rushes. The parched ground is rather the mirage, Hebrew Sharab, the sun's heat, which will become a real lake: the sun's rays refracted on the glowing sands at midday give the appearance of a lake of water and often deceive the thirsty traveller. The dragons are rather jackals, each namely jackal, and grass is rather a dwelling or receptacle, answering to the previous habitation, for reeds, which only grow where there is water; where once there was no water, water will abound. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: A highway will be there, and a way, and it will be called The way of holiness; the unclean will not pass over it, but it will be for those, and the wayfaring men, though fools, will not err therein. The highway is such a causeway, a raised way, as was used for the march of armies, with valleys filled up and hills and obstructions removed. The way of holiness is a Hebraism for the holy way, or the way of the Holy One, and the words that follow show it is the way leading the redeemed back to Jerusalem, both the literal and the heavenly, with Christ at His coming again as Leader on the way, for which reason it is called the way of the Lord. It will be for those is rather, He the Holy One will be with them, walking in the way, and though fools is rather, and even fools, that is, the simple will not go astray, because He will be with them. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: No lion will be there, nor any ravenous beast will go up thereon, it will not be found there, but the redeemed will walk there. No lion such as might be feared on the way through the wilderness abounding in wild beasts back to Judea: every danger will be warded off the returning people, as in chapters 11 and 34 and Hosea, and spiritually as in Proverbs 3. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: The ransomed of the Lord will return, and come to Zion with songs and everlasting joy upon their heads, and they will obtain joy and gladness, and sorrow and sighing will flee away. This language literally applies to the return from Babylon, and figuratively and more fully to the completed redemption of both literal and spiritual Israel; joy upon their heads means joy manifested in their countenances, with some fancying an allusion to pouring oil upon the head or wearing chaplets in times of public festivity. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
