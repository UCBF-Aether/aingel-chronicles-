Study Notes — Isaiah

Chapter 10

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Woe to those who decree unrighteous decrees and write grievousness which they have prescribed — the fourth strophe's charge. The decreers are unrighteous judges, and the writers are magistrates who caused unjust decisions, literally injustice, to be recorded by their scribes. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: Their decrees turn aside the needy from judgment and take away the right from the poor of God's people, making widows their prey and robbing the fatherless. The effect of their conduct is to pervert the cause of the needy and to make plunder of rightful claims. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: What will they do in the day of visitation, when desolation comes from far — from Assyria — and to whom will they flee for help, and where will they leave their glory? The visitation is God's wrath, and their wealth, which they would deposit for safekeeping, will have no safe keeper. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: Without God to flee to, they will bow down under the prisoners and fall under the slain, for all this his anger is not turned away but his hand is stretched out still. Bereft of strength they will fall, fettered among prisoners going into captivity or overwhelmed under heaps of slain on the battlefield. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: O Assyrian, the rod of God's anger, in whose hand is the staff of his indignation: the Assyrian is God's instrument in punishing, as in Jeremiah and the Psalms. God has put into Assyrian hands the execution of his indignation against his people; this section, written when Assyria proposed to destroy Judah as it had Samaria, is the first part of Isaiah's prophecies under Hezekiah. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: God will send the Assyrian against a hypocritical, polluted nation, against the people of his wrath — Judah, against whom Sennacherib was forming designs — to take the spoil and prey and tread them down like mire in the streets. Kings' hearts are in the hand of the Lord, and the charge is given as to an instrument. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: Howbeit the Assyrian means not so, neither does his heart think so; but it is in his heart to destroy and cut off nations not a few. He thinks only of his own schemes while God overrules them to his purposes, and sinners' plans are no less culpable though they unconsciously fulfil God's designs — the sinner's motive, not the result, will be the test in judgment. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: For the Assyrian says: Are not my princes altogether kings? Eastern satraps and governors of provinces often had the title and diadem of kings, hence the title King of kings for him who is over them. This begins the vauntings of the Assyrians, illustrated by their own self-laudatory inscriptions. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: Is not Calno as Carchemish? Is not Hamath as Arpad? Is not Samaria as Damascus? Was any one of these cities able to withstand him? Not one, as Rabshakeh later vaunts. Calno was Nimrod's capital on the Tigris, Carchemish on the Euphrates, Hamath in Syria taken about 753 B.C., and Samaria now overthrown, with Damascus facing the same fate. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: As my hand has found the kingdoms of the idols, whose graven images excelled those of Jerusalem and Samaria, the Assyrian boasts he has overcome them and they could not resist him. He regards Jerusalem as idolatrous, and Jehovah as a mere local god of Judea, inferior in power to some national gods. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: Shall I not, as I have done to Samaria and her idols, so do to Jerusalem and her idols? The agitation of boasting makes him accumulate sentences, doubling the protasis as he claims the same conquest over Jerusalem that he achieved over Samaria. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: When the Lord has performed his whole work upon Mount Zion and Jerusalem — his entire plan regarding the punishment of the Jews — he will punish the fruit of the stout heart of the king of Assyria and the glory of his high looks. Zion here is the royal residence, the court and nobles, distinguished from Jerusalem the people in general, and the fruit punished is the pride and haughtiness his success produced. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: For the Assyrian says: By the strength of my hand I have done it, and by my wisdom, for I am prudent; I have removed the bounds of the people, robbed their treasures, and put down the inhabitants like a valiant man. He ascribes success to his own prudence, not God's providence, and setting aside boundaries Jehovah himself appointed is a criminal act. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: My hand has found as a nest the riches of the people, and as one gathers eggs left by the parent bird, so I have gathered all the earth, and there was none that moved the wing, or opened the mouth, or peeped. The nest image expresses the ease of his conquests: no resistance was offered, by deed or even by a chirping word. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: Shall the axe boast itself against him that hews with it, or the saw magnify itself against him that shakes it, as if the rod should shake itself against them that lift it up, or the staff should lift up itself as if it were no wood? The instrument must not boast against him who uses it: the Assyrian, though carrying out his own plans, was unconsciously carrying out God's purposes. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: Therefore the Lord, the Lord of hosts, will send among his fat ones leanness, and under his glory he will kindle a burning like the burning of a fire. The fat ones are the robust, choice soldiers of Assyria, and leanness is destruction, fulfilled when the host was struck down, while the burning under Assyria's glory consumes like fire in dry materials. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: The light of Israel will be for a fire, and his Holy One for a flame, and it will burn and devour his thorns and briers in one day. Jehovah, a light to Israel, will be the fire that ignites the Assyrians, like dry fuel a ready prey to flame. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

18 COMMENTARY: The glory of his forest and of his fruitful field — literally Carmel, a rich mountain, figurative for Sennacherib's mighty army — will be consumed together, both soul and body, as when a sick man wastes away. Common soldiers, princes, and officers alike will be consumed, utterly and entirely. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

19 COMMENTARY: The rest of the trees of his forest will be few, that a child may write them. Those who survive the destruction of the once-dense army will be so few a child could count them, in contrast to the remnant of Israel that follows. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

20 COMMENTARY: In that day the remnant of Israel and such as are escaped of the house of Jacob will no more stay upon him that smote them, but will stay upon the Lord, the Holy One of Israel, in truth. Those left after Sennacherib's invasion will return from dependence on external idolatrous nations, Assyria and Egypt, to the God of the theocracy, fulfilled in part under Hezekiah and fully in the portion of Jews left after Antichrist's overthrow who return to the Lord. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

21 COMMENTARY: The remnant will return, even the remnant of Jacob, to the mighty God — the God who has evinced such might in destroying Israel's enemies, the mighty God of chapter 9. Since the Assyrians in Sennacherib's reign did not carry Judah captive, this returning remnant cannot mainly refer to that time alone. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

22 COMMENTARY: For though your people Israel be as the sand of the sea, yet a remnant of them will return, for the consumption decreed will overflow with righteousness. As Paul quotes it, though Israel is numerous as sand, a remnant only will return, because the fully completed destruction is decided on and overflows with just punishment. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

23 COMMENTARY: For the Lord God of hosts will make a consumption, even determined, in the midst of all the land. The determined destruction is decreed in Zion, the central point of the earth as to Jehovah's presence, and extends over the land, or in the Septuagint's reading, the whole habitable world. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

24 COMMENTARY: Therefore thus says the Lord God of hosts: O my people that dwell in Zion, be not afraid of the Assyrian, who smites you with a rod and lifts up his staff against you after the manner of Egypt. God returns to his main proposition — Assyria's ultimate punishment though employed as his rod — with tenderness toward his elect nation, implying that as Israel was delivered from Egypt so now it will be from Sennacherib. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

25 COMMENTARY: For yet a very little while, and the indignation will cease, and my anger in their destruction. The punishments of God against Israel will be consummated and ended, and then his anger will turn to the Assyrians' destruction. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

26 COMMENTARY: The Lord of hosts will stir up a scourge for him according to the slaughter of Midian at the rock of Oreb, and as his rod was upon the sea, so will he lift it up after the manner of Egypt. His rod on the Assyrian stands in bold contrast to the Assyrian used as a rod to strike others, and the stroke recalls both Gideon's victory and the rod lifted against Egypt at the Red Sea. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

27 COMMENTARY: In that day his burden will depart from off your shoulder and his yoke from off your neck, and the yoke will be destroyed because of the anointing. Judah was still tributary to Assyria, but the burden will be lifted because of Messiah, just as in chapter 9 the breaking of the yoke is attributed to the child born. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

28 COMMENTARY: He is come to Aiath, he is passed to Migron, at Michmash he has laid up his carriages. This begins the vivid picture of Sennacherib's gradual march toward Jerusalem: Aiath is Ai in the north of Benjamin, Michmash nine miles northeast of Jerusalem, where he left his heavier baggage to move lightly toward the siege. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

29 COMMENTARY: They are gone over the passage and have taken up their lodging at Geba; Ramah is afraid, Gibeah of Saul is fled. The passage is the jaws of the defile at Michmash, which might easily have been guarded, and the towns on the line of march — Ramah seven miles from Jerusalem, Gibeah of Saul in Benjamin — panic in turn. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

30 COMMENTARY: Lift up your voice, O daughter of Gallim; cause it to be heard unto Laish, O poor Anathoth. Gallim and her sons are called to cry aloud in consternation, Laish is a town near Jerusalem, and Anathoth, three miles from Jerusalem in Benjamin and Jeremiah's birthplace, is called poor in pity for the impending calamity. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

31 COMMENTARY: Madmenah is removed, and the inhabitants of Gebim gather themselves to flee. Madmenah is a village near Jerusalem, not the city in Simeon, and Gebim's people put their goods in a place of safety as they flee from fear. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

32 COMMENTARY: As yet will he remain at Nob that day, and shake his hand against the mount of the daughter of Zion, the hill of Jerusalem. Nob, northeast of Jerusalem on Olivet and a town of priests, is his one day's halt, and shaking his hand in menace implies he now stands within sight of Jerusalem. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

33 COMMENTARY: Behold, the Lord, the Lord of hosts, will lop the bough with terror, and the high ones of stature will be hewn down, and the haughty will be humbled. The bough is the beautiful branch of the tree, and the high ones are the upright stem, both cut down in the sudden arrest of Assyria at its height. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

34 COMMENTARY: He will cut down the thickets of the forest with iron, and Lebanon will fall by a mighty one. Lebanon and its forest are the Assyrian army, and the iron axe that fells it is the stroke that destroyed the 185,000 Assyrians, wielded by the Mighty One, Jehovah. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
