Study Notes — Isaiah

Chapter 12

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: In that day the restored people will say: O Lord, I will praise you, for though you were angry with me, your anger is turned away and you comforted me. This thanksgiving hymn of the restored and converted Jews answers to Miriam's ode after the Red Sea deliverance, which chapter 11's second exodus recalls. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: Behold, God is my salvation; I will trust and not be afraid, for the Lord Jehovah is my strength and my song, and he has become my salvation. The repeated name, Jah Jehovah, denotes emphasis and the unchangeableness of God's character, and the words are drawn from Exodus 15 and Psalm 118, with salvation peculiarly associated with the feast of tabernacles and its Hosanna cry. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: Therefore with joy you will draw water out of the wells of salvation, an expressive image in a hot country. The wells are not mere streams that may run dry but ever-flowing fountains of living water, recalling the water poured from Siloam's fountain on the last day of tabernacles and Jesus' words on that feast day, with a latter outpouring of the Spirit like the former at Pentecost. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: In that day you will say: Praise the Lord, call upon his name, declare his doings among the people, and make mention that his name is exalted. To make mention is in Hebrew to cause his name to be remembered, proclaiming his deeds among the peoples so that his exalted name is kept in memory. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: Sing unto the Lord, for he has done excellent things, and this is known in all the earth, alluding to Miriam's song in Exodus 15. The hymn's praise is to be sung because God's excellent deeds deserve worldwide renown. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: Cry out and shout, you inhabitant of Zion — in Hebrew the inhabitress, daughter of Zion, that is, Zion and its people — for great is the Holy One of Israel in the midst of you. The Holy One dwells in Jerusalem literally, as Jeremiah, Ezekiel, and Zephaniah promise, and his presence in their midst is the ground of their shouting. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
