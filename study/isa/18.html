Study Notes — Isaiah

Chapter 18

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Woe — rather, Ho — to the land shadowing with wings, which is beyond the rivers of Ethiopia. The Hebrew does not express a threat but an appeal calling attention, as in chapter 55: Isaiah speaks not against but to the Ethiopians, calling them to hear his announcement of their enemies' destruction. The land is rather the land of the winged bark, with wing-like sails answering to the vessels of bulrushes in verse 2, and beyond the rivers is Meroe, the island between the Nile and Astaboras famed for commerce and perhaps seat of the Ethiopian government, representing the whole empire. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: The land sends ambassadors by the sea, even in vessels of bulrushes upon the waters, saying: Go, swift messengers, to a nation scattered and peeled, to a people terrible from their beginning hitherto, a nation meted out and trodden under foot, whose land the rivers have spoiled. The ambassadors are messengers sent to Jerusalem while negotiations passed between Tirhakah and Hezekiah against Sennacherib's expected attack; the sea is the Nile, and the vessels are light canoes of papyrus daubed with pitch, like Moses' ark. The nation described is strong and energetic, tall and comely, famed for warlike prowess and victorious, treading enemies under foot, whose land the Nile's many streams wash and divide. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: All you inhabitants of the world and dwellers on the earth, see when he lifts up an ensign on the mountains, and when he blows a trumpet, hear. This is a call to the whole earth to be witnesses of what Jehovah is about to do: he will lift up an ensign calling the Assyrian hosts together on the mountains round Jerusalem to their own destruction, declaring the overthrow whose presence chapter 17 announced. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: For so the Lord said to Isaiah: I will take my rest, and I will consider in my dwelling place like a clear heat upon herbs, and like a cloud of dew in the heat of harvest. God will calmly look on and not interpose while all seems to promise success to the enemy, as the sun's heat and night dews ripen their harvest — but before it reaches maturity he will destroy it. God's silence is mistaken by the ungodly for consent, and his delay for forgetfulness, as before the vengeance of the last day. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: For before the harvest, when the bud is perfect and the sour grape is ripening in the flower, he will both cut off the sprigs with pruning hooks and take away and cut down the branches. When the enemy's plans are on the verge of completion and the flower becomes the ripening grape, God will not only disconcert their present plans but prevent them forming any future ones, cutting off the shoots with their grapes. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: They will be left together unto the fowls of the mountains and to the beasts of the earth, and the fowls will summer upon them and all the beasts of the earth will winter upon them. The Assyrian soldiers and leaders will be prey to birds and beasts the whole year through, winter and summer, so numerous will be their carcasses. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: In that time a present will be brought to the Lord of hosts of a people scattered and peeled, and from a people terrible from their beginning hitherto, a nation meted out and trodden under foot, whose land the rivers have spoiled, to the place of the name of the Lord of hosts, Mount Zion. The Ethiopians, wonder-struck at Jehovah's interposition for his people, will send gifts to Jerusalem in his honour, to the place where he peculiarly manifests his glory; or, as the English version reads, the present will be the Ethiopian people themselves converted to God, with full accomplishment still future. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
