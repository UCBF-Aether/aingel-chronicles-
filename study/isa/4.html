Study Notes — Isaiah

Chapter 4

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: In that day seven women will take hold of one man, asking only to be called by his name to take away their reproach, while eating their own bread and wearing their own apparel. So many men will have been slain that the women, contrary to their natural bashfulness, will forego the legal privileges of wives to escape the reproach of being unwedded and childless. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: In contrast to those on whom vengeance falls, the Branch of the Lord will be beautiful and glorious to the escaped of Israel. The Branch is Messiah, a fruit-bearing sprout from David's family when it was low and reduced, springing as from a decayed tree, and of the earth in his birth and death while also of the Lord. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: He who is left in Zion and remains in Jerusalem will be called holy, being written among the living in the book of life. Primarily this is the register of Israel's families; antitypically it is the book of life, and the reference is specially to the elect of Israel. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: When the Lord has washed away the moral filth of the daughters of Zion and purged the blood of Jerusalem, it will be by the spirit of judgment and of burning. Judgments purify by destroying the ungodly and refining the godly, and the same Spirit who sanctifies believers by affliction's fire dooms unbelievers to perdition's fire. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: The Lord will create upon every dwelling place of Mount Zion and upon her assemblies a cloud and smoke by day and the shining of a flaming fire by night, and upon all the glory a defence. As the pillar of cloud stood over the tabernacle as a symbol of God's favour, so his presence will ensure safety, as it did against the Egyptians at the Red Sea. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: There will be a tabernacle for a shadow from the heat by day and a place of refuge and covert from storm and rain. Christ's body is the tabernacle in which the Word dwelt, a shadow from the heat and refuge from the storm of divine wrath, and the true mercy seat beneath whom man is covered. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
