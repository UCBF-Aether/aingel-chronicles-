Study Notes — Isaiah

Chapter 49

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Listen, O isles, unto me, and hearken, you people, from far; The Lord has called me from the womb; from the bowels of my mother has he made mention of my name. This chapter is similar to chapter 42 verses 1 to 7, in verses 1 to 9. Messiah, as the ideal Israel in verse 3, states the object of His mission, His want of success for a time, yet His certainty of ultimate success. O isles means Messiah is here regarded as having been rejected by the Jews in verses 4 and 5, and as now turning to the Gentiles, to whom the Father hath given Him for a light and salvation. Isles mean all regions beyond sea. From the womb recalls chapters 44 and Luke and John. From bowels, mention of my name means His name Jesus, that is, God-Saviour, was designated by God before His birth. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: He has made my mouth like a sharp sword; in the shadow of his hand has he hid me, and made me a polished shaft; in his quiver has he hid me. Sword recalls chapters 11 and Revelation; the double office of the Word of God, saving and damnatory, is implied. Shaft recalls Psalm 45; polished, that is, free from all rust, implies His unsullied purity. In quiver hid me means like a sword in its scabbard, or a shaft in the quiver, Messiah, before His appearing, was hid with God, ready to be drawn forth at the moment God saw fit; also always protected by God, as the arrow by the quiver, as in chapter 51. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: He said unto me: You are my servant, O Israel, in whom I will be glorified. Israel is applied to Messiah, according to the true import of the name, the Prince who had power with God in wrestling in behalf of man, and who prevails, as in Genesis and Hosea. He is also the ideal Israel, the representative man of the nation, as in Matthew with Hosea. In whom glorified recalls John. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: Then I said: I have laboured in vain, I have spent my strength for nought, and in vain; yet surely my judgment is with the Lord, and my work with my God. I means Messiah. In vain means comparatively in the case of the greater number of His own countrymen: He came unto His own, and His own received Him not, as in chapters 53 and Luke and John. Only a hundred and twenty disciples met after His personal ministry was ended. Yet my judgment with the Lord means ultimately, God will do justice to My cause, and reward, margin for work, as in chapters 40 and 62, My labours and sufferings. He was never discouraged, as in chapters 42 and 50. He calmly, in spite of seeming ill success for the time, left the result with God, confident of final triumph, as in chapter 53 and 1 Peter. So the ministers of Christ. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: And now, says the Lord that formed me from the womb to be his servant, to bring Jacob again to him, Though Israel be not gathered, yet will I be glorious in the eyes of the Lord, and my God will be my strength. The reason why He was confident that His work would be accepted and rewarded, namely, because He is glorious in the eyes of Jehovah, etc. To bring Jacob again to him recalls Matthew and Acts. Though Israel be not gathered is a metaphor from a scattered flock which the shepherd gathers together again, or a hen and her chickens, as in Matthew. Instead of the text not, the Keri has the similar Hebrew word, to Him, which the parallelism favours: And that Israel may be gathered to Him. Yet is rather, parenthetically, For I am glorious, etc., and My God is My strength. Then in verse 6, resuming the words from the beginning of verse 5, He saith, I repeat, etc. Some explain, Notwithstanding the incredulity of the Jews, Messiah shall be glorified in the conversion of the Gentiles, reading as in the English version; but if the Keri be read, Israel shall at one time or other be gathered, notwithstanding their incredulity during Messiah's sojourn on earth. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: He said: It is a light thing that you should be my servant to raise up the tribes of Jacob, and to restore the preserved of Israel; I will also give you for a light to the Gentiles, that you may be my salvation unto the end of the earth. It is a light thing means It is too little that Thou shouldest, that is, It is not enough honour to Thee to raise up Jacob and Israel, but I design for Thee more, namely, that Thou shouldest be the means of enlightening the Gentiles, as in chapters 42 and 60. The preserved means namely, those remaining after the judgments of God on the nation, the elect remnant of Israel reserved for mercy. Some, with a slight but needless change of the Hebrew, translate for tribes and preserved, the scions, the branches. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: Thus says the Lord, the Redeemer of Israel, and his Holy One, to him whom man despises, to him whom the nation abhors, to a servant of rulers: Kings will see and arise, princes also will worship, because of the Lord that is faithful, and the Holy One of Israel, and he will choose you. Whom man despiseth is Hebrew, the despised of soul, that is, by every soul, by all men, as in chapters 52, 53, and 50 and Psalm 22; some translate, whose person is despised. Abhorreth is literally, who is an abomination to the nation, as in Luke. The Jews contemptuously call Him always, the crucified. Some prefer, on account of Goi, the Hebrew term for nation being usually applied to the Gentiles, and that for people to the Jews, so the Greek terms respectively also, Laos and Ethne, to take nation here collectively for the Gentile world, which also spurned Him, as in Psalm 2 and Acts. Servant of rulers means, as in Matthew, He who would not exert His power against the rulers. Shall see means namely the fulfilment of God's promises in verses 3 and 6, when He shall be a light to the Gentiles. Arise means to reverence Thee, as in Psalm 72 and Philippians. Princes also is rather, for the parallelism, supply the ellipsis, thus, Princes shall see and shall worship. Faithful means namely, to His promises. Choose thee means as God's elect, as in chapter 42. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: Thus says the Lord: In an acceptable time have I heard you, and in a day of salvation have I helped you, and I will preserve you, and give you for a covenant of the people, to establish the earth, to cause to inherit the desolate heritages. Messiah is represented as having asked for the grace of God in behalf of sinners; this verse contains God the Father's favourable answer. An acceptable time means In a time of grace; a limited time, as in chapter 61 and 2 Corinthians. The time judged by God to be the best fitted for effecting the purposes of His grace by Messiah. Heard thee recalls Psalm 2 and Hebrews. Day of salvation means when the fullness of time shall have come; the day of salvation is to-day, as in Hebrews. Helped means given Thee the help needed to enable Thee, as man, to accomplish man's salvation. Preserve means from the assaults and efforts of Satan, to divert Thee from Thy voluntary death to save man. Covenant of the people recalls chapter 42; the people, in the singular, is always applied exclusively to Israel. Establish the earth is rather, to restore the land, namely, Canaan to Israel. Spiritually, the restoration of the church, the spiritual Israel, to the heavenly land forfeited by man's sin is also included. Cause to inherit desolate heritages is an image from the desolate state of Judea during the Babylonish captivity. Spiritually, the Gentile world, a moral waste, shall become a garden of the Lord. Literally, Judea lying desolate for ages shall be possessed again by Israel, as in chapter 61. Jesus, the antitype of, and bearing the same name as Joshua, shall, like him, divide the land among its true heirs, as in chapters 54 and 61. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: That you may say to the prisoners: Go forth; to them that are in darkness: Show yourselves. They will feed in the ways, and their pastures will be in all high places. This recalls chapters 42 and Zechariah. Prisoners are the Jews bound in legal bondage. Them in darkness are the Gentiles having no light as to the one true God. Show yourselves means not only see but be seen; come forth from the darkness of your prison into the light of the Sun of righteousness. In the ways, etc., means in a desert there are no ways, nor high places, with pastures; thus the sense is: They shall have their pastures, not in deserts, but in cultivated and inhabited places. Laying aside the figure, the churches of Christ at the first shall be gathered, not in obscure and unknown regions, but in the most populous parts of the Roman empire, Antioch, Alexandria, Rome, etc. Another sense probably is the right one: Israel, on its way back to the Holy Land, shall not have to turn aside to devious paths in search of necessaries, but shall find them in all places wherever their route lies, so Rosenmuller. God will supply them as if He should make the grass grow in the trodden ways and on the barren high places. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: They will not hunger nor thirst, neither will the heat nor sun smite them, for he that has mercy on them will lead them, even by the springs of water will he guide them. Messiah will abundantly satisfy all the wants, both of literal Israel on their way to Palestine, and of the spiritual on their way to heaven, as their Shepherd, as in chapters 65 and Matthew, also in heaven, as in Revelation. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: I will make all my mountains a way, and my highways will be exalted. My means all things are God's. Mountains a way means I will remove all obstructions out of the way, as in chapter 40. Exalted means that is, cast up, as in chapters 57 and 62, for instance, over valleys. Some explain mountains as great kingdoms, Egypt, Syria, etc., subjected to Rome, to facilitate the spreading of the Gospel; highways, the Christian doctrine wherein those who join the church walk, and which, at the time of Constantine, was to be raised into prominence before all, and publicly protected, as in chapter 35. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: Behold, these will come from far, and, lo, these from the north and from the west, and these from the land of Sinim. Sinim means the Arabians and other Asiatics called China Sin, or Tchin; the Chinese had no special name for themselves, but either adopted that of the reigning dynasty or some high-sounding titles. This view of Sinim suits the context which requires a people to be meant from far, and distinct from those from the north and from the west. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: Sing, O heavens, and be joyful, O earth, and break forth into singing, O mountains, for the Lord has comforted his people, and will have mercy upon his afflicted. So Revelation; God will have mercy on the afflicted, because of His compassion; on His afflicted, because of His covenant. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: But Zion said: The Lord has forsaken me, and my Lord has forgotten me. Zion is the literal Israel's complaint, as if God had forsaken her in the Babylonian captivity; also in their dispersion previous to their future restoration; thereby God's mercy shall be called forth, as in chapters 63 and Psalms 77 and 102. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: Can a woman forget her sucking child, that she should not have compassion on the son of her womb? Yes, they may forget, yet will I not forget you. This recalls chapters 44 and Psalm 103 and Matthew. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: Behold, I have graven you upon the palms of my hands; your walls are continually before me. This alludes to the Jews' custom, perhaps drawn from Exodus, of puncturing on their hands a representation of their city and temple, in token of zeal for them, as in Song of Solomon. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: Your children will make haste; your destroyers and they that made you waste will go forth of you. Thy children means Israel, as in verses 20 and 21 and chapter 43. Jerome reads, for Thy children, Thy builders; they that destroyed thee shall hasten to build thee. Haste means to rebuild thy desolate capital. Shall go forth means thy destroyers shall leave Judea to Israel in undisturbed possession. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

18 COMMENTARY: Lift up your eyes round about, and behold; all these gather themselves together, and come to you. As I live, says the Lord, you will surely clothe yourself with them all, as with an ornament, and bind them on you, as a bride does. As Zion is often compared to a bride, as in chapter 54, so the accession of converts is like bridal ornaments, jewels, as in chapters 62 and Malachi. Her literal children are, however, more immediately meant, as the context refers to their restoration; and only secondarily to her spiritual children by conversion to Christ. Israel shall be the means of the final complete conversion of the nations, as in Micah and Romans. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

19 COMMENTARY: For your waste and your desolate places, and the land of your destruction, will even now be too narrow by reason of the inhabitants, and they that swallowed you up will be far away. Land of thy destruction means thy land once the scene of destruction. Too narrow recalls chapters 54 and Zechariah. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

20 COMMENTARY: The children which you will have, after you have lost the other, will say again in your ears: The place is too strait for me; give place to me that I may dwell. After other is rather, the children of thy widowhood, that is, the children of whom thou hast been bereft during their dispersion in other lands, as in chapter 47. Again is rather, yet. Give place is rather, stand close to me, namely, in order that we may be the more able to dwell in the narrow place. Compare as to Israel's spiritual children, and the extension of the gospel sphere, Romans and Corinthians. But verse 22, as in chapters 66, shows that her literal children are primarily meant. Some translate, Make room. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

21 COMMENTARY: Then will you say in your heart: Who has begotten me these, seeing I have lost my children, and am desolate, a captive, and removing to and fro? And who has brought up these? Behold, I was left alone; these, where had they been? Who, etc., means Zion's joyful wonder at the unexpected restoration of the ten tribes. Secondarily, the accession of spiritual Israelites to the mother church of Jerusalem from the Gentiles is meant; this created surprise at first, as in Acts. Lost, am desolate, a captive, and removing to and fro is rather, bereaved of, have been barren, an exile and outcast. She had been put away by Jehovah, her husband, as in chapter 50; hence her wonder at the children begotten to her. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

22 COMMENTARY: Thus says the Lord God: Behold, I will lift up my hand to the Gentiles, and set up my standard to the people, and they will bring your sons in their arms, and your daughters will be carried upon their shoulders. Hand means that is, beckon to, as in chapter 13. Standard recalls chapter 11. Bring sons in arms means the Gentiles shall aid in restoring Israel to its own land, as in chapters 60 and 66. Children able to support themselves are carried on the shoulders in the East; but infants, in the arms, or astride on one haunch, as in chapter 60. Thy sons must be distinct from the Gentiles, who carry them; and therefore cannot primarily refer to converts among the Gentiles. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

23 COMMENTARY: Kings will be your nursing fathers, and their queens your nursing mothers; they will bow down to you with their face toward the earth, and lick up the dust of your feet, and you will know that I am the Lord, for they will not be ashamed that wait for me. Dust means that is, kiss thy feet in token of humble submission. For they not ashamed that wait for me means the restoration of Israel shall be in answer to their prayerful waiting on the Lord, as in chapters 30 and Psalm 102 and Zechariah. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

24 COMMENTARY: Will the prey be taken from the mighty, or the lawful captive delivered? The prey means Israel, long a prey to mighty Gentile nations, whose oppression of her shall reach its highest point under Antichrist. Lawful captive means the Jews justly consigned for their sins, as in chapter 50, as captives to the foe. Secondarily, Satan and Death are the mighty conquerors of man, upon whom his sin give them their lawful claim. Christ answers that claim for the sinners, and so the captive is set free. Others not so well translate, the captives taken from among the just Israelites. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

25 COMMENTARY: But thus says the Lord: Even the captives of the mighty will be taken away, and the prey of the terrible will be delivered, for I will contend with him that contends with you, and I will save your children. This recalls chapters 53 and Psalm 68 and Colossians. Contend with him, etc., recalls chapter 54. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

26 COMMENTARY: I will feed them that oppress you with their own flesh, and they will be drunken with their own blood, as with sweet wine, and all flesh will know that I the Lord am your Saviour and your Redeemer, the mighty One of Jacob. Own flesh is a phrase for internal strifes, as in chapter 9. Own blood is a just retribution for their having shed the blood of God's servants, as in Revelation. Sweet wine means that is, must, or new wine, the pure juice which flows from the heap of grapes before they are pressed; the ancients could preserve it for a long time, so as to retain its flavour. It was so mild that it required a large quantity to intoxicate; thus the idea here is that very much blood would be shed, as in Revelation. All flesh shall, etc., means the effect on the world of God's judgments, as in chapters 66 and Revelation. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
