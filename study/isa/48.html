Study Notes — Isaiah

Chapter 48

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Hear this, O house of Jacob, which are called by the name of Israel, and are come forth out of the waters of Judah, which swear by the name of the Lord, and make mention of the God of Israel, but not in truth, nor in righteousness. This chapter treats how the things that befall Babylon Jehovah predicted long before, lest Israel should attribute them, in its obstinate perversity, to strange gods, as in verses 1 to 5. The waters of Judah means spring from the fountain of Judah; Judah has the fountain attributed to it, because it survived the ten tribes, and from it Messiah was to spring. Swear by the Lord recalls chapters 19, 45, and 65. Mention means in prayers and praises. Not in truth recalls Jeremiah and John. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: For they call themselves of the holy city, and stay themselves upon the God of Israel; The Lord of hosts is his name. For means ye deserve these reproofs, for ye call yourselves citizens of the holy city, as in chapter 52, but not in truth, as in verse 1 and Nehemiah and Daniel; so the inscription on their coins of the time of the Maccabees, Jerusalem the Holy. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: I have declared the former things from the beginning, and they went forth out of my mouth, and I showed them; I did them suddenly, and they came to pass. Former means things which have happened in time past to Israel, as in chapters 42, 44, 45, and 46. Suddenly means they came to pass so unexpectedly that the prophecy could not have resulted from mere human sagacity. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: Because I knew that you are obstinate, and your neck is an iron sinew, and your brow brass. Obstinate is Hebrew, hard, as in Deuteronomy and Ezekiel. Iron sinew means inflexible, as in Acts. Brow brass means shameless as a harlot, as in Jeremiah and Ezekiel. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: I have even from the beginning declared it to you; before it came to pass I showed it to you, lest you should say: My idol has done them, and my graven image, and my molten image, has commanded them. This recalls verses 1 and 3: Jehovah declared it from the beginning and showed it before it came to pass, lest Israel should say, My idol has done them. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: You have heard, see all this, and will not you declare it? I have showed you new things from this time, even hidden things, and you did not know them. Thou, etc., means so ye are my witnesses, as in chapter 43 verse 10. Thou canst testify the prediction was uttered long before the fulfilment: see all this, namely, that the event answers to the prophecy. Declare means make the fact known as a proof that Jehovah alone is God, as in chapter 44 verse 8. New things means namely, the deliverance from Babylon by Cyrus, new in contradistinction from former predictions that had been fulfilled, as in chapters 42 and 43. Antitypically, the prophecy has in view the new things of the gospel treasury. From this point forward, the prophecies as to Messiah's first and second advents and the restoration of Israel, have a new circumstantial distinctness, such as did not characterise the previous ones, even of Isaiah. Babylon, in this view, answers to the mystical Babylon of Revelation. Hidden means which could not have been guessed by political sagacity. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: They are created now, and not from the beginning; even before the day when you heard them not, lest you should say: Behold, I knew them. Not like natural results from existing causes, the events when they took place were like acts of creative power, such as had never before been from the beginning. Even before the day when means rather, And before the day of their occurrence thou hast not heard of them, that is, by any human acuteness; they are only heard of by the present inspired announcement. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: Yes, you heard not, yes, you knew not, yes, from that time that your ear was not opened, for I knew that you would deal very treacherously, and were called a transgressor from the womb. Heardest not is repeated, as also knewest not, from verse 7. From that time means omit that: Yea, from the first thine ear did not open itself, namely, to obey them. To open the ear denotes obedient attention, as in chapter 50; or, was not opened to receive them, that is, they were not declared by Me to thee previously, since, if thou hadst been informed of them, such is thy perversity, thou couldst not have been kept in check. In the former view, the sense of the words following is, For I knew that, if I had not foretold the destruction of Babylon so plainly that there could be no perverting of it, thou wouldst have perversely ascribed it to idols, or something else than to Me, as in verse 5. Thus they would have relapsed into idolatry, to cure them of which the Babylonian captivity was sent: so they had done in Exodus. After the return, and ever since, they have utterly forsaken idols. Wast called means as thine appropriate appellation, as in chapter 9. From the womb means from the beginning of Israel's national existence, as in chapter 44. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: For my name's sake will I defer my anger, and for my praise will I refrain for you, that I cut you not off. Refrain is literally, muzzle; His wrath, after the return, was to be restrained a while, and then, because of their sins, let loose again. For thee means that is, mine anger towards thee. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: Behold, I have refined you, but not with silver; I have chosen you in the furnace of affliction. This recalls chapter 1 verse 25. With silver is rather, for silver: I sought by affliction to purify thee, but thou wast not as silver obtained by melting, but as dross. Thy repentance is not complete: thou art not yet as refined silver. Some explain, not as silver, not with the intense heat needed to melt silver, it being harder to melt than gold, that is, not with the most extreme severity. The former view is better, as in chapters 1 and 42 and Ezekiel. Chosen is or else, tried, proved, according to some, literally, to rub with the touchstone, or to cut in pieces so as to examine, as in Zechariah, Malachi, and 1 Peter. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: For my own sake, even for my own sake, will I do it, for how should my name be polluted? And I will not give my glory unto another. How should my name means some, instead of My name from verse 9, supply My glory from the next clause, and translate, How shamefully My glory has been profaned! In the English version the sense is, I will refrain in verse 9, that is, not utterly destroy thee, for why should I permit My name to be polluted, which it would be, if the Lord utterly destroyed His elect people, as in Ezekiel. Not give my glory unto another means if God forsook His people for ever, the heathen would attribute their triumph over Israel to their idols, so God's glory would be given to another. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: Hearken unto me, O Jacob and Israel, my called; I am he; I am the first, I also am the last. Verses 12 to 15 form the argument: the Almighty, who has founded heaven and earth, can, and will, restore His people. The first and last recalls chapters 41 and 44. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: My hand also has laid the foundation of the earth, and my right hand has spanned the heavens; when I call unto them, they stand up together. Spanned means measured out, as in chapter 40. When I call, stand up together recalls chapters 40 and Jeremiah; but it is not their creation so much which is meant, as that, like ministers of God, the heavens and the earth are prepared at His command to execute His decrees. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: All you, assemble yourselves, and hear; which among them has declared these things? The Lord has loved him; he will do his pleasure on Babylon, and his arm will be on the Chaldeans. Among them means among the gods and astrologers of the Chaldees, as in chapters 41, 43, and 44. Lord loved him, he will, etc., means that is, He whom the Lord hath loved will do, etc., namely, Cyrus, as in chapters 44, 45, and 46. However, Jehovah's language of love is too strong to apply to Cyrus, except as type of Messiah, to whom alone it fully applies. His pleasure means not Cyrus' own, but Jehovah's. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: I, even I, have spoken; yes, I have called him; I have brought him, and he will make his way prosperous. Brought means led him on his way. He means change from the first to the third person: Jehovah shall make his, Cyrus', way prosperous. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: Come near unto me, hear this; I have not spoken in secret from the beginning; from the time that it was, there am I, and now the Lord God, and his Spirit, has sent me. In secret recalls chapter 45 verse 19: Jehovah foretold Cyrus' advent, not with the studied ambiguity of heathen oracles, but plainly. From the time, etc., means from the moment that the purpose began to be accomplished in the raising up of Cyrus I was present. Sent me means the prophet here speaks, claiming attention to his announcement as to Cyrus, on the ground of his mission from God and His Spirit. But he speaks not in his own person so much as in that of Messiah, to whom alone in the fullest sense the words apply, as in chapters 61 and John. Plainly, chapter 49 verse 1, which is the continuation of the forty-eighth chapter, from verse 16, where the change of speaker from God in verses 1 and 12 to 15 begins, is the language of Messiah. Luke shows that the Spirit combined with the Father in sending the Son: therefore His Spirit is nominative to sent, not accusative, following it. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: Thus says the Lord, your Redeemer, the Holy One of Israel: I am the Lord your God which teaches you to profit, which leads you by the way that you should go. To profit means by affliction, such as the Babylonish captivity, and the present long-continued dispersion of Israel, as in Hebrews. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

18 COMMENTARY: O that you had hearkened to my commandments! Then had your peace been as a river, and your righteousness as the waves of the sea. Peace recalls Psalm 119; compare the desire expressed by the same Messiah in Matthew and Luke. River recalls chapters 33 and 41: a river flowing from God's throne is the symbol of free, abundant, and ever flowing blessings from Him, as in Ezekiel, Zechariah, and Revelation. Righteousness means religious prosperity, the parent of peace or national prosperity; therefore peace corresponds to righteousness in the parallelism, as in chapter 32. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

19 COMMENTARY: Your seed also had been as the sand, and the offspring of your bowels like the gravel thereof; his name should not have been cut off nor destroyed from before me. Sand means retaining the metaphor of the sea in verse 18. Like the gravel thereof is rather, as the Hebrew, like that, the offspring, of its, the sea's, bowels, referring to the countless living creatures, fishes, etc., of the sea, rather than the gravel; Jerome, Chaldee, and Syriac support the English version. His name cut off means transition from the second person, thy, to the third, his. Israel's name was cut off as a nation during the Babylonish captivity; also it is so now, to which the prophecy especially looks, as in Romans. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

20 COMMENTARY: Go forth of Babylon, flee from the Chaldeans, with a voice of singing declare, tell this, utter it even to the end of the earth; say: The Lord has redeemed his servant Jacob. Forth to the end of the earth means primarily, a prophecy of their joyful deliverance from Babylon, and a direction that they should leave it when God opened the way. But the publication of it to the ends of the earth shows it has a more world-wide scope antitypically; Revelation shows that the mystical Babylon is ultimately meant. Redeemed Jacob recalls chapters 43 and 44. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

21 COMMENTARY: They thirsted not when he led them through the deserts; he caused the waters to flow out of the rock for them; he clave the rock also, and the waters gushed out. Ezra, in describing the return, makes no mention of God cleaving the rock for them in the desert. The circumstances, therefore, of the deliverance from Egypt and of that from Babylon, are blended together; the language, while more immediately referring to the latter deliverance, yet, as being blended with circumstances of the former not strictly applicable to the latter, cannot wholly refer to either, but to the mystic deliverance of man under Messiah, and literally to the final restoration of Israel. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

22 COMMENTARY: There is no peace, says the Lord, unto the wicked. This is repeated in chapter 57. All the blessings just mentioned in verse 21 belong only to the godly, not to the wicked. Israel shall first cast away its wicked unbelief before it shall inherit national prosperity, as in Zechariah. The sentiment holds good also as to all wicked men, as in Job. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
