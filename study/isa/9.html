Study Notes — Isaiah

Chapter 9

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Nevertheless, the dimness will not be as the former time: as the former time brought contempt on Zebulun and Naphtali through deportation under Tiglath-pileser, so the after time will bring honour to the way of the sea, beyond Jordan, and Galilee of the Gentiles. The same region once darkened will be among the first to receive Messiah's light, for in despised Galilee he first and most publicly exercised his ministry. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: The people who walked in darkness have seen a great light, and those dwelling in the land of the shadow of death — the darkest misery of captivity — have had light shine upon them. The people here are the whole nation, Judah and Israel together. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: God has multiplied the nation — primarily the rapid increase of Israelites after the return from Babylon, more fully the rapid spread of Christianity — and they rejoice before him as with harvest joy and as men rejoice when they divide the spoil. The joy is taken from sacrificial feasts, where the tithe of harvest was eaten before God, and spoil-dividing follows judgments on the Lord's enemies that accompany revelations of his grace. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: For God has broken the yoke of his burden, the staff of his shoulder, and the rod of his oppressor, as in the day of Midian. As Gideon with a handful conquered Midian's hosts, so Messiah the child will prove the Prince of Peace, and the small Israel under him will overcome mighty hosts, not by man's prowess but by God's special interposition. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: For every battle of the warrior with confused noise and garments rolled in blood will be for burning, fuel for fire. All warlike accoutrements — greaves and bloodied martial cloaks — will be destroyed as no longer required in the new era of peace foretold in chapter 2. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: For unto us a child is born, unto us a son is given, and the government will be upon his shoulder, and his name will be called Wonderful, Counsellor, the mighty God, the everlasting Father, the Prince of Peace. The gift is God's gratuitous gift, on which man had no claim; the government on his shoulder, the ensign of office, stands against the oppressor's yoke on Israel's shoulder; and each name marks his essential characteristics — Wonderful as in chapter 8, Counsellor in wisdom, mighty God equivalent to Immanuel, everlasting Father who reigns forever unlike earthly kings, and Prince of Peace, the Tranquillizer. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: Of the increase of his government and peace there will be no end, upon the throne of David, ordered and established with judgment and justice from henceforth even forever. It is not a kingdom of mere might but of righteousness, attainable only in and by Messiah, and the zeal of the Lord of hosts will perform this, including his open victory over Antichrist and every enemy at his second coming. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: The Lord sent a word into Jacob, and it has lighted upon Israel — against the ten tribes. This begins a prophecy delivered a little later than the previous one, after the Assyrian invasion of Syria, foretelling the invasion of Ephraim that follows, and the whole section to chapter 10:4 falls into four strophes of crime and punishment, each ending "For all this his anger is not turned away." — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: All the people will know it, even Ephraim and the inhabitants of Samaria, its capital, to their cost and by experience. Samaria stands for Ephraim, as Jerusalem stood for Judah in chapter 1. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: In pride and stoutness of heart they say: The bricks are fallen down, but we will build with hewn stones; the sycamores are cut down, but we will change them into cedars. Eastern bricks were sun-dried and soon dissolved by rain, so the boast is self-confident unwillingness to see God's judgments: we will restore our affairs to more than their former magnificence, replacing cottages with palaces. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: Therefore the Lord will set up the adversaries of Rezin against him — the Assyrians, who first attacked Damascus, will next advance against Ephraim — and join his enemies together. This is the punishment of Ephraim's pride in making light of the judgment already inflicted through Tiglath-pileser: a second Assyrian invasion will follow. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: The Syrians before and the Philistines behind will devour Israel with open mouth, for all this his anger is not turned away, but his hand is stretched out still. Though once allies of Ephraim, after Rezin's death the Syrians will join the Assyrians against Ephraim, while the Philistines attack from the west, and this refrain is the burden of each strophe. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: For the people do not turn to him who smites them, neither do they seek the Lord of hosts. This is the second strophe's charge: the design of God's chastisements is not fulfilled in them, which becomes a new cause for punishment. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: Therefore the Lord will cut off from Israel head and tail, branch and rush, in one day. Head and tail are proverbial for highest and lowest, and branch and rush repeat the image — the branch elevated on the tree-top, the rush coarse and low. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: The ancient and honourable man is the head, and the prophet who teaches lies is the tail. There were many such lying prophets in Samaria, and they are named as the lowest because their flattery drags the nation down. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: For the leaders of this people cause them to err, and those who are led by them are destroyed, as in chapter 3. The leaders who flatter the people with promises of safety in sin swallow up the right way of the law. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: Therefore the Lord will have no joy in their young men, neither will he have mercy on their fatherless and widows, for everyone is a hypocrite and an evildoer, and every mouth speaks folly — and for all this his anger is not turned away. God will take no delight in their youthful warriors, the nation's delight and reliance, as to save them from the sword, and not even the usual objects of his pity will be spared, since folly here means wickedness. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

18 COMMENTARY: For wickedness burns as a fire, devouring briers and thorns and kindling the thickets of the forest, which mount up like the lifting up of smoke. Wickedness makes consumption, spreading rapidly and consuming like fire, for sin is its own punishment; from humble shrubbery the flame spreads to the vast forest, reaching high as well as low. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

19 COMMENTARY: Through the wrath of the Lord of hosts the land is darkened — darkened with smoke, or burnt up with suffocating heat — and the people are as fuel for the fire, and no man spares his brother. Intestine discord snaps asunder the dearest ties of nature. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

20 COMMENTARY: One will snatch on the right hand and be hungry, and eat on the left hand and not be satisfied, each eating the flesh of his own arm. The hunger is not literal but pictures internal factions, reckless of the tenderest ties and insatiably spreading misery, destroying those nearest akin, their former support. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

21 COMMENTARY: Manasseh will devour Ephraim and Ephraim Manasseh, and together they will be against Judah, and for all this his anger is not turned away. Manasseh and Ephraim, the two sons of Joseph once so united as to form but one tribe, will be rent into factions thirsting for each other's blood, yet united together against their brother Judah. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
