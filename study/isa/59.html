Study Notes — Isaiah

Chapter 59

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Behold, the Lord's hand is not shortened, that it cannot save, neither his ear heavy, that it cannot hear. This chapter treats the people's sin as the cause of judgments, their at last owning it themselves, and the Redeemer's future interposition in their extremity. The reason why Jehovah does not deliver His people, notwithstanding their religious services in chapter 58 verse 3, is not want of power on His part, but because of their sins in verses 1 to 8; verses 9 to 15 contain their confession; verses 16 to 21, the consequent promise of the Messiah. Hand shortened recalls chapter 50 verse 2. Ear heavy recalls chapter 6. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: But your iniquities have separated between you and your God, and your sins have hid his face from you, that he will not hear. Hid is Hebrew, caused Him to hide, as in Lamentations. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: For your hands are defiled with blood, and your fingers with iniquity; your lips have spoken lies, your tongue has muttered perverseness. This recalls chapters 1 and Romans. Hands, fingers means not merely the hands perpetrate deeds of grosser enormity, blood, but the fingers commit more minute acts of iniquity. Lips, tongue means the lips speak openly lies, the tongue mutters malicious insinuations, perverseness, perverse misrepresentations of others, as in Jeremiah. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: None calls for justice, nor any pleads for truth; they trust in vanity, and speak lies; they conceive mischief, and bring forth iniquity. Rather, No one calleth an adversary into court with justice, that is, None bringeth a just suit: No one pleadeth with truth. They trust iniquity recalls Job and Psalm 7. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: They hatch cockatrice' eggs, and weave the spider's web; he that eats of their eggs dies, and that which is crushed breaks out into a viper. Cockatrice is probably the basilisk serpent, cerastes. Instead of crushing evil in the egg, they foster it. Spider's web means this refers not to the spider's web being made to entrap, but to its thinness, as contrasted with substantial garments, as verse 6 shows. Their works are vain and transitory, as in Job and Proverbs. Eateth their eggs means he who partakes in their plans, or has anything to do with them, finds them pestiferous. That which is crushed means the egg, when it is broken, breaketh out as a viper; their plans, however specious in their undeveloped form like the egg, when developed, are found pernicious. Though the viper is viviparous, from which vi-per is derived, yet during gestation, the young are included in eggs, which break at the birth, as Bochart; however, metaphors often combine things without representing everything to the life. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: Their webs will not become garments, neither will they cover themselves with their works; their works are works of iniquity, and the act of violence is in their hands. Garments means like the fig leaves wherewith Adam and Eve vainly tried to cover their shame, as contrasted with the coats of skins which the Lord God made to clothe them with, as in chapters 64 and Romans and Galatians and Philippians. The artificial self-deceiving sophisms of human philosophy, as in 1 Timothy and 2 Timothy. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: Their feet run to evil, and they make haste to shed innocent blood; their thoughts are thoughts of iniquity; wasting and destruction are in their paths. Feet means all their members are active in evil; in verse 3, the hands, fingers, lips, and tongue, are specified. Run, haste recalls Romans: contrast David's running and hasting in the ways of God, as in Psalm 119. Thoughts means not merely their acts, but their whole thoughts. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: The way of peace they know not, and there is no judgment in their goings; they have made them crooked paths; whosoever goes therein will not know peace. Peace means whether in relation to God, to their own conscience, or to their fellow men, as in chapter 57. Judgment means justice. Crooked means the opposite of straightforward, as in Proverbs. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: Therefore is judgment far from us, neither does justice overtake us; we wait for light, but behold obscurity; for brightness, but we walk in darkness. Judgment far means retribution in kind because they had shown no judgment in their goings in verse 8. The vindication of our just rights by God is withheld by Him from us. Us means in verse 8 and previous verses, it was they, the third person; here, us, we, the first person. The nation here speaks: God thus making them out of their own mouth condemn themselves; just as He by His prophet had condemned them before. Isaiah includes himself with his people and speaks in their name. Justice means God's justice bringing salvation, as in chapter 46. Light means the dawn of returning prosperity. Obscurity means adversity, as in Jeremiah. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: We grope for the wall like the blind, and we grope as if we had no eyes; we stumble at noonday as in the night; we are in desolate places as dead men. Grope means fulfilling Moses' threat, as in Deuteronomy. Stumble at noon, as night means there is no relaxation of our evils; at the time when we might look for the noon of relief, there is still the night of our calamity. In desolate places is rather, to suit the parallel words at noonday, in fertile, literally, fat, as in Genesis, fields, where all is promising, we are like the dead, who have no hope left them; or, where others are prosperous, we wander about as dead men; true of all unbelievers, as in chapters 26 and Luke. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: We roar all like bears, and mourn sore like doves; we look for judgment, but there is none; for salvation, but it is far off from us. Roar means moan plaintively, like a hungry bear which growls for food. Doves recalls chapters 38 and Ezekiel. Salvation means retribution in kind: because not salvation, but destruction was in their paths, as in verse 7. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: For our transgressions are multiplied before you, and our sins testify against us, for our transgressions are with us, and as for our iniquities, we know them. This recalls Daniel. Thee, us means antithesis. With us means that is, we are conscious of them, as in Job. Know means acknowledge they are our iniquities. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: In transgressing and lying against the Lord, and departing away from our God, speaking oppression and revolt, conceiving and uttering from the heart words of falsehood. This gives the particulars of the sins generally confessed in verse 12, as in chapters 48 and Jeremiah. The act, the word, and the thought of apostasy, are all here marked: transgression and departing, etc.; lying, compare verse 4, and speaking, etc.; conceiving and uttering from the heart. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: Judgment is turned away backward, and justice stands afar off, for truth is fallen in the street, and equity cannot enter. Justice and righteousness are put away from our legal courts. In the street means in the forum, the place of judicature, usually at the gate of the city, as in Zechariah. Cannot enter means is shut out from the forum, or courts of justice. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: Yes, truth fails, and he that departs from evil makes himself a prey, and the Lord saw it, and it displeased him that there was no judgment. Faileth means is not to be found. He that departeth, prey means he that will not fall in with the prevailing iniquity exposes himself as a prey to the wicked, as in Psalm 10. Lord saw it means the iniquity of Israel, so desperate as to require nothing short of Jehovah's interposition to mend it, typifies the same necessity for a Divine Mediator existing in the deep corruption of man; Israel, the model nation, was chosen to illustrate his awful fact. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: He saw that there was no man, and wondered that there was no intercessor, therefore his arm brought salvation unto him, and his righteousness, it sustained him. No man means namely, to atone by his righteousness for the unrighteousness of the people. Man is emphatic, as in 1 Kings; no representative man able to retrieve the cause of fallen men, as in chapters 41, 63, and Jeremiah and Ezekiel. No intercessor means no one to interpose, to help, uphold, as in chapter 63. His arm recalls chapters 40 and 51: not man's arm, but His alone, as in Psalms. His righteousness means the arm of Messiah. He won the victory for us, not by mere might as God, but by His invincible righteousness, as man having the Spirit without measure, as in chapters 11, 42, 51, 53, and 1 John. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: For he put on righteousness as a breastplate, and a helmet of salvation upon his head, and he put on the garments of vengeance for clothing, and was clad with zeal as a cloak. Messiah is represented as a warrior armed at all points, going forth to vindicate His people. Owing to the unity of Christ and His people, their armour is like His, except that they have no garments of vengeance, which is God's prerogative, as in Romans, or cloak of zeal, in the sense of judicial fury punishing the wicked; this zeal belongs properly to God, as in 2 Kings, Romans, and Philippians; zeal, in the sense of anxiety for the Lord's honour, they have, as in Numbers, Psalm 69, and Corinthians; and for salvation, which is of God alone, as in Psalm 3, they have as their helmet, the hope of salvation, as in 1 Thessalonians. The helmet of salvation is attributed to them, as in Ephesians, in a secondary sense; namely, derived from Him, and as yet only in hope, not fruition, as in Romans. The second coming here, as often, is included in this representation of Messiah. His zeal, as in John, at His first coming was but a type of His zeal and vengeance against the foes of God at His second coming, as in 2 Thessalonians and Revelation. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

18 COMMENTARY: According to their deeds, accordingly he will repay, fury to his adversaries, recompense to his enemies; to the islands he will repay recompense. Deeds is Hebrew, recompenses; according as their deeds demand. This verse predicts the judgments at the Lord's second coming, which shall precede the final redemption of His people, as in chapter 66. Islands recalls chapter 41: distant countries. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

19 COMMENTARY: So will they fear the name of the Lord from the west, and his glory from the rising of the sun. When the enemy will come in like a flood, the Spirit of the Lord will lift up a standard against him. This recalls chapters 45 and Malachi: the result of God's judgments, as in chapters 26 and 66. Like a flood recalls Jeremiah and Revelation. Lift up a standard is rather, from a different Hebrew root, shall put him to flight, drive him away. Some, giving a different sense to the Hebrew for enemy from that in verse 18, and a forced meaning to the Hebrew for Spirit of the Lord, translate, When He shall come as a river straitened in its course, which a mighty wind drives along. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

20 COMMENTARY: The Redeemer will come to Zion, and unto them that turn from transgression in Jacob, says the Lord. To Zion means Romans quotes it, out of Zion. Thus Paul, by inspiration, supplements the sense from Psalm 14: He was, and is come to Zion, first with redemption, being sprung as man out of Zion. The Septuagint translates for the sake of Zion. Paul applies this verse to the coming restoration of Israel spiritually. Them that turn from means, as in Romans, shall turn away ungodliness from Jacob; so the Septuagint, Paul herein gives the full sense under inspiration. They turn from transgression, because He first turns them from it, and it from them, as in Psalm 130 and Lamentations. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

21 COMMENTARY: As for me, this is my covenant with them, says the Lord: My spirit that is upon you, and my words which I have put in your mouth, will not depart out of your mouth, nor out of the mouth of your seed, nor out of the mouth of your seed's seed, says the Lord, from henceforth and for ever. Thee means the covenant is with Christ, and with them only as united to Him, as in Hebrews. Jehovah addresses Messiah the representative and ideal Israel. The literal and spiritual Israel are His seed, to whom the promise is to be fulfilled, as in Psalm 22. Spirit, not depart, for ever recalls Jeremiah and Matthew. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
