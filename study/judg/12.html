Study Notes — Judges

Chapter 12

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: The men of Ephraim gathered and went northward to Jephthah, complaining he had not called them to fight Ammon, and JFB calls this a fresh development of their jealous, rash, and irritable temper, desiring the credit of patriotism without sharing the victory. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: Jephthah answered that he had called them and they had not delivered him, and JFB notes his straightforward answer shows the charge was false and their boast came with ill grace from people who had delayed appearing until the crisis was past. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: Seeing they would not deliver him, Jephthah put his life in his hands and passed over against Ammon, and JFB explains the phrase as undertaking a duty of imminent peril, which he had done with Gileadite volunteers alone, so Ephraim ought rather to have been thankful the war ended without their labour. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: Jephthah gathered Gilead and fought Ephraim, because Ephraim called Gileadites fugitives of Ephraim, and JFB notes the reasonable remonstrances were met with insulting sneers that Gileadites were outcasts, the scum of the common stock, and a feud immediately ensued. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: Gilead took the passages of Jordan, and when an Ephraimite fugitive said he would pass over, they asked whether he was an Ephraimite, and JFB notes the Gileadites, having defeated Ephraim, rushed to the fords to intercept every fugitive. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: They told him to say Shibboleth, and he said Sibboleth, for he could not frame to pronounce it rightly, and they slew him at the fords, and JFB explains Shibboleth means a stream and Sibboleth a burden, and the Ephraimites could not bring their organs to pronounce the Eastern dialect's sound. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: Jephthah judged Israel six years, died, and was buried in a city of Gilead, and JFB notes that however difficult some passages of his history are, apostolic authority ranks him among the worthies of the ancient church. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: After him Ibzan of Bethlehem judged Israel, the first of the minor judges of whom JFB notes the only memorials preserved relate to the number of their families and their state. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: Ibzan had thirty sons and thirty daughters, whom he sent abroad and took in from abroad, and judged Israel seven years, a household spread by marriage alliances across the tribes. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: Ibzan died and was buried at Bethlehem, closing a judgeship remembered, as JFB notes, chiefly by family and place. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: After him Elon a Zebulonite judged Israel ten years, another of the minor judges whose memorial is his tribe and term. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: Elon died and was buried in Aijalon in Zebulon, his burial place preserving his tribal home. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: After him Abdon son of Hillel, a Pirathonite, judged Israel, continuing the succession of minor judges. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: Abdon had forty sons and thirty nephews who rode on seventy ass colts, and judged Israel eight years, a family train that displayed his state as JFB notes of these judges. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: Abdon died and was buried in Pirathon in Ephraim, in the mount of the Amalekites, and the notice of place closes the roll of minor judges before Samson's story. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
