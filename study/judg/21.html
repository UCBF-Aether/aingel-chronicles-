Study Notes — Judges

Chapter 21

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: The men of Israel had sworn in Mizpeh that none of them would give his daughter to Benjamin to wife, an oath whose purport, JFB notes, was to treat the Gibeah perpetrators as the doomed Canaanites, entered into with the same inconsiderate haste as the rest of the affair. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: The people came to the house of God, abode till even, lifted up their voices, and wept sore, and JFB notes their characteristic fickleness: scarcely cooled from sanguinary vengeance, they rushed to the opposite extreme of self-accusation and grief, in a public service of expiation that vented emotion without relieving the fetter of their vow and anathema. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: They asked, O Lord God of Israel, why has this come to pass in Israel, that today one tribe should be lacking, turning victory into lamentation over the gap it had made. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: On the morrow the people rose early, built an altar there, and offered burnt and peace offerings, formalising their grief in sacrifice. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: They asked who among all the tribes had not come up to the assembly, for a great oath had been made that whoever came not to Mizpeh should surely be put to death, so that a second oath now compounded the first. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: Israel repented for Benjamin their brother, saying one tribe is cut off from Israel this day, and JFB explains cut off as in danger of extinction, since all Benjamin's women and children had been massacred and only six hundred men survived, a blank in the twelve tribes too painful to contemplate. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: They asked how to do for wives for those who remained, seeing they had sworn by the Lord not to give them their daughters, the oath now standing between the remnant and its survival. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: When the people were numbered, no one from Jabesh-gilead had come to the camp, and JFB notes Jabesh lay in eastern Manasseh about fifteen miles east of Jordan, and the Mizpeh ban seemed to impose punishing its inhabitants, so that to repair one rash proceeding they rushed to another, smaller tragedy, with the additional object of supplying wives. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: The people were numbered, and behold there were none of Jabesh-gilead's inhabitants among them, confirming the city's absence. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: The congregation sent twelve thousand valiant men, commanding them to smite Jabesh-gilead with the sword, with women and children, the ban executed as a crusade. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: They were commanded to utterly destroy every male and every woman that had lain by man, and JFB notes this shows the intemperate fury of the Israelites in the indiscriminate slaughter of women and children. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: Among Jabesh's inhabitants they found four hundred young virgins who had known no man, and brought them to the camp at Shiloh in Canaan, the spared remnant that was to become Benjamin's wives. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: The whole congregation sent to speak peaceably to Benjamin's children at the rock Rimmon and call them, ending the remnant's four months in the caves. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: Benjamin came again, and they gave them the women saved alive of Jabesh-gilead, but they sufficed not for them, since four hundred wives could not match six hundred men. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: The people repented for Benjamin, because the Lord had made a breach in Israel's tribes, and the breach itself was now acknowledged as the Lord's doing. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: The elders asked how to do for wives for those who remained, since Benjamin's women were destroyed, and JFB notes the carefully spared young women of Jabesh had proved inadequate, so another expedient must be found. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: They answered that there must be an inheritance for those escaped of Benjamin, that a tribe be not destroyed out of Israel, and JFB notes that as the only rightful owners of the territory, provision must be made to transmit it to legitimate heirs, though a new act of violence was meditated to do it. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

18 COMMENTARY: They said they could not give them wives of their daughters, for Israel had sworn, Cursed be he that giveth a wife to Benjamin, so that the oath barred the honest remedy and forced a dishonest one. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

19 COMMENTARY: But, they said, there is a yearly feast of the Lord in Shiloh, north of Bethel, on the east side of the highway from Bethel to Shechem, and south of Lebonah, and JFB notes the exact site was described evidently for the Benjamites' direction, while the feast, generally supposed to be tabernacles, was Shiloh's most social and hilarious festival. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

20 COMMENTARY: They commanded Benjamin to go and lie in wait in the vineyards, stationing the remnant for the seizure the elders had planned. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

21 COMMENTARY: When Shiloh's daughters came out to dance in dances, each Benjamite was to catch himself a wife and go to Benjamin's land, and JFB notes dancing was anciently part of religious observance, done in the open air with women by themselves, and their light, buoyant spirits, apprehensive of no danger, facilitated a scheme closely resembling the Sabine rape. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

22 COMMENTARY: When the daughters' fathers or brethren complained, the elders would say, Be favourable unto them, since they had not reserved wives in the war, and the givers had not given, so they would not be guilty, and JFB notes the elders' public sanction of this violence was new evidence of the evils into which precipitancy had involved them. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

23 COMMENTARY: Benjamin did so, taking wives according to their number from among the dancers they caught, and went and returned to their inheritance, repaired the cities, and dwelt in them. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

24 COMMENTARY: Then all Israel departed, every man to his tribe and family, to his inheritance, and the great assembly dissolved into its separate tribes. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

25 COMMENTARY: In those days there was no king in Israel, and every man did that which was right in his own eyes, and the book's closing sentence stands as the explanation of all the disorder, violence, and idolatry that fills its appendix. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
