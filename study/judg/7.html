Study Notes — Judges

Chapter 7

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Jerubbaal, that is Gideon, rose early with all his people and pitched beside the well of Harod, while Midian lay north by the hill of Moreh, the armies now face to face. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: The Lord said Gideon's people were too many, lest Israel vaunt itself and say its own hand had saved it, and Henry notes that God provides that the praise of victory may be wholly his own. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: The fearful were proclaimed free to return, and twenty-two thousand went home, leaving ten thousand, so that the army was purified by the departure of the faint-hearted. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: Even ten thousand were too many, and God ordered a second trial at the water, since he chooses instruments not only well affected but zealously affected in a good thing. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: The test divided those who lapped water with their tongues as a dog laps from those who bowed on their knees to drink, a small outward act made the sign of readiness. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: Three hundred lapped, putting their hands to their mouths, while the rest bowed down, and the few were set apart from the many by that very test. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: By the three hundred God promised to save Israel, and the rest were sent home, and Henry notes that a society made fewer in numbers may gain in purity and expect an increased blessing. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: The three hundred took victuals and trumpets, the rest were sent to their tents, and Gideon retained only the tested company with the host below him in the valley. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: That night God told Gideon to go down against the host, for it was delivered into his hand, giving the promise before the battle rather than after it. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: If Gideon was afraid, he might go down with his servant Phurah to the host, and God thus met fear with a permitted reconnaissance instead of rebuking it outright. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: He was to hear what the Midianites said, and then his hands would be strengthened, so that even the enemy's own talk would become encouragement. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: Midian, Amalek, and the east lay in the valley like grasshoppers for multitude, with camels beyond number, the same innumerable host Gideon had feared, now seen at close quarters. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: Gideon overheard a man telling his fellow a dream: a cake of barley bread tumbled into the Midianite camp and overturned a tent, a lowly thing undoing a great one. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: The fellow interpreted it at once as the sword of Gideon, into whose hand God had delivered Midian, and Henry notes that the name of Gideon had already filled Midian with terror. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: Hearing dream and interpretation, Gideon worshipped, returned to his camp, and cried, Arise, for the Lord hath delivered the host into your hand, giving God the praise for what encouraged his faith. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: Gideon divided the three hundred into three companies, giving each man a trumpet, an empty pitcher, and a lamp within the pitcher, weapons of noise and light rather than of iron. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: He told them to look on him and do likewise, coming to the outside of the camp and acting exactly as he acted, so that one example would govern three hundred men. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: When Gideon blew and his company with him, the rest were to blow and cry, The sword of the Lord, and of Gideon, joining God's name to his servant's in the battle cry. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: At the beginning of the middle watch, just as the watch was set, Gideon and his hundred blew and brake their pitchers, timing the alarm for the changing of the guard. — Matthew Henry

20 COMMENTARY: All three companies blew, brake their pitchers, held lamps in their left hands and trumpets in their right, and cried the sword of the Lord and of Gideon, and Henry sees in this the gospel's conquest: light out of earthen vessels, a sword in the mouth rather than the hand. — Matthew Henry

21 COMMENTARY: Every man stood in his place around the camp while the whole host ran, cried, and fled, so that steadiness on Israel's side met panic on Midian's. — Matthew Henry

22 COMMENTARY: The three hundred blew again, and the Lord set every man's sword against his fellow, so that the host fled to Beth-shittah and beyond, and God often makes the church's enemies instruments to destroy one another. — Matthew Henry

23 COMMENTARY: The men of Naphtali, Asher, and all Manasseh gathered and pursued Midian, the tribes that had been sent home or left uncalled now joining the pursuit. — Matthew Henry

24 COMMENTARY: Gideon sent messengers through Ephraim to take the waters as far as Beth-barah and Jordan, cutting off the fugitives' escape across the river. — Matthew Henry

25 COMMENTARY: Ephraim took Oreb and Zeeb, the two princes of Midian, and brought their heads to Gideon beyond Jordan, and Henry wishes all would likewise do readily what help is needed when another calls for it. — Matthew Henry
