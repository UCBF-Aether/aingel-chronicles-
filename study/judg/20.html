Study Notes — Judges

Chapter 20

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: All Israel was gathered as one man from Dan to Beersheba with Gilead unto the Lord in Mizpeh, and JFB notes the immense sensation of Gibeah's tragedy convened a national assembly of chiefs from all parts, including the eastern tribes, at Mizpeh on Judah and Benjamin's confines, near Shiloh, where unto the Lord likely denotes consultation of the oracle. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: The chief of all the people, four hundred thousand footmen that drew sword, presented themselves in the assembly of the people of God, a muster that showed the whole nation, not a faction, had taken up the cause. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: Benjamin heard Israel had gone up to Mizpeh, and Israel demanded how this wickedness was done, and JFB notes Benjamin had probably received a formal summons like other tribes but treated it with indifference or haughty disdain. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: The Levite, husband of the slain woman, answered with a brief and unvarnished recital of the outrage, and JFB notes force was used which he could not resist, his testimony corroborated by servant and old Ephraimite, and the facts alone produced one common sentiment of detestation and vengeance. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: He told how he came to Gibeah of Benjamin to lodge, and the men of Gibeah rose against him by night and beset the house, thinking to slay him, and instead humbled his concubine till she died. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: He took his concubine, divided her, and sent her into all the inheritance of Israel, because they had committed lewdness and folly in Israel, the summons that had convened the assembly. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: Behold, ye are all children of Israel, he said, give here your advice and counsel, turning his recital into a demand for national judgment. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: All the people arose as one man, saying none would go to his tent or house, and JFB notes this extraordinary unanimity showed the people were sound at the core, remembering their covenant and feeling the necessity of wiping out so foul a stain, though their resolutions were conditional on inquiry and a demand for satisfaction first. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: This was the thing they would do to Gibeah: go up by lot against it, so that vengeance would proceed by ordered decision, not by mob rush. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: They would take ten men of a hundred throughout the tribes to fetch victual for the people, that they might do to Gibeah according to all the folly committed in Israel, provisioning the expedition by a tribal levy. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: All the men of Israel gathered against the city, knit together as one man, the unanimity of verse 8 now embodied in a single army. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: Israel sent men through all the tribe of Benjamin, asking what wickedness this was and demanding the sons of Belial in Gibeah be delivered to be put to death, so that evil might be put away from Israel, and JFB notes this request was just and reasonable, and Benjamin's refusal virtually made the tribe a party to the quarrel. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: But Benjamin would not hearken to their brethren, and JFB notes the tribe was not insensible to the crime's atrocity, but its patriotism and pride were offended by the other tribes' hostile demonstration, and it incurred awful responsibility by resistance. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: Benjamin gathered out of the cities unto Gibeah to go out to battle against Israel, and JFB notes nothing but blind passion and unbending obstinacy could impel them to take the field against such disparity of numbers. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: Benjamin numbered that day twenty-six thousand men that drew sword, besides seven hundred chosen men of Gibeah, the small but formidable force that would twice defeat a host. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: Among all these people there were seven hundred chosen men left-handed, who could sling stones at a hair's breadth and not miss, and JFB describes the Hebrew sling, a leather thong with loop and lash, carrying full force two hundred yards in skilled hands. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: The men of Israel, besides Benjamin, numbered four hundred thousand men that drew sword, all men of war, the unwieldy force whose size JFB later blames for its early defeats. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

18 COMMENTARY: Israel arose, went up to the house of God, and asked which tribe should go up first against Benjamin, and the Lord said Judah first, and JFB notes this consultation at Shiloh was right but came too late, after plans were formed, and had counsel been sought sooner the expedition might have been reduced like Gideon's army. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

19 COMMENTARY: Israel arose in the morning and encamped against Gibeah, moving from oracle to battlefield. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

20 COMMENTARY: The men of Israel went out to battle against Benjamin and put themselves in array at Gibeah, the first pitched engagement. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

21 COMMENTARY: Benjamin came forth out of Gibeah and destroyed twenty-two thousand men of Israel that day, the first disaster of the vast host against the compact army. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

22 COMMENTARY: The people, the men of Israel, encouraged themselves and set their battle again in array in the place where they had put themselves the first day, refusing to abandon the field after one defeat. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

23 COMMENTARY: Israel went up and wept before the Lord until even, and asked whether to go up again against Benjamin, and the Lord said, Go up against him, so that the second battle was fought on divine permission, not mere obstinacy. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

24 COMMENTARY: Israel came near against Benjamin the second day, renewing the attack as commanded. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

25 COMMENTARY: Benjamin again came forth from Gibeah and destroyed eighteen thousand men of Israel that day, all drawing sword, the second successive loss that JFB says overwhelmed Israel with shame and sorrow and led them to reflect on their idolatries and proud reliance on numbers. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

26 COMMENTARY: Then all Israel went up to the house of God, wept, sat there before the Lord, fasted until even, and offered burnt and peace offerings, humbling themselves by prayer and fasting and the appointed expiation, after which they were assured of acceptance and victory. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

27 COMMENTARY: Israel enquired of the Lord, the ark of God's covenant being there, and Phinehas son of Eleazar, son of Aaron, standing before it, and JFB notes Phinehas's presence fixes the occurrence shortly after Joshua's death. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

28 COMMENTARY: They asked whether to go out again to battle against Benjamin or cease, and the Lord said, Go up, for tomorrow I will deliver them into thine hand, a promise that turned the third battle from a gamble into an assurance. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

29 COMMENTARY: Israel set liers in wait round about Gibeah, and JFB notes the plan of taking the city by stratagem resembled the capture of Ai. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

30 COMMENTARY: Israel put themselves in array against Gibeah the third day, as at other times, concealing the ambush behind the familiar formation. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

31 COMMENTARY: Benjamin went out against the people and was drawn away from the city, smiting about thirty men in the highways and field as at first, and the apparent repetition of victory lured them out. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

32 COMMENTARY: Benjamin said, They are smitten down before us as at the first, but Israel said, Let us flee and draw them from the city unto the highways, the stratagem now declared in the narrative. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

33 COMMENTARY: All Israel rose out of their place and put themselves in array at Baal-tamar, a palm-grove where Baal was worshipped, while the liers in wait came forth out of the meadows, or caves, of Gibeah. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

34 COMMENTARY: Ten thousand chosen men came against Gibeah, a third division distinct from both ambuscade and main army, and the battle was sore, though Benjamin did not know evil was near them. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

35 COMMENTARY: The Lord smote Benjamin before Israel, and Israel destroyed twenty-five thousand one hundred Benjamites that day, all drawing sword, the general account that the detailed narrative then follows. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

36 COMMENTARY: The Benjamites saw they were smitten, for Israel had given place to the liers in wait, yielding ground deliberately to spring the trap. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

37 COMMENTARY: The liers in wait hasted, rushed upon Gibeah, drew themselves along, and smote all the city with the edge of the sword. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

38 COMMENTARY: The appointed sign between Israel and the liers in wait was to make a great flame with smoke rise up out of the city. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

39 COMMENTARY: When Israel retired in the battle, Benjamin began to smite and kill about thirty men, supposing Israel fled before them as at the first. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

40 COMMENTARY: When the flame began to arise from the city with a pillar of smoke, the Benjamites looked behind them, and behold the flame of the city ascended to heaven. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

41 COMMENTARY: When Israel turned again, Benjamin was amazed, for they saw evil was come upon them, the battle reversing in a moment. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

42 COMMENTARY: They turned and fled toward the wilderness, but the battle followed hard after them, and those from the cities destroyed them in the midst of them. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

43 COMMENTARY: They were compassed about, chased, and trodden down with ease over against Gibeah toward the sunrising, the pursuit completing the encirclement. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

44 COMMENTARY: Eighteen thousand Benjamites fell, all valiant men, the first count of the slaughter in the field. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

45 COMMENTARY: They turned and fled toward the wilderness unto the rock of Rimmon, and JFB notes many fugitives found refuge in the caves of this rocky mountain northeast of Bethel, where a few men could sustain a siege for months. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

46 COMMENTARY: All who fell that day of Benjamin were twenty-five thousand men that drew sword, all valiant, and JFB notes this round number is confined to the third day, with a thousand fallen in the two previous engagements making the aggregate of chapter 20 verse 15. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

47 COMMENTARY: Six hundred men turned and fled to the rock Rimmon and abode there four months, the remnant of a tribe preserved in caves. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

48 COMMENTARY: The men of Israel turned again upon Benjamin and smote them with the sword, men of every city, beast, and all that came to hand, and set on fire all the cities they came to, and JFB notes this frightful vengeance, executed in highly excited passion, was yet the righteous retribution of God, and its memory served the whole nation's future good. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
