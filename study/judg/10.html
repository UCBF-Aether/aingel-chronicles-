Study Notes — Judges

Chapter 10

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: After Abimelech there arose Tola son of Puah to defend Israel, and JFB notes deliverance was necessary from intestine usurpation as well as foreign aggression, and that Tola, whose name means to save, was Gideon's brother by the father's side though of Issachar while Gideon was of Manasseh. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: Tola judged Israel twenty-three years, died, and was buried in Shamir, having made that central place in Mount Ephraim the seat of government. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: After him arose Jair the Gileadite, and JFB distinguishes this judge from the earlier conqueror and founder of Havoth-jair, Jair's villages. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: Jair had thirty sons who rode on thirty ass colts and had thirty cities called Havoth-jair, and JFB notes this as a trait of Eastern manners and a proof of his extensive possessions, not evidence of many wives at once. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: Jair judged Israel twenty-two years, died, and was buried in Camon, closing a quiet judgeship between two storms. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: Israel again did evil and served Baalim, Ashtaroth, and the gods of Syria, Zidon, Moab, Ammon, and Philistia, forsaking the Lord, and JFB notes this apostasy exceeded every former one in grossness and universality. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: The Lord's anger was hot, and he sold them into the Philistines and Ammon, whose incursions fell naturally on the lands contiguous to them, while Ammon, animated by conquest, crossed the Jordan and desolated the central and southern provinces. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: That year they vexed and oppressed Israel eighteen years, all Israel beyond Jordan in the land of the Amorites in Gilead, a long oppression matching the wide apostasy. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: The Ammonites also passed over Jordan to fight Judah, Benjamin, and Ephraim, so that Israel was sore distressed on both sides of the river. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: Israel cried to the Lord, saying, We have sinned against thee, because we have forsaken our God and served Baalim, and JFB notes the first step of repentance is confession, proved sincere when the sinner mourns the evil against God, not only its painful consequences. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: The Lord answered by rehearsing deliverances from Egyptians, Amorites, Ammonites, Philistines, Zidonians, Amalekites, and Maonites, and JFB notes these circumstances were probably not declared through the high priest, and that Maonites means Midianites. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: When Israel cried in those oppressions, God delivered them, and the rehearsal makes the present complaint answerable by the whole history of rescue. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: Yet ye have forsaken me and served other gods, the Lord said, so that he would deliver them no more, and the refusal is the just answer to repeated forsaking. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: Go and cry to the gods ye have chosen, the Lord said, let them deliver you in your tribulation, sending Israel to the gods whose service had brought the tribulation. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: Israel answered, We have sinned, do thou unto us whatsoever seemeth good unto thee, deliver us only this day, a confession that leaves the punishment to God while begging present deliverance. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: They put away the strange gods and served the Lord, and his soul was grieved for Israel's misery, and JFB notes that on abandoning idolatry and returning to pure worship, God graciously abridged the affliction and restored peace. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: The Ammonites gathered at Gilead while Israel assembled at Mizpeh, and JFB notes Ammon had moved from guerrilla warfare to a continued campaign aiming to wrest all trans-Jordanic territory, meeting a general tribal gathering at Mizpeh in eastern Manasseh. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

18 COMMENTARY: The people and princes of Gilead asked who would begin the fight against Ammon and become head over all Gilead, so that the crisis forced Israel to choose a captain before it could field an army. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
