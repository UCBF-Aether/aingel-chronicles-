Study Notes — Judges

Chapter 2

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: The angel of the Lord came from Gilgal to Bochim to call Israel to account, and Henry identifies this speaker as the great Angel of the covenant, the Son of God speaking with divine authority. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: The charge was precise: Israel had made leagues with the inhabitants and left their altars standing, disobeying the voice that had commanded separation from idolatry. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: God therefore declared he would not drive the nations out, and that they would be thorns in Israel's sides and their gods a snare, making men's sin itself their punishment. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: When the angel had spoken, the people lifted up their voice and wept, trembling at the word, though Henry observes that tears and even amendment cannot atone for sin. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: They named the place Bochim, meaning weepers, and sacrificed there, so that the memory of the rebuke was fixed in the very name of the place. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: Joshua had sent the people away, each to his inheritance, closing the era of united conquest under one commander and leaving each tribe to finish its own lot. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: The people served the Lord throughout Joshua's days and the days of the elders who had seen God's great works, showing how much a faithful generation can hold a nation steady. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: Joshua the servant of the Lord died at a hundred and ten, ending the generation that had entered the land under God's hand. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: He was buried in Timnath-heres in Mount Ephraim, in the inheritance he himself had received, a leader laid to rest in the land he had helped to win. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: Another generation arose that knew not the Lord nor his works, and Henry traces the whole dark history that follows to this failure of knowledge passed from fathers to children. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: Israel did evil in the sight of the Lord and served Baalim, exchanging the God who had delivered them for the gods of the peoples around them. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: They forsook the Lord God of their fathers, followed other gods, bowed to them, and provoked the Lord to anger, a deliberate apostasy rather than a mere lapse. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: They served Baal and Ashtaroth together, the male and female idols of Canaan, so that their worship became wholly conformed to their neighbours. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: The Lord's anger was hot against them, and he delivered them to spoilers and sold them into their enemies' hands, so that they could not stand before them. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: Wherever they went out, the hand of the Lord was against them for evil, as he had sworn, and they were greatly distressed, proving him as true to his threatenings as to his promises. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: Nevertheless the Lord raised up judges who delivered them from the spoilers, and JFB explains that these judges were God's vicegerents, raised by a special call and the Spirit's impulse rather than by regular succession. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: Yet Israel would not hearken to their judges but went whoring after other gods, turning quickly out of the way their fathers had walked, and Henry notes that those who forsake known good ways commonly grow most daring in sin. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: When the Lord raised a judge he was with him and delivered Israel all that judge's days, moved by their groanings under oppression, so that in the church's greatest distress God still finds or makes fit helpers. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: But when the judge died they returned and corrupted themselves more than their fathers, following other gods and refusing to cease from their doings, so that no reformation outlived its reformer. — Matthew Henry

20 COMMENTARY: The anger of the Lord was hot against Israel because this people had transgressed his covenant and not hearkened to his voice, and the sentence that follows is given as the reason for all the oppressions. — Matthew Henry

21 COMMENTARY: God declared he would no longer drive out the nations Joshua had left at his death, leaving the unfinished conquest as the standing consequence of Israel's disobedience. — Matthew Henry

22 COMMENTARY: Those nations were left to prove Israel, to test whether they would keep the way of the Lord as their fathers had, so that the enemy's presence became a trial of loyalty. — Matthew Henry

23 COMMENTARY: The Lord therefore left those nations and did not speedily drive them out, nor deliver them into Joshua's hand, and Henry notes they were thus beaten with their own rod, left under the power of the sins they had cherished. — Matthew Henry
