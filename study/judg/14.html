Study Notes — Judges

Chapter 14

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Samson went down to Timnath, now Tibna, about three miles from Zorah, and saw a Philistine woman, and JFB notes that in Philistine ascendency the two peoples often lived familiarly enough for matrimonial relations, and the Philistines were not among the seven devoted nations whose daughters the law forbade. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: He told his father and mother and asked them to get her for him, since in the East parents negotiated their sons' marriages, and Samson followed that custom even in an unusual match. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: His parents asked whether no woman among his own tribe would do, rather than an uncircumcised Philistine, but Samson insisted, she pleaseth me well, and JFB explains she was right in his eyes, fit for his purpose, not merely beautiful, since the Lord sought an occasion against the Philistines through this private quarrel. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: His father and mother knew not that it was of the Lord, that he sought an occasion against the Philistines, who then had dominion over Israel, and JFB notes the means God meant to use was not a numerous army but the miraculous prowess of a single-handed champion. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: As Samson went down to Timnath with his parents, a young lion roared against him in the vineyards, and JFB notes the wild passes of Judah were lions' lairs, and the lion was in the pride of youthful prime. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: The Spirit of the Lord came mightily upon him, and he rent the lion as he would a kid, with nothing in his hand, and told neither father nor mother, and JFB notes this superhuman strength, put forth in private incidents, trained him to trust it for his public work. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: He went down and talked with the woman, and she pleased Samson well, and JFB notes such social intercourse between young people in the East was rare and generally followed betrothal. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: After a time he returned to take her, probably after the usual year between betrothal and marriage, and turning aside to see the lion's carcass, he found a swarm of bees and honey in it, which JFB explains by the climate's insects, birds, and sun reducing the carcass in a few months to a state inviting to bees. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: He took the honey, went on eating, and gave some to his parents, who ate, but he told them not that he had taken it from the lion's carcass, keeping the wonder private as he had the killing. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: His father went down to the woman, and Samson made a feast there, as young men used to do, and JFB notes the wedding festivity lasted a week, with the bride among her female relatives and Samson apart as a stranger, attended by paranymphs who were really spies on his proceedings. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: When the Philistines saw him they brought thirty companions to be with him, the large number of bridegroom's friends furnished by the bride's family, ostensibly to honour the nuptials. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: Samson put forth a riddle, offering thirty sheets and thirty changes of garments if they declared it within the seven feast days, and JFB notes riddles were a favourite Oriental amusement at such feasts, and Samson's riddle related to honey in the lion's carcass. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: If they could not declare it, they would give him thirty sheets and thirty garments, so that the wager bound both sides equally. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: The riddle was, Out of the eater came forth meat, and out of the strong came forth sweetness, and for three days they could not declare it, as JFB notes, until the festive week drew to a close. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: On the seventh day they told Samson's wife to entice her husband and declare the riddle, lest they burn her and her father's house, and JFB notes they secretly enlisted the newly married wife, who got the secret and revealed it to her friends. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: Samson's wife wept before him, saying he hated her and did not love her, since he had put a riddle to her people and not told her, and her tears pressed the secret from him. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: She wept the seven days of the feast, and on the seventh day he told her because she lay sore upon him, and she told the riddle to her people, completing the betrayal JFB traces to the companions' secret enlistment of the wife. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

18 COMMENTARY: Before sunset on the seventh day the men of the city answered, What is sweeter than honey, and what is stronger than a lion, and Samson replied, If ye had not plowed with my heifer ye had not found out my riddle, and JFB explains the ploughing metaphor as resorting to his wife's aid, an unworthy expedient. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

19 COMMENTARY: The Spirit of the Lord came upon Samson, and he went to Ashkelon, slew thirty men, took their spoil, and gave the changes of garments to those who had expounded the riddle, and JFB notes Ashkelon, about twenty-four miles from Timnah, was chosen by the Spirit probably for its bitter hostility to Israel, though stripping a slain enemy was unknown in Hebrew warfare. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

20 COMMENTARY: Samson's anger was kindled, and he went up to his father's house, while his wife was given to his companion, whom he had used as his friend, and JFB calls this acceptance an act of base treachery, since the friend of the bridegroom had been the medium between husband and bride. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
