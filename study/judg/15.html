Study Notes — Judges

Chapter 15

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: In wheat harvest time Samson visited his wife with a kid, intending to go into her chamber, and JFB notes the harvest season left grain in a most combustible state, and the kid was a visitor's present, perhaps a token of reconciliation. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: Her father would not let him in, saying he had thought Samson utterly hated her and had given her to his companion, offering the younger sister instead, and JFB calls the allegation a mere sham and pretext, and the sister proposal an insult unlawful for an Israelite to accept. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: Samson said he would now be more blameless than the Philistines though he did them a displeasure, and JFB notes this nefarious conduct provoked his just indignation and a resolve to take signal vengeance. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: He caught three hundred foxes, or rather jackals, which prowl in large packs in Palestine's mountains, and tied them tail to tail with firebrands between them, and JFB explains the slow-burning torches would blaze when blown by wind, and the pained animals would kindle one great conflagration in the Philistine corn plain. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: He let them go into the Philistines' standing corn, and burnt up both the shocks and the standing corn with vineyards and olives, the general devastation and panic leaving no neighbour able to help another. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: The Philistines asked who had done this, learned Samson had done it because his father-in-law had given his wife to his companion, and burnt her and her father with fire, and JFB calls this remarkable retribution, since to avoid that very menace she had betrayed her husband. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: Samson said that though they had done this he would yet be avenged of them, and then cease, and JFB notes that by this act the Philistines had been instruments in avenging his private wrong, but as divinely appointed judge his work of retribution was not yet accomplished. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: He smote them hip and thigh with a great slaughter, a proverbial expression for merciless slaughter, and then dwelt in the cleft of the rock Etam, a cave in the cliff. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: The Philistines went up and spread themselves in Lehi, now El-Lekieh among limestone cliffs and caves, to apprehend Samson in revenge, and JFB notes Samson allowed himself to be bound and surrendered to free his countrymen from danger, then with a new jawbone of an ass slew a thousand men at Ramath-lehi, the hill of the jawbone. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: The men of Judah asked why the Philistines had come up, and were told, to bind Samson and do to him as he had done to them, stating the expedition's purpose plainly. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: Three thousand men of Judah went to Etam and asked Samson whether he knew the Philistines ruled over them, and what he had done, and he answered, As they did to me, so have I done to them, claiming his vengeance as requital. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: They said they had come to bind him and deliver him to the Philistines, and asked him to swear they would not fall upon him themselves, and JFB's account of his willing surrender explains why he accepted bonds from his own countrymen. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: They bound him with two new cords and brought him up from the rock, and the new cords would make the coming breaking of them the more evidently miraculous. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: When he came to Lehi the Philistines shouted against him, the Spirit of the Lord came mightily upon him, and the cords on his arms became as flax burnt with fire, and his bands loosed from his hands, the prelude to the jawbone slaughter JFB describes. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: He found a new jawbone of an ass, took it, and slew a thousand men with it, the weapon JFB notes as the instrument of the slaughter at Ramath-lehi. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: Samson sang, With the jawbone of an ass, heaps upon heaps, with the jaw of an ass have I slain a thousand men, and JFB notes the weapon's inadequacy plainly shows a miraculous feat of supernatural strength. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: When he had finished speaking he cast away the jawbone and called the place Ramath-lehi, the hill of the jawbone, naming the battlefield after its weapon. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

18 COMMENTARY: Sore athirst, he called on the Lord, saying he had given this great deliverance, and now he would die for thirst and fall into the uncircumcised, and JFB notes his exhausted strength was about to be recruited by a refreshing draft, in a prayer that records his piety. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

19 COMMENTARY: God clave a hollow place in the jaw, that is, in Lehi, and water came out, and when Samson drank his spirit returned, and he called the place En-hakkore, the supplication well, which JFB notes records the piety of this heroic champion. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

20 COMMENTARY: Samson judged Israel twenty years in the days of the Philistines, closing the account of a judge whose deliverance, as chapter 13 foretold, was only begun. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
