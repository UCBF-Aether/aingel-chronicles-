Study Notes — Judges

Chapter 9

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Abimelech son of Jerubbaal went to Shechem, where idolatry once creeping in was now openly professed, and JFB notes he plied the arts of a demagogue among his maternal relatives to raise himself to a throne. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: He asked whether seventy sons of Jerubbaal reigning or one man reigning was better, and JFB calls this a false insinuation, since Gideon had rejected kingship with abhorrence and no other son is shown coveting the title. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: His mother's brethren spoke for him to all Shechem, and their hearts inclined to follow him because he was their brother, so that kinship, not merit, carried the demagogue's case. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: They gave him seventy pieces of silver from the house of Baal-berith, god of the covenant by whom the league of cities was formed, and with it he hired vain and light persons, whom JFB calls idle vagabonds, the scum of society, with nothing to lose and much to gain from revolution. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: At Ophrah he slew his brethren, seventy persons, upon one stone, and JFB notes this first recorded Eastern massacre of rivals, probably a sacrifice on a stone altar in revenge for his father's demolition of Baal's altar, with two wanting to complete the round number. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: All Shechem and the house of Millo, that is, the priests of Baal in the temple, made Abimelech king by the plain of the pillar, or rather by the oak near a raised mound, so the coronation might be conspicuous to a crowd. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: Jotham stood on Mount Gerizim and lifted up his voice, and JFB notes the spot was Shechem's public place, the parable fitted to the valley's foliage, and the overhanging hill carried his voice distinctly to listeners below. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: The trees went forth to anoint a king and said to the olive, Reign thou over us, opening Jotham's parable of reproach against a people choosing a king from among the worthless. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: The olive refused, asking whether it should leave its fatness, by which God and man are honoured, to be promoted over the trees, and JFB notes the oil honoured God in accepted sacrifice. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: The trees then said to the fig tree, Come and reign over us, turning from the olive's refusal to the next fruitful tree. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: The fig tree likewise refused, asking whether it should forsake its sweetness and good fruit to be promoted over the trees. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: The trees then said to the vine, Come and reign over us, pressing the crown on a third fruitful tree. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: The vine refused, asking whether it should leave its wine which cheers God and man, and JFB explains God is said to be cheered when sacrifices are accepted, as he is honoured by oil. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: Then all the trees said to the bramble, Come and reign over us, and the parable's sting lands: only the worthless thorn accepts the crown the fruitful trees refused. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: The bramble answered that if they truly anointed it king they must trust in its shadow, and if not, fire would come out of the bramble and devour the cedars of Lebanon, a threat as empty as its shade. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: Jotham applied the parable, asking whether they had acted in truth and sincerity in making Abimelech king and in dealing well with Jerubbaal and his house. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: He reminded them his father had fought for them, adventured his life, and delivered them from Midian, the service Abimelech's kingship repaid with murder. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

18 COMMENTARY: They had risen against his father's house that day, slain his sons on one stone, and made the son of a maidservant king because he was their brother, the very kinship argument of verse 3 turned into an indictment. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

19 COMMENTARY: If they had acted truly, Jotham said, let them rejoice in Abimelech and let him rejoice in them, an ironic blessing that leaves the curse to follow. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

20 COMMENTARY: But if not, let fire come out from Abimelech and devour Shechem and Millo, and fire from Shechem and Millo devour Abimelech, so that king and makers would destroy each other. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

21 COMMENTARY: Jotham then ran away and went to Beer and dwelt there for fear of Abimelech, and JFB identifies Beer as the modern El-Bireh on the ridge north of Jerusalem. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

22 COMMENTARY: Abimelech reigned three years over Israel, and JFB notes his reign at first extended little beyond Shechem, growing by stealthy encroachments, and that the word used signifies despotism, not the divinely authorised rule of a judge. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

23 COMMENTARY: God sent an evil spirit between Abimelech and the men of Shechem, and they dealt treacherously with him, and JFB explains this in providence as jealousy, distrust, and smothered rebellion permitted to punish the fratricide and idolatrous usurper. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

24 COMMENTARY: This came that the cruelty to Jerubbaal's seventy sons might come, and their blood be laid on Abimelech who slew them and on Shechem's men who strengthened his hands, so that both murderer and accomplices share the guilt. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

25 COMMENTARY: Shechem's men set ambushers in the mountain tops who robbed all who passed by, and the conspiracy against their own king began with highway robbery. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

26 COMMENTARY: Gaal son of Ebed came with his brethren, and Shechem put confidence in him, and JFB calls this an insurrection of the original Canaanites breaking out in Shechem under a new head. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

27 COMMENTARY: The Shechemites went into the fields, gathered grapes, trod them, made merry, and went into their god's house to eat, drink, and curse Abimelech, turning harvest joy into open treason. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

28 COMMENTARY: Gaal boasted, Who is Abimelech and who is Shechem, that we should serve him, and JFB calls him boastful, impudent, and cowardly, totally unfit to lead a revolution, whose end would be ambush, defeat, a city destroyed and sown with salt, and a stronghold burned. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

29 COMMENTARY: Gaal wished the people were under his hand so he could remove Abimelech, and told Abimelech to increase his army and come out, a challenge issued before any army existed to back it. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

30 COMMENTARY: Zebul, ruler of the city, heard Gaal's words and his anger was kindled, so that the conspiracy now had an enemy inside the walls as well as outside. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

31 COMMENTARY: Zebul sent messengers secretly to Abimelech, warning that Gaal and his brethren had come to Shechem and were fortifying the city against him. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

32 COMMENTARY: He advised Abimelech to rise by night with his people and lie in wait in the field, turning Gaal's open boast into a trap. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

33 COMMENTARY: In the morning at sunrise Abimelech was to rise early and fall upon the city, and when Gaal came out, do to him as occasion served. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

34 COMMENTARY: Abimelech rose by night with four companies and lay in wait against Shechem, following Zebul's counsel exactly. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

35 COMMENTARY: Gaal went out and stood in the gate's entrance while Abimelech and his people rose from their ambush, so that the challenger found himself already outmanoeuvred. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

36 COMMENTARY: Gaal saw the people and told Zebul they were coming down from the mountain tops, and Zebul answered that he saw only the shadow of the mountains as men, stalling Gaal until the ambush was unmistakable. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

37 COMMENTARY: Gaal insisted the people were coming down by the middle of the land and another company by the plain of Meonenim, and his alarm came too late to save him. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

38 COMMENTARY: Zebul then taunted him, asking where his mouth was now, and whether this was not the people he had despised, and sent him out to fight them. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

39 COMMENTARY: Gaal went out before Shechem's men and fought with Abimelech, the boaster now forced to back his words with battle. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

40 COMMENTARY: Abimelech chased Gaal, who fled, and many fell wounded even to the gate's entrance, so that the insurrection collapsed at the city gate. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

41 COMMENTARY: Abimelech dwelt at Arumah while Zebul thrust Gaal and his brethren out of Shechem, expelling the Canaanite rising from the city it had seized. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

42 COMMENTARY: The next day the people went out into the field, and it was told to Abimelech, so that the city's punishment, not merely Gaal's expulsion, followed. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

43 COMMENTARY: Abimelech took his people, divided them into three companies, laid wait in the field, and when the people came out of the city, rose up and smote them. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

44 COMMENTARY: Abimelech and his company rushed forward and stood in the gate's entrance while the other two companies slew those in the field, cutting off both escape and return. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

45 COMMENTARY: He fought against the city all day, took it, slew its people, beat down the city, and sowed it with salt, fulfilling Jotham's curse in the very terms of desolation. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

46 COMMENTARY: The men of the tower of Shechem, hearing this, entered the hold of the house of the god Berith, fleeing from the sacked city to their god's stronghold. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

47 COMMENTARY: It was told to Abimelech that all the men of the tower were gathered together, so that the last refuge became his next target. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

48 COMMENTARY: Abimelech went to Mount Zalmon, took an axe, cut a bough, laid it on his shoulder, and told his people to do as they had seen him do. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

49 COMMENTARY: All the people cut boughs, followed him, put them to the hold, and set the hold on fire, so that about a thousand men and women of the tower died, the fire fulfilling the bramble's threat from the parable. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

50 COMMENTARY: Abimelech then went to Thebez and encamped against it, and JFB identifies Thebez as the modern Tubas, not far from Shechem. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

51 COMMENTARY: All the men and women of Thebez fled to the tower, shut themselves in, and went up to its top, and JFB notes Canaanite forts often had such a strong tower as a last refuge, a scene matched in Assyrian bas-reliefs. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

52 COMMENTARY: Abimelech came to the tower, fought against it, and drew near to its door to burn it with fire, repeating at Thebez the very tactic that had destroyed Shechem's tower. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

53 COMMENTARY: A certain woman cast a piece of a millstone on Abimelech's head and broke his skull, and JFB's description of huge stone fragments dropping from the battlements on assailants is exactly this stroke. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

54 COMMENTARY: Abimelech called his armourbearer to draw his sword and slay him, lest men say a woman slew him, and the young man thrust him through and he died, pride outliving the king to his last breath. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

55 COMMENTARY: When Israel saw Abimelech was dead, they departed every man to his place, and the usurper's army dissolved with its head. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

56 COMMENTARY: Thus God rendered Abimelech's wickedness, which he did to his father in slaying his seventy brethren, returning the fratricide's blood on his own head. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

57 COMMENTARY: And all the evil of Shechem's men God rendered upon their heads, and upon them came the curse of Jotham son of Jerubbaal, so that parable, conspiracy, and judgment close together. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
