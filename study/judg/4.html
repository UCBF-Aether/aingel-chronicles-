Study Notes — Judges

Chapter 4

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: After Ehud's death Israel again did evil, and Henry observes that eighty years of rest had made them secure and indulgent rather than confirming them in religion, so that the prosperity of fools destroyed them. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: The Lord sold them to Jabin of Hazor, whose captain Sisera commanded nine hundred iron chariots, and this nearer enemy mightily oppressed Israel twenty years. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: Israel cried to the Lord because of the iron chariots, and Henry notes the pattern: those who slight God in prosperity find themselves under necessity of seeking him in trouble. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: Deborah, a prophetess and wife of Lapidoth, judged Israel, instructed in divine knowledge by the Spirit, and acting as God's mouth to correct abuses and redress grievances. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: She dwelt under the palm tree of Deborah between Ramah and Bethel, and Israel came to her for judgment, a court held in the open under a tree rather than in a palace. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: Deborah sent for Barak of Kedesh-naphtali and gave him God's command to draw toward Mount Tabor with ten thousand men of Naphtali and Zebulun. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: God promised to draw Sisera with his chariots and multitude to the river Kishon and deliver him into Barak's hand, so that the battle's place and outcome were appointed before the army marched. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: Barak answered that he would go only if Deborah went with him, valuing her presence and the assurance of God's word more than mere honour. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: Deborah promised to go, but warned that the honour would not be Barak's, for the Lord would sell Sisera into the hand of a woman, and she would not send him where she would not go herself. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: Barak called Zebulun and Naphtali to Kedesh, ten thousand men went up at his feet, and Deborah went up with him, so that the prophetess kept her word on the field. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: Heber the Kenite had separated from his kin and pitched his tent by the oak in Zaanaim near Kedesh, a detail that sets the stage for where Sisera would flee. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: Sisera was told that Barak had gone up to Mount Tabor, and the report drew the Canaanite army exactly where God had said it would be drawn. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: Sisera gathered all his nine hundred iron chariots and all his people to the river Kishon, trusting, as Henry notes, chiefly in his chariots. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: Deborah told Barak to arise, for the Lord had gone before him, and Barak went down from Tabor with ten thousand men, quitting the mountain in dependence on divine power though the plain favoured chariots. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: The Lord discomfited Sisera and all his chariots and host with the edge of the sword, and Sisera himself fled away on foot, his pride in iron undone by the God who goes before his people. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: Barak pursued the chariots and the host to Harosheth, and all Sisera's host fell by the sword until not a man was left. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: Sisera fled on foot to the tent of Jael, wife of Heber the Kenite, because there was peace between Jabin and Heber's house, and the fugitive sought shelter where he expected alliance. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: Jael came out to meet Sisera, invited him in, and covered him with a mantle, receiving the enemy general with the outward signs of hospitality. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: When he asked for water she gave him milk and covered him again, and Henry allows that Jael may at first have intended kindness before a divine impulse led her to see him as the determined enemy of the Lord. — Matthew Henry

20 COMMENTARY: Sisera told her to stand at the tent door and deny he was there, asking her to lie for his life and making her tent his last defence. — Matthew Henry

21 COMMENTARY: Jael took a tent nail and hammer, went softly to the sleeping Sisera, and smote the nail into his temples until it fastened into the ground, so that he died, and Henry notes that the weak things of the world thus confound the mighty. — Matthew Henry

22 COMMENTARY: When Barak pursued Sisera, Jael came out and showed him the man lying dead with the nail in his temples, so that the pursuer found his enemy already judged. — Matthew Henry

23 COMMENTARY: So God subdued Jabin king of Canaan before Israel that day, and the victory is credited to God rather than to Barak or Jael alone. — Matthew Henry

24 COMMENTARY: Israel's hand prevailed more and more against Jabin until he was destroyed, and Henry adds that Israel would have prevented much mischief had they destroyed the Canaanites sooner, though better to be wise late than never. — Matthew Henry
