Study Notes — Judges

Chapter 18

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: In those days there was no king in Israel, and the Danites sought an inheritance to dwell in, since their lot had not yet fallen to them, and Henry notes one family of Dan, whose lot included distant Laish, had sponged upon their brethren rather than go far to provide for themselves, until necessity aroused them. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: Dan sent five valiant men from Zorah and Eshtaol to spy out the land, and when they came to Mount Ephraim they lodged at Micah's house, and Henry notes it is prudent to look before leaping, and Dan had both the serpent's subtlety and the lion's whelp's courage. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: By Micah's house they knew the young Levite's voice, turned in, and asked who brought him there, what he did, and what he had there, recognising by voice a man they had known in his rambles. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: He told them Micah had hired him and he was his priest, explaining the business and encouragement of his strange preferment. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: They asked him to ask counsel of God whether their way would be prosperous, and Henry notes their carelessness: they would not have enquired of the Lord at all if the Levite's mention of teraphim had not put it into their heads, and they had passed by Shiloh without enquiring of God's high priest. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: The priest said, Go in peace, for before the Lord is your way, and Henry notes he humoured the consultation so well they believed he had an answer from God, while true care should be that our way be such as God approves, and then we may go in peace. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: The five men came to Laish and saw its people dwelling careless, quiet, and secure after the manner of the Zidonians, with no magistrate to put them to shame, far from Zidon, and with no business with any man, and Henry calls it a place ill governed and ill guarded, and therefore an easy prey. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: They returned to their brethren at Zorah and Eshtaol, who asked what they said, and the report that follows was the spies' answer. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: They urged, Arise, let us go up against them, for the land is very good, and they chided their brethren's sloth, and Henry notes the spies were not evil spies in this, representing the place as desirable and attainable. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: When they went they would come to a secure people and a large land God had given into their hands, a place lacking nothing, and Henry applies it: heaven is a very good land God has given into our hands, and we must not be slothful in laying hold on eternal life. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: Six hundred Danites appointed with weapons of war went from Zorah and Eshtaol, and Henry notes this was but a hundredth part of the tribe, which had numbered above sixty-four thousand, showing how little public spirit yet appeared in Israel. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: They pitched in Kirjath-jearim in Judah, and called the place Mahaneh-dan, the camp of Dan, unto this day, military encampments being then so rare that the night's resting place kept the name. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: They passed on to Mount Ephraim and came to Micah's house, where the march paused and the second half of the story begins. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: The five spies told their brethren that in these houses there were an ephod, teraphim, a graven image, and a molten image, and bade them consider what to do, and Henry notes they thought the gods worth stealing, if masters of these gods they might prosper and master Laish. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: They turned aside to the young Levite's house at Micah's and saluted him, while the six hundred stood by the gate, so that the priest was kept in talk while the theft was done. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: The six hundred appointed with weapons stood by the entering of the gate, a show of force that kept the priest occupied and the household overawed. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: The five spies went in and took the graven image, the ephod, the teraphim, and the molten image, while the priest stood at the gate with the six hundred, and Henry asks how gods that could not keep themselves from being stolen should protect those who stole them. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: When they fetched the carved image, ephod, teraphim, and molten image from Micah's house, the priest asked what they were doing, and Henry notes the sorry priest, sauntering at the gate, had let his treasure go, and the Danites' idolatry began in theft, breaking the eighth commandment to break the second. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: They told him to hold his peace, lay his hand on his mouth, and go with them to be father and priest to a tribe and family rather than to one man's house, and Henry notes they tempted him with better preferment, more dependants, sacrifices, and fees. — Matthew Henry

20 COMMENTARY: The priest's heart was glad, and he took the ephod, teraphim, and graven image and went in the midst of the people, and Henry notes a little persuasion served his rambling fancy, covetousness, and ambition, and that if ten shekels won him, eleven would lose him, for the hireling flees because he is a hireling. — Matthew Henry

21 COMMENTARY: They turned and departed, putting the little ones, cattle, and carriage before them, so confident of success that families and effects marched in the van. — Matthew Henry

22 COMMENTARY: When they were a good way from Micah's house, the men of the neighbouring houses gathered and overtook the Danites, mustering to recover the stolen gods. — Matthew Henry

23 COMMENTARY: They cried to the Danites, who turned and asked Micah what ailed him that he came with such a company, the rear guard turning to face the pursuers. — Matthew Henry

24 COMMENTARY: Micah said they had taken away the gods he had made and the priest, and asked what he had left, and Henry notes the folly of calling gods what he himself had made, and the spiritual warning that whatever we say What have we more of, we have made an idol. — Matthew Henry

25 COMMENTARY: The Danites answered that he should not let his voice be heard, lest angry fellows run upon him and he lose his life and his household's, justifying robbery with a threat of murder. — Matthew Henry

26 COMMENTARY: The Danites went their way, and Micah, seeing they were too strong for him, turned back to his house, and Henry notes he had not courage to venture his life for gods he knew could not protect him, and if the loss cured him of loving idols, it was unspeakable gain. — Matthew Henry

27 COMMENTARY: They took Micah's things and his priest, came to Laish, to a quiet and secure people, smote them with the sword, and burnt the city with fire, and Henry notes many are brought to destruction by security, since Satan gains advantage when we are careless and off our watch. — Matthew Henry

28 COMMENTARY: There was no deliverer, because Laish was far from Zidon and had no business with any man, and it lay in the valley by Beth-rehob, and they built a city and dwelt there, rebuilding what they had burned. — Matthew Henry

29 COMMENTARY: They called the city Dan, after Dan their father, though its first name was Laish, and Henry notes the name witnessed that, though far separated, they were Danites by birth, and we should likewise preserve the remembrance of our relation to God's Israel. — Matthew Henry

30 COMMENTARY: The children of Dan set up the graven image, and Jonathan son of Gershom, son of Manasseh, he and his sons were priests to Dan until the day of the captivity, so that the stolen idolatry gained a settled priesthood. — Matthew Henry

31 COMMENTARY: They set up Micah's graven image all the time the house of God was in Shiloh, and Henry's chapter heading states the point plainly: how great a matter a little fire kindles, when idolatry creeps from one family into a tribe and gains settlement in a city of note. — Matthew Henry
