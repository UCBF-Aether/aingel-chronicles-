Study Notes — Judges

Chapter 13

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Israel again did evil, and the Lord delivered them to the Philistines forty years, and JFB notes this Philistine ascendency, reckoned from Elon's time to Samson's death, chastised the gross and confirmed idolatry described in chapter 10. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: There was a man of Zorah, of the Danites, named Manoah, whose wife was barren, and JFB notes Zorah lay on Judah and Dan's common boundary, near the Philistine border, where the deliverer was most needed. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: The angel of the Lord appeared to the woman and promised a son, and JFB identifies this messenger as the messenger of the covenant, the divine personage of so many earlier appearances. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: She was told to drink no wine or strong drink and eat no unclean thing, since the mother must practise the Nazarite abstinence for the sake of her promised offspring. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: She would conceive and bear a son, no razor coming on his head, for the child would be a Nazarite from the womb, and JFB notes he would only begin to deliver Israel, indicating the deliverance was still distant. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: The woman told her husband a man of God had come, whose countenance was like an angel's, very terrible, and she had not asked whence he was nor had he told his name, and JFB notes Manoah on hearing this made the promise a subject of earnest prayer, a remarkable instance of the connection between prayer and the fulfilment of promises. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: She repeated the angel's words, that she would bear a son who would be a Nazarite to God from the womb to his death, fixing the lifelong character of the promised child. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: Manoah entreated the Lord that the man of God would come again and teach them what to do for the child, and JFB traces this desire not to doubt but to lively faith and anxiety to follow the instructions exactly. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: God hearkened to Manoah, and the angel came again to the woman as she sat in the field, though Manoah was not with her, so that the answer came to the same person who had first received the promise. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: The woman made haste and ran to tell her husband that the man who had come before had appeared again, carrying the second visit to the husband who had prayed for it. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: Manoah arose, followed his wife, and asked the man whether he was the one who had spoken to the woman, and JFB notes his intense desire for the repetition was the fruit of faith, blessed was he who had not seen, yet had believed. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: Manoah asked how they should order the child and what they should do to him, seeking the practical rule for a Nazarite's upbringing. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: The angel answered that of all he had said to the woman she must beware, referring Manoah back to the original charge rather than adding a new one. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: She might eat nothing of the vine, drink no wine or strong drink, eat no unclean thing, and observe all that was commanded, the Nazarite discipline restated in full. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: Manoah asked the angel to stay until they had made ready a kid, but the stranger declined hospitality, saying if it were an offering it must be presented to the Lord, and JFB notes Manoah had meant to offer the viands to him as a prophet or human messenger, not yet knowing him as an angel. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: The angel added that though Manoah detained him he would not eat his bread, but if he offered a burnt offering he must offer it to the Lord, for Manoah did not know he was an angel of the Lord. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: Manoah asked the angel's name, so that they might honour him when his sayings came to pass, and JFB notes the request drew unequivocal proofs of divinity, in the name secret, or wonderful, and in the miraculous flame of the accepted sacrifice. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

18 COMMENTARY: The angel asked why Manoah asked after his name, seeing it is secret, or wonderful, and the unanswerable name itself became the proof Manoah sought. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

19 COMMENTARY: Manoah took a kid and a meat offering and offered it on a rock to the Lord, and the angel did wondrously while Manoah and his wife looked on, the offering becoming the occasion of revelation. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

20 COMMENTARY: As the flame went up from the altar toward heaven, the angel ascended in the flame, and Manoah and his wife fell on their faces to the ground, the miraculous flame betokening acceptance and revealing the visitor's nature. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

21 COMMENTARY: The angel did no more appear, and then Manoah knew he was an angel of the Lord, the departure itself completing the recognition. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

22 COMMENTARY: Manoah said they would surely die because they had seen God, expressing the ancient fear that a vision of the divine brings death. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

23 COMMENTARY: His wife answered that if the Lord were pleased to kill them he would not have received their offering, nor shown them all these things, nor told them such things as these, and her calm reasoning answered his fear with the logic of grace received. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

24 COMMENTARY: The woman bore a son and called his name Samson, and the child grew and the Lord blessed him, and JFB notes the child of promise, with his national services foretold, must from the first have been an object of peculiar interest and careful instruction. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

25 COMMENTARY: The Spirit of the Lord began to move Samson at times in the camp of Dan between Zorah and Eshtaol, and JFB explains this not as prophetic inspiration but as a spirit of high and devoted patriotism kindled in his youthful bosom, at places on the Judah-Dan border. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
