Study Notes — Judith

Chapter 11

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Then said Holofernes unto her, "Woman, be of good comfort, fear not in thine heart: for I never hurt any that was willing to serve Nabuchodonosor"; the Greek adds, "of all the earth" to the king's title. — George Leo Haydock

3 COMMENTARY: But now tell me wherefore thou art fled from them, and art come unto us, for thou art come for safeguard; be of good comfort, thou shalt live this night and hereafter; the Greek reads, "And art come to us? For thou art come for thy own safety. Take courage, for thou shalt live this night and henceforth, as no one shall hurt thee, but shall do thee good, as the servants of my lord, Nabuchodonosor, the king, are treated." — George Leo Haydock

4 COMMENTARY: For none shall hurt thee, but entreat thee well, as they do the servants of king Nabuchodonosor my lord — the same Greek assurance continues: no one shall hurt thee, but shall do thee good, as the king's servants are treated. — George Leo Haydock

5 COMMENTARY: Then Judith said unto him, "Receive the words of thy servant, and suffer thine handmaid to speak in thy presence, and I will declare no lie to my lord this night"; the Greek adds, "and I will tell no untruth to my lord this night, for," and so on. — George Leo Haydock

6 COMMENTARY: And if thou wilt follow the words of thine handmaid, God will bring the thing perfectly to pass by thee, and my lord shall not fail of his purposes; the Greek adds, "and none of the projects of my lord shall fail." All that Judith said was true in her sense, though the enemy did not understand her properly — so Josue lawfully entrapped the citizens of Hai (St. Augustine). — George Leo Haydock

7 COMMENTARY: As Nabuchodonosor king of all the earth liveth, and as his power liveth, who hath sent thee for the upholding of every living thing; she imitates Joseph (Genesis 42:15) and Eliseus (2 Kings 2:4) in swearing by the king's life, and speaks the truth, as the general had subjected many to his master. Her speech was not superstitious, but showed a civil respect. — George Leo Haydock

10 COMMENTARY: Therefore, O lord and governor, respect not his word, but lay it up in thine heart, for it is true: for our nation shall not be punished, neither can the sword prevail against them, except they sin against their God; the Greek intimates that she advised him to follow his counsel: "For our nation is not punished with the sword, unless they sin against their God." — George Leo Haydock

11 COMMENTARY: And now, that my lord be not defeated and frustrate of his purpose, even death is now fallen upon them, and their sin hath overtaken them, wherewith they have provoked their God to anger; the Greek continues, "But now let not my lord be at a loss what to do. For death has fallen upon them, and sin has taken possession of them, so that they have irritated God by their folly." This might also be true, though they had not fallen into idolatry (chapter 8:18) at Bethulia. — George Leo Haydock

12 COMMENTARY: For their victuals fail them, and all their water is scant, and they have determined to lay hands upon their cattle, and purposed to consume all those things, that God hath forbidden them to eat by his laws; they might have provisions, but they would be useless on account of excessive thirst. The Greek reads, "and all that God had forbidden them to eat by his laws, without making any distinction between the clean and unclean" — blood was prohibited even before the law (Genesis 9:4; Leviticus 17:14). — George Leo Haydock

13 COMMENTARY: And are resolved to spend the firstfruits of the tenths of wine and oil, which they had sanctified, and reserved for the priests that serve in Jerusalem; the Greek explains, "And the first-fruits of corn, and the tithes of wine and of oil, which they had reserved as sacred for the priests, standing in Jerusalem, before our God, they had decreed to consume; though none of the people ought so much as to touch these things." — George Leo Haydock

14 COMMENTARY: For they have sent some to Jerusalem, because they also that dwell there have done the like, to bring them a licence from the senate; the Greek continues, "Yet they have sent to obtain permission of the senate at Jerusalem, where the people have done the like." She insinuates that her countrymen are in the utmost distress and not much attached to religion, though in extreme want the distinction of meats is not to be enforced. — George Leo Haydock

15 COMMENTARY: Now when they shall bring them word, they will forthwith do it, and they shall be given to thee to be destroyed the same day; the Greek continues, "And when they shall have obtained an answer, they will fall to work, and then they will be abandoned to be destroyed by thee." — George Leo Haydock

16 COMMENTARY: Wherefore I thine handmaid, knowing all this, am fled from their presence, and God hath sent me to work things with thee, whereat all the earth shall be astonished; the Greek reads, "To perform with thee a feat which will astonish all who shall hear of these things," and, "My foreknowledge; and I was sent to inform thee" — she flatters Holofernes with the idea that he is under the special care of heaven. — George Leo Haydock

17 COMMENTARY: For thy servant is religious, and serveth the God of heaven day and night: now therefore, my lord, I will remain with thee, and thy servant will go out into the valley, and pray unto God; the Greek continues, "For thy handmaid is religious, and serveth the God of heaven day and night; and now my lord, I am" with thee, and adds, "in the night, in the valley" — she will go out by night into the valley to pray. — George Leo Haydock

18 COMMENTARY: And I will come and show it unto thee: then thou shalt go forth with all thine army, and there shall be none of them that shall resist thee; the Greek reads, "They shall have completed their sins," and adds, "and thou shalt go out with all thy forces, and none of them shall resist thee." — George Leo Haydock

19 COMMENTARY: And I will lead thee through the midst of Judea, until thou come before Jerusalem, and I will set thy throne in the midst thereof, and thou shalt drive them as sheep that have no shepherd, and a dog shall not so much as open his mouth at thee; the Greek adds, "and I will bring thee through Judea, over-against Jerusalem, and will place thy throne in the midst of it, and thou shalt drive them like sheep" — "dog" is proverbial (Exodus 11:7). All Israel was at rest after the death of Holofernes, and Judith brought his head in triumph through the country; the promise of dominion was ironical (1 Kings 22:15). — George Leo Haydock

22 COMMENTARY: Likewise Holofernes said unto her, "God hath done well to send thee before the people, that strength might be in our hands and destruction upon them that lightly esteem my lord"; the Greek reads, "To increase our strength, and to bring destruction on those who have despised my lord." — George Leo Haydock

23 COMMENTARY: And now thou art both beautiful in thy countenance, and witty in thy words: surely if thou do as thou hast spoken thy God shall be my God, and thou shalt dwell in the house of king Nabuchodonosor, and shalt be renowned through the whole earth; the Greek reads, "And now thou art comely in thy appearance, and good in thy discourse. If then thou perform what thou hast spoken, thy God shall," be my God. It is hardly probable that he spoke in earnest; perhaps he did not know that the true God allowed of no other, and being captivated with Judith's beauty and perceiving her attachment to religion, he had a mind to remove all her objections to his person. — George Leo Haydock

No note for verse(s) 2, 8, 9, 20, 21 — the public-domain sources consulted give no distinct explanation for these verses beyond the list itself, so nothing was invented for them.
