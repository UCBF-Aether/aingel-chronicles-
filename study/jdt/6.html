Study Notes — Judith

Chapter 6

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: When the tumult of men about the council was ceased, Holofernes spoke unto Achior and all the Moabites; the Greek adds, "before all the populace of the strangers" — Achior stood before all this company of foreigners. — George Leo Haydock

2 COMMENTARY: "And who art thou, Achior, and the hirelings of Ephraim, that thou hast prophesied against us as to day?" — the question is aimed at Achior before all the company of foreigners and the sons of Moab. — George Leo Haydock

3 COMMENTARY: He will send his power and destroy them from the face of the earth, and their God shall not deliver them; the Greek reads, "and who is God but Nabuchodonosor? He will" destroy them — they allow the title to none but their king, just as God will admit of no rival. Achior's generous advice was highly resented, as Charidemus, who spoke with like boldness to Darius when Alexander approached, was even put to death, though the king repented too late. — George Leo Haydock

7 COMMENTARY: Now therefore my servants shall bring thee back into the hill country and set thee in one of the cities of the passages; the Greek reads, "my slaves shall station thee on the mountainous country, and in one of the cities on the ascents, (of which thou hast so much spoken)." — George Leo Haydock

8 COMMENTARY: And thou shalt not perish till thou be destroyed with them; the Greek continues, "and thou shalt not perish till thou perish with them." — George Leo Haydock

9 COMMENTARY: And if thou persuade thyself that they shall be taken, let not thy countenance fall: I have spoken it, and none of my words shall be in vain; the Greek continues, "But if thou believest in thy heart that they will not be taken, let not thy countenance fall. I have spoken, and nothing that I have said shall be without effect." — George Leo Haydock

10 COMMENTARY: Then Holofernes commanded his servants to take Achior and bring him to Bethulia and deliver him into the hands of the children of Israel; Bethulia is placed by travellers, on uncertain traditions, in the tribe of Zebulon about three miles west of Tiberias, but St. Jerome places it near Egypt, and Scripture speaks of Bethul in the tribe of Simeon (Joshua 19:4), to which Judith and the ancients belonged. Holofernes left his camp near Scythopolis (chapter 7:1) when he proceeded to attack the southern countries. — George Leo Haydock

15 COMMENTARY: The governors were in those days Ozias son of Micha of the tribe of Simeon, and Chabris son of Gothoniel, and Charmis son of Melchiel; the Greek adds, "Chabris, the son of Othoniel, and Charmis, the son of Melchiel" — the former is in effect mentioned in chapter 8:9. — George Leo Haydock

16 COMMENTARY: And they called together all the ancients of the city, and all their youth ran together, and their women, to the assembly, and they set Achior in the midst; "the church" here is the synagogue or place where they met in prayer — such places were established particularly after the captivity, though some have denied there were any synagogues even in the days of the Maccabees (see Matthew 4:23; Esther 4:16). — George Leo Haydock

19 COMMENTARY: "O Lord God of heaven, behold their pride, and pity the low estate of our nation, and look upon the face of those that are sanctified unto thee this day" — God will not fail to reward those who trust in him, and to humble the presumptuous; "saints" is in Syriac "sanctuary," and the Greek reads only, "Look upon the face of the persons (or things) sanctified unto thee on this day." — George Leo Haydock

20 COMMENTARY: Then they comforted Achior and praised him greatly; the Greek continues, "And they consoled Achior, and praised him greatly." — George Leo Haydock

21 COMMENTARY: And Ozias took him out of the assembly unto his house and made a feast to the elders, and they called on the God of Israel all that night for help; the Greek continues, "And Ozias took him from the assembly to his own house, and made a feast (literally, drinking) for the ancients, and they invoked the God of Israel to assist them during all that night." — George Leo Haydock

No note for verse(s) 4, 5, 6, 11, 12, 13, 14, 17, 18 — the public-domain sources consulted give no distinct explanation for these verses beyond the list itself, so nothing was invented for them.
