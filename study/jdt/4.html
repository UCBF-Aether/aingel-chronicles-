Study Notes — Judith

Chapter 4

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: The children of Israel in Judea heard what Holofernes had done; Judea is whither those had retreated who had escaped the fury of the Assyrians under Tiglath-pileser and others. — George Leo Haydock

2 COMMENTARY: They were exceedingly afraid for Jerusalem and for the temple of the Lord their God, because surrendering would not secure these things, so that their very life and religion were at stake; resistance in other circumstances would have been rashness. — George Leo Haydock

3 COMMENTARY: They were newly returned from captivity, and all the people of Judea lately gathered together, with vessels, altar, and house purified from profanation — the Greek adds that they were "recently come up from captivity, and lately all the people had been collected out of Judea, and the vessels, and the altar, and the house, had been purified from profanation" to which they had been exposed under Manasses, who with several subjects had been made prisoner and was now at Babylon, or, if returned, was wholly taken up with his soul's concerns and committed affairs to Eliachim. — George Leo Haydock

4 COMMENTARY: They sent into all the coasts of Samaria and the villages, though Samaria was an enemy's country, as the danger was common; Ezechias and Josias seemed to claim a right over the country (2 Chronicles 30:1; 34:6, 33). — George Leo Haydock

5 COMMENTARY: They possessed themselves beforehand of the tops of the high mountains, fortified the villages, and laid up victuals; the Greek adds, "as the harvest was just ended" — the timing of their preparation. — George Leo Haydock

6 COMMENTARY: Joacim the high priest in Jerusalem wrote to them that dwelt in Bethulia and Betomestham, over against Esdrelon and the plain near Dothaim, giving orders to seize the mountainous ascents by which there was a passage into Judea; it was easy to stop their progress, as the passage was narrow and would not admit above two men at once — recalling Leonidas with his 300 Spartans resisting Xerxes' millions at Thermopylae. — George Leo Haydock

7 COMMENTARY: He charged them to keep the passages of the hill country, for by them there was an entrance into Judea and it was easy to stop those who would come up; the Jews attempted to defend these defiles, having received the orders before the enemy encamped near Scythopolis. — George Leo Haydock

8 COMMENTARY: The children of Israel did as Joacim commanded, with the ancients of all the people; the Greek adds "and the senate of all the people of Israel, which sat at Jerusalem," and Serarius thinks Manasses, busy fortifying the city, had committed the rest of the kingdom to Eliachim, though it is more probable he was at Babylon. — George Leo Haydock

10 COMMENTARY: Both they and their wives, children, cattle, strangers, hirelings, and bought servants put on sackcloth; the Greek adds "and their little ones, and beasts, and every sojourner, and hired servant and slave. They put sackcloth on their loins" — the very beasts were covered with sackcloth to denote the general sorrow. — George Leo Haydock

11 COMMENTARY: Thus every man and woman, the little children, and the inhabitants of Jerusalem fell before the temple and cast ashes upon their heads; the Greek adds that "every man, woman, and child, and the inhabitants of Jerusalem, fell prostrate before the temple, and sprinkled ashes on their heads." — George Leo Haydock

12 COMMENTARY: They cried to the God of Israel with one consent earnestly that he would not give their children for a prey and their wives for a spoil; "off" is literally "divided" from their husbands, or among the victors. — George Leo Haydock

13 COMMENTARY: So God heard their prayers and looked upon their afflictions; the Greek adds, "and God heard their petition, and rescued them from their affliction; and all the people fasted many days in all Judea and Jerusalem, before the sanctuary of the Lord almighty." — George Leo Haydock

14 COMMENTARY: Joacim the high priest and all the priests that stood before the Lord and ministered unto him had their loins girt with sackcloth; the Greek adds that they "spread their sackclothes before the Lord, and arrayed the altar with sackcloth," and the high priest Joachim and all the priests, having loins girded with sackcloth, offered the accustomed holocaust and the vows and presents of the people, with ashes upon their caps — a spectacle that must have made an impression on the most obdurate. — George Leo Haydock

15 COMMENTARY: They had ashes on their mitres and cried unto the Lord with all their power that he would look upon all the house of Israel graciously; the sackcloth dress follows the advice of Joel 1:13. — George Leo Haydock

No note for verse(s) 9 — the public-domain sources consulted give no distinct explanation for these verses beyond the list itself, so nothing was invented for them.
