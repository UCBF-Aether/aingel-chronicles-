Study Notes — Judith

Chapter 12

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Then he commanded to bring her in where his plate was set, and bade that they should prepare for her of his own meats, and that she should drink of his own wine; the Greek reads, "and ordered a bed (or table) to be prepared for her, to eat of his own meat, and drink of his wine," that her beauty might be enhanced (Daniel 1) and to honour her. — George Leo Haydock

2 COMMENTARY: And Judith said, "I will not eat thereof, lest there be an offence: but provision shall be made for me of the things that I have brought"; "upon me" — as these meats had been offered to idols, or she might fear they had, and wished to avoid all scandal (2 Maccabees 6:21; Tobit 1:12). — George Leo Haydock

3 COMMENTARY: Then Holofernes said unto her, "If thy provision should fail, how should we give thee the like? for there be none with us of thy nation"; the Greek adds, "for there is none of thy race." — George Leo Haydock

4 COMMENTARY: Then said Judith unto him, "As thy soul liveth, my lord, thine handmaid shall not spend those things that I have, before the Lord work by mine hand the things that he hath determined"; on "which I," the Greek reads "he" — a pronoun variant in the versions. — George Leo Haydock

5 COMMENTARY: Then the servants of Holofernes brought her into the tent, and she slept till midnight, and she arose when it was toward the morning watch; the Greek reads, "and she slept till midnight and she arose at the morning watch." The mode of counting by hours prevailed after the captivity. — George Leo Haydock

6 COMMENTARY: And sent to Holofernes, saying, "Let my lord now command that thine handmaid may go forth unto prayer"; the Greek reads, "and she sent to Holofernes, saying: May my lord please to order that thy handmaid may go out to pray." She had prepared him to grant this request before, but out of civility, and that he may have greater confidence in her, she asks again. The choice of a retired place for prayer is very commendable, but she made choice of the fields, that she might go out of the camp afterwards without being suspected. — George Leo Haydock

7 COMMENTARY: Then Holofernes commanded his guard that they should not stay her: thus she abode in the camp three days, and went out in the night into the valley of Bethulia, and washed herself in a fountain of water by the camp; "chamberlains" are in Greek "life-guards, not to hinder her; and she remained in the camp three days," and "water" is in Greek "in the camp" — perhaps she washed only her hands and face. — George Leo Haydock

9 COMMENTARY: So she came in clean, and remained in the tent, until she did eat her meat at evening; "pure" means from forbidden food, and "evening" — thus she continued to fast, to draw down the blessing of God. — George Leo Haydock

10 COMMENTARY: And in the fourth day Holofernes made a feast to his own servants only, and called none of the officers to the banquet; the Greek adds, "only, and he did not call any of those whom he usually employed," that they might not witness his excesses. Vagao, or Bagoas, is the Persian name for an "eunuch," or chief officer, though such were generally to wait on the ladies. — George Leo Haydock

11 COMMENTARY: Then said he to Bagoas the eunuch, who had charge over all that he had, "Go now, and persuade this Hebrew woman which is with thee, that she come unto us, and eat and drink with us"; the Greek and Syriac add, "who was appointed over all his affairs. Persuade the Hebrew woman who is with thee to come to us, and to eat and drink with us. For we deem it shameful to dismiss such a woman, without having commerce with her; and if we do not attract her, she will deride us." — George Leo Haydock

12 COMMENTARY: For, lo, it will be a shame for our person, if we shall let such a woman go, not having had her company; for if we draw her not unto us, she will laugh us to scorn; "merry" would pave the way for greater liberties. The Greek adds, "and to become this day as a daughter of the Assyrians, standing to wait in the house of Nabuchodonosor" — probably alluding to those courtezans who sung at night before the palace. The Persians admitted women to their feasts, though they were generally excluded in the East (Esther 1:12; Herodotus v. 18). — George Leo Haydock

14 COMMENTARY: Then said Judith unto him, "Who am I now, that I should gainsay my lord? surely whatsoever pleaseth him I will do speedily, and it shall be my joy unto the day of my death"; "best" is in Greek "a boast, or matter of exultation." This answer seems to shock our delicacy: did she not understand the eunuch's meaning, which was sufficiently plain? She only passed a compliment, which always implies a tacit condition, if the thing be practicable and honest, as the words might have two meanings, and she was bound in charity to interpret them in the best sense. It was surely lawful to be merry. — George Leo Haydock

15 COMMENTARY: So she arose, and decked herself with her apparel and all her woman's attire, and her maid went and laid soft skins on the ground for her over against Holofernes, and she sat down; the Greek adds, "and all her female ornaments; and her servant came and spread on the ground, before Holofernes, the fleeces which she had received from Bagoas, for her daily use, to eat lying upon them. And coming in, Judith fell prostrate." The custom of sitting on the ground upon skins to eat is very ancient (Targum, Esther 1) and is still observed by the Turks, and the kings of Persia let none eat with them at the same table. The character of drunkenness with which this nation has been branded seems not unfounded. — George Leo Haydock

16 COMMENTARY: Now when Judith came in and sat down, Holofernes his heart was ravished with her, and his mind was moved, and he desired greatly her company; for he waited a time to deceive her; the Greek adds, "company; and he had sought an opportunity of deluding her, from the day when he first beheld her." He justly, therefore, fell into the snare which he had laid. — George Leo Haydock

20 COMMENTARY: And Holofernes took great delight in her, and drank more wine than he had drunk at any time in one day since he was born; the Greek adds, "in any one day," at supper. — George Leo Haydock

No note for verse(s) 8, 13, 17, 18, 19 — the public-domain sources consulted give no distinct explanation for these verses beyond the list itself, so nothing was invented for them.
