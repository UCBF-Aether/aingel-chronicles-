Study Notes — Judith

Chapter 7

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

2 COMMENTARY: Then their strong men removed their camps, and the army of men of war was an hundred and seventy thousand footmen and twelve thousand horsemen; the numbers vary by version — Greek seventy (thousand footmen?), Syriac sixty-two, and for horsemen Greek twelve as in chapter 2:7, Syriac twenty-two — and some texts must be inaccurate, though Holofernes' cavalry might have increased since he began the war. "Taken" is in Syriac "servants, and those who had joined them in great numbers." — George Leo Haydock

3 COMMENTARY: And they camped in the valley near unto Bethulia, by the fountain, spreading in breadth over Dothaim even to Belmaim; the hill side lay on the coast of the Mediterranean, leaving the mountains of Ephraim, which were so strait, on the left, as well as Jerusalem, as he intended to go into Egypt, and Bethulia alone opposed his progress (chapter 6:7). — George Leo Haydock

4 COMMENTARY: Now the children of Israel, when they saw the multitude, were greatly troubled, and said every one to his neighbour, "Now will these men lick up the face of the earth"; the Greek adds, "were greatly afraid, and each one said to his neighbour: Now these will consume (Complutensian, shut up) the face of the earth, and neither the high mountains, nor the vales, nor the hills, will bear their weight." — George Leo Haydock

5 COMMENTARY: Then every man took up his weapons of war, and when they had kindled fires upon their towers, they remained and watched all that night; the Greek continues, "Then taking their arms, and lighting fires upon their towers, they kept guard all that night" — the servants of God first humble themselves, and then take arms, confiding in God. — George Leo Haydock

6 COMMENTARY: But in the second day Holofernes brought forth all his horsemen in the sight of the children of Israel in Bethulia; the Greek continues, "but the second day, Holofernes brought out all his cavalry against the Israelites, in Bethulia." — George Leo Haydock

7 COMMENTARY: And viewed the passages up to the city, and came to the fountains of their waters, and took them, and set garrisons of men of war over them; the Greek continues that he "reconnoitred the ascent of their city, and came to the fountains of their waters, and took them; and leaving a guard of soldiers, he returned to his men." An aqueduct is implied, so the fountain must have been on higher ground than the city, though the city was on an eminence — yet water may be forced to ascend by pipes. The Syriac is silent about the aqueduct and the small springs. — George Leo Haydock

8 COMMENTARY: Then came unto him all the chief of the children of Esau, and all the governors of the people of Moab, and the captains of the sea coast; the Greek and Syriac read "the chiefs of the sons of Esau," as Idumea had been conquered (chapter 3:14), omitting Ammon, and after Moab subjoining "the generals of the maritime countries," Tyrians and Philistines. — George Leo Haydock

17 COMMENTARY: So the camp of the children of Ammon departed, and with them five thousand of the Assyrians, and they pitched in the valley, and took the waters, and the fountains of the waters of the children of Israel; the Greek and Syriac intimate that the Ammonites and Moabites, with 5,000 Assyrian infantry, guarded the springs, while the rest of the army was stationed in the plain, for it was agreed only to blockade the place, that no men might be lost and the army might be ready to march against the more distant and powerful nations of Egypt. — George Leo Haydock

18 COMMENTARY: Then the children of Esau went up with the children of Ammon, and camped in the hill country over against Dothaim; the Greek and Syriac specify over-against Dothaim, which seems too remote, though such an immense army might cover a great part of the country, and another detachment was "southward, at Ecrebel (Syriac Ekarbat, probably Akrabim, the ascent of the scorpions) near Cush (or Arabia), which is above the torrent Mochmur (Syriac Peor, or Bezor), and the rest of the army of the Assyrians encamped in the plain, and covered the face of the earth." — George Leo Haydock

20 COMMENTARY: Thus all the company of Assur remained about them, both footmen, chariots, and horsemen, four and thirty days, so that all their vessels of water failed; the Greek reads "thirty-four days," the Old Vulgate "twenty-four days," and the Syriac "two months and four days." — George Leo Haydock

21 COMMENTARY: And the cisterns were emptied, and they had not water to drink their fill for one day, for they gave them drink by measure; "measure" marks the rationing under the blockade. — George Leo Haydock

22 COMMENTARY: Therefore their young children were out of heart, and their women and young men fainted for thirst, and fell down in the streets of the city; the Greek adds, "the infants, women, and young men fainted, and fell down dead." — George Leo Haydock

26 COMMENTARY: Now therefore call them unto you, and deliver the whole city for a spoil to the people of Holofernes and to all his army; the Greek reads, "call them, and give up all the city to plunder." — George Leo Haydock

28 COMMENTARY: We take to witness against you the heaven and the earth, and our God and Lord of our fathers, which punisheth us according to our sins and the sins of our parents; the Greek reads, "and the sins of our parents, that he may not treat us thus to-day," and deliver us up to death. — George Leo Haydock

30 COMMENTARY: Then said Ozias to them, "Brethren, be of good courage, let us yet endure five days, in the which space the Lord our God may turn his mercy toward us"; the Greek reads, "And Ozias said to them: Take courage, brethren." On "five," Sulpitius reads fifteen, but instead of decim we should read demum. — George Leo Haydock

32 COMMENTARY: And he dispersed the people, every one to their own charge, and they went unto the walls and towers of their city, and sent the women and children into their houses; the Greek and Syriac add, "and he dispersed the people to their tents, and they went upon the walls and towers of the city, and sent their wives and children home. And they were under great humiliation in the city." — George Leo Haydock

No note for verse(s) 1, 9, 10, 11, 12, 13, 14, 15, 16, 19, 23, 24, 25, 27, 29, 31 — the public-domain sources consulted give no distinct explanation for these verses beyond the list itself, so nothing was invented for them.
