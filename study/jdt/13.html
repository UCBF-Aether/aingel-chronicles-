Study Notes — Judith

Chapter 13

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Now when the evening was come, his servants made haste to depart, and Bagoas shut his tent without, and dismissed the waiters from the presence of his lord, and Judith was left alone; "doors" are in Greek "without," yet so that Judith could open them. — George Leo Haydock

2 COMMENTARY: And Judith was left alone in the tent, and Holofernes lying along upon his bed: for he was filled with wine; "drunk" is in Greek "drowned in wine." — George Leo Haydock

3 COMMENTARY: Now Judith had commanded her maid to stand without her bedchamber, and to wait for her coming forth, as she did daily: for she said she would go forth to her prayers; "the" is in Greek "her chamber," and "watch" is in Greek, "her coming out, as on other days; for she had said she would go out to pray, and she had told this to Bagoas." — George Leo Haydock

4 COMMENTARY: So all went forth and none was left in the bedchamber, neither little nor great. Then Judith, standing by his bed, said in her heart, "O Lord God of all power, look at this present upon the works of mine hands for the exaltation of Jerusalem"; the Greek continues, "And all had departed," and so on, and all these precautions were necessary. She seems not to have disclosed her secret even to her companion, taking the whole upon herself; "lips" is in Greek "in her heart" — yet the Jews generally use some words, thinking that prayer too cold which is barely mental. — George Leo Haydock

5 COMMENTARY: For now is the time to help thine inheritance, and to execute thine enterprises to the destruction of the enemies which are risen against us; "raise up" — if Bethulia had been taken, perhaps Jerusalem had also fallen. — George Leo Haydock

6 COMMENTARY: Then she came to the pillar of the bed, which was at Holofernes' head, and took down his fauchion from thence; "pillar" is in Greek kanoni, "the rule," or the pole on which the arms were hung, and the "sword" (acinace) was used by the Persians and shorter than the Greek or Roman sword. If she had not been inspired by God to act thus, like Aod (Judges 3), it would be difficult to excuse her in thus hurrying a man, in the most wretched state, before the judgment-seat of God. — George Leo Haydock

9 COMMENTARY: And tumbled his body down from the bed, and pulled down the canopy from the pillars; and anon after she went forth, and gave Holofernes his head to her maid; the "canopy" was pulled down to convince all that it was the head of Holofernes, and the "body" was left on the ground (chapter 14:14), perhaps to cut off the head more easily, and that it might appear more ghastly at the first entrance. — George Leo Haydock

10 COMMENTARY: And she put it in her bag of meat: so they twain went together according to their custom unto prayer: and when they passed the camp, they compassed the valley, and came to the gate of the city; "while" — having composed herself, and done everything necessary to prevent detection. — George Leo Haydock

11 COMMENTARY: Then said Judith afar off, to the watchmen at the gate, "Open, open now the gate: God, even our God, is with us, to show his power yet in Jerusalem, and his force against the enemy, as he hath done this day"; the Greek repeats, "open ye now the gate; God, our God is with us, still to show power in Israel, and strength against the enemies, as he hath done to-day." — George Leo Haydock

12 COMMENTARY: Now when the men of her city heard her voice, they made haste to go down to the gate of their city, and they called the elders of the city; the Greek reads, "they hastened to descend to the gate of their city, and called," and so on. — George Leo Haydock

13 COMMENTARY: And then they ran all together, both small and great, for it was strange unto them that she was come: so they opened the gate, and received them, and made a fire for a light, and stood round about them; the Greek reads, "it was a paradox to them that she should come, and they opened the gate, and received them." — George Leo Haydock

14 COMMENTARY: Then she said to them with a loud voice, "Praise, praise God, praise God, I say, for he hath not taken away his mercy from the house of Israel, but hath destroyed our enemies by mine hand this night"; the Greek reads, "said to them with a loud voice: Praise God, praise, yea praise God, because he hath not withdrawn his mercy from the house of Israel, but hath destroyed our enemies by my hand this night." — George Leo Haydock

16 COMMENTARY: As the Lord liveth, who hath kept me in my way that I went, my countenance hath deceived him to his destruction, and yet hath he not committed sin with me, to defile and shame me; the Greek reads, "for my face has deluded him to his ruin, and he has not committed sin with me, for defilement and shame." Judith's guardian angel defended her, as Jacob's did him (Genesis 48). — George Leo Haydock

17 COMMENTARY: Then all the people were wonderfully astonished, and bowed themselves and worshipped God, and said with one accord, "Blessed be thou, O our God, which hast this day brought the enemies of thy people to nought"; the Greek continues, "And all the people were much amazed; and bowing down, they adored the Lord, and said, with one accord: Our God, thou art blessed, because this day thou hast brought the enemies of thy people to nought." — George Leo Haydock

18 COMMENTARY: Then said Ozias unto her, "O daughter, blessed art thou of the most high God above all the women upon the earth; and blessed be the Lord God, which hath created the heavens and the earth"; "prince" is Ozias, prince in that town, though Manasses was king, and the Greek reads simply, "Ozias said." — George Leo Haydock

20 COMMENTARY: And God turn these things to thee for a perpetual praise, to visit thee in good things because thou hast not spared thy life for the affliction of our nation, but hast revenged our ruin by walking in the right way before our God; "praise" is in Greek "hope," and "for that" is in Greek, "and may God make these things turn to thy eternal exaltation, to regard thee for good; because thou hast not" spared thy life. "Ruin" is in Greek, "walking aright in," the right way. — George Leo Haydock

No note for verse(s) 7, 8, 15, 19 — the public-domain sources consulted give no distinct explanation for these verses beyond the list itself, so nothing was invented for them.
