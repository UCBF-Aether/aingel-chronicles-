Study Notes — Judith

Chapter 9

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Judith fell upon her face, and put ashes upon her head, and uncovered the sackcloth wherewith she was clothed, about the time the incense of the evening was offered in Jerusalem; "oratory" is of such as our Saviour speaks (Matthew 6), and Baronius treats at large. The Greek reads, "But Judith fell prostrate, and sprinkled ashes upon her head (Syriac adds, and tore her tunic) and uncovered the sackcloth which she had on. That evening the incense had just been offered, in the house of the Lord, at Jerusalem. And Judith cried aloud." — George Leo Haydock

2 COMMENTARY: "O Lord God of my father Simeon, to whom thou gavest a sword to take vengeance of the strangers, who loosened the girdle of a maid to defile her" — the justice of God is here praised in punishing by Simeon's sword the crime of the Sichemites, not the fact of Simeon, which was justly condemned by his father (Genesis 49:5). We may distinguish between his zeal against the crime of the ravishers, which may be considered just, and the manner of punishing it, which was irregular and excessive; the former is here commended, yet Simeon was not blameless, and God put the sword into his hand as he makes use of tyrants, in which sense Nabuchodonosor is styled his servant (Jeremiah 25:9; Ezekiel 29:18). — George Leo Haydock

3 COMMENTARY: Wherefore thou gavest their rulers to be slain, so that they dyed their bed in blood, being deceived, and smotest the servants with their lords, and the princes upon their thrones; the Greek reads, "For thou didst say: It shall not be so: and they did it because thou hadst given their princes unto slaughter, and their bed, which had perceived their deceit, unto blood; and thou didst slay the slaves with the princes, and the princes on their thrones; and thou gavest," and so on. This style seems rather poetical. — George Leo Haydock

5 COMMENTARY: For thou hast wrought not only those things, but also the things which fell out before, and which ensued after; thou hast thought upon the things which are now, and the things to come; the Greek reads, "before these, and these, and what followed, and is at present, and things to come, thou hast foreseen." All events depend on thee: "For the things which thou hadst decreed were at hand, and said: Behold, we are here." — George Leo Haydock

6 COMMENTARY: Yea, what things thou didst determine were ready at hand, and said, "Lo, we are here": for all thy ways are prepared, and thy judgments are in thy foreknowledge; "judgments" — all is foreseen, and nothing can resist the decrees of God. — George Leo Haydock

7 COMMENTARY: For, behold, the Assyrians are multiplied in their power; they are exalted with horse and man; they glory in the strength of their footmen; they trust in their shield, and spear, and bow, and sling, and know not that thou art the Lord that breakest the battles; the Greek speaks not of the Egyptians: "For behold the Assyrians are multiplied in their power, and exalted on account of their cavalry; they have boasted on the strong arm of the infantry, have trusted in their shield, and bow, and sling; and they have not known that thou art the Lord, making an end of wars: Thy name is Lord; break their force, by thy power," and so on. — George Leo Haydock

9 COMMENTARY: Behold their pride, and send thy wrath upon their heads: give into mine hand, which am a widow, the power that I have conceived; the Greek reads, "Give that power which I have devised to the hand of me, a widow." She meant innocently to engage the affections of Holofernes, and prays that he may receive her in that manner, as he might have done without sin; but when he abused his free-will, God turned his sin to the good of others (see Exodus 7). — George Leo Haydock

10 COMMENTARY: Smite by the deceit of my lips the servant with the prince, and the prince with the servant: break down their stateliness by the hand of a woman; the Greek reads, "Strike the slave by the lips of my delusion (or by my ensnaring words) with the prince; and the chief, with his minister, break their haughtiness by the hand of a female." This prayer seems contrary to sound morality, as does Judith's indiscreet conduct in exposing herself to danger, though her intention was good — but are not stratagems lawful in war (see 2 Kings 15)? Is it sinful for a woman to endeavour to captivate the heart? The situation might be dangerous, but it was not criminal, and Judith was certainly determined to yield to no dishonest proposals; God therefore approved of her designs, and enhanced her beauty (chapter 10:4). — George Leo Haydock

11 COMMENTARY: For thy power standeth not in multitude nor thy might in strong men: for thou art a God of the afflicted, an helper of the oppressed, an upholder of the weak, a protector of the forsaken, a Saviour of them that are without hope; the Greek reads, "the potent, but thou art the Lord of the humble." — George Leo Haydock

13 COMMENTARY: And make my speech and deceit to be their wound and stripe, who have purposed cruel things against thy covenant, and thy hallowed house, and against the top of Sion, and against the house of the possession of thy children; the Greek reads, "give my word and deceit to be a wound and a scar to them, who, against thy covenant and sanctified house, and the summit of Sion, have devised cruel things." — George Leo Haydock

14 COMMENTARY: And make every nation and tribe to acknowledge that thou art the God of all power and might, and that there is none other that protecteth the people of Israel but thee; the Greek continues, "and do for all thy nation according to thy power and strength; for there is no other to shield Israel but Thou." — George Leo Haydock

No note for verse(s) 4, 8, 12 — the public-domain sources consulted give no distinct explanation for these verses beyond the list itself, so nothing was invented for them.
