Study Notes — Judith

Chapter 14

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Then said Judith unto them, "Hear me now, my brethren, and take this head, and hang it upon the highest place of your walls"; "walls" is in Greek "upon the pinnacle." — George Leo Haydock

2 COMMENTARY: And so soon as the morning shall appear, and the sun shall come forth upon the earth, take ye every one his weapons, and go forth every valiant man out of the city, and set a captain over them, as if ye would go down into the field toward the advanced guard of the Assyrians; "beneath" means into the valley, but make your appearance just out of the walls, as if rushing upon the enemy. The Greek adds after "arms," "and you shall appoint a leader over them, as if descending into the plain to the advanced guard of the Assyrians; but ye shall not go down." — George Leo Haydock

3 COMMENTARY: Then they shall take their armour, and shall go into their camp, and raise up the captains of the army of Assur, and shall run to the tent of Holofernes, and shall not find him; the Greek continues, "Then these seizing all their armour, will go to the camp, and awaken the leaders of the Assyrians, and they will run to the tent of Holofernes, and shall not find him." — George Leo Haydock

4 COMMENTARY: So ye, and all that inhabit the coast of Israel, shall pursue them, and overthrow them as they go; the Greek continues, "Then fear shall seize upon them, and they will flee before us. Whereupon ye, and all who inhabit the confines of Israel, shall follow after, and strew them in their paths," slain and wounded. — George Leo Haydock

5 COMMENTARY: But before ye do these things, call me Achior the Ammonite, that he may see and know him that despised the house of Israel, and that sent him to us as to death; the Greek reads, "But before ye do this, call to me Achior, the Ammonite, that seeing, he may recognize the contemner of the house of Israel, and the man who sent him away to us unto certain death." — George Leo Haydock

6 COMMENTARY: Then they called Achior out of the house of Ozias; and when he was come, and saw the head of Holofernes in a man's hand in the assembly of the people, he fell down; the Greek reads, "And they called Achior from the house of Ozias. But as soon as he came, and saw the head of Holofernes in the hand of one of the assembly of the people, he fell," as in chapter 13:29, 30. — George Leo Haydock

7 COMMENTARY: But when they had recovered him, he fell at Judith's feet, and reverenced her, and said, "Blessed art thou in all the tabernacles of Juda, and in all nations, which hearing thy name shall be astonished"; the Greek continues, "Blessed art thou in every tent of Juda, and in every nation. All who shall hear thy name shall be amazed." "Reverenced" is literally "adored," a word often used in this sense. — George Leo Haydock

8 COMMENTARY: Now therefore tell me all the things that thou hast done in these days. Then Judith declared unto him in the midst of the people all that she had done, from the day that she went out until that same day; the Greek continues, "But now tell me what thou hast done during these days. And Judith, in the midst of the people, informed him of all that she had performed, since the day of her departure till the time when she was speaking." — George Leo Haydock

9 COMMENTARY: And when she had left off speaking, the people shouted with a loud voice, and made a joyful noise in their city; the Greek continues, "She had no sooner ended her speech, than all the people set up a loud shout of joy in their city. Then," and so on. — George Leo Haydock

10 COMMENTARY: And when Achior had seen all that the God of Israel had done, he believed in God greatly, and circumcised the flesh of his foreskin, and was joined unto the house of Israel unto this day; the Greek reads, "He believed in God greatly, or with the utmost sincerity." Hitherto Achior had only considered the Deity as the peculiar God of Israel, or as the common gods revered by all nations, but not as the only supreme Being; he now becomes a proselyte, not merely, like Naaman, to adore one God, but also to observe all the rites of the Jewish religion. It is certain that no nation could be forbidden to worship God (Deuteronomy 23:3), and St. Thomas Aquinas believes a dispensation was granted to Achior, so that he might be treated like an Israelite in all respects. "With" is in Greek "till this day." — George Leo Haydock

11 COMMENTARY: And as soon as the morning arose, they hanged the head of Holofernes upon the wall, and every man took his weapons, and they went forth by bands unto the ascent of the mountain; "with" is in Greek, "In companies, to the ascent of the mountain." — George Leo Haydock

12 COMMENTARY: But when the Assyrians saw them, they sent to their leaders, which came to their captains and tribunes, and to every one of their rulers; the Greek reads, "Sent to their officers. But they went to the leaders and captains of thousands, and to every chief among them." — George Leo Haydock

13 COMMENTARY: So they came to Holofernes' tent, and said to him that had the charge of all his things, "Waken now our lord: for the slaves have been bold to come down to fight against us"; the Greek continues, "and they proceeded to the tent of Holofernes, and said to his chief steward: Awaken now our lord, for the slaves have been so bold as to come down to offer us battle for their utter ruin." They speak contemptuously of their foes, like the Philistines (1 Samuel 14:11). — George Leo Haydock

14 COMMENTARY: Then went in Bagoas, and knocked at the door of the tent; for he thought that he had slept with Judith; the Greek reads, "In, knocked at the hall (aule, or antichamber) of the tent, for he suspected that he was sleeping with Judith." The curtain separated this apartment from the rest and hung before the door, as Heraclides describes the tent of the Persian kings; Holofernes assumed the like importance and was treated almost as a deity, hence he must not be awaked in a rude manner, and civility requires that we should knock modestly at the doors of the great. — George Leo Haydock

15 COMMENTARY: But because none answered, he opened it, and went into the bedchamber, and found him cast upon the floor dead, and his head was taken from him; the Greek continues, "But as no one answered, he opened and went into the bed-chamber, and found him thrown upon the floor, dead, and his head taken from him, and he cried," and so on. — George Leo Haydock

16 COMMENTARY: Therefore he cried with a loud voice, with weeping, and sighing, and a mighty cry, and rent his garments; the Greek reads, "cried aloud, the slaves have prevaricated." — George Leo Haydock

17 COMMENTARY: After he went into the tent where Judith lodged: and when he found her not, he leaped out to the people, and cried — perhaps he thought that Judith had introduced some of the Jews. — George Leo Haydock

18 COMMENTARY: These slaves have dealt treacherously; one woman of the Hebrews hath brought shame upon the house of king Nabuchodonosor: for, behold, Holofernes lieth upon the ground without a head; the Greek reads, "cried aloud, the slaves have prevaricated. One" woman of the Hebrews hath brought this shame upon the house of the king. — George Leo Haydock

19 COMMENTARY: When the captains of the Assyrians' army heard these words, they rent their coats and their minds were wonderfully troubled, and there was a cry and a very great noise throughout the camp; "cry" is in Greek, "and shouting of them (the captains) in," the camp. — George Leo Haydock
