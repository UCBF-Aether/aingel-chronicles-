Study Notes — Judith

Chapter 3

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: The Greek reads that they "sent to him ambassadors, with proposals for peace, saying: Behold we, the servants of Nabuchodonosor, the great king, lay before thee; use us as thou thinkest best"; these proposals were made by those on the sea-coast, from Sidon and Tyre to Ascalon, from whom Holofernes took the choicest men, being received with honour. Sobal, or Soba, lies near Damascus, and "Lybia" here is probably Lycia or Lydia, which Holofernes had cut in pieces. — George Leo Haydock

2 COMMENTARY: "Great" was Nabuchodonosor's usual title (Daniel 2:30; 2 Kings 18:19), and the submission offered is unconditional slavery — yet they judge it better to submit to servitude than to be slaughtered like their brethren. — George Leo Haydock

3 COMMENTARY: "At thy disposal" (Genesis 13:9; Proverbs 15:11): houses, places, fields of wheat, flocks, herds, and tents are all laid before Holofernes' face, to use as seems good to him. — George Leo Haydock

7 COMMENTARY: The reception with garlands, dances, and timbrels is testified by lights: torches were used to testify joy (2 Maccabees 4:22). — George Leo Haydock

8 COMMENTARY: Yet he cast down their frontiers and cut down their groves, for he had decreed to destroy all the gods of the land; the Greek reads, "He demolished all their borders, and cut down their groves," and, "Worshipped by all nations, and that all their tongues and tribes might call him god" — behold to what lengths ambition may be carried, and he is a figure of Antichrist, for whom all heretics pave the way (2 Thessalonians 2). — George Leo Haydock

9 COMMENTARY: He came over against Esdraelon near Judea, over against the great strait of Judea; the Greek reads, "He came to Esdrelon, near Dotaia (or Dothaim, north of Sichem), which is opposite the great saw of Judea," the range of Ephraim's mountains, and the kings of Israel had fortified the defiles (1 Kings 15:17). The order of conquests is not strictly observed, for while the commander-in-chief was in one place his generals were dispersed elsewhere, and he had now advanced as far as Gabaa of Saul when, meditating an attack upon Egypt, he concentrated his forces, little expecting such a check at Bethulia. — George Leo Haydock

10 COMMENTARY: He pitched between Geba and Scythopolis and tarried a whole month to gather together all the carriages of his army; the Greek reads, "encamped between Gaba and Scythopolis (Bethsan, Joshua 17:11), and he was there a month of days, to collect all the burden of his army." — George Leo Haydock

No note for verse(s) 4, 5, 6 — the public-domain sources consulted give no distinct explanation for these verses beyond the list itself, so nothing was invented for them.
