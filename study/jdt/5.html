Study Notes — Judith

Chapter 5

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: It was declared to Holofernes that Israel had prepared for war and shut up the passages of the hill country; the Greek adds that they "had fortified every summit of a high mountain, and had placed scandals in the plains," obstructing the passage with ditches, trees, and snares of every description. — George Leo Haydock

2 COMMENTARY: He was very angry and called all the princes of Moab, captains of Ammon, and governors of the sea coast; the Greek adds "and the satraps of the maritime country," the Philistines, who had submitted like the rest. — George Leo Haydock

3 COMMENTARY: He said, "Tell me now, ye sons of Chanaan, who this people is"; the Greek adds, "Ye sons of Chanaan tell" — he was not acquainted with their origin, for the title belonged only to the Phoenicians, who might also be present. — George Leo Haydock

4 COMMENTARY: He asked why they had determined not to come and meet him more than all the inhabitants of the west; the Greek reads "west," which seems more accurate unless Holofernes was on the sea-coast, and he was not absolutely unacquainted with the Jews but spoke in contempt, wishing to know if they had formed a league with the Egyptians. — George Leo Haydock

5 COMMENTARY: Then said Achior, captain of all the sons of Ammon, "Let my lord now hear a word from the mouth of thy servant"; the Greek reads "the mouth of thy slave." — George Leo Haydock

6 COMMENTARY: "This people are descended of the Chaldeans": Abraham was a native of Ur (Genesis 12), and this tended to conciliate the favour of the general, who was of the same country. — George Leo Haydock

7 COMMENTARY: They sojourned heretofore in Mesopotamia because they would not follow the gods of their fathers in Chaldea; the Greek adds that they knew the God of heaven, "and they (the Chaldean idolaters) cast them out from the face of the gods, and they fled into Mesopotamia, and dwelt there many days," about two years. — George Leo Haydock

8 COMMENTARY: For they left the way of their ancestors and worshipped the God of heaven, the God whom they knew, so they cast them out from the face of their gods — the same Greek addition explains the expulsion from Chaldea. — George Leo Haydock

9 COMMENTARY: Then their God commanded them to depart from where they sojourned and go into the land of Chanaan, where they dwelt and were enriched; the Greek adds, "and their God commanded them to leave their abode, and to go into the land of Chanaan; and they dwelt there, and were enriched," and "there they became innumerable." — George Leo Haydock

11 COMMENTARY: Therefore the king of Egypt rose up against them and dealt subtilly with them, bringing them low with labouring in brick; "oppressed" is in the Greek "craftily inveigled them" (Exodus 1:10). — George Leo Haydock

12 COMMENTARY: Then they cried unto their God, and he smote all the land of Egypt with incurable plagues, so the Egyptians cast them out of their sight; the Greek adds, "for which there was no remedy. And the Egyptians cast them out from among them." — George Leo Haydock

13 COMMENTARY: And God dried the Red Sea before them; the Greek continues, "And God dried up the Red Sea before them, and conducted them to Mount Sinai and Cades-Barne." — George Leo Haydock

14 COMMENTARY: And brought them to Mount Sina and Cades-Barne, and cast forth all that dwelt in the wilderness; the Greek adds that God "cast out all the inhabitants of the desert" — attested in Deuteronomy 32:10 and Jeremiah 2:6. — George Leo Haydock

15 COMMENTARY: So they dwelt in the land of the Amorites, and destroyed by their strength all them of Esebon, and passing over Jordan possessed all the hill country; the Greek adds that they "dwelt in the land of the Amorrhites, and exterminated all the Hesebon, by their power. Then crossing the Jordan, they took as their inheritance all the mountainous parts." — George Leo Haydock

16 COMMENTARY: And they cast forth before them the Chanaanite, Pherezite, Jebusite, Sychemite, and all the Gergesites, and dwelt in those lands; the Greek adds that they "ejected the Chanaanite, Pherezite, Jebusite, Sichem, and all the Gergesites, and dwelt therein many days," and the environs of Sichem belonged to the Hevites; they overcame in the days of Josue, at Jericho and elsewhere. — George Leo Haydock

18 COMMENTARY: But when they departed from the way which he appointed them, they were destroyed in many battles very sore and led captives into a land that was not theirs; he seems to speak of the captivity of Manasses, of the ten tribes, and of the loss sustained by Achaz (2 Chronicles 28:5), and the Greek reads, "exceedingly; and they were led captive into a land which was not their own, and the temple of their God became as a pavement, and their cities were taken by the enemies." — George Leo Haydock

19 COMMENTARY: But now are they returned to their God and are come up from the places where they were scattered, and have possessed Jerusalem, where their sanctuary is, and are seated in the hill country; the Greek continues, "But now, returning to their God, they are come up from the places to which they had been scattered, and have possession of Jerusalem, where is their sanctuary; and they have inhabited the mountainous country, for it was a desert." Adversaries infer from this that the siege of Bethulia could not have taken place till after the Babylonian captivity, and many Catholics allow it, but the proof is not conclusive, as all this might be verified under Manasses, when the temple was trampled on and profaned. — George Leo Haydock

21 COMMENTARY: But if there be no iniquity in their nation, let my lord now pass by, lest their Lord defend them, and their God be for them, and we become a reproach; the Greek reads, "let my lord indeed pass by, lest their Lord should cover them with a shield, for their God is for them." — George Leo Haydock

22 COMMENTARY: And when Achior had finished these sayings, all the people standing round about the tent murmured, and the chief men of Holofernes and all that dwelt by the sea side and in Moab spake; the Greek reads, "all the people round the tent murmured; and the great men of Holofernes, and all who dwelt in the maritime country, and in Moab, threatened to cut him to pieces." — George Leo Haydock

23 COMMENTARY: For, say they, we will not be afraid of the face of the children of Israel, for lo, it is a people that have no strength nor power for a strong battle; the Greek continues, "For we shall not fear the children of Israel. Lo! a people without power, strength, or army in battle array!" — George Leo Haydock

24 COMMENTARY: Now therefore, lord Holofernes, we will go up, and they shall be a prey to be devoured of all thine army; the Greek continues, "We shall then go up, and they shall be for food to all thy army, lord Holofernes." — George Leo Haydock

No note for verse(s) 10, 17, 20 — the public-domain sources consulted give no distinct explanation for these verses beyond the list itself, so nothing was invented for them.
