Study Notes — 2 Peter

Chapter 3

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: This second epistle, Peter writes to stir up his readers' pure minds by way of remembrance. Henry notes purified minds are to be stirred up so believers are active and lively in the work of holiness. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

2 COMMENTARY: Readers are to be mindful of the words spoken before by the holy prophets and of the commandment of the apostles of the Lord and Saviour. Henry's design for the chapter is to remind believers of Christ's final coming to judgment, grounded in that prophetic and apostolic word. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

3 COMMENTARY: In the last days scoffers will come, walking after their own lusts. Henry describes them as men who make light of sin and mock at salvation by Jesus Christ. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

4 COMMENTARY: The scoffers ask, "Where is the promise of his coming?" arguing that since the fathers fell asleep all things continue as from the beginning of creation. Henry notes one principal article of faith rests only on a promise, and scoffers will attack it until the Lord comes; because they see no changes, they fear not God. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

5 COMMENTARY: The scoffers are willingly ignorant that by God's word the heavens were of old, and the earth stood out of the water and in the water. Henry answers that had they considered God's past vengeance, they would not scoff at his threatening an equally terrible judgment. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

6 COMMENTARY: By that word the world that then was, overflowed with water, perished. Henry points to the flood as proof God once swept away a whole world of ungodly men at once. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

7 COMMENTARY: The present heavens and earth are by the same word kept in store, reserved unto fire against the day of judgment and perdition of ungodly men. Henry declares this destruction by fire as sure as the truth and power of God can make it. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

8 COMMENTARY: One day is with the Lord as a thousand years, and a thousand years as one day. Henry explains that in God's account there is no difference: all things past, present, and future are ever before him, and a delay of a thousand years is less to him than putting off a thing for a day is to us. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

9 COMMENTARY: The Lord is not slack concerning his promise, but is longsuffering to us-ward, not willing that any should perish but that all should come to repentance. Henry stresses what men count slackness is longsuffering, giving God's people more time to advance in knowledge, holiness, and good works. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

10 COMMENTARY: The day of the Lord will come as a thief in the night, when the heavens pass away, the elements melt, and the earth and its works are burned up. Henry warns that palaces and all desirable things of worldly-minded men shall be burned, and asks what will become of us if we make this earth our portion, seeing all these things shall be dissolved. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

11 COMMENTARY: Seeing all these things shall be dissolved, what manner of persons ought we to be in holy conversation and godliness? Henry infers from the second coming a call to purity: very exact and universal holiness is enjoined, not resting in any low measure. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

12 COMMENTARY: Believers look for and hasten unto the coming of the day of God, when the heavens on fire shall be dissolved and the elements melt with fervent heat. Henry notes this is the effect of real knowledge, pressing believers toward godliness. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

13 COMMENTARY: According to his promise we look for new heavens and a new earth, wherein dwelleth righteousness. Henry says true Christians look for this new world freed from present vanity and pollution, and only those clothed with Christ's righteousness and sanctified by the Holy Ghost shall dwell there. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

14 COMMENTARY: Seeing we look for such things, we must be diligent to be found of him in peace, without spot, and blameless. Henry warns never to expect to be found in peace at that day if lazy and idle in this day, in which we must finish the work given us to do. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

15 COMMENTARY: We are to account the longsuffering of our Lord as salvation, as our beloved brother Paul also wrote according to the wisdom given him. Henry urges a right use of the Lord's patience, who as yet delays his coming. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

16 COMMENTARY: In all his epistles Paul speaks of these things, in which are some things hard to be understood, which the unlearned and unstable wrest to their own destruction. Henry notes proud, carnal men wrest some things into agreement with wicked doctrines, but this is no reason to lay aside Paul's epistles or any Scripture, since men left to themselves pervert every gift of God. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

17 COMMENTARY: Seeing we know these things before, we must beware lest, being led away with the error of the wicked, we fall from our own stedfastness. Henry warns those led away by error fall from their stedfastness, and believers must disclaim and abhor opinions not warranted by God's law. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

18 COMMENTARY: Instead we are to grow in grace and in the knowledge of our Lord and Saviour Jesus Christ, to whom be glory now and for ever. Henry closes with the remedy against error: labour to know Christ more clearly and fully, so as to be more like him and love him better, giving him thanks and praise in full assurance of doing the same hereafter. — Matthew Henry (Concise)
