Study Notes — 2 Peter

Chapter 1

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Simon Peter, servant and apostle of Jesus Christ, writes to those who have obtained like precious faith through the righteousness of God and our Saviour Jesus Christ. Henry notes faith unites the weak believer to Christ as really as the strong, and every sincere believer is justified by it in God's sight. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

2 COMMENTARY: Grace and peace are multiplied to believers through the knowledge of God and of Jesus our Lord. Henry stresses that in Christ all fulness dwells, and pardon, peace, grace, and knowledge are given through the Holy Spirit. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

3 COMMENTARY: God's divine power has given us all things that pertain to life and godliness, through the knowledge of him who called us to glory and virtue. Henry urges turning these promises into prayers for the transforming grace of the Holy Spirit. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

4 COMMENTARY: By exceeding great and precious promises we become partakers of the divine nature, escaping the corruption in the world through lust. Henry says these promises should make us inquire whether we are really renewed in the spirit of our minds. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

5 COMMENTARY: Beside this, believers are to give all diligence to add virtue to their faith. Henry notes faith worketh godliness and produces effects no other grace in the soul can produce. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

6 COMMENTARY: To virtue we must add knowledge, increasing acquaintance with the whole truth and will of God; to knowledge, temperance, moderation about worldly things; to temperance, patience, cheerful submission to the will of God; and to patience, godliness. Henry explains that tribulation worketh patience, whereby we bear calamities and crosses with silence and submission. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

7 COMMENTARY: To godliness we must add brotherly kindness, tender affection to fellow Christians as children of the same Father, servants of the same Master, and heirs of the same inheritance; and to brotherly kindness, charity. Henry stresses this family bond among believers, travellers to the same country. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

8 COMMENTARY: If these graces be in us and abound, we shall be neither barren nor unfruitful in the knowledge of our Lord Jesus Christ. Henry presents this fruitfulness as the proof that faith is alive and working godliness. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

9 COMMENTARY: Whoever lacks these things is blind and cannot see afar off, having forgotten he was purged from his old sins. Henry contrasts such forgetfulness with the diligence that keeps the believer mindful of cleansing grace. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

10 COMMENTARY: We are therefore to give diligence to make our calling and election sure, for by doing these things we shall never fall. Henry says labouring for assurance by believing and well-doing is itself a firm argument of God's grace upholding us so we shall not utterly fall. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

11 COMMENTARY: So an entrance shall be ministered abundantly into the everlasting kingdom of our Lord and Saviour Jesus Christ. Henry promises the diligent a triumphant entrance into that kingdom where Christ reigns and they shall reign with him for ever. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

12 COMMENTARY: Peter will therefore always put his readers in remembrance of these things, though they know them and are established in the present truth. Henry notes we must be established in the truth, especially the truth necessary for our own day, so as not to be shaken by every wind of doctrine. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

13 COMMENTARY: As long as Peter is in this tabernacle, he thinks it meet to stir his readers up by putting them in remembrance. Henry pictures the body as but a tabernacle, or tent, of the soul — a mean and movable dwelling. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

14 COMMENTARY: Peter knows he must shortly put off this tabernacle, as the Lord Jesus Christ has showed him. Henry observes the nearness of death made the apostle diligent in the business of life, and nothing gives composure in death's prospect like knowing we have faithfully followed the Lord Jesus. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

15 COMMENTARY: Peter will endeavour that after his decease his readers may always have these things in remembrance. Henry notes that by the written word, those who fear the Lord and talk of his loving-kindness are enabled to spread the knowledge of the Lord. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

16 COMMENTARY: Peter insists they did not follow cunningly devised fables when they made known Christ's power and coming, but were eyewitnesses of his majesty. Henry declares the gospel is no weak thing but comes in power, and it is the sight of Jesus crucified that heals the soul. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

17 COMMENTARY: Christ received honour and glory from God the Father when the voice from the excellent glory said, "This is my beloved Son, in whom I am well pleased." Henry notes God is well pleased with Christ, and with us in him, the promised Messiah through whom believers are accepted. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

18 COMMENTARY: Peter and his companions heard this voice when they were with Christ in the holy mount. Henry treats this eyewitness testimony, joined to prophecy, as the firm ground of faith. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

19 COMMENTARY: We have also a more sure word of prophecy, to which we do well to take heed, as to a light shining in a dark place until the day dawn and the day star arise in our hearts. Henry likens Scripture's light, darted into the blind mind by the Holy Spirit, to daybreak diffusing itself through the soul till it makes perfect day. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

20 COMMENTARY: No prophecy of Scripture is of any private interpretation. Henry explains that the prophets and penmen of the Old Testament spoke and wrote under the influence and direction of the Spirit of God, so every man ought to search Scripture to understand its sense and meaning. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

21 COMMENTARY: Prophecy came not in old time by the will of man, but holy men of God spoke as they were moved by the Holy Ghost. Henry concludes the Scriptures are to be accounted the words of the Holy Ghost, to be esteemed and reverenced as a book written by holy men taught by him. — Matthew Henry (Concise)
