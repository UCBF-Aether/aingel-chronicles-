Study Notes — 2 Peter

Chapter 2

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: As false prophets once rose among the people, so false teachers will secretly bring in destructive heresies, even denying the Lord who bought them. Henry warns that the way of error is a hurtful way, yet many are always ready to walk in it. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

2 COMMENTARY: Many will follow the seducers' pernicious ways, and the way of truth will be evil spoken of because of them. Henry urges believers to give the enemy no occasion to blaspheme the holy name by which they are called. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

3 COMMENTARY: Through covetousness the seducers will use feigned words to make merchandise of their followers, but their judgment does not linger. Henry notes such deceivers are condemned already, and the wrath of God abides upon them. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

4 COMMENTARY: God did not spare the angels that sinned, but cast them down to hell in chains of darkness reserved for judgment. Henry draws the lesson that if creatures sin even in heaven, they must suffer in hell, for sin is the work of darkness and darkness its wages. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

5 COMMENTARY: God did not spare the old world, but brought the flood on the ungodly while saving Noah, the eighth person and a preacher of righteousness. Henry notes that neither the number nor the quality of offenders procures favour: where sin is universal, punishment likewise extends to all. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

6 COMMENTARY: Sodom and Gomorrha were turned into ashes and condemned with an overthrow, as an ensample to those who would live ungodly afterward. Henry observes that God can at once turn a fruitful land into barrenness, and no plans or politics can keep judgments from a sinful people. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

7 COMMENTARY: Just Lot, vexed with the filthy conversation of the wicked, was delivered out of Sodom. Henry notes that when God sends destruction on the ungodly, he commands deliverance for the righteous. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

8 COMMENTARY: Dwelling among the wicked, the righteous man vexed his soul day by day with their unlawful deeds, in seeing and hearing. Henry observes that in bad company we cannot but get either guilt or grief, yet a child of the Lord may retain integrity among the most profane, since the grace of Christ in him is stronger than temptation. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

9 COMMENTARY: The Lord knows how to deliver the godly out of temptations and to reserve the unjust for punishment at the day of judgment. Henry adds that wicked men often escape suffering here precisely because they are kept to that day, to be punished with the devil and his angels. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

10 COMMENTARY: Chiefly punished are those who walk after the flesh in the lust of uncleanness and despise government, presumptuous and self-willed, unafraid to speak evil of dignities. Henry describes such seducers as giving themselves up to their own fleshly minds, refusing to bring every thought to the obedience of Christ, and despising those whom God has set in authority. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

11 COMMENTARY: Angels, greater in power and might, bring no railing accusation against dignities before the Lord, in contrast to the seducers' boldness. Henry's point is that those who despise God-set authority presume further than even mightier angels dare. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

12 COMMENTARY: Like natural brute beasts made to be taken and destroyed, the seducers speak evil of things they understand not and shall perish in their own corruption. Henry notes they walk after the flesh, going on in sinful courses and increasing to greater degrees of impurity and wickedness. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

13 COMMENTARY: They shall receive the reward of unrighteousness, counting it pleasure to riot in the daytime, spots and blemishes sporting in their own deceivings while they feast with believers. Henry observes that outward temporal good things are the wages such sinners expect and promise themselves. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

14 COMMENTARY: Having eyes full of adultery that cannot cease from sin, they beguile unstable souls, with hearts exercised in covetous practices, cursed children. Henry warns that none have more cause to tremble than those bold to gratify sinful lusts by presuming on divine grace and mercy. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

15 COMMENTARY: Having forsaken the right way, they have gone astray following the way of Balaam, who loved the wages of unrighteousness. Henry's account of the seducers fits here: they leave the straight road for the wages sin promises, speaking lightly of the restraints of God's law. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

16 COMMENTARY: Balaam was rebuked for his iniquity when the dumb ass, speaking with a man's voice, forbade the madness of the prophet. Henry's warning stands behind the example: Christians should stand at a distance from such presumptuous teachers rather than follow their folly. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

17 COMMENTARY: These are wells without water, clouds carried with a tempest, for whom the mist of darkness is reserved for ever. Henry explains that deceivers are empty because there is no truth in them, and as they increase darkness in this world, the mist of darkness is justly their portion in the next. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

18 COMMENTARY: With great swelling words of vanity they allure through fleshly lusts and wantonness those who had clean escaped from error. Henry notes that as clouds hinder the light of the sun, so these darken counsel by words wherein there is no truth. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

19 COMMENTARY: While they promise liberty, they are themselves servants of corruption, for whoever is overcome is brought into bondage. Henry observes that in the midst of their talk of liberty, these men are the vilest slaves, their own lusts gaining complete victory over them. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

20 COMMENTARY: If those who escaped the pollutions of the world through knowing Christ are again entangled and overcome, their latter end is worse than the beginning. Henry warns that when men are entangled they are easily overcome, so Christians should keep close to the word of God and watch against all who seek to bewilder them. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

21 COMMENTARY: It had been better not to have known the way of righteousness than, having known it, to turn from the holy commandment delivered to them. Henry states plainly that a state of apostasy is worse than a state of ignorance, and bringing an evil report on the good way exposes a person to the heaviest condemnation. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

22 COMMENTARY: The true proverb has happened to them: the dog returns to his own vomit, and the washed sow to her wallowing in the mire. Henry calls the state dreadful, yet adds that such a case is not utterly hopeless: the leper may be made clean, and the backslider who grieves should believe in the Lord Jesus and be saved. — Matthew Henry (Concise)
