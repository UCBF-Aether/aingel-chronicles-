Study Notes — Baruch

Chapter 3

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

3 COMMENTARY: To remain for ever is asked as a plea, whether so short-lived a creature is an object worthy of eternal wrath, with Job and the Psalms urging the same brevity of man against the length of anger. — George Leo Haydock

4 COMMENTARY: The dead of Israel are the banished, or the ancient patriarchs, whose names are urged as a motive, as Moses pleaded them, and people in sin and misery seem dead, yet may revive by grace. — George Leo Haydock

5 COMMENTARY: Think upon thy hand means save us by thy power, and for thy name's sake, as Josue pleaded, so the prayer asks God to act for his own hand and name, not for the people's merit. — George Leo Haydock

8 COMMENTARY: The offence is in the Greek a debt, since parents might sell their children, and God threatens the rebellious with being forced to borrow, so captivity itself is described as a debt owed for the iniquities of the fathers. — George Leo Haydock

9 COMMENTARY: Hear opens the second part, in which God replies, and the Church has long read this passage as holy scripture on the eves of Easter and Pentecost, since it is an instruction in true wisdom, found in God alone and in the people to whom he communicates it. — George Leo Haydock

11 COMMENTARY: Grown old is read by some as growing, since five years had not elapsed, yet the exiles already knew their captivity would be long, and defiled with the dead means they were as if buried, dwelling among defilements as if near a dead body. — George Leo Haydock

13 COMMENTARY: Peace belongs only to observers of the law, for there is none for the wicked, and for ever is read by many as upon the land, conformably to the Greek, so the promise of dwelling in peace is tied to walking in God's way. — George Leo Haydock

15 COMMENTARY: The treasures are the place of wisdom, and the question, who has found her place, exposes how few are truly wise and virtuous, since the great men of the world missed their aim, while Israel received the law, yet shows little fruit of it. — George Leo Haydock

16 COMMENTARY: The princes of the nations are those of Assyria and like empires, who esteemed themselves lords of nature and delighted in hunting, with beasts and birds subject to them, yet none of their dominion found wisdom. — George Leo Haydock

18 COMMENTARY: The works are unsearchable in the sense that the hoarders shall not find the fruit of their works, since silver and gold heaped without end yield nothing that lasts. — George Leo Haydock

20 COMMENTARY: The young men are heirs of those conquerors, equally in the dark, so the wisdom the fathers missed is missed by the sons as well, and the way of knowledge is not inherited with the empire. — George Leo Haydock

22 COMMENTARY: Theman is the capital city of Edom, where Eliphaz was king, and the Phoenicians, so famous for industry, had no idea of true wisdom, no more than the Ismaelites, so neither commerce nor royal counsel found her. — George Leo Haydock

23 COMMENTARY: Agar is the mother of the Ismaelites, and Merrha probably in Arabia, while the tellers of fables may be reproached or commended, since those nations delighted in enigmatical discourses, but instructors of mankind deserve praise, not those who devise false gods for lucre. — George Leo Haydock

24 COMMENTARY: The possession is all the world, which belongs to God, yet how few partake of his wisdom, so the vastness of God's house only sharpens the question of why so few enter into its wisdom. — George Leo Haydock

26 COMMENTARY: The giants are those before and after the deluge, renowned and expert in war, yet Noe and Israel were preferred before them, since God chose not the giants and they did not find the way of knowledge. — George Leo Haydock

29 COMMENTARY: The clouds are invoked because no human industry can procure wisdom from above, since God alone dispenses it, and his grace makes the discovery easy, as the yoke is made easy in the Gospel. — George Leo Haydock

32 COMMENTARY: Evermore is literally in the eternal time, or so long ago, designed to continue while kingdoms change, so the earth prepared by God outlasts every empire that rises upon it. — George Leo Haydock

33 COMMENTARY: Trembling describes the sun, which stops, goes back, or withdraws its light at God's command, as at Josue's bidding and at the passion, so light itself obeys with trembling. — George Leo Haydock

34 COMMENTARY: The watches are the stations of the stars, which are like God's soldiers, shining with cheerfulness when called, and answering, here we are, to him that made them. — George Leo Haydock

37 COMMENTARY: The verse is read by most of the Fathers as the incarnation, since afterwards he was seen upon earth and conversed with men, and the prophets speak of things to come as past to express certainty, while Christ, as the wisdom of God, is the same wisdom given to Jacob and Israel. — George Leo Haydock

No note for verse(s) 1, 2, 6, 7, 10, 12, 14, 17, 19, 21, 25, 27, 28, 30, 31, 35, 36 — the public-domain sources consulted give no distinct explanation for these verses beyond the list itself, so nothing was invented for them.
