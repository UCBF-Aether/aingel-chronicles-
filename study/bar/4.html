Study Notes — Baruch

Chapter 4

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Ever means true wisdom may be found in the law, which constitutes the happiness of Israel, and Christ perfected and fulfilled it, while life is promised to keepers of the law, since Moses promised temporal blessings, yet the faithful would be rewarded eternally. — George Leo Haydock

3 COMMENTARY: Give not thy honour to another nation means be faithful, lest another take thy place, which Christians have done, so the honour of possessing the law is lost by unfaithfulness and given to another. — George Leo Haydock

5 COMMENTARY: The memorial is in the Greek literally, O memorable Israel, and ye are left to support and restore the nation, so this part of the letter is for the comfort of the remnant. — George Leo Haydock

6 COMMENTARY: Sold means sold like slaves, or people taken in war, not for destruction, but because the people provoked God to wrath and were delivered to their adversaries. — George Leo Haydock

8 COMMENTARY: God is literally him, in the Greek the Eternal, and the verse is taken from Deuteronomy, while Jerusalem is beautifully personified as a widow who nursed her children, so forgetting God is also grieving the mother who reared them. — George Leo Haydock

9 COMMENTARY: Near is in the Hebrew daughters of Sion, so all that dwell near Sion are summoned to hear Jerusalem's mourning over the captivity of her sons and daughters. — George Leo Haydock

15 COMMENTARY: The nation from afar is of a strange tongue, as the Chaldeans are described in Deuteronomy and Isaias, a wicked nation that neither reverences the ancient nor pities children. — George Leo Haydock

16 COMMENTARY: The children are in the Greek daughters, and many of both sexes were taken with Jechonias, while Jerusalem comforts her children and assures them of God's mercy even as she stands childless. — George Leo Haydock

20 COMMENTARY: Peace is the robe of prosperity put off, and sackcloth is the rough garment of penitents in public distress, so Jerusalem exchanges the robe of peace for the sackcloth of supplication. — George Leo Haydock

25 COMMENTARY: The neck is the place where the enemy's foot rests, and Mardochai and Daniel were governors, so the promise that the children shall get up upon the enemy's neck was verified in part then, and more fully when the Gentiles embraced the Gospel. — George Leo Haydock

28 COMMENTARY: When is in the Greek, now ten times as much, being converted, seek him, so the return seeks God ten times as much as the going astray sought idols, and the Jews became much more docile and attached to the law. — George Leo Haydock

30 COMMENTARY: Named means God named Jerusalem as his servant, spouse and intimate friend, so the exhortation to be of good heart rests on the name God himself gave her. — George Leo Haydock

31 COMMENTARY: The wicked are in the Greek, wretched those who have injured thee and rejoiced in thy fall, wretched the cities that received thy sons, so the curse falls both on the afflicters and on the cities that profited from the captivity. — George Leo Haydock

32 COMMENTARY: She is Babylon, and in the Greek, wretched she that received thy sons, for Babylon shall fall a prey to Cyrus, as she rejoiced at Jerusalem's ruin and was glad of her fall. — George Leo Haydock

35 COMMENTARY: The fire is the fire of war, and the devils are those said to dwell in ruins, whether really or in common opinion, so Babylon's desolation is described as a haunt of devils for a great time. — George Leo Haydock

37 COMMENTARY: East and west means from Babylon and from the islands, as Isaias and Zacharias describe the gathering, so the children come gathered from east even to west at the word of the Holy One. — George Leo Haydock

No note for verse(s) 2, 4, 7, 10, 11, 12, 13, 14, 17, 18, 19, 21, 22, 23, 24, 26, 27, 29, 33, 34, 36 — the public-domain sources consulted give no distinct explanation for these verses beyond the list itself, so nothing was invented for them.
