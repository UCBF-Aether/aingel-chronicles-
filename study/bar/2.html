Study Notes — Baruch

Chapter 2

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

2 COMMENTARY: Heaven is invoked because after the death of Josias the nation was exposed to the greatest misery, its kings imprisoned first by the Egyptians and then by the Chaldeans, with surprising changes in eight or nine years. — George Leo Haydock

3 COMMENTARY: The daughter eaten is not mentioned elsewhere in sacred history for this time, except in the siege of Samaria, though captives of Israel now at Babylon under the same king may have suffered it, and all distinction of the kingdoms is now at an end, while the prayer greatly resembles that of Daniel. — George Leo Haydock

4 COMMENTARY: The kings are those of Egypt and Babylon, while the Ammonites and their neighbours derided the scattered people, so the reproach comes from every side. — George Leo Haydock

5 COMMENTARY: Brought under, and not uppermost, is a proverbial expression from Deuteronomy, describing a people made the tail and not the head because they sinned by not obeying God's voice. — George Leo Haydock

9 COMMENTARY: The Lord watched over us for evil, that is, watched to punish, for when he spares he seems to slumber, so the watching here is a watching for judgment, not for protection. — George Leo Haydock

12 COMMENTARY: God's justices are so called because God's law is frequently called justice, since its observance makes men just, so the confession of sin against his justices is a confession against the law that justifies. — George Leo Haydock

16 COMMENTARY: The holy house is the temple, or the prayer is heard from heaven, so looking down from the holy house means either the temple on earth or the dwelling above. — George Leo Haydock

17 COMMENTARY: The dead in hell shall not give glory and justice to the Lord, that is, they shall not acknowledge and glorify his justice as penitent sinners do upon earth, and all in the grave are incapable of making known God's perfections, which is why the prayer appeals to his glory. — George Leo Haydock

18 COMMENTARY: The phrase of evil is not in the Greek, and a soul which bears grandeur with pain, like Esther, and humbles itself before God, is most graciously received, while failing by fasting marks the dispositions required for prayer. — George Leo Haydock

19 COMMENTARY: The fathers are joined in the Greek by kings, and prayers are rendered mercy, for the exiles boast not of good works, trusting not in the just works of their fathers or of themselves to receive pity, since mercy here denotes prayer or humiliation. — George Leo Haydock

21 COMMENTARY: To serve the king of Babylon is as Jeremias repeatedly admonished, so the command to bow the shoulder and serve is the prophet's own counsel, not a new doctrine. — George Leo Haydock

23 COMMENTARY: Without any footstep of inhabitants means the land shall become a desert, as Isaias and Lamentations describe, with no voice of mirth or of bridegroom or bride left in it. — George Leo Haydock

24 COMMENTARY: The place is explained by the soldiers ransacking the tombs in hopes of plunder, and Joakim was left unburied, which would be very afflicting to the Jews, since the bones of kings and fathers were removed from their resting places. — George Leo Haydock

25 COMMENTARY: Banishment is rendered captivity in the Syriac, or pestilence inflicted by God, so the deaths by famine, sword and banishment cover every form of the judgment. — George Leo Haydock

26 COMMENTARY: The day refers to the temple whose vessels were taken away, with few left to attend, and the prophet foresees the event, so the desolation of the temple is spoken of as already present. — George Leo Haydock

30 COMMENTARY: The heart means to be seriously converted, as the prophets had foretold, so turning to the heart in captivity is conversion itself, not merely a change of place. — George Leo Haydock

33 COMMENTARY: The neck is literally the back, insensible to stripes, so the stiff neck is a back that no longer feels the blows that should have corrected it. — George Leo Haydock

34 COMMENTARY: Thereof is literally of them, though the other reading is more conformable to the Greek, a textual point that does not change the promise of return to the land sworn to the fathers. — George Leo Haydock

35 COMMENTARY: Another covenant is in the Greek an everlasting covenant, by Christ, prefigured by the covenant Nehemias renewed, for the law of Moses was in force till Christ came, and his law continues unto the end. — George Leo Haydock

No note for verse(s) 1, 6, 7, 8, 10, 11, 13, 14, 15, 20, 22, 27, 28, 29, 31, 32 — the public-domain sources consulted give no distinct explanation for these verses beyond the list itself, so nothing was invented for them.
