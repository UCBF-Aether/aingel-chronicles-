Study Notes — Baruch

Chapter 1

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: The book was written in Babylon, where Jechonias was in prison, in the fifth year, and Haydock notes that Baruch accompanied his brother Saraias, so the letter comes from the exiles themselves, not from distant onlookers. — George Leo Haydock

2 COMMENTARY: The fifth year is counted from the taking of the king, and the taking of the city was effected at intervals over eleven years, in the fifth of which this book was written, while priests and a temple still stood at Jerusalem. — George Leo Haydock

3 COMMENTARY: Jechonias was in prison, where the Jews met to hear the words of Jeremias, or the king might hear the letter in private, so the reading reached both the captive king and the people gathered to hear. — George Leo Haydock

4 COMMENTARY: The river Sodi is read as the great river Euphrates, the Hebrew word denoting pride, with other readings naming the city Sura there, so the exiles are placed by the river of Babylon. — George Leo Haydock

7 COMMENTARY: Joakim, or Eliacim, is the high priest named here, and Haydock notes the dispute whether there was a pontiff then, since the text only says that he was priest. — George Leo Haydock

8 COMMENTARY: Sivan is a Syriac month corresponding to May, and the vessels are silver vessels, which Sedecias had made to supply the place of the golden vessels taken away, so the return of vessels is a partial restoration, not the full treasure. — George Leo Haydock

9 COMMENTARY: The Greek reads prisoners where the Latin reads all, so the carrying away is of bound prisoners, princes and mighty men and people of the land, brought bound to Babylon. — George Leo Haydock

10 COMMENTARY: The meat offering is literally manna in the Septuagint rendering, or mincha, an oblation of bread, wine and the like, and the Greek adds for sin before frankincense, so the money is sent to buy the full range of offerings. — George Leo Haydock

11 COMMENTARY: Prayer for the king is prompted by gratitude and duty, as Jeremias commanded prayer for Babylon, and Baltassar is named as the king's son, with the prayer that their days may be as the days of heaven, as long and prosperous as possible, in the customary salutation, O king, live for ever. — George Leo Haydock

12 COMMENTARY: Enlightened eyes mean either an end to the captivity or a hatred of sin, and the days are those of God's decree, to which the exiles submit, preferring service under the Chaldeans to service under other foreign nations. — George Leo Haydock

14 COMMENTARY: The proper days are the festival days, from the Hebrew word for appointed times, which were still observed in the temple, so the book is to be read publicly at the appointed feasts. — George Leo Haydock

20 COMMENTARY: The curses against transgressors are those of Leviticus and Daniel, and the day means that the people already feel the effects of sin, though the land of Juda was not yet laid waste, so the confession is made while ruin is still incomplete. — George Leo Haydock

22 COMMENTARY: To serve is here to serve or sacrifice unto God, or to worship, as in the second chapter, so going after strange gods is described as service given to the wrong master. — George Leo Haydock

No note for verse(s) 5, 6, 13, 15, 16, 17, 18, 19, 21 — the public-domain sources consulted give no distinct explanation for these verses beyond the list itself, so nothing was invented for them.
