Study Notes — Acts

Chapter 1

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Luke's former treatise is his Gospel, and Acts continues the same work of Jesus. JFB divides Christ's work into two branches: what he did on earth in his own person, and what he continues from heaven by his Spirit through the apostles. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: Before he was taken up, the risen Lord gave commandments to the apostles he had chosen, and JFB notes these parting charges are said to be given through the Holy Ghost. This marks his new relation to the church after the resurrection, as the Spirit now overflows from him to his disciples. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: Jesus showed himself alive after his suffering by many infallible proofs over forty days, speaking of the kingdom of God. JFB stresses that the disciples were not predisposed to believe, but were overpowered by the evidence of their own senses. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: The disciples were ordered not to depart from Jerusalem but to wait for the promised Spirit. JFB explains that the Spirit was to glorify the existing order by descending at its metropolitan seat, so that the word of the Lord might go forth from Jerusalem. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: John baptized with water, but the disciples would soon be baptized with the Holy Ghost. JFB counts this as ten days hence, to Pentecost, expressed indefinitely in order to exercise their faith. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: The disciples still asked whether the kingdom would now be restored to Israel. Henry warns against a desire for forbidden knowledge, while JFB notes their carnal views had been modified and they are neither rebuked nor contradicted on this point. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: It is not for the disciples to know the times or seasons the Father has kept in his own power. JFB reads this as implying not only that this was not the time, but that the question was irrelevant to their present business and future work. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: They would receive power when the Holy Ghost came upon them and be witnesses in Jerusalem, Judea, Samaria, and to the uttermost part of the earth. JFB calls this order the key to the plan of Acts: Jerusalem through chapter 9, then the wider world to chapter 28. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: While the disciples watched, Jesus was taken up, and a cloud received him out of their sight. JFB stresses the point of their watching: he did not merely disappear unnoticed, but was seen to ascend. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: As they looked steadfastly toward heaven, two men in white apparel stood by them. JFB identifies these as angels in human form, and reads their eager gazing as part of the sense-evidence on which their later testimony would rest. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: The angels asked why the men of Galilee stood gazing up into heaven, and promised that this same Jesus would come again in like manner. Henry adds that thoughts of his second coming should quicken the trifling and comfort the trembling. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: The disciples returned to Jerusalem from the Mount of Olives, a sabbath day's journey away. JFB reckons that distance at about two thousand cubits. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: They went up into an upper room, which JFB takes as their place of rendezvous, perhaps the same large upper room of the last Passover and first Supper. There the eleven apostles are named together. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: They continued with one accord in prayer and supplication, with the women, Mary the mother of Jesus, and his brethren. Henry notes that those in the best frame to receive spiritual blessings are in a praying frame, and JFB adds that this is the last mention of Mary in the New Testament, found here with the rest in prayer, not above them. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: In those days of expectant prayer Peter stood up among about a hundred and twenty disciples. JFB notes Peter as the leading spirit of the infant community, and observes that many of the five hundred brethren who saw the risen Lord must have remained in Galilee. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

18 COMMENTARY: Judas fell headlong and burst asunder, an account JFB says supplements but by no means contradicts what Matthew records of his hanging. The two accounts together fill out the manner of the traitor's death. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

20 COMMENTARY: Peter cites the Psalms for letting another take Judas's charge, or bishopric. JFB sees a combination of Psalm 69:25 and Psalm 109:8, in which the apostle discerns a greater than David and a worse than Ahithophel. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

21 COMMENTARY: The replacement must be a man who companied with the apostles all the time the Lord Jesus went in and out among them. JFB glosses this as the close intimacies of a three years' public life. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

22 COMMENTARY: The witness must span from the baptism of John to the day Jesus was taken up, so that he can be ordained a witness of the resurrection. JFB states the apostles' primary office plainly: to testify from personal observation to the resurrection, and to show how it glorified Christ's whole previous life. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

23 COMMENTARY: Two men were put forward in nomination, Joseph called Barsabas and Matthias. JFB notes the nomination came from the whole company, of whom Peter was the spokesman, not from the eleven alone. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

24 COMMENTARY: They prayed to the Lord who knows the hearts of all men to show which of the two he had chosen. JFB observes that the word Lord used absolutely in the New Testament almost universally denotes the Son, making this the first example of prayer offered to the exalted Redeemer. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

25 COMMENTARY: The chosen man was to take part in the ministry and apostleship from which Judas fell, that he might go to his own place. JFB reads that last phrase as a softened expression of the traitor's awful future, implying both a destined habitation and a congenial element. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

26 COMMENTARY: They gave forth their lots, the lot fell upon Matthias, and he was numbered with the eleven apostles. JFB glosses numbered as voted in by general suffrage, completing the broken twelve, and Henry adds that the great thing the apostles were to attest was Christ's resurrection, the foundation of hope in him. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

No note for verse(s) 16, 17, 19 — the public-domain sources consulted give no distinct explanation for these verses beyond the list itself, so nothing was invented for them.
