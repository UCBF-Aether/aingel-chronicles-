Study Notes — Acts

Chapter 11

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: The apostles and brethren in Judaea heard that the Gentiles had also received the word of God. Henry sees the imperfect state of human nature in what follows: godly persons are displeased even to hear that the word of God has been received, because their own system has not been attended to. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: When Peter was come up to Jerusalem, they that were of the circumcision contended with him. Henry calls this shutting out the bane and damage of the church: to shut out those from it, and from the benefit of the means of grace, who are not in every thing as we are. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: Saying, Thou wentest in to men uncircumcised, and didst eat with them. Henry's contention point is the same exclusion: the circumcision party contended because Peter ate with uncircumcised men, judging fellowship by their own system rather than by God's reception. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: But Peter rehearsed the matter from the beginning, and expounded it by order unto them. Henry commends the method: Peter stated the whole affair, and we should at all times bear with the infirmities of our brethren, and instead of taking offence or answering with warmth, explain our motives and show the nature of our proceedings. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: The Spirit bade me go with them, nothing doubting. Moreover these six brethren accompanied me, and we entered into the man's house. Henry's defence principle is that Peter explained his motives and showed the nature of his proceedings: he went because the Spirit bade him, nothing doubting, with six brethren as witnesses. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: As I began to speak, the Holy Ghost fell on them, as on us at the beginning. Henry's test for the preaching is this fall: that preaching is certainly right, with which the Holy Ghost is given, and the Gentiles' reception matches the beginning at Pentecost. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: Then remembered I the word of the Lord, how that he said, John indeed baptized with water, but ye shall be baptized with the Holy Ghost. Henry's frame for the gift is remembrance of the Lord's word: the same baptism promised at the beginning has now been given to the Gentiles, and Peter recognizes the pattern. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: Forasmuch then as God gave them the like gift as he did unto us, who believed on the Lord Jesus Christ; what was I, that I could withstand God? Henry warns the zealous directly: while men are very zealous for their own regulations, they should take care that they do not withstand God. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: When they heard these things, they held their peace, and glorified God, saying, Then hath God also to the Gentiles granted repentance unto life. Henry says those who love the Lord will glorify him when made sure that he has given repentance to life to any fellow-sinners, for repentance is God's gift: not only his free grace accepts it, but his mighty grace works it in us, taking away the heart of stone and giving a heart of flesh. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: Now they which were scattered abroad upon the persecution that arose about Stephen travelled as far as Phenice, and Cyprus, and Antioch, preaching the word to none but unto the Jews only. Henry's providence point opens the Antioch story: the first preachers at Antioch were dispersed from Jerusalem by persecution, and thus what was meant to hurt the church was made to work for its good, for the wrath of man is made to praise God. — Matthew Henry

20 COMMENTARY: Some of them were men of Cyprus and Cyrene, which, when they were come to Antioch, spake unto the Grecians, preaching the Lord Jesus. Henry asks what ministers of Christ should preach but Christ, and him crucified, Christ, and him glorified, and notes their preaching was accompanied with divine power. — Matthew Henry

21 COMMENTARY: The hand of the Lord was with them, and a great number believed, and turned unto the Lord. Henry describes the conversion: the hand of the Lord brought home to hearts and consciences what the preachers could but speak to the outward ear, and they believed, convinced of the truth of the gospel, turning from a careless, carnal way of living to a holy, heavenly, spiritual life, and from worship in show and ceremony to worship in the Spirit and in truth, until the Lord Jesus became all in all with them. — Matthew Henry

23 COMMENTARY: Who, when he came, and had seen the grace of God, was glad, and exhorted them all, that with purpose of heart they would cleave unto the Lord. Henry says when sinners are thus brought to the Lord, really good men, who are full of faith and of the Holy Ghost, will admire and rejoice in the grace of God bestowed on them, and Barnabas's gladness and exhortation are that rejoicing. — Matthew Henry

24 COMMENTARY: For he was a good man, and full of the Holy Ghost and of faith: and much people was added unto the Lord. Henry describes Barnabas himself: he was full of faith, full of the grace of faith, and full of the fruits of the faith that works by love, and under such a man much people was added unto the Lord. — Matthew Henry

26 COMMENTARY: When he had found him, he brought him unto Antioch. And it came to pass, that a whole year they assembled themselves with the church, and taught much people. And the disciples were called Christians first in Antioch. Henry explains the new name: hitherto the followers of Christ were called disciples, learners, scholars, but from that time they were called Christians, whose proper meaning is a follower of Christ, one who from serious thought embraces the religion of Christ, believes his promises, and makes it his chief care to shape his life by Christ's precepts and example, while multitudes take the name to whom it does not rightly belong, and the name without the reality will only add to guilt. — Matthew Henry

29 COMMENTARY: Then the disciples, every man according to his ability, determined to send relief unto the brethren which dwelt in Judaea. Henry's fruit of true Christianity is this relief: true Christians will feel for their brethren under afflictions, and thus fruit is brought forth to the praise and glory of God, for if all mankind were true Christians, how cheerfully would they help one another, the whole earth like one large family. — Matthew Henry

30 COMMENTARY: Which also they did, and sent it to the elders by the hands of Barnabas and Saul. Henry's family picture is fulfilled in the sending: they did what they determined, and the relief went by the hands of Barnabas and Saul, cheerful help from one part of the family of Christians to another under affliction. — Matthew Henry

No note for verse(s) 5, 6, 7, 8, 9, 10, 11, 13, 14, 22, 25, 27, 28 — the public-domain sources consulted give no distinct explanation for these verses beyond the list itself, so nothing was invented for them.
