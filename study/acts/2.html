Study Notes — Acts

Chapter 2

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: When the day of Pentecost was fully come they were all with one accord in one place. Henry contrasts this with their earlier strifes over who should be greatest, now ended by praying together, and draws the lesson that where brethren dwell in unity, there the Lord commands his blessing. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: A sound like a rushing mighty wind filled the house where they sat. Henry reads the wind as signifying the powerful influences and working of the Spirit upon the minds of men and thereby upon the world, with convictions making way for comforts. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: Cloven tongues like fire appeared and sat upon each of them. Henry explains the fire: the Spirit melts the heart, burns up the dross, and kindles pious and devout affections, in which the spiritual sacrifices are offered up. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: They were all filled with the Holy Ghost and began to speak with other tongues as the Spirit gave them utterance. Henry distinguishes the filling: graces of the Spirit, comforts in Christ's love and the hope of heaven, and miraculous gifts for furthering the gospel, speaking not from previous meditation but as the Spirit gave utterance. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: Devout Jews from every nation under heaven were dwelling in Jerusalem for the feast. Henry's point on the whole passage is that the first instruments of spreading Christianity could have made no progress without this gift of tongues, which proved their authority was from God. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: The multitude came together and were confounded because each heard them speak in his own language. Henry frames this against Babel: the difference of languages that had much hindered the spread of knowledge and religion was now overcome by the gift. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: They asked how it was that each heard in his own tongue wherein he was born. Henry's answer for the passage is the same reversal of Babel: the gift removed the language hindrance to the gospel and proved the speakers' authority was from God. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: Peter, standing up with the eleven, lifted up his voice to the mockers and the amazed alike. Henry reads the sermon as proof Peter was thoroughly recovered from his fall and restored to divine favour: he who had denied Christ now boldly confessed him. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: Peter declares this is that which was spoken by the prophet Joel. Henry stresses that Peter, though filled with the Spirit and speaking with tongues, did not set aside the Scriptures: the pouring forth of the Spirit was the fulfilling of Scripture and the fruit of Christ's resurrection and ascension. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: In the last days God would pour out his Spirit upon all flesh, and sons and daughters would prophesy. Henry treats this pouring forth as the fulfilling of Scripture and the fruit of Christ's resurrection and ascension, given so hearers would embrace the faith of Christ and join his church. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: God's servants and handmaidens would prophesy in those days as the Spirit was poured out. Henry presents this as part of the same scriptural fulfilment, the miraculous pouring forth that proves Christ's resurrection and ascension. — Matthew Henry

21 COMMENTARY: Whosoever shall call on the name of the Lord shall be saved. Henry draws the warning plainly: none will escape the condemnation of the great day except those who call upon the name of the Lord, in and through his Son Jesus Christ, as Saviour of sinners and Judge of all. — Matthew Henry

22 COMMENTARY: Peter preaches Jesus of Nazareth, a man approved of God by miracles, wonders and signs. Henry calls this the history of Christ, beginning from the gift of the Holy Ghost and moving to the death and sufferings the hearers had witnessed but a few weeks before. — Matthew Henry

23 COMMENTARY: Jesus was delivered by the determinate counsel and foreknowledge of God, yet taken and crucified by wicked hands. Henry holds both sides together: as God's act the death was of wonderful grace and wisdom under an eternal counsel, and as the people's act it was awful sin and folly. — Matthew Henry

24 COMMENTARY: God raised Jesus up, having loosed the pains of death, because it was not possible he should be holden of it. Henry says Christ's resurrection did away the reproach of his death, and Peter speaks largely upon it. — Matthew Henry

27 COMMENTARY: David's words are applied to Christ: God would not leave his soul in hell, neither suffer his Holy One to see corruption. Henry identifies Christ as God's Holy One, sanctified and set apart to the work of redemption, whose death becomes for all his people the entrance to a blessed life for evermore. — Matthew Henry

28 COMMENTARY: Thou hast made known to me the ways of life, and wilt make me full of joy with thy countenance. Henry draws the encouragement: through the Saviour the ways of life are made known, and believers are encouraged to expect God's presence and favour for evermore. — Matthew Henry

31 COMMENTARY: David, seeing this before, spake of the resurrection of Christ, that his soul was not left in hell, neither his flesh did see corruption. Henry says this event had taken place as foretold, and the resurrection did away the reproach of Christ's death. — Matthew Henry

32 COMMENTARY: This Jesus hath God raised up, whereof we all are witnesses. Henry stresses that the resurrection did not rest on prophecy alone: Christ had poured upon his disciples the miraculous gifts and divine influences whose effects the hearers themselves witnessed. — Matthew Henry

33 COMMENTARY: Exalted by the right hand of God, Jesus received the promise of the Holy Ghost and shed forth what the crowd now saw and heard. Henry treats this outpouring as proof of both resurrection and ascension, the fruit of Christ's exaltation. — Matthew Henry

36 COMMENTARY: Therefore let all the house of Israel know assuredly that God hath made that same Jesus, whom ye crucified, both Lord and Christ. Henry sums the sermon up here: all springs from assured belief that Jesus is the Lord and the anointed Saviour. — Matthew Henry

37 COMMENTARY: When they heard this they were pricked in their heart and asked what they should do. Henry says neither Peter's words nor the miracle could have produced such effects without the Holy Spirit: sinners whose eyes are opened cannot but be pricked for sin and feel an inward uneasiness. — Matthew Henry

38 COMMENTARY: Peter exhorted them to repent and be baptized in the name of Jesus Christ for the remission of sins, and they would receive the gift of the Holy Ghost. Henry reads baptism as openly avowing belief in Jesus as Messiah, with remission of sins and a share in the Spirit's gifts and graces following. — Matthew Henry

39 COMMENTARY: The promise is unto you and your children and to all that are afar off, as many as the Lord shall call. Henry extends the encouragement: repentance and remission are still preached to the chief of sinners, the promises are to us and our children, and the blessings are offered to all that are afar off. — Matthew Henry

40 COMMENTARY: With many other words Peter testified and exhorted them to save themselves from this untoward generation. Henry explains the separation: to break off from wicked people, avoiding them with dread and holy fear, is the only way to save ourselves from them. — Matthew Henry

41 COMMENTARY: They that gladly received his word were baptized, and about three thousand souls were added that day. Henry is careful about what gift they received: the Spirit of adoption, converting, guiding and sanctifying grace bestowed on all members of the heavenly Father's family, from which no true believer is shut out. — Matthew Henry

42 COMMENTARY: They continued steadfastly in the apostles' doctrine and fellowship, in breaking of bread and in prayers. Henry calls this the truly primitive church in its infancy and greatest innocence, keeping close to holy ordinances and abounding in piety and devotion. — Matthew Henry

44 COMMENTARY: All that believed were together and had all things common, selling possessions and parting them as every man had need. Henry explains that this did not destroy property but did away selfishness: love made every one to be to another as to himself, and God, who moved them to it, knew they were quickly to be driven from their possessions in Judea. — Matthew Henry

45 COMMENTARY: They sold their possessions and goods and parted them to all men as every man had need. Henry's point is charity over ownership: the Holy Ghost filled them with such love that selfishness gave way, and distribution followed need rather than claim. — Matthew Henry

47 COMMENTARY: Praising God and having favour with all the people, they saw the Lord add to the church daily such as should be saved. Henry stresses these were not merely professors but such as were actually brought into acceptance with God, partakers of regenerating grace, effectually brought to Christ. — Matthew Henry

No note for verse(s) 7, 9, 10, 11, 12, 13, 15, 19, 20, 25, 26, 29, 30, 34, 35, 43, 46 — the public-domain sources consulted give no distinct explanation for these verses beyond the list itself, so nothing was invented for them.
