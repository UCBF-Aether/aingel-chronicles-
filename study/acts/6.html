Study Notes — Acts

Chapter 6

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: When the number of the disciples was multiplied, there arose a murmuring of the Grecians against the Hebrews because their widows were neglected in the daily ministration. Henry notes the contrast: hitherto the disciples had been of one accord, often noticed to their honour, but now, multiplied, they began to murmur. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: It is not reason that we should leave the word of God and serve tables. Henry's principle is that the word of God was enough to take up all the thoughts, cares and time of the apostles, so the ministry of the word must not be displaced by the care of tables. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: Look ye out among you seven men of honest report, full of the Holy Ghost and wisdom, whom we may appoint over this business. Henry sets the qualification: those chosen to serve tables must be duly qualified, filled with gifts and graces of the Holy Ghost necessary to rightly managing the trust, men of truth and hating covetousness. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: But we will give ourselves continually to prayer and to the ministry of the word. Henry presents this division of labour as the apostles' proper work: prayer and the ministry of the word claim their whole time, while the daily ministration is committed to qualified men. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: Whom they set before the apostles, and when they had prayed, they laid their hands on them. Henry's rule for church appointments fits here: all who are employed in the service of the church ought to be commended to the divine grace by the prayers of the church, and they blessed them in the name of the Lord. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: The word of God increased, and the number of the disciples multiplied in Jerusalem greatly, and a great company of the priests were obedient to the faith. Henry marvels at the reach of it: the word and grace of God are greatly magnified when those are wrought upon by it who were least likely. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: Stephen, full of faith and power, did great wonders and miracles among the people. Henry's account of the opposition that follows begins here: when they could not answer Stephen's arguments as a disputant, they prosecuted him as a criminal. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: They were not able to resist the wisdom and the spirit by which he spake. Henry's point is that argument failed first: it was only when they could not answer Stephen's arguments that they turned to prosecution and false witness. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: Then they suborned men, which said, We have heard him speak blasphemous words against Moses and against God. Henry is blunt about the method: it is next to a miracle of providence that no greater number of religious persons have been murdered in the world by perjury and pretence of law, when so many thousands hate them who make no conscience of false oaths. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: And set up false witnesses, which said, This man ceaseth not to speak blasphemous words against this holy place and the law. Henry asks what to say of man, a rational being, yet attempting to uphold a religious system by false witness and murder, and answers that the blame rests not so much upon the understanding as upon the heart of a fallen creature, deceitful above all things and desperately wicked. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: For we have heard him say that this Jesus of Nazareth shall destroy this place and shall change the customs which Moses delivered us. Henry treats the charge as the same false-witness prosecution: unable to answer Stephen, his enemies manufactured a criminal case out of his preaching about Jesus. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: All that sat in the council, looking stedfastly on him, saw his face as it had been the face of an angel. Henry closes with the contrast: wisdom and holiness make a man's face to shine, yet will not secure men from being treated badly, but the servant of the Lord, possessing a clear conscience, cheerful hope and divine consolations, may smile in the midst of danger and death. — Matthew Henry

No note for verse(s) 5, 9, 12 — the public-domain sources consulted give no distinct explanation for these verses beyond the list itself, so nothing was invented for them.
