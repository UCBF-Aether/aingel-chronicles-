Study Notes — Acts

Chapter 17

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

2 COMMENTARY: Paul, as his manner was, went in unto them, and three sabbath days reasoned with them out of the scriptures. Henry states the drift and scope of Paul's preaching and arguing: it was to prove that Jesus is the Christ. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: Opening and alleging, that Christ must needs have suffered, and risen again from the dead; and that this Jesus, whom I preach unto you, is Christ. Henry explains the must: he must needs suffer for us, because he could not otherwise purchase our redemption for us, and he must needs have risen again, because he could not otherwise apply the redemption to us, and we are to preach concerning Jesus that he is Christ, therefore we may hope to be saved by him, and are bound to be ruled by him. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: Some of them believed, and consorted with Paul and Silas; and of the devout Greeks a great multitude, and of the chief women not a few. Henry's preaching drift bears this fruit: the argument that Jesus is the Christ, who suffered and rose, draws believers who consort with Paul and Silas. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: But the Jews which believed not, moved with envy, took unto them certain lewd fellows of the baser sort, and gathered a company, and set all the city on an uproar, and assaulted the house of Jason, and sought to bring them out to the people. Henry says the unbelieving Jews were angry because the apostles preached to the Gentiles, that they might be saved, and asks how strange it is that men should grudge others the privileges they will not themselves accept, while turbulent spirits make religion the pretext for evil designs, from whom we should withdraw, showing a desire to act aright in society while claiming our right to worship God according to our consciences. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: When they found them not, they drew Jason and certain brethren unto the rulers of the city, crying, These that have turned the world upside down are come hither also. Henry says neither rulers nor people need be troubled at the increase of real Christians, even though turbulent spirits should make religion the pretext for evil designs, and Jason drawn before the rulers is that pretext at work. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: Whom Jason hath received: and these all do contrary to the decrees of Caesar, saying that there is another king, one Jesus. Henry's pretext charge is the another-king cry: turbulent spirits make religion the pretext for evil designs, turning the preaching of Jesus as Christ into a charge of contrary decrees. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: When they had taken security of Jason, and of the other, they let them go. Henry's society rule fits the release: let us show a desire to act aright in society, while we claim our right to worship God according to our consciences, and the brethren, security taken, are let go. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: The brethren immediately sent away Paul and Silas by night unto Berea: who coming thither went into the synagogue of the Jews. Henry's Berean study begins at the synagogue: the Jews in Berea applied seriously to the study of the word preached unto them. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: These were more noble than those in Thessalonica, in that they received the word with all readiness of mind, and searched the scriptures daily, whether those things were so. Henry says they not only heard Paul preach on the sabbath, but daily searched the Scriptures, and compared what they read with the facts related to them, for the doctrine of Christ does not fear inquiry, and advocates for his cause desire no more than that people will fully and fairly examine whether things are so or not, and those are truly noble, and likely to be more and more so, who make the Scriptures their rule, and consult them accordingly. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: Therefore many of them believed; also of honourable women which were Greeks, and of men, not a few. Henry's therefore is the fruit of searching: receiving the word with readiness and searching the Scriptures daily, whether those things were so, many believed. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: Now while Paul waited for them at Athens, his spirit was stirred in him, when he saw the city wholly given to idolatry. Henry says Athens was then famed for polite learning, philosophy, and the fine arts, but none are more childish and superstitious, more impious, or more credulous, than some persons deemed eminent for learning and ability, and the city was wholly given to idolatry, which stirred Paul's spirit. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: Therefore disputed he in the synagogue with the Jews, and with the devout persons, and in the market daily with them that met with him. Henry says the zealous advocate for the cause of Christ will be ready to plead for it in all companies, as occasion offers, in synagogue, with devout persons, and in the market daily. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: Then certain philosophers of the Epicureans, and of the Stoicks, encountered him. And some said, What will this babbler say? other some, He seemeth to be a setter forth of strange gods: because he preached unto them Jesus, and the resurrection. Henry says the apostle ever dwelt upon two points, which are indeed the principal doctrines of Christianity, Christ and a future state, Christ our way, and heaven our end, and most of these learned men took no notice of Paul, but some, whose principles were most directly contrary to Christianity, made remarks upon him. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: They took him, and brought him unto Areopagus, saying, May we know what this new doctrine, whereof thou speakest, is? Henry says they led him to the place where judges sat who inquired into such matters, and they asked about Paul's doctrine, not because it was good, but because it was new. — Matthew Henry

20 COMMENTARY: For thou bringest certain strange things to our ears: we would know therefore what these things mean. Henry says they desire to know more of it, but only because it was new and strange, and they asked about the doctrine because it was new, not because it was good. — Matthew Henry

21 COMMENTARY: For all the Athenians and strangers which were there spent their time in nothing else, but either to tell, or to hear some new thing. Henry says great talkers are always busy-bodies, spending their time in nothing else, and a very uncomfortable account they have to give of their time who thus spend it, for time is precious, and we are concerned to employ it well, because eternity depends upon it, but much is wasted in unprofitable conversation. — Matthew Henry

22 COMMENTARY: Then Paul stood in the midst of Mars' hill, and said, Ye men of Athens, I perceive that in all things ye are too superstitious. Henry frames the sermon to heathens as different in scope from the sermon to Jews: to the Jews, his business was to lead hearers by prophecies and miracles to the knowledge of the Redeemer and faith in him, but to heathens, it was to lead them, by the common works of providence, to know the Creator, and worship him. — Matthew Henry

23 COMMENTARY: For as I passed by, and beheld your devotions, I found an altar with this inscription, TO THE UNKNOWN GOD. Whom therefore ye ignorantly worship, him declare I unto you. Henry says the apostle spoke of an altar he had seen, with the inscription, TO THE UNKNOWN GOD, and after multiplying their idols to the utmost, some at Athens thought there was another god of whom they had no knowledge, and asks whether there are not many now called Christians, zealous in their devotions, yet the great object of their worship is to them an unknown God. — Matthew Henry

24 COMMENTARY: God that made the world and all things therein, seeing that he is Lord of heaven and earth, dwelleth not in temples made with hands. Henry bids us observe what glorious things Paul here says of that God whom he served, and would have them to serve: the Maker of the world, Lord of heaven and earth, who dwelleth not in temples made with hands. — Matthew Henry

25 COMMENTARY: Neither is worshipped with men's hands, as though he needed any thing, seeing he giveth to all life, and breath, and all things. Henry's glorious God needs nothing from men's hands, seeing he giveth to all life, and breath, and all things, and the sermon to heathens leads by such works of providence to know the Creator. — Matthew Henry

26 COMMENTARY: And hath made of one blood all nations of men for to dwell on all the face of the earth, and hath determined the times before appointed, and the bounds of their habitation. Henry's providence scope is this ordering: the Creator made all nations of one blood and determined their times and bounds, and by those common works of providence Paul leads the Athenians to know him. — Matthew Henry

27 COMMENTARY: That they should seek the Lord, if haply they might feel after him, and find him, though he be not far from every one of us. Henry's purpose for providence is seeking: the works of providence are to lead men to know the Creator and worship him, and to seek the Lord, feeling after him, though he is not far from every one. — Matthew Henry

28 COMMENTARY: For in him we live, and move, and have our being; as certain also of your own poets have said, For we are also his offspring. Henry's nearness point is the life itself: in him we live, and move, and have our being, and even their own poets said we are his offspring, so the Creator is not far. — Matthew Henry

29 COMMENTARY: Forasmuch then as we are the offspring of God, we ought not to think that the Godhead is like unto gold, or silver, or stone, graven by art and man's device. Henry says each sect of the learned men would feel themselves powerfully affected by the discourse, which tended to show the emptiness or falsity of their doctrines, and the Godhead-not-graven argument empties the idols of Athens. — Matthew Henry

30 COMMENTARY: And the times of this ignorance God winked at; but now commandeth all men every where to repent. Henry says the Lord had long borne with idolatry, but the times of this ignorance were now ending, and by his servants he now commanded all men every where to repent of their idolatry. — Matthew Henry

31 COMMENTARY: Because he hath appointed a day, in the which he will judge the world in righteousness by that man whom he hath ordained; whereof he hath given assurance unto all men, in that he hath raised him from the dead. Henry says considering the judgement to come, and Christ as our Judge, should urge all to repent of sin, and turn to him, and whatever matter is used, all discourses must lead to him, and show his authority, for our salvation, and resurrection, come from and by him. — Matthew Henry

32 COMMENTARY: When they heard of the resurrection of the dead, some mocked: and others said, We will hear thee again of this matter. Henry says the apostle was treated with more outward civility at Athens than in some other places, but none more despised his doctrine, or treated it with more indifference, for of all subjects, that which deserves the most attention gains the least, but those who scorn will have to bear the consequences, and the word will never be useless. — Matthew Henry

34 COMMENTARY: Howbeit certain men clave unto him, and believed: among the which was Dionysius the Areopagite, and a woman named Damaris, and others with them. Henry says some will be found who cleave to the Lord, and listen to his faithful servants, and the word will never be useless, for certain men clave unto Paul and believed. — Matthew Henry

No note for verse(s) 1, 8, 13, 14, 15, 33 — the public-domain sources consulted give no distinct explanation for these verses beyond the list itself, so nothing was invented for them.
