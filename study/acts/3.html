Study Notes — Acts

Chapter 3

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Peter and John went up together into the temple at the hour of prayer, the ninth hour. Henry notes that the apostles and first believers attended the temple worship at the hours of prayer, keeping the appointed rhythms of devotion. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: A man lame from his mother's womb was carried daily to the temple gate called Beautiful to ask alms. Henry identifies him as a cripple from his birth, above forty years old, on whom Peter and John were led by a divine direction to work a miracle. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: Peter said, Silver and gold have I none, but in the name of Jesus Christ of Nazareth rise up and walk. Henry draws the application: to heal men's souls to good purpose we must go forth in the name and power of Jesus Christ, calling helpless sinners to arise and walk in holiness by faith in him. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: Peter took him by the right hand and lifted him up, and immediately his feet and ankle bones received strength. Henry's reflection on the cure is that the name of Jesus Christ of Nazareth can make whole all the crippled faculties of our fallen nature. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: Leaping up, the man stood and walked, and entered with them into the temple, walking and leaping and praising God. Henry pictures the holy joy of it: with what rapture shall we tread the holy courts when God the Spirit causes us to enter therein by his strength. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: Peter answered the wondering people, Why marvel ye at this, or why look ye so earnestly on us, as though by our own power or holiness we had made this man to walk? Henry marks the difference from Christ's manner: the Lord spoke as having almighty power, but the apostles referred all to their Lord and refused honour except as his undeserving instruments. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: The God of Abraham, Isaac and Jacob hath glorified his Son Jesus, whom ye delivered up and denied before Pilate. Henry stresses the apostles' humility again: every crown must be cast at the feet of Christ, and useful men must be very humble, giving glory to his name, not their own. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: Peter showed the Jews the greatness of their crime in denying the Holy One and the Just and desiring a murderer. Henry adds the pastoral guard: the apostle would not anger them or drive them to despair, and though rejection of Christ is done through ignorance, ignorance can in no case be an excuse. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: And now, brethren, I wot that through ignorance ye did it, as did also your rulers. Henry allows the ignorance but removes the excuse: those who reject, refuse, or deny Christ do it through ignorance, but this can in no case be an excuse. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: Repent ye therefore, and be converted, that your sins may be blotted out, when the times of refreshing shall come from the presence of the Lord. Henry charges the absolute necessity of repentance upon every conscience that desires sins blotted out and the refreshment that only a sense of Christ's pardoning love can afford. — Matthew Henry

20 COMMENTARY: And he shall send Jesus Christ, which before was preached unto you. Henry places this sending within the times of refreshing promised to the penitent: to the converted and believing, refreshment comes from the presence of the Lord. — Matthew Henry

21 COMMENTARY: Whom the heaven must receive until the times of restitution of all things. Henry notes it was not needful for the Spirit to make known the times and seasons of these dispensations, which are still left obscure, and that in this state of trial the glorified Redeemer is out of sight because we must live by faith in him. — Matthew Henry

22 COMMENTARY: Moses said a prophet shall the Lord raise up unto you of your brethren, like unto me, and him shall ye hear in all things. Henry calls this a powerful warning to the Jews in the very words of Moses, their favourite prophet, out of pretended zeal for whom they were ready to reject Christianity. — Matthew Henry

23 COMMENTARY: Every soul which will not hear that prophet shall be destroyed from among the people. Henry reads the passage as warning of the dreadful consequences of unbelief, spoken in Moses' own words to those who claimed Moses against Christ. — Matthew Henry

24 COMMENTARY: All the prophets from Samuel and those that follow after have likewise foretold of these days. Henry's summary of the address is that Christ came into the world to bring a blessing with him, and sent his Spirit to be the great blessing. — Matthew Henry

25 COMMENTARY: Ye are the children of the prophets and of the covenant God made with Abraham, in whose seed all kindreds of the earth shall be blessed. Henry closes the chapter on the blessing itself: Christ came to bless us by turning us from our iniquities and saving us from our sins, and none can be happy continuing in sin when God declares the blessing lies in being turned from all iniquity. — Matthew Henry

26 COMMENTARY: Unto you first God, having raised up his Son Jesus, sent him to bless you, in turning away every one of you from his iniquities. Henry closes on this blessing: Christ came to bless us by turning us from our iniquities and saving us from our sins, and none should expect to be turned from sin except by believing in and receiving Christ the Son of God. — Matthew Henry

No note for verse(s) 3, 4, 5, 9, 10, 11, 15, 16, 18 — the public-domain sources consulted give no distinct explanation for these verses beyond the list itself, so nothing was invented for them.
