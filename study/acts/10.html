Study Notes — Acts

Chapter 10

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: There was a certain man in Caesarea called Cornelius, a centurion of the band called the Italian band. Henry marks the turning point: hitherto none had been baptized into the Christian church but Jews, Samaritans, and converts who had been circumcised and observed the ceremonial law, but now the Gentiles were to be called to partake all the privileges of God's people, without first becoming Jews, and pure and undefiled religion is sometimes found where we least expect it. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: A devout man, and one that feared God with all his house, which gave much alms to the people, and prayed to God alway. Henry says wherever the fear of God rules in the heart, it will appear both in works of charity and of piety, neither excusing the other, and Cornelius had true faith in God's word as far as he understood it, though not as yet clear faith in Christ, this being the work of the Spirit of God through the mediation of Jesus, even before Cornelius knew him. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: He saw in a vision evidently about the ninth hour of the day an angel of God coming in to him, and saying unto him, Cornelius. Henry's lesson for the whole direction is obedience: without dispute or delay Cornelius was obedient to the heavenly vision, and in the affairs of our souls, let us not lose time. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: When he looked on him, he was afraid, and said, What is it, Lord? And he said unto him, Thy prayers and thine alms are come up for a memorial before God. Henry adds the ground of acceptance: through Christ also his prayers and alms were accepted, which otherwise would have been rejected. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: And now send men to Joppa, and call for one Simon, whose surname is Peter. Henry's obedience point governs the sending: without dispute or delay Cornelius was obedient to the heavenly vision, and in the affairs of our souls we should not lose time. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: When he had declared all these things unto them, he sent them to Joppa. Henry repeats the obedience: without dispute or delay Cornelius acted on the heavenly vision, declaring it fully to his household and sending at once. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: There came a voice to him, Rise, Peter; kill, and eat. Henry explains the force of the command: to tell a Jew that God had directed those animals to be reckoned clean which were hitherto deemed unclean was in effect saying that the law of Moses was done away. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: But Peter said, Not so, Lord; for I have never eaten any thing that is common or unclean. Henry names the obstacle plainly: the prejudices of Peter against the Gentiles would have prevented his going to Cornelius, unless the Lord had prepared him for this service, and his refusal shows the prejudice at work. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: The voice spake unto him again the second time, What God hath cleansed, that call not thou common. Henry says Peter was soon made to know the meaning of it: what God has cleansed is not to be called common, and the vision prepares him for a service his prejudices would have refused. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: This was done thrice, and the vessel was received up again into heaven. Henry's preparation point is the repetition itself: God knows what services are before us, and how to prepare us, and the thrice-repeated vision drives the cleansing home before the messengers arrive. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: Now while Peter doubted in himself what this vision which he had seen should mean, behold, the men which were sent from Cornelius had made enquiry for Simon's house, and stood before the gate. Henry says we know the meaning of what God has taught us when we find what occasion we have to make use of it, and the men at the gate are that occasion arriving while Peter still doubts. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: While Peter thought on the vision, the Spirit said unto him, Behold, three men seek thee. Henry's preparation is completed by the Spirit's word: God knows what services are before us and how to prepare us, and the Spirit joins the vision to the men at the gate. — Matthew Henry

20 COMMENTARY: Arise therefore, and get thee down, and go with them, doubting nothing: for I have sent them. Henry's rule for a clear call fits: when we see our call clear to any service, we should not be perplexed with doubts and scruples arising from prejudices or former ideas. — Matthew Henry

22 COMMENTARY: They said, Cornelius the centurion, a just man, and one that feareth God, and of good report among all the nation of the Jews, was warned from God by an holy angel to send for thee into his house, and to hear words of thee. Henry's later summary of Cornelius's side is the declaration of the direction God gave him to send for Peter, and the messengers' report carries both his character and that heavenly warning. — Matthew Henry

24 COMMENTARY: The morrow after they entered into Caesarea. And Cornelius waited for them, and he had called together his kinsmen and near friends. Henry commends the gathering: Cornelius had called together his friends to partake with him of the heavenly wisdom he expected from Peter, for we should not covet to eat our spiritual morsels alone, and it is kindness and respect to kindred and friends to invite them to join us in religious exercises. — Matthew Henry

27 COMMENTARY: As he talked with him, he went in, and found many that were come together. Henry's invited company is this many: Cornelius called his kinsmen and near friends to partake of the heavenly wisdom he expected, and Peter finds a company gathered, not a solitary hearer. — Matthew Henry

28 COMMENTARY: He said unto them, Ye know how that it is an unlawful thing for a man that is a Jew to keep company, or come unto one of another nation; but God hath shewed me that I should not call any man common or unclean. Henry says Peter was soon made to know the meaning of the vision, and here he applies it: the cleansing of the animals meant the cleansing of men, and his prejudice against the Gentiles gives way to God's showing. — Matthew Henry

29 COMMENTARY: Therefore came I unto you without gainsaying, as soon as I was sent for: I ask therefore for what intent ye have sent for me? Henry's rule is fulfilled in the phrase without gainsaying: when our call is clear, we should not be perplexed with doubts and scruples arising from prejudices or former ideas. — Matthew Henry

30 COMMENTARY: Cornelius said, Four days ago I was fasting until this hour, and at the ninth hour I prayed in my house, and, behold, a man stood before me in bright clothing. Henry says Cornelius declared the direction God gave him to send for Peter, and we are right in our aims in attending a gospel ministry when we do it with regard to the divine appointment requiring us to make use of that ordinance. — Matthew Henry

31 COMMENTARY: And said, Cornelius, thy prayer is heard, and thine alms are had in remembrance in the sight of God. Henry's ground from the chapter's opening returns: through Christ his prayers and alms were accepted, which otherwise would have been rejected, and the angel's word confirms that acceptance. — Matthew Henry

33 COMMENTARY: Immediately therefore I sent to thee, and thou hast well done that thou art come. Now therefore are we all here present before God, to hear all things that are commanded thee of God. Henry marvels at the company: how seldom ministers are called to speak to such companies, however small, in which it may be said that they are all present in the sight of God, to hear all things that are commanded of God, but these were ready to hear what Peter was commanded to say. — Matthew Henry

34 COMMENTARY: Then Peter opened his mouth, and said, Of a truth I perceive that God is no respecter of persons. Henry states the ground and the limit together: acceptance cannot be obtained on any other ground than that of the covenant of mercy through the atonement of Christ, but wherever true religion is found, God will accept it without regarding names or sects. — Matthew Henry

35 COMMENTARY: But in every nation he that feareth him, and worketh righteousness, is accepted with him. Henry explains that the fear of God and works of righteousness are the substance of true religion, the effects of special grace, and though these are not the cause of a man's acceptance, yet they show it, and whatever may be wanting in knowledge or faith will in due time be given by him who has begun it. — Matthew Henry

36 COMMENTARY: The word which God sent unto the children of Israel, preaching peace by Jesus Christ: he is Lord of all. Henry gives the purport of the word: God by it published the good tidings of peace by Jesus Christ, and let them know that this Jesus, by whom peace is made between God and man, is Lord of all, not only as over all, God blessed for evermore, but as Mediator, with all power in heaven and earth put into his hand and all judgment committed to him. — Matthew Henry

37 COMMENTARY: That word, I say, ye know, which was published throughout all Judaea, and began from Galilee, after the baptism which John preached. Henry notes what they already knew: they knew in general the word, the gospel God sent to the children of Israel, they knew the several matters of fact relating to the gospel, and they knew the baptism of repentance which John preached. — Matthew Henry

38 COMMENTARY: How God anointed Jesus of Nazareth with the Holy Ghost and with power, who went about doing good, and healing all that were oppressed of the devil, for God was with him. Henry's assurance for the anointed is that God will go with those whom he anoints, and he will be with those to whom he has given his Spirit. — Matthew Henry

39 COMMENTARY: And we are witnesses of all things which he did both in the land of the Jews, and in Jerusalem, whom they slew and hanged on a tree. Henry grounds faith in testimony: faith has reference to a testimony, and the Christian faith is built upon the foundation of the apostles and prophets, on the testimony given by them, and Peter's witness is that testimony. — Matthew Henry

40 COMMENTARY: Him God raised up the third day, and shewed him openly. Henry's centre of the discourse is the resurrection declared with its proofs: Peter declares Christ's resurrection from the dead and the proofs of it, and the sermon turns on that fact. — Matthew Henry

41 COMMENTARY: Not to all the people, but unto witnesses chosen before God, even to us, who did eat and drink with him after he rose from the dead. Henry's testimony point continues: the Christian faith is built on the testimony of apostles and prophets, and these chosen witnesses, who ate and drank with the risen Lord, are its foundation. — Matthew Henry

42 COMMENTARY: He commanded us to preach unto the people, and to testify that it is he which was ordained of God to be the Judge of quick and dead. Henry draws the accountability: we are all accountable to Christ as our Judge, so every one must seek his favour and have him as our Friend. — Matthew Henry

43 COMMENTARY: To him give all the prophets witness, that through his name whosoever believeth in him shall receive remission of sins. Henry closes the sermon on remission: if we believe in him, we shall all be justified by him as our Righteousness, and the remission of sins lays a foundation for all other favours and blessings, by taking out of the way that which hinders the bestowing of them, for if sin be pardoned, all is well and shall end well for ever. — Matthew Henry

44 COMMENTARY: While Peter yet spake these words, the Holy Ghost fell on all them which heard the word. Henry marks the order as the point: the Holy Ghost fell upon these Gentiles before they were baptized, to show that God does not confine himself to outward signs, for it is the Spirit that quickeneth, and the flesh profiteth nothing. — Matthew Henry

45 COMMENTARY: They of the circumcision which believed were astonished, as many as came with Peter, because that on the Gentiles also was poured out the gift of the Holy Ghost. Henry explains the astonishment: by mistaken notions of things, we make difficult for ourselves as to the methods of divine providence and grace, and the believing Jews had not expected the gift on the Gentiles also. — Matthew Henry

46 COMMENTARY: For they heard them speak with tongues, and magnify God. Then answered Peter. Henry says they magnified God and spake of Christ and the benefits of redemption, and whatever gift we are endued with, we ought to honour God with it. — Matthew Henry

47 COMMENTARY: Can any man forbid water, that these should not be baptized, which have received the Holy Ghost as well as we? Henry calls the argument conclusive: as they were undeniably baptized with the Holy Ghost, Peter concluded they were not to be refused the baptism of water, for can we deny the sign to those who have received the things signified? — Matthew Henry

48 COMMENTARY: He commanded them to be baptized in the name of the Lord. Then prayed they him to tarry certain days. Henry adds the ongoing need: those who have some acquaintance with Christ cannot but desire more, and even those who have received the Holy Ghost must see their need of daily learning more of the truth. — Matthew Henry

No note for verse(s) 6, 7, 9, 10, 11, 12, 18, 21, 23, 25, 26, 32 — the public-domain sources consulted give no distinct explanation for these verses beyond the list itself, so nothing was invented for them.
