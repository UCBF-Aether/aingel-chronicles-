Study Notes — Acts

Chapter 13

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Now there were in the church at Antioch certain prophets and teachers: Barnabas, Simeon called Niger, Lucius of Cyrene, Manaen brought up with Herod the tetrarch, and Saul. Henry marvels at the assemblage: in these names we see that the Lord raises up instruments for his work from various places and stations in life, and zeal for his glory induces men to give up flattering connexions and prospects to promote his cause. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: As they ministered to the Lord, and fasted, the Holy Ghost said, Separate me Barnabas and Saul for the work whereunto I have called them. Henry says it is by the Spirit of Christ that his ministers are made both able and willing for his service, and taken from other cares that would hinder in it, and they are separated to take pains, and not to take state. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: When they had fasted and prayed, and laid their hands on them, they sent them away. Henry notes a blessing upon Barnabas and Saul in their present undertaking was sought, and that they might be filled with the Holy Ghost in their work, for whatever means are used or rules observed, the Holy Ghost alone can fit ministers for their important work, and call them to it. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: So they, being sent forth by the Holy Ghost, departed unto Seleucia, and from thence they sailed to Cyprus. Henry's sending point is the Spirit's: they are sent forth by the Holy Ghost, and Christ's ministers are to be employed in Christ's work, under the Spirit's guidance, acting for the glory of God the Father. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: Which was with the deputy of the country, Sergius Paulus, a prudent man, who called for Barnabas and Saul, and desired to hear the word of God. Henry warns that Satan is in a special manner busy with great men and men in power, to keep them from being religious, for their example will influence many, and the prudent deputy's desire to hear the word is set against that busy opposition. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: But Elymas the sorcerer, for so is his name by interpretation, withstood them, seeking to turn away the deputy from the faith. Henry generalizes the withstanding: those who are enemies to the doctrine of Jesus are enemies to all righteousness, for in it all righteousness is fulfilled, and the ways of the Lord Jesus are the only right ways to heaven and happiness, yet many not only wander from these ways themselves, but set others against them. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: Then Saul, who also is called Paul, filled with the Holy Ghost, set his eyes on him. Henry notes the name change here: Saul is here for the first time called Paul, and never after Saul, Saul being his name as he was a Hebrew, Paul his name as he was a citizen of Rome, and under the direct influence of the Holy Ghost he gave Elymas his true character, but not in passion. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: O full of all subtilty and all mischief, thou child of the devil, thou enemy of all righteousness, wilt thou not cease to pervert the right ways of the Lord? Henry explains the charge: a fulness of deceit and mischief together make a man indeed a child of the devil, and those who will not cease to do evil are commonly so hardened that they will not cease. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: And now, behold, the hand of the Lord is upon thee, and thou shalt be blind, not seeing the sun for a season. And immediately there fell on him a mist and a darkness, and he went about seeking some to lead him by the hand. Henry's confirmation point follows in the deputy's astonishment: he was astonished at the power of God by which the doctrine was confirmed, and the blindness is that confirming power made visible. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: Then the deputy, when he saw what was done, believed, being astonished at the doctrine of the Lord. Henry says the proconsul was astonished at the force of the doctrine upon his own heart and conscience, and at the power of God by which it was confirmed, and the doctrine of Christ astonishes, and the more we know of it, the more reason we shall see to wonder at it. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: Now when Paul and his company loosed from Paphos, they came to Perga in Pamphylia, and John departing from them returned to Jerusalem. Henry's warning for the departure is that those who put their hand to the plough and look back are not fit for the kingdom of God, and those who are not prepared to face opposition and to endure hardship are not fitted for the work of the ministry. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: But when they departed from Perga, they came to Antioch in Pisidia, and went into the synagogue on the sabbath day, and sat down. Henry's worship rule opens the sermon: when we come together to worship God, we must do it, not only by prayer and praise, but by the reading and hearing of the word of God. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: After the reading of the law and the prophets the rulers of the synagogue sent unto them, saying, Ye men and brethren, if ye have any word of exhortation for the people, say on. Henry adds the preaching rule: the bare reading of the Scriptures in public assemblies is not enough; they should be expounded, and the people exhorted out of them, helping people to apply the word to themselves. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: Then Paul stood up, and beckoning with his hand said, Men of Israel, and ye that fear God, give audience. Henry's frame for the sermon is its aim: everything is touched upon in it which might best prevail with Jews to receive and embrace Christ as the promised Messiah, and Paul addresses both Israel and the God-fearers as that audience. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: The God of this people of Israel chose our fathers, and exalted the people when they dwelt as strangers in the land of Egypt, and with an high arm brought he them out of it. Henry says every view, however short or faint, of the Lord's dealings with his church reminds us of his mercy and long-suffering, and of man's ingratitude and perverseness, and Paul's history begins with that mercy in choosing and exalting. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: About the time of forty years suffered he their manners in the wilderness. Henry's long-suffering point is the wilderness itself: the Lord's dealings remind us of his mercy and long-suffering against man's ingratitude and perverseness, and forty years of suffered manners display both. — Matthew Henry

22 COMMENTARY: When he had removed him, he raised up unto them David to be their king, to whom also he gave testimony, and said, I have found David the son of Jesse, a man after mine own heart, which shall fulfil all my will. Henry marks the turn: Paul passes from David to the Son of David, and shows that this Jesus is his promised Seed. — Matthew Henry

23 COMMENTARY: Of this man's seed hath God according to his promise raised unto Israel a Saviour, Jesus. Henry says this Jesus is the promised Seed, a Saviour to do that for them which the judges of old could not do, to save them from their sins, their worst enemies. — Matthew Henry

26 COMMENTARY: Men and brethren, children of the stock of Abraham, and whosoever among you feareth God, to you is the word of this salvation sent. Henry says everything is touched upon in this sermon which might best prevail with Jews to receive and embrace Christ as the promised Messiah, and the word of salvation is sent to this very audience, Abraham's children and God-fearers alike. — Matthew Henry

27 COMMENTARY: For they that dwell at Jerusalem, and their rulers, because they knew him not, nor yet the voices of the prophets which are read every sabbath day, they have fulfilled them in condemning him. Henry notes that when the apostles preached Christ as the Saviour, they were so far from concealing his death that they always preached Christ crucified, and here the rulers, not knowing the prophets read every sabbath, fulfilled them by condemning him. — Matthew Henry

28 COMMENTARY: Though they found no cause of death in him, yet desired they Pilate that he should be slain. Henry's open-preaching point stands: the apostles did not conceal Christ's death but always preached Christ crucified, and the demand for Pilate to slay a man in whom no cause of death was found is that crucifixion preached plainly. — Matthew Henry

29 COMMENTARY: When they had fulfilled all that was written of him, they took him down from the tree, and laid him in a sepulchre. Henry says our complete separation from sin is represented by our being buried with Christ, and the burial follows the fulfilment of all that was written of him. — Matthew Henry

30 COMMENTARY: But God raised him from the dead. Henry's great truth to be preached is this sentence: he rose again from the dead, and saw no corruption, and the resurrection is the sermon turning point. — Matthew Henry

31 COMMENTARY: He was seen many days of them which came up with him from Galilee to Jerusalem, who are his witnesses unto the people. Henry calls the resurrection the great proof of his being the Son of God, and the many days of appearances supply its witnesses. — Matthew Henry

32 COMMENTARY: We declare unto you glad tidings, how that the promise which was made unto the fathers. Henry's glad tidings are the resurrection as proof: the resurrection of Christ was the great proof of his being the Son of God, and the promise made to the fathers is declared as fulfilled. — Matthew Henry

33 COMMENTARY: God hath fulfilled the same unto us their children, in that he hath raised up Jesus again, as it is also written in the second psalm, Thou art my Son, this day have I begotten thee. Henry explains why death could not hold him: it was not possible he should be held by death, because he was the Son of God, and therefore had life in himself, which he could not lay down but with a design to take it again. — Matthew Henry

34 COMMENTARY: As concerning that he raised him up from the dead, now no more to return to corruption, he said on this wise, I will give you the sure mercies of David. Henry says the sure mercies of David are that everlasting life, of which the resurrection was a sure pledge, and the blessings of redemption in Christ are a certain earnest, even in this world. — Matthew Henry

35 COMMENTARY: Wherefore he saith also in another psalm, Thou shalt not suffer thine Holy One to see corruption. Henry's contrast is David against the Holy One: David saw corruption, but he whom God raised again saw no corruption, and the psalm's Holy One is the risen Christ. — Matthew Henry

36 COMMENTARY: For David, after he had served his own generation by the will of God, fell on sleep, and was laid unto his fathers, and saw corruption. Henry notes David was a great blessing to the age wherein he lived, and we were not born for ourselves, but there are those living around us to whom we must study to be serviceable, yet here is the difference: David served his own generation and saw corruption. — Matthew Henry

37 COMMENTARY: But he, whom God raised again, saw no corruption. Henry draws the difference to its end: Christ was to serve all generations, and we may look to him who is declared to be the Son of God by his resurrection from the dead, that by faith in him we may walk with God and serve our generation according to his will, and when death comes, fall asleep in him with a joyful hope of a blessed resurrection. — Matthew Henry

38 COMMENTARY: Be it known unto you therefore, men and brethren, that through this man is preached unto you the forgiveness of sins. Henry's first thing for all hearers of the gospel is this: through this Man, who died and rose again, is preached unto you the forgiveness of sins, and your sins, though many and great, may be forgiven, without any injury to God's honour. — Matthew Henry

39 COMMENTARY: By him all that believe are justified from all things, from which ye could not be justified by the law of Moses. Henry's second thing is justification: it is by Christ only that those who believe in him, and none else, are justified from all things, from all the guilt and stain of sin, from which they could not be justified by the law of Moses, for the great concern of convinced sinners is to be justified, acquitted from all guilt and accepted as righteous in God's sight, and by Jesus Christ we obtain a complete justification, for by him a complete atonement was made for sin. — Matthew Henry

40 COMMENTARY: Beware therefore, lest that come upon you, which is spoken of in the prophets. Henry says the threatenings are warnings: what we are told will come upon impenitent sinners is designed to awaken us to beware lest it come upon us. — Matthew Henry

41 COMMENTARY: Behold, ye despisers, and wonder, and perish: for I work a work in your days, a work which ye shall in no wise believe, though a man declare it unto you. Henry's closing warning is that it ruins many that they despise religion, and those that will not wonder and be saved shall wonder and perish. — Matthew Henry

42 COMMENTARY: When the Jews were gone out of the synagogue, the Gentiles besought that these words might be preached to them the next sabbath. Henry contrasts the audiences: while many judge themselves unworthy of eternal life, others, who appear less likely, desire to hear more of the glad tidings of salvation, and this is according to what was foretold in the Old Testament. — Matthew Henry

43 COMMENTARY: Now when the congregation was broken up, many of the Jews and religious proselytes followed Paul and Barnabas, who, speaking to them, persuaded them to continue in the grace of God. Henry's desire for more continues here: the followers are persuaded to continue in the grace of God, the glad tidings taking hold beyond a single sabbath hearing. — Matthew Henry

44 COMMENTARY: The next sabbath day came almost the whole city together to hear the word of God. Henry's treasure point fits the crowd: what light, what power, what a treasure does this gospel bring with it, how excellent are its truths, its precepts, its promises, and almost the whole city gathers to hear it. — Matthew Henry

45 COMMENTARY: But when the Jews saw the multitudes, they were filled with envy, and spake against those things which were spoken by Paul, contradicting and blaspheming. Henry traces the descent: the Jews opposed the doctrine, and when they could find no objection, they blasphemed Christ and his gospel, for commonly those who begin with contradicting end with blaspheming. — Matthew Henry

46 COMMENTARY: Then Paul and Barnabas waxed bold, and said, It was necessary that the word of God should first have been spoken to you: but seeing ye put it from you, and judge yourselves unworthy of everlasting life, lo, we turn to the Gentiles. Henry's boldness rule is that when adversaries of Christ's cause are daring, its advocates should be the bolder, and the turning follows the Jews judging themselves unworthy of eternal life. — Matthew Henry

47 COMMENTARY: For so hath the Lord commanded us, saying, I have set thee to be a light of the Gentiles, that thou shouldest be for salvation unto the ends of the earth. Henry says this turning is according to what was foretold in the Old Testament, and the light of the Gentiles is the Lord's command, not the apostles' invention. — Matthew Henry

48 COMMENTARY: When the Gentiles heard this, they were glad, and glorified the word of the Lord: and as many as were ordained to eternal life believed. Henry explains the believing: those came to Christ whom the Father drew, and to whom the Spirit made the gospel call effectual, and as many as were disposed to eternal life, as many as had concern about their eternal state and aimed to make sure of eternal life, believed in Christ, in whom God has treasured up that life, and who is the only Way to it, and it was the grace of God that wrought it in them. — Matthew Henry

49 COMMENTARY: The word of the Lord was published throughout all the region. Henry's treasure spreads: what light, what power, what a treasure does this gospel bring with it, and the word published throughout the region is that treasure carried outward. — Matthew Henry

50 COMMENTARY: But the Jews stirred up the devout and honourable women, and the chief men of the city, and raised persecution against Paul and Barnabas, and expelled them out of their coasts. Henry notes it is good to see honourable women devout, for the less they have to do in the world, the more they should do for their own souls and the souls of others, but it is sad when, under colour of devotion to God, they try to show hatred to Christ, and here devotion is stirred into persecution. — Matthew Henry

52 COMMENTARY: The disciples were filled with joy, and with the Holy Ghost. Henry closes on the preparation joy gives: the more we relish the comforts and encouragements we meet with in the power of godliness, and the fuller our hearts are of them, the better prepared we are to face difficulties in the profession of godliness, and the disciples, expelled yet filled with joy and the Holy Ghost, show it. — Matthew Henry

No note for verse(s) 5, 6, 19, 20, 21, 24, 25, 51 — the public-domain sources consulted give no distinct explanation for these verses beyond the list itself, so nothing was invented for them.
