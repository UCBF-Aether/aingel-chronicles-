Study Notes — Acts

Chapter 14

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: It came to pass in Iconium, that they went both together into the synagogue of the Jews, and so spake, that a great multitude both of the Jews and also of the Greeks believed. Henry says the apostles spake so plainly, with such evidence and proof of the Spirit, and with such power, so warmly, and with such concern for the souls of men, that those who heard them could not but say God was with them of a truth, yet the success was not to be reckoned to the manner of their preaching, but to the Spirit of God who used that means. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: But the unbelieving Jews stirred up the Gentiles, and made their minds evil affected against the brethren. Henry notes the strange alliance: the Gentiles and Jews were at enmity with one another, yet united against Christians, and asks, if the church's enemies join to destroy it, shall not its friends unite for its preservation? — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: Long time therefore abode they speaking boldly in the Lord, which gave testimony unto the word of his grace, and granted signs and wonders to be done by their hands. Henry says perseverance in doing good, amidst dangers and hardships, is a blessed evidence of grace, and when they went on in Christ's name and strength, he failed not to give testimony to the word of his grace, which he has assured us is the word of God, upon which we may venture our souls. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: When there was an assault made both of the Gentiles, and also of the Jews with their rulers, to use them despitefully, and to stone them. Henry's perseverance point is the assault itself: perseverance in doing good amidst dangers and hardships is a blessed evidence of grace, and God has a shelter for his people in a storm, being their Hiding-place. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: They were ware of it, and fled unto Lystra and Derbe, cities of Lycaonia, and unto the region that lieth round about. Henry allows the flight: in times of persecution, believers may see cause to quit a spot, though they do not quit their Master's work, and wherever God's servants are driven, they should seek to declare the truth. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: And there they preached the gospel. Henry's flight rule is fulfilled here: they quit the spot but not the Master's work, and wherever God's servants are driven, they seek to declare the truth. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: There sat a certain man at Lystra, impotent in his feet, being a cripple from his mother's womb, who never had walked. Henry reads the cripple as the figure of our condition: all things are possible to those that believe, and when we have faith, that most precious gift of God, we shall be delivered from the spiritual helplessness in which we were born, and from the dominion of sinful habits since formed. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: The same heard Paul speak, who stedfastly beholding him, and perceiving that he had faith to be healed. Henry's faith point is the perception: all things are possible to those that believe, and Paul perceives that most precious gift of God in the hearer. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: Said with a loud voice, Stand upright on thy feet. And he leaped and walked. Henry's uprightness follows: with faith we shall be made able to stand upright and walk cheerfully in the ways of the Lord, and the cripple's leaping and walking is that uprightness given. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: When the people saw what Paul had done, they lifted up their voices, saying in the speech of Lycaonia, The gods are come down to us in the likeness of men. Henry contrasts the receptions: when Christ, the Son of God, appeared in the likeness of men and did many miracles, men were so far from doing sacrifice to him that they made him a sacrifice to their pride and malice, but Paul and Barnabas, upon working one miracle, were treated as gods, for the same power of the god of this world which closes the carnal mind against truth makes errors and mistakes find easy admission. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: They called Barnabas, Jupiter, and Paul, Mercurius, because he was the chief speaker. Henry's error-admission point is the naming: the carnal mind, closed against truth, finds errors and mistakes of easy admission, and the crowd readily names the apostles after its own gods. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: Which when the apostles, Barnabas and Paul, heard of, they rent their clothes, and ran in among the people, crying out. Henry notes what they did not rend their clothes over: we do not learn that they rent their clothes when the people spake of stoning them, but when they spake of worshipping them, they could not bear it, being more concerned for God's honour than their own. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: And saying, Sirs, why do ye these things? We also are men of like passions with you, and preach unto you that ye should turn from these vanities unto the living God, which made heaven, and earth, and the sea, and all things that are therein. Henry says the servants of God might easily obtain undue honours if they would wink at men's errors and vices, but they must dread and detest such respect more than any reproach, and when they had to do with the Gentiles, they must set right their mistakes in natural religion, against the false opinion that worship of a god under any name or in any manner is equally acceptable to the Lord Almighty. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: Who in times past suffered all nations to walk in their own ways. Henry's correction continues: the living God suffered nations to walk in their own ways in times past, yet he did not leave himself without witness, and past sufferance is not approval of the vanities. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: Nevertheless he left not himself without witness, in that he did good, and gave us rain from heaven, and fruitful seasons, filling our hearts with food and gladness. Henry's witness is this goodness: the living God left himself not without witness in doing good, giving rain from heaven and fruitful seasons, filling hearts with food and gladness. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: With these sayings scarce restrained they the people, that they had not done sacrifice unto them. Henry says the most powerful arguments, the most earnest and affectionate addresses, even with miracles, are scarcely enough to keep men from absurdities and abominations, much less can they, without special grace, turn the hearts of sinners to God and to holiness. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: There came thither certain Jews from Antioch and Iconium, who persuaded the people, and, having stoned Paul, drew him out of the city, supposing he had been dead. Henry says see how restless the rage of the Jews was against the gospel: the people stoned Paul in a popular tumult, for so strong is the bent of the corrupt and carnal heart that, as it is with great difficulty that men are kept back from evil on one side, so it is with great ease they are persuaded to evil on the other, and if Paul would have been Mercury, he might have been worshipped, but if he will be a faithful minister of Christ, he shall be stoned and thrown out of the city. — Matthew Henry

20 COMMENTARY: Howbeit, as the disciples stood round about him, he rose up, and came into the city, and the next day he departed with Barnabas to Derbe. Henry's perseverance point returns: perseverance in doing good, amidst dangers and hardships, is a blessed evidence of grace, and Paul, stoned and left for dead, rises and goes on the next day. — Matthew Henry

21 COMMENTARY: When they had preached the gospel to that city, and had taught many, they returned again to Lystra, and to Iconium, and to Antioch. Henry's establishing work begins in the return: ministers' work is to establish saints as well as to awaken sinners, and the apostles return to the very cities of persecution to do it. — Matthew Henry

22 COMMENTARY: Confirming the souls of the disciples, and exhorting them to continue in the faith, and that we must through much tribulation enter into the kingdom of God. Henry says all who are converted need to be confirmed in the faith, and all who are planted need to be rooted, and the grace of God, and nothing less, effectually establishes the souls of the disciples, and though we must count upon much tribulation, it is encouragement that we shall not be lost and perish in it. — Matthew Henry

23 COMMENTARY: When they had ordained them elders in every church, and had prayed with fasting, they commended them to the Lord, on whom they believed. Henry says the Person to whose power and grace the converts and newly-established churches are commended clearly was the Lord Jesus, on whom they had believed, and it was an act of worship, with the praise of all the little good we do ascribed to God, who not only worketh in us both to will and to do, but also worketh with us to make what we do successful. — Matthew Henry

26 COMMENTARY: Thence sailed to Antioch, from whence they had been recommended to the grace of God for the work which they fulfilled. Henry's ascription fits the homecoming: the praise of all the little good we do must be ascribed to God, and the work is fulfilled by the grace to which they had been recommended. — Matthew Henry

27 COMMENTARY: When they were come, and had gathered the church together, they rehearsed all that God had done with them, and how he had opened the door of faith unto the Gentiles. Henry says all who love the Lord Jesus will rejoice to hear that he has opened the door of faith wide to those who were strangers to him and to his salvation, and the rehearsal to the gathered church is that rejoicing. — Matthew Henry

28 COMMENTARY: There they abode long time with the disciples. Henry closes with abiding: let us, like the apostles, abide with those who know and love the Lord. — Matthew Henry

No note for verse(s) 4, 13, 24, 25 — the public-domain sources consulted give no distinct explanation for these verses beyond the list itself, so nothing was invented for them.
