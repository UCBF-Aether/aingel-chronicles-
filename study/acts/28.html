Study Notes — Acts

Chapter 28

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: When they were escaped, then they knew that the island was called Melita. Henry says God can make strangers to be friends, friends in distress, and the escaped knowing that the island was called Melita opens the strangers who prove friends in distress. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: The barbarous people shewed us no little kindness: for they kindled a fire, and received us every one, because of the present rain, and because of the cold. Henry says God can make strangers to be friends, friends in distress, and those who are despised for homely manners are often more friendly than the more polished, and the conduct of heathens, or persons called barbarians, condemns many in civilized nations, professing to be Christians, and the Lord raises up friends for his people in every place whither he leads them, and makes them blessings to those in affliction, and the barbarous people kindling a fire and receiving every one because of the present rain and cold is that kindness. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: When Paul had gathered a bundle of sticks, and laid them on the fire, there came a viper out of the heat, and fastened on his hand. Henry's viper is the danger from which deliverance comes: Paul gathered a bundle of sticks and laid them on the fire, and there came a viper out of the heat, and fastened on his hand, and observe Paul's deliverance from the danger in the next verse. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: When the barbarians saw the venomous beast hang on his hand, they said among themselves, No doubt this man is a murderer, whom, though he hath escaped the sea, yet vengeance suffereth not to live. Henry says the people thought that Paul was a murderer, and that the viper was sent by divine justice, to be the avenger of blood, for they knew that there is a God who governs the world, so that things do not come to pass by chance, no, not the smallest event, but all by divine direction, and that evil pursues sinners, and that there are good works which God will reward, and wicked works which he will punish, and also, that murder is a dreadful crime, one which shall not long go unpunished, but they thought all wicked people were punished in this life. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: He shook off the beast into the fire, and felt no harm. Henry says observe Paul's deliverance from the danger, and thus in the strength of the grace of Christ, believers shake off the temptations of Satan, with holy resolution, for when we despise the censures and reproaches of men, and look upon them with holy contempt, having the testimony of our consciences for us, then, like Paul, we shake off the viper into the fire, and it does us no harm, except we are kept by it from our duty. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: Howbeit they looked when he should have swollen, or fallen down dead suddenly: but after they had looked a great while, and saw no harm come to him, they changed their minds, and said that he was a god. Henry says though some are made examples in this world, to prove that there is a God and a Providence, yet many are left unpunished, to prove that there is a judgment to come, and they also thought all who were remarkably afflicted in this life were wicked people, but divine revelation sets this matter in a true light, for good men often are greatly afflicted in this life, for the trial and increase of their faith and patience, and God hereby made Paul remarkable among these people, and so made way for the receiving of the gospel. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: In the same quarters were possessions of the chief man of the island, whose name was Publius; who received us, and lodged us three days courteously. Henry says the Lord raises up friends for his people in every place whither he leads them, and makes them blessings to those in affliction, and Publius, the chief man of the island, receiving and lodging them three days courteously, is that raised-up friend. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: It came to pass, that the father of Publius lay sick of a fever and of a bloody flux: to whom Paul entered in, and prayed, and laid his hands on him, and healed him. Henry says God hereby made Paul remarkable among these people, and so made way for the receiving of the gospel, and the Lord raises up friends for his people in every place whither he leads them, and makes them blessings to those in affliction, and Paul entering in, praying, laying his hands on him, and healing the father of Publius sick of a fever and a bloody flux, is that blessing. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: So when this was done, others also, which had diseases in the island, came, and were healed. Henry's others healed are the gospel's way made: when this was done, others also, which had diseases in the island, came, and were healed, and God made Paul remarkable among these people, and so made way for the receiving of the gospel. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: Who also honoured us with many honours; and when we departed, they laded us with such things as were necessary. Henry's honours and necessaries are friendship's fruit: they honoured them with many honours, and when they departed, they laded them with such things as were necessary, and the Lord raises up friends for his people in every place whither he leads them, and makes them blessings to those in affliction. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: Where we found brethren, and were desired to tarry with them seven days: and so we went toward Rome. Henry says the common events of travelling are seldom worthy of being told, but the comfort of communion with the saints, and kindness shown by friends, deserve particular mention, and the brethren found at Puteoli, desiring them to tarry seven days, and so going toward Rome, is that communion and kindness. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: From thence, when the brethren heard of us, they came to meet us as far as Appii forum, and The three taverns: whom when Paul saw, he thanked God, and took courage. Henry says the Christians at Rome were so far from being ashamed of Paul, or afraid of owning him, because he was a prisoner, that they were the more careful to show him respect, and he had great comfort in this, and if our friends are kind to us, God puts it into their hearts, and we must give him the glory, and when we see those even in strange places, who bear Christ's name, fear God, and serve him, we should lift up our hearts to heaven in thanksgiving, and the brethren coming to meet them as far as Appii forum and The three taverns, whom Paul saw, thanked God, and took courage, is that comfort. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: When we came to Rome, the centurion delivered the prisoners to the captain of the guard: but Paul was suffered to dwell by himself with a soldier that kept him. Henry says how many great men have made their entry into Rome, crowned and in triumph, who really were plagues to the world, but here a good man makes his entry into Rome, chained as a poor captive, who was a greater blessing to the world than any other merely a man, and is not this enough to put us for ever out of conceit with worldly favour? And this may encourage God's prisoners, that he can give them favour in the eyes of those that carry them captives, and when God does not soon deliver his people out of bondage, yet makes it easy to them, or them easy under it, they have reason to be thankful, and Paul suffered to dwell by himself with a soldier that kept him is that easy bondage. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: It came to pass, that after three days Paul called the chief of the Jews together: and when they were come together, he said unto them, Men and brethren, though I have committed nothing against the people, or customs of our fathers, yet was I delivered prisoner from Jerusalem into the hands of the Romans. Henry says it was for the honour of Paul that those who examined his case, acquitted him, and in his appeal he sought not to accuse his nation, but only to clear himself, and true Christianity settles what is of common concern to all mankind, and is not built upon narrow opinions and private interests, for it aims at no worldly benefit or advantage, but all its gains are spiritual and eternal, and Paul calling the chief of the Jews together after three days, having committed nothing against the people or customs of the fathers, yet delivered prisoner from Jerusalem into the hands of the Romans, is that clearing of himself. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: Who, when they had examined me, would have let me go, because there was no cause of death in me. Henry says it was for the honour of Paul that those who examined his case, acquitted him, and the examiners would have let him go, because there was no cause of death in him. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: But when the Jews spake against it, I was constrained to appeal unto Caesar; not that I had ought to accuse my nation of. Henry says in his appeal he sought not to accuse his nation, but only to clear himself, and the constraint to appeal unto Caesar, not that he had ought to accuse his nation of, is that appeal. — Matthew Henry

20 COMMENTARY: For this cause therefore have I called for you, to see you, and to speak with you: because that for the hope of Israel I am bound with this chain. Henry's hope of Israel is the chain's cause: for this cause Paul called for them, to see them, and to speak with them, because that for the hope of Israel he is bound with this chain, and true Christianity's gains are spiritual and eternal, not worldly benefit. — Matthew Henry

21 COMMENTARY: They said unto him, We neither received letters out of Judaea concerning thee, neither any of the brethren that came shewed or spake any harm of thee. Henry's clean slate is the Roman Jews' answer: they neither received letters out of Judaea concerning Paul, neither did any of the brethren that came show or speak any harm of him, and it was for the honour of Paul that those who examined his case, acquitted him. — Matthew Henry

22 COMMENTARY: But we desire to hear of thee what thou thinkest: for as concerning this sect, we know that every where it is spoken against. Henry says it is, and always has been, the lot of Christ's holy religion, to be every where spoken against, and look through every town and village where Christ is exalted as the only Saviour of mankind, and where the people are called to follow him in newness of life, and we see those who give themselves up to Christ, still called a sect, a party, and reproached, and this is the treatment they are sure to receive, so long as there shall continue an ungodly man upon earth, and the sect every where spoken against is that lot. — Matthew Henry

23 COMMENTARY: When they had appointed him a day, there came many to him into his lodging; to whom he expounded and testified the kingdom of God, persuading them concerning Jesus, both out of the law of Moses, and out of the prophets, from morning till evening. Henry says Paul persuaded the Jews concerning Jesus, and the apostle kept to his principle, to know and preach nothing but Christ and him crucified, and he preached not himself, but Christ, and he was not ashamed of the gospel of Christ, and the appointed day's many coming into his lodging, to whom he expounded and testified the kingdom of God, persuading them concerning Jesus out of the law of Moses and the prophets, from morning till evening, is that persuasion. — Matthew Henry

24 COMMENTARY: Some believed the things which were spoken, and some believed not. Henry says Paul persuaded the Jews concerning Jesus, and some were wrought upon by the word, and others hardened, for some received the light, and others shut their eyes against it, and the same has always been the effect of the gospel. — Matthew Henry

25 COMMENTARY: When they agreed not among themselves, they departed, after that Paul had spoken one word, Well spake the Holy Ghost by Esaias the prophet unto our fathers. Henry says Paul parted with them, observing that the Holy Ghost had well described their state, and let all that hear the gospel, and do not heed it, tremble at their doom, for who shall heal them, if God does not? And they agreed not among themselves, and departed, after Paul had spoken one word, Well spake the Holy Ghost by Esaias the prophet unto our fathers. — Matthew Henry

26 COMMENTARY: Saying, Go unto this people, and say, Hearing ye shall hear, and shall not understand; and seeing ye shall see, and not perceive. Henry's Holy Ghost description is the saying: Go unto this people, and say, Hearing ye shall hear, and shall not understand, and seeing ye shall see, and not perceive, and the Holy Ghost had well described their state. — Matthew Henry

27 COMMENTARY: For the heart of this people is waxed gross, and their ears are dull of hearing, and their eyes have they closed; lest they should see with their eyes, and hear with their ears, and understand with their heart, and should be converted, and I should heal them. Henry's gross heart is the state described: the heart of this people is waxed gross, and their ears are dull of hearing, and their eyes have they closed, lest they should see, and hear, and understand, and be converted, and he should heal them, and let all that hear the gospel, and do not heed it, tremble at their doom. — Matthew Henry

28 COMMENTARY: Be it known therefore unto you, that the salvation of God is sent unto the Gentiles, and that they will hear it. Henry says while we mourn on account of such despisers, we should rejoice that the salvation of God is sent to others, who will receive it, and if we are of that number, we should be thankful to him who hath made us to differ, and be it known unto them, that the salvation of God is sent unto the Gentiles, and that they will hear it. — Matthew Henry

29 COMMENTARY: When he had said these words, the Jews departed, and had great reasoning among themselves. Henry says the Jews had afterwards much reasoning among themselves, and many have great reasoning, who do not reason aright, for they find fault with one another's opinions, yet will not yield to truth, and nor will men's reasoning among themselves convince them, without the grace of God to open their understandings. — Matthew Henry

30 COMMENTARY: Paul dwelt two whole years in his own hired house, and received all that came in unto him. Henry says though Paul was placed in a very narrow opportunity for being useful, he was not disturbed in it, and though it was not a wide door that was opened to him, yet no man was suffered to shut it, and to many it was an effectual door, so that there were saints even in Nero's household, and we learn also how God overruled Paul's imprisonment for the furtherance of the gospel, and not the residents at Rome only, but all the church of Christ, to the present day, and in the most remote corner of the globe, have abundant reason to bless God, that during the most mature period of his Christian life and experience, he was detained a prisoner, and Paul dwelt two whole years in his own hired house, and received all that came in unto him. — Matthew Henry

31 COMMENTARY: Preaching the kingdom of God, and teaching those things which concern the Lord Jesus Christ, with all confidence, no man forbidding him. Henry says the apostle kept to his principle, to know and preach nothing but Christ and him crucified, and Christians, when tempted from their main business, should bring themselves back with this question, What does this concern the Lord Jesus? What tendency has it to bring us to him, and to keep us walking in him? And the apostle preached not himself, but Christ, and he was not ashamed of the gospel of Christ, and though it was not a wide door that was opened to him, yet no man was suffered to shut it, and it was from his prison, probably chained hand to hand to the soldier who kept him, that the apostle wrote the epistles to the Ephesians, Philippians, Colossians, and Hebrews, epistles showing, perhaps more than any others, the Christian love with which his heart overflowed, and the Christian experience with which his soul was filled, and the believer of the present time may have less of triumph, and less of heavenly joy, than the apostle, but every follower of the same Saviour, is equally sure of safety and peace at the last, and Paul preached the kingdom of God, and taught those things which concern the Lord Jesus Christ, with all confidence, no man forbidding him. — Matthew Henry

No note for verse(s) 11, 12, 13 — the public-domain sources consulted give no distinct explanation for these verses beyond the list itself, so nothing was invented for them.
