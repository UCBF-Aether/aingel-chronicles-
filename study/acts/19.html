Study Notes — Acts

Chapter 19

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Paul having passed through the upper coasts came to Ephesus, and finding certain disciples. Henry says Paul, at Ephesus, found some religious persons, who looked to Jesus as the Messiah, but they had not been led to expect the miraculous powers of the Holy Ghost, nor were they informed that the gospel was especially the ministration of the Spirit, though they spake as ready to welcome the notice of it. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: He said unto them, Have ye received the Holy Ghost since ye believed? And they said unto him, We have not so much as heard whether there be any Holy Ghost. Henry says though we do not now expect miraculous powers, yet all who profess to be disciples of Christ should be called on to examine whether they have received the seal of the Holy Ghost, in his sanctifying influences, to the sincerity of their faith, for many seem not to have heard that there is a Holy Ghost, and many deem all that is spoken concerning his graces and comforts to be delusion. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: He said unto them, Unto what then were ye baptized? And they said, Unto John's baptism. Henry says of such it may properly be inquired, Unto what, then, were ye baptized? for they evidently know not the meaning of that outward sign on which they place great dependence. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: Then said Paul, John verily baptized with the baptism of repentance, saying unto the people, that they should believe on him which should come after him, that is, on Christ Jesus. Henry says Paul shows them that John never designed that those he baptized should rest there, but told them that they should believe on him who should come after him, that is, on Christ Jesus. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: When they heard this, they were baptized in the name of the Lord Jesus. Henry says they thankfully accepted the discovery, and were baptized in the name of the Lord Jesus. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: When Paul had laid his hands upon them, the Holy Ghost came on them, and they spake with tongues, and prophesied. Henry says the Holy Ghost came upon them in a surprising, overpowering manner, and they spake with tongues, and prophesied, as the apostles and the first Gentile converts did. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: He went into the synagogue, and spake boldly for the space of three months, disputing and persuading the things concerning the kingdom of God. Henry's bold teaching is the work God was pleased to confirm, that if hearers believed the teachers not, they might believe the works, and Paul's three months of disputing and persuading are that teaching. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: But when divers were hardened, and believed not, but spake evil of that way before the multitude, he departed from them, and separated the disciples, disputing daily in the school of one Tyrannus. Henry's separation rule is this departure: when arguments and persuasions only harden men in unbelief and blasphemy, we must separate ourselves and others from such unholy company. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: This continued by the space of two years, so that all they which dwelt in Asia heard the word of the Lord Jesus, both Jews and Greeks. Henry's separated school bears this fruit: the teaching continued two years, so that all in Asia heard the word of the Lord Jesus, confirmed by the works that followed. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: God wrought special miracles by the hands of Paul. Henry says God was pleased to confirm the teaching of these holy men of old, that if their hearers believed them not, they might believe the works, and the special miracles are that confirmation. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: So that from his body were brought unto the sick handkerchiefs or aprons, and the diseases departed from them, and the evil spirits went out of them. Henry's confirmation by works is these handkerchiefs and aprons: God confirmed the teaching, that if hearers believed not the teachers, they might believe the works. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: Then certain of the vagabond Jews, exorcists, took upon them to call over them which had evil spirits the name of the Lord Jesus, saying, We adjure you by Jesus whom Paul preacheth. Henry says it was common, especially among the Jews, for persons to profess or to try to cast out evil spirits, and warns that if we resist the devil by faith in Christ, he will flee from us, but if we think to resist him by the using of Christ's name, or his works, as a spell or charm, Satan will prevail against us. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: There were seven sons of one Sceva, a Jew, and chief of the priests, which did so. Henry's spell-users are these seven sons: professing exorcists who took upon them to call over evil spirits the name of the Lord Jesus, using Christ's name as a charm rather than resisting by faith in Christ. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: The evil spirit answered and said, Jesus I know, and Paul I know; but who are ye? Henry's prevailing of Satan is the spirit's answer: the name used as a spell has no authority in the users, for the spirit knows Jesus and Paul, but asks, who are ye? — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: The man in whom the evil spirit was leaped on them, and overcame them, and prevailed against them, so that they fled out of that house naked and wounded. Henry's warning is fulfilled in the flight: if we think to resist the devil by using Christ's name as a spell or charm, Satan will prevail against us, and the possessed man overcomes the exorcists. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: This was known to all the Jews and Greeks also dwelling at Ephesus, and fear fell on them all, and the name of the Lord Jesus was magnified. Henry's disgrace turns to honour: the exorcists' defeat was known to all, fear fell on them, and the name of the Lord Jesus, misused as a charm, was magnified. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: Many that believed came, and confessed, and shewed their deeds. Henry says where there is true sorrow for sin, there will be free confession of sin to God in every prayer and to man whom we have offended, when the case requires it, and the believers' coming, confessing, and showing their deeds is that sorrow made free. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: Many of them also which used curious arts brought their books together, and burned them before all men, and they counted the price of them, and found it fifty thousand pieces of silver. Henry says surely if the word of God prevailed among us, many lewd, infidel, and wicked books would be burned by their possessors, and asks whether these Ephesian converts will not rise up in judgement against professors who traffic in such works for gain or allow themselves to possess them, for if we desire to be in earnest in the great work of salvation, every pursuit and enjoyment must be given up which hinders the effect of the gospel upon the mind, or loosens its hold upon the heart. — Matthew Henry

20 COMMENTARY: So mightily grew the word of God and prevailed. Henry's prevailing word is the book-burning's fruit: the word of God prevailed among the Ephesians, and so mightily grew the word of God and prevailed. — Matthew Henry

24 COMMENTARY: For a certain man named Demetrius, a silversmith, which made silver shrines for Diana, brought no small gain unto the craftsmen. Henry explains the trade: persons who came from afar to pay their devotions at the temple of Ephesus bought little silver shrines, or models of the temple, to carry home with them, and craftsmen make advantage to themselves of people's superstition, and serve their worldly ends by it. — Matthew Henry

25 COMMENTARY: Whom he called together with the workmen of like occupation, and said, Sirs, ye know that by this craft we have our wealth. Henry says men are jealous for that by which they get their wealth, and many set themselves against the gospel of Christ, because it calls men from all unlawful crafts, however much wealth is to be gotten by them, and Demetrius's assembly is jealousy for the craft's wealth. — Matthew Henry

26 COMMENTARY: Moreover ye see and hear, that not alone at Ephesus, but almost throughout all Asia, this Paul hath persuaded and turned away much people, saying that they be no gods, which are made with hands. Henry's gospel threat to the craft is Paul's persuasion itself: the gospel calls men from unlawful crafts, and Paul, persuading and turning away much people with the word that gods made with hands are no gods, threatens the shrine trade throughout Asia. — Matthew Henry

27 COMMENTARY: So that not only this our craft is in danger to be set at nought; but also that the temple of the great goddess Diana should be despised, and her magnificence should be destroyed, whom all Asia and the world worshippeth. Henry says there are persons who will stickle for what is most grossly absurd, unreasonable, and false, as this, that those are gods which are made with hands, if it has but worldly interest on its side, and Demetrius's defence of Diana's magnificence is that stickling. — Matthew Henry

28 COMMENTARY: When they heard these sayings, they were full of wrath, and cried out, saying, Great is Diana of the Ephesians. Henry says the whole city was full of confusion, the common and natural effect of zeal for false religion, and the wrathful cry, Great is Diana, is that zeal breaking out. — Matthew Henry

29 COMMENTARY: The whole city was filled with confusion: and having caught Gaius and Aristarchus, men of Macedonia, Paul's companions in travel, they rushed with one accord into the theatre. Henry repeats the effect: the whole city was full of confusion, the common and natural effect of zeal for false religion, and the seizure of Paul's companions and rush into the theatre are that confusion. — Matthew Henry

30 COMMENTARY: When Paul would have entered in unto the people, the disciples suffered him not. Henry says zeal for the honour of Christ, and love to the brethren, encourage zealous believers to venture into danger, and Paul would have entered, but the disciples suffered him not, restraining the venture. — Matthew Henry

31 COMMENTARY: Certain of the chief of Asia, which were his friends, sent unto him, desiring him that he would not adventure himself into the theatre. Henry says friends will often be raised up among those who are strangers to true religion, but have observed the honest and consistent behaviour of Christians, and the chief of Asia, Paul's friends, sending to desire he not adventure himself, are those friends. — Matthew Henry

32 COMMENTARY: Some therefore cried one thing, and some another: for the assembly was confused: and the more part knew not wherefore they were come together. Henry's confused assembly is the false zeal's fruit: the whole city full of confusion, the assembly confused, with the more part not knowing wherefore they were come together. — Matthew Henry

33 COMMENTARY: They drew Alexander out of the multitude, the Jews putting him forward. And Alexander beckoned with the hand, and would have made his defence unto the people. Henry says the Jews came forward in this tumult, and those who are thus careful to distinguish themselves from the servants of Christ now, and are afraid of being taken for them, shall have their doom accordingly in the great day, and Alexander, put forward by the Jews, is that distinguishing. — Matthew Henry

34 COMMENTARY: But when they knew that he was a Jew, all with one voice about the space of two hours cried out, Great is Diana of the Ephesians. Henry's false zeal drowns the defence: the crowd, knowing Alexander was a Jew, cried with one voice for two hours, Great is Diana, and it is as safe to contend with wild beasts as with men enraged by party zeal and disappointed covetousness. — Matthew Henry

35 COMMENTARY: When the townclerk had appeased the people, he said, Ye men of Ephesus, what man is there that knoweth not how that the city of the Ephesians is a worshipper of the great goddess Diana, and of the image which fell down from Jupiter? Henry says one, having authority, at length stilled the noise, and the townclerk's appeasing is that stilling. — Matthew Henry

36 COMMENTARY: Seeing then that these things cannot be spoken against, ye ought to be quiet, and to do nothing rashly. Henry says it is a very good rule at all times, both in private and public affairs, not to be hasty and rash in our motions, but to take time to consider, and always to keep our passions under check, and we ought to be quiet, and to do nothing rashly, to do nothing in haste, of which we may repent at leisure. — Matthew Henry

37 COMMENTARY: For ye have brought hither these men, which are neither robbers of churches, nor yet blasphemers of your goddess. Henry's lawful-methods point fits the innocence: the regular methods of the law ought always to stop popular tumults, and the men brought hither are neither robbers of churches nor blasphemers of the goddess, so no tumult charge stands. — Matthew Henry

38 COMMENTARY: Wherefore if Demetrius, and the craftsmen which are with him, have a matter against any man, the law is open, and there are deputies: let them implead one another. Henry says the regular methods of the law ought always to stop popular tumults, and in well-governed nations will do so, and the townclerk's open law and deputies are those regular methods. — Matthew Henry

39 COMMENTARY: But if ye enquire any thing concerning other matters, it shall be determined in a lawful assembly. Henry repeats the rule: the regular methods of the law ought always to stop popular tumults, and other matters shall be determined in a lawful assembly, not by the theatre's confusion. — Matthew Henry

40 COMMENTARY: For we are in danger to be called in question for this day's uproar, there being no cause whereby we may give an account of this concourse. Henry says most people stand in awe of men's judgments more than of the judgement of God, and how well it were if we would thus quiet our disorderly appetites and passions, by considering the account we must shortly give to the Judge of heaven and earth, and see how the overruling providence of God keeps the public peace, by an unaccountable power over the spirits of men, so that the world is kept in some order, and men are held back from devouring each other. — Matthew Henry

41 COMMENTARY: When he had thus spoken, he dismissed the assembly. Henry closes on the stilling: the Lord on high is mightier than the noise of many waters, he can still the rage of the people, and the townclerk's dismissal ends the tumult. — Matthew Henry

No note for verse(s) 7, 21, 22, 23 — the public-domain sources consulted give no distinct explanation for these verses beyond the list itself, so nothing was invented for them.
