Study Notes — Acts

Chapter 4

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: As they spake to the people, the priests, the captain of the temple and the Sadducees came upon them. Henry's general point for the passage is that the harmless and useful servants of Christ are often troubled for their work of faith and labour of love, while wicked men escape, yet obeying Christ's precepts brings his support. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: They were grieved that the apostles taught the people and preached through Jesus the resurrection from the dead. Henry explains what was at stake: the resurrection preached through Jesus includes all the happiness of the future state, to be had through him only, so miserable is the case of those to whom the glory of Christ's kingdom is a grief. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: They laid hands on them and put them in hold until the next day, for it was now eventide. Henry notes that to this day reading the Scriptures, social prayer and religious conversation meet with frowns and checks, but if we obey the precepts of Christ, he will support us. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: Set in the midst, they were asked by what power or by what name they had done this. Henry answers with Peter's own answer: the miracle had been wrought by the name, or power, of Jesus of Nazareth, the Messiah whom the rulers had crucified. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: Then Peter, filled with the Holy Ghost, said unto them, Ye rulers of the people and elders of Israel. Henry stresses the filling: Peter would have all understand the miracle was by the name of Jesus, and this confirmed the apostles' testimony to his resurrection, which proved him to be the Messiah. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: Be it known unto you all that by the name of Jesus Christ of Nazareth, whom ye crucified, whom God raised from the dead, doth this man stand here before you whole. Henry presses the alternative: these rulers must either be saved by that Jesus whom they had crucified, or perish for ever. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: Neither is there salvation in any other, for there is none other name under heaven given among men whereby we must be saved. Henry generalizes the claim: the name of Jesus is given to men of every age and nation as that whereby alone believers are saved from the wrath to come. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: When they saw the boldness of Peter and John and perceived they were unlearned and ignorant men, they marvelled and took knowledge that they had been with Jesus. Henry turns this into a rule for followers of Christ: act so that all who converse with them may take knowledge they have been with Jesus, which makes them holy, heavenly, spiritual and cheerful, and raises them above this world. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: Beholding the man which was healed standing with them, they could say nothing against it. Henry's point for the council's deliberations fits here first: they cannot say the doctrine is false or dangerous or of any ill tendency, and are ashamed to own the true reason for opposing it. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: Commanded to go aside out of the council, the apostles left the rulers conferring among themselves. Henry diagnoses their care: all of it is that the doctrine of Christ spread not among the people, because it testifies against their hypocrisy, wickedness and tyranny. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: What shall we do to these men, for a notable miracle hath been done by them and we cannot deny it? Henry repeats the rulers' own admission as the key: the miracle is manifest to all in Jerusalem, yet their concern is only containment, not truth. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: But that it spread no further among the people, let us straitly threaten them that they speak henceforth to no man in this name. Henry reads the threat as the rulers' whole policy: they cannot refute the work, so they aim only to stop its spread. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: They called them and commanded them not to speak at all nor teach in the name of Jesus. Henry contrasts threatenings with promises: those who know how to put a just value upon Christ's promises know how to put just contempt upon the world's threatenings. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: Peter and John answered, Whether it be right in the sight of God to hearken unto you more than unto God, judge ye. Henry's rule is to guide conduct by the fixed standard of truth, not by the shifting opinions and fancies of men, and to beware the vain attempt to serve two masters, God and the world. — Matthew Henry

20 COMMENTARY: For we cannot but speak the things which we have seen and heard. Henry explains the apostles' refusal: they look with concern on perishing souls who cannot escape eternal ruin but by Jesus Christ, and therefore are faithful in warning and showing the right way. — Matthew Henry

23 COMMENTARY: Being let go, they went to their own company and reported all that the chief priests and elders had said. Henry observes that Christ's followers do best in company, provided it is their own company, and that it encourages God's servants in doing and suffering work to serve the God who made all things. — Matthew Henry

24 COMMENTARY: They lifted up their voice to God with one accord: Lord, thou art God, which hast made heaven and earth and the sea and all that in them is. Henry draws the comfort: they serve the God who made all things and therefore has the disposal of all events, and the Scriptures must be fulfilled. — Matthew Henry

25 COMMENTARY: Who by the mouth of thy servant David hast said, Why did the heathen rage, and the people imagine vain things? Henry treats the prayer as resting on fulfilled Scripture: what David spoke is now coming to pass, and the servants' confidence rests on a God whose word is being kept. — Matthew Henry

27 COMMENTARY: For of a truth against thy holy child Jesus, whom thou hast anointed, both Herod and Pontius Pilate, with the Gentiles and the people of Israel, were gathered together. Henry explains the anointing: Jesus was anointed to be a Saviour, therefore it was determined he should be a sacrifice to make atonement for sin, though sin is not the less evil for God's bringing good out of it. — Matthew Henry

28 COMMENTARY: For to do whatsoever thy hand and thy counsel determined before to be done. Henry holds the two truths together: the sacrifice was determined by God's counsel, yet the evil of the deeds is not lessened because God brings good out of them. — Matthew Henry

29 COMMENTARY: And now, Lord, behold their threatenings, and grant unto thy servants that with all boldness they may speak thy word. Henry notes what they do not pray: not that troubles may be prevented, but that they may go on with cheerfulness and courage in their work and duty, not fearing the face of man. — Matthew Henry

30 COMMENTARY: By stretching forth thine hand to heal, and that signs and wonders may be done by the name of thy holy child Jesus. Henry generalizes the prayer: those who desire divine aid and encouragement may depend upon having them, and ought to go forth and go on in the strength of the Lord God. — Matthew Henry

31 COMMENTARY: When they had prayed, the place was shaken where they were assembled, and they were all filled with the Holy Ghost and spake the word of God with boldness. Henry reads the shaking as God's sign of acceptance, given that their faith might be established and unshaken, with greater degrees of his Spirit enabling bold speech. — Matthew Henry

32 COMMENTARY: The multitude of them that believed were of one heart and of one soul, neither said any of them that aught of the things he possessed was his own, but they had all things common. Henry calls this love the blessed fruit of Christ's dying precept and dying prayer, possible because they sat so loose to the wealth of this world, dead to it and expecting to be stripped of all for cleaving to him. — Matthew Henry

33 COMMENTARY: With great power gave the apostles witness of the resurrection of the Lord Jesus, and great grace was upon them all. Henry says the doctrine preached was the resurrection of Christ, a matter of fact which, duly explained, was a summary of all the duties, privileges and comforts of Christians, with evident fruits of grace in all they said and did. — Matthew Henry

34 COMMENTARY: Neither was there any among them that lacked, for as many as were possessors of lands or houses sold them and brought the prices of the things sold. Henry explains the sharing: they did not take away others' property but were indifferent to their own, having in affection forsaken all for Christ, so that care was taken for everyone's supply. — Matthew Henry

35 COMMENTARY: And laid them down at the apostles' feet, and distribution was made unto every man according as he had need. Henry adds the stewardship rule: great care ought to be taken in distributing public charity, that it be given to such as have need, especially those reduced to want for well-doing and for the testimony of a good conscience. — Matthew Henry

36 COMMENTARY: Joses, surnamed by the apostles Barnabas, the son of consolation, a Levite of Cyprus, is named for this generous charity. Henry notes he was one designed to be a preacher of the gospel, and so disentangled himself from the affairs of this life, a disposition that gives the testimony very great power upon others. — Matthew Henry

37 COMMENTARY: Having land, Barnabas sold it and brought the money and laid it at the apostles' feet. Henry presents him as the particular example of the common life: when such generous dispositions prevail and are exercised according to the circumstances of the times, the testimony has very great power. — Matthew Henry

No note for verse(s) 4, 5, 6, 9, 11, 21, 22, 26 — the public-domain sources consulted give no distinct explanation for these verses beyond the list itself, so nothing was invented for them.
