Study Notes — Joel

Chapter 3

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: In the days when God brings again the captivity of Judah and Jerusalem, the deliverance promised at the end of chapter 2 is given its historical ground, and Henry notes this chapter is a comment on that promise, showing how the deliverance is wrought and perfected. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: God will gather all nations into the valley of Jehoshaphat and plead with them for his people and heritage Israel, whom they scattered and whose land they parted, and Henry notes that God's people are his heritage, his peculiar portion and treasure, so he has a good action against those who trespass upon them. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: They cast lots for God's people, giving a boy for a harlot and selling a girl for wine, treating human lives as small change for their pleasures, and Henry notes that even little persecutors will be called to account according to the wickedness of their endeavours. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: Tyre, Zidon, and Philistia are asked what they have to do with God, and whether they will recompense him, with swift recompense returning on their own heads, since vengeance is his and he will repay. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: They took God's silver and gold and carried his goodly pleasant things into their temples, robbing his house to enrich their idols. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: They sold the children of Judah and Jerusalem to the Grecians to remove them far from their border, turning captivity into exile at the far edge of the world. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: God will raise his people from the place where they were sold and return the recompense upon the sellers' own heads, reversing the exile deed for deed. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: Their sons and daughters will be sold to Judah, who will sell them to the Sabeans, a people far off, and Henry notes this was in part accomplished by the Maccabees' victories and looks forward to the last day, when the upright shall have dominion. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: The challenge is proclaimed among the Gentiles: prepare war, wake up the mighty men, let all men of war draw near, and Henry reads it ironically, as God bidding defiance to all opposition, since he who sits in heaven laughs at confederacies against him. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: They are told to beat plowshares into swords and pruning-hooks into spears, and let the weak say, I am strong, so that the enemy's total mobilisation becomes the measure of his coming defeat. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: The heathen must assemble and gather round about, while the prophet prays, Cause thy mighty ones to come down, O Lord, setting God's army against the gathered nations. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: The heathen must be wakened and come up to the valley of Jehoshaphat, where God will sit to judge them, and Henry notes Jehoshaphat signifies the judgment of the Lord, and that the challenge has now become a summons they cannot escape. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: The sickle must be put in because the harvest is ripe, the press is full and the fats overflow because their wickedness is great, so that judgment is pictured as a harvest whose ripeness is sin itself. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: Multitudes, multitudes stand in the valley of decision, for the day of the Lord is near in that valley, and the gathered nations become the crowd awaiting sentence. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: The sun and moon are darkened and the stars withdraw their shining, the heavens themselves dimming at the judgment of the nations. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: The Lord roars out of Zion and utters his voice from Jerusalem, so that heaven and earth shake, yet the Lord will be the hope of his people and the strength of Israel, and Henry notes that as blessings out of Zion are the sweetest blessings, so terrors out of Zion are the sorest terrors. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: Then they shall know that the Lord their God dwells in Zion, his holy mountain, and Jerusalem shall be holy with no strangers passing through her any more, and Henry calls this an appropriating and experimental knowledge, found in the worst of times. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: The mountains will drop down new wine, the hills flow with milk, all Judah's rivers run with waters, and a fountain from the house of the Lord will water the valley of Shittim, and Henry notes the fountain is Christ himself, whose sufferings, merit, and grace cleanse, refresh, and make fruitful. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: Egypt will be a desolation and Edom a desolate wilderness for their violence against Judah and innocent blood shed in their land, while the enemies' lands wither as God's house overflows. — Matthew Henry

20 COMMENTARY: Judah shall dwell for ever and Jerusalem from generation to generation, the permanence of God's people set against the desolation of their enemies. — Matthew Henry

21 COMMENTARY: God will cleanse their blood that he had not cleansed, for the Lord dwells in Zion, and the book closes where it began, with the Lord's own dwelling as the ground of cleansing and of rest. — Matthew Henry
