Study Notes — Joel

Chapter 2

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: The trumpet must be blown in Zion and an alarm sounded in the holy mountain, and Henry notes it is the work of ministers to warn of the fatal consequences of sin and reveal the wrath from heaven against ungodliness. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: The day of the Lord is a day of darkness and gloominess, with a great and strong people like none before or after, and Henry reads the description as both the locust devastation and the ravaging of the land by the Chaldeans. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: A fire devours before them and a flame burns behind them, the land as Eden before them and a desolate wilderness behind, with nothing escaping, so that the army leaves ruin in both directions. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: Their appearance is like horses, and as horsemen they run, swift and disciplined, the insect host taking on the terror of cavalry. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: Like the noise of chariots on the mountains and the crackling of flame devouring stubble, they advance as a strong people set in battle array, sound and fire joined in one image. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: Before their face the people are much pained and all faces gather blackness, the fear of the invader written on the faces of the invaded. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: They run like mighty men and climb the wall like men of war, each marching in his ways without breaking ranks, an army whose discipline makes it more dreadful. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: None thrusts another, each walks in his path, and falling on the sword they are not wounded, a host that cannot be disordered or stopped by ordinary resistance. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: They run to and fro in the city, run upon the wall, climb into houses, and enter windows like a thief, so that no wall, house, or shut door keeps them out. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: The earth quakes before them, the heavens tremble, the sun and moon are dark, and the stars withdraw their shining, creation itself darkening at the army's advance. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: The Lord utters his voice before his army, for his camp is very great and strong to execute his word, and the day of the Lord is great and very terrible, and Henry asks with the prophet who can abide it. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: Therefore the Lord calls his people to turn to him with all their heart, with fasting, weeping, and mourning, since the alarm is meant to produce repentance, not merely terror. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: They must rend their hearts and not their garments, and turn to the Lord who is gracious and merciful, slow to anger and of great kindness, and Henry stresses that rending garments is vain unless hearts are rent by abasement, sorrow for sin, and separation from it. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: Who knows if God will return and repent and leave a blessing behind him, even a meat and drink offering, and Henry notes that while forgiveness is certain to the truly penitent, removal of affliction is not promised, yet the probability of it should encourage repentance. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: The trumpet is blown again, a fast sanctified, and a solemn assembly called, and Henry notes the priests and rulers must appoint this fast, since outward expressions of sorrow must be ordered and shared. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: The whole people must gather, elders, children, and sucklings, with bridegroom from his chamber and bride from her closet, so that no age or joy exempts anyone from the common humiliation. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: The priests must weep between the porch and the altar, praying, Spare thy people, O Lord, and give not thy heritage to reproach, and Henry calls this the sinner's supplication: spare us, good Lord, lest the heathen ask where their God is. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: Then will the Lord be jealous for his land and pity his people, and Henry notes that God is ready to succour his people and waits to be gracious, answering prayer with real promises. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: The Lord answers by promising corn, wine, and oil in plenty, so that his people are satisfied and no more a reproach among the heathen, and Henry notes that with God saying and doing are not two things. — Matthew Henry

20 COMMENTARY: The northern army will be removed and driven into a barren and desolate land, its face toward the east sea and its hinder part toward the utmost sea, its stink rising because it has done great things, so that the invader's end matches its pride. — Matthew Henry

21 COMMENTARY: The land itself is told to fear not but be glad and rejoice, for the Lord will do great things, and the promise turns from the army's removal to the land's restoration. — Matthew Henry

22 COMMENTARY: The beasts of the field need not fear, for the pastures spring, the tree bears fruit, and fig and vine yield their strength, so that the animal creation shares in the restored plenty. — Matthew Henry

23 COMMENTARY: The children of Zion must rejoice in the Lord, who gives the former rain moderately and the latter rain in the first month, restoring the seasons themselves to their proper order. — Matthew Henry

24 COMMENTARY: The floors will be full of wheat and the fats overflow with wine and oil, a picture of plenty that some understand figuratively of gospel grace and the abundant comforts treasured up in the covenant. — Matthew Henry

25 COMMENTARY: God will restore the years the locust, cankerworm, caterpillar, and palmerworm have eaten, his great army which he sent, so that time itself, devoured by judgment, is given back. — Matthew Henry

26 COMMENTARY: The people will eat in plenty and be satisfied and praise the name of the Lord who has dealt wondrously with them, and his people shall never be ashamed. — Matthew Henry

27 COMMENTARY: They shall know that the Lord is in the midst of Israel, their God and none else, and his people shall never be ashamed, the presence of God being the ground of the promise. — Matthew Henry

28 COMMENTARY: Afterward God will pour out his Spirit upon all flesh, sons and daughters prophesying, old men dreaming dreams, and young men seeing visions, and Henry notes this promise began to be fulfilled on the day of Pentecost. — Matthew Henry

29 COMMENTARY: Upon servants and handmaids also God will pour out his Spirit in those days, so that the gift falls without regard to rank or station. — Matthew Henry

30 COMMENTARY: Wonders will appear in heaven and earth, blood, fire, and pillars of smoke, signs that accompany the great movements of God's judgment and grace. — Matthew Henry

31 COMMENTARY: The sun will turn to darkness and the moon to blood before the great and terrible day of the Lord, and Henry notes that God's judgments on a sinful world only go before the judgment of the world in the last day. — Matthew Henry

32 COMMENTARY: Whosoever calls on the name of the Lord shall be delivered, with deliverance in Mount Zion and Jerusalem and among the remnant whom the Lord calls, and Henry notes that calling on God supposes knowledge, faith, desire, dependence, and conscientious obedience as evidence of sincerity. — Matthew Henry
