Study Notes — Nahum

Chapter 3

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Woe to the bloody city, all full of lies and robbery, the prey departing not, the city of blood, shed by Nineveh, now to have its own blood shed, never ceasing to live by rapine and extortion. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: The noise of a whip, and the noise of rattling wheels, and of prancing horses, and of jumping chariots, the reader being transported into the midst of the fight, whips urging chariot horses, wheels rattling, horses prancing, chariots jumping. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: The horseman lifts up both the bright sword and the glittering spear, and there is a multitude of slain, and a great number of carcases, and none end of corpses, they stumble upon their corpses, the horseman advancing to assault, glitter of sword and flash of spear presenting the battle graphically, the Medo-Babylonian enemy stumbling upon Assyrian corpses. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: Because of the multitude of the whoredoms of the wellfavoured harlot, mistress of witchcrafts, that sells nations through her whoredoms and families through her witchcrafts, this assigning the reason for Nineveh's destruction, her harlot-like artifices alluring neighbouring states, as Ahaz was made tributary by Tiglath-pileser, and her witchcrafts being subtle machinations and love incantations ensnaring nations, selling them into loss of liberty. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: Behold, I am against thee, says the LORD of hosts, and I will discover thy skirts upon thy face, and show nations thy nakedness, and kingdoms thy shame, Nineveh being treated not as a matron but as a harlot whose shame is exposed, stripped of glory and defences on which she prides herself. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: I will cast abominable filth upon thee, and make thee vile, and set thee as a gazingstock, as infamous harlots used to be treated, exposed to public ignominy as a warning to others. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: It shall come to pass that all they that look upon thee shall flee from thee, and say, Nineveh is laid waste, who will bemoan her, whence shall I seek comforters for thee, onlookers fleeing her as horrible to look upon, with no comforters to be found. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: Art thou better than populous No, that was situate among the rivers, that had waters round about it, whose rampart was the sea, and her wall was from the sea, No-ammon being Thebes in Upper Egypt, possession of Ammon, famed for a hundred gates and a circumference of twenty-seven miles, with the Nile as sea-like rampart, yet overcome by Assyria, so Nineveh in turn shall be overcome by Babylon. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: Ethiopia and Egypt were her strength, and it was infinite, Put and Lubim were thy helpers, Cush mistress of Upper Egypt, Lower Egypt, Phut famed as archers west of Lower Egypt, and Lubim the Libyans, all endless resources and auxiliaries for No, yet insufficient. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: Yet was she carried away, she went into captivity, her young children also were dashed in pieces at the top of all streets, and they cast lots for her honourable men, and all her great men were bound in chains, notwithstanding all her might, her honourable men divided by lot as slaves. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: Thou also shalt be drunken, thou shalt be hid, thou also shalt seek strength because of the enemy, made to drink the cup of Jehovah's wrath, compelled to seek a hiding-place from the foe, and, like Thebes, to have recourse to other nations for help. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: All thy strong holds shall be like fig trees with the firstripe figs, if they be shaken, they shall even fall into the mouth of the eater, border strongholds protecting approaches to Nineveh falling with the rapidity and ease of first ripe figs shaken into the eater's mouth. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: Behold, thy people in the midst of thee are women, the gates of thy land shall be set wide open unto thine enemies, the fire shall devour thy bars, thy soldiers unable to fight, fortified passes through hills northeast of Nineveh thrown open, and bars of fortresses at the passes devoured by fire. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: Draw thee waters for the siege, fortify thy strong holds, go into clay, and tread the morter, make strong the brickkiln, an ironical exhortation to defend herself, drawing water lest cut off from fountains, and repairing the brick-kiln to supply kiln-burnt bricks for breaches or inner fortifications. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: There shall the fire devour thee, the sword shall cut thee off, it shall eat thee up like the cankerworm, make thyself many as the cankerworm, make thyself many as the locusts, in the very scene of her great preparations and security, fire as at Sardanapalus' palace conflagration, and however many her forces like swarming or licking locusts, the foe consuming her as the licking locust licks up all before it. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: Thou hast multiplied thy merchants above the stars of heaven, the cankerworm spoils, and flies away, Nineveh by canals having easy access to Babylon and the sea, Phoenicians carrying its wares everywhere, yet the licking locust, the Medo-Babylonian invaders, spoiling its merchants and flying away. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: Thy crowned are as the locusts, and thy captains as the great grasshoppers, which camp in the hedges in the cold day, but when the sun arises they flee away, and their place is not known where they are, princes wearing the tiara and captains, satraps or military leaders, as many as swarming locusts, cold depriving locusts of flight till the sun scatters them traceless, so Assyrian multitudes suddenly disappear. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

18 COMMENTARY: Thy shepherds slumber, O king of Assyria, thy nobles shall dwell in the dust, thy people is scattered upon the mountains, and no man gathers them, leaders lying in death's sleep, nobles in the dust, and people scattered as a necessary consequence of leaders laid low, with none to gather them. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

19 COMMENTARY: There is no healing of thy bruise, thy wound is grievous, all that hear the bruit of thee shall clap hands over thee, for upon whom has not thy wickedness passed continually, the report of her fall bringing joyful hand-clapping, her long-forborne and continual wickedness rendering her case one admitting no hope of restoration. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
