Study Notes — Nahum

Chapter 2

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: He that dashes in pieces is come up before thy face, keep the munition, watch the way, make thy loins strong, fortify thy power mightily, the dasher being God's battle axe, the Medo-Babylonian army under Cyaxares and Nabopolassar, and the advice to defend being ironical, since all defence will be in vain. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: For the LORD has turned away the excellency of Jacob, as the excellency of Israel, for the emptiers have emptied them out and marred their vine branches, the time for Nineveh's overthrow being ripe because Judah and Israel have been sufficiently chastised, and the Assyrian rod, having done its work, is to be thrown into the fire. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: The shield of his mighty men is made red, valiant men are in scarlet, chariots shall be with flaming torches in the day of his preparation, and fir trees shall be terribly shaken, the Medo-Babylonian general's men dyeing bull's-hide shields red lest blood be perceived, wearing crimson tunics, and their chariots flashing like torches with glitter of steel weapons, their fir-tree lances brandished. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: The chariots shall rage in the streets, they shall jostle one against another in the broad ways, they shall seem like torches, they shall run like lightnings, driven in furious haste through large open spaces in Nineveh's suburbs, flashing in the sun with rapid violence. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: He shall recount his worthies, they shall stumble in their walk, they shall make haste to the wall, and the defence shall be prepared, the Assyrian king reviewing his nobles and choosing the bravest for the walls in vain, stumbling through fear and hurry, while the covering machine of the besiegers advances. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: The gates of the rivers shall be opened, and the palace shall be dissolved, the Tigris river wall being 4,530 yards long with moats and dams that the foe turned to a dry bed, and in the earlier capture the river by flood breaking walls twenty furlongs, fire and water being the means of destruction. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: Huzzab shall be led away captive, she shall be brought up, and her maids shall lead her as with the voice of doves, tabering upon their breasts, Nineveh personified as a queen long standing in supreme prosperity, now laid bare and stripped, her people going up to Babylon, her handmaids moaning dove-like and beating their breasts as tambourines. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: But Nineveh is of old like a pool of water, yet they shall flee away, Stand, stand, shall they cry, but none shall look back, though Nineveh has from earliest times been defended by surrounding water, her inhabitants flee panic-stricken, and patriotic cries to stand go unheeded. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: Take the spoil of silver, take the spoil of gold, for there is none end of the store and glory out of all the pleasant furniture, the conquerors being summoned to plunder riches from tribute and merchandise, gold and silver being taken for spoil before palaces were fired, as the ruins confirm. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: She is empty, and void, and waste, and the heart melts, and knees smite together, and much pain is in all loins, and faces gather blackness, literally emptiness, emptiedness, devastation, a climax of substantives intensifying gloom, faces growing pale. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: Where is the dwelling of the lions, and the feedingplace of the young lions, where the lion, even the old lion, walked, and the lion's whelp, and none made them afraid, Nineveh being the seat of rapacious warriors typified by lions, frequent in Assyrian sculpture, full of spoils as a lion's den, and the question expressing wonder that its very site could not be found. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: The lion did tear in pieces enough for his whelps, and strangled for his lionesses, and filled his holes with prey, and his dens with ravin, the Assyrian lion filling its dens with prey of different kinds from the nations. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: Behold, I am against thee, says the LORD of hosts, and I will burn her chariots in the smoke, and the sword shall devour thy young lions, and I will cut off thy prey from the earth, and the voice of thy messengers shall no more be heard, chariots burnt entirely into smoke, prey cut off from the earth, and royal messengers no longer heard exacting tribute in the provinces. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
