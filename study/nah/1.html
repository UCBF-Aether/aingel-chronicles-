Study Notes — Nahum

Chapter 1

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: The burden of Nineveh, the book of the vision of Nahum the Elkoshite, is the prophetic doom of Nineveh, prophesied a hundred and fifty years after Jonah. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: God is jealous, and the LORD revengeth, the LORD revengeth and is furious, the LORD will take vengeance on adversaries and reserve wrath for enemies, jealousy here being stern yet tender affection, since God will not bear a rival in His claims on those He loves, nor admit His friends being oppressed while enemies flourish. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: The LORD is slow to anger and great in power, and will not at all acquit the wicked, the LORD having His way in whirlwind and storm, and clouds being the dust of His feet, His long-suffering not being want of power, and His vengeance sweeping the Assyrian foe like a whirlwind. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: He rebukes the sea and makes it dry, and dries up all rivers, Bashan and Carmel languish, and the flower of Lebanon languishes, Bashan famed for pastures, Carmel for corn and vineyards, Lebanon for forests, and nothing so blooming that God cannot change it when wroth. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: The mountains quake at Him, and the hills melt, and the earth is burned up at His presence, yea, the world and all that dwell therein, the earth heaving before Him. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: Who can stand before His indignation, and who can abide in the fierceness of His anger, His fury being poured out like fire, and rocks thrown down by Him, like liquid fire from volcanoes, breaking rocks asunder. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: The LORD is good, a strong hold in the day of trouble, and He knows them that trust in Him, Nahum here entering his special subject, assuring his people of safety under Sennacherib's impending attack, He knowing His own and so caring for and guarding them. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: But with an overrunning flood He will make an utter end of the place thereof, and darkness shall pursue His enemies, the flood being irresistible might overrunning barriers, perhaps alluding to Nineveh's capture through a river flood breaking the wall, and Nineveh's place itself being utterly destroyed. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: What do you imagine against the LORD, He will make an utter end, affliction shall not rise up the second time, an abrupt address to the Assyrians, mad to resist so powerful a God, Sennacherib's overthrow being an earnest of Nineveh's utter end, and Judah's affliction never rising again. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: For while they are folded together as thorns, and while drunken as drunkards, they shall be devoured as stubble fully dry, thorns entangled and thrown in a mass into fire picturing speedy destruction, and drunkards, bold yet weak and easily thrown down, picturing self-confident Assyrians rushing of their own accord into ruin. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: There is one come out of thee, that imagines evil against the LORD, a wicked counsellor, the cause of Nineveh's overthrow being Sennacherib's plots against Judah, from Nineveh itself arising the source of its own ruin, Belial meaning worthless and bad. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: Thus says the LORD, Though they be quiet and likewise many, yet thus shall they be cut down when he passes through, though I have afflicted thee, I will afflict thee no more, the quiet and many Assyrians being shorn as hair by a razor, retribution in kind, while Judah's affliction, temporary and corrective, ends. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: For now will I break his yoke from off thee, and burst thy bonds asunder, the Assyrian yoke being the tribute imposed by Sennacherib on Hezekiah, broken from off Judah. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: The LORD has given a commandment concerning thee, that no more of thy name be sown, out of the house of thy gods will I cut off graven and molten images, I will make thy grave, for thou art vile, the Assyrian king's dynasty becoming extinct, his gods cut off by the idol-hating Medes, and Nisroch's house becoming Sennacherib's grave. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: Behold upon the mountains the feet of him that brings good tidings, that publishes peace, O Judah, keep thy solemn feasts, perform thy vows, for the wicked shall no more pass through thee, he is utterly cut off, the messenger announcing Sennacherib's overthrow and Jerusalem's deliverance on the mountains round Jerusalem, a type of the Gospel heralding of spiritual deliverance by Messiah. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
