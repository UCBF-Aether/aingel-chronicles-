Study Notes — 1 John

Chapter 1

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: John writes as an eyewitness, not a theorist: the Word of life was heard, seen, looked upon, and handled. JFB notes the rising force of those verbs, from hearing to seeing to touching, as proof that Christ came in a real body and was known by the apostles over years of company with him. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: This verse breaks in as a parenthesis to explain the first: the life itself was manifested, having first been with the Father. The apostles saw that eternal life and now bear witness to it, so the epistle opens and closes on the same gift. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: The apostles declare what they saw and heard so that readers may share their fellowship, and that fellowship is with the Father and with his Son Jesus Christ. Henry stresses that this communion is open to any number, since its joy, unlike worldly goods, is not lessened by sharing. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: John writes these things so that joy may be full, the joy that belongs to fellowship with God. Henry treats this communion as all-sufficient happiness, and JFB notes that Christ himself is the source, object, and centre of that joy. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: The message handed on from Christ is that God is light, and in him is no darkness at all. As light is the source of all natural life and growth, so God is the fountain of wisdom, purity, and joy, with no speck of ignorance, error, or sin in him. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: To claim fellowship with God while walking in darkness is to lie and to fail the truth in practice, whatever is said. Henry's point is blunt: God holds no heavenly fellowship with unholy souls, and a profession contradicted by conduct shows its own falsehood. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: Walking in the light, as God himself is in the light, brings fellowship with one another, and the blood of Jesus Christ cleanses from all sin. JFB explains the cleansing as present and ongoing, the daily sanctification of believers already justified, rather than a single past act alone. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: Saying we have no sin deceives ourselves, not God, and shows the truth is not in us. JFB distinguishes this present sin, the corruption of the old nature still adhering to believers, from the actual sins confessed in the next verse, so that even holy walkers must own their need. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: Confession made with a contrite heart meets a God who is faithful to his promises and just in keeping them, so that he forgives sins and cleanses from all unrighteousness. Henry urges believers to plead guilty honestly and rely wholly on mercy through the righteousness of Christ. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: Claiming we have not sinned goes further than the self-deception of verse 8 and makes God a liar, driving his word from our hearts. Since the whole law and gospel rest on the fact that all have sinned, denying it rejects his word as a whole, not merely on one point. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
