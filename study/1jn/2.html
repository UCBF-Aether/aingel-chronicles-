Study Notes — 1 John

Chapter 2

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: John writes so that his readers may not sin at all, not so that grace becomes a licence for sin. If a believer does sin, he has an Advocate with the Father, Jesus Christ the righteous, who pleads on the ground of his own perfect obedience and endurance of the penalty, not on any goodness in us. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: Christ is himself the propitiation, the sacrifice that removes estrangement and appeases righteous wrath against sin. His advocacy is for believers, but his propitiation extends as widely as sin extends, to the sins of the whole world, so that no reader need think the Lord died for others but not for him. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: The test of knowing God is keeping his commandments, guarding them as a precious thing rather than faultlessly conforming at every point. JFB notes that this answers the boasted knowledge of those who set obedience aside: hearty acceptance of God's whole revealed will is the mark of real knowledge. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: A claim to know God, joined to disobedience, is a lie, and the truth is not in such a person. Henry puts it as a question: what knowledge of Christ can that be which does not see him as most worthy of entire obedience, and what honesty is there in a disobedient profession? — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: Whoever keeps his word shows the love of God perfected in him, growing toward its full maturity, and hereby we know we are in him. JFB traces a gradation here, from knowing him, to being in him, to abiding in him, as love and knowledge grow together. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: Claiming to abide in him obliges a person to walk as he walked, with deeds consistent with words. JFB stresses that the model is Christ's ordinary walk on earth, above all in love, rather than his wonders, and Henry adds that such obedience, if universal, would make earth resemble heaven. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: The commandment is no new invention: it is the old word of love heard from the beginning, when the readers first heard the gospel preached. Henry notes it had been in force from the beginning of the world, though it comes to Christians with new motives, rules, and obligations. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: The same commandment is new because Christianity first promulgated love on its highest motive, loving as Christ loved us, and because it is now being realised in believers. The darkness is passing away and the true light already shines, so the command is new in experience as well as in promulgation. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: Whoever claims to be in the light while hating his brother is in darkness even until now, for there is no middle ground between light and darkness, love and hatred. JFB notes that where spiritual life exists, however weak, love supplants hatred, and where love is absent hatred prevails. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: Loving one's brother is abiding in the light, and in such a person there is no occasion of stumbling. Gospel light not only illumines the understanding but warms the heart into love, giving an unimpeded path, whereas hatred brings both blindness and stumbling. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: Hatred keeps a person in darkness and walking in darkness, not knowing where he goes, because darkness has blinded his eyes. Henry warns that where spiritual darkness dwells, judgment and conscience are darkened and mistake the way to heavenly life. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: John addresses all his readers as little children in the sense of dear sons and daughters, and assures them their sins are forgiven for his name's sake. JFB treats this forgiveness as the universal first privilege of all God's children, shared alike by every stage of growth. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: Fathers are addressed for their settled knowledge of him who is from the beginning, young men for having overcome the wicked one, and little children for knowing the Father. Henry observes that even the longest-standing Christians still need advice and instruction, and none are too old to learn. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: The address is repeated with a difference: young men are strong because the word of God abides in them, and by that word they have overcome the wicked one. Henry makes it their glory to be strong in Christ and his grace, breaking off bad habits and connections and entering the strait gate of conversion. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: Love for the world and love for the Father cannot grow together: the more world-love prevails, the more Father-love decays. Henry allows that worldly things may be used for God's purposes and to his glory, but not sought or valued for the ends to which sin abuses them. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: All that is in the world is summed up in three ruling inclinations of depraved nature: the lust of the flesh, the lust of the eyes, and the pride of life. Henry glosses these as sensual appetite, delight in riches and possessions, and craving for grandeur, honour, and applause. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: The world passes away, and its lust with it, but the one who does the will of God abides forever. Henry contrasts fading vanities, whose desire itself will soon fail, with holy affection, which is not like the lust that passes away, and with the love of God, which shall never fail. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: It is the last time, and the presence of many antichrists, as foretold, is itself the sign of it. Henry treats every denier of Christ's person or offices as an antichrist, and the prophecy of seducers rising in the Christian world as a guard meant to keep believers from being seduced. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: Those who went out from the church were not of it, for had they been, they would have continued; their departure made manifest that they were not all of the body. Henry notes the church does not always know its true members, and that such separations prove true Christians and render them more watchful and humble. — Matthew Henry

20 COMMENTARY: True Christians have an unction from the Holy One, an anointing with grace, gifts, and spiritual privileges by the Holy Spirit, and so know all things needful to the truth. Henry insists this unction alone keeps believers from delusions, especially the falsehoods spread about the person of Christ. — Matthew Henry

21 COMMENTARY: John has not written because his readers do not know the truth, but because they do know it, and no lie is of the truth. Henry's counsel follows from this: judge favourably all who trust in Christ as the divine Saviour and obey his word, while pitying and praying for deniers of his Godhead and atonement. — Matthew Henry

22 COMMENTARY: The liar, in the pointed sense, is the one who denies that Jesus is the Christ, and such a one is antichrist, denying the Father and the Son together. Henry warns that the most hurtful lies the father of lies spreads are falsehoods about the person of Christ. — Matthew Henry

23 COMMENTARY: Whoever denies the Son does not have the Father, while whoever acknowledges the Son has the Father also. Denying the Son forfeits all part in the Father's favour while the great salvation is rejected, since the Father is known only in and through the Son. — Matthew Henry

24 COMMENTARY: Let what was heard from the beginning abide in you, and if it remains, you will continue in the Son and in the Father. Henry calls the truth of Christ abiding in us the means that severs us from sin and unites us to the Son of God. — Matthew Henry

25 COMMENTARY: The promise attached to abiding in the original gospel is eternal life itself, a promise suited to God's own greatness, power, and goodness. Henry urges readers to prize gospel truth accordingly, since thereby the promise of eternal life is made sure. — Matthew Henry

26 COMMENTARY: These things are written concerning those who would seduce the readers, naming the danger plainly rather than leaving it vague. The warning belongs with the encouragement that follows: the readers already possess, in the anointing, the safeguard against being led astray. — Matthew Henry

27 COMMENTARY: The anointing received from Christ abides in believers and teaches them all things, being truth and no lie, so that they need no seducing teacher to replace it. Henry says the Spirit of truth teaches all things necessary to the knowledge of God in Christ in this dispensation, and will not lie. — Matthew Henry

28 COMMENTARY: Abide in him, so that when he appears we may have confidence and not be ashamed before him at his coming. Henry repeats the affectionate address, little children, and notes that gospel privileges oblige gospel duties, and that constancy in religion in trying times shows birth from above. — Matthew Henry

29 COMMENTARY: If we know that he is righteous, we know that everyone who does righteousness is born of him. Henry's closing test for the chapter is the same: only those born of God bear his holy image and walk in his most righteous ways. — Matthew Henry
