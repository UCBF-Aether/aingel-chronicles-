Study Notes — 1 John

Chapter 5

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Whoever believes Jesus is the Christ is born of God, and everyone who loves him who begot loves also the one begotten of him. True love for God's people is distinguished from natural kindness or party attachment by being united with love of God and obedience to his commands. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: By this we know we love the children of God: when we love God and keep his commandments. The same Spirit that taught love will also have taught obedience, and a person cannot truly love God's children while habitually committing sin or neglecting known duty. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: This is the love of God, that we keep his commandments, and his commandments are not grievous. As holy, just, and good rules of liberty and happiness, they are not counted burdensome by those born of God, who lament only that they cannot serve him more perfectly. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: Whatever is born of God overcomes the world, and the victory that overcomes the world is our faith. Faith is the cause, means, instrument, and spiritual armour of victory, carrying believers above hindrances, though the conflict is often sharp and the regenerate may be cast down and rise again. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: Who overcomes the world but the one who believes Jesus is the Son of God? The real Christian, seeing in Christ's earthly life that this world is to be renounced and overcome, cannot be satisfied with it but presses toward heaven, for we must overcome the world or it will overcome us. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: This is he who came by water and blood, Jesus Christ, not by water only but by water and blood, and the Spirit bears witness because the Spirit is truth. Henry explains the water as washing and purification for heaven, and the blood as justification and reconciliation, both coming from the side of the sacrificed Redeemer. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: There are three that bear record in heaven, the Father, the Word, and the Holy Ghost, and these three are one. Henry notes the Father's voice from heaven declaring Jesus his beloved Son, the Word declaring he and the Father are one, and the Holy Ghost descending on Christ at his baptism and testifying by the prophets and apostles. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: There are three that bear witness on earth, the Spirit, the water, and the blood, and these three agree in one. The Spirit's regenerating work, the water's picture of purity and holiness, and the blood's ransom all testify to the same person and salvation, for one and the same purpose. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: If we receive the witness of men, the witness of God is greater, for this is the witness God testified of his Son. Henry calls it absurd to doubt Christianity while proceeding on human testimony in common affairs, and deeming out of his senses anyone who declined to do so. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: Whoever believes on the Son of God has the witness in himself, having felt the power of Christ's word humbling, healing, quickening, and comforting the soul, while whoever does not believe God has made him a liar. Unbelief is awful precisely because it rejects the record God gave of his Son. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: This is the record: God has given us eternal life, and this life is in his Son. The gift is not a thing apart from Christ but is located in him, so that possessing the Son and possessing life stand or fall together. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: Whoever has the Son has life, and whoever does not have the Son of God does not have life. No outward morality, learning, forms, or confidences can substitute for the Son, and one who refuses to trust his teaching, rely on his atonement, or obey him as King remains dead in sin. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: These things are written to those who believe on the name of the Son of God, that they may know they have eternal life and may go on believing. Believers already hold eternal life in the covenant of the gospel, and the letter's purpose is assured knowledge, not anxious guessing. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: This is the confidence we have in him: if we ask anything according to his will, he hears us. Prayer must always be offered in submission to God's will, and Christ invites believers to come in all circumstances, notwithstanding the sin that besets them. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: If we know he hears us, whatever we ask, we know we have the petitions we desired of him. Some prayers are speedily answered and others granted in the best manner though not as requested, but a hearing God is the ground of certainty either way. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: If a brother sins a sin not unto death, prayer should be made for him, and life will be given to such sinners, but for a sin unto death John does not say prayer for forgiveness should be made. Henry explains the limit: we cannot pray that impenitent, unbelieving sin be forgiven while the sinner wilfully continues in it, though we may pray for repentance and faith. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: All unrighteousness is sin, and there is a sin not unto death. Sins differ in their war against spiritual life, and the distinction matters for prayer: fallen believers are to be prayed for, recovered, and relieved, while no sin of which anyone truly repents is unto death. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: We know that whoever is born of God does not go on sinning, but the one begotten of God keeps himself, and the wicked one does not touch him. This repeats the letter's central incompatibility between the new birth and the practice of sin, now as a ground of assurance rather than warning alone. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: We know that we are of God, and the whole world lies in wickedness. All mankind divide into two dominions, God's and the wicked one's, and this general declaration includes unbelievers of every profession, station, and name, who do the wicked one's works and support his cause. — Matthew Henry

20 COMMENTARY: We know the Son of God has come and given us understanding, that we may know him who is true, and we are in him who is true, even in his Son Jesus Christ, who is the true God and eternal life. The Son leads believers to the Father, and they are in the love and favour of both by the indwelling Spirit. — Matthew Henry

21 COMMENTARY: Little children, keep yourselves from idols. The letter closes with a guard against every false god and false doctrine, and against the idolatrous love of worldly objects, so that believers are kept by God's power through faith unto eternal salvation. — Matthew Henry
