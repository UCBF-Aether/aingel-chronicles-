Study Notes — 1 John

Chapter 3

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Behold the surpassing love bestowed on us, that we should be called sons of God, and be so in reality. JFB notes that with God to call is to make really to be, and that doing righteousness is a sign of adoption, not the price that purchases it; the world does not know us because it did not know the Father. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: Beloved, we are sons of God now, though what we shall be has not yet been visibly manifested. When he appears we shall be like him, for we shall see him as he is, and continual beholding generates likeness, as the moon reflects the sun it faces. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: Everyone who has this hope resting on Christ purifies himself, even as he is pure. Henry warns that a hope which makes allowance for gratifying impure desires is the hope of hypocrites, not of the sons of God, and JFB adds that purification is worked by Christ's Spirit dwelling in us. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: Sin is defined here as transgression of the law: committing sin is going against God's law of purity, and so shows a person has no such hope of being pure as God is pure. John often states negatively what he has stated positively, showing where sin is, birth from God is not. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: Christ was manifested to take away our sins entirely, as the scapegoat did typically, and in him is no sin. Henry notes he took the sinless weaknesses that follow the fall, the infirmities that expose us to suffering and temptation, but never our moral proneness to sin. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: Whoever abides in him, as the branch in the vine, does not go on sinning, and whoever sins has not seen him nor known him. JFB qualifies the ideal carefully: believers do fall into sins, but such sins are alien to the life of God and need Christ's cleansing blood at once, and in so far as a person abides in Christ, he is free from sin. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: Let no man deceive you, as antinomians try to do: he who does righteousness is righteous, even as he is righteous. JFB explains the logic is reversed from earning: his doing does not make him righteous, but his being righteous through faith makes him do righteousness, as good fruit shows a good tree. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: He who commits sin is of the devil, who sins from the beginning, and for this purpose the Son of God was manifested, to destroy the works of the devil. Christians cannot practise what Christ came to destroy, and Henry adds that a man cannot act like the devil and at the same time be a disciple of Christ Jesus. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: Whoever is born of God does not commit sin, because his seed, the living word of God made by the Spirit the seed of new life, remains in him. JFB explains this as a present, energetic abiding: so long as the new life principle rules, sin can have no place, though the regenerate are sometimes surprised into sins when that principle lies dormant. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: In this the children of God and the children of the devil are manifest: whoever does not do righteousness is not of God, nor whoever does not love his brother. There is no middle class between the two families, and love of the brethren is the token that distinguishes them, not loud professions or seemingly good works. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: The message heard from the beginning is that we should love one another, the gospel announcement of him who loved us. JFB notes this love is aimed first at brethren in Christ, children of the same family of God, rather than mankind in the abstract. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: Cain is the counter-example: he was of the wicked one and slew his brother because his own works were evil and his brother's righteous. Envy and hatred of a brother's piety, because God accepted Abel's offering and rejected Cain's, is the seed of the woman's seed and the serpent's seed at enmity from the first. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: Do not marvel if the world hates you, for Cain is its representative and the marvel would be if the world loved you. Henry observes that none who rightly know the heart of man can wonder at the contempt and enmity of ungodly people against the children of God. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: We know we have passed from death to life because we love the brethren, and whoever does not love his brother abides in death. Henry treats this love as the special fruit of faith and a certain sign of being born again, not party zeal or affection for those of the same name and sentiments. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: Whoever hates his brother is a murderer in heart, and no murderer has eternal life abiding in him. The life of grace in a regenerate heart is the beginning of a life of glory, of which haters of their brethren must be destitute. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: Hereby we perceive the love of God, because Christ laid down his life for us, and we ought to lay down our lives for the brethren. Henry calls this the condescension, miracle, and mystery of divine love, that God would redeem the church with his own blood, and we should love those whom God so loved. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: Whoever has this world's goods, sees his brother in need, and shuts up compassion from him shows the love of God does not dwell in him. Henry warns that when the love of the world and its goods overcomes compassion for a perishing brother, every instance weakens the evidences of conversion, and habitual selfishness decides against a person. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: Let us not love in word or in tongue only, but in deed and in truth. The test of a true sense of Christ's love is practical compassion, not profession, and the Spirit is grieved at selfishness, leaving the selfish heart without comfort. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: Hereby we know we are of the truth and shall assure our hearts before him: deeds of love, not words alone, give the conscience its quiet ground. A well-informed conscience, heard and diligently attended to, is the way assurance is settled before God. — Matthew Henry

20 COMMENTARY: If our heart condemns us, God is greater than our heart and knows all things, so that condemnation must be weighed against his fuller knowledge, not merely our own feeling. Henry's counsel is to let conscience be well-informed and heard, since God also condemns known sin and neglected duty. — Matthew Henry

21 COMMENTARY: If our heart does not condemn us, then we have confidence toward God. This confidence belongs to believers who, through the Spirit of adoption and faith in the great High Priest, can approach their reconciled Father. — Matthew Henry

22 COMMENTARY: Whatever we ask, we receive, because we keep his commandments and do what is pleasing in his sight. Henry adds the condition that prayer is answered if it is good for us, and that good-will toward others, especially the brethren, must fill the hearts of those who go to God. — Matthew Henry

23 COMMENTARY: His commandment is twofold and one: believe on the name of his Son Jesus Christ, and love one another, as he gave commandment. Faith and love belong together here, and obedience to both is the shape of the Christian life. — Matthew Henry

24 COMMENTARY: Whoever keeps his commandments dwells in him and he in him, and we know he abides in us by the Spirit he has given us. Henry pictures this union as dwelling in Christ as an ark, refuge, and rest, and in the Father through him, a union worked by the Spirit who changes us from the power of Satan to the power of God. — Matthew Henry
