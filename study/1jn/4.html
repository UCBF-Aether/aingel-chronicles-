Study Notes — 1 John

Chapter 4

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Do not believe every spirit, but try the spirits whether they are of God, because many false prophets have gone out into the world. Henry notes that Christians well acquainted with Scripture may, in humble dependence on divine teaching, discern teachers who follow the apostles from those who contradict them. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: The Spirit of God is known by this: every spirit that confesses Jesus Christ come in the flesh is of God. The sum of revealed religion lies in the doctrine concerning Christ, his person and office, and confession of his true incarnation is the mark of the spirit of truth. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: Every spirit that does not confess Jesus Christ come in the flesh is not of God, and this is the spirit of antichrist, already in the world. Henry warns that false teachers speak according to the world's maxims and tastes so as not to offend carnal people, which is why the world approves and follows them. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: Little children, you are of God and have overcome them, because greater is he who is in you than he who is in the world. The indwelling Spirit is stronger than the world's spirit, and that is the ground of the believers' victory over false teaching. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: False teachers are of the world, so they speak of the world and the world hears them. Their rapid progress and many followers are no proof of truth: the world loves its own, and its own love it, suiting schemes and discourses to corrupt taste. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: We are of God: whoever knows God hears us, and whoever is not of God does not hear us, and hereby we know the spirit of truth and the spirit of error. Henry's rule is that the purer and holier a doctrine is, the more likely it is of God, and no other rule can try the spirits. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: Let us love one another, for love is of God, and everyone who loves is born of God and knows God. The Spirit of God is the Spirit of love, and one who does not love the image of God in his people has no saving knowledge of God. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: Whoever does not love does not know God, for God is love. It is God's nature to be kind and to give happiness, and the law of God is love, so that lovelessness betrays ignorance of him, whatever is professed. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: In this the love of God was manifested toward us: God sent his only begotten Son into the world that we might live through him. The provision of the gospel, forgiving sin and saving sinners consistently with God's glory and justice, is itself the display that God is love. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: Herein is love, not that we loved God, but that he loved us and sent his Son to be the propitiation for our sins. The origin of love between God and sinners is not our love for him but his free love for us, shown in the method by which we were saved when strict justice would have condemned us. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: Beloved, if God so loved us, we ought also to love one another. His love is not designed to be fruitless in us, and when its proper end is gained it may be said to be perfected, as faith is perfected by its works. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: No one has seen God at any time, yet if we love one another God dwells in us and his love is perfected in us. A loving Christian is, in Henry's phrase, a perfect Christian for any good duty, expert at it, because love oils the wheels of his affections and sets him on what helps his brethren. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: Hereby we know that we dwell in him and he in us, because he has given us of his Spirit. The test is inward and practical: those truly persuaded they are sons of God call him Father, hate sin and whatever disagrees with his will, and desire heartily to do his will. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: We have seen and testify that the Father sent the Son to be the Saviour of the world. The apostle attests this sending as an eyewitness truth: the Father willed the Son's coming into this world, and the Son came for the world's salvation. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: Whoever confesses that Jesus is the Son of God has God dwelling in him, and he in God. Henry explains this confession as faith in the heart as foundation, acknowledgment with the mouth to the glory of God and Christ, and profession in life and conduct against the flatteries and frowns of the world. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: We have known and believed the love God has toward us, and God is love, so whoever dwells in love dwells in God and God in him. That God dwells in us and we in him would be words too high for mortals, had not God himself put them before us. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: Herein is our love made perfect, that we may have boldness in the day of judgment, because as he is, so are we in this world. Happy are those with holy boldness before the Judge at that day, knowing he is their Friend and Advocate, and looking and waiting for his appearance. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: There is no fear in love, but perfect love casts out fear, because fear has torment, and whoever fears is not made perfect in love. Henry distinguishes the fear of God, high regard and veneration, from being afraid of him: obedience from love is a dutiful child's service to a beloved father, not a slave's toil from dread. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: We love him because he first loved us. This is the whole ground stated in one line: our love is response, not origin, and it is proved by its effects on temper and conduct toward the brethren. — Matthew Henry

20 COMMENTARY: If a person says he loves God and hates his brother, he is a liar, for how can he love God whom he has not seen if he does not love his brother whom he has seen? Henry is blunt: professed love of God joined to anger, revenge, or selfishness gives the profession the lie. — Matthew Henry

21 COMMENTARY: This commandment we have from him: whoever loves God must love his brother also. Love of God and love of brother are commanded together, and the change from natural enmity into affection and gratitude is the seal and earnest of eternal happiness. — Matthew Henry
