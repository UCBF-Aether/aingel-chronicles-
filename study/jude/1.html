Study Notes — Jude

Chapter 1

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Jude presents himself modestly as a servant of Christ and brother of James, whose standing in Jerusalem was widely known. His readers are described as loved within God the Father, kept continually by him for Jesus Christ against the day of his coming, and effectually called — their safety rests on God's keeping, not their own. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: Mercy comes first because the readers live in a time of wretchedness, and it is the mercy of Christ. Peace is peace in the Holy Spirit and love is the love of God, so the threefold greeting answers to the Trinity, and Jude prays all three may be multiplied in them and toward them. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: Jude had meant to write generally about the salvation all believers share, but the danger at hand forced a sharper letter. The faith was delivered once for all, so no new revelation can supersede it, and believers must both build themselves up in it and contend for it, like Nehemiah's workmen with a tool in one hand and a weapon in the other. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: The intruders slipped in stealthily and unlawfully, and their doom was forewritten in Scripture's long pattern of judgment on apostasy, from fallen angels to Sodom, Balaam, and Korah. They turn the gospel of grace into a license for sin and deny the only Master and Lord, Jesus Christ, whose absolute ownership over his creatures they refuse. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: Jude only reminds his readers of what they already know once for all: the same Lord who brought Israel safely out of Egypt afterward destroyed those who did not believe. The second act is the deliberate contrast to the first — being rescued does not exempt anyone from judgment for unbelief. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: The angels who would not stay content with the dignity assigned them under the Son of God left their bright heavenly habitation and aspired higher. God has kept them, in his purpose, in darkness for the judgment of the great day — already shut out from heaven and roaming the earth with restricted power, like condemned prisoners awaiting their doom. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: Sodom and the cities around it gave themselves over to unnatural sin, going after strange flesh, and now stand as a public example. The lasting marks of fire around the Dead Sea picture the eternal fire to which their inhabitants are consigned, a warning set before everyone's eyes. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: These men are dreamers in the sense that, like sleepers, they do not know what they are doing: their lusts stir them as dreams stir a sleeper, yet they have no self-command. Dreaming, they defile the flesh, despise lordship, and speak evil of dignities both earthly and heavenly, reviling what they do not understand. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: Michael, the one archangel of Scripture and champion of Israel, disputed with the devil in a judicial contest over the literal body of Moses, which Satan opposed raising on the ground of Moses' sins. Even so, Michael would not bring a railing accusation, but said, 'The Lord rebuke thee' — not from fear of Satan, but from reverence for the delegated dignity God had once given him. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: The intruders speak evil of the whole spiritual world, things they do not understand at all. What they do grasp, they grasp only by blind natural instinct, like unreasoning animals, and in those very things they corrupt themselves. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: Woe falls on three patterns at once: the way of Cain, whose root sin was hatred and envy of the godly; the error of Balaam, poured out like a burst torrent after gain whatever it costs; and the gainsaying of Korah, who aspired beyond his appointed ministry. The same seed bears the same harvest, so their end is already written in Korah's. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: At the love-feasts that accompanied the Lord's Supper these men are hidden rocks on which the fellowship may shipwreck, feasting without the fear such sacred meals require and pasturing themselves instead of the flock. They are clouds promising rain but carrying none, windswept and fruitless, trees of late autumn without leaves or fruit, twice dead and plucked up by the roots. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: Their shame foams out like raging waves of the sea, uncontrolled and public. They are wandering stars that burst on the world with a strange glare like erratic meteors instead of moving in a steady orbit as lights, and their end is to fall back into the blackness of darkness forever. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: Enoch, seventh from Adam, prophesied from the very beginning of the world, and the sacred number seven marks how ancient this witness is. His prophecy of the Lord coming with his holy angels looked beyond the flood to the final judgment, and its certainty is so sure that prophecy speaks of it as already past. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: The Lord comes to convict, that is, to prove guilty, every ungodly person of ungodly deeds and of the hard speeches they have aimed at God. Those who speak against God's children are counted as speaking against God himself. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: These are secret murmurers against God's ordinances and ministers, and complainers never satisfied with their lot — the restless insatiability of their own lusts is the secret of both. Their mouths speak great swelling words, yet they flatter the wealthy and powerful for whatever they can gain from them. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: In contrast to the reprobates, the beloved are told to remember words already spoken by the apostles, Peter and Paul among them. Jude does not exclude himself from the apostles; he points out that his readers themselves heard those apostles speak, which dates their warnings before this letter. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

18 COMMENTARY: The apostles used to tell them plainly that mockers, or scoffers, would come in the last time, walking after their own ungodly lusts. Jude quotes Peter's very words, showing that the present intruders are the fulfilment of that prediction, not a surprise. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

19 COMMENTARY: These men separate themselves from the vital, spiritual reality of church communion, even while they still outwardly take part in its ordinances. They are sensual, literally animal-souled: their spirit, which should receive the Holy Spirit and rule the soul, has sunk into serving earthly appetites, so they do not have the Spirit. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

20 COMMENTARY: Against that separation Jude sets the opposite work: building yourselves up on your most holy faith, as on a foundation, which is the same as building on Christ, the object of faith. That building goes together with praying in the Holy Spirit, who teaches what to pray and how, since no one prays aright except within his influence. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

21 COMMENTARY: Keeping yourselves does not mean keeping by your own strength, but staying in the love of God, which is the only guarantee of being kept safe. Verses 20 and 21 join Father, Son, and Holy Spirit with faith, hope, and love: faith built up, prayer in the Spirit, and hope looking for the mercy of the Lord Jesus Christ at his coming. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

22 COMMENTARY: Only those who keep themselves are likely to save others, and different cases need different treatment. Some manuscripts read 'convict' here rather than 'have compassion': those who are contending or wavering should be reproved until convinced, with the difference between cases carefully kept in view. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

23 COMMENTARY: Others must be snatched like brands out of the fire, a narrow escape from a hell-fire already licking at them, and a third class met with compassion accompanied by fear. That fear guards pity from becoming connivance: believers must hate even the garment spotted by the flesh, avoiding the remotest contact with sin. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

24 COMMENTARY: The letter closes in doxology to the one able to guard believers so they do not stumble, and to present them blameless before the presence of his glory. That presentation is made with exultation, like those who leap for joy, because the keeping from first to last is his work. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

25 COMMENTARY: Glory, majesty, dominion, and power belong to the only God our Saviour, through Jesus Christ our Lord — the oldest manuscripts add those words, since the Father saves through the mediation of the Son. Dominion is might and power is legitimate authority, his before all ages past, now, and to all ages to come. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
