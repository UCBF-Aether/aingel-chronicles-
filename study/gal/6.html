Study Notes — Galatians

Chapter 6

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Brethren, if a man is overtaken in a fault, the spiritual are to restore him in meekness, considering themselves, lest they also be tempted, since the better we know our own hearts the less we despise others. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: Bear one another's burdens, and so fulfill the law of Christ, mutual forbearance and compassion being obligatory after Christ's example, as fellow travelers help one another. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: For if a man thinks himself something when he is nothing, he deceives himself, pretending to what he has not and putting a cheat upon himself. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: But let every man prove his own work, and then he shall have rejoicing in himself alone and not in another, self-knowledge disposing him to help others under infirmities rather than to dictate to them. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: For every man shall bear his own burden, since no man can pay a ransom for his brother and each must reckon with God about his own sins. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: Let him taught in the word communicate to him who teaches in all good things, the taught supporting the teacher, a duty many excuse themselves from while making a show of religion. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: Be not deceived: God is not mocked, for whatever a man sows, that shall he also reap, since God knows hearts as well as actions and cannot be imposed upon. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: He who sows to his flesh shall of the flesh reap corruption, but he who sows to the Spirit shall of the Spirit reap life everlasting, present time being seed time and the other world harvest. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: Let them not be weary in well doing, for in due season they shall reap if they faint not, only perseverance in well doing having the reward promised. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: As they have opportunity, let them do good to all men, especially to those of the household of faith, making doing good the business of their lives while occasions offer. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: See how large a letter Paul has written with his own hand, a mark of his personal earnestness in this closing warning. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: As many as desire to make a fair show in the flesh constrain the Galatians to be circumcised, only lest they suffer persecution for the cross of Christ, proud and carnal hearts content with just enough religion to keep up a fair show. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: For neither do the circumcised themselves keep the law, but they desire the Galatians circumcised that they may glory in their flesh, seeking credit from others' conformity. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: But God forbid Paul should glory save in the cross of the Lord Jesus Christ, by whom the world is crucified to him and he to the world, his principal glory being a crucified Redeemer and salvation by him. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: For in Christ Jesus neither circumcision nor uncircumcision avails anything, but a new creature, old things passed away and new views and dispositions brought in by the Holy Spirit. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: As many as walk according to this rule, peace be on them, and mercy, and upon the Israel of God, the new creation to Christ's image being the greatest distinction between men. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: From henceforth let no man trouble Paul, for he bears in his body the marks of the Lord Jesus, scars from persecuting enemies for cleaving to Christ and the gospel. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: Brethren, the grace of the Lord Jesus Christ be with their spirit, Paul closing not with the law or works' righteousness but with Christ's grace, quickening, comforting, and strengthening them, to which he sets his Amen. — Matthew Henry
