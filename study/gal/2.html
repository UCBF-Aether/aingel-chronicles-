Study Notes — Galatians

Chapter 2

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Fourteen years after, Paul went up again to Jerusalem with Barnabas, taking Titus also, faithfully giving a full account of the doctrine he preached among the Gentiles, free from all mixture of Judaism. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: He went up by revelation and communicated privately to those of reputation the gospel he preaches among the Gentiles, using proper caution lest his past labors be lessened or his future usefulness hindered. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: But neither Titus, a Greek, was compelled to be circumcised, since what may lawfully be complied with must be refused when it would betray the truth. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: This was because of false brethren secretly brought in, who came privily to spy out the liberty believers have in Christ Jesus, that they might bring them into bondage. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: To them Paul gave place by subjection not for an hour, that the truth of the gospel might continue with the Galatians, since the gospel is God's, not men's, and men are only its keepers. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: Of those who seemed to be somewhat, Paul adds that whatever they were makes no matter, since God accepts no man's person, and they added nothing to his knowledge or authority. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: Contrariwise, when they saw the gospel of the uncircumcision committed to Paul as the gospel of the circumcision was to Peter, they acknowledged his apostleship. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: For he who worked effectually in Peter for the circumcision was mighty in Paul toward the Gentiles, the same God authoring both missions. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: When James, Cephas, and John, who seemed to be pillars, perceived the grace given to Paul, they gave him and Barnabas the right hand of fellowship, acknowledging him designed to the same apostolic office. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: Only they asked that they remember the poor, which Paul was forward to do, a pattern of Christian charity extending to all Christ's disciples regardless of differences of opinion. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: But when Peter came to Antioch, Paul withstood him to the face, because he was to be blamed, not fearing to reprove even Peter when the truth of the gospel and the church's peace were hurt. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: Before certain men came from James, Peter ate with the Gentiles, but when they came he withdrew and separated himself, fearing those of the circumcision. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: The other Jews dissembled likewise with him, so that even Barnabas was carried away with their dissimulation, Peter's timid conduct leading others to think the ceremonies necessary. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: When Paul saw they did not walk uprightly according to the truth of the gospel, he said to Peter before them all that if he, a Jew, lived as a Gentile and not as a Jew, he could not compel Gentiles to live as Jews, since by Christ's death the partition wall was taken down. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: Paul and Peter are Jews by nature, not sinners of the Gentiles, yet even they must be justified the same way. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: Knowing a man is not justified by works of the law but by faith in Jesus Christ, even they believed in Christ to be justified by faith, not by works, since the argument holds as strongly against dependence on moral works as on ceremonial ones. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: If, while seeking justification by Christ, believers themselves are found sinners, is Christ therefore the minister of sin? God forbid: that would dishonor Christ and hurt believers, and taking occasion from free grace to live in sin is what Christian hearts shudder at. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: For if Paul builds again the things he destroyed, he makes himself a transgressor, returning to a law whose sacrifices and cleansings are done away in Christ. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: For through the law Paul is dead to the law, that he might live to God, no more hoping or fearing anything from it, any more than a dead man from enemies. — Matthew Henry

20 COMMENTARY: Paul is crucified with Christ, yet he lives, yet not he, but Christ lives in him, the old man crucified and the new man living, grace quickened and sin mortified. — Matthew Henry

21 COMMENTARY: He does not frustrate God's grace, for if righteousness came by the law, then Christ died in vain, and grace cannot stand with man's merit. — Matthew Henry
