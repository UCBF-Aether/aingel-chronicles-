Study Notes — Galatians

Chapter 1

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Paul is an apostle not of men nor by man, but by Jesus Christ and God the Father who raised him from the dead, expressly appointed by Christ and therefore by the Father. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: He writes with all the brethren with him to the churches of Galatia, churches formed partly of converted Jews and partly of Gentile converts. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: Grace and peace come from God the Father and the Lord Jesus Christ, grace being God's good will and good work in us, and peace the inward comfort and outward prosperity truly needful, with grace first, since there is no true peace without grace. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: Christ gave himself for our sins to deliver us from this present evil world, according to God's will, an atonement God's justice required and to which Christ freely submitted, redeeming from wrath and from wicked practices alike. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: To God be glory forever and ever, since the greatness of the price shows sin to be the most horrible thing that can be expressed. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: Paul marvels that they are so soon removed from him who called them into the grace of Christ unto another gospel, a guilt he presses with tenderness, as people drawn away by troublers. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: That other gospel is not another at all; some trouble them and would pervert the gospel of Christ, setting up works of the law in place of Christ's righteousness. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: Though Paul himself or an angel from heaven preached any other gospel than the one preached to them, let him be accursed, so solemnly does Paul denounce any false foundation. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: As said before, so he says again: if any man preaches any other gospel than what they received, let him be accursed, since all other gospels are devices of Satan, whether flattering self-righteous pride or favoring worldly lusts. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: Paul does not seek to please men or persuade men rather than God, for if he still pleased men he would not be Christ's servant, and he will not alter Christ's doctrine to gain favor or avoid fury. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: He certifies that the gospel he preached is not after man, since in so important a matter he must not fear men's frowns or use men's wisdom. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: He neither received it of man nor was taught it, but by the revelation of Jesus Christ, not merely by education. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: They have heard of his former conduct in the Jews' religion, how beyond measure he persecuted the church of God and wasted it. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: He profited in the Jews' religion above many his equals, being exceedingly zealous for the traditions of his fathers, which makes his reversal the more evidently God's work. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: But when it pleased God, who separated him from his mother's womb and called him by his grace, his conversion was wrought by God's power and grace working in him. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: God revealed his Son in Paul, that he might preach him among the heathen, and Paul immediately conferred not with flesh and blood, obeying without hesitating over worldly interest, credit, ease, or life. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: Neither did he go up to Jerusalem to the apostles before him, but went into Arabia and returned to Damascus, showing his gospel came by revelation, not by apostolic instruction. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: Then after three years he went up to Jerusalem to see Peter and stayed fifteen days, a visit of fellowship, not of instruction. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: Other apostles he saw none, save James the Lord's brother, underscoring how little his doctrine owed to men. — Matthew Henry

20 COMMENTARY: What he writes, behold, before God, he lies not, solemnly affirming the account of his calling. — Matthew Henry

21 COMMENTARY: Afterwards he came into the regions of Syria and Cilicia, preaching where he had not been known. — Matthew Henry

22 COMMENTARY: He was unknown by face to the churches of Judea which were in Christ, so their praise of God for him rested on report alone. — Matthew Henry

23 COMMENTARY: They had heard only that he who once persecuted them now preaches the faith he once destroyed, a reversal that is itself matter of thanksgiving. — Matthew Henry

24 COMMENTARY: And they glorified God in him, as churches do when they hear of such instances to the praise of the glory of his grace, whether they have seen the person or not. — Matthew Henry
