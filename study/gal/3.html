Study Notes — Galatians

Chapter 3

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Paul calls them foolish Galatians, bewitched from obeying the truth, though Jesus Christ had been evidently set forth crucified before their eyes in preaching and in the Lord's Supper. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: He asks only this: did they receive the Spirit by works of the law or by hearing of faith, since God owned not the ministry of the law but the doctrine of faith with tokens of favor. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: Are they so foolish, having begun in the Spirit, to now be made perfect by the flesh, turning from the ministry blessed to their spiritual advantage to useless distinctions? — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: Have they suffered so many things in vain, if it be yet in vain, a loss that would make their past sufferings pointless. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: He who ministers the Spirit and works miracles among them does so not by works of the law but by hearing of faith, as the fruits of the Spirit appear most evidently among preachers of faith, not of law-works. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: Even as Abraham believed God and it was accounted to him for righteousness, his faith fastening on God's word and promise, so justification by faith is no new doctrine. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: Therefore those of faith are the children of Abraham, blessed in the same way he was blessed, through faith in God's promise. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: Scripture, foreseeing God would justify the heathen through faith, preached the gospel beforehand to Abraham, saying all nations would be blessed in him, the Holy Spirit who indited Scripture foreseeing this. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: So then those of faith are blessed with faithful Abraham, sharing his blessing by the same means. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: As many as are of works of the law are under the curse, for it is written that cursed is everyone who does not continue in all things written in the law, and all have sinned and stand guilty before God. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: That no man is justified by the law in God's sight is evident, for the just shall live by faith, and only those freed from death and wrath and restored to God's favor are righteous, which happens only through faith. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: The law is not of faith, but the man who does them shall live in them, so law and faith offer different terms, and mingling promise with law makes it nothing but law. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: Christ redeemed us from the curse of the law, being made a curse for us, made sin, a sin-offering, laid for a time under divine punishment, not separated from God. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: That the blessing of Abraham might come on the Gentiles through Jesus Christ, and that we might receive the promise of the Spirit through faith, Christ from the cross freely invites sinners to take refuge in him. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: Paul speaks after the manner of men: though it be but a man's covenant, once confirmed no man disannuls or adds to it, so much less can the law disannul God's covenant. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: To Abraham and his seed were the promises made, not to seeds as of many, but as of one, to thy seed, which is Christ, who abides forever with his spiritual seed, his by faith. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: The covenant confirmed before by God in Christ cannot be disannulled by the law given four hundred and thirty years afterward, so as to make the promise of no effect. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: For if the inheritance is of the law, it is no more of promise, but God gave it to Abraham by promise, the gospel promises being first made to Christ and then by him to those ingrafted into Christ by faith. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: Wherefore then serves the law? It was added because of transgressions, till the seed should come, to show the sinfulness of sin and lead men to see their need of the promise. — Matthew Henry

20 COMMENTARY: Now a mediator is not a mediator of one, but God is one, the law being given by angels and Moses as mediator, while the promise was given by God himself, so the law could not be designed to set the promise aside. — Matthew Henry

21 COMMENTARY: Is the law then against God's promises? God forbid, for if a law had been given which could give life, righteousness would indeed have come by law, but the holy, just, good law tends every way to promote the gospel. — Matthew Henry

22 COMMENTARY: Scripture has concluded all under sin, that the promise by faith in Jesus Christ might be given to those who believe, convincing them of guilt and of the law's insufficiency. — Matthew Henry

23 COMMENTARY: Before faith came, they were kept under the law, shut up unto the faith afterwards revealed, like children led to school by servants who have charge of them. — Matthew Henry

24 COMMENTARY: Wherefore the law was their schoolmaster to bring them to Christ, that they might be justified by faith, pointing by its rites and sacrifices to the one through whom alone pardon comes. — Matthew Henry

25 COMMENTARY: But after faith has come, they are no longer under a schoolmaster, enjoying a clearer discovery of divine grace than the Jews of old. — Matthew Henry

26 COMMENTARY: For all are children of God by faith in Christ Jesus, no longer servants kept at a distance under restraints, but sons. — Matthew Henry

27 COMMENTARY: As many as have been baptized into Christ have put on Christ, professing to be his disciples, baptized into his death to die to sin and walk in newness of life. — Matthew Henry

28 COMMENTARY: There is neither Jew nor Greek, bond nor free, male nor female, for all are one in Christ Jesus, the putting on of Christ consisting not in outward imitation but in a new birth. — Matthew Henry

29 COMMENTARY: And if they are Christ's, then they are Abraham's seed and heirs according to the promise, and he who makes believers heirs will provide for them. — Matthew Henry
