Study Notes — Galatians

Chapter 4

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: The heir, as long as he is a child, differs nothing from a servant, though he is lord of all, a picture of believers under the law's tutelage. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: But he is under tutors and governors until the time appointed by the father, as the law was a dispensation of darkness and bondage with burdensome rites. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: Even so, when they were children, they were in bondage under the elements of the world, tied to observances by which they were taught and kept subject. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: But when the fullness of time came, God sent forth his Son, made of a woman, made under the law, the Father's sending, the Son's low submission, and the Spirit's dwelling showing the wonders of divine love. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: He came to redeem those under the law, that they might receive the adoption of sons, by nature children of wrath becoming by grace children of love, all heirs as eldest sons. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: Because they are sons, God has sent the Spirit of his Son into their hearts, crying, Abba, Father, the Spirit witnessing with their spirits that they are children and heirs of God. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: Wherefore a believer is no more a servant but a son, and if a son, then an heir of God through Christ, and the temper and conduct of sons should ever show their adoption. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: Then, when they knew not God, they served those which by nature are no gods, idols being no gods at all. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: But now, after knowing God, or rather being known by God, how do they turn again to the weak and beggarly elements, to which they desire again to be in bondage, since all knowledge of God begins on his part. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: They observe days, months, times, and years, returning to legal observances for justification, a folly greater because of the mercy already shown them. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: Paul is afraid for them, lest he has bestowed labor on them in vain, yet he still labors, which, whatever follows, is true wisdom and the fear of God. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: Brethren, Paul beseeches them to be as he is, for he is as they are, and they have not injured him at all, desiring one mind with him about the law and union in love. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: They know how through infirmity of the flesh he preached the gospel to them at first, laboring under difficulty when he first came among them. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: His temptation in his flesh they despised not nor rejected, but received him as an angel of God, even as Christ Jesus, welcoming the messenger despite his infirmity. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: Where then is the blessedness they spoke of, for Paul bears record that if possible they would have plucked out their own eyes and given them to him, so uncertain are the favor and respect of men. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: Am I therefore become your enemy, because I tell you the truth? Christians must not forbear speaking truth for fear of offending, and Paul was their friend, not their enemy. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: The false teachers zealously affect them, but not well, seeking to exclude them that they might affect the teachers in return, pretending affection without sincerity. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: But it is good to be zealously affected always in a good thing, and not only when Paul is present, zeal being a rule to maintain always, not for a time only. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: My little children, of whom Paul travails in birth again until Christ be formed in them, shows his parental feeling toward them, anxious over their present delusions. — Matthew Henry

20 COMMENTARY: He desires to be present with them now and to change his voice, for he stands in doubt of them, and nothing so surely proves justification as Christ formed in a sinner by the Spirit's renewal. — Matthew Henry

21 COMMENTARY: Tell me, you who desire to be under the law, do you not hear the law itself, which Paul will now use against legal confidence? — Matthew Henry

22 COMMENTARY: For it is written that Abraham had two sons, one by a bondmaid, the other by a freewoman, the histories of Isaac and Ishmael explaining the two covenants. — Matthew Henry

23 COMMENTARY: He who was of the bondwoman was born after the flesh, but he of the freewoman was by promise, works of man's own strength being legal, while fruit arising from faith in Christ is evangelical. — Matthew Henry

24 COMMENTARY: These things are an allegory, for these are the two covenants, Hagar and Sarah being apt emblems of the two dispensations, beyond the literal sense the Spirit pointing further. — Matthew Henry

25 COMMENTARY: For Hagar is Mount Sinai in Arabia, answering to Jerusalem which now is, in bondage with her children, the first covenant spirit being bondage unto sin and death, and a spirit of persecution. — Matthew Henry

26 COMMENTARY: But Jerusalem which is above is free, which is the mother of us all, the heavenly Jerusalem, the true church from above, free and mother of all believers born of the Spirit. — Matthew Henry

27 COMMENTARY: For it is written, rejoice, barren that bearest not, break forth and cry, thou that travailest not, for the desolate has many more children than she who has a husband, the free covenant bearing children by promise. — Matthew Henry

28 COMMENTARY: Now they, brethren, as Isaac was, are children of promise, by regeneration and true faith made part of Abraham's true seed. — Matthew Henry

29 COMMENTARY: But as then he born after the flesh persecuted him born after the Spirit, even so it is now, the first covenant spirit persecuting while the second is a spirit of love. — Matthew Henry

30 COMMENTARY: Nevertheless what says Scripture? Cast out the bondwoman and her son, for the son of the bondwoman shall not be heir with the son of the freewoman, a text Paul applies directly. — Matthew Henry

31 COMMENTARY: So then, brethren, they are not children of the bondwoman but of the free, and it is absurd for Gentile converts to be under a law which could not deliver unbelieving Jews from bondage or condemnation. — Matthew Henry
