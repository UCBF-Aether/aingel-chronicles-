Study Notes — Galatians

Chapter 5

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Stand fast therefore in the liberty with which Christ has made them free, and be not entangled again with the yoke of bondage, since Christ will not be the Savior of any who will not own and rely on him as their only Savior. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: Behold, Paul says, if they are circumcised as a means of justification, Christ shall profit them nothing, since dependence on ceremonies displaces reliance on Christ alone. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: Paul testifies again to every man circumcised that he is a debtor to do the whole law, having taken up the whole covenant of works, not part of it. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: Christ has become of no effect to those justified by the law, for they are fallen from grace, grace being no grace unless freely given every way, and it cannot stand with man's merit. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: For through the Spirit believers wait for the hope of righteousness by faith, taught by the Spirit to expect eternal life as God's gift by faith in Christ, not for their own works. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: For in Jesus Christ neither circumcision nor uncircumcision avails anything, but faith which works by love, a working grace that loves God and brethren, without which all else is worthless. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: They did run well in the Christian race, but who hindered them from obeying the truth, since profession alone is not enough and they must run and hold on to obtain the prize. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: This persuasion comes not from him who calls them, the opinion of mixing law-works with faith being owing to none but Satan, as Paul leaves them to judge. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: A little leaven leavens the whole lump, so the first beginnings of such leaven must be opposed before destructive error spreads through a church. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: Paul has confidence in them through the Lord that they will be none otherwise minded, but he who troubles them shall bear his judgment, distinguishing between leaders and the led in reproof. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: And Paul asks, if he still preached circumcision, why does he still suffer persecution, since then the offense of the cross would have ceased, the Jews being offended precisely because Christ is preached as the only salvation. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: He wishes those who trouble them were even cut off, a sharp word against persistent disturbers of Christ's church, who must bear their judgment. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: Brethren have been called unto liberty, only not to use liberty as an occasion to the flesh, but by love to serve one another, since the gospel is a doctrine according to godliness and lays the strongest obligation to avoid sin. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: For all the law is fulfilled in one word, thou shalt love thy neighbor as thyself, love being the summary of the law's duty to others. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: But if they bite and devour one another, let them take heed they are not consumed by one another, since quarreling Christians invite the Spirit of love to depart and the evil spirit to prevail. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: Walk in the Spirit, and they shall not fulfill the lust of the flesh, acting under the Spirit's guidance and power so corrupt nature does not have dominion. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: For the flesh lusts against the Spirit and the Spirit against the flesh, contrary to one another, so believers cannot simply do whatever they would, being engaged in a real conflict. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: But if they are led by the Spirit, they are not under the law as a covenant of works nor exposed to its awful curse, their hatred of sin and desires after holiness showing their part in gospel salvation. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: Now the works of the flesh are manifest: adultery, fornication, uncleanness, lasciviousness, sins many and plain, which shut men out of heaven. — Matthew Henry

20 COMMENTARY: Idolatry, witchcraft, hatred, variance, emulations, wrath, strife, seditions, heresies continue the list, chiefly sins hurtful to men and tending to set them against one another. — Matthew Henry

21 COMMENTARY: Envyings, murders, drunkenness, revelings, and such like complete it, and Paul warns beforehand, as before, that those who practice such things shall not inherit God's kingdom. — Matthew Henry

22 COMMENTARY: But the fruit of the Spirit is love, joy, peace, longsuffering, gentleness, goodness, faith, fruits tending to make Christians agreeable to one another as well as happy. — Matthew Henry

23 COMMENTARY: Meekness and temperance complete the fruit, and against such there is no law, since the law condemns the works of the flesh, not the Spirit's fruit. — Matthew Henry

24 COMMENTARY: Those who are Christ's have crucified the flesh with its affections and lusts, sin no longer reigning in their mortal bodies so that they obey it. — Matthew Henry

25 COMMENTARY: If they live in the Spirit, let them also walk in the Spirit, conversation always answering to the principle that guides them. — Matthew Henry

26 COMMENTARY: Let them not be desirous of vain glory, provoking or envying one another, but seek to bring forth more abundantly the good fruits which are through Jesus Christ to God's praise and glory. — Matthew Henry
