Study Notes — Philemon

Chapter 1

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Paul, a prisoner of Jesus Christ, and Timothy our brother, unto Philemon our dearly beloved, and fellowlabourer, for faith in Christ and love to him should unite saints more closely than any outward relation can unite the people of the world. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

2 COMMENTARY: And to our beloved Apphia, and Archippus our fellowsoldier, and to the church in thy house, for faith in Christ and love to him should unite saints more closely than any outward relation. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

3 COMMENTARY: Grace to you, and peace, from God our Father and the Lord Jesus Christ, for grace is the best wish for ourselves and others, with which the apostle begins and ends, and all grace is from Christ, who purchased and bestows it. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

4 COMMENTARY: I thank my God, making mention of thee always in my prayers, for Paul in his private prayers was particular in remembering his friends, and we must remember Christian friends much and often, bearing them in our thoughts and upon our hearts before our God. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

5 COMMENTARY: Hearing of thy love and faith, which thou hast toward the Lord Jesus, and toward all saints, for Paul inquired concerning his friends as to the truth, growth, and fruitfulness of their graces, their faith in Christ and love to him and to all the saints. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

6 COMMENTARY: That the communication of thy faith may become effectual by the acknowledging of every good thing which is in you in Christ Jesus, for different sentiments and ways in what is not essential must not make difference of affection as to the truth, and the good Philemon did was matter of joy and comfort to him and others. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

7 COMMENTARY: For we have great joy and consolation in thy love, because the bowels of the saints are refreshed by thee, brother, for the good which Philemon did was matter of joy and comfort to him and others, who therefore desired that he would continue and abound in good fruits, more and more, to God's honour. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

8 COMMENTARY: Wherefore, though I might be much bold in Christ to enjoin thee that which is convenient, for it does not lower any one to condescend, and sometimes even to beseech, where in strictness of right we might command, and the apostle argues from love rather than authority. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

9 COMMENTARY: Yet for love's sake I rather beseech thee, being such an one as Paul the aged, and now also a prisoner of Jesus Christ, for the apostle argues from love rather than authority in behalf of one converted through his means, and this was Onesimus. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

10 COMMENTARY: I beseech thee for my son Onesimus, whom I have begotten in my bonds, for the apostle argues from love rather than authority in behalf of one converted through his means, and what happy changes conversion makes, of evil, good, and of unprofitable, useful. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

11 COMMENTARY: Which in time past was to thee unprofitable, but now profitable to thee and to me, for in allusion to that name, which signifies profitable, the apostle allows that in time past he had been unprofitable, but hastens to mention the change by which he had become profitable, and unholy persons are unprofitable, answering not the great end of their being. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

12 COMMENTARY: Whom I have sent again, thou therefore receive him, that is, mine own bowels, for religious servants are treasures in a family, making conscience of their time and trusts, and one great evidence of true repentance consists in returning to practise the duties which have been neglected. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

13 COMMENTARY: Whom I would have retained with me, that in thy stead he might have ministered unto me in the bonds of the gospel, for no prospect of usefulness should lead any to neglect their obligations, or to fail in obedience to superiors, and in his unconverted state Onesimus had withdrawn to his master's injury, but now, having seen his sin and repented, he was willing and desirous to return to his duty. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

14 COMMENTARY: But without thy mind would I do nothing, that thy benefit should not be as it were of necessity, but willingly, for no prospect of usefulness should lead any to neglect obligations, and little do men know for what purposes the Lord leaves some to change their situations, or engage in undertakings, perhaps from evil motives. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

15 COMMENTARY: For perhaps he therefore departed for a season, that thou shouldest receive him for ever, for when we speak of the nature of any sin or offence against God, its evil is not to be lessened, but in a penitent sinner, as God covers it, so must we, and such changed characters often become a blessing to all among whom they reside. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

16 COMMENTARY: Not now as a servant, but above a servant, a brother beloved, specially to me, but how much more unto thee, both in the flesh, and in the Lord, for Christianity does not do away our duties to others, but directs to the right doing of them, and the communion of saints does not destroy distinction of property. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

17 COMMENTARY: If thou count me therefore a partner, receive him as myself, for Philemon was Paul's son in the faith, yet he entreated him as a brother, and Onesimus was a poor slave, yet Paul besought for him as if seeking some great thing for himself. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

18 COMMENTARY: If he hath wronged thee, or oweth thee ought, put that on mine account, for true penitents will be open in owning their faults, especially in cases of injury done to others, and this passage is an instance of that being imputed to one, which is contracted by another, and of one becoming answerable for another by voluntary engagement, that he might be freed from punishment due to his crimes. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

19 COMMENTARY: I Paul have written it with mine own hand, I will repay it, albeit I do not say to thee how thou owest unto me even thine own self besides, for this is an instance of one becoming answerable for another by voluntary engagement, according to the doctrine that Christ of his own will bore the punishment of our sins, that we might receive the reward of his righteousness. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

20 COMMENTARY: Yea, brother, let me have joy of thee in the Lord, refresh my bowels in the Lord, for Christians should do what may give joy to the hearts of one another, and from the world they expect trouble, so they should find comfort and joy in one another. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

21 COMMENTARY: Having confidence in thy obedience I wrote unto thee, knowing that thou wilt also do more than I say, for Christians should do what may give joy to the hearts of one another, and the example of Onesimus may encourage the vilest sinners to return to God, but it is shamefully perverted if any are made bold thereby to persist in evil courses. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

22 COMMENTARY: But withal prepare me also a lodging, for I trust that through your prayers I shall be given unto you, for when any mercies are taken away, our trust and hope must be in God, we must diligently use the means, and if no other should be at hand, abound in prayer, yet though prayer prevails, it does not merit the things obtained. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

23 COMMENTARY: There salute thee Epaphras, my fellowprisoner in Christ Jesus, for never have believers found more enjoyment of God than when suffering together for him. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

24 COMMENTARY: Marcus, Aristarchus, Demas, Lucas, my fellowlabourers, for never have believers found more enjoyment of God than when suffering together for him. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

25 COMMENTARY: The grace of our Lord Jesus Christ be with your spirit, Amen, for grace is the best wish for ourselves and others, with this the apostle begins and ends, and all grace is from Christ, who purchased and bestows it, and what need we more to make us happy than to have the grace of our Lord Jesus Christ with our spirit, and if Christians do not meet on earth, still the grace of the Lord Jesus will be with their spirits, and they will soon meet before the throne to join for ever in admiring the riches of redeeming love. — Matthew Henry (Concise)
