Study Notes — Deuteronomy

Chapter 32

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Give ear, O you heavens, and I will speak, and hear, O earth, the words of my mouth: Moses begins with a solemn appeal to heaven and earth concerning the truth and importance of what he is about to sing. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: My doctrine shall drop as the rain, my speech shall distil as the dew, as the small rain upon the tender herb, and as the showers upon the grass: his doctrine is the gospel, the speech of God, the doctrine of grace and mercy through Christ. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: Because I will publish the name of the Lord: ascribe greatness to our God, the song's first duty being to give God his due honour. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: He is the Rock, his work is perfect, for all his ways are judgment, a God of truth and without iniquity, just and right is he: this is the first time God is called Rock in Scripture, denoting power, faithfulness, and love as an unchangeable foundation. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: They have corrupted themselves, their spot is not the spot of his children, they are a perverse and crooked generation: even God's children have spots in this imperfect state, but Israel's sin was wilful apostasy, not mere infirmity. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: Do you thus requite the Lord, O foolish people and unwise? Is not he your father who has bought you? Has he not made you, and established you? They were fools to forsake their mercies for lying vanities, unwise and ungrateful sinners. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: Memory is commanded, not suggested: the days of old and the years of generations are to be learned from fathers and elders, so that Israel's faith rests on transmitted history, not on present feeling. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: Among Moses' instances of God's kindness, Henry counts the apportioning of the nations: their bounds were set with Israel in view, an ordering of the peoples that displays God's particular concern for his own. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: For the Lord's portion is his people; Jacob is the lot of his inheritance, Israel being God's own chosen share among the nations. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: Found in a desert land and a howling wilderness, Israel was led about, instructed and guarded as the apple of God's eye, one of Henry's particular instances of God's kindness and concern for his people. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: As an eagle stirs up her nest, flutters over her young, spreads abroad her wings, takes them, and bears them on her wings, so God's care is pictured; the eagle's care is a beautiful emblem of Christ, who came between divine justice and guilty souls. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: So the Lord alone did lead him, and there was no strange god with him, the deliverance being wholly God's work. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: Riding on the high places and eating the increase of the fields are, for Henry, emblems of the conquest believers have over their spiritual enemies, sin, Satan and the world, in and through Christ, and of their safety and triumph in him. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: Butter of kine, and milk of sheep, with fat of lambs, and rams of the breed of Bashan, and goats, with the fat of kidneys of wheat, and you drank the pure blood of the grape: emblems of the conquest believers have over spiritual enemies, and of their safety and triumph in Christ. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: But Jeshurun waxed fat, and kicked; you are waxen fat, grown thick, covered with fatness; then he forsook God who made him, and lightly esteemed the Rock of his salvation; they indulged their appetites as if they had nothing to do but provide for the flesh. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: Henry charges this as apostasy: a people called Jeshurun, the upright, indulged their appetites as if they had nothing to do but provide for the flesh, made a god of themselves and their bellies, and thereby forsook God and esteemed him lightly. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: They sacrificed to devils, not to God, to gods whom they knew not, to new gods that came newly up, whom your fathers feared not; how mad are idolaters, who forsake the Rock of salvation to run upon the rock of perdition. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: Unmindfulness is the charge: the Rock that begat them and the God that formed them are forgotten, for apostasy here begins not with a new god but with a failure to remember the old one. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: And when the Lord saw it, he abhorred them, because of the provoking of his sons and daughters; sin makes us hateful in the sight of the holy God. — Matthew Henry

20 COMMENTARY: God's hiding his face is the resolve of divine justice against a faithless generation; Henry warns that we deceive ourselves if we think God will be mocked by a people who forsake him. — Matthew Henry

21 COMMENTARY: Israel moved God to jealousy with what is not God and provoked him with vanities, and Henry places the answering judgment in the same section: the revolt is met by the resolves of divine justice, not overlooked. — Matthew Henry

22 COMMENTARY: Anger is pictured as fire reaching the lowest depths and consuming earth and mountain foundations: judgment in the song is total, from the roots upward, not a surface chastisement. — Matthew Henry

23 COMMENTARY: Mischiefs are heaped and arrows spent: the song piles judgment upon judgment, so that divine anger is shown as a full quiver emptied, not a single blow. — Matthew Henry

24 COMMENTARY: Hunger, burning heat, bitter destruction, beasts and serpents are the mischief sin does; Henry's point in vv 19-25 is to reckon fools those who mock at sin, since a holy God will not be mocked by a faithless people. — Matthew Henry

25 COMMENTARY: The sword without, and terror within, shall destroy both the young man and the virgin, the suckling also with the man of gray hairs, judgment reaching every age and condition. — Matthew Henry

26 COMMENTARY: Justice seemed to demand that idolatrous Israel be rooted out and their remembrance made to cease, but Henry stresses that God withheld deserved vengeance and preserved them as living witnesses of the Bible's truth, to silence unbelievers. — Matthew Henry

27 COMMENTARY: Were it not that I feared the wrath of the enemy, lest their adversaries should behave themselves strangely, and lest they should say, Our hand is high, and the Lord has not done all this; God spared Israel, though justice seemed to demand their rooting out, lest his enemies boast and his glory be disgraced. — Matthew Henry

28 COMMENTARY: The nation is void of counsel and understanding: the song turns from enemies' boast to Israel's own blindness, and the wish that follows is that they would consider their latter end. — Matthew Henry

29 COMMENTARY: O that they were wise, that they understood this, that they would consider their latter end! It is great wisdom to consider seriously what will be in the end of a course of sin. — Matthew Henry

30 COMMENTARY: How should one chase a thousand, and two put ten thousand to flight, except their Rock had sold them, and the Lord had shut them up? Israel's defeats prove their Rock had withdrawn, not that their enemies were stronger. — Matthew Henry

31 COMMENTARY: For their rock is not as our Rock, even our enemies themselves being judges, the gods of the nations failing their worshippers when most needed. — Matthew Henry

32 COMMENTARY: Within Henry's withheld-vengeance section, the vine of Sodom and fields of Gomorrah mark fruit that is gall and bitterness to the root; those who trust any rock but God, he warns, will find it fail them when they most need it. — Matthew Henry

33 COMMENTARY: Within the withheld-vengeance section, the wine of apostasy is poison to the drinker: what Israel's enemies trusted as nourishment turns out to be dragons' poison and asps' venom, and Henry's section warns that any rock but God fails when most needed. — Matthew Henry

34 COMMENTARY: Is not this laid up in store with me, and sealed up among my treasures? Vengeance is reserved in God's keeping, not forgotten. — Matthew Henry

35 COMMENTARY: To me belongs vengeance and recompence; their foot shall slide in due time, for the day of their calamity is at hand, and the things that shall come upon them make haste; when sinners deem themselves most secure, they suddenly fall. — Matthew Henry

36 COMMENTARY: For the Lord shall judge his people, and repent himself for his servants, when he sees that their power is gone, and there is none shut up or left; God's time to appear for his people is when things are at their worst. — Matthew Henry

37 COMMENTARY: And he shall say, Where are their gods, their rock in whom they trusted, those who trust any rock but God will find it fail them when most needed. — Matthew Henry

38 COMMENTARY: Which did eat the fat of their sacrifices, and drank the wine of their drink offerings? Let them rise up and help you, and be your protection; the challenge exposes the idols' impotence. — Matthew Henry

39 COMMENTARY: The song's conclusion gives glory to God alone: no escape can be made from his power, terror belongs to his enemies, and comfort to his own people, for it shall go well with the people of God whatever judgments fall on sinners. — Matthew Henry

40 COMMENTARY: For I lift up my hand to heaven, and say, I live for ever, God's oath grounding the certainty of both judgment and deliverance. — Matthew Henry

41 COMMENTARY: The sword is whetted and the hand takes hold on judgment: vengeance is presented as deliberate, sharpened and grasped, not as a passion that escapes control. — Matthew Henry

42 COMMENTARY: Vengeance is pictured as sated and total: the weapons of judgment are shown glutted, and the retribution runs from the battlefield dead to prisoners to the very first acts of hostility, leaving no rank untouched. — Matthew Henry

43 COMMENTARY: Rejoice, O you nations, with his people, for he will avenge the blood of his servants, and will render vengeance to his adversaries, and will be merciful to his land, and to his people; the song concludes with joy, for whatever judgments fall on sinners, it shall go well with the people of God. — Matthew Henry

44 COMMENTARY: And Moses came and spoke all the words of this song in the ears of the people, he, and Hoshea the son of Nun, Joshua joining in the solemn delivery. — Matthew Henry

45 COMMENTARY: Moses' finishing of these words is the solemn delivery of the song with a charge to mind it; Henry insists it is no trifle but a matter of life and death, mind it and be made for ever, neglect it and be for ever undone. — Matthew Henry

46 COMMENTARY: Setting the heart is the command, and children are its horizon: the words testified today must be commanded to the next generation, so that obedience is inherited by teaching, not by chance. — Matthew Henry

47 COMMENTARY: For it is not a vain thing for you, because it is your life, and through this thing you shall prolong your days in the land where you go over Jordan to possess it; religion is the life of the soul, and to neglect it is to be for ever undone. — Matthew Henry

48 COMMENTARY: The same day the song ends, God speaks: Moses' death and the sight of the land are announced at once, so that the song's warnings and Moses' own end are joined in a single day. — Matthew Henry

49 COMMENTARY: Nebo is a viewing, not an entering: from Abarim over against Jericho, Moses beholds the land given for possession, for sight is granted where entry is denied. — Matthew Henry

50 COMMENTARY: Death on the mount gathers Moses to his people as Aaron was gathered on Hor: the parallel makes Moses' end a patterned one, shared with his brother, not a solitary disgrace. — Matthew Henry

51 COMMENTARY: The reason is named twice over: trespass at Meribah-Kadesh and failure to sanctify God before Israel, so one act of dishonour at the waters costs the entry, and the song's warnings close on its own author. — Matthew Henry

52 COMMENTARY: Yet you shall see the land before you, but you shall not go there to the land which I give the children of Israel; now Moses had done his work, and it is good for the best of men to die repenting their infirmities, with a believing prospect of eternal life beyond death. — Matthew Henry
