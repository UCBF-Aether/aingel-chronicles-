Study Notes — Deuteronomy

Chapter 34

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: And Moses went up from the plains of Moab to the mountain of Nebo, to the top of Pisgah, over against Jericho, and the Lord showed him all the land of Gilead, to Dan; this chapter appears from internal evidence to have been written after Moses' death, and probably once formed an introduction to Joshua, the Pisgah summit being the head of the Abarim range east of Jordan. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: And all Naphtali, and the land of Ephraim, and Manasseh, and all the land of Judah, to the utmost sea; westward lay Naphtali's distant hills, nearer Ephraim and Manasseh, opposite all Judah, and beyond it the Mediterranean, the four great marks of the future inheritance, seen in that subtle, vapour-free air without any miraculous power of vision being imparted. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: And the south, and the plain of the valley of Jericho, the city of palm trees, to Zoar; immediately below him lay the circle of Jericho's oasis, and far to his left Zoar, the last inhabited spot before the great desert, while the deep valley of Jordan intervened between him and the good land he was not to enter. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: And the Lord said to him, This is the land which I swore to Abraham, to Isaac, and to Jacob, saying, I will give it to your seed; I have caused you to see it with your eyes, but you shall not go over there. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: So Moses the servant of the Lord died there in the land of Moab, according to the word of the Lord, after having governed the Israelites forty years. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: And he buried him in a valley in the land of Moab, over against Beth-peor, but no man knows of his sepulchre to this day; this concealment seems a special and wise arrangement of Providence, to prevent the grave being ranked among holy places and made a resort of superstitious pilgrims or idolatrous veneration. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: And Moses was a hundred and twenty years old when he died; his eye was not dim, nor his natural force abated, his powers having suffered no decay even at the end. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: And the children of Israel wept for Moses in the plains of Moab thirty days; so the days of weeping and mourning for Moses were ended; seven days was the usual mourning period, but for persons of high rank it was extended to thirty. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: And Joshua son of Nun was full of the spirit of wisdom, for Moses had laid his hands upon him, and the children of Israel hearkened to him, and did as the Lord commanded Moses; Joshua was appointed to a peculiar office, not as prophet or civil ruler in Moses' place, but as general to head the invasion and the allocation of the tribes. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: And there arose not a prophet since in Israel like to Moses, whom the Lord knew face to face; in whatever light we view this extraordinary man, the eulogy is just, for no Hebrew prophet or ruler equalled him in character, official dignity, or knowledge of God's will and opportunities of announcing it. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: In all the signs and wonders, which the Lord sent him to do in the land of Egypt to Pharaoh, and to all his servants, and to all his land. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: And in all that mighty hand, and in all the great terror which Moses showed in the sight of all Israel, closing both the book and the life with the signs, wonders, and mighty acts that marked him out above all other prophets. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
