Study Notes — Deuteronomy

Chapter 27

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Moses with the elders commands the keeping of the law as the prelude to writing it on stones in Canaan; Henry stresses that word and prayer must go together, and that the law, with its curse annexed, is set up plainly before the people. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: The law is written where it will be lived: great stones, plastered and set up on crossing Jordan, make the covenant a public monument in the land rather than a scroll kept by priests alone. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: And you shall write upon them all the words of this law, when you are passed over, that you may go in to the land flowing with milk and honey, as the Lord God of your fathers has promised you. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: Therefore when you are gone over Jordan, you shall set up these stones, which I command you this day, in Mount Ebal, and plaster them with plaster, a monument of the law in the land itself. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: And there you shall build an altar to the Lord your God, an altar of stones, and you shall not lift up any iron tool upon them, the word and prayer going together, and the altar being of unhewn stones as found in the field. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: Whole stones, untouched by iron, carry the burnt offerings: the altar must come to God as found, not shaped by human craft, and the offering rises from unhewn stone. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: Peace offerings are eaten and rejoiced over before God: after the curse is set up in stone, the altar also feasts, so obedience ends in rejoicing, not only in fear. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: And you shall write upon the stones all the words of this law very plainly, the end of ministry being to make God's word as plain as possible, though only the Spirit's power makes it wise to salvation. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: Moses and the priests speak together, and the day itself is the point: this day Israel becomes God's people, not by crossing Jordan alone but by hearkening to the voice that commands. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: Obedience follows identity: because this day they are become God's people, therefore they obey his voice and do his statutes, for the command rests on who they now are. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: Moses' charge that day appoints the curses on Ebal, to which the people must answer Amen, professing their faith that these curses are real declarations of God's wrath, and acknowledging that those who do such things deserve to fall under them. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: These shall stand upon Mount Gerizim to bless the people, when you are come over Jordan: Simeon, Levi, Judah, Issachar, Joseph, and Benjamin, the six tribes of blessing, children of the free women, to whom the promise belongs. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: Six tribes stand on Ebal for the curse as six stood on Gerizim for blessing: the division itself preaches that the same law which blesses obedience curses disobedience, and both mountains are heard. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: And the Levites shall speak, and say to all the men of Israel with a loud voice, ministers applying to themselves the blessing and curse they preach, and setting their own Amen to it. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: Cursed be the man who makes any graven or molten image, an abomination to the Lord, the work of the hands of the craftsman, and puts it in a secret place, and all the people shall answer and say, Amen. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: The curse on dishonouring parents opens the list where family order begins: the people's Amen is not a curse they pronounce on others but a confession that such contempt deserves God's wrath. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: Moving a neighbour's landmark steals by stealth what no court may see: the curse names a quiet injustice, and the Amen confesses it worthy of wrath though no witness watched. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: Leading the blind astray abuses trust no victim can check: the curse reaches sins done where the sufferer cannot see the wrong, and the Amen owns that hidden cruelty as God's business. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: Justice perverted against the defenceless is cursed precisely because they have no advocate: stranger, fatherless and widow depend on the court, and the Amen says God judges the judge. — Matthew Henry

20 COMMENTARY: The curse guards the father's household: uncovering his skirt is not a private liaison but a breach of family order, and the Amen acknowledges it under the law's wrath. — Matthew Henry

21 COMMENTARY: Bestiality is cursed without a reason clause: the act itself, named bare, is enough for the people's Amen to own it as abomination under God's wrath. — Matthew Henry

22 COMMENTARY: Incest within the family is cursed in both lines, father's daughter and mother's: the Amen covers the whole household, leaving no kinship loophole. — Matthew Henry

23 COMMENTARY: The curse extends the household boundary to marriage kin: a mother-in-law is family by covenant, not a stranger, and the Amen treats the breach accordingly. — Matthew Henry

24 COMMENTARY: Secret murder is cursed though no court can prove it: the Amen is the people's confession that God's wrath reaches the blow struck where no eye saw. — Matthew Henry

25 COMMENTARY: Bribed blood is cursed as hired murder: taking reward makes the killer a merchant of innocent life, and the Amen says such a bargain falls under God's wrath. — Matthew Henry

26 COMMENTARY: Cursed be he who confirms not all the words of this law to do them, and all the people shall say, Amen; the last curse reaches all, not only those who do forbidden evil, but those who omit required good, and without Christ's atoning blood the law condemns every transgressor. — Matthew Henry
