Study Notes — Deuteronomy

Chapter 25

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: If there is a controversy between men, and they come to judgment, that the judges may judge them, then they shall justify the righteous, and condemn the wicked. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: And it shall be, if the wicked man is worthy to be beaten, that the judge shall cause him to lie down, and be beaten before his face, according to his fault, by a certain number. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: Forty stripes he may give him, and not exceed, lest, if he should exceed and beat him above these with many stripes, then your brother should seem vile to you; punishment should be solemn, to warn onlookers, yet the criminal must not be made to appear totally vile. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: You shall not muzzle the ox when he treads out the corn, a charge to husbandmen teaching justice and kindness to animals that serve us, and by extension to all employed for the good of our souls. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: If brethren dwell together, and one of them dies, and has no child, the wife of the dead shall not marry without to a stranger; her husband's brother shall go in to her, take her to wife, and perform the duty of a husband's brother to her, a custom regulated to keep inheritances distinct. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: And it shall be, that the firstborn which she bears shall succeed in the name of his brother who is dead, that his name is not put out of Israel. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: And if the man does not like to take his brother's wife, then his brother's wife shall go up to the gate to the elders, and say, My husband's brother refuses to raise up to his brother a name in Israel, and will not perform the duty of my husband's brother. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: Then the elders of his city shall call him, and speak to him, and if he stands to it, and says, I like not to take her. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: Then his brother's wife shall come to him in the presence of the elders, loose his shoe from off his foot, and spit in his face, and answer and say, So shall it be done to that man who will not build up his brother's house. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: And his name shall be called in Israel, The house of him that has his shoe loosed, a public mark of disgrace for refusing the duty. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: When men strive together, and the wife of one draws near to deliver her husband out of the hand of him who smites him, and puts forth her hand, and takes him by the secrets. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: Then you shall cut off her hand, and your eye shall not pity her, the severity guarding the modesty and order that the law protects in all its precepts. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: You shall not have in your bag divers weights, a great and a small, for dishonest gain brings a curse on property, families, and souls. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: You shall not have in your house divers measures, a great and a small, the prohibition covering every instrument of trade. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: But you shall have a perfect and just weight, a perfect and just measure, that your days may be lengthened in the land which the Lord gives you. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: For all that do such things, and all that do unrighteously, are an abomination to the Lord your God. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: Remember what Amalek did to you by the way, when you came forth out of Egypt. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: How he met you by the way, and smote the hindmost of you, even all that were feeble behind you, when you were faint and weary, and he feared not God; let every persecutor of God's people take warning from Amalek. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: Therefore when the Lord has given you rest from all your enemies round about, in the land he gives you for an inheritance to possess it, you shall blot out the remembrance of Amalek from under heaven, and you shall not forget it; the longer judgment is delayed, the more dreadful it will be at last, and Amalek reminds us of the foes of our souls to be slain. — Matthew Henry
