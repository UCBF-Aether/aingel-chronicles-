Study Notes — Deuteronomy

Chapter 14

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: You are the children of the Lord your God; you shall not cut yourselves, nor make baldness between your eyes for the dead, pagan mourning rites being unfit for God's adopted children. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: For you are a holy people to the Lord, and the Lord has chosen you to be a peculiar people to himself, above all nations on the earth; election here means he chose them that they might become peculiar by his grace. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: You shall not eat any abominable thing, the food laws marking Israel as separate from idolatrous neighbours. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: These are the beasts you shall eat: the ox, the sheep, and the goat, the ordinary clean domestic animals. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: The hart, roebuck, fallow deer, wild goat, pygarg, wild ox, and chamois, clean wild animals added to the domestic list. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: And every beast that parts the hoof, cleaves the cleft into two claws, and chews the cud, among the beasts, that you shall eat, the twofold test of clean beasts. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: Nevertheless, of those that chew the cud, or divide the cloven hoof, you shall not eat the camel, hare, and coney, for though they chew the cud, they divide not the hoof, and are unclean. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: And the swine, because it divides the hoof, yet chews not the cud, is unclean to you; you shall not eat of their flesh, nor touch their dead carcass. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: These you shall eat of all that are in the waters: all that have fins and scales shall you eat. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: And whatever has not fins and scales you may not eat; it is unclean to you, the water test corresponding to the land test. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: Of all clean birds you shall eat, the general permission preceding the exceptions. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: But these are they of which you shall not eat: the eagle, ossifrage, and ospray, birds of prey first in the list. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: And the glede, kite, and vulture after his kind, further raptors forbidden. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: And every raven after his kind, the carrion crow family. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: And the owl, night hawk, cuckow, and hawk after his kind. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: The little owl, great owl, and swan. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: And the pelican, gier eagle, and cormorant. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: And the stork, heron after her kind, lapwing, and bat, completing the unclean bird list; Henry notes these laws kept Israel from mingling with idolatrous neighbours, and are now done away in the gospel. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: And every creeping thing that flies is unclean to you; they shall not be eaten. — Matthew Henry

20 COMMENTARY: But of all clean fowls you may eat, the permission standing after the prohibitions. — Matthew Henry

21 COMMENTARY: You shall not eat anything that dies of itself; you shall give it to the stranger within your gates, that he may eat it, or sell it to an alien, for you are a holy people to the Lord; you shall not seethe a kid in his mother's milk, a rite Henry's law of separation also covers. — Matthew Henry

22 COMMENTARY: You shall truly tithe all the increase of your seed, that the field brings forth year by year, the second tithe for the sanctuary feast. — Matthew Henry

23 COMMENTARY: And you shall eat before the Lord in the place he shall choose to place his name, the tithe of your corn, wine, oil, and firstlings of herds and flocks, that you may learn to fear the Lord always. — Matthew Henry

24 COMMENTARY: And if the way is too long for you, so that you cannot carry it, or if the place is too far from you, where the Lord shall choose to set his name, when he has blessed you. — Matthew Henry

25 COMMENTARY: Then you shall turn it into money, bind up the money in your hand, and go to the place which the Lord shall choose. — Matthew Henry

26 COMMENTARY: And you shall bestow that money for whatever your soul lusts after, for oxen, sheep, wine, strong drink, or whatever your soul desires, and eat there before the Lord, and rejoice, you and your household. — Matthew Henry

27 COMMENTARY: And the Levite within your gates, you shall not forsake him, for he has no part nor inheritance with you; the tithe feast was to be shared, not hoarded. — Matthew Henry

28 COMMENTARY: At the end of three years you shall bring forth all the tithe of your increase the same year, and lay it up within your gates. — Matthew Henry

29 COMMENTARY: And the Levite, because he has no part nor inheritance with you, and the stranger, fatherless, and widow within your gates, shall come, eat, and be satisfied, that the Lord may bless you in all the work of your hand; the appointment was against covetousness and selfishness, raising a fund for the poor and teaching that our portion is most comfortably enjoyed when shared. — Matthew Henry
