Study Notes — Deuteronomy

Chapter 24

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: When a man has taken a wife, and married her, and it comes to pass that she finds no favour in his eyes, because he has found some uncleanness in her, then let him write her a bill of divorcement, and give it in her hand, and send her out of his house. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: And when she has departed out of his house, she may go and be another man's wife. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: And if the latter husband hates her, and writes her a bill of divorcement, gives it in her hand, and sends her out of his house, or if the latter husband dies, who took her to be his wife. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: Her former husband, who sent her away, may not take her again to be his wife, after she is defiled, for that is abomination before the Lord, and you shall not cause the land to sin, which the Lord gives you for an inheritance. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: When a man has taken a new wife, he shall not go out to war, neither shall he be charged with any business, but he shall be free at home one year, and shall cheer up his wife whom he has taken; love between husband and wife is to be carefully kept up. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: No man shall take the nether or the upper millstone to pledge, for he takes a man's life to pledge, the mill being his daily bread. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: If a man is found stealing any of his brethren of the children of Israel, and makes merchandise of him, or sells him, then that thief shall die, and you shall put evil away from among you; man-stealing was a capital crime, not to be settled by restitution. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: Take heed in the plague of leprosy, that you observe diligently, and do according to all that the priests the Levites shall teach you, as I commanded them, so shall you observe to do. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: Remember what the Lord did to Miriam by the way, after you came forth out of Egypt, her leprosy following her challenge to Moses' authority. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: When you lend your brother anything, you shall not go into his house to fetch his pledge. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: You shall stand abroad, and the man to whom you lend shall bring out the pledge abroad to you, respecting the debtor's house and dignity. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: And if the man is poor, you shall not sleep with his pledge. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: In any case you shall deliver him the pledge again when the sun goes down, that he may sleep in his own raiment, and bless you, and it shall be righteousness to you before the Lord; consult the comfort and subsistence of others as much as your own advantage. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: You shall not oppress a hired servant who is poor and needy, whether he is of your brethren, or of strangers in your land within your gates. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: At his day you shall give him his hire, neither shall the sun go down upon it, for he is poor, and sets his heart upon it, lest he cry against you to the Lord, and it be sin to you. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: The fathers shall not be put to death for the children, neither shall the children be put to death for the fathers; every man shall be put to death for his own sin, individual responsibility in judgment. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: You shall not pervert the judgment of the stranger, nor of the fatherless, nor take a widow's raiment to pledge. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: But you shall remember that you were a bondman in Egypt, and the Lord redeemed you from there, therefore I command you to do this thing. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: When you cut down your harvest in your field, and have forgot a sheaf in the field, you shall not go again to fetch it; it shall be for the stranger, fatherless, and widow, that the Lord may bless you in all the work of your hands. — Matthew Henry

20 COMMENTARY: When you beat your olive tree, you shall not go over the boughs again; it shall be for the stranger, fatherless, and widow. — Matthew Henry

21 COMMENTARY: When you gather the grapes of your vineyard, you shall not glean it afterward; it shall be for the stranger, fatherless, and widow. — Matthew Henry

22 COMMENTARY: And you shall remember that you were a bondman in the land of Egypt, therefore I command you to do this thing; purity, justice, mercy, and generosity to the poor are pleasing to God and becoming in his redeemed people. — Matthew Henry
