Study Notes — Deuteronomy

Chapter 3

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Then they turned and went up the way to Bashan, and Og its king came out against them with all his people to battle at Edrei. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: The Lord said to Moses, Fear him not, for he would deliver Og, his people, and his land into Israel's hand, to be dealt with as Sihon had been; Og's gigantic stature need not discourage them. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: So the Lord delivered Og also, king of Bashan, and all his people, and they smote him until none remained alive. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: They took all his cities at that time, sixty cities, the whole region of Argob in Bashan, not one city escaping them. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: All these cities were fenced with high walls, gates, and bars, besides a great many unwalled towns, yet their fortifications did not save them. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: They utterly destroyed them as they had done to Sihon, destroying men, women, and children of every city. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: But all the cattle and the spoil of the cities they took for prey to themselves. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: They took at that time from the two Amorite kings the land east of Jordan, from the river Arnon to Mount Hermon. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: Hermon the Sidonians call Sirion, and the Amorites call it Shenir, different tribal names for the same lofty, snow-capped mountain chain. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: All the cities of the plain, all Gilead, and all Bashan as far as Salchah and Edrei, cities of Og's kingdom, became Israel's possession. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: For only Og remained of the remnant of giants, and his iron bedstead, nine cubits long, was in Rabbath of the Ammonites, a relic of a stature reckoned at about eleven or twelve feet. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: This land, from Aroer by the Arnon and half Mount Gilead with its cities, Moses gave to the Reubenites and Gadites. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: The rest of Gilead and all Bashan, the kingdom of Og, he gave to the half-tribe of Manasseh, with all the region of Argob. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: Jair son of Manasseh took all the country of Argob as far as the coasts of Geshuri and Maachathi, and called it after his own name, Bashan-havoth-jair, villages of tents. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: Gilead he gave to Machir, though Machir himself was now dead, the grant passing to his descendants. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: To the Reubenites and Gadites he gave from Gilead to the river Arnon, half the valley, and to the river Jabbok, the border of the Ammonites. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: The plain also, and Jordan with its coast, from Chinnereth to the sea of the plain, the salt sea, under Ashdoth-pisgah eastward, completed their allotment. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

18 COMMENTARY: Moses commanded them at that time: the Lord had given them this land to possess, but their armed men must pass over before their brethren. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

19 COMMENTARY: Their wives, little ones, and cattle, for they had much cattle, were to stay in the cities Moses had given them. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

20 COMMENTARY: Until the Lord had given rest to their brethren as to them, and they also possessed the land beyond Jordan, the eastern tribes must not return; when at rest, we should desire to see our brethren at rest too. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

21 COMMENTARY: Moses commanded Joshua at that time, saying his eyes had seen all the Lord had done to Sihon and Og, and so he would do to all the kingdoms they were going over to. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

22 COMMENTARY: You shall not fear them, for the Lord your God himself shall fight for you. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

23 COMMENTARY: Moses then besought the Lord at that time, praying that he might be allowed to cross over after all. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

24 COMMENTARY: O Lord God, he prayed, you have begun to show your servant your greatness and mighty hand; what god in heaven or earth can do according to your works and mighty acts? — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

25 COMMENTARY: I pray you, let me go over and see the good land beyond Jordan, that goodly mountain, and Lebanon, typifying the beauty of Palestine. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

26 COMMENTARY: But the Lord was wroth with Moses for their sakes and would not hear him, saying, Let it suffice you; speak no more to me of this matter, for the decree was unalterable. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

27 COMMENTARY: Get up to the top of Pisgah, lift your eyes westward, northward, southward, and eastward, and behold the land with your eyes, for you shall not go over Jordan. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

28 COMMENTARY: But charge Joshua, encourage him, and strengthen him, for he shall go over before this people and cause them to inherit the land they would see. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

29 COMMENTARY: So they abode in the valley over against Beth-peor, with Moses' prayer answered in mercy mixed with judgment: a sight of Canaan, though not possession of it. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
