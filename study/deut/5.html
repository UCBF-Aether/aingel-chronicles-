Study Notes — Deuteronomy

Chapter 5

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Moses called all Israel to hear the statutes and judgments, to learn them and keep and do them, for hearing ends in practice, not in talk. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: The Lord our God made a covenant with us in Horeb, a covenant given to the generation now standing, not merely to their fathers. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: The Lord made this covenant not with our fathers only, but with us, even us, all of us here alive this day, binding the new generation directly. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: The Lord talked with them face to face in the mount out of the midst of the fire, speaking directly to the whole assembly. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: Moses stood between the Lord and the people at that time, to show them the word of the Lord, for they were afraid by reason of the fire and did not go up into the mount. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: I am the Lord your God, who brought you out of Egypt, from the house of bondage: deliverance is the preface and ground of the whole law that follows. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: You shall have none other gods before me, the first commandment, requiring sole worship of the one true God. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: You shall not make any graven image, or any likeness of anything in heaven above, on earth beneath, or in the waters under the earth, for worship. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: You shall not bow down to them nor serve them, for the Lord your God is a jealous God, visiting the iniquity of the fathers upon the children to the third and fourth generation of those who hate him. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: And showing mercy to thousands of those who love him and keep his commandments, mercy far outrunning judgment in its reach. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: You shall not take the name of the Lord your God in vain, for the Lord will not hold him guiltless who takes his name in vain. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: Keep the sabbath day to sanctify it, as the Lord your God commanded you, setting it apart from common work. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: Six days you shall labour and do all your work, the ordinary pattern of human labour under God's ordering. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: But the seventh day is the sabbath of the Lord your God, in which no work is to be done by you, your household, your servants, your cattle, or the stranger within your gates. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: And remember that you were a servant in Egypt, and the Lord brought you out with a mighty hand and outstretched arm; therefore he commanded you to keep the sabbath, deliverance, not only creation, being its reason here, a type of redemption by Christ. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: Honour your father and your mother, as the Lord commanded, that your days may be prolonged and it may go well with you in the land he gives you. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: You shall not kill, the command guarding human life against murder. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: Neither shall you commit adultery, guarding the marriage bond. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: Neither shall you steal, guarding your neighbour's property. — Matthew Henry

20 COMMENTARY: Neither shall you bear false witness against your neighbour, guarding truth in judgment and speech. — Matthew Henry

21 COMMENTARY: Neither shall you desire your neighbour's wife, nor covet his house, field, servants, cattle, or anything that is his, striking at the inward lust from which outward sins spring. — Matthew Henry

22 COMMENTARY: These words the Lord spoke to all the assembly in the mount, out of the midst of the fire, cloud, and thick darkness, with a great voice, and added no more, writing them on two tables of stone and delivering them to Moses. — Matthew Henry

23 COMMENTARY: When they heard the voice out of the midst of the darkness, for the mountain burned with fire, their chiefs and elders came near to Moses. — Matthew Henry

24 COMMENTARY: They said, Behold, the Lord has showed us his glory and greatness, and we have heard his voice out of the midst of the fire; we have seen that God can speak with man and he still live. — Matthew Henry

25 COMMENTARY: Now therefore why should we die, they asked, for this great fire will consume us if we hear the voice of the Lord our God any more. — Matthew Henry

26 COMMENTARY: For who of all flesh has heard the voice of the living God speaking out of the midst of the fire, as we have, and lived? God's appearances have always been terrible to fallen man. — Matthew Henry

27 COMMENTARY: Go near yourself, they said to Moses, and hear all the Lord shall say, and speak to us all that he says, and we will hear it and do it, preferring a mediator to direct speech. — Matthew Henry

28 COMMENTARY: The Lord heard their words and said to Moses, I have heard what they have spoken; they have well said all that they have spoken. — Matthew Henry

29 COMMENTARY: O that there were such a heart in them, that they would fear me and keep all my commandments always, that it might be well with them and their children for ever: God commended their good mind, while warning that fair promises under conviction are not yet rooted principles. — Matthew Henry

30 COMMENTARY: Go say to them, Get into your tents again, dismissing the assembly to their ordinary life under the law now given. — Matthew Henry

31 COMMENTARY: But as for you, Moses, stand here by me, and I will speak to you all the commandments, statutes, and judgments, which you shall teach them, that they may do them in the land they are going to possess. — Matthew Henry

32 COMMENTARY: You shall observe to do therefore as the Lord commanded you, turning neither to the right hand nor to the left, for the only way to be happy is to be holy. — Matthew Henry

33 COMMENTARY: You shall walk in all the ways the Lord commanded you, that you may live, and it may be well with you, and you may prolong your days in the land you shall possess. — Matthew Henry
