Study Notes — Deuteronomy

Chapter 33

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: And this is the blessing, with which Moses the man of God blessed the children of Israel before his death; man of God is a common designation of a prophet, here applied to Moses as, like Jacob, he delivers a prophetic benediction before dying. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: And he said, The Lord came from Sinai, and rose up from Seir to them; he shined forth from Mount Paran, and he came with ten thousands of saints; from his right hand went a fiery law for them; under a metaphor borrowed from the dawn, God's majesty is described as light appearing at Sinai and scattering beams over the region on Israel's march, the fiery law being so called for the thunder and lightning of its promulgation and the fierce curse against its violation, yet given in kindness and love. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: Love precedes law: the people are loved, the saints held in God's hand, seated at his feet to receive his words, so the blessing's opening makes obedience a posture of sitting and listening, not of bargaining. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: Moses commanded us a law, even the inheritance of the congregation of Jacob; the law was Israel's grand distinction and peculiar privilege, not only a hereditary obligation. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: JFB heads this whole blessing (vv 1-28) with the majesty of God, displayed at Sinai as a divine light scattering its beams; here that majesty presides as king over Jeshurun when the heads of the people and the tribes are gathered together. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: Let Reuben live, and not die, and let not his men be few; though deprived of primogeniture, Reuben was still to hold rank as a tribe, gradually sinking into a nomadic tribe with enough merely to live and not die. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: And this is the blessing of Judah: and he said, Hear, Lord, the voice of Judah, and bring him to his people; let his hands be sufficient for him, and be a help to him from his enemies; its general purport points to Judah's great power and independence, and its lead in all military expeditions. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: And of Levi he said, Let your Thummim and your Urim be with your holy one, whom you proved at Massah, and with whom you strove at the waters of Meribah; the blessing appoints the Levites to the priesthood, a reward for their zeal for God and severity even against relatives who joined the calf idolatry. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: Levi's zeal is measured by kinship refused: father, mother, brethren and children unacknowledged where God's word and covenant are at stake, for the priesthood's severity at the calf is the ground of its blessing. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: Levi's double office follows its zeal: teaching Jacob judgments and Israel law, and putting incense and whole burnt sacrifice on the altar, so word and worship are the priesthood's two hands. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: For Levi the blessing is twofold in JFB's reading: prosperity for the tribe's substance, and a final end to its opponents, for the prayer does not ask for victory in one battle but for enemies who cannot rise a second time. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: And of Benjamin he said, The beloved of the Lord shall dwell in safety by him, and the Lord shall cover him all the day long, and he shall dwell between his shoulders; a distinguishing favour was Benjamin's portion near the temple, Mount Moriah lying within its borders. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: Joseph's blessing begins with heaven and depth: precious things above in dew, and the deep couching beneath, so the land's bounty is drawn from both skies and subterranean waters, not from husbandry alone. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: Joseph's portion, diversified by hill and dale, wood and water, is rich in all productions, JFB notes; the precious fruits of sun and moon are part of that promised plenty of a mountainous and level land alike. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: The chief things of the ancient mountains and the lasting hills belong to the same Joseph blessing: a territory rich in olives, grapes, figs and grain, whose emblems of the bullock's firstling and the unicorn's horns signify glory and strength. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: And for the precious things of the earth and fulness of it, and for the good will of him who dwelt in the bush; let the blessing come upon the head of Joseph, and upon the top of the head of him who was separated from his brethren; Joseph's diversified territory of hill, dale, wood, and water would be rich in olives, grapes, figs, grain, and herbs. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: His glory is like the firstling of his bullock, and his horns are like the horns of unicorns; with them he shall push the people together to the ends of the earth, and they are the ten thousands of Ephraim, and the thousands of Manasseh; glory and strength are shadowed here, in emblems thought to point to Joshua and Jeroboam, both of Ephraim. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

18 COMMENTARY: And of Zebulun he said, Rejoice, Zebulun, in your going out, and, Issachar, in your tents; Zebulun rejoices in commercial enterprises and voyages by sea, Issachar preferring to reside in maritime towns. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

19 COMMENTARY: They shall call the people to the mountain; there they shall offer sacrifices of righteousness, for they shall suck of the abundance of the seas, and of treasures hid in the sand, trafficking with the Phoenicians in gold, silver, pearl, coral, Tyrian dye from the murex, and glass from the sand of the river Belus. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

20 COMMENTARY: And of Gad he said, Blessed be he who enlarges Gad; he dwells as a lion, and tears the arm with the crown of the head; Gad's possessions were larger than if west of Jordan, settled by Moses himself in the first conquered land, and dwelling as a lion south of the Jabbok. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

21 COMMENTARY: And he provided the first part for himself, because there, in a portion of the lawgiver, was he seated, and he came with the heads of the people, he executed the justice of the Lord, and his judgments with Israel; Gad faithfully kept its engagement to join the heads of the people in invading Canaan. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

22 COMMENTARY: And of Dan he said, Dan is a lion's whelp; he shall leap from Bashan; his southern settlement being too small, Dan by a sudden successful irruption established a colony in the northern extremity, like a young lion's leap. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

23 COMMENTARY: And of Naphtali he said, O Naphtali, satisfied with favour, and full with the blessing of the Lord, possess the west and the south; its pleasant, fertile territory lay west on the lakes of Merom and Chinnereth, and south of the northern Danites. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

24 COMMENTARY: And of Asher he said, Let Asher be blessed with children; let him be acceptable to his brethren, and let him dip his foot in oil; Asher's condition combines all elements of earthly felicity, its district being fertile for the olive, whether by foot presses or by culture. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

25 COMMENTARY: Your shoes shall be iron and brass, and as your days, so shall your strength be; such shoes suited Asher's rocky coast from Carmel to Sidon, and warriors wore metallic greaves. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

26 COMMENTARY: There is none like to the God of Jeshurun, who rides upon the heaven in your help, and in his excellency on the sky; the chapter concludes with congratulation on Israel's happiness in having Jehovah as God and protector, the cloud and fire being both guide and shelter. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

27 COMMENTARY: Refuge is eternal and arms everlasting: the God who shelters from above also thrusts the enemy out before Israel and commands destruction, so protection and expulsion are one act of the same arms. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

28 COMMENTARY: Safety alone is the blessing's end: the fountain of Jacob rests on corn, wine and dew from heaven, so dwelling alone is not isolation but provision under God's own sky. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

29 COMMENTARY: Happiness is defined by rescue: a people saved by the Lord, shielded in help and sworded in excellency, whose enemies are found liars and whose feet tread high places, so the blessing ends not with wealth but with vindication. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
