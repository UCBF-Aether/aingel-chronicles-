Study Notes — Deuteronomy

Chapter 17

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: You shall not sacrifice to the Lord any bullock or sheep with blemish or any evilfavouredness, for that is an abomination; no creature with any blemish was to be offered, reminding Israel of the perfect, pure, spotless sacrifice of Christ, and that God must be served with our best. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: Henry treats a case found among you as no private sin: worship of other gods breaks the covenant itself, which is why the law that follows deals with it as a public crime punishable by death rather than a matter of conscience. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: The forbidden worship is named by its objects, sun, moon and the host of heaven, and Henry's section reads all such worship as setting up idols in the heart: the crime is not the rite's strangeness but its displacement of God, punished in Israel as a warning to later idolaters. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: Diligent inquiry must precede any sentence because, in Henry's reading, the penalty here is death by stoning: a charge this grave must be proved true and certain before the community acts, not taken on report alone. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: Then you shall bring forth that man or woman who has committed that wicked thing, to your gates, and stone them with stones till they die; so great a punishment, and so remarkable a death, was inflicted on the Jewish idolater. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: For so grave a sentence as death by stoning, Henry insists the safeguard is the number of witnesses: a capital charge for idolatry may rest on two or three witnesses, never on one alone, lest a single accuser take a life. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: The witnesses' hands go first: Henry's section makes the idolater's death a communal sentence, and the order puts the accusers' hands on the execution before the people's, so the charge is owned by those who proved it. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: Hard cases go up, not around: blood, plea and stroke too hard for local judgment are carried to the place God chooses, where priests and judge, not the parties, give the sentence. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: And you shall come to the priests the Levites, and to the judge that shall be in those days, and enquire, and they shall show you the sentence of judgment; courts were to be set up in every city, but hard cases went to this central court of wise, prudent, experienced men. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: The sentence binds in full: what the court of the chosen place informs must be observed in all, with no declining to either side, for Henry's section grounds that obedience in the court's wisdom, not in the litigant's agreement. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: According to the sentence of the law which they shall teach you, and according to the judgment which they shall tell you, you shall do; you shall not decline from the sentence shown you, to the right hand nor to the left. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: And the man who will do presumptuously, and will not hearken to the priest standing to minister there before the Lord, or to the judge, even that man shall die, and you shall put away the evil from Israel. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: The sentence on the presumptuous man is heard by all Israel: Henry's court section makes the judgment public precisely so that hearing breeds fear and presumption stops, and the verdict educates the nation, not only the offender. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: The desire for a king is framed as imitation, like all the nations around them, and Henry's section answers it by insisting that God's choice must direct, determine and overrule the people's own. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: You shall in any wise set him king over you, whom the Lord shall choose; one from among your brethren shall you set king over you, and you may not set a stranger over you who is not your brother; God himself was Israel's King in a particular manner, so if they had another, he must choose the person. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: Horses mean Egypt: multiplying them would send the people back down the road God closed, trading dependence on God for cavalry strength and a return to the house of bondage. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: Neither shall he multiply wives to himself, that his heart turn not away, neither shall he greatly multiply to himself silver and gold; riches, honours, and pleasures are three great hindrances of godliness, especially to those in high stations. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: The king writes his own copy from the priests' book: the law is not merely received but copied by the royal hand, so that the throne's first work is transcription, not legislation. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: Reading is lifelong and daily: the copy stays with the king all his life so that fear of God and keeping of the law are learned by constant use, for Henry's point is that having Bibles is nothing without using them daily. — Matthew Henry

20 COMMENTARY: That his heart be not lifted up above his brethren, and that he turn not aside from the commandment, to the right hand or to the left, to the end that he may prolong his days in his kingdom, he, and his children, in the midst of Israel; writing and reading were as nothing if he did not practise what he wrote and read. — Matthew Henry
