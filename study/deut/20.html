Study Notes — Deuteronomy

Chapter 20

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: When you go out to battle against your enemies, and see horses, chariots, and a people more than you, be not afraid of them, for the Lord your God is with you, who brought you up out of Egypt; in wars fought by God's will Israel might expect divine assistance, and the Lord was to be their only confidence. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: And it shall be, when you come near to the battle, that the priest shall approach and speak to the people. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: And shall say to them, Hear, O Israel, you approach this day to battle against your enemies; let not your hearts faint, fear not, do not tremble, neither be terrified because of them. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: For the Lord your God is he who goes with you, to fight for you against your enemies, to save you, the ground of courage in the Christian's warfare as well. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: And the officers shall speak to the people, saying, What man is there who has built a new house and has not dedicated it? Let him go and return to his house, lest he die in the battle and another man dedicate it. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: And what man is he who has planted a vineyard and has not yet eaten of it? Let him also go and return to his house, lest he die in the battle and another man eat of it. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: And what man is there who has betrothed a wife and has not taken her? Let him go and return to his house, lest he die in the battle and another man take her; God would not be served by men forced against their will, and outward conditions excusing a man were honoured. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: And the officers shall speak further to the people, saying, What man is there who is fearful and fainthearted? Let him go and return to his house, lest his brethren's heart fail as well as his heart. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: And it shall be, when the officers have made an end of speaking to the people, that they shall make captains of the armies to lead the people, the willing army then being ordered under its commanders. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: When you come near to a city to fight against it, then proclaim peace to it, showing God's grace in dealing with sinners, who proclaims peace and beseeches reconciliation. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: And it shall be, if it makes you an answer of peace, and opens to you, then all the people found in it shall be tributaries to you, and shall serve you. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: And if it will make no peace with you, but will make war against you, then you shall besiege it. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: And when the Lord has delivered it into your hands, you shall smite every male in it with the edge of the sword. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: But the women, little ones, cattle, and all that is in the city, even all the spoil of it, shall you take to yourself, and eat the spoil of your enemies, which the Lord has given you. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: Thus shall you do to all the cities which are very far off from you, which are not of the cities of these nations. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: But of the cities of these people, which the Lord gives you for an inheritance, you shall save alive nothing that breathes. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: But you shall utterly destroy them, namely, the Hittites, Amorites, Canaanites, Perizzites, Hivites, and Jebusites, as the Lord commanded you. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: That they teach you not to do after all their abominations, which they have done to their gods, so you sin against the Lord your God; since they could not be cured of idolatry, they would hurt Israel if spared. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: When you besiege a city a long time, making war against it to take it, you shall not destroy its trees by forcing an axe against them, for you may eat of them, and you shall not cut them down, for the tree of the field is man's life, to employ them in the siege. — Matthew Henry

20 COMMENTARY: Only the trees which you know are not trees for meat, you shall destroy and cut them down, and build bulwarks against the city that makes war with you, until it is subdued; God's law consults our interests, restraining wilful waste of what is for our life and food. — Matthew Henry
