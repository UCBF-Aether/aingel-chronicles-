Study Notes — Deuteronomy

Chapter 23

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: He that is wounded in the stones, or has his privy member cut off, shall not enter into the congregation of the Lord, the assembly for worship and office being guarded in this ceremonial law. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: A bastard shall not enter into the congregation of the Lord, even to his tenth generation shall he not enter, a long exclusion marking the seriousness of illicit birth under the old order. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: An Ammonite or Moabite shall not enter into the congregation of the Lord, even to their tenth generation, for ever. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: Because they met you not with bread and water in the way, when you came forth out of Egypt, and because they hired against you Balaam son of Beor of Pethor in Mesopotamia, to curse you. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: Nevertheless the Lord your God would not hearken to Balaam, but the Lord turned the curse into a blessing to you, because the Lord loved you. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: You shall not seek their peace nor their prosperity all your days for ever, no treaty of friendship being sought with peoples who had so treated Israel. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: You shall not abhor an Edomite, for he is your brother, nor abhor an Egyptian, because you were a stranger in his land, a distinction Henry notes between enemies who wronged Israel and kindred or former hosts. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: The children begotten of them shall enter into the congregation of the Lord in their third generation, the exclusion softening with time. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: When the host goes forth against your enemies, then keep yourself from every wicked thing, the camp of the Lord having nothing offensive in it. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: If there is among you any man not clean by reason of uncleanness that chances him by night, then he shall go abroad out of the camp, and shall not come within the camp. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: But when evening comes on, he shall wash himself with water, and when the sun is down, he shall come into the camp again. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: You shall have a place also without the camp, where you shall go forth abroad, for the camp's outward cleanliness. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: And you shall have a paddle upon your weapon, and when you ease yourself abroad, you shall dig with it, and turn back and cover that which comes from you. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: For the Lord your God walks in the midst of your camp, to deliver you, and to give up your enemies before you, therefore shall your camp be holy, that he see no unclean thing in you, and turn away from you; if the body must be kept clean, much more the mind pure. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: You shall not deliver to his master the servant who has escaped from his master to you, it being honourable to shelter and protect the weak, provided they are not wicked. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: He shall dwell with you, even among you, in that place which he shall choose in one of your gates, where it likes him best; you shall not oppress him. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: There shall be no whore of the daughters of Israel, nor a sodomite of the sons of Israel, the sanctuary community being kept from cultic prostitution. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: You shall not bring the hire of a whore, or the price of a dog, into the house of the Lord for any vow, for even both these are abomination to the Lord; God cannot be honoured with substance dishonestly or dishonourably come by. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: You shall not lend upon usury to your brother, usury of money, usury of victuals, usury of anything that is lent upon usury. — Matthew Henry

20 COMMENTARY: To a stranger you may lend upon usury, but to your brother you shall not lend upon usury, that the Lord may bless you in all that you set your hand to in the land you go to possess; where the borrower gains, the lender may share, but to one borrowing for necessary food, pity must be shown. — Matthew Henry

21 COMMENTARY: When you vow a vow to the Lord your God, you shall not slack to pay it, for the Lord will surely require it of you, and it would be sin in you. — Matthew Henry

22 COMMENTARY: But if you forbear to vow, it shall be no sin in you, vows being free, not compulsory. — Matthew Henry

23 COMMENTARY: That which has gone out of your lips you shall keep and perform, even a freewill offering, according as you have vowed to the Lord, which you have promised with your mouth; a solemn and deliberate vow must not be recalled. — Matthew Henry

24 COMMENTARY: When you come into your neighbour's vineyard, then you may eat grapes your fill at your own pleasure, but you shall not put any in your vessel. — Matthew Henry

25 COMMENTARY: When you come into the standing corn of your neighbour, then you may pluck the ears with your hand, but you shall not move a sickle to your neighbour's standing corn; travellers might eat by the roadside, but must not carry away, a law providing for poor travellers and forbidding abuse of kindness. — Matthew Henry
