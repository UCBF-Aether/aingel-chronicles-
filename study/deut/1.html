Study Notes — Deuteronomy

Chapter 1

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Moses speaks all the Lord's words to all Israel in the plains of Moab, repeating the law to a new generation before they enter Canaan. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: Horeb was only eleven days' journey from Kadesh-barnea, a reminder that their own bad conduct, not the distance, caused their long wanderings. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: In the fortieth year, in the eleventh month, Moses spoke to Israel according to all the Lord had commanded him, about a month before his death. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: This came after Sihon of Heshbon and Og of Bashan had been defeated, when peace had been restored and the people could listen. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: On this side of Jordan, in Moab, Moses began to declare, that is to explain, this law, first recounting God's deeds and then rehearsing the precepts. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: God had said at Horeb, You have dwelt long enough in this mount: after about a year there laying the foundations of the new community, it was time to move. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: They were to turn and journey to the hill country of the Amorites and all the neighbouring places, toward the Canaanites and Lebanon as far as the Euphrates. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: God had set the land before them, accessible and unimpeded, and they were to go in and possess the land sworn to Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: Moses had told them at that time that he could not bear them alone, taking up Jethro's counsel for subordinate officers to share the administration of justice. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: God had multiplied them until they were as the stars of heaven for multitude, already far exceeding three thousand, a seasonable encouragement to trust the rest of the promise. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: Moses prayed that the Lord God of their fathers would make them a thousand times more and bless them as he had promised. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: How could Moses alone bear their cumbrance, burden, and strife? The sheer growth of the people made shared government necessary. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: They were to choose wise, understanding, reputable men from their tribes, whom Moses would make rulers over them. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: The people agreed that Moses' proposal was good, showing the plan had their consent as well as God's wisdom behind it. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: So Moses took the chief men of the tribes and made them heads: captains over thousands, hundreds, fifties, and tens, with officers among the tribes. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: He charged their judges to hear causes between brothers and strangers alike and judge righteously, showing God's goodness in giving both good laws and good men to execute them. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: In judgment they must not respect persons, but hear small and great alike, fearing no man's face, for the judgment is God's; hard cases were to be brought to Moses. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

18 COMMENTARY: Moses also commanded them at that time all the things they should do, completing the orderly constitution of their government. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

19 COMMENTARY: They departed from Horeb and went through the great and terrible wilderness toward the mountain of the Amorites, coming to Kadesh-barnea. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

20 COMMENTARY: Moses told them they had come to the mountain of the Amorites which the Lord their God was giving them, so near were they to a happy settlement. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

21 COMMENTARY: God had set the land before them: go up and possess it as the God of their fathers said, fearing not and not being discouraged. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

22 COMMENTARY: Yet the people proposed instead to send men ahead to search out the land and report the way up and the cities they would meet. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

23 COMMENTARY: The saying pleased Moses, and he took twelve men, one from each tribe, to search the land. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

24 COMMENTARY: They went up into the mountain, came to the valley of Eshcol, and searched it out. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

25 COMMENTARY: They brought back fruit of the land and reported that it was a good land which the Lord their God was giving them, as every spy must own. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

26 COMMENTARY: Nevertheless they would not go up, but rebelled against the commandment of the Lord their God, an unbelieving heart lying at the bottom of all this disobedience. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

27 COMMENTARY: They murmured in their tents, claiming God hated them and had brought them out of Egypt only to destroy them by the Amorites, a charge Henry calls flowing from disbelief of God's word. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

28 COMMENTARY: Their brethren discouraged their hearts, saying the people were greater and taller, the cities great and walled up to heaven, and the Anakim were there. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

29 COMMENTARY: Moses urged them, Dread not, neither be afraid of them, for courage in the path of duty enables the believer to follow the Lord fully. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

30 COMMENTARY: The Lord their God going before them would fight for them, as he had done in Egypt before their eyes. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

31 COMMENTARY: In the wilderness God had borne them as a man bears his son all the way until they came to this place, a tender proof of his care. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

32 COMMENTARY: Yet in this very thing they did not believe the Lord their God, though he had led them all along. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

33 COMMENTARY: He had gone before them to search out a camping place, in fire by night and cloud by day, to show them the way they should go. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

34 COMMENTARY: The Lord heard their words and was wroth, and swore that this evil generation would not see the good land. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

35 COMMENTARY: Surely not one of these men of this evil generation would see the good land sworn to their fathers, a judgment their unbelief had earned. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

36 COMMENTARY: Only Caleb son of Jephunneh would see it, and receive the land he had trodden, because he had wholly followed the Lord. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

37 COMMENTARY: The Lord was also angry with Moses for their sakes, saying he too would not go in; JFB notes this as a parenthesis, since Moses' exclusion was pronounced later at the waters of strife. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

38 COMMENTARY: But Joshua son of Nun, who stood before Moses, would go in, and Moses was to encourage him, for he would cause Israel to inherit the land. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

39 COMMENTARY: Their little ones, whom they feared would be prey, and their children who then knew neither good nor evil, would go in and possess the land, since they had no part in the parents' rebellion. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

40 COMMENTARY: As for the condemned generation, they were to turn and journey into the wilderness by the way of the Red Sea. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

41 COMMENTARY: Then they confessed, We have sinned, and said they would go up and fight as commanded, girding on their weapons ready for the hill. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

42 COMMENTARY: But the Lord told Moses to say, Go not up, neither fight, for I am not among you, lest you be smitten before your enemies. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

43 COMMENTARY: They would not hear, but rebelled again and went presumptuously up into the hill, their own plans seldom availing to good purpose. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

44 COMMENTARY: The Amorites in the mountain came out against them and chased them like bees, destroying them in Seir as far as Hormah. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

45 COMMENTARY: They returned and wept before the Lord, but he would not listen to their voice, for their sorrow was for the sad effects of their conduct, not true penitence for guilt. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

46 COMMENTARY: So they abode in Kadesh many days, according to the days they had abode there, the long stay marking the sentence they had brought on themselves. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
