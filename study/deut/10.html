Study Notes — Deuteronomy

Chapter 10

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: At that time the Lord said to Moses, Hew two tables of stone like the first, and come up to me into the mount, and make an ark of wood, the first of four signs that God was reconciled to Israel after their rebellion. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: And I will write on the tables the words that were in the first tables which you broke, and you shall put them in the ark: God gave them his law anew, a token of his presence and favour. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: And Moses made an ark of shittim wood, hewed two tables like the first, and went up into the mount with the two tables in his hand. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: And he wrote on the tables, according to the first writing, the ten commandments, which the Lord spoke to them in the mount out of the midst of the fire in the day of the assembly, and the Lord gave them to Moses. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: And Moses turned and came down from the mount, and put the tables in the ark he had made, and there they were, as the Lord commanded him. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: And the children of Israel journeyed from Beeroth of the children of Jaakan to Mosera, where Aaron died and was buried, and Eleazar his son ministered in his stead. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: From there they journeyed to Gudgodah, and from Gudgodah to Jotbath, a land of rivers of waters, the march resuming under God's renewed favour. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: At that time the Lord separated the tribe of Levi, to bear the ark of the covenant, to stand before the Lord to minister to him and bless in his name to this day: God appointed a standing ministry among them for holy things. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: Wherefore Levi has no part nor inheritance with his brethren; the Lord is his inheritance, as the Lord God said to him. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: And Moses stayed in the mount, according to the first time, forty days and forty nights, and the Lord hearkened to him at that time also, and would not destroy them. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: And the Lord said to Moses, Arise, take your journey before the people, that they may go in and possess the land sworn to their fathers: God led them forward toward Canaan. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: And now, Israel, what does the Lord your God require of you, but to fear the Lord, to walk in all his ways, to love him, and to serve the Lord with all your heart and soul? Duty is taught here in principles and practices. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: To keep the commandments of the Lord, and his statutes, which I command you this day for your good: obedience is for our benefit, and there is true honour and pleasure in it. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: Behold, the heaven and the heaven of heavens is the Lord's your God, the earth also, with all that is in it, showing the greatness of the God whose commands are so small a thing to keep. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: Only the Lord had a delight in your fathers to love them, and he chose their seed after them, even you above all people, as it is this day, out of free grace, not merit. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: Circumcise therefore the foreskin of your heart, and be no more stiffnecked: cast away corrupt affections that hinder fearing and loving God, for by nature we do not love God. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: For the Lord your God is God of gods, and Lord of lords, a great God, a mighty, and a terrible, who regards not persons nor takes reward. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: He executes the judgment of the fatherless and widow, and loves the stranger, giving him food and raiment; God's common gifts to mankind oblige us to honour all men. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: Love therefore the stranger, for you were strangers in the land of Egypt, and those who have found mercy in distress should show kindness to those in like distress. — Matthew Henry

20 COMMENTARY: You shall fear the Lord your God, him shall you serve, to him shall you cleave, and swear by his name, cleaving to him as one loved, trusted, and expected from. — Matthew Henry

21 COMMENTARY: He is your praise, and he is your God, who has done for you these great and terrible things, which your eyes have seen. — Matthew Henry

22 COMMENTARY: Your fathers went down into Egypt with seventy persons, and now the Lord your God has made you as the stars of heaven for multitude, a visible proof of his faithfulness. — Matthew Henry
