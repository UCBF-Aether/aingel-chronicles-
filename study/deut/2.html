Study Notes — Deuteronomy

Chapter 2

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Then they turned and journeyed into the wilderness by the way of the Red Sea, as the Lord had spoken to Moses, breaking up from Kadesh after their failed attack. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: The Lord spoke to Moses, directing the next stage of the long wandering that would last thirty-eight years in all. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: You have compassed this mountain long enough: turn northward, ending the nomadic shifting from place to place for pasture and water. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: Moses was to command the people that they were to pass through the coast of their brethren the children of Esau in Seir, who would be afraid of them now that Israel came round upon their weaker side. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: They must not meddle with Edom, for God would not give them so much as a foot's breadth of Edom's land, since he had given Mount Seir to Esau; religion must never be a cloak for injustice. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: They were to buy meat and water from the Edomites for money, using what they had cheerfully rather than any crooked method of supply, though manna still came daily. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: For the Lord had blessed them in all the work of their hands and knew their walking through the great wilderness these forty years, so that they had lacked nothing. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: When they passed by their brethren in Seir through the plain, by Elath and Ezion-geber, they kept to this peaceful course along Edom's border. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: The Lord then said, Distress not the Moabites, nor contend with them in battle, for he would not give them any of Moab's land, since he had given Ar to Lot's children. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: The Emim had dwelt there in times past, a people great, many, and tall as the Anakim, whom the Moabites had displaced. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: These Emim were also accounted giants like the Anakim, but the Moabites called them Emim, a name marking their terrible stature. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: The Horim had likewise dwelt in Seir before, but Esau's children succeeded them after destroying them, just as God had given other peoples their lands by conquest. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: Then Moses said, Rise up and get over the brook Zered, the southern border of Moab, and they went over it. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: The time from Kadesh-barnea until crossing the Zered was thirty-eight years, until all the generation of the men of war were wasted out. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: Indeed the hand of the Lord was against that generation, to destroy them from the host until they were consumed, the decree of Numbers 14 working itself out. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: So when all the men of war were consumed and dead from among the people, the new generation stood ready to move forward. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: Then the Lord spoke to Moses, beginning the next stage of the march toward the promised land. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

18 COMMENTARY: You are to pass through Ar, the coast of Moab, this day, still without molesting Moab, whose rich country lay between the Zered and the Arnon. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

19 COMMENTARY: When you come near the children of Ammon, distress them not nor meddle with them, for God would not give Israel Ammon's land, since he had given it to Lot's children. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

20 COMMENTARY: That also was accounted a land of giants, where the Zamzummim had dwelt in old time, whom the Ammonites so called. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

21 COMMENTARY: They were a people great, many, and tall as the Anakim, but the Lord destroyed them before the Ammonites, who succeeded them in their place. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

22 COMMENTARY: As he did to Esau's children in Seir when he destroyed the Horim before them, and as he did to the children of Lot, so God had ordered these ancient revolutions of peoples. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

23 COMMENTARY: And the Avim who dwelt in Hazerim as far as Azzah were destroyed by the Caphtorim from Caphtor, another instance of how uncertain worldly possessions are, and how God transfers lands between peoples. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

24 COMMENTARY: Rise up, take your journey, and pass over the river Arnon: behold, God had given Sihon the Amorite king of Heshbon and his land into Israel's hand, and they were to begin to possess it. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

25 COMMENTARY: This day God would begin to put the dread and fear of Israel upon the nations under heaven, so that hearing of them they would tremble in anguish. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

26 COMMENTARY: Moses sent messengers from the wilderness of Kedemoth to Sihon king of Heshbon with words of peace, as was customary before a march, requesting passage. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

27 COMMENTARY: Let me pass through your land, Moses asked, keeping to the highway without turning right or left, and without taking anything by force. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

28 COMMENTARY: You shall sell me meat and water for money, that I may eat and drink, and I will pass through on foot only. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

29 COMMENTARY: As the children of Esau in Seir and the Moabites in Ar had done, so Sihon was asked to let them pass until they crossed the Jordan into the promised land. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

30 COMMENTARY: But Sihon would not let them pass, for the Lord hardened his spirit and made his heart obstinate, so that he would be delivered into Israel's hand. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

31 COMMENTARY: The Lord said, Behold, I have begun to give Sihon and his land before you: begin to possess it, that you may inherit his land. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

32 COMMENTARY: Then Sihon came out against Israel, he and all his people, to fight at Jahaz, rejecting the peaceful request and drawing down the predicted doom. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

33 COMMENTARY: The Lord their God delivered him before them, and they smote him, his sons, and all his people. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

34 COMMENTARY: They took all his cities at that time and utterly destroyed the men, women, and little ones of every city, leaving none remaining, as a war of extermination against a doomed people. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

35 COMMENTARY: Only the cattle they took for prey, and the spoil of the cities they had taken. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

36 COMMENTARY: From Aroer on the brink of the Arnon, and from the city by the river, even to Gilead, no city was too strong for them; the Lord delivered them all. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

37 COMMENTARY: Only to the land of the Ammonites they did not come, nor to any place by the river Jabbok, nor to the hill country, keeping God's prohibition exactly even in victory. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
