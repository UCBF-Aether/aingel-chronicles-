Study Notes — Deuteronomy

Chapter 11

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Therefore you shall love the Lord your God, and keep his charge, statutes, judgments, and commandments always; love works in obedience, and only obedience flowing from love is acceptable. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: And know you this day, for I speak not with your children who have not known and have not seen the chastisement of the Lord, his greatness, mighty hand, and stretched-out arm. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: And his miracles, and his acts, which he did in the midst of Egypt to Pharaoh king of Egypt and to all his land. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: And what he did to the army of Egypt, to their horses and chariots, how he made the water of the Red Sea overflow them as they pursued you, and destroyed them to this day. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: And what he did to you in the wilderness, until you came to this place, the whole course of providence that should affect us long afterwards. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: And what he did to Dathan and Abiram, sons of Eliab son of Reuben, how the earth opened her mouth and swallowed them, their households, tents, and all their substance in the midst of Israel. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: But your eyes have seen all the great acts of the Lord which he did, so the obligation rests on eyewitnesses, not on hearsay. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: Therefore you shall keep all the commandments which I command you this day, that you may be strong, and go in and possess the land you are going over to possess. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: And that you may prolong your days in the land sworn to your fathers to give to them and their seed, a land flowing with milk and honey. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: For the land you are going to possess is not as the land of Egypt, from which you came, where you sowed seed and watered it with your foot, as a garden of herbs. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: But the land you go to possess is a land of hills and valleys, drinking water of the rain of heaven. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: A land which the Lord your God cares for; the eyes of the Lord your God are always upon it, from the beginning of the year even to the end of the year. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: And it shall come to pass, if you hearken diligently to my commandments, to love the Lord and serve him with all your heart and soul. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: That I will give you the rain of your land in its due season, the first rain and the latter rain, that you may gather in your corn, wine, and oil. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: And I will send grass in your fields for your cattle, that you may eat and be full; godliness has the promise of the life that now is, and God's favour puts gladness in the heart beyond increase of corn and wine. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: Take heed to yourselves, that your heart be not deceived, and you turn aside and serve other gods and worship them. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: And then the Lord's wrath be kindled against you, and he shut up heaven, that there be no rain, and the land yield not her fruit, and you perish quickly from off the good land he gives you; revolt to idols would certainly be their ruin. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: Therefore you shall lay up these my words in your heart and soul, bind them for a sign upon your hand, that they may be as frontlets between your eyes: heart, eyes, and hands must all be governed by the word. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: And you shall teach them to your children, speaking of them when you sit in your house, walk by the way, lie down, and rise up, for nothing keeps up religion in a nation like the good education of children. — Matthew Henry

20 COMMENTARY: And you shall write them upon the door posts of your house and upon your gates, keeping the word constantly in view at home and abroad. — Matthew Henry

21 COMMENTARY: That your days may be multiplied, and the days of your children, in the land sworn to your fathers, as the days of heaven upon the earth. — Matthew Henry

22 COMMENTARY: For if you diligently keep all these commandments, to love the Lord, walk in all his ways, and cleave to him. — Matthew Henry

23 COMMENTARY: Then will the Lord drive out all these nations from before you, and you shall possess greater nations and mightier than yourselves. — Matthew Henry

24 COMMENTARY: Every place whereon the soles of your feet shall tread shall be yours, from the wilderness and Lebanon, from the river Euphrates even to the uttermost sea, shall your coast be. — Matthew Henry

25 COMMENTARY: There shall no man be able to stand before you, for the Lord shall lay the fear of you and the dread of you upon all the land you tread, as he has said to you. — Matthew Henry

26 COMMENTARY: Behold, I set before you this day a blessing and a curse, summing up all the arguments for obedience in two words, and charging the people to choose. — Matthew Henry

27 COMMENTARY: A blessing, if you obey the commandments of the Lord your God, which I command you this day. — Matthew Henry

28 COMMENTARY: And a curse, if you will not obey the commandments of the Lord, but turn aside out of the way commanded you, to go after other gods you have not known. — Matthew Henry

29 COMMENTARY: And when the Lord brings you into the land you are going to possess, you shall put the blessing upon Mount Gerizim, and the curse upon Mount Ebal. — Matthew Henry

30 COMMENTARY: Are they not on the other side Jordan, by the way where the sun goes down, in the land of the Canaanites, who dwell in the plain over against Gilgal, beside the plains of Moreh? — Matthew Henry

31 COMMENTARY: For you shall pass over Jordan to go in to possess the land which the Lord gives you, and you shall possess it and dwell in it. — Matthew Henry

32 COMMENTARY: And you shall observe to do all the statutes and judgments which I set before you this day, the public proclamation of blessing and curse being itself a call to choose while it is called today. — Matthew Henry
