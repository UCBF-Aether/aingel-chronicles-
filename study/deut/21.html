Study Notes — Deuteronomy

Chapter 21

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: If one is found slain in the land the Lord gives you to possess, lying in the field, and it is not known who slew him, the land itself bears guilt that must be solemnly put away. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: Then your elders and judges shall come forth, and measure to the cities round about him who is slain, to determine which city bears responsibility for the expiation. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: And the city nearest the slain man, even the elders of that city, shall take a heifer, which has not been wrought with, and which has not drawn in the yoke. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: And the elders of that city shall bring down the heifer to a rough valley, neither eared nor sown, and shall strike off the heifer's neck there in the valley. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: And the priests, sons of Levi, shall come near, for the Lord has chosen them to minister to him, and to bless in the name of the Lord, and by their word shall every controversy and every stroke be tried. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: And all the elders of that city, nearest the slain man, shall wash their hands over the heifer that is beheaded in the valley. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: And they shall answer and say, Our hands have not shed this blood, neither have our eyes seen it, professing they had no way aided or abetted the sin. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: Be merciful, O Lord, to your people Israel, whom you have redeemed, and lay not innocent blood to your people Israel's charge, and the blood shall be forgiven them; the priests pray for the country, emptying by prayer the measure others fill by sin. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: So you shall put away the guilt of innocent blood from among you, when you do what is right in the sight of the Lord, the solemnity teaching all to prevent, discover, and punish murder. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: When you go forth to war against your enemies, and the Lord delivers them into your hands, and you have taken them captive. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: And see among the captives a beautiful woman, and have a desire to her, that you would have her to your wife. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: Then you shall bring her home to your house, and she shall shave her head, and pare her nails. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: And she shall put the raiment of her captivity from off her, and remain in your house, bewailing her father and mother a full month, and after that you shall go in to her and be her husband, and she shall be your wife. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: And it shall be, if you have no delight in her, then you shall let her go where she will, but you shall not sell her at all for money, nor make merchandise of her, because you have humbled her; the law, Henry notes, does not approve the marriage, but binds it with justice and honour as a sacred engagement. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: If a man has two wives, one beloved and another hated, and they have borne him children, both the beloved and the hated, and the firstborn son is hers that was hated. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: Then when he makes his sons inherit what he has, he may not make the son of the beloved firstborn before the son of the hated, who is indeed the firstborn. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: But he shall acknowledge the son of the hated for the firstborn, by giving him a double portion of all that he has, for he is the beginning of his strength, and the right of the firstborn is his; parents must give children their right without partiality. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: If a man has a stubborn and rebellious son, who will not obey the voice of his father or mother, and though they chasten him, will not hearken to them. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: Then his father and mother shall lay hold on him, and bring him out to the elders of his city, and to the gate of his place. — Matthew Henry

20 COMMENTARY: And they shall say to the elders of his city, This our son is stubborn and rebellious, he will not obey our voice, he is a glutton and a drunkard; the crime is wilfulness and obstinacy, not weakness or dulness, and drunkenness more than anything draws men into wickedness and hardens them in it. — Matthew Henry

21 COMMENTARY: And all the men of his city shall stone him with stones, that he die, so you shall put evil away from among you, and all Israel shall hear and fear; disobedience to parents must be very evil when such a punishment was ordered. — Matthew Henry

22 COMMENTARY: And if a man has committed a sin worthy of death, and he is to be put to death, and you hang him on a tree. — Matthew Henry

23 COMMENTARY: His body shall not remain all night upon the tree, but you shall in any wise bury him that day, for he that is hanged is accursed of God, and your land shall not be defiled, which the Lord gives you for an inheritance; Henry notes this phrase was later applied to Christ, who underwent the curse of the law for us. — Matthew Henry
