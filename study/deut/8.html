Study Notes — Deuteronomy

Chapter 8

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: All the commandments which I command you this day you shall observe to do, that you may live and multiply, and go in and possess the land sworn to your fathers; obedience must be careful, universal, and from a good principle. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: And you shall remember all the way which the Lord led you these forty years in the wilderness, to humble you, to prove you, to know what was in your heart, whether you would keep his commandments or not. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: And he humbled you, suffered you to hunger, and fed you with manna which neither you nor your fathers knew, that he might make you know that man does not live by bread only, but by every word proceeding out of the mouth of the Lord. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: Your raiment did not grow old upon you, neither did your foot swell, these forty years, miraculous supplies matching the miraculous discipline. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: You shall also consider in your heart that, as a man chastens his son, so the Lord your God chastens you; afflictions are to quicken us to duty. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: Therefore you shall keep the commandments of the Lord, to walk in his ways, and to fear him. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: For the Lord your God brings you into a good land, a land of brooks of water, fountains and depths springing out of valleys and hills. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: A land of wheat, barley, vines, fig trees, and pomegranates, a land of oil olive and honey. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: A land in which you shall eat bread without scarceness, lacking nothing in it, a land whose stones are iron and out of whose hills you may dig brass. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: When you have eaten and are full, then you shall bless the Lord your God for the good land which he has given you, giving thanks in prosperity as well as need. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: Beware that you forget not the Lord your God, in not keeping his commandments, judgments, and statutes which I command you this day. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: Lest when you have eaten and are full, and have built goodly houses and dwelt in them. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: And when your herds and flocks multiply, and your silver and gold is multiplied, and all that you have is multiplied. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: Then your heart be lifted up, and you forget the Lord your God, who brought you out of Egypt, from the house of bondage; pride, forgetfulness, and carnal-mindedness are the strong temptations of a prosperous condition. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: Who led you through that great and terrible wilderness, with fiery serpents, scorpions, and drought, where there was no water, and who brought you water out of the rock of flint. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: Who fed you in the wilderness with manna which your fathers knew not, that he might humble you and prove you, to do you good at your latter end. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: And you say in your heart, My power and the might of my hand has gotten me this wealth, the proud claim prosperity forgets to examine. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: But you shall remember the Lord your God, for it is he who gives you power to get wealth, that he may establish his covenant sworn to your fathers, as it is this day; God's giving and our getting are reconciled in his promises. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: And it shall be, if you do at all forget the Lord, and walk after other gods, and serve and worship them, I testify against you this day that you shall surely perish. — Matthew Henry

20 COMMENTARY: As the nations which the Lord destroys before your face, so shall you perish, because you would not be obedient to the voice of the Lord your God; those who follow sinners in sin will follow them to destruction. — Matthew Henry
