Study Notes — Deuteronomy

Chapter 19

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Possession brings obligation: once Israel dwells in the conquered cities and houses, the refuge law must already be in place, so that bloodguilt does not take root in the new inheritance. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: Three more cities west of Jordan complete the pattern Henry traces through the chapter: refuge must lie within reach on both sides of the river, so that no slayer need outrun the avenger to find safety. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: The way must be prepared and the land divided in three, so that refuge lies within reach wherever the slayer stands: Henry's section makes the whole provision turn on a man not dying for what was not his willing act, and a refuge too far away would defeat it. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: The case turns on ignorance and absence of hatred: the slayer may live not because killing is light, but because the law distinguishes a death without malice from murder. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: The axe-head case is Henry's pattern of unknowing manslaughter: no prior hatred, no intent, only a slipped tool, and that absence of malice is exactly what separates a man who may live in refuge from a murderer. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: The cities of refuge exist because a slayer pursued in hot blood might be killed for what was not his willing act; Henry reads that refuge as Christ, to whom those who flee from their sins are safe, though never as shelter for continuing in them. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: Wherefore I command you, saying, You shall separate three cities for yourself, the provision being that a man should not die for what was not his willing act. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: Enlargement is promise, not blank grant: the extra land comes as God swore to the fathers, and the next verse ties three added cities to keeping the commandments, so blessing expands only with obedience. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: If you keep all these commandments to do them, commanded this day, to love the Lord and walk ever in his ways, then you shall add three cities more for yourself, beside these three. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: Innocent blood shed without refuge would leave bloodguilt on the whole land: the cities exist so that an unknowing slayer's death does not become Israel's corporate guilt. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: The marks are premeditation, hatred, ambush and rising up, so the law can tell murder from mischance before the man ever reaches a city. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: Then the elders of his city shall send and fetch him from there, and deliver him into the hand of the avenger of blood, that he may die; there is no refuge in Christ for presumptuous sinners who go on in their trespasses. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: Pity must not spare the murderer: sparing him would leave innocent blood unpurged from Israel, and the nation's welfare turns on purging it rather than on mercy to the guilty. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: You shall not remove your neighbour's landmark, which they of old time have set in your inheritance, which you shall inherit in the land the Lord gives you to possess; it is God's will that everyone should know his own, and this moral precept is still binding. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: The rule guards both ways: it protects the accused from a lone accuser and gives the court a test, agreement of independent witnesses, before any matter is treated as established. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: A witness who rises to testify falsely faces, in Henry's judgment, the justest of laws: he must suffer the very punishment he sought to have inflicted on the man he accused. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: The trial is held as before the Lord himself: priests and judges sit, but the controversy is judged in God's presence, which is why a false oath there carries the weight it does. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: Judges must inquire diligently before condemning: a witness is not presumed true because he swore, and the court must test whether his testimony against his brother is false. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: Then shall you do to him as he had thought to have done to his brother, so you shall put the evil away from among you; a false witness suffers the punishment he sought for the accused, and no law could be more just. — Matthew Henry

20 COMMENTARY: Public punishment of the false witness is meant to be heard and feared, so that the rest commit no more such evil; Henry adds the private warning not to join in slander even outside the courtroom. — Matthew Henry

21 COMMENTARY: The talion measures punishment to the injury: life for life, eye for eye, so that vengeance cannot exceed the wrong, and the false witness in particular suffers exactly what he sought for his brother. — Matthew Henry
