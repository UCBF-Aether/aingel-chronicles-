Study Notes — Deuteronomy

Chapter 31

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: And Moses went and spoke these words to all Israel, returning to the assembly on what was probably the last and most important day of his rehearsal of the law, which may have extended over several successive days. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: And he said to them, I am a hundred and twenty years old this day; I can no more go out and come in; also the Lord has said to me, You shall not go over this Jordan; though neither his physical nor intellectual powers had decayed, he knew by special revelation that his superintendence of Israel was ending. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: The leadership changes but the vanguard does not: God himself goes over before Israel to destroy the nations, and Joshua goes over before them only as the Lord has said, for the promise, not the man, leads. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: Past conquest is the pledge for the next: what God did to Sihon and Og and their land is the measure of what he will do to the nations ahead, so Israel faces Canaan with precedent, not hope alone. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: Victory comes with a condition attached: the nations are given up before Israel so that Israel may deal with them according to all the commandments, for conquest is obedience, not licence. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: Be strong and of a good courage, fear not, nor be afraid of them, for the Lord your God, he it is who goes with you, and he will not fail you, nor forsake you; Moses exhorted them not to be intimidated, but to rest on the same divine power that had discomfited their first assailants east of Jordan. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: The charge is given in public: Moses calls Joshua in the sight of all Israel, so the succession is witnessed by the whole nation and no rival claim can arise after Moses. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: Joshua hears the same promise Moses gave the people, now spoken to him alone: the Lord goes before him, stays with him, and will neither fail nor forsake, so courage rests on presence, not on ability. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: And Moses wrote this law, and delivered it to the priests, sons of Levi, who bore the ark of the covenant of the Lord, and to all the elders of Israel; two copies were made, as in public contracts, one deposited with the public representatives, the priests and elders, for reference and preservation. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: At the return of the sabbatic year, during the feast of tabernacles, the law was to be read publicly before women and children as well as men; JFB notes this periodical rehearsal both impressed divine truth in the sacred season and guaranteed the faithful transmission of the book to successive ages. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: Reading is hearing for the whole nation: when all Israel appears at the chosen place, the law is read in their hearing, so no Israelite's obedience rests on secondhand knowledge. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: Gather the people together, men and women, and children, and your stranger within your gates, that they may hear, and learn, and fear the Lord, and observe to do all the words of this law. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: And that their children, who have not known anything, may hear, and learn to fear the Lord your God, as long as you live in the land where you go over Jordan to possess it; the public periodical rehearsal, with women and children present, was a guarantee for the preservation and faithful transmission of the sacred book. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: Moses' death is announced as approaching, not sudden: Joshua is called to the tabernacle so that the charge passes in God's presence, in the place of meeting, before Moses is gone. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: And the Lord appeared in the tabernacle in a pillar of a cloud, and the pillar of the cloud stood over the door of the tabernacle; Joshua, already designated commander, was now confirmed by the visible symbols of God's presence and approval. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: And the Lord said to Moses, Behold, you shall sleep with your fathers, and this people will rise up, and go a whoring after the gods of the strangers of the land, where they go to be among them, and will forsake me, and break my covenant which I have made with them; Moses was distinctly apprised of Israel's coming infidelity through intercourse with Canaan's idolaters. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: Then my anger shall be kindled against them in that day, and I will forsake them, and I will hide my face from them, and they shall be devoured, and many evils and troubles shall befall them, so that they will say in that day, Are not these evils come upon us, because our God is not among us? The hiding of God's face announced the withdrawal of the favour of which the Shekinah was symbol and pledge. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

18 COMMENTARY: The hiding is doubled, surely hide, and its cause is named: evils wrought by turning to other gods, so God's absence is presented as the consequence of Israel's turning, not as divine caprice. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

19 COMMENTARY: Now therefore write this song for you, and teach it the children of Israel; put it in their mouths, that this song may be a witness for me against the children of Israel; national songs take deep hold of memory, and this song, embodying the substance of Moses' addresses, was to be learnt in every age. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

20 COMMENTARY: For when I shall have brought them into the land which I swore to their fathers, flowing with milk and honey, and they shall have eaten and filled themselves, and waxen fat, then will they turn to other gods, and serve them, and provoke me, and break my covenant. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

21 COMMENTARY: The song is a witness that outlives memory: even before Israel enters the land, God knows the imagination they go about, and the unforgotten song will testify against them when evils befall. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

22 COMMENTARY: JFB explains the song's purpose at v 19: national songs take deep hold of memory and stir a people's deepest feelings, so this song was to be learnt by Israel and taught to their children in every age, embodying God's favour to the nation. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

23 COMMENTARY: The charge passes from Moses to God himself: he who gives Joshua his charge says, be strong, for you shall bring Israel into the sworn land, and I will be with you, so the same presence promised to Moses now rests on his successor. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

24 COMMENTARY: The writing delivered to the priests at v 9 here reaches its end as a finished book; JFB traces that same written law to its deposit beside the ark at v 26, kept for security and reverence as the nation's covenant record. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

25 COMMENTARY: The command goes to the Levites who bear the ark: the law's keepers are those who carry the covenant chest itself, so custody and carriage belong together. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

26 COMMENTARY: Take this book of the law, and put it in the side of the ark of the covenant of the Lord your God, that it may be there for a witness against you; this second copy was deposited beside the ark for greater security and reverence, and is thought to be the copy found in Josiah's time. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

27 COMMENTARY: Moses names rebellion while still alive with them: if they rebelled under his eye, he asks how much more after his death, and the book beside the ark is left as witness precisely because he knows their neck. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

28 COMMENTARY: Elders and officers are gathered as witnesses in their own right: Moses speaks in their ears and calls heaven and earth to record, so the song that follows is heard by representatives and by creation itself. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

29 COMMENTARY: Corruption after Moses' death is foreknown, not merely feared: turning aside from the commanded way and provoking God by handmade work brings evil in the latter days, exactly as the song will have warned. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

30 COMMENTARY: The song is spoken whole, in the ears of all the congregation, until ended: nothing is held back from the assembly that must one day answer for it. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
