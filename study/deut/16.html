Study Notes — Deuteronomy

Chapter 16

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Observe the month of Abib, and keep the passover to the Lord your God, for in the month of Abib the Lord brought you out of Egypt by night; the believer should never forget his low estate, deliverance, and its cost. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: You shall therefore sacrifice the passover to the Lord, of the flock and the herd, in the place which the Lord shall choose to place his name there. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: You shall eat no leavened bread with it; seven days you shall eat unleavened bread with it, the bread of affliction, for you came out of Egypt in haste, that you may remember the day you came out all the days of your life. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: And there shall be no leavened bread seen with you in all your coast seven days, neither shall any of the flesh sacrificed the first day at evening remain all night until the morning. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: You may not sacrifice the passover within any of your gates, which the Lord your God gives you, the feast being tied to the chosen place, not to private convenience. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: But at the place which the Lord shall choose to place his name in, there you shall sacrifice the passover at evening, at the going down of the sun, at the season that you came out of Egypt. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: And you shall roast and eat it in the place which the Lord shall choose, and you shall turn in the morning and go to your tents. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: Six days you shall eat unleavened bread, and on the seventh day shall be a solemn assembly to the Lord, in which you shall do no work. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: Seven weeks you shall number to you; begin to number the seven weeks from the time you begin to put the sickle to the corn, the feast of weeks following the harvest's beginning. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: And you shall keep the feast of weeks to the Lord with a tribute of a freewill offering of your hand, which you shall give according as the Lord has blessed you. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: And you shall rejoice before the Lord, you, your son and daughter, manservant and maidservant, the Levite within your gates, the stranger, fatherless, and widow among you, in the place the Lord shall choose to place his name. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: And you shall remember that you were a bondman in Egypt, and you shall observe and do these statutes, gratitude and joy mingling with sorrow for sin. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: You shall observe the feast of tabernacles seven days, after you have gathered in your corn and wine. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: And you shall rejoice in your feast, you, your son and daughter, manservant and maidservant, the Levite, stranger, fatherless, and widow within your gates, rejoicing in receivings from God and in returns of service to him. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: Seven days you shall keep a solemn feast to the Lord in the place which the Lord shall choose, because the Lord shall bless you in all your increase and in all the works of your hands, therefore you shall surely rejoice. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: Three times in a year shall all your males appear before the Lord in the place he shall choose, at the feast of unleavened bread, the feast of weeks, and the feast of tabernacles, and they shall not appear before the Lord empty. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: Every man shall give as he is able, according to the blessing of the Lord which he has given you, duty being delight as well as enjoyment. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: Judges and officers you shall make in all your gates, which the Lord gives you, throughout your tribes, and they shall judge the people with just judgment. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: You shall not wrest judgment, respect persons, nor take a gift, for a gift blinds the eyes of the wise and perverts the words of the righteous; all personal regards must be laid aside so right is done to all. — Matthew Henry

20 COMMENTARY: That which is altogether just you shall follow, that you may live, and inherit the land which the Lord gives you. — Matthew Henry

21 COMMENTARY: You shall not plant a grove of any trees near the altar of the Lord, which you shall make for yourself, copying heathen idolatrous customs beside God's altar. — Matthew Henry

22 COMMENTARY: Neither shall you set up any image, which the Lord your God hates, for nothing belies God more than representing by an image the God who is an almighty and eternal Spirit, present everywhere. — Matthew Henry
