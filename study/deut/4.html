Study Notes — Deuteronomy

Chapter 4

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Now therefore hearken, O Israel, to the statutes and judgments Moses taught them, to do them, that they might live and possess the land the God of their fathers gave them; statutes covered worship, judgments covered civil matters, together the whole law. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: You shall not add to the word commanded, by introducing heathen superstition or other forms of worship, nor diminish anything from it by neglecting any observance, however trivial it seemed. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: Your eyes had seen what the Lord did because of Baal-peor, destroying from among them all the men who followed Baal-peor, a recent and appalling judgment meant as a dissuasive from idolatry. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: But those who clung to the Lord their God were alive every one of them that day, spared while only the guilty perished. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: Behold, Moses had taught them statutes and judgments just as the Lord his God commanded him, that they should do so in the land they were going to possess. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: Keep therefore and do them, for this is your wisdom and understanding in the sight of the nations, who hearing these statutes would admire a people so wisely governed. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: For what nation is so great, who has God so near to them as the Lord our God is in all things they call upon him for? God's readiness to hear was one of their two great national advantages. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: And what nation is so great, that has statutes and judgments so righteous as all this law set before them that day? The excellence of their religion was the other. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: Only take heed to yourself and keep your soul diligently, lest you forget the things your eyes have seen and they depart from your heart all your life; teach them to your sons and grandsons. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: Especially remember the day you stood before the Lord in Horeb, when he said, Gather me the people, and I will make them hear my words, that they may learn to fear me always and teach their children. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: You came near and stood under the mountain, and the mountain burned with fire to the midst of heaven, with darkness, clouds, and thick darkness. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: The Lord spoke to you out of the midst of the fire; you heard the voice of the words but saw no similitude, no form to indicate God's nature as heathen gods were pictured. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: He declared to you his covenant, which he commanded you to perform, even the ten commandments, and wrote them upon two tables of stone. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: The Lord commanded Moses at that time to teach them statutes and judgments, that they might do them in the land they were going over to possess. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: Take therefore good heed to yourselves, for you saw no manner of similitude on the day the Lord spoke to you in Horeb out of the midst of the fire. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: Lest you corrupt yourselves and make a graven image, the similitude of any figure, male or female, for worship. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: The likeness of any beast on the earth, or any winged fowl that flies in the air, was likewise forbidden as an object of worship. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

18 COMMENTARY: The likeness of anything creeping on the ground, or any fish in the waters beneath the earth, was forbidden too, covering every form of creature idolatry. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

19 COMMENTARY: And lest you lift your eyes to heaven and, seeing sun, moon, and stars, the whole host of heaven, be driven to worship and serve them, which the Lord had given to all nations under heaven. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

20 COMMENTARY: But the Lord had taken them and brought them out of the iron furnace, out of Egypt, to be his people of inheritance, his peculiar possession from age to age. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

21 COMMENTARY: Furthermore the Lord was angry with Moses for their sakes, and swore that he should not go over Jordan into the good land God was giving them for an inheritance. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

22 COMMENTARY: But Moses must die in this land and not go over Jordan, while they would go over and possess that good land, a sober reminder to the new generation. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

23 COMMENTARY: Take heed to yourselves, lest you forget the covenant of the Lord your God which he made with you, and make a graven image in the likeness of anything the Lord forbade. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

24 COMMENTARY: For the Lord your God is a consuming fire, even a jealous God, who will not share his worship with idols. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

25 COMMENTARY: When you beget children and grandchildren and have remained long in the land, and corrupt yourselves and make a graven image and do evil in the Lord's sight, provoking him to anger. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

26 COMMENTARY: I call heaven and earth to witness against you this day, that you shall soon utterly perish from off the land you are going over Jordan to possess, and not prolong your days, but be utterly destroyed. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

27 COMMENTARY: The Lord shall scatter you among the nations, and you shall be left few in number among the heathen to whom the Lord leads you. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

28 COMMENTARY: There you shall serve gods, the work of men's hands, wood and stone, which neither see nor hear nor eat nor smell; their chosen idolatry would become their punishment. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

29 COMMENTARY: But if from there you shall seek the Lord your God, you shall find him, if you seek him with all your heart and all your soul. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

30 COMMENTARY: When you are in tribulation and all these things have come upon you, even in the latter days, if you turn to the Lord your God and obey his voice. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

31 COMMENTARY: For the Lord your God is a merciful God; he will not forsake you, nor destroy you, nor forget the covenant he swore to your fathers. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

32 COMMENTARY: For ask now of the days past, before you, since God created man upon the earth, and from one side of heaven to the other, whether any such great thing has happened or been heard. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

33 COMMENTARY: Did ever a people hear the voice of God speaking out of the midst of the fire, as you have heard, and live? No other nation's history held such an event. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

34 COMMENTARY: Or has God assayed to take a nation from the midst of another nation, by temptations, signs, wonders, war, a mighty hand, a stretched-out arm, and great terrors, as the Lord did for Israel in Egypt before their eyes? — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

35 COMMENTARY: To you it was showed, that you might know that the Lord he is God, and there is none else beside him. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

36 COMMENTARY: Out of heaven he made you hear his voice, that he might instruct you, and upon earth he showed you his great fire, and you heard his words out of the midst of the fire. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

37 COMMENTARY: And because he loved your fathers, therefore he chose their seed after them, and brought you out in his sight with his mighty power out of Egypt. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

38 COMMENTARY: To drive out nations greater and mightier than you, to bring you in and give you their land for an inheritance, as it is this day. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

39 COMMENTARY: Know therefore this day, and consider it in your heart, that the Lord he is God in heaven above and upon the earth beneath, and there is none else. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

40 COMMENTARY: You shall keep therefore his statutes and commandments, which Moses commanded that day, that it may go well with you and your children after you, and that you may prolong your days upon the land the Lord gives you for ever. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

41 COMMENTARY: Then Moses severed three cities on this side Jordan toward the sunrising, as cities of refuge east of Jordan. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

42 COMMENTARY: That the slayer might flee there who killed his neighbour unawares, without hating him in times past, and live, protected from the avenger. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

43 COMMENTARY: Namely, Bezer in the wilderness in the plain country for the Reubenites, Ramoth in Gilead for the Gadites, and Golan in Bashan for the Manassites. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

44 COMMENTARY: And this is the law which Moses set before the children of Israel, a preface to the rehearsal of the law in the following chapters. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

45 COMMENTARY: These are the testimonies, statutes, and judgments which Moses spoke to the children of Israel after they came forth out of Egypt. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

46 COMMENTARY: On this side Jordan, in the valley over against Beth-peor, in the land of Sihon king of the Amorites, who dwelt at Heshbon, whom Moses and Israel smote after leaving Egypt. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

47 COMMENTARY: And they possessed his land, and the land of Og king of Bashan, two kings of the Amorites east of Jordan. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

48 COMMENTARY: From Aroer on the bank of the Arnon, even to Mount Sion, which is Hermon, stretched this conquered territory. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

49 COMMENTARY: And all the plain east of Jordan, even to the sea of the plain, under the springs of Pisgah, completed the account of the land already possessed. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
