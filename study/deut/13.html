Study Notes — Deuteronomy

Chapter 13

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: If there arise among you a prophet, or a dreamer of dreams, and he gives a sign or a wonder, the danger warned against here is idolatry arising from within Israel itself, not only from the Canaanites. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: And the sign or wonder comes to pass, of which he spoke, saying, Let us go after other gods, which you have not known, and let us serve them: even a fulfilled prediction does not authenticate a call away from God. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: You shall not hearken to the words of that prophet or dreamer, for the Lord your God proves you, to know whether you love the Lord with all your heart and soul; temptations of error in the guise of truth are met only by the plain testimony of God's word to the contrary. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: You shall walk after the Lord, fear him, keep his commandments, obey his voice, serve him, and cleave to him, the positive course that leaves no room for following the enticer. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: And that prophet or dreamer shall be put to death, because he has spoken to turn you away from the Lord, who brought you out of Egypt and redeemed you from bondage, seeking to thrust you out of the way commanded you; so you shall put the evil away from your midst. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: If your brother, your mother's son, or your son, or daughter, or the wife of your bosom, or your friend who is as your own soul, entices you secretly, saying, Let us go and serve other gods, which neither you nor your fathers have known. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: Namely, of the gods of the peoples round about you, near or far off, from one end of the earth to the other: Satan's policy is to lead us by those we love, least suspect, and most desire to please. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: You shall not consent to him, nor hearken to him, nor shall your eye pity him, nor spare him, nor conceal him, not for company, curiosity, or affection; God and religion are to be preferred before the nearest and dearest friends. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: But you shall surely kill him; your hand shall be first upon him to put him to death, and afterwards the hand of all the people, so that private affection does not hinder the course of God's justice. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: And you shall stone him with stones, that he die, because he has sought to thrust you away from the Lord, who brought you out of Egypt, from the house of bondage. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: And all Israel shall hear, and fear, and shall do no more any such wickedness as this is among you, the public punishment teaching the whole nation. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: If you hear say in one of your cities, which the Lord gives you to dwell in, that a revolt is reported there. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: Certain men, children of Belial, have gone out from among you, and have withdrawn the inhabitants of their city, saying, Let us go and serve other gods, which you have not known. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: Then you shall enquire, make search, and ask diligently, and behold, if it is truth and the thing certain, that such abomination is wrought among you: even a city is not condemned without careful inquiry. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: You shall surely smite the inhabitants of that city with the edge of the sword, destroying it utterly, and all that is in it, and its cattle, with the edge of the sword. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: And you shall gather all its spoil into the midst of its street, and burn the city with fire, with all its spoil, for the Lord, and it shall be a heap for ever, never to be built again. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: And there shall cleave nothing of the cursed thing to your hand, that the Lord may turn from the fierceness of his anger, and show you mercy, have compassion on you, and multiply you, as he swore to your fathers. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: When you hearken to the voice of the Lord, to keep all his commandments commanded this day, and to do what is right in the eyes of the Lord your God, the severity against apostasy is itself the guard of mercy to the nation. — Matthew Henry
