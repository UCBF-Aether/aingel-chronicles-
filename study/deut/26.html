Study Notes — Deuteronomy

Chapter 26

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: The land is inheritance before it is harvest: firstfruits are owed because possession itself is gift, and the offering that opens the chapter is the acknowledgement of that gift. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: That you shall take of the first of all the fruit of the earth, which you shall bring of your land given you, and put it in a basket, and go to the place which the Lord shall choose to place his name there. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: The offerer professes, not merely presents: he tells the priest that the promised land has arrived, turning a basket of fruit into a spoken confession of God's fulfilled oath to the fathers. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: The priest receives the basket and sets it before the altar: the gift passes out of the offerer's hands into God's presence, so firstfruits are worship, not a fee. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: And you shall speak and say before the Lord, A Syrian ready to perish was my father, and he went down into Egypt, and sojourned there with a few, and became there a nation, great, mighty, and populous; the offerer must own the mean origin of his nation, Jacob called a Syrian, and a people poor and oppressed in Egypt. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: In the first-fruits confession Israel must own its mean origin: the nation sojourned in Egypt as strangers, served there as slaves, and was poor, despised and oppressed, so that, now rich and great, it has no reason to be proud or forgetful of God. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: The confession moves from affliction to audience: Israel cried, God heard and looked, so deliverance begins not with Israel's strength but with God's attention to its labour and oppression. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: Exodus is told as God's hand, not Israel's escape: mighty hand, outstretched arm, terror, signs and wonders together say the deliverance was wholly God's work. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: The gift completes the journey: from Egypt's bondage to a land flowing with milk and honey, the same God who brought them out is the God who brings them in. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: And now, behold, I have brought the firstfruits of the land, which you, O Lord, have given me, and you shall set it before the Lord, and worship before the Lord; creature comforts are doubly sweet when seen flowing from the fountain of promise, and thankfulness for present mercies should join remembrance of former ones. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: And you shall rejoice in every good thing which the Lord has given to you, and to your house, you, the Levite, and the stranger among you, tracing the streams to the Fountain of all consolation. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: When you have made an end of tithing all the tithes of your increase the third year, which is the year of tithing, and have given it to the Levite, stranger, fatherless, and widow, that they may eat within your gates, and be filled. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: Then you shall say before the Lord, I have brought away the hallowed things out of my house, and also have given them to the Levite, stranger, fatherless, and widow, according to all your commandments, and I have not transgressed your commandments, neither have I forgotten them. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: The tithe confession is a clearing of conscience: nothing eaten in mourning, nothing put to unclean use, nothing given to the dead, so obedience is proved by what the offerer has refused to do with holy things. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: Look down from your holy habitation, from heaven, and bless your people Israel, and the land which you have given us, as you swore to our fathers, a land flowing with milk and honey; without God's blessing, what comfort can we take in the increase of the earth? — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: This day the Lord your God has commanded you to do these statutes and judgments; you shall therefore keep and do them with all your heart, and with all your soul, not carelessly or hypocritically, but with your whole heart. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: Avouching is mutual covenant language: Israel takes the Lord as its God and binds itself to walk in his ways and hearken to his voice, in the same breath as God takes Israel as his people. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: God's avouching answers Israel's: a peculiar people, kept by promise, whose distinctness is measured by keeping all his commandments, not by privilege alone. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: And to make you high above all nations which he has made, in praise, and in name, and in honour, and that you may be a holy people to the Lord your God, as he has spoken; holiness is true honour, and the only way to everlasting honour. — Matthew Henry
