Study Notes — Deuteronomy

Chapter 18

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: The priests the Levites, and all the tribe of Levi, shall have no part nor inheritance with Israel; they shall eat the offerings of the Lord made by fire, and his inheritance, care being taken that they entangle not themselves with worldly affairs nor enrich themselves. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: Therefore shall they have no inheritance among their brethren; the Lord is their inheritance, as he has said to them. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: And this shall be the priest's due from the people, from those that offer a sacrifice, whether ox or sheep: they shall give to the priest the shoulder, the two cheeks, and the maw. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: The firstfruit also of your corn, wine, and oil, and the first of the fleece of your sheep, shall you give him, so that those who benefit from religious assemblies help support those who minister in them. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: For the Lord your God has chosen him out of all your tribes, to stand to minister in the name of the Lord, him and his sons for ever. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: And if a Levite come from any of your gates out of all Israel, where he sojourned, and come with all the desire of his mind to the place which the Lord shall choose. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: Then he shall minister in the name of the Lord his God, as all his brethren the Levites do, who stand there before the Lord. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: They shall have like portions to eat, beside that which comes of the sale of his patrimony, the visiting Levite sharing equally with the resident ministers. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: When you come into the land which the Lord gives you, you shall not learn to do after the abominations of those nations. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: There shall not be found among you anyone who makes his son or daughter to pass through the fire, or who uses divination, or is an observer of times, or an enchanter, or a witch. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: Or a charmer, or a consulter with familiar spirits, or a wizard, or a necromancer; all reckoning of lucky days, charms, amulets, spells, and fortune-telling are here forbidden. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: For all that do these things are an abomination to the Lord, and because of these abominations the Lord drives them out from before you; such practices were a chief cause of the Canaanites being rooted out. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: You shall be perfect with the Lord your God, whole-hearted and upright before him, without resort to forbidden arts. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: For these nations, which you shall possess, hearkened to observers of times and diviners, but as for you, the Lord your God has not suffered you so to do. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: The Lord your God will raise up to you a Prophet from the midst of you, of your brethren, like unto me; to him you shall hearken; Henry reads this as Christ, the great Prophet above all prophets, by whom God makes himself known more fully than ever before. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: According to all that you desired of the Lord in Horeb in the day of the assembly, saying, Let me not hear again the voice of the Lord, nor see this great fire any more, that I die not. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: And the Lord said to Moses, They have well spoken that which they have spoken, approving their request for a mediator. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: I will raise them up a Prophet from among their brethren, like unto you, and will put my words in his mouth, and he shall speak to them all that I shall command him; he should be one of their nation, like Moses yet above him. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: And it shall come to pass, that whoever will not hearken to my words which he shall speak in my name, I will require it of him; whoever refuses to listen to Christ does so at his peril, for the Prophet is also to be his Judge. — Matthew Henry

20 COMMENTARY: But the prophet who shall presume to speak a word in my name, which I have not commanded him to speak, or who shall speak in the name of other gods, even that prophet shall die. — Matthew Henry

21 COMMENTARY: And if you say in your heart, How shall we know the word which the Lord has not spoken? the touchstone is given in the next verse. — Matthew Henry

22 COMMENTARY: When a prophet speaks in the name of the Lord, if the thing follows not, nor comes to pass, that is the thing which the Lord has not spoken, but the prophet has spoken presumptuously; you shall not be afraid of him; whatever is against the plain sense of the written word, or encourages sin, is not from the Lord. — Matthew Henry
