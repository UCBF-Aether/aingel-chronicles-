Study Notes — Deuteronomy

Chapter 15

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: At the end of every seven years you shall make a release, the sabbatical year of debts. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: And this is the manner of the release: every creditor who lends anything to his neighbour shall release it, and shall not exact it of his neighbour or brother, because it is called the Lord's release. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: Of a foreigner you may exact it again, but what is yours with your brother your hand shall release, the release being a duty within the covenant community. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: Save when there shall be no poor among you, for the Lord shall greatly bless you in the land he gives you for an inheritance to possess it. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: Only if you carefully hearken to the voice of the Lord, to observe to do all these commandments commanded you this day, the full blessing being conditioned on full obedience. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: For the Lord your God blesses you, as he promised you, and you shall lend to many nations, but shall not borrow, and you shall reign over many nations, but they shall not reign over you. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: If there is among you a poor man of one of your brethren within any of your gates in your land which the Lord gives you, you shall not harden your heart, nor shut your hand from your poor brother. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: But you shall open your hand wide to him, and shall surely lend him sufficient for his need, in that which he wants. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: Beware that there be not a thought in your wicked heart, saying, The seventh year, the year of release, is at hand, and your eye be evil against your poor brother, and you give him nothing, and he cry to the Lord against you, and it be sin to you; the law lays restraint on the very thoughts of the heart. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: You shall surely give him, and your heart shall not be grieved when you give to him, because for this thing the Lord will bless you in all your works, and in all that you put your hand to; God loves a cheerful giver. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: For the poor shall never cease out of the land, therefore I command you, saying, You shall open your hand wide to your brother, to your poor, and to your needy, in your land. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: And if your brother, a Hebrew man or Hebrew woman, be sold to you, and serve you six years, then in the seventh year you shall let him go free from you. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: And when you send him out free from you, you shall not let him go away empty. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: You shall furnish him liberally out of your flock, floor, and winepress, of that with which the Lord has blessed you you shall give to him; masters were to put a small stock into a freed servant's hands to set up with. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: And you shall remember that you were a bondman in Egypt, and the Lord redeemed you, therefore I command you this thing today. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: And it shall be, if he says to you, I will not go away from you, because he loves you and your house, because he is well with you. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: Then you shall take an awl, thrust it through his ear to the door, and he shall be your servant for ever; and also to your maidservant you shall do likewise. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: It shall not seem hard to you when you send him away free, for he has been worth a double hired servant to you in serving six years, and the Lord will bless you in all that you do. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: All the firstling males that come of your herd and flock you shall sanctify to the Lord your God; you shall do no work with the firstling of your bullock, nor shear the firstling of your sheep. — Matthew Henry

20 COMMENTARY: You shall eat it before the Lord your God year by year in the place which the Lord shall choose, you and your household, devoting the first of your strength to God. — Matthew Henry

21 COMMENTARY: And if there is any blemish in it, as if it is lame or blind or has any ill blemish, you shall not sacrifice it to the Lord your God, for God is to have the best, not the defective. — Matthew Henry

22 COMMENTARY: You shall eat it within your gates; the unclean and the clean person shall eat it alike, as the roebuck and the hart. — Matthew Henry

23 COMMENTARY: Only you shall not eat the blood of it; you shall pour it upon the ground as water, keeping the life-sign sacred even in the common meal. — Matthew Henry
