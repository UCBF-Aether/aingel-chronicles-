Study Notes — Deuteronomy

Chapter 9

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Hear, O Israel: you are to pass over Jordan this day, to go in to possess nations greater and mightier than yourself, cities great and fenced up to heaven. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: A people great and tall, the children of the Anakim, whom you know and of whom you have heard, Who can stand before the children of Anak? Their strength was meant to drive Israel to God, not to despair. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: Understand therefore this day that the Lord your God goes over before you as a consuming fire; he shall destroy them and bring them down before your face, so you shall drive them out and destroy them quickly, as the Lord has said. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: Speak not in your heart, after the Lord has cast them out, saying, For my righteousness the Lord has brought me in to possess this land; but for the wickedness of these nations the Lord drives them out before you. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: Not for your righteousness or the uprightness of your heart do you go to possess their land, but for the wickedness of these nations the Lord drives them out, and that he may perform the word sworn to Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: Understand therefore that the Lord gives you not this good land to possess for your righteousness, for you are a stiffnecked people; boasting is for ever done away, since none whom God accepts are accepted for their own righteousness. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: Remember, and forget not, how you provoked the Lord to wrath in the wilderness, from the day you departed from Egypt until you came to this place, you have been rebellious against the Lord. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: Also in Horeb you provoked the Lord to wrath, so that he was angry with you to have destroyed you, at the very mount where the law was given. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: When Moses went up into the mount to receive the tables of stone, the tables of the covenant, he abode in the mount forty days and forty nights, eating no bread and drinking no water. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: And the Lord delivered to him two tables of stone written with the finger of God, on which were written all the words the Lord had spoken to them in the mount out of the midst of the fire, in the day of the assembly. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: And at the end of forty days and forty nights, the Lord gave Moses the two tables of stone, the tables of the covenant. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: And the Lord said to Moses, Arise, get down quickly from here, for your people whom you brought out of Egypt have corrupted themselves, turning quickly out of the way commanded them, and making a molten image. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: Furthermore the Lord said, I have seen this people, and behold, it is a stiffnecked people. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: Let me alone, that I may destroy them and blot out their name from under heaven, and I will make of you a nation mightier and greater than they. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: So Moses turned and came down from the mount, the mount burning with fire, and the two tables of the covenant in his two hands. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: And he looked, and behold, they had sinned against the Lord, making a molten calf, and had turned aside quickly out of the way commanded them. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: And Moses took the two tables, cast them out of his two hands, and broke them before their eyes, a sign that the covenant was broken by their idolatry. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: And he fell down before the Lord, as at the first, forty days and forty nights, eating no bread and drinking no water, because of all their sins in doing wickedly in the Lord's sight, to provoke him to anger. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: For Moses was afraid of the anger and hot displeasure with which the Lord was wroth against them to destroy them, but the Lord hearkened to him at that time also. — Matthew Henry

20 COMMENTARY: And the Lord was very angry with Aaron to have destroyed him, and Moses prayed for Aaron also at the same time. — Matthew Henry

21 COMMENTARY: And Moses took their sin, the calf they had made, burnt it with fire, stamped it, ground it very small, and cast its dust into the brook that descended out of the mount. — Matthew Henry

22 COMMENTARY: And at Taberah, and at Massah, and at Kibroth-hattaavah, they provoked the Lord to wrath, a catalogue of rebellions meant to strip away any pretence of merit. — Matthew Henry

23 COMMENTARY: Likewise when the Lord sent them from Kadesh-barnea, saying, Go up and possess the land given you, they rebelled against the commandment of the Lord and believed him not, nor hearkened to his voice. — Matthew Henry

24 COMMENTARY: You have been rebellious against the Lord from the day that Moses knew you; remembering former sins with sorrow shows how much we are indebted to free grace. — Matthew Henry

25 COMMENTARY: Thus Moses fell down before the Lord forty days and forty nights, as at the first, because the Lord had said he would destroy them. — Matthew Henry

26 COMMENTARY: He prayed therefore to the Lord, saying, O Lord God, destroy not your people and your inheritance, whom you have redeemed through your greatness and brought out of Egypt with a mighty hand. — Matthew Henry

27 COMMENTARY: Remember your servants Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob; look not to the stubbornness of this people, nor to their wickedness, nor to their sin. — Matthew Henry

28 COMMENTARY: Lest the land from which you brought us out say, Because the Lord was not able to bring them into the land he promised them, and because he hated them, he has brought them out to slay them in the wilderness. — Matthew Henry

29 COMMENTARY: Yet they are your people and your inheritance, which you brought out by your mighty power and stretched-out arm; Moses pleads God's own name and covenant, not Israel's worth. — Matthew Henry
