Study Notes — Deuteronomy

Chapter 29

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: These are the words of the covenant which the Lord commanded Moses to make with the children of Israel in the land of Moab, beside the covenant he made with them in Horeb; the discourse continues, its subject being Israel's covenant, its privileges and its obligations, renewed now in different circumstances after they had violated its conditions. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: And Moses called all Israel, and said, You have seen all that the Lord did before your eyes in Egypt to Pharaoh, his servants, and all his land; the appeal was to those who had been very young at the Exodus and remembered the marvellous transactions that preceded and followed it. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: The temptations, signs and miracles are recalled as seen things: Moses appeals to eyes that watched Egypt and the wilderness, so the covenant rests on witnessed wonders, not on report. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: Yet the Lord has not given you a heart to perceive, and eyes to see, and ears to hear, unto this day; those wonderful events made no good impression on them, for they were strangers to the grace of wisdom given liberally to all who ask. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: And I have led you forty years in the wilderness; your clothes are not waxen old upon you, and your shoe is not waxen old upon your foot, miracles continued without interruption, proclaiming the immediate hand of God and training them to habitual confidence in him. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: You have not eaten bread, neither have you drunk wine or strong drink, that you might know that I am the Lord your God; the daily manna and water were themselves the training. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: The victories over Sihon and Og are the recent proof before the renewal: Israel has already seen two kings fall, so the covenant is made by a people who know God's power in battle, not only in Egypt. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: And you took their land, and gave it for an inheritance to the Reubenites, Gadites, and half-tribe of Manasseh, brilliant successes won with little exertion or loss on Israel's part. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: Keep therefore the words of this covenant, and do them, that you may prosper in all that you do; faithful adherence to the covenant is the direct and sure means of obtaining its promised blessings. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: The whole assembly stands: captains, elders, officers and all men together, so the renewal binds the nation as one body, with no rank standing apart from the oath. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: Your little ones, your wives, and your stranger in your camp, from the hewer of your wood to the drawer of your water; the whole congregation, of all ages and conditions, native and naturalized stranger alike, was assembled to renew the Sinaitic covenant, none exempt from its terms. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: Entering the covenant means entering its oath: the bond is sworn, not merely agreed, and the oath is what makes breaking it a matter of curses, not of changed minds. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: Establishment runs both ways: Israel becomes a people to God, and God becomes their God, on the ground of what he said and swore to Abraham, Isaac and Jacob, for the Moab covenant renews the patriarchal oath. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: But with him who stands here with us this day before the Lord, and also with him who is not here with us this day, the covenant binding future generations as well as the present assembly. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: Knowledge is firsthand: Israel dwelt in Egypt and passed through the nations, so the idolatries warned against are not hearsay but sights the people themselves walked past. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: The idols are catalogued by material, wood, stone, silver, gold, to show their worthlessness: gods made of the same stuff as furniture and coin cannot keep a covenant. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

18 COMMENTARY: Lest there should be among you man, woman, family, or tribe, whose heart turns away this day from the Lord our God, to go and serve the gods of these nations, lest there should be among you a root that bears gall and wormwood; any lapsing into idolatry might prove a root of bitterness, spreading its corrupt influence all around. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

19 COMMENTARY: And it comes to pass, when he hears the words of this curse, that he blesses himself in his heart, saying, I shall have peace, though I walk in the imagination of my heart, to add drunkenness to thirst; some might delude themselves that taking the oath would secure the blessings even while following their own heart's devices. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

20 COMMENTARY: The Lord will not spare him, but then the anger of the Lord and his jealousy shall smoke against that man, and all the curses written in this book shall lie upon him, and the Lord shall blot out his name from under heaven; the covenant of grace has curses in reserve for transgressors, as certain, lasting, and severe as its blessings. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

21 COMMENTARY: Separation to evil is the reverse of election: the self-blessing man is cut out from all the tribes by the very curses of the covenant he swore, singled out for judgment as Israel was singled out for blessing. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

22 COMMENTARY: The warning is written for onlookers: a later generation and a stranger from far off will see the plagues and sicknesses and ask, so desolation itself becomes a witness that preaches the covenant. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

23 COMMENTARY: The land's ruin is pictured as Sodom's overthrow: brimstone, salt and burning leave nothing sown and no grass growing, so apostasy ends in a country as sterile as the cities of the plain. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

24 COMMENTARY: Even all nations ask the question: why this land, why this heat of anger, and the question itself forces the answer onto Israel's forsaken covenant rather than onto chance. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

25 COMMENTARY: The answer the nations receive names the cause plainly: forsaking the covenant made at the Exodus, not bad fortune, is why the land lies under anger. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

26 COMMENTARY: The gods served were unknown and ungiven: Israel traded a God who had revealed himself and given himself to them for deities with no history and no gift behind them. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

27 COMMENTARY: Anger is not formless: it brings upon the land exactly the curses written in this book, so judgment follows the text Israel swore rather than inventing new penalties. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

28 COMMENTARY: And the Lord rooted them out of their land in anger, wrath, and great indignation, and cast them into another land, as it is this day; travellers in later ages, seeing Palestine desolate, would read in its ruined cities the execution of this threatening. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

29 COMMENTARY: The secret things belong to the Lord our God, but those things which are revealed belong to us and to our children for ever, that we may do all the words of this law; this verse answers astonishment at whether Israel could ever be so wicked, for God's providence combines goodness and severity in mystery too profound for our capacities, yet the revealed part is enough for obedience. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

No note for verse(s) 14 — the public-domain sources consulted give no distinct explanation for these verses beyond the list itself, so nothing was invented for them.
