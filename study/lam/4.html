Study Notes — Lamentations

Chapter 4

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: How is the gold become dim, how is the most fine gold changed, the sanctuary stones poured out at the top of every street, sin tarnishing the beauty of the most exalted powers, though the gold Christ bestows can never be taken away. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: The precious sons of Zion, comparable to fine gold, how are they esteemed as earthen pitchers, the work of a potter's hands, former glory now counted cheap. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: Even sea monsters draw out the breast and suckle their young, but the daughter of God's people has become cruel, like ostriches in the wilderness, maternal care itself failing under siege. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: The sucking child's tongue cleaves to the roof of his mouth for thirst, young children ask bread and no man breaks it to them, famine striking the youngest first. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: Those who fed delicately are desolate in the streets, and those brought up in scarlet embrace dunghills, luxury reversed into destitution. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: For the punishment of the daughter of God's people's iniquity is greater than Sodom's sin, which was overthrown in a moment without hands staying on her, Jerusalem's prolonged ruin being the heavier judgment. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: Her Nazarites were purer than snow, whiter than milk, more ruddy than rubies, their polishing of sapphire, consecrated beauty once conspicuous. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: Now their visage is blacker than coal, unknown in the streets, skin cleaving to bones, withered like wood, the change from splendor to starvation complete. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: Those slain by the sword are better than those slain by hunger, for the hungry pine away, stricken through for want of the fruits of the field, a slow death worse than a swift one. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: The hands of pitiful women have sodden their own children, who were their meat in the destruction of the daughter of God's people, the horrors of the siege reaching their most unnatural depth. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: The Lord has accomplished his fury, poured out fierce anger, and kindled a fire in Zion that devoured its foundations, judgment carried through to its determined end. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: The kings of the earth and all the world's inhabitants would not have believed adversary and enemy could enter Jerusalem's gates, the city's fall surprising even outsiders. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: It came for the sins of her prophets and the iniquities of her priests, who shed the blood of the just in her midst, nothing ripening a people for ruin faster than the sins of priests and prophets. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: They have wandered as blind men in the streets, polluted with blood so men could not touch their garments, guilt making them untouchable. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: Men cried to them, depart, it is unclean, depart, depart, touch not, and when they fled and wandered, nations said they shall no more sojourn there, the defiled leaders driven out as lepers. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: The anger of the Lord has divided them, and he will no more regard them, since they respected not priests and favored not elders, divine vengeance pursuing even the king. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: As for the people, their eyes failed for vain help, watching for a nation that could not save them, trust in Egypt or any ally proving empty. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: Their steps were hunted so they could not go in the streets, their end near, their days fulfilled, for their end had come, no street safe from pursuers. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: Their persecutors were swifter than eagles of heaven, pursuing on mountains and laying wait in the wilderness, escape cut off on every side. — Matthew Henry

20 COMMENTARY: The breath of their nostrils, the Lord's anointed, was taken in their pits, he of whom they said, under his shadow we shall live among the heathen, yet only the true anointed King is the life of souls, under whose shadow believers may safely live. — Matthew Henry

21 COMMENTARY: Rejoice and be glad, daughter of Edom dwelling in Uz, for the cup shall also pass to thee, thou shalt be drunken and make thyself naked, Edom's triumph being temporary and her turn coming. — Matthew Henry

22 COMMENTARY: The punishment of Zion's iniquity is accomplished, and she shall no more be carried into captivity, while God will visit Edom's iniquity and discover her sins, an end put to Zion's troubles, not the full punishment deserved but what God determined, and Edom standing for all the church's enemies whose doom approaches. — Matthew Henry
