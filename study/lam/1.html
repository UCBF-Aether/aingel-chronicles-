Study Notes — Lamentations

Chapter 1

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: How does the city sit solitary that was full of people, become as a widow, she who was great among nations and princess among provinces now tributary, since Jerusalem became captive and slave by reason of the greatness of her sins. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: She weeps sore in the night with tears on her cheeks, and among all her lovers none comforts her, her friends dealing treacherously, showing the miseries of a nation that let sin, its greatest adversary, have dominion. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: Judah has gone into captivity because of affliction and great servitude, dwelling among the heathen and finding no rest, her persecutors overtaking her in straits. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: The ways of Zion mourn because none come to the solemn feasts, her gates desolate, priests sighing, virgins afflicted, and she in bitterness, the people enduring famine and distress. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: Her adversaries are chief and her enemies prosper, for the Lord has afflicted her for the multitude of her transgressions, her children gone into captivity before the enemy. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: From the daughter of Zion all her beauty has departed, her princes like harts finding no pasture, gone without strength before the pursuer, since sin brings enemies to dominion. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: Jerusalem remembers in affliction all her pleasant things of old, when her people fell into the enemy's hand and none helped, memory sharpening present misery. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: Jerusalem has grievously sinned, therefore she is removed, and all who honored her despise her, having seen her nakedness, so that she sighs and turns backward. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: Her filthiness is in her skirts, she remembered not her last end, therefore she came down wonderfully, with no comforter, and cries to the Lord to behold her affliction, since acknowledging sin and entreating the Lord is the only way to be easy under burdens. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: The adversary has spread his hand over all her pleasant things, and she has seen heathen enter the sanctuary, those whom God commanded not to enter the congregation, the sanctuary itself violated. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: All her people sigh and seek bread, giving pleasant things for food to relieve the soul, and the city asks the Lord to see and consider, for she has become vile. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: Is it nothing to you, all who pass by, to behold if any sorrow is like hers, with which the Lord has afflicted her in fierce anger, Jerusalem sitting dejected calling on passersby to consider whether her example concerns them. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: From above God has sent fire into her bones, prevailing against them, spread a net for her feet, turned her back, and made her desolate all the day, outward suffering great but inward suffering harder through the sense of guilt. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: The yoke of her transgressions is bound by God's hand, wreathed and come up upon her neck, making her strength fall, the Lord delivering her into hands from which she cannot rise. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: The Lord has trodden under foot all her mighty men, called an assembly against her to crush her young men, and trodden the virgin daughter of Judah as in a winepress. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: For these things the prophet weeps, his eye running down with water, because the comforter who should relieve his soul is far from him, and his children are desolate because the enemy prevailed. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: Zion spreads forth her hands, and there is none to comfort her, the Lord having commanded concerning Jacob that adversaries should be round about him, and Jerusalem becoming as an unclean thing among them. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: The Lord is righteous, for she has rebelled against his commandment, and she calls all people to hear and behold her sorrow, her virgins and young men gone into captivity, confessing God's justice rather than charging him with wrong. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: She called for her lovers, but they deceived her, and her priests and elders gave up the ghost in the city while seeking food to relieve their souls, former allies proving treacherous. — Matthew Henry

20 COMMENTARY: Behold, O Lord, for she is in distress, her bowels troubled, her heart turned within her, for she has grievously rebelled, with the sword bereaving abroad and death at home, sorrow for sin being great sorrow that must affect the soul. — Matthew Henry

21 COMMENTARY: They have heard that she sighs, and none comforts her, all her enemies hearing her trouble and glad that God has done it, yet she trusts he will bring the day he has called and they shall be like her. — Matthew Henry

22 COMMENTARY: Let all their wickedness come before God, and do to them as he has done to her for all her transgressions, for her sighs are many and her heart faint, a prayer that leaves vengeance to God while sorrows lead onward to Christ's cross, where he too asks, is it nothing to you, all who pass by. — Matthew Henry
