Study Notes — Lamentations

Chapter 5

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Remember, O Lord, what has come upon us, consider and behold our reproach, the afflicted pouring out complaint to God, not of evils feared but of evils felt, and finding ease at the throne of grace. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: Their inheritance is turned to strangers and their houses to aliens, the land and homes of God's people in foreign hands. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: They are orphans and fatherless, their mothers as widows, the nation broken at the level of the family. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: They drink their own water for money and their wood is sold to them, paying for what was once their own. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: Their necks are under persecution, they labor and have no rest, bondage without respite. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: They have given their hand to Egyptians and Assyrians to be satisfied with bread, submitting to former enemies for mere food. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: Their fathers sinned and are no more, and they have borne their iniquities, penitent and patient under what they suffer for their fathers' sins, hoping he who punishes will return in mercy. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: Servants have ruled over them, and none delivers them out of their hand, the lowest ruling the once-princely people. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: They get their bread with peril of their lives because of the sword of the wilderness, every loaf fetched at risk of death. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: Their skin is black like an oven because of the terrible famine, hunger scorching them from within. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: Enemies ravished the women in Zion and the maids in Judah's cities, violation added to conquest. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: Princes are hanged up by their hand, and elders' faces were not honored, rank and age alike dishonored. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: Young men were taken to grind, and children fell under loads of wood, strength spent on servile labor. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: Elders have ceased from the gate, and young men from their music, public counsel and public joy both silenced. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: The joy of their heart has ceased, their dance turned into mourning, gladness itself extinguished. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: The crown has fallen from their head, and they cry, woe unto us, for we have sinned, acknowledging that all woes are owing to their own sin and folly. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: For this their heart is faint, and for these things their eyes are dim, grief weakening heart and sight alike. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: Because Mount Zion is desolate, foxes walk upon it, the temple mount itself overrun by wild creatures, a ruin deeper than any other calamity to God's people. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: Thou, O Lord, remainest forever, thy throne from generation to generation, whatever changes on earth, God remaining wise, holy, just, and good, without variableness or shadow of turning. — Matthew Henry

20 COMMENTARY: Wherefore dost thou forget them forever and forsake them so long, the prayer honestly voicing the fear of abandonment. — Matthew Henry

21 COMMENTARY: Turn thou them unto thee, O Lord, and they shall be turned, renew their days as of old, since God never leaves any till they first leave him, and if he turns them in duty he will quickly return in mercy. — Matthew Henry

22 COMMENTARY: But thou hast utterly rejected us, thou art very wroth against us, the prayer ending on the edge of despair, yet still addressed to God, trusting in the end it shall be well with all who trust, fear, love, and serve the Lord, and watching against repining and despondency. — Matthew Henry
