Study Notes — Lamentations

Chapter 3

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: The prophet says, I am the man who has seen affliction by the rod of God's wrath, relating the gloomy and discouraging part of his experience and how he found support and relief. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: God has led him and brought him into darkness, but not into light, the trial making the Lord seem terrible to him. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: Surely against him God is turned, turning his hand against him all the day, affliction unrelenting. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: God has made his flesh and skin old and broken his bones, bodily wasting under prolonged sorrow. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: God has built against him and compassed him with gall and travail, besieged by bitterness on every side. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: God has set him in dark places, as those dead of old, shut away from light and hope as in a grave. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: God has hedged him about so he cannot get out and made his chain heavy, escape itself barred. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: Also when he cries and shouts, God shuts out his prayer, the darkest part of the trial, when heaven seems closed. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: God has enclosed his ways with hewn stone and made his paths crooked, every road forward blocked or twisted. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: God was to him as a bear lying in wait and a lion in secret places, danger felt as a predator's ambush. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: God has turned aside his ways, pulled him in pieces, and made him desolate, his course broken apart. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: God has bent his bow and set him as a mark for the arrow, singled out for judgment. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: God has caused the arrows of his quiver to enter into his reins, wounds striking at the seat of feeling. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: He was a derision to all his people and their song all the day, mocked by those he warned. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: God has filled him with bitterness and made him drunken with wormwood, affliction becoming his daily portion. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: God has also broken his teeth with gravel stones and covered him with ashes, fed on grit and humiliation. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: God has removed his soul far off from peace, and he forgot prosperity, peace itself becoming a memory. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: And he said, my strength and my hope is perished from the Lord, the weakest believer being wrong, Henry notes, to think his strength and hope have perished from the Lord, since the struggle between unbelief and faith is often severe. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: Remembering his affliction and misery, the wormwood and the gall, keeps the bitterness freshly in mind. — Matthew Henry

20 COMMENTARY: His soul still remembers them and is humbled in him, remembrance producing humility rather than hardness. — Matthew Henry

21 COMMENTARY: This he recalls to mind, therefore he has hope, the turn from gloom to faith beginning with deliberate remembrance of God's character. — Matthew Henry

22 COMMENTARY: It is of the Lord's mercies that they are not consumed, because his compassions fail not, bad as things are, it is owing to mercy that they are not worse. — Matthew Henry

23 COMMENTARY: God's compassions are new every morning, and great is his faithfulness, fresh instances arriving daily. — Matthew Henry

24 COMMENTARY: The Lord is his portion, says his soul, therefore will he hope in him, earthly portions perishing but God being a portion forever. — Matthew Henry

25 COMMENTARY: The Lord is good to those who wait for him, to the soul that seeks him, and it is duty, comfort, and satisfaction to hope and quietly wait for his salvation. — Matthew Henry

26 COMMENTARY: It is good for a man to both hope and quietly wait for the Lord's salvation, waiting being itself part of faith. — Matthew Henry

27 COMMENTARY: It is good for a man to bear the yoke in his youth, affliction working much good, making many humble and serious and weaning them from the world. — Matthew Henry

28 COMMENTARY: He sits alone and keeps silence, because he has borne the yoke upon him, solitude and silence becoming the posture of submission. — Matthew Henry

29 COMMENTARY: He puts his mouth in the dust, if so be there may be hope, humility opening the door hope enters by. — Matthew Henry

30 COMMENTARY: He gives his cheek to him who smites him and is filled full with reproach, bearing reproach without retaliation. — Matthew Henry

31 COMMENTARY: For the Lord will not cast off forever, his anger not being his final word to his people. — Matthew Henry

32 COMMENTARY: But though he causes grief, yet he will have compassion according to the multitude of his mercies, grief and compassion both from the same hand. — Matthew Henry

33 COMMENTARY: For he does not afflict willingly nor grieve the children of men, affliction being his strange work, not his delight. — Matthew Henry

34 COMMENTARY: To crush under his feet all the prisoners of the earth is not approved by the Lord, God not delighting in oppression. — Matthew Henry

35 COMMENTARY: To turn aside the right of a man before the face of the Most High is likewise not approved, justice not being perverted by him who is just. — Matthew Henry

36 COMMENTARY: To subvert a man in his cause, the Lord approves not, since due thoughts of sin convince us it is of his mercies we are not consumed, not of his injustice. — Matthew Henry

37 COMMENTARY: Who is he that says and it comes to pass, when the Lord commands it not, no word standing without God's ordering. — Matthew Henry

38 COMMENTARY: Out of the mouth of the Most High proceeds not evil and good, both calamity and blessing coming under his one providence, so complaint must be measured. — Matthew Henry

39 COMMENTARY: Wherefore does a living man complain, a man for the punishment of his sins, since what is complained of is far less than sins deserve, and while there is life there is hope. — Matthew Henry

40 COMMENTARY: Let them search and try their own ways and turn again to the Lord, instead of reflecting on others' ways and blaming them in calamity. — Matthew Henry

41 COMMENTARY: Let them lift up their hearts with their hands to God in the heavens, hearts going with prayers, lest outward expressions without inward impressions mock God. — Matthew Henry

42 COMMENTARY: They confess, we have transgressed and rebelled, and thou hast not pardoned, looking on desolations and grieving the more the more they look. — Matthew Henry

43 COMMENTARY: God has covered with anger and persecuted them, slain and not pitied, the confession continuing without excuse. — Matthew Henry

44 COMMENTARY: God has covered himself with a cloud, that their prayer should not pass through, the sense of closed heaven returning even in confession. — Matthew Henry

45 COMMENTARY: God has made them as offscouring and refuse in the midst of the people, cast aside as worthless by the nations. — Matthew Henry

46 COMMENTARY: All their enemies have opened their mouths against them, scorn added to ruin. — Matthew Henry

47 COMMENTARY: Fear and a snare have come upon them, desolation and destruction, terror surrounding them on every side. — Matthew Henry

48 COMMENTARY: The prophet's eye runs down with rivers of water for the destruction of the daughter of his people, weeping continuing while waiting, and expecting relief from none but the Lord. — Matthew Henry

49 COMMENTARY: His eye trickles down and ceases not, without intermission, grief unbroken. — Matthew Henry

50 COMMENTARY: Till the Lord looks down and beholds from heaven, waiting directed upward until God himself intervenes. — Matthew Henry

51 COMMENTARY: His eye affects his heart because of all the daughters of his city, personal sorrow widened to the whole city's daughters. — Matthew Henry

52 COMMENTARY: His enemies chased him sore, like a bird, without cause, hunted though innocent of their charges. — Matthew Henry

53 COMMENTARY: They have cut off his life in the dungeon and cast a stone upon him, buried alive in the pit. — Matthew Henry

54 COMMENTARY: Waters flowed over his head, and he said, I am cut off, drowning imagery for despair itself. — Matthew Henry

55 COMMENTARY: He called upon the Lord's name out of the low dungeon, prayer proving the breath of the new man, drawing in mercy and returning praise. — Matthew Henry

56 COMMENTARY: God has heard his voice, not hiding his ear at his breathing, at his cry, the prayer heard even from the pit. — Matthew Henry

57 COMMENTARY: God drew near in the day he called, saying, fear not, God's grace silencing fears and quieting spirits by his Spirit's witness. — Matthew Henry

58 COMMENTARY: O Lord, thou hast pleaded the causes of his soul and redeemed his life, God himself becoming his advocate. — Matthew Henry

59 COMMENTARY: O Lord, thou hast seen his wrong, and he asks God to judge his cause, referring judgment to the one who sees. — Matthew Henry

60 COMMENTARY: God has seen all their vengeance and all their imaginations against him, nothing hidden from the Judge. — Matthew Henry

61 COMMENTARY: God has heard their reproach and all their imaginations against him, every taunt registered. — Matthew Henry

62 COMMENTARY: The lips of those risen against him and their device against him all the day are known to God, malice unceasing yet observed. — Matthew Henry

63 COMMENTARY: Behold their sitting down and rising up: he is their music, mocked in their leisure and their business alike. — Matthew Henry

64 COMMENTARY: Render to them a recompense, O Lord, according to the work of their hands, vengeance left to God, whose enemies perish with everlasting destruction while he saves believers. — Matthew Henry

65 COMMENTARY: Give them sorrow of heart, thy curse unto them, the prophet asking God to deal with persecutors as justice requires. — Matthew Henry

66 COMMENTARY: Persecute and destroy them in anger from under the heavens of the Lord, faith coming off conqueror, since God will deliver his people from every trouble and revive his church from every persecution. — Matthew Henry
