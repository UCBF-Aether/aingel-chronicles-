Study Notes — Lamentations

Chapter 2

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: How has the Lord covered the daughter of Zion with a cloud in his anger, casting down from heaven to earth the beauty of Israel and not remembering his footstool, a sad representation of God's church in which the hand of the Lord in their calamities is chiefly noticed. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: The Lord has swallowed up all Jacob's habitations without pity, throwing down Judah's strongholds and polluting kingdom and princes, gates and bars standing in no stead when God withdraws protection. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: He has cut off in fierce anger all the horn of Israel, drawn back his right hand from before the enemy, and burned against Jacob like flaming fire devouring round about, strength withdrawn because they debased themselves by sin. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: He has bent his bow like an enemy, stood with his right hand as an adversary, and slain all pleasant to the eye in Zion's tabernacle, pouring out fury like fire, yet God is not truly an enemy to his people when he corrects them in anger. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: The Lord was as an enemy, swallowing Israel and her palaces, destroying strongholds, and increasing mourning and lamentation in Judah. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: He has violently taken away his tabernacle as if it were a garden hut, destroyed places of assembly, and caused feasts and Sabbaths to be forgotten in Zion, despising king and priest in anger, since those who did not duly value ordinances justly lose their benefit. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: The Lord has cast off his altar, abhorred his sanctuary, and given the walls of her palaces into the enemy's hand, who made noise in the Lord's house as on a feast day. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: The Lord has purposed to destroy Zion's wall, stretched out a line, and not withdrawn his hand, so rampart and wall lament together, judgment measured and deliberate. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: Her gates are sunk into the ground, bars destroyed and broken, king and princes among the Gentiles, the law no more, and prophets finding no vision from the Lord, those who misused God's prophets justly losing them. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: The elders of Zion sit on the ground in silence, casting dust on their heads and girded with sackcloth, and Jerusalem's virgins hang their heads to the ground, causes for lamentation now described. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: The prophet's eyes fail with tears, his bowels troubled, his liver poured on the earth for his people's destruction, because children and sucklings swoon in the city's streets, multitudes perishing by famine. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: Children say to their mothers, where is corn and wine, swooning as wounded in the streets, their souls poured out into their mothers' bosoms, the famine's extremity made personal. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: What shall the prophet take to witness for Jerusalem, what liken to her, virgin daughter of Zion, for her breach is great like the sea, and who can heal her, no comparison sufficing for such ruin. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: Her prophets have seen vain and foolish things, not discovering her iniquity to turn away captivity, but seeing false burdens and causes of banishment, false prophets deceiving the people instead of exposing sin. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: All who pass by clap hands, hiss, and wag their heads at Jerusalem, asking if this is the city called the perfection of beauty and joy of the whole earth, neighbors laughing at her misery, a great sin that adds affliction to the afflicted. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: All her enemies have opened their mouths, hissing and gnashing teeth, saying they have swallowed her up, this is the day they looked for and have found, enemies mistaking the church's shocks for its ruin, in which they will be deceived. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: The Lord has done what he devised, fulfilling his word commanded in days of old, throwing down without pity and causing the enemy to rejoice and setting up adversaries' horn, God's threatened word proving true. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: Their heart cried to the Lord, O wall of Zion, let tears run down like a river day and night, give no rest, let not the apple of the eye cease, a call to lamentation answered by prayer. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: Arise, cry out in the night, at the beginning of the watches pour out the heart like water before the Lord, lifting hands for the life of young children fainting from hunger at every street corner, since prayer is a salve for every sore. — Matthew Henry

20 COMMENTARY: Behold, O Lord, and consider to whom thou hast done this, shall women eat their fruit, children of a span long, shall priest and prophet be slain in the Lord's sanctuary, the prophet referring the case to the Lord and leaving it with him. — Matthew Henry

21 COMMENTARY: Young and old lie on the ground in the streets, virgins and young men fallen by the sword, slain in the day of anger, killed and not pitied, multitudes falling by the sword as well as by famine. — Matthew Henry

22 COMMENTARY: God has called as on a solemn day her terrors round about, so that in the day of the Lord's anger none escaped or remained, and those she bore and brought up her enemy has consumed, the lament ending where it must, with fear of God, humble walking, and heed lest we fall. — Matthew Henry
