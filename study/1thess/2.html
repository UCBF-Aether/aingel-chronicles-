Study Notes — 1 Thessalonians

Chapter 2

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: You yourselves, brethren, know our entrance in to you was not in vain. It is a great comfort to a minister, Henry says, to have his own conscience and others' consciences witness that he set out well, with good designs and principles, and that his preaching was not fruitless or deceitful. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: Even after suffering before and being shamefully entreated at Philippi, we were bold in our God to speak to you the gospel of God with much contention. Suffering in a good cause, Henry notes, should sharpen rather than blunt holy resolution, and Paul, put in the stocks at Philippi, preached at Thessalonica with as much boldness as ever. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: Our exhortation was not of deceit, nor of uncleanness, nor in guile. The gospel preached was true and faithful, not a cunningly devised fable, pure and holy, tending to discountenance all impurity, and spoken without pretending one thing and intending another. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: As we were allowed of God to be put in trust with the gospel, even so we speak, not as pleasing men, but God, who tries our hearts. Ministers are stewards put in trust, Henry says, who must not corrupt the word and shall be called to account; and God, who searches hearts and knows our aims, is the judge from whom reward comes. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: Neither at any time used we flattering words, as you know, nor a cloak of covetousness; God is witness. Paul avoided flattery, never fawning or promising life to those living as they listed, and never made the ministry a cloak for covetousness, unlike false apostles who made merchandise of the people. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: Nor of men sought we glory, neither of you nor of others, when we might have been burdensome as apostles of Christ. They expected neither people's purses nor their caps, Henry observes, neither enrichment nor adoration, though as apostles they might have demanded maintenance. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: But we were gentle among you, even as a nurse cherishes her children. He showed mildness who might have acted with apostolic authority, Henry says, accommodating himself to all capacities, bearing with frowardness as a nursing mother does, for this is the way to win people rather than rule with rigour. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: Being affectionately desirous of you, we were willing to impart to you not the gospel of God only, but also our own souls, because you were dear to us. He sought them, not theirs, Henry notes, and was willing to spend and be spent, venturing his very life in preaching. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: You remember, brethren, our labour and travail, labouring night and day because we would not be chargeable to any of you, while we preached the gospel of God. To the ministry he added tent-making, foregoing rest at night to have opportunity to do good to souls by day, that his ministry might not be expensive to them. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: You are witnesses, and God also, how holily and justly and unblameably we behaved ourselves among you that believe. Their behaviour was holy toward God, just toward all men, and without scandal, Henry says, so that preaching and living were all of a piece. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: As you know how we exhorted and comforted and charged every one of you, as a father does his children. Paul exhorted, excited, and quickened them by proper motives, comforted them under difficulties, and charged them personally, as a father charges his children with affection rather than authority. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: That you would walk worthy of God, who has called you to his kingdom and glory. The great privilege is the call into the kingdom of grace here and glory hereafter, Henry observes, and the great duty is a temper and life answerable to that high and holy calling. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: For this cause we thank God without ceasing, because when you received the word of God which you heard of us, you received it not as the word of men, but as it is in truth, the word of God, which effectually works also in you that believe. The word is preached by men in earthen vessels, Henry notes, yet it is in truth God's word, and those who receive it by faith find it converting souls, enlightening minds, and rejoicing hearts. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: You, brethren, became followers of the churches of God in Judaea in Christ Jesus, for you also suffered like things of your own countrymen, even as they have of the Jews. The cross is the Christian's mark, Henry says, and it is a good effect of the gospel when we are enabled to suffer for its sake with like courage and constancy. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: Who both killed the Lord Jesus and their own prophets, and have persecuted us; they please not God and are contrary to all men. The unbelieving Jews, Henry says, killed the Lord, killed their prophets, persecuted the apostles from place to place, and lost all sense of religion, thinking they did God service by killing his servants. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: Forbidding us to speak to the Gentiles that they might be saved, to fill up their sins always, for the wrath is come upon them to the uttermost. Envious that Gentiles should share salvation, they opposed the gospel and hindered precious souls, Henry notes, and when any people's iniquity is full, wrath comes to the uttermost, as Jerusalem's destruction soon showed. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: But we, brethren, being taken from you for a short time in presence, not in heart, endeavoured the more abundantly to see your face with great desire. He was unwillingly sent away by night to Berea, Henry recalls, yet absent in body he was present in heart, and his absence was but for a short time, the time of an hour. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: Wherefore we would have come to you, even I Paul, once and again, but Satan hindered us. Men of business are not masters of their own time, Henry observes, and Satan, the constant enemy of God's work, stirred up opposition or contentions that made Paul's presence necessary elsewhere. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: For what is our hope, or joy, or crown of rejoicing? Are not even you in the presence of our Lord Jesus Christ at his coming? Faithful people will be the glory and joy of faithful ministers in that great day, when ministers and people must all appear before Christ. — Matthew Henry

20 COMMENTARY: For you are our glory and joy. These are expressions of great and endeared affection, Henry says, and it is happy when ministers and people have such mutual affection, especially if those who sow and those who reap rejoice together at Christ's coming. — Matthew Henry
