Study Notes — 1 Thessalonians

Chapter 5

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: But of the times and the seasons, brethren, you have no need that I write to you. It is needless to ask about the particular time of Christ's coming, Henry says, for Christ did not reveal it to the apostles; there are times and seasons for us to work in, and those we must know and observe, but the time of giving up our account we know not, nor need we. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

2 COMMENTARY: For you yourselves know perfectly that the day of the Lord so comes as a thief in the night. Christ's coming will be a great surprise to men, Henry notes, and as the hour of death is to each person, so the judgment will be to mankind in general. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

3 COMMENTARY: For when they shall say, Peace and safety, then sudden destruction comes upon them, as travail upon a woman with child, and they shall not escape. Christ's coming will be terrible to the ungodly, Henry says, overtaking them while they dream of happiness and please themselves with vain amusements, with no means to escape its terror or punishment. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

4 COMMENTARY: But you, brethren, are not in darkness, that that day should overtake you as a thief. They have fair warning and sufficient helps to provide against that day, and may hope to stand with comfort before the Son of man, to whom the day will be a refreshing, not a surprise. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

5 COMMENTARY: You are all the children of light and the children of the day; we are not of the night nor of darkness. They are no longer under heathen darkness or the law's shadows, Henry notes, but under the gospel, which brings life and immortality to light, with the day-star risen upon them. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

6 COMMENTARY: Therefore let us not sleep, as do others, but let us watch and be sober. Most of mankind do not consider another world at all because they are asleep, Henry says, or do not consider it aright because they sleep and dream; watchfulness and sobriety mutually befriend one another, for without guard we shall not keep sober, and without sobriety we shall not long watch. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

7 COMMENTARY: For they that sleep sleep in the night, and they that are drunken are drunken in the night. Sleep and drunkenness belong to the night, and those of the day must not borrow the night's habits. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

8 COMMENTARY: But let us, who are of the day, be sober, putting on the breastplate of faith and love, and for a helmet, the hope of salvation. We need the spiritual armour, Henry says, the three Christian graces: faith, believing God's eye is always upon us and another world is to be prepared for; love, keeping us watchful and sober; and hope, guarding our trust in the Lord. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

9 COMMENTARY: For God has not appointed us to wrath, but to obtain salvation by our Lord Jesus Christ. We have ground for unshaken hope, Henry notes, when we consider that salvation is by Christ, who died for us to atone for our sins and ransom our souls. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

10 COMMENTARY: Who died for us, that, whether we wake or sleep, we should live together with him. We should join in prayer and praise, Henry says, and set a good example one before another, learning how to live to him with whom we hope to live for ever. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

11 COMMENTARY: Wherefore comfort yourselves together and edify one another, even as also you do. Mutual comfort and building up is the use of these truths, and the Thessalonians already practise it. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

12 COMMENTARY: And we beseech you, brethren, to know them which labour among you, and are over you in the Lord, and admonish you. Ministers are described by their work, Henry says, which is to serve and honour the Lord, giving good counsel, warning of dangers, and reproving what is amiss. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

13 COMMENTARY: And to esteem them very highly in love for their work's sake, and be at peace among yourselves. The people should honour and love their ministers because their business is the welfare of souls, Henry notes, and be at peace among themselves, guarding against differences, though love of peace must not make us wink at sin. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

14 COMMENTARY: Now we exhort you, brethren, warn them that are unruly, comfort the feebleminded, support the weak, be patient toward all men. The fearful and sorrowful should be encouraged, Henry says, for a kind word may do much good, and we must bear and forbear, keeping down anger toward all. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

15 COMMENTARY: See that none render evil for evil to any man, but ever follow that which is good, both among yourselves and to all men. Whatever man does to us, Henry observes, we must do good to others. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

16 COMMENTARY: Rejoice evermore. We are to rejoice in creature comforts as if we rejoiced not, Henry says, but if we rejoice in God, we may do that evermore, for a truly religious life is a life of constant joy. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

17 COMMENTARY: Pray without ceasing. Prayer helps forward all lawful business and every good work, Henry notes, and we should rejoice more if we prayed more. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

18 COMMENTARY: In everything give thanks, for this is the will of God in Christ Jesus concerning you. If we pray without ceasing, Henry says, we shall not want matter for thanksgiving, seeing cause to thank God for sparing and preventing, common and uncommon, past and present mercies, and even afflicting providences, which God designs for our good. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

19 COMMENTARY: Quench not the Spirit. The Spirit works as fire, enlightening, enlivening, and purifying, Henry explains, and as fire is quenched by water or earth heaped on it, so we quench him by indulging carnal lusts and minding only earthly things, hindering our growth in grace. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

20 COMMENTARY: Despise not prophesyings. By prophesyings, Henry understands the preaching of the word, interpreting and applying the Scriptures, and we must not despise preaching though plain, or though it tells us no more than we knew before. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

21 COMMENTARY: Prove all things; hold fast that which is good. We must search the Scriptures, Henry says, and proving all things must end in holding fast what is good. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

22 COMMENTARY: Abstain from all appearance of evil. We should abstain from sin and whatever looks like sin, leads to it, or borders on it, Henry warns, for he who is not shy of sin's appearances and occasions will not long keep from sin itself. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

23 COMMENTARY: And the very God of peace sanctify you wholly, and I pray God your whole spirit and soul and body be preserved blameless to the coming of our Lord Jesus Christ. The apostle prays they might be sanctified more perfectly, Henry notes, for the best are sanctified but in part in this world, and we must fall if God does not carry on his work till we are presented faultless before his throne. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

24 COMMENTARY: Faithful is he that calls you, who also will do it. The faithfulness of the caller is the ground of the prayer: he who began the work will perfect it. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

25 COMMENTARY: Brethren, pray for us. We should pray for one another, Henry says, and brethren thus express brotherly love, even the apostle asking the prayers of those he taught. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

26 COMMENTARY: Greet all the brethren with a holy kiss. The greeting seals the fellowship the whole letter has built, holy in its character as the church is holy in its calling. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

27 COMMENTARY: I charge you by the Lord that this epistle be read to all the holy brethren. This epistle was to be read to all, Henry observes, for not only are the common people allowed to read the Scriptures, but it is their duty, and the word should be read in all public congregations for the benefit of the unlearned especially. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

28 COMMENTARY: The grace of our Lord Jesus Christ be with you. Amen. We need no more to make us happy, Henry concludes, than to know the grace of our Lord Jesus Christ, an ever-flowing and overflowing fountain to supply all our wants. — Matthew Henry (Concise)
