Study Notes — 1 Peter

Chapter 5

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Peter exhorts the elders among his readers, as one who is also an elder, a witness of Christ's sufferings, and a partaker of the glory to be revealed. He does not command but exhorts, claiming no power to rule over all pastors and churches, and it was the peculiar honour of Peter and a few more to be witnesses of Christ's sufferings. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: Feed the flock of God among you, taking oversight willingly, not by constraint, not for filthy lucre, but of a ready mind. These poor, dispersed, suffering Christians were the flock of God, redeemed by the great Shepherd, and their shepherds are to serve willingly and from readiness, not compulsion or gain. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: Elders are not to be lords over God's heritage, but examples to the flock. The flock is dignified with the title of God's heritage, his peculiar lot chosen for his own people, and Christ is the chief Shepherd of the whole flock, so human shepherds lead by example rather than domination. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: When the chief Shepherd appears, faithful ministers will receive a crown of glory that does not fade away. That crown is infinitely better and more honourable than all the authority, wealth, and pleasure of the world, and it belongs to all true Christians as partakers of the glory to be revealed. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: Likewise, younger Christians are to submit to the elders, and all are to be subject to one another and clothed with humility, for God resists the proud and gives grace to the humble. Humility preserves peace and order in all Christian churches and societies, while pride disturbs them, and where God gives grace to be humble he will give wisdom, faith, and holiness. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: Humble yourselves under the mighty hand of God, that he may exalt you in due time. Exaltation comes not in our fancied time but in God's own wisely appointed time, and humble submission to his hand brings greater comfort than gratifying pride and ambition. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: Cast all your care upon him, for he cares for you. Personal cares, family cares, cares for the present and future, for ourselves, others, and the church, are to be cast on God, since cares become burdensome and sinful when they arise from unbelief, torture the mind, and hinder delight in God's service. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: Be sober and vigilant, because your adversary the devil, as a roaring lion, walks about seeking whom he may devour. The whole design of Satan is to devour and destroy souls, always contriving whom he may ensnare to eternal ruin, so the duty is plain: govern outward and inward life by temperance and stay watchful against constant danger. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: Resist him, steadfast in the faith, knowing the same afflictions are accomplished in your brethren throughout the world. Steadfastness is solid standing by faith, which lifts the soul to the firm ground of the promises, and the thought that brethren suffer the same is proper encouragement to bear one's share. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: The God of all grace, who called us to his eternal glory by Christ Jesus, after you have suffered a while, will make you perfect, establish, strengthen, and settle you. Perfect implies progress toward perfection, establish the curing of natural lightness and inconstancy, strengthen growth where graces are weakest, and settle fixing on the sure foundation who is Christ himself. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: To him be glory and dominion forever and ever. Amen. The doxology closes the prayer for growth and establishment, ascribing all glory and rule to the God of all grace who called believers to eternal glory. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: By Silvanus, a faithful brother, Peter has written briefly, exhorting and testifying that this is the true grace of God in which believers stand. The power of these doctrines on hearts and their fruits in lives show who are partakers of God's grace, and the letter's testimony is meant to settle readers in that grace. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: The church at Babylon, elected together with the readers, greets them, and so does Mark, Peter's son. The greeting binds the scattered readers to a wider elect fellowship, and the cherishing of Christian love and affection is the stamp and badge of Christ on his followers. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: Greet one another with a kiss of charity, and peace be with all who are in Christ Jesus. Amen. Others may have a false peace for a time, but all solid peace is founded on Christ and flows from him, and the letter ends by wishing that peace to everyone in Christ. — Matthew Henry
