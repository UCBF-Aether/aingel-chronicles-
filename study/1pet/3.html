Study Notes — 1 Peter

Chapter 3

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Likewise, wives are to be in subjection to their own husbands, so that, if any do not obey the word, they may without the word be won by the wives' conduct. Henry notes how narrowly evil people watch the ways of professors of religion, and a godly life may persuade where argument cannot. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: Husbands may be won while beholding a chaste conversation coupled with fear, that is, conduct governed by reverence for God rather than dread of man. Religious people should take care that all their behaviour answers to their profession, since conduct is watched more closely than words. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: Adorning is not to be the outward kind, plaiting of hair, wearing of gold, or putting on of apparel. Henry clarifies that apparel itself is not forbidden, but vanity and costliness in ornament are, and few know the right measure of food and clothing without desiring something beyond what is good for them. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: The true ornament is the hidden person of the heart, in what is incorruptible, even a meek and quiet spirit, which in God's sight is of great price. Henry directs Christian women to put on the graces of the Holy Spirit, since right ordering of one's own spirit fixes affection and excites esteem more than studied ornaments with a froward temper. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: After this manner in old time the holy women who trusted in God adorned themselves, being in subjection to their own husbands. Their example shows that godly subjection and trust in God, not display, were the ancient pattern of beauty. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: Sarah obeyed Abraham, calling him lord, and Christian wives are her daughters as long as they do well and are not afraid with any amazement. Henry stresses subjection from desire to do well and please God, not from dread and amazement, and that doing duty from a willing mind in obedience to God is the whole point. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: Likewise, husbands are to dwell with their wives according to knowledge, giving honour to the wife as the weaker vessel and as heirs together of the grace of life, so that prayers are not hindered. The husband's duty implies respect, protection, and trust, living peaceably together, since husband and wife should pray together by themselves and with their children, and holiness and prayer feed each other. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: Finally, be all of one mind, having compassion one toward another, loving as brethren, pitiful and courteous. Though Christians cannot always be exactly of the same mind, they should have compassion and love as brethren, and this agreement in affection is the mark of the community. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: Do not render evil for evil or railing for railing, but contrariwise blessing, knowing you are called to this, that you should inherit a blessing. Returning blessing for insult is not weakness but the calling itself, and the blessing inherited answers the blessing given. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: Whoever would love life and see good days must refrain the tongue from evil and lips from speaking guile. Henry's rule for a comfortable life on earth and eternal life in heaven begins with bridling the tongue from wicked, abusive, or deceitful words. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: Let such a person eschew evil and do good, seeking peace and pursuing it. Forsaking evil actions is not enough: positive good must be done, and peace must be sought with all people, not merely waited for. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: For the eyes of the Lord are over the righteous and his ears open to their prayers, but the face of the Lord is against those who do evil. God, all-wise and everywhere present, watches over the righteous and takes care of them, and that watchfulness is the ground of the previous exhortations. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: Who will harm you if you are followers of what is good? None could or should harm those who copy the example of Christ, who is perfect goodness, and do good to others as his followers, though harm may still come in this world, it cannot touch the blessing promised. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: Even if you suffer for righteousness' sake, happy are you, and do not be afraid of their terror, nor troubled. Where the great fear of God is, there is no room for other fears, and it disturbs not, since reverence for God steadies the heart against human threats. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: Sanctify the Lord God in your hearts, and be ready always to give an answer to everyone who asks a reason for the hope in you, with meekness and fear. We sanctify God before others when our conduct invites them to glorify him, and we should be able to defend our religion with meekness, in the fear of God. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: Keep a good conscience, so that those who speak evil of you as evildoers may be ashamed, falsely accusing your good conversation in Christ. The conscience is good when it does its office well, and a life that contradicts slander is itself the answer to it. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: It is better, if God's will be so, to suffer for well-doing than for evil-doing. A person on whom sin and suffering meet is in a sad condition, since sin makes suffering extreme and comfortless, while suffering for good has a clear conscience behind it, whatever natural impatience suggests. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: For Christ also once suffered for sins, the just for the unjust, that he might bring us to God, being put to death in the flesh but quickened by the Spirit. The blessed end and design of his sufferings was to reconcile us to God and bring us to eternal glory, and his example is the argument for patience under suffering. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: By the Spirit Christ also went and preached to the spirits in prison. Henry reads this with the next verse: as to the old world, Christ sent his Spirit and gave warning by Noah, and God's patience waited long, so the preaching belongs to the days of Noah's warning, not to a later mission after death. — Matthew Henry

20 COMMENTARY: Those spirits were disobedient when God's longsuffering waited in the days of Noah, while the ark was being prepared, in which few, eight souls, were saved by water. Though God's patience waits long, it will cease at last, and the spirits of disobedient sinners, once out of their bodies, are committed to prison, from which there is no redemption. — Matthew Henry

21 COMMENTARY: The like figure, baptism, now also saves us, not the putting away of the filth of the flesh but the answer of a good conscience toward God, by the resurrection of Jesus Christ. Henry warns against resting on outward forms: Noah's salvation in the ark on the water was a type of believers' eternal salvation by baptism of the Holy Spirit, meaning regeneration, repentance, faith, and a new life professed uprightly before God. — Matthew Henry

22 COMMENTARY: Christ has gone into heaven and is at the right hand of God, angels, authorities, and powers being made subject to him. His resurrection is what assures believers of purifying and peace, and his exaltation over every power is the ground of their confidence under suffering. — Matthew Henry
