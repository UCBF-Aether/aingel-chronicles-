Study Notes — 1 Peter

Chapter 1

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Peter writes as an apostle of Jesus Christ to strangers scattered through Pontus, Galatia, Cappadocia, Asia, and Bithynia. Henry notes the epistle is addressed to believers in general, strangers in every city or country where they live, scattered through the nations. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: These strangers are elect according to the foreknowledge of God the Father, through sanctification of the Spirit, unto obedience and sprinkling of the blood of Jesus Christ, with grace and peace multiplied to them. Henry traces salvation to the electing love of the Father, the redemption of the Son, and the sanctification of the Spirit, giving glory to one God in three persons. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: Blessed be the God and Father of our Lord Jesus Christ, who according to his abundant mercy has begotten us again to a living hope by the resurrection of Jesus Christ from the dead. Worldly hope is uncertain and built on sand, but this hope is living in both its object and its effect, enlivening and comforting in all distress. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: The hope is set on an inheritance incorruptible, undefiled, and unfading, reserved in heaven for believers. Henry contrasts it with earthly possessions, which are stained, uncertain, and soon pass away like flowers of the field, while this estate cannot be spent and never grows less pleasant. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: Believers are kept by the power of God through faith unto salvation, ready to be revealed in the last time. God not only gives his people grace but preserves them unto glory, so that every believer always has something wherein to greatly rejoice. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: In this salvation believers greatly rejoice, though now for a season, if need be, they are in heaviness through manifold temptations. Henry notes the Lord does not willingly afflict, yet his wise love often appoints sharp trials to show his people their hearts and to do them good at the latter end. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: The trial of faith is much more precious than gold that perishes, though tried with fire, and will be found to praise, honour, and glory at the appearing of Jesus Christ. Gold becomes less by trial in the fire, but faith is made firm and multiplied by troubles and afflictions, and this should reconcile believers to present affliction. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: Christ, whom believers have not seen, they love, and believing in him they rejoice with joy unspeakable and full of glory. Henry says believing Christ's excellence in himself and his love toward us kindles a fire in the heart that rises up in a sacrifice of love to him. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: Believers receive the end of their faith, even the salvation of their souls. The certainty of this hope is, in Henry's words, as if believers had already received it, since God's glory and their own happiness are so united that seeking the one now secures the other. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: Of this salvation the prophets enquired and searched diligently, prophesying of the grace that would come to believers. Christ was the main subject of the prophets' studies, and their inquiry into his sufferings and the glories to follow leads to a view of the whole gospel. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: The prophets searched what, or what manner of time, the Spirit of Christ in them signified when testifying beforehand of Christ's sufferings and the glory that should follow. The sum of their message, Henry notes, is that Christ Jesus was delivered for our offences and raised again for our justification. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: It was revealed to the prophets that they ministered these things not to themselves but to us, as now reported by gospel preachers with the Holy Ghost sent down from heaven, things angels desire to look into. The doctrine of the prophets and of the apostles exactly agree, coming from the same Spirit of God. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: Wherefore gird up the loins of your mind, be sober, and hope to the end for the grace to be brought at the revelation of Jesus Christ. As travellers, racers, warriors, and labourers gathered their long garments to be ready, so Christians should gather their minds and affections, watching against spiritual dangers and trusting fully in God's grace. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: As obedient children, believers must not fashion themselves according to their former lusts in ignorance. Holiness is the desire and duty of every Christian, and the written word is the surest rule of life, commanding holiness in every way. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: As he who called you is holy, so be holy in all manner of conversation. Holiness must extend to all affairs, every condition, and toward all people, and believers must especially watch and pray against the sins to which they are inclined. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: It is written, Be holy, for I am holy. God makes those holy whom he saves, so the command is not a bare demand but the pattern of the salvation itself, measured by God's own character. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: If you call on the Father, who without respect of persons judges according to every person's work, pass the time of your sojourning here in fear. Holy confidence in God as Father and awful fear of him as Judge agree together: regarding God always as Judge makes him dear to us as Father, and keeps believers from both fearless presumption and enslaving dread. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: Believers were not redeemed with corruptible things, silver and gold, from the vain conversation received by tradition from their fathers. Henry warns that merely customary religion is dangerous: it is folly to resolve to live and die in a way only because one's forefathers did so. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: Redemption was with the precious blood of Christ, as of a lamb without blemish and without spot. The price paid for man's redemption was nothing less than that blood, given for openly wicked and merely unprofitable lives alike. — Matthew Henry

20 COMMENTARY: Christ was foreordained before the foundation of the world, but manifested in these last times for believers. God had purposes of special favour toward his people long before he made such grace manifest, and the light, supports, and power of ordinances are all greater since Christ came than before. — Matthew Henry

21 COMMENTARY: By Christ believers trust in God, who raised him from the dead and gave him glory, so that faith and hope are in God. Being by faith made one with Christ, his present glory assures believers that where he is, they shall be also. — Matthew Henry

22 COMMENTARY: Having purified their souls in obeying the truth through the Spirit unto unfeigned love of the brethren, believers are to love one another with a pure heart fervently. The soul must be purified before it can give up its own desires, and the word planted by the Spirit works a total change of dispositions and affections. — Matthew Henry

23 COMMENTARY: Believers are born again, not of corruptible seed but of incorruptible, by the word of God which lives and abides forever. The word of God planted in the heart by the Holy Ghost is the means of spiritual life, stirring up duty and bringing the soul to eternal life. — Matthew Henry

24 COMMENTARY: All flesh is as grass, and all the glory of man as the flower of grass: the grass withers and its flower falls away. In his life and fall, natural man is like grass, soon withering and dying, in contrast with the excellence of the renewed spiritual person. — Matthew Henry

25 COMMENTARY: But the word of the Lord endures forever, and this is the word preached to believers by the gospel. Henry's counsel is to hear, receive, and love this holy, living word, lodging it in the heart as the only treasure here and the certain pledge of the treasure of glory laid up in heaven. — Matthew Henry
