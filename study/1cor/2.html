Study Notes — 1 Corinthians

Chapter 2

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Paul came to Corinth as one of the foolish, weak, and despised instruments God uses, glorying in the Lord rather than in human wisdom. Though trained at Tarsus in secular learning, he declared the testimony of God without excellency of speech — the testimony of Christ, who is therefore God. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: Paul made it his one definite business among the Corinthians to know Jesus Christ and him crucified — his person and his office, the sum of the gospel. The seekers after human wisdom had played down the crucifixion to avoid offending learned hearers; Paul put it at the center. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: Paul was with them in bodily weakness and in trembling — not personal fear, but an anxious conscientiousness to perform his duty aright. His trembling was the opposite of eye-service: a careful desire to be faithful. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: His private speech and public preaching did not rest on persuasive words of human wisdom. Persuasion is man's means of moving men; God's means is demonstration by the Spirit, working outwardly by miracles then and inwardly on the heart now, leaving no doubt and inspiring implicit faith. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: The Corinthians' faith was meant to rest on God's power, not to owe its origin or continuance to human wisdom. A faith built on eloquence would stand or fall with the speaker. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: Yet the gospel is no enemy of true wisdom — it is a wisdom infinitely higher than that of this age, spoken among the mature in Christian experience and knowledge. The rulers of this age, great and learned as they seem, are being brought to nothing, transient rather than immortal. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: The wisdom Paul speaks is God's wisdom in a mystery — not a secret to be kept, but what was once hidden in God's counsels and is now announced to all who will obey the truth. Foreordained before the ages, from eternity, it was appointed for our glory, now and hereafter, from the Lord of glory. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: None of this age's rulers knew this wisdom, and that is the strongest proof of the natural man's lack of heavenly wisdom. Had they known it, they would not have crucified the Lord of glory — the one who held glory in his own right before the world was, crucified in his humanity. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: What eye has not seen nor heart imagined, God prepared for those who love him — Paul gives an inspired exposition of Isaiah, not a verbatim quote. Isaiah spoke to those waiting for Messiah as future; Paul speaks to those who love him as already come. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: God revealed these things to the mature by his Spirit, who searches the deep things of God — his nature, attributes, and counsels. The Spirit delights to explore the infinite depths of his own divine mind and reveal them as believers are capable of receiving them, which proves the Spirit's personality and Godhead. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: As no fellow man knows the hidden things of a man except that man's own spirit, so none knows the things of God except the Spirit of God. Any knowledge of God, therefore, must come by his Spirit alone. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: Believers have received not the spirit of the world — the evil spirit at work in the disobedient — but the Spirit who comes from God, received only as his gift. The purpose is present experimental knowledge, to their comfort, of the things freely given by God, including the future good he has prepared. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: The apostles not only knew these things by the Spirit but also spoke them in words the Spirit taught. Spiritual things are expounded by comparing spiritual things with spiritual — Old Testament Scripture with the gospel, and gospel mysteries with Old Testament types — and spiritual truths can only be joined to spiritual words, not to words of worldly wisdom. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: The natural man, governed by his animal soul with his spirit overridden and without the Spirit of God, does not receive the things of the Spirit, though they are offered to him. They seem foolishness to him, and he not only does not know them but cannot know them, so he has no wish to receive them. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: The spiritual man, in whom the Spirit rules, discerns all things by their true standard and has practical insight into gospel truth, though he is not infallible on every point. He himself is discerned by no ordinary man, for judging him would require knowing the mind of the Lord. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: Who has known the mind of the Lord so as to instruct him as his counselor? Natural men who judge spiritual men are virtually trying to set God right. Believers, in their measure, have the mind of Christ — and since Isaiah's words about Jehovah are applied here to Christ, Christ is Jehovah. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
