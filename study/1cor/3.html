Study Notes — 1 Corinthians

Chapter 3

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: As the natural man cannot receive the deep things of God, Paul could not speak them to the Corinthians as he would to the spiritual, but had to speak to them as to men of flesh. They were not wholly unregenerate, but babes in Christ with weak life, still carnal in tendency when by now they ought to have grown to full maturity. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: Paul fed them with milk, the elementary principles of the doctrine of Christ, rather than solid food. They were not yet able to bear deeper teaching. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: Envying, strife, and divisions proved they were still carnal — envy in feelings, strife in words, divisions in actions, each feeding the next. Strife is a work of the flesh, and walking in it is walking as unregenerate men walk, after the flesh rather than after the Spirit. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: Saying 'I am of Paul' or 'I am of Apollos' was itself the proof of carnality. The question stands: are you not merely men, walking as unregenerate men do? — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: Who is Paul, and who is Apollos, that believers should strive over them? They are mere servants by whose ministry the Corinthians believed, each as the Lord gave to him. If so great an apostle reasons so humbly of himself, humility becomes ordinary ministers far more. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: Paul planted and Apollos watered, following up the work Paul had begun at Corinth, but God gave the growth. Though ministers are nothing and God is all in all, God still works through instruments and promises his Spirit in the faithful use of means. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: Neither planter nor waterer is anything; God who gives the increase is all in all, and his name stands emphatically last. The whole discussion of glorying in men is set in contrast to God. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: Planter and waterer are one in aim and ministry, so they must not be made occasions for separate parties. Yet each minister is separately responsible for his own work and will receive his own reward according to his own labor — not according to success, but faithfulness, as to a good and faithful servant. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: Ministers are fellow workers belonging to God and laboring under him, while the church is God's field and God's building. The building image suits what follows about different kinds of teaching and their results, since to edify the church is to build it up. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: By the grace given him, proportioned to his work, Paul laid the foundation as a skilled master builder — skill shown precisely in laying a foundation, which the unskillful builder omits. His warning to every successor to take heed how he builds concerns the material and manner of teaching added afterward. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: No man can lay any other foundation than the one already laid by God, Jesus Christ — the person himself, not merely doctrine about him. The warning concerns the superstructure, not the foundation, since the only foundation recognized by God is already in place. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: On the solid foundation teachers build partly with durable gold, silver, and precious stones that withstand fire, and partly with perishable wood, hay, and stubble. The durable materials are teachings that will stand judgment; the perishable are teachings mixed with human philosophy and Judaism, curious rather than useful — not outright heresy, which would destroy the foundation. The superstructure also pictures the persons won by such teaching, whose conversion will be tested at the last day. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: The day of the Lord will make every teacher's work clear, for it is revealed in fire — a probatory fire, not purgatory. It tests works, not persons for cleansing, and it is universal, trying godly and ungodly alike at the last day, not beginning at death. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: If a teacher's work abides the testing fire, he will receive wages as a builder. His converts, built on Christ through faithful teaching, will be his crown of rejoicing. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: If a teacher's work is burned, he forfeits the special reward but not salvation, which is a free gift rather than wages, since he remains on the foundation. He is saved yet so as through fire — like a builder escaping a burning building, or a shipwrecked merchant saved through the waves with his merchandise lost. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: Believers together are not many temples but the one temple of God, living stones in a spiritual house. God's indwelling and the Spirit's indwelling are one, so the Holy Spirit is God; the New Testament recognizes no literal temple in the Christian church, only this spiritual temple of worshippers in whom the Spirit dwells. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: Whoever destroys God's temple, God will destroy, repaying in kind — the same verb is used for both. This destroyer is distinct from the unskillful builder who keeps the foundation and is saved; the destroyer assails the foundation itself with false teaching and subverts the temple, and as violating the temple's sanctity he will perish eternally. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

18 COMMENTARY: Whoever seems wise in this world's wisdom must become a fool in the world's sight by receiving the gospel in its unworldly simplicity. He must stop thinking himself wise and seek true wisdom from God, bringing his understanding captive to the obedience of faith. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

19 COMMENTARY: The wisdom of this world is foolishness in God's judgment. As Job says, God takes the wise in their own craftiness, making their wisdom the very snare that catches them. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

20 COMMENTARY: Quoting the Psalm, Paul says the Lord knows the thoughts of the wise, that they are vanity. The Psalm speaks of men generally; Paul by inspiration names the class the Spirit meant — the proud and worldly-wise who boast of their wisdom while God calls them fools. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

21 COMMENTARY: Let no man glory in men, resuming the argument from the party slogans: the true object of glorying is the Lord. Glorying in teachers lowers believers from their high position as heirs of all things, for teachers belong to Christ and therefore to them — they are for the believers' sake, not believers for theirs. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

22 COMMENTARY: All things are enumerated as theirs: Paul, Apollos, Cephas, the world, life, death, things present, and things to come. These do not separate believers from God's love in Christ; they belong to them as they belong to Christ their Head, and work together for their good. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

23 COMMENTARY: Believers are Christ's — not Paul's, Apollos's, or Cephas's — all of them, not merely one section. And Christ is God's, God being the ultimate end of all, even of Christ his co-equal Son. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
