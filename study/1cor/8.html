Study Notes — 1 Corinthians

Chapter 8

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: As for food offered to idols — portions of temple victims belonged to priests and offerers and were eaten at temple and private feasts or sold in markets, so Christians constantly faced the temptation. The Corinthians appealed to their knowledge that such meats are indifferent, and Paul agrees in theory, but adds the decisive qualification: knowledge puffs up when without love, while love edifies, building up the spiritual temple. Knowledge says all things are lawful; love adds that all things do not edify. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: If anyone thinks he knows something, he does not yet know as he ought to know. The first step to knowledge is knowing one's own ignorance, and without love there is only the appearance of knowledge — personal experimental acquaintance, not mere awareness of a fact. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: If anyone loves God — the source of love to neighbor — that man is known by God, known with the knowledge of approval and acknowledged as his. To love God is to know God, and he who thus knows God has first been known by God. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: As for eating food offered to idols, an idol is nothing — it has no true being, since the god it supposedly represents is not a living reality. This does not contradict the truth that idolaters worship devils, for it is the supposed gods, not the deluding devils behind them, that are denied existence. There is no God but one. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: Even supposing there are so-called gods, whether in heaven like sun, moon, and stars, or on earth like deified kings and beasts — as indeed there are many gods and many lords in men's reckoning — angels and men in authority are called gods in Scripture only as exercising divinely delegated power under God. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: To believers there is one God, the Father, from whom as Creator all things derive existence, and for whom and for whose glory believers live. And there is one Lord, Jesus Christ, contrasted with the many lords of heathendom, by whom are all things, and by whom believers are restored to the Father by the new creation. Father and Son are entirely one in this work. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: Yet this knowledge is not in everyone in the same degree. Some Gentile Christians, from old association of ideas or misdirected conscience, still ate such meats feeling as if the idol were something real that had made the meat holy or polluted. Their conscience was defiled by eating consciously as a thing offered to idols, sinning against conscience, since they lacked the knowledge that an idol is nothing and can neither pollute nor sanctify meat. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: Meat does not commend us to God: neither if we eat are we the better, nor if we do not eat are we the worse. Food in itself does not affect our standing before God. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: But take heed lest this liberty of yours — the watchword of lax Corinthians — become a stumbling-block to the weak. The very indifference of meats is the reason for care: liberty must not tempt weak brethren to act against conscience, which itself constitutes sin. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: If a weak brother sees you, a man with knowledge, reclining in an idol's temple, his weak conscience will be emboldened — literally built up — to eat what he believes wrong. You ought to have built him up in good, but your example builds him up to violate his conscience. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: And so the weak brother perishes through your knowledge — a single seemingly unimportant act may have everlasting consequences, for he loses his faith and, if he does not recover it, his salvation. This is the brother for whom Christ died, and for whose sake believers ought to be willing to die, rather than tempt him toward damnation while sacrificing nothing for his salvation. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: To sin against the brethren and wound their weak conscience — literally to smite a conscience still in a weak state, as if striking an invalid — is to sin against Christ, because of the sympathy between Christ and his members. What is done to the weak member is done to him. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: Therefore, if food makes my brother stumble, I will eat no flesh at all while the world stands, lest I make my brother stumble. To ensure avoiding idol-offered flesh, Paul would abstain from every kind of flesh rather than be a stumbling-block. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
