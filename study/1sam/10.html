Study Notes — 1 Samuel

Chapter 10

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Samuel took a vial of oil, poured it on Saul's head, kissed him, and said, Is it not because the LORD hath anointed thee to be captain over his inheritance? The sacred anointing, Henry notes, pointed at the great Messiah, the Anointed One, King of the church, anointed with the oil of the Spirit without measure. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

2 COMMENTARY: When thou art departed today, Samuel said, thou shalt find two men by Rachel's sepulchre at Zelzah, who will say the asses are found and thy father sorrows for thee. The first sign directed Saul to an ancestor's grave, Henry observes, to remind him of his mortality — with a crown before him, he must think of the grave where all his honour would be laid in the dust. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

3 COMMENTARY: Then at the plain of Tabor three men going up to God to Bethel would meet him, one carrying three kids, another three loaves, another a bottle of wine. The signs came punctually, confirming Samuel's word step by step. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

4 COMMENTARY: They will salute thee and give thee two loaves of bread, which thou shalt receive. The worshippers' gift would mark Saul as one blessed on his way. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

5 COMMENTARY: After that thou shalt come to the hill of God, where the Philistine garrison is, and meet a company of prophets coming down with psaltery, tabret, pipe, and harp, prophesying. From Samuel's time, Henry notes, there appear to have been schools where pious young men were brought up in Divine things. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

6 COMMENTARY: The Spirit of the LORD will come upon thee, and thou shalt prophesy with them, and shalt be turned into another man. The Spirit of God changes men, Henry says, wonderfully transforming them; Saul, praising God in the communion of saints, became another man — though it may be questioned if he became a new man. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

7 COMMENTARY: When these signs are come unto thee, do as occasion serves thee, for God is with thee. The signs were both confirmation and commission. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

8 COMMENTARY: Thou shalt go down before me to Gilgal, and I will come down to offer burnt offerings and peace offerings; seven days shalt thou tarry until I come and show thee what to do. The Gilgal appointment would later test Saul's obedience. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

9 COMMENTARY: When Saul turned his back to go from Samuel, God gave him another heart, and all those signs came to pass that day. He found God had given him another disposition of mind, Henry notes, yet an outward show of devotion and a sudden change should not be too much relied on. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

10 COMMENTARY: When they came to the hill, a company of prophets met him, the Spirit of God came upon him, and he prophesied among them. The promised transformation happened in public, before witnesses. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

11 COMMENTARY: All who knew him before said, What is this come unto the son of Kish? Is Saul also among the prophets? Saul among the prophets was Saul still, Henry warns, despite the outward change. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

12 COMMENTARY: One of the same place answered, But who is their father? Therefore it became a proverb, Is Saul also among the prophets? Prophecy was God's gift, not a family inheritance. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

13 COMMENTARY: When he had made an end of prophesying, he came to the high place. The ecstasy passed; the man remained to live out the calling. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

14 COMMENTARY: Saul's uncle asked where he and his servant had gone, and Saul said, To seek the asses, and when we found them nowhere, we came to Samuel. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

15 COMMENTARY: His uncle asked what Samuel had said to them, pressing for the prophet's word. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

16 COMMENTARY: Saul said his uncle plainly that the asses were found, but of the matter of the kingdom he told him not. His anointing was kept private; he left it to God to carry on his own work by Samuel, and sat still to see how the matter would fall. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

17 COMMENTARY: Samuel called the people together unto the LORD to Mizpeh, to choose the king publicly before God. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

18 COMMENTARY: Samuel said, Thus saith the LORD God of Israel, I brought up Israel out of Egypt and delivered you from Egyptians and oppressors. The choice was set against the whole history of God's deliverance. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

19 COMMENTARY: Ye have this day rejected your God, who saved you out of all adversities and tribulations, saying, Nay, but set a king over us; now present yourselves before the LORD by tribes and thousands. Samuel told the people plainly that they had rejected their God. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

20 COMMENTARY: When Samuel caused all the tribes to come near, the tribe of Benjamin was taken. Lot by lot, God's choice narrowed to the smallest tribe. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

21 COMMENTARY: The tribe of Benjamin coming near by families, the family of Matri was taken, and Saul son of Kish was taken; and when they sought him, he could not be found. The chosen man was missing at his own choosing. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

22 COMMENTARY: They enquired further of the LORD if the man should yet come thither, and the LORD answered, Behold, he hath hid himself among the stuff. So little fond was Saul now of the power he would soon be unable to part with, Henry notes, that he hid himself. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

23 COMMENTARY: They ran and fetched him, and when he stood among the people he was higher than any from his shoulders upward. The hidden man, once produced, looked every inch the king. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

24 COMMENTARY: Samuel said, See ye him whom the LORD hath chosen, that there is none like him among all the people? And all the people shouted, God save the king. It is good to be conscious of unworthiness for the services to which we are called, Henry observes, but men should not go to the contrary extreme of refusing the employments to which the Lord and the church call them. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

25 COMMENTARY: Samuel told the people the manner of the kingdom, wrote it in a book, and laid it up before the LORD, and sent all the people away every man to his house. The kingdom's manner was recorded as a standing witness. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

26 COMMENTARY: Saul also went home to Gibeah, and there went with him a band of men whose hearts God had touched. If the heart bends the right way at any time, Henry says, it is because God has touched it; one touch is enough when it is Divine. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

27 COMMENTARY: But the children of Belial said, How shall this man save us? And they despised him and brought no presents, but he held his peace. Thus differently are men affected toward our exalted Redeemer, Henry reflects: a remnant submit to him, while others despise him and ask how this man shall save us, and they will be punished. — Matthew Henry (Concise)
