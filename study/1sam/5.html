Study Notes — 1 Samuel

Chapter 5

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: The Philistines took the ark from Ebenezer to Ashdod. The captured ark, which Israel had trusted as a charm, now travelled as a prisoner into enemy country. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

2 COMMENTARY: They brought the ark into the house of Dagon and set it by Dagon, placing the God of Israel beside their idol as a trophy. Thus the kingdom of Satan seemed to triumph — but Henry reads the chapter as the ark's triumph over Dagon instead. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

3 COMMENTARY: When the men of Ashdod arose, Dagon had fallen on his face before the ark of the LORD, and they set him back in his place. The idol bowed first, a sign that error falls before truth. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

4 COMMENTARY: The next morning Dagon lay fallen again, his head and both hands broken off upon the threshold, only the stump remaining. Every attempt to set the idol up again was vain, just as Henry says every endeavour to restore dethroned idols in the heart proves vain when Christ, the true Ark, enters. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

5 COMMENTARY: Therefore neither the priests of Dagon nor any who come into Dagon's house tread on the threshold in Ashdod to this day. The broken idol left a lasting superstition in place of a lasting repentance. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

6 COMMENTARY: The hand of the LORD was heavy on Ashdod, destroying them and smiting them with emerods, Ashdod and its coasts. God not only convinced the Philistines of their folly but severely chastised their insolence. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

7 COMMENTARY: When the men of Ashdod saw this, they said the ark of the God of Israel should not abide with them, for his hand was sore on them and on Dagon their god. Yet they would not renounce Dagon; instead of seeking God's mercy, they desired to be rid of his ark. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

8 COMMENTARY: They gathered all the Philistine lords and asked what to do with the ark, and the lords answered, carry it to Gath. Carnal hearts, Henry notes, when they smart under God's judgments, would rather put him far from them than enter into covenant with him. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

9 COMMENTARY: After the ark was carried about to Gath, the hand of the LORD was against the city with a very great destruction, smiting small and great with emerods. Their devices to escape Divine judgment only increased it. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

10 COMMENTARY: Therefore they sent the ark to Ekron, and the Ekronites cried out that it had been brought to slay them and their people. Each city in turn learned what Ashdod had learned. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

11 COMMENTARY: So they gathered all the lords again and said, Send away the ark of the God of Israel, let it go to its own place, that it slay us not and our people, for a deadly destruction was throughout the city and God's hand was very heavy there. Those that fight against God will soon have enough of it. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

12 COMMENTARY: The men who died not were smitten with emerods, and the cry of the city went up to heaven. Pride, self-love, and worldly lusts, Henry warns, though dethroned, still remain within us like the stump of Dagon, and we must watch and pray that they do not prevail. — Matthew Henry (Concise)
