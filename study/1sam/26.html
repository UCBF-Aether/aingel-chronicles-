Study Notes — 1 Samuel

Chapter 26

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: The Ziphites came unto Saul to Gibeah, saying, Doth not David hide himself in the hill of Hachilah, which is before Jeshimon? The betrayal of chapter 23 was repeated, and the hunt renewed. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

2 COMMENTARY: Then Saul arose and went down to the wilderness of Ziph, having three thousand chosen men with him, to seek David there. How soon do unholy hearts lose the good impressions convictions have made upon them, Henry observes: the weeping confession at Engedi was already forgotten. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

3 COMMENTARY: Saul pitched in the hill of Hachilah before Jeshimon, by the way, but David abode in the wilderness, and saw that Saul came after him. The hunter camped on the very hill the betrayers had named. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

4 COMMENTARY: David sent out spies and understood that Saul was come in very deed. The fugitive verified the report before he moved. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

5 COMMENTARY: David arose and came to the place where Saul had pitched, and beheld where Saul lay, and Abner son of Ner, captain of his host; Saul lay in the trench, and the people pitched round about him. David reconnoitred the royal camp in person. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

6 COMMENTARY: David said to Ahimelech the Hittite and to Abishai son of Zeruiah, Who will go down with me to Saul to the camp? And Abishai said, I will go down with thee. Two men volunteered for the night venture into the enemy camp. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

7 COMMENTARY: So David and Abishai came to the people by night, and behold, Saul lay sleeping within the trench, his spear stuck in the ground at his bolster, with Abner and the people round about him. How helpless were Saul and all his men, Henry says: all as though disarmed and chained, yet nothing was done to them; they were only asleep. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

8 COMMENTARY: Then said Abishai, God hath delivered thine enemy into thine hand this day; now let me smite him with the spear even to the earth at once, and I will not smite him the second time. The temptation was very strong, Henry notes, and Abishai offered to do the deed in one stroke. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

9 COMMENTARY: David said to Abishai, Destroy him not, for who can stretch forth his hand against the LORD's anointed, and be guiltless? David still resolved to wait until God thought fit to avenge him on Saul, Henry says, and would by no means force his way to the promised crown by wrong methods. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

10 COMMENTARY: David said furthermore, As the LORD liveth, the LORD shall smite him, or his day shall come to die, or he shall descend into battle and perish. If David yielded, Henry observes, he would sin against God; therefore he resisted the temptation and trusted God with the event. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

11 COMMENTARY: The LORD forbid that I should stretch forth mine hand against the LORD's anointed, but take now the spear at his bolster, and the cruse of water, and let us go. The spear and the water-cruse would be the evidence, as the skirt had been before. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

12 COMMENTARY: So David took the spear and the cruse of water from Saul's bolster, and they gat them away, and no man saw it, nor knew it, neither awaked, for they were all asleep, because a deep sleep from the LORD was fallen upon them. How easily can God weaken the strongest, befool the wisest, and baffle the most watchful, Henry reflects. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

13 COMMENTARY: Then David went over to the other side and stood on the top of a hill afar off, a great space being between them. From a safe height, David would reason with the camp he had just left asleep. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

14 COMMENTARY: David cried to the people and to Abner son of Ner, Answerest thou not, Abner? Then Abner answered, Who art thou that criest to the king? The voice from the hill woke the captain before it woke the king. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

15 COMMENTARY: David challenged Abner as a valiant man without peer in Israel, asking why he had not kept his lord the king when one of the people had come in to destroy him. David reasoned seriously and affectionately with Saul, Henry notes, beginning with the captain's failed watch. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

16 COMMENTARY: David pronounced Abner's watch not good and said the guards were worthy to die for not keeping their master, the LORD's anointed, and pointed to the missing spear and water-cruse at Saul's bolster. The missing spear and cruse proved the charge. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

17 COMMENTARY: Saul knew David's voice and said, Is this thy voice, my son David? And David said, It is my voice, my lord, O king. The king woke to his enemy's voice and his own spear gone. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

18 COMMENTARY: David asked why his lord pursued his servant, and what he had done or what evil was in his hand. His question again exposed the pursuit as causeless. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

19 COMMENTARY: Now therefore, let my lord hear the words of his servant: if the LORD have stirred thee up against me, let him accept an offering; but if they be the children of men, cursed be they before the LORD, for they have driven me out this day from abiding in the inheritance of the LORD, saying, Go, serve other gods. Those who forbid our attendance on God's ordinances, Henry observes, do what they can to estrange us from God and make us heathens; and we are to reckon that which exposes us to sin the greatest injury that can be done us. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

20 COMMENTARY: Now therefore, let not my blood fall to the earth before the face of the LORD, for the king of Israel is come out to seek a flea, as when one doth hunt a partridge in the mountains. The king's hunt was again a chase after a flea and a partridge. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

21 COMMENTARY: Then said Saul, I have sinned: return, my son David, for I will no more do thee harm, because my soul was precious in thine eyes this day; behold, I have played the fool, and have erred exceedingly. Saul repeated his good words and good wishes, Henry notes, but showed no evidence of true repentance towards God. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

22 COMMENTARY: David answered, Behold the king's spear! And let one of the young men come over and fetch it. The spear was returned, but David did not return with it. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

23 COMMENTARY: The LORD render to every man his righteousness and his faithfulness, for the LORD delivered thee into my hand today, but I would not stretch forth mine hand against the LORD's anointed. In sinning against God, Henry says, men play the fool and err exceedingly. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

24 COMMENTARY: And behold, as thy life was much set by this day in mine eyes, so let my life be much set by in the eyes of the LORD, and let him deliver me out of all tribulation. David prayed his own mercy back upon himself as a pattern of God's mercy. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

25 COMMENTARY: Then Saul said to David, Blessed be thou, my son David; thou shalt both do great things, and also shalt still prevail. So David went on his way, and Saul returned to his place. David and Saul parted to meet no more, Henry notes; no reconciliation among men is firm which is not founded in and cemented by peace with God. — Matthew Henry (Concise)
