Study Notes — 1 Samuel

Chapter 28

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: In those days the Philistines gathered their armies for warfare against Israel, and Achish said to David, Know thou assuredly, thou shalt go out with me to battle, thou and thy men. David could not refuse Achish without danger, Henry notes, and his position seemed impossible to escape with a clear conscience. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

2 COMMENTARY: David said to Achish, Surely thou shalt know what thy servant can do, and Achish said, Therefore will I make thee keeper of mine head for ever. His evasive answer, intended to gain time, was not consistent with the character of an Israelite indeed, Henry observes. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

3 COMMENTARY: Now Samuel was dead, and all Israel had lamented him and buried him in Ramah, and Saul had put away those with familiar spirits and wizards out of the land. The prophet's death left Saul without his counsellor, and the king's own law had cleared the land of mediums — the very people he would soon seek. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

4 COMMENTARY: The Philistines gathered and pitched in Shunem, and Saul gathered all Israel and pitched in Gilboa. The two hosts faced each other for the final campaign. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

5 COMMENTARY: When Saul saw the Philistine host, he was afraid, and his heart greatly trembled. Troubles are terrors to the children of disobedience, Henry says. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

6 COMMENTARY: When Saul enquired of the LORD, the LORD answered him not, neither by dreams, nor by Urim, nor by prophets. In his distress Saul enquired, Henry notes, but he did not seek in faith — he sought with a double, unstable mind, and heaven was silent by every channel. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

7 COMMENTARY: Then said Saul to his servants, Seek me a woman that hath a familiar spirit, that I may go to her and enquire of her, and his servants said, Behold, there is a woman at Endor. Many seem zealous against sin when they are hurt by it, Henry observes, who have no concern for God's glory nor dislike of sin as sin; Saul would drive the devil out of his kingdom yet harbour him in his heart by envy and malice. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

8 COMMENTARY: Saul disguised himself, put on other raiment, and went with two men by night to the woman, saying, Divine unto me by the familiar spirit, and bring me up him whom I shall name. How foolish, Henry asks, to consult those whom, according to God's law, he had endeavoured to root out! — Matthew Henry (Concise)

9 COMMENTARY: The woman said, Thou knowest what Saul hath done, how he hath cut off those with familiar spirits and wizards out of the land; wherefore then layest thou a snare for my life, to cause me to die? The medium feared the king's own law was a trap. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

10 COMMENTARY: Saul sware to her by the LORD, As the LORD liveth, there shall no punishment happen to thee for this thing. The king swore by the LORD to protect a practice the LORD had forbidden. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

11 COMMENTARY: Then said the woman, Whom shall I bring up unto thee? And he said, Bring me up Samuel. The name he wanted was the prophet he had despised in life. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

12 COMMENTARY: When the woman saw Samuel, she cried with a loud voice, and said to Saul, Why hast thou deceived me? For thou art Saul. The woman's surprise and terror, Henry notes, proved this was an unusual and unexpected appearance, and no human fraud: though the woman could not cause Samuel's being sent, Saul's inquiry might be the occasion of it. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

13 COMMENTARY: The king said, Be not afraid; for what sawest thou? And the woman said, I saw gods ascending out of the earth. The apparition rose as something more than a spirit of the dead. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

14 COMMENTARY: He asked, What form is he of? And she said, An old man cometh up, covered with a mantle, and Saul perceived it was Samuel, and stooped with his face to the ground and bowed himself. The mantle identified the prophet. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

15 COMMENTARY: Samuel asked why Saul had disquieted him to bring him up, and Saul answered that he was sore distressed, with the Philistines at war against him and God departed and answering no more by prophets or dreams. While Samuel lived, Henry observes, we never read of Saul going to advise with him in difficulties, yet now he sought the dead prophet's counsel. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

16 COMMENTARY: Then said Samuel, Wherefore then dost thou ask of me, seeing the LORD is departed from thee and is become thine enemy? The question exposed the folly: counsel was sought from the prophet of the God who had become his enemy. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

17 COMMENTARY: The LORD hath done to him as he spake by me, for the LORD hath rent the kingdom out of thine hand and given it to thy neighbour, even to David. The sentence pronounced in life was confirmed from beyond the grave. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

18 COMMENTARY: Because thou obeyedst not the voice of the LORD, nor executedst his fierce wrath upon Amalek, therefore hath the LORD done this thing unto thee this day. Amalek, the old disobedience, was named as the cause of the present doom. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

19 COMMENTARY: Moreover the LORD will also deliver Israel with thee into the hand of the Philistines, and tomorrow shalt thou and thy sons be with me, and the LORD shall deliver Israel's host into Philistine hands. The expression, Thou and thy sons shall be with me, Henry explains, means no more than that they shall be in the eternal world; and there appears much solemnity in God's permitting a departed prophet to come as a witness from heaven to confirm the word he spoke on earth. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

20 COMMENTARY: Then Saul fell straightway all along on the earth, and was sore afraid because of Samuel's words, and there was no strength in him, for he had eaten no bread all day nor all night. Those who expect good counsel or comfort otherwise than from God, Henry warns, will be as wretchedly disappointed as Saul. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

21 COMMENTARY: The woman came unto Saul, saw he was sore troubled, and said, Behold, thine handmaid hath obeyed thy voice, and I have put my life in my hand, and have hearkened unto thy words. The medium, who had risked her life, now became the king's nurse. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

22 COMMENTARY: Now therefore, I pray thee, hearken also unto the voice of thine handmaid, and let me set a morsel of bread before thee, and eat, that thou mayest have strength when thou goest on thy way. Bread was pressed on the man who would not eat. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

23 COMMENTARY: But he refused, saying, I will not eat, but his servants together with the woman compelled him, and he hearkened, arose from the earth, and sat upon the bed. Though terrified even to despair, Henry notes, he was not humbled: he confessed not his sins, offered no sacrifices, and presented no supplications. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

24 COMMENTARY: The woman had a fat calf in the house, and she hasted and killed it, took flour, kneaded it, and baked unleavened bread thereof. The last meal of the doomed king was prepared in haste by the medium's hand. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

25 COMMENTARY: She brought it before Saul and his servants, and they did eat, then rose up and went away that night. He does not seem to have cared about his sons or his people, Henry observes, nor attempted any escape, but in sullen despair rushed upon his doom; God sets up a few such beacons to warn men not to stifle convictions or despise his word. — Matthew Henry (Concise)
