Study Notes — 1 Samuel

Chapter 12

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Samuel said to all Israel, I have hearkened to your voice in all you said to me, and have made a king over you. The old judge, having given them their king, now gives them his farewell reckoning. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

2 COMMENTARY: Now the king walks before you, Samuel said, and I am old and grayheaded, my sons are with you, and I have walked before you from my childhood to this day. His whole public life stood open to their judgment. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

3 COMMENTARY: Behold, here I am, he said: witness against me before the LORD and his anointed — whose ox or ass have I taken, whom have I defrauded or oppressed, from whose hand have I taken a bribe? And I will restore it. Samuel not only cleared his own character but set an example before Saul, Henry notes, while showing the people their ingratitude to God and to himself. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

4 COMMENTARY: They said, Thou hast not defrauded us, nor oppressed us, neither hast thou taken ought of any man's hand. The unanimous acquittal sealed his integrity. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

5 COMMENTARY: He said, The LORD is witness against you, and his anointed is witness this day, that ye have not found ought in my hand, and they answered, He is witness. There is a just debt all men owe their own good name, Henry observes, especially men in public stations: to guard it against unjust blame, that they may finish their course with honour as well as joy. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

6 COMMENTARY: Samuel said, It is the LORD that advanced Moses and Aaron and brought your fathers up out of Egypt. The history of deliverance, not the new king, is the foundation of his reproof. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

7 COMMENTARY: Now stand still, that I may reason with you before the LORD of all the righteous acts of the LORD, which he did to you and to your fathers. The work of ministers, Henry says, is to reason with people — not only to exhort, but to persuade and convince judgments, and so gain wills and affections. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

8 COMMENTARY: When Jacob was come into Egypt and your fathers cried to the LORD, he sent Moses and Aaron, who brought them out and made them dwell in this place. Every deliverance came through God's appointed servants. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

9 COMMENTARY: When they forgot the LORD their God, he sold them into the hand of Sisera, the Philistines, and the king of Moab, and they fought against them. Forgetting God brought the very enemies they had feared. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

10 COMMENTARY: They cried to the LORD, confessing, We have sinned, because we have forsaken the LORD and served Baalim and Ashtaroth; but now deliver us, and we will serve thee. Confession and return always preceded deliverance in the old story. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

11 COMMENTARY: The LORD sent Jerubbaal, Bedan, Jephthah, and Samuel, and delivered you from your enemies on every side, and ye dwelled safe. Judge after judge proved that God, not a king, was Israel's saviour. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

12 COMMENTARY: When ye saw Nahash king of Ammon come against you, ye said, Nay, but a king shall reign over us, when the LORD your God was your king. The demand for a king was made while God himself was already their king. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

13 COMMENTARY: Now therefore behold the king whom ye have chosen and desired, and the LORD hath set a king over you. The chosen king stood before them as both God's gift and their own demand. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

14 COMMENTARY: If ye will fear the LORD, serve him, obey his voice, and not rebel, then both ye and the king that reigns over you shall continue following the LORD your God. Those who follow God faithfully, Henry notes, he will enable to continue following him. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

15 COMMENTARY: But if ye will not obey the voice of the LORD but rebel, then the hand of the LORD shall be against you, as against your fathers. Disobedience would certainly be Israel's ruin, Henry warns; we mistake if we think we can escape God's justice by trying to shake off his dominion. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

16 COMMENTARY: Now stand and see this great thing which the LORD will do before your eyes. A visible sign would settle what argument alone had not. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

17 COMMENTARY: Is it not wheat harvest today? I will call unto the LORD, and he shall send thunder and rain, that ye may perceive how great your wickedness is in asking a king. At Samuel's word God sent thunder and rain at a season when the like was not seen, Henry notes — in wheat harvest, on a clear day, and foretold beforehand — to convince them they had done wickedly. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

18 COMMENTARY: Samuel called unto the LORD, and the LORD sent thunder and rain that day, and all the people greatly feared the LORD and Samuel. Some will not be brought to see their sins by any gentler methods than storms and thunders, Henry observes. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

19 COMMENTARY: All the people said to Samuel, Pray for thy servants unto the LORD thy God, that we die not, for we have added unto all our sins this evil, to ask us a king. Now they saw their need of him whom shortly before they had slighted — as many who will not have Christ to reign over them would yet be glad to have him intercede for them. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

20 COMMENTARY: Samuel said, Fear not: ye have done all this wickedness, yet turn not aside from following the LORD, but serve the LORD with all your heart. Samuel aimed to confirm the people in their religion even after their sin, not to drive them away. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

21 COMMENTARY: Turn ye not aside, for then should ye go after vain things which cannot profit nor deliver, for they are vain. Whatever we make a god of, Henry warns, we shall find it deceive us; creatures in their own places are good, but put in God's place they are vain things. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

22 COMMENTARY: For the LORD will not forsake his people for his great name's sake, because it hath pleased the LORD to make you his people. God's faithfulness to his own name secures a people who have forfeited every other claim. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

23 COMMENTARY: Moreover as for me, God forbid that I should sin against the LORD in ceasing to pray for you; but I will teach you the good and the right way. We sin if we restrain prayer, Henry notes, and in particular if we cease praying for the church; they asked him only to pray, but he promised to do more, to teach them. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

24 COMMENTARY: Only fear the LORD, and serve him in truth with all your heart, for consider how great things he hath done for you. They were bound in gratitude to serve God, considering what great things he had done for them. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

25 COMMENTARY: But if ye shall still do wickedly, ye shall be consumed, both ye and your king. They were bound in interest to serve him, considering what he would do against them if they still did wickedly; thus, as a faithful watchman, Samuel gave warning and delivered his own soul. — Matthew Henry (Concise)
