Study Notes — 1 Samuel

Chapter 8

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: When Samuel was old, he made his sons judges over Israel. Age passed the burden to the next generation, but the sons would not carry it as he had. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

2 COMMENTARY: His firstborn was Joel and his second Abiah, judges in Beersheba. The southern post put them far from Samuel's eye at Ramah. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

3 COMMENTARY: His sons walked not in his ways but turned aside after lucre, took bribes, and perverted judgment. They were not so profane as Eli's sons, Henry notes, but they were corrupt judges; Samuel took no bribes, but his sons did. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

4 COMMENTARY: Then all the elders of Israel gathered and came to Samuel at Ramah. The grievance was sharpened, Henry adds, by the threat of invasion from Nahash king of the Ammonites. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

5 COMMENTARY: They said, Thou art old, thy sons walk not in thy ways; now make us a king to judge us like all the nations. The elders turned a real grievance into a demand for a new kind of government. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

6 COMMENTARY: The thing displeased Samuel when they said, Give us a king to judge us, and Samuel prayed unto the LORD. He could patiently bear what reflected on himself and his family, Henry observes, but this reflected upon God, and it drove him to his knees. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

7 COMMENTARY: The LORD said to Samuel, Hearken to the people's voice in all they say, for they have not rejected thee, but they have rejected me, that I should not reign over them. The demand was, at root, a rejection of God's own kingship. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

8 COMMENTARY: According to all their works since Egypt, forsaking God and serving other gods, so they do also unto thee. The rejection of Samuel was of a piece with Israel's long history of forsaking God. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

9 COMMENTARY: Now therefore hearken to their voice, yet protest solemnly unto them and show them the manner of the king that shall reign over them. God would grant the request, but not without a solemn warning of its cost. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

10 COMMENTARY: Samuel told all the words of the LORD to the people who asked for a king, delivering God's answer faithfully, warning and all. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

11 COMMENTARY: This will be the manner of the king, he said: he will take your sons and appoint them for his chariots and horsemen, and some shall run before his chariots. If they would have a king to rule as eastern kings ruled, Henry notes, they would find the yoke exceedingly heavy. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

12 COMMENTARY: He will appoint captains over thousands and over fifties, and set them to ear his ground, reap his harvest, and make his instruments of war and of his chariots. The king's glory would be built on the people's sons and labour. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

13 COMMENTARY: He will take your daughters to be confectionaries, cooks, and bakers. The court would consume the household's daughters as well as its sons. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

14 COMMENTARY: He will take your fields, vineyards, and oliveyards, even the best of them, and give them to his servants. The law of God and the manner of men widely differ, Henry observes; the latter should measure our expectations from others, not rule our relations. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

15 COMMENTARY: He will take the tenth of your seed and vineyards and give it to his officers and servants. A second tithe, owed not to God but to the crown. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

16 COMMENTARY: He will take your menservants, maidservants, goodliest young men, and asses, and put them to his work. Nothing the household owned would be exempt from the king's taking. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

17 COMMENTARY: He will take the tenth of your sheep, and ye shall be his servants. The people who asked for a king to judge them would end as the king's servants. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

18 COMMENTARY: Ye shall cry out in that day because of your chosen king, and the LORD will not hear you in that day. When we bring ourselves into distress by our own wrong desires and projects, Henry warns, we justly forfeit the comfort of prayer and the benefit of Divine aid. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

19 COMMENTARY: Nevertheless the people refused to obey Samuel and said, Nay, but we will have a king over us. They were obstinate and urgent in their demand; sudden resolves and hasty desires make work for long and leisurely repentance. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

20 COMMENTARY: That we also may be like all the nations, they said, and that our king may judge us, go out before us, and fight our battles. Likeness to the nations, not faithfulness to God, was the prize they wanted. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

21 COMMENTARY: Samuel heard all the people's words and rehearsed them in the ears of the LORD. When anything disturbs us, Henry counsels, it is our interest as well as our duty to show our trouble before God. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

22 COMMENTARY: The LORD said, Hearken to their voice and make them a king, and Samuel sent every man to his city. God was not pleased with their request, Henry notes, but as he sometimes opposes us from loving-kindness, so at other times he gratifies us in wrath; he knows how to bring glory to himself even by men's foolish counsels. — Matthew Henry (Concise)
