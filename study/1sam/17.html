Study Notes — 1 Samuel

Chapter 17

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: The Philistines gathered their armies at Shochoh in Judah, pitching between Shochoh and Azekah in Ephesdammim. The old enemy returned to the field for the war that would make David known. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

2 COMMENTARY: Saul and the men of Israel pitched by the valley of Elah and set the battle in array against the Philistines. Two armies faced each other, neither yet daring to close. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

3 COMMENTARY: The Philistines stood on a mountain on one side, Israel on a mountain on the other, with a valley between them. The valley was the stage for single combat. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

4 COMMENTARY: There went out a champion from the Philistine camp, Goliath of Gath, six cubits and a span tall. The giant's sheer size was itself a weapon against Israel's courage. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

5 COMMENTARY: He wore a helmet of brass and a coat of mail weighing five thousand shekels of brass. His armour was as massive as the man. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

6 COMMENTARY: He had greaves of brass upon his legs and a target of brass between his shoulders. From head to foot he was cased in brass. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

7 COMMENTARY: The staff of his spear was like a weaver's beam, and its head weighed six hundred shekels of iron, and a shield-bearer went before him. His weapons alone outweighed a man's whole panoply. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

8 COMMENTARY: Goliath stood and challenged Israel's armies to choose a man to come down to him, mocking them as servants of Saul. The challenge reduced two armies to two men. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

9 COMMENTARY: If he can fight and kill me, we will be your servants; but if I prevail and kill him, ye shall be our servants. The fate of nations was staked on one duel. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

10 COMMENTARY: I defy the armies of Israel this day, the Philistine said; give me a man, that we may fight together. The defiance was aimed at Israel's armies, and through them at Israel's God. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

11 COMMENTARY: When Saul and all Israel heard those words, they were dismayed and greatly afraid. Men so entirely depend upon God, Henry observes, that when he withdraws his help, the most valiant and resolute cannot find their hearts or hands. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

12 COMMENTARY: Now David was the son of Jesse the Ephrathite of Bethlehem-judah, who had eight sons and was an old man in the days of Saul. The youngest son of an aged father was about to enter the crisis. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

13 COMMENTARY: The three eldest sons of Jesse followed Saul to the battle: Eliab, Abinadab, and Shammah. David's brothers were already in the army he would soon deliver. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

14 COMMENTARY: David was the youngest, and the three eldest followed Saul. The youngest remained behind, shuttling between court and flock. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

15 COMMENTARY: But David went and returned from Saul to feed his father's sheep at Bethlehem. The anointed king-in-waiting was still a shepherd boy running errands. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

16 COMMENTARY: The Philistine drew near morning and evening and presented himself forty days. Forty days of unanswered defiance wore Israel's spirit down. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

17 COMMENTARY: Jesse said to David, Take for thy brethren an ephah of parched corn and ten loaves, and run to the camp of thy brethren. Jesse little thought of sending his son to the army at that critical juncture, Henry notes, but the wise God orders actions and affairs to serve his designs. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

18 COMMENTARY: Carry ten cheeses to the captain of their thousand, look how thy brethren fare, and take their pledge. The errand was a father's care for his soldier sons. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

19 COMMENTARY: Now Saul and they and all Israel were in the valley of Elah, fighting with the Philistines. The errand led straight to the front line. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

20 COMMENTARY: David rose early, left the sheep with a keeper, took the provisions, and went as Jesse commanded, coming to the trench as the host went forth to the fight and shouted for battle. The shepherd's diligence matched the soldiers' array. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

21 COMMENTARY: For Israel and the Philistines had put the battle in array, army against army. The daily array was a daily stalemate. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

22 COMMENTARY: David left his carriage with the keeper, ran into the army, and came and saluted his brethren. He arrived as a brother, not as a challenger. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

23 COMMENTARY: As he talked with them, the champion Goliath came up and spoke the same words, and David heard them. The boy heard the defiance for the first time, fresh, while the army had heard it forty days. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

24 COMMENTARY: All the men of Israel, when they saw the man, fled from him and were sore afraid. Forty days had taught the army to run at the sight of him. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

25 COMMENTARY: The men of Israel said, Have ye seen this man? Surely to defy Israel is he come up; and the man who kills him, the king will enrich, give his daughter, and make his father's house free in Israel. The reward was proclaimed alongside the fear. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

26 COMMENTARY: David asked the men standing by, What shall be done to the man that kills this Philistine and takes away the reproach from Israel? For who is this uncircumcised Philistine, that he should defy the armies of the living God? David's question turned the issue from the giant's size to God's honour. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

27 COMMENTARY: The people answered him after this manner: So shall it be done to the man that killeth him. The reward was confirmed to the eager listener. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

28 COMMENTARY: Eliab's anger was kindled against David, accusing him of pride and of coming down only to see the battle, after asking with whom he had left those few sheep in the wilderness. In times of formality, Henry observes, every degree of zeal that ventures further in God's cause will be blamed as pride and ambition, by none more than near relations like Eliab. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

29 COMMENTARY: David said, What have I now done? Is there not a cause? He answered his brother's passion with meekness, Henry notes, and with a soft answer turned away wrath; this conquest of his own passion was more honourable than that of Goliath. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

30 COMMENTARY: He turned from Eliab toward another and spoke after the same manner, and the people answered again after the former manner. David humbly went on with his inquiry in the face of friends' slights, not only enemies' threats. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

31 COMMENTARY: When David's words were heard, they rehearsed them before Saul, and Saul sent for him. The shepherd's words reached the king who had no answer of his own. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

32 COMMENTARY: David said to Saul, Let no man's heart fail because of him; thy servant will go and fight with this Philistine. A shepherd lad, come that morning from keeping sheep, had more courage than all Israel's mighty men, Henry observes; thus God often does great things by the weak and foolish things of the world. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

33 COMMENTARY: Saul said, Thou art not able to go against this Philistine, for thou art but a youth, and he a man of war from his youth. The king measured the match by experience, and found it hopeless. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

34 COMMENTARY: David said, Thy servant kept his father's sheep, and there came a lion and a bear and took a lamb out of the flock. The shepherd's past became his credentials. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

35 COMMENTARY: I went out after him, smote him, delivered the lamb from his mouth, and when he arose against me, I caught him by his beard, smote him, and slew him. David had proved himself very careful and tender of his flock, Henry notes — a reminder of Christ, the good Shepherd, who laid down his life for the sheep. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

36 COMMENTARY: Thy servant slew both the lion and the bear, and this uncircumcised Philistine shall be as one of them, seeing he hath defied the armies of the living God. As David answered his brother's passion with meekness, Henry says, so he answered Saul's fear with faith. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

37 COMMENTARY: David said moreover, The LORD that delivered me from the paw of the lion and the bear, he will deliver me from the hand of this Philistine, and Saul said, Go, and the LORD be with thee. Our experience ought to encourage us to trust in God and be bold in the way of duty, Henry counsels: he that has delivered, does and will continue to do so. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

38 COMMENTARY: Saul armed David with his armour, putting a helmet of brass on his head and a coat of mail on him. The king lent the boy his own panoply. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

39 COMMENTARY: David girded Saul's sword upon his armour and assayed to go, for he had not proved it, and said, I cannot go with these, for I have not proved them, and put them off. Not being used to such armour, Henry notes, David was not satisfied to go in that manner — and this was from the Lord, that it might plainly appear he fought and conquered in faith. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

40 COMMENTARY: He took his staff, chose five smooth stones from the brook, put them in his shepherd's bag, with his sling in his hand, and drew near to the Philistine. Let Saul's coat be ever so rich and his armour ever so strong, Henry asks, what is David the better if they fit him not? Faith, prayer, truth, and righteousness — the whole armour of God — are what his servants need. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

41 COMMENTARY: The Philistine came on and drew near to David, and the shield-bearer went before him. Giant and shepherd closed the distance between them. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

42 COMMENTARY: When the Philistine looked about and saw David, he disdained him, for he was but a youth, ruddy and fair of countenance. The security and presumption of fools destroy them, Henry warns. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

43 COMMENTARY: The Philistine said, Am I a dog, that thou comest to me with staves? And he cursed David by his gods. The giant's contempt turned to cursing. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

44 COMMENTARY: The Philistine said, Come to me, and I will give thy flesh unto the fowls of the air and beasts of the field. The threat promised David's body to scavengers. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

45 COMMENTARY: Then said David, Thou comest to me with sword, spear, and shield, but I come to thee in the name of the LORD of hosts, the God of the armies of Israel, whom thou hast defied. Nothing can excel the humility, faith, and piety in David's words, Henry says; he gloried in his mean appearance and arms, that the victory might be ascribed to the Lord alone. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

46 COMMENTARY: This day will the LORD deliver thee into mine hand, and I will smite thee and take thine head, and give the Philistine host's carcases to fowls and beasts, that all the earth may know there is a God in Israel. David expressed his assured expectation of success, with the purpose that the whole earth should know God. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

47 COMMENTARY: And all this assembly shall know that the LORD saveth not with sword and spear, for the battle is the LORD's, and he will give you into our hands. The victory would teach Israel itself that God saves by his own means. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

48 COMMENTARY: When the Philistine arose and drew nigh to meet David, David hastened and ran toward the army to meet the Philistine. The shepherd ran toward the giant, not away. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

49 COMMENTARY: David put his hand in his bag, took a stone, slang it, and smote the Philistine in his forehead, so the stone sunk into his forehead, and he fell on his face to the earth. See how frail life is, Henry reflects, even when a man thinks himself best fortified: how quickly, by how small a matter, the passage may be opened for life to go out. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

50 COMMENTARY: So David prevailed over the Philistine with a sling and a stone, and smote and slew him, but there was no sword in David's hand. The weaponless victory proved the battle was the LORD's. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

51 COMMENTARY: David ran, stood upon the Philistine, took his sword, drew it from its sheath, slew him, and cut off his head, and when the Philistines saw their champion dead, they fled. Let not the strong man glory in his strength, Henry warns, nor the armed man in his armour; God resists the proud and pours contempt on those who defy him and his people. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

52 COMMENTARY: The men of Israel and Judah arose, shouted, and pursued the Philistines to the valley and the gates of Ekron, and the Philistine wounded fell by the way to Shaaraim, even unto Gath and Ekron. The army that had fled at the giant's sight now chased his whole host. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

53 COMMENTARY: The children of Israel returned from chasing the Philistines and spoiled their tents. The abandoned camp became Israel's plunder. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

54 COMMENTARY: David took the head of the Philistine and brought it to Jerusalem, but he put his armour in his tent. The trophy went to the future capital; the armour stayed with the shepherd. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

55 COMMENTARY: When Saul saw David go forth against the Philistine, he said to Abner, Whose son is this youth? And Abner said, As thy soul liveth, O king, I cannot tell. The king who had kept David as his harpist now could not name his father. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

56 COMMENTARY: The king said, Enquire thou whose son the stripling is. Saul pressed the question, as if the victor's lineage mattered more than the victory. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

57 COMMENTARY: As David returned from the slaughter, Abner took him and brought him before Saul with the Philistine's head in his hand. The trophy was presented to the king in person. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

58 COMMENTARY: Saul said, Whose son art thou, thou young man? And David answered, I am the son of thy servant Jesse the Bethlehemite. The history is recorded, Henry concludes, that all may exert themselves for God's honour with bold and unshaken reliance on him, going forth with the faith of David against powers of darkness. — Matthew Henry (Concise)
