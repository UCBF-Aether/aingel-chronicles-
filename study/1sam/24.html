Study Notes — 1 Samuel

Chapter 24

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: When Saul was returned from following the Philistines, it was told him, Behold, David is in the wilderness of Engedi. The respite from the invasion ended, and the hunt resumed at once. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

2 COMMENTARY: Saul took three thousand chosen men out of all Israel, and went to seek David and his men upon the rocks of the wild goats. A chosen army went hunting one fugitive band among the cliffs. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

3 COMMENTARY: He came to the sheepcotes by the way, where was a cave, and Saul went in to cover his feet, while David and his men remained in the sides of the cave. God delivered Saul into David's hand, Henry notes; it was an opportunity given to David to exercise faith and patience. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

4 COMMENTARY: David's men said, Behold the day of which the LORD said unto thee, I will deliver thine enemy into thine hand, that thou mayest do to him as it shall seem good. Then David arose and cut off the skirt of Saul's robe privily. He had a promise of the kingdom, Henry observes, but no command to slay the king. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

5 COMMENTARY: Afterward David's heart smote him, because he had cut off Saul's skirt. Even the symbolic cut troubled the conscience that would not touch the king himself. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

6 COMMENTARY: He said to his men, The LORD forbid that I should do this thing unto my master, the LORD's anointed, to stretch forth mine hand against him, seeing he is the anointed of the LORD. He reasoned strongly, Henry says, both with himself and with his men, against doing Saul any hurt; sin is a thing it becomes us to startle at and resist. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

7 COMMENTARY: So David stayed his servants with these words, and suffered them not to rise against Saul, but Saul rose out of the cave and went on his way. He not only would not do this bad thing himself, Henry notes, but would not suffer those about him to do it, thus rendering good for evil to him from whom he received evil for good. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

8 COMMENTARY: David also arose afterward, went out of the cave, and cried after Saul, My lord the king, and when Saul looked behind him, David stooped with his face to the earth and bowed himself. The fugitive came out of hiding to bow before the king he had spared. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

9 COMMENTARY: David said to Saul, Wherefore hearest thou men's words, saying, Behold, David seeketh thy hurt? David was falsely charged with seeking Saul's hurt, Henry observes, and he showed Saul that God's providence had given him opportunity to do it. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

10 COMMENTARY: Behold, this day thine eyes have seen how the LORD delivered thee today into mine hand in the cave, and some bade me kill thee, but mine eye spared thee, and I said, I will not put forth mine hand against my lord, for he is the LORD's anointed. It was upon a good principle that he refused, Henry says: he declared his fixed resolution never to be his own avenger. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

11 COMMENTARY: Moreover, my father, see, yea, see the skirt of thy robe in my hand; for in that I cut off thy skirt and killed thee not, know and see that there is neither evil nor transgression in mine hand, and I have not sinned against thee, yet thou huntest my soul to take it. The skirt was the evidence: he could have taken the life, and took only cloth. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

12 COMMENTARY: The LORD judge between me and thee, and the LORD avenge me of thee, but mine hand shall not be upon thee. If men wrong us, Henry notes, God will right us, at farthest, in the judgment of the great day. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

13 COMMENTARY: As saith the proverb of the ancients, Wickedness proceedeth from the wicked, but mine hand shall not be upon thee. The proverb set David's refusal in the wisdom of the ages. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

14 COMMENTARY: David asked after whom the king of Israel had come out and whom he pursued, answering himself: after a dead dog, after a flea. The king's great hunt was, in David's image, a chase after nothing. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

15 COMMENTARY: The LORD therefore be judge, and judge between me and thee, and see, and plead my cause, and deliver me out of thine hand. David left his cause in God's court and walked away. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

16 COMMENTARY: When David had made an end of speaking, Saul said, Is this thy voice, my son David? And Saul lifted up his voice and wept. Saul spoke as quite overcome with David's kindness, Henry observes. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

17 COMMENTARY: He said, Thou art more righteous than I, for thou hast rewarded me good, whereas I have rewarded thee evil. The confession was exact: good for evil on one side, evil for good on the other. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

18 COMMENTARY: Thou hast showed this day how thou hast dealt well with me, forasmuch as when the LORD delivered me into thine hand, thou killedst me not. The spared life was the proof of the dealing. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

19 COMMENTARY: For if a man find his enemy, will he let him go well away? Wherefore the LORD reward thee good for that thou hast done unto me this day. Saul himself named the strangeness of the mercy. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

20 COMMENTARY: Now I know well that thou shalt surely be king, and that the kingdom of Israel shall be established in thine hand. The king who hunted the successor now confessed the succession. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

21 COMMENTARY: Swear now therefore unto me by the LORD, that thou wilt not cut off my seed after me, and that thou wilt not destroy my name out of my father's house. The confession turned at once to a bargain for his house. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

22 COMMENTARY: David sware unto Saul, and Saul went home, but David and his men gat them up unto the hold. They parted in peace, Henry notes, but Saul went home convinced, not converted — ashamed of his envy, yet retaining the root of bitterness; malice often seems dead when it is only asleep, and will revive with double force. — Matthew Henry (Concise)
