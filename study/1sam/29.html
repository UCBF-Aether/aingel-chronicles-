Study Notes — 1 Samuel

Chapter 29

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: The Philistines gathered all their armies to Aphek, and the Israelites pitched by a fountain in Jezreel. The battle that would end Saul was assembling, with David caught between the two hosts. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

2 COMMENTARY: The lords of the Philistines passed on by hundreds and thousands, but David and his men passed on in the rereward with Achish. David waited with a secret hope that the Lord would help him out of his difficulty, Henry notes, but he seems to have been influenced too much by the fear of man in consenting to attend Achish. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

3 COMMENTARY: Then said the princes of the Philistines, What do these Hebrews here? And Achish said, Is not this David, the servant of Saul king of Israel, who hath been with me these days, or these years, and I have found no fault in him since he fell unto me? Achish vouched for his guest's long fidelity. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

4 COMMENTARY: The princes were wroth and said, Make this fellow return to his place, and let him not go down with us to battle, lest in the battle he be an adversary to us; for wherewith should he reconcile himself unto his master? Should it not be with the heads of these men? God inclined the princes to oppose David's being employed in the battle, Henry observes; thus their dislike befriended him, when no friend could do him such a kindness. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

5 COMMENTARY: Is not this David, of whom they sang one to another in dances, Saul slew his thousands, and David his ten thousands? The old song was quoted against him by the enemy's lords. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

6 COMMENTARY: Then Achish called David and said, Surely, as the LORD liveth, thou hast been upright, and thy going out and coming in with me in the host is good in my sight, for I have not found evil in thee since thou camest unto me; nevertheless the lords favour thee not. David scarcely ever had a greater deliverance, Henry says, than when dismissed from such ensnaring service. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

7 COMMENTARY: Wherefore now return, and go in peace, that thou displease not the lords of the Philistines. The dismissal came as a command wrapped in peace. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

8 COMMENTARY: David said to Achish, But what have I done? And what hast thou found in thy servant so long as I have been with thee, that I may not go fight against the enemies of my lord the king? His protest kept up the appearance of loyalty to Achish. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

9 COMMENTARY: Achish answered, I know that thou art good in my sight, as an angel of God; notwithstanding the princes have said, He shall not go up with us to the battle. God's people should always behave so, Henry counsels, as if possible to get the good word of all they have dealings with, and it is due to those who have acted well to speak well of them. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

10 COMMENTARY: Wherefore now rise up early in the morning with thy master's servants that are come with thee, and as soon as ye be up early and have light, depart. The order was to leave at first light. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

11 COMMENTARY: So David and his men rose up early to depart in the morning, to return into the land of the Philistines, and the Philistines went up to Jezreel. It is hard to come near to the brink of sin and not fall in, Henry warns — and David was pulled back from the brink by the enemy's own suspicion. — Matthew Henry (Concise)
