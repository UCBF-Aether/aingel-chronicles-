Study Notes — 1 Samuel

Chapter 19

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Saul spake to Jonathan his son and to all his servants, that they should kill David. The king's envy, once private, became a standing order to his whole court. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

2 COMMENTARY: But Jonathan delighted much in David, and told him, Saul my father seeketh to kill thee; take heed until the morning, abide in a secret place, and hide thyself. The covenant friend became the warning friend. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

3 COMMENTARY: I will go out and stand beside my father in the field where thou art, and I will commune with my father of thee, and what I see, that I will tell thee. Jonathan would plead David's cause in person before reporting back. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

4 COMMENTARY: Jonathan spake good of David unto Saul, saying, Let not the king sin against his servant David, because he hath not sinned against thee, and his works have been to thee-ward very good. How forcible are right words, Henry observes — Saul was for a time convinced of the unreasonableness of his enmity. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

5 COMMENTARY: For he did put his life in his hand, and slew the Philistine, and the LORD wrought a great salvation for all Israel; thou sawest it and didst rejoice; wherefore then wilt thou sin against innocent blood, to slay David without a cause? Jonathan's argument recalled Saul's own joy at the victory. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

6 COMMENTARY: Saul hearkened unto Jonathan's voice, and sware, As the LORD liveth, he shall not be slain. The oath gave David a reprieve, though not a lasting peace. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

7 COMMENTARY: Jonathan called David, showed him all those things, and brought David to Saul, and he was in his presence as in times past. The reconciliation restored David to court, for a season. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

8 COMMENTARY: There was war again, and David went out, fought the Philistines, slew them with a great slaughter, and they fled from him. Fresh victory renewed the old pattern: triumph, then trouble. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

9 COMMENTARY: The evil spirit from the LORD was upon Saul as he sat in his house with his javelin in his hand, and David played with his hand. The harpist served the king even as the king's hand closed on the javelin. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

10 COMMENTARY: Saul sought to smite David even to the wall with the javelin, but David slipped away out of his presence, and the javelin smote the wall, and David fled and escaped that night. But Saul continued his malice against David, Henry notes; so incurable is the hatred of the serpent's seed, and so deceitful and desperately wicked is the heart of man without God's grace. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

11 COMMENTARY: Saul also sent messengers to David's house, to watch him and slay him in the morning, and Michal his wife told him, If thou save not thy life tonight, tomorrow thou shalt be slain. The king's daughter warned the husband her father hunted. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

12 COMMENTARY: So Michal let David down through a window, and he went and fled and escaped. The escape was by night, through a window, like a fugitive's flight. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

13 COMMENTARY: Michal took an image, laid it in the bed, put a pillow of goats' hair for his bolster, and covered it with a cloth. The household image in the bed kept the watchers deceived. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

14 COMMENTARY: When Saul sent messengers to take David, she said, He is sick. Michal's stratagem gained time for David to get to a distance, Henry notes, and was allowable in its purpose, but her falsehood had not even the plea of necessity to excuse it. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

15 COMMENTARY: Saul sent the messengers again to see David, saying, Bring him up to me in the bed, that I may slay him. The king would kill even a sick man in his bed. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

16 COMMENTARY: When the messengers came in, behold, there was an image in the bed, with a pillow of goats' hair for his bolster. The deception was exposed, and the prey was gone. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

17 COMMENTARY: Saul said to Michal, Why hast thou deceived me so, and sent away mine enemy, that he is escaped? And Michal answered, He said unto me, Let me go; why should I kill thee? Her falsehood, Henry observes, manifests that she was not influenced by the same spirit of piety which had dictated Jonathan's language to Saul. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

18 COMMENTARY: So David fled and escaped, and came to Samuel to Ramah, and told him all Saul had done to him, and he and Samuel dwelt in Naioth. In flying to Samuel, Henry says, David made God his refuge; Samuel as prophet was best able to advise him in this day of distress. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

19 COMMENTARY: It was told Saul, Behold, David is at Naioth in Ramah. The fugitive's refuge was soon reported to the king. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

20 COMMENTARY: Saul sent messengers to take David, and when they saw the company of prophets prophesying, with Samuel standing as appointed over them, the Spirit of God was upon Saul's messengers, and they also prophesied. The arresting party was arrested by the Spirit instead. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

21 COMMENTARY: When it was told Saul, he sent other messengers, and they prophesied likewise, and Saul sent messengers a third time, and they prophesied also. Three companies in succession were turned from hunters into prophets. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

22 COMMENTARY: Then Saul himself went to Ramah, and came to a great well in Sechu, and asked, Where are Samuel and David? And one said, Behold, they be at Naioth in Ramah. So impatient was Saul after David's blood, Henry notes, so restless against him, that although baffled by one providence after another, he could not see that David was under God's special protection. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

23 COMMENTARY: He went to Naioth in Ramah, and the Spirit of God was upon him also, and he went on and prophesied until he came to Naioth. The hunter himself was overtaken by the Spirit on the road. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

24 COMMENTARY: He stripped off his clothes also, and prophesied before Samuel, and lay down naked all that day and night. Wherefore they say, Is Saul also among the prophets? When God will protect David this way, Henry observes, even Saul prophesies; many have great gifts yet no grace, and may prophesy in Christ's name yet be disowned by him. — Matthew Henry (Concise)
