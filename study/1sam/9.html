Study Notes — 1 Samuel

Chapter 9

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: There was a man of Benjamin named Kish, a mighty man of power, whose family line is given in full. From this ordinary Benjamite house God would raise Israel's first king. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

2 COMMENTARY: Kish had a son named Saul, a choice young man, goodly beyond all Israel, taller from the shoulders up than any of the people. The people's king would first commend himself to their eyes. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

3 COMMENTARY: Kish's asses were lost, and he told Saul to take a servant and go seek them. Saul readily went, and his obedience to his father was praiseworthy, Henry notes. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

4 COMMENTARY: He passed through Mount Ephraim, Shalisha, and Shalim, and through Benjamin, but found them not. The long, fruitless search carried him steadily toward Ramah and Samuel. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

5 COMMENTARY: In the land of Zuph, Saul said to his servant, let us return, lest his father stop caring for the asses and start worrying for them. Saul's thoughtfulness for his father shows the same obedient character. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

6 COMMENTARY: The servant said, there is in this city a man of God, an honourable man whose every word comes to pass; let us go to him, and perhaps he can show us our way. Wherever we are, Henry counsels, we should use our opportunities of acquainting ourselves with those who are wise and good. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

7 COMMENTARY: Saul asked what they could bring the man, since their bread was spent and they had no present for the man of God. Many, Henry observes, will consult a man of God if he comes in their way who would not go a step out of their way to get wisdom. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

8 COMMENTARY: The servant had at hand the fourth part of a shekel of silver, which he would give the man of God to tell them their way. Samuel needed not their money, Henry notes, nor would he have denied advice without it; the gift was a token of respect for his office, according to the usage of those times. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

9 COMMENTARY: Beforetime in Israel, one who went to enquire of God said, Come, let us go to the seer, for he that is now called a Prophet was beforetime called a Seer. The note explains the older name for the office Samuel held. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

10 COMMENTARY: Saul said, Well said, come, let us go, and they went to the city where the man of God was. A small errand and a servant's suggestion were turning Saul's steps toward his destiny. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

11 COMMENTARY: As they went up the hill to the city, they found young maidens going out to draw water and asked, Is the seer here? Even the maid-servants of the city could direct them to the prophet — it is no small benefit, Henry says, to live in religious and holy places, and we should always be ready to help those seeking God's prophets. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

12 COMMENTARY: The maidens answered that he was before them and to make haste, for he had come that day to the city because there was a sacrifice of the people that day in the high place. They had heard of the sacrifice and could tell of the necessity for Samuel's presence. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

13 COMMENTARY: As soon as they entered the city they would find him before he went up to eat, for the people would not eat until he blessed the sacrifice, and afterwards those bidden would eat. The maidens' directions were exact, and Saul was to hurry. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

14 COMMENTARY: They went up into the city, and as they entered, Samuel came out against them to go up to the high place. The seeker and the prophet met at the very gate, by God's ordering. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

15 COMMENTARY: Now the LORD had told Samuel in his ear a day before Saul came. God had prepared his prophet before the king arrived. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

16 COMMENTARY: Tomorrow about this time, God said, I will send thee a man out of Benjamin, and thou shalt anoint him captain over my people Israel, that he may save them from the Philistines, for I have looked upon my people because their cry is come unto me. Though God had granted Israel's request in displeasure, Henry notes, he still sent them a man to save them from the Philistines, listening graciously to their cry. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

17 COMMENTARY: When Samuel saw Saul, the LORD said, Behold the man whom I spake to thee of! This same shall reign over my people. The sign and the man arrived together. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

18 COMMENTARY: Saul drew near to Samuel in the gate and asked where the seer's house was, not knowing he spoke to the seer himself. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

19 COMMENTARY: Samuel answered, I am the seer; go up before me to the high place, for ye shall eat with me today, and tomorrow I will let thee go and tell thee all that is in thine heart. Samuel, so far from envying Saul, Henry observes, was the first and most forward to do him honour. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

20 COMMENTARY: As for the asses lost three days ago, set not thy mind on them, for they are found; and on whom is all the desire of Israel? Is it not on thee and on thy father's house? The lost asses were already found, and a far greater matter — the desire of Israel — now rested on Saul. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

21 COMMENTARY: Saul answered, Am not I a Benjamite, of the smallest tribe, and my family the least of Benjamin's families? Wherefore speakest thou so to me? His humility at the word was genuine; the smallest tribe's least family was chosen. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

22 COMMENTARY: Samuel took Saul and his servant into the parlour and set them in the chiefest place among about thirty bidden guests. Honour was publicly given before the anointing was explained. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

23 COMMENTARY: Samuel told the cook to bring the portion he had set by, the portion reserved for Saul. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

24 COMMENTARY: The cook set the shoulder before Saul, and Samuel said, Behold that which is left, set it before thee and eat, for it has been kept for thee since I said I had invited the people. So Saul ate with Samuel that day, the reserved portion marking him out. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

25 COMMENTARY: When they came down from the high place into the city, Samuel communed with Saul upon the top of the house. That evening, and early the next morning, Samuel communed with Saul on the flat roof, Henry notes, convincing him that he was the person God had fixed upon for the government. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

26 COMMENTARY: They arose early, and about the spring of the day Samuel called Saul to the top of the house, saying, Up, that I may send thee away, and they went out together. How different are the Lord's purposes for us from our own intentions, Henry reflects: Saul went out to seek asses and literally found a kingdom. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

27 COMMENTARY: As they went down to the end of the city, Samuel told Saul to bid the servant pass on, but to stand still a while, that he might show him the word of God. The private word would come only after the servant had gone ahead. — Matthew Henry (Concise)
