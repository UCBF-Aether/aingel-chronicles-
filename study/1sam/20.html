Study Notes — 1 Samuel

Chapter 20

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: David fled from Naioth in Ramah and came and said before Jonathan, What have I done? What is mine iniquity, and what is my sin before thy father, that he seeketh my life? The trials David met with, Henry notes, prepared him for future advancement; the Lord leads those he prepares for glory to the kingdom through much tribulation, which fits them for it. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

2 COMMENTARY: Jonathan said, God forbid; thou shalt not die: behold, my father will do nothing great or small but that he will show it me, and why should he hide this from me? It is not so. Jonathan could not yet believe his father's murderous intent. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

3 COMMENTARY: David sware moreover, Thy father certainly knoweth I have found grace in thine eyes, and he saith, Let not Jonathan know this, lest he be grieved; but truly as the LORD liveth, and as thy soul liveth, there is but a step between me and death. Sometimes it appears there is but a step between us and death, Henry reflects, and at all times it may be so, and we should prepare for the event — yet we cannot die until God's purpose concerning us is accomplished. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

4 COMMENTARY: Then said Jonathan unto David, Whatsoever thy soul desireth, I will even do it for thee. Jonathan generously offered David his services, Henry says; this is true friendship. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

5 COMMENTARY: David said, Behold, tomorrow is the new moon, and I should not fail to sit with the king at meat; but let me go, that I may hide myself in the field unto the third day at even. The new moon feast would test Saul's temper by David's absence. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

6 COMMENTARY: If thy father at all miss me, then say, David earnestly asked leave of me to run to Bethlehem his city, for there is a yearly sacrifice there for all the family. The Bethlehem excuse would explain the empty seat without revealing the flight. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

7 COMMENTARY: If he say, It is well, thy servant shall have peace; but if he be very wroth, then be sure that evil is determined by him. Saul's reaction to the excuse would be the sign David needed. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

8 COMMENTARY: Therefore thou shalt deal kindly with thy servant, for thou hast brought thy servant into a covenant of the LORD with thee; notwithstanding, if there be iniquity in me, slay me thyself, for why shouldest thou bring me to thy father? David appealed to the covenant and to Jonathan's own hand rather than Saul's. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

9 COMMENTARY: Jonathan said, Far be it from thee; for if I knew certainly that evil were determined by my father to come upon thee, then would not I tell it thee? The friend pledged full disclosure. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

10 COMMENTARY: Then said David, Who shall tell me? Or what if thy father answer thee roughly? David needed a messenger and a signal that could survive a rough answer. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

11 COMMENTARY: Jonathan said, Come, let us go out into the field, and they went out both into the field. The covenant was renewed away from listening ears. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

12 COMMENTARY: Jonathan said, O LORD God of Israel, when I have sounded my father about tomorrow or the third day, and behold, if there be good toward David, and I then send not unto thee and show it thee — the oath began, binding Jonathan to report truly. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

13 COMMENTARY: The LORD do so and much more to Jonathan, but if it please my father to do thee evil, then I will show it thee, and send thee away, that thou mayest go in peace, and the LORD be with thee, as he hath been with my father. Jonathan faithfully promised to let David know how he found his father affected toward him, Henry notes. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

14 COMMENTARY: And thou shalt not only while yet I live show me the kindness of the LORD, that I die not — Jonathan asked David's kindness for his own life in the days to come. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

15 COMMENTARY: But also thou shalt not cut off thy kindness from my house for ever, no, not when the LORD hath cut off the enemies of David every one from the face of the earth. It will be kindness to ourselves, Henry observes, to secure an interest in those whom God favours, and to make his friends ours. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

16 COMMENTARY: So Jonathan made a covenant with the house of David, saying, Let the LORD even require it at the hand of David's enemies. The covenant reached beyond the two friends to their houses. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

17 COMMENTARY: Jonathan caused David to swear again, because he loved him, for he loved him as he loved his own soul. True friendship, Henry says, rests on a firm basis and is able to silence ambition, self-love, and undue regard for others. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

18 COMMENTARY: Then Jonathan said, Tomorrow is the new moon, and thou shalt be missed, because thy seat will be empty. The empty seat was the first part of the signal. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

19 COMMENTARY: When thou hast stayed three days, then go down quickly and come to the place where thou didst hide thyself when the business was in hand, and remain by the stone Ezel. The old hiding place became the appointed meeting ground. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

20 COMMENTARY: I will shoot three arrows on the side thereof, as though I shot at a mark. The archery would carry the message without a word. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

21 COMMENTARY: Behold, I will send a lad, saying, Go, find out the arrows; if I say, Behold, the arrows are on this side of thee, take them, then come thou, for there is peace to thee and no hurt, as the LORD liveth. The arrows on this side meant safety. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

22 COMMENTARY: But if I say to the young man, Behold, the arrows are beyond thee, go thy way, for the LORD hath sent thee away. Arrows beyond meant flight. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

23 COMMENTARY: As touching the matter which thou and I have spoken of, behold, the LORD be between thee and me for ever. The LORD himself was made the covenant's witness and keeper. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

24 COMMENTARY: So David hid himself in the field, and when the new moon was come, the king sat down to eat meat. The feast began with David already hidden. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

25 COMMENTARY: The king sat on his seat as at other times, by the wall, and Jonathan arose, and Abner sat by Saul's side, and David's place was empty. None were more constant than David in attending holy duties, Henry notes, nor had he been absent but that self-preservation obliged him to withdraw. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

26 COMMENTARY: Nevertheless Saul spoke not anything that day, for he thought, Something hath befallen him, he is not clean; surely he is not clean. The first day's silence was explained away as uncleanness. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

27 COMMENTARY: On the morrow, the second day of the month, David's place was empty, and Saul said to Jonathan, Wherefore cometh not the son of Jesse to meat, neither yesterday nor today? The second absence forced the question. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

28 COMMENTARY: Jonathan answered, David earnestly asked leave of me to go to Bethlehem. The agreed excuse was given. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

29 COMMENTARY: He said, Let me go, I pray thee, for our family hath a sacrifice in the city, and my brother hath commanded me to be there; and now, if I have found favour in thine eyes, let me get away and see my brethren. Therefore he cometh not to the king's table. The family sacrifice explained the absence fully. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

30 COMMENTARY: Then Saul's anger was kindled against Jonathan, and he said, Thou son of the perverse rebellious woman, do not I know that thou hast chosen the son of Jesse to thine own confusion and thy mother's shame? Jonathan did wisely and well for himself and family to secure an interest in David, Henry observes, yet for this he was blamed. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

31 COMMENTARY: For as long as the son of Jesse liveth upon the ground, thou shalt not be established, nor thy kingdom; wherefore now send and fetch him unto me, for he shall surely die. Saul named the dynastic fear plainly: David alive meant Jonathan uncrowned. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

32 COMMENTARY: Jonathan answered his father, Wherefore shall he be slain? What hath he done? The son's question exposed the murder as causeless. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

33 COMMENTARY: Saul cast a javelin at him to smite him, whereby Jonathan knew it was determined of his father to slay David. Saul was outrageous, Henry says; what savage beasts, and worse, does anger make men! — Matthew Henry (Concise)

34 COMMENTARY: So Jonathan arose from the table in fierce anger and ate no meat the second day of the month, for he was grieved for David, because his father had done him shame. The feast ended in grief and an empty stomach. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

35 COMMENTARY: In the morning Jonathan went out into the field at the time appointed with David, and a little lad with him. The signal was to be given at dawn. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

36 COMMENTARY: He said to his lad, Run, find out now the arrows which I shoot, and as the lad ran, he shot an arrow beyond him. The arrow flew beyond the boy — the sign of flight. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

37 COMMENTARY: When the lad came to the arrow's place, Jonathan cried after him, Is not the arrow beyond thee? The coded message was spoken aloud for David to hear. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

38 COMMENTARY: Jonathan cried after the lad, Make speed, haste, stay not, and Jonathan's lad gathered up the arrows and came to his master. The urgency in the cry was itself part of the message. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

39 COMMENTARY: But the lad knew not anything; only Jonathan and David knew the matter. The innocent messenger carried the signal without understanding it. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

40 COMMENTARY: Jonathan gave his artillery to his lad and said, Go, carry them to the city. The boy was sent away so the friends could meet alone. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

41 COMMENTARY: As soon as the lad was gone, David arose out of a place toward the south, fell on his face to the ground, bowed himself three times, and they kissed one another and wept together, until David exceeded. The separation of two such faithful friends was grievous to both, Henry notes, but David's case was more deplorable, for David was leaving all his comforts, even those of God's sanctuary. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

42 COMMENTARY: Jonathan said to David, Go in peace, forasmuch as we have sworn both of us in the name of the LORD, saying, The LORD be between me and thee, and between my seed and thy seed for ever. And he arose and departed, and Jonathan went into the city. Christians need not sorrow as men without hope, Henry concludes; being one with Christ, they are one with each other, and will meet in his presence ere long, to part no more. — Matthew Henry (Concise)
