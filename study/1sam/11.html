Study Notes — 1 Samuel

Chapter 11

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Nahash the Ammonite came up and encamped against Jabesh-gilead, and the men of Jabesh asked for a covenant, offering to serve him. The first fruit of Saul's government, Henry notes, would be the rescue of this besieged city. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

2 COMMENTARY: Nahash answered that he would covenant only on condition of thrusting out all their right eyes, as a reproach upon all Israel. To save their lives, Henry observes, men will part with liberty and even consent to have their eyes put out. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

3 COMMENTARY: The elders of Jabesh asked seven days' respite to send messengers throughout Israel, promising to come out if no man saved them. The week of grace bought time for the very deliverance Nahash scorned. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

4 COMMENTARY: The messengers came to Gibeah of Saul and told the tidings, and all the people lifted up their voices and wept. The city's danger became the nation's grief. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

5 COMMENTARY: Behold, Saul came after the herd out of the field and asked what ailed the people that they wept, and they told him the tidings of Jabesh. The king was still at his farm work when the call came. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

6 COMMENTARY: The Spirit of God came upon Saul when he heard the tidings, and his anger was kindled greatly. See the faith and confidence of Saul, Henry says, and grounded thereon his courage and resolution. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

7 COMMENTARY: He took a yoke of oxen, hewed them in pieces, and sent them throughout Israel, saying, Whosoever cometh not forth after Saul and after Samuel, so shall it be done unto his oxen. The fear of the LORD fell on the people, and they came out with one consent. When the Spirit of the Lord comes upon men, Henry notes, it makes them expert even without experience, and when zeal for God's glory and love for the brethren urge earnest efforts, great effects may speedily be produced. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

8 COMMENTARY: When he numbered them in Bezek, Israel were three hundred thousand and Judah thirty thousand. The muster proved the summons had worked across the whole land. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

9 COMMENTARY: They told Jabesh's messengers: tomorrow, by the time the sun is hot, ye shall have help. The messengers showed it to Jabesh, and they were glad. Help was promised before the battle was fought. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

10 COMMENTARY: Therefore the men of Jabesh told the Ammonites, Tomorrow we will come out unto you, and ye shall do with us all that seemeth good. Their bold message masked the coming relief. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

11 COMMENTARY: On the morrow Saul put the people in three companies, came into the midst of the host in the morning watch, and slew the Ammonites until the heat of the day, scattering the remnant so that two were not left together. See Saul's activity in this business, Henry says: with God pleased to help, great effects were speedily produced. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

12 COMMENTARY: The people said to Samuel, Who is he that said, Shall Saul reign over us? Bring the men, that we may put them to death. Victory made the people eager to punish Saul's former despisers. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

13 COMMENTARY: Saul said, There shall not a man be put to death this day, for today the LORD hath wrought salvation in Israel. They now honoured Saul whom they had despised, Henry notes, and if an enemy be made a friend, that is more to our advantage than to have him slain. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

14 COMMENTARY: Then Samuel said to the people, Come, let us go to Gilgal and renew the kingdom there. The kingship, proved in battle and sealed by mercy, would be publicly renewed. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

15 COMMENTARY: All the people went to Gilgal, made Saul king before the LORD, sacrificed peace offerings, and Saul and all Israel rejoiced greatly. The once-despised Saviour, Henry reflects, will at length be acknowledged by all as the Lord's own anointed king. — Matthew Henry (Concise)
