Study Notes — 1 Samuel

Chapter 31

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: The Philistines fought against Israel, and the men of Israel fled before the Philistines and fell down slain in Mount Gilboa. The doom pronounced at Endor fell on the mountain where Saul had pitched. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

2 COMMENTARY: The Philistines followed hard upon Saul and his sons, and slew Jonathan, Abinadab, and Melchishua, Saul's sons. The king's house fell with the king's army; Jonathan, the covenant friend, died in his father's battle. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

3 COMMENTARY: The battle went sore against Saul, and the archers hit him, and he was sore wounded of the archers. The wounded king could neither resist nor flee. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

4 COMMENTARY: Saul told his armourbearer to thrust him through lest the uncircumcised abuse him, but the armourbearer would not for fear, so Saul took a sword and fell upon it. Saul, sorely wounded, expressed no concern about his never-dying soul, Henry observes, but only desired that the Philistines might not insult him or put him to pain. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

5 COMMENTARY: When his armourbearer saw that Saul was dead, he fell likewise upon his sword and died with him. The servant followed his master even in self-destruction. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

6 COMMENTARY: So Saul died, and his three sons, and his armourbearer, and all his men, that same day together. We cannot judge of the spiritual or eternal state of any by the manner of their death, Henry warns, for in that there is one event to the righteous and to the wicked. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

7 COMMENTARY: When the men of Israel on the other side of the valley and beyond Jordan saw that Israel fled and that Saul and his sons were dead, they forsook the cities and fled, and the Philistines came and dwelt in them. The kingdom emptied before the conquerors. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

8 COMMENTARY: On the morrow, when the Philistines came to strip the slain, they found Saul and his three sons fallen in Mount Gilboa. The strippers found the king among the stripped. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

9 COMMENTARY: They cut off his head, stripped off his armour, and sent into the land of the Philistines round about, to publish it in the house of their idols and among the people. The defeat was proclaimed as a triumph of Philistine gods. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

10 COMMENTARY: They put his armour in the house of Ashtaroth, and fastened his body to the wall of Bethshan. The king's armour became a temple trophy, and his body a wall display. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

11 COMMENTARY: When the inhabitants of Jabesh-gilead heard of what the Philistines had done to Saul — the city Saul had delivered in the first fruit of his reign now heard of his disgrace. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

12 COMMENTARY: All the valiant men arose, went all night, took the body of Saul and the bodies of his sons from the wall of Bethshan, and came to Jabesh, and burnt them there. The men of the rescued city risked a night march to rescue the fallen king's body. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

13 COMMENTARY: They took their bones, buried them under a tree at Jabesh, and fasted seven days. Scripture makes no mention of what became of the souls of Saul and his sons after death, Henry notes, but of their bodies only, and secret things belong not to us. — Matthew Henry (Concise)
