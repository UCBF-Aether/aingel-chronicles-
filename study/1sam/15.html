Study Notes — 1 Samuel

Chapter 15

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Samuel said to Saul, The LORD sent me to anoint thee king over Israel; now therefore hearken unto the voice of the words of the LORD. The command that follows comes with the full authority of the anointing itself. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

2 COMMENTARY: Thus saith the LORD of hosts, I remember what Amalek did to Israel, laying wait for him in the way when he came up from Egypt. The sentence against Amalek had gone forth long before, Henry notes, but they had been spared until they filled up the measure of their sins. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

3 COMMENTARY: Now go and smite Amalek and utterly destroy all they have, sparing nothing, man and woman, infant and suckling, ox and sheep, camel and ass. The commandment was express, Henry observes, and a test of Saul's obedience. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

4 COMMENTARY: Saul gathered and numbered the people in Telaim, two hundred thousand footmen and ten thousand men of Judah. A great host was mustered for a total destruction. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

5 COMMENTARY: Saul came to a city of Amalek and laid wait in the valley, setting the ambush in the enemy's own manner. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

6 COMMENTARY: Saul told the Kenites to depart from among the Amalekites, lest they be destroyed with them, for they had shown kindness to Israel coming up from Egypt, and the Kenites departed. Remembering the Kenites' ancestors' kindness amid punishment of Amalek's ancestors' injuries, Henry says, tended to clear the righteousness of God in this dispensation; and it is dangerous to be found in the company of God's enemies — we must come out from among them lest we share their sins and plagues. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

7 COMMENTARY: Saul smote the Amalekites from Havilah to Shur, over against Egypt. The campaign swept the whole Amalekite range. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

8 COMMENTARY: He took Agag king of the Amalekites alive, and utterly destroyed all the people with the sword. The king alone was spared from the people. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

9 COMMENTARY: But Saul and the people spared Agag and the best sheep, oxen, fatlings, lambs, and all that was good, destroying utterly only what was vile and refuse. His conduct, Henry says, was evidently the effect of a proud, rebellious spirit: he destroyed only the refuse, that which was good for little. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

10 COMMENTARY: Then came the word of the LORD unto Samuel. The prophet, not the king, first heard God's verdict on the campaign. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

11 COMMENTARY: It repenteth me that I have set up Saul to be king, for he is turned back from following me and hath not performed my commandments, and it grieved Samuel, and he cried unto the LORD all night. Repentance in God, Henry explains, is not a change of mind as in us, but a change of method; the change was in Saul — he is turned back from following me — and thereby he made God his enemy. Samuel spent a whole night pleading for Saul, for the rejection of sinners is the grief of believers. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

12 COMMENTARY: When Samuel rose early to meet Saul, he was told Saul had come to Carmel, set himself up a place, and gone down to Gilgal. The king was already erecting his own monument before facing the prophet. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

13 COMMENTARY: Samuel came to Saul, and Saul said, Blessed be thou of the LORD: I have performed the commandment of the LORD. Saul boasted to Samuel of his obedience, Henry notes; thus sinners think, by justifying themselves, to escape being judged of the Lord. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

14 COMMENTARY: Samuel said, What meaneth then this bleating of sheep in mine ears, and the lowing of oxen which I hear? The noise the cattle made, like the rust of silver, witnessed against him. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

15 COMMENTARY: Saul said, They have brought them from the Amalekites, for the people spared the best sheep and oxen to sacrifice unto the LORD thy God, and the rest we have utterly destroyed. Many boast of obedience, Henry asks, but what mean their indulgence of the flesh, love of the world, and neglect of holy duties, which witness against them? See of what evil covetousness is the root. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

16 COMMENTARY: Samuel said, Stay, and I will tell thee what the LORD hath said to me this night, and Saul said, Say on. The king would hear the night's word, though it condemned him. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

17 COMMENTARY: Samuel said, When thou wast little in thine own sight, wast thou not made head of the tribes of Israel, and the LORD anointed thee king over Israel? His rise had come in his lowliness; his fall came with his pride. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

18 COMMENTARY: The LORD sent thee on a journey and said, Go and utterly destroy the sinners the Amalekites, and fight against them until they be consumed. The command left no room for the king's amendments. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

19 COMMENTARY: Wherefore then didst thou not obey the voice of the LORD, but didst fly upon the spoil, and didst evil in the sight of the LORD? The sinfulness of sin, Henry says, lies above all in this: it is disobedience — Thou didst not obey the voice of the Lord. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

20 COMMENTARY: Saul said, Yea, I have obeyed the voice of the LORD, and have gone the way he sent me, and have brought Agag, and have utterly destroyed the Amalekites. Carnal, deceitful hearts, Henry observes, think to excuse themselves from God's commandments by what pleases themselves. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

21 COMMENTARY: But the people took of the spoil, sheep and oxen, the chief of the things which should have been utterly destroyed, to sacrifice unto the LORD thy God in Gilgal. Blame shifted from king to people, and disobedience dressed itself as worship. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

22 COMMENTARY: Samuel said, Hath the LORD as great delight in burnt offerings and sacrifices, as in obeying the voice of the LORD? Behold, to obey is better than sacrifice, and to hearken than the fat of rams. Humble, sincere, conscientious obedience, Henry says, is more pleasing to God than all burnt-offering and sacrifice; God is more glorified and self more denied by obedience than by sacrifice. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

23 COMMENTARY: For rebellion is as the sin of witchcraft, and stubbornness as iniquity and idolatry. Because thou hast rejected the word of the LORD, he hath also rejected thee from being king. Those unfit to be ruled by God, Henry notes, are unfit and unworthy to rule over men. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

24 COMMENTARY: Saul said, I have sinned, for I have transgressed the commandment of the LORD and thy words, because I feared the people and obeyed their voice. The confession came, but with its excuse attached. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

25 COMMENTARY: Now therefore, I pray thee, pardon my sin, and turn again with me, that I may worship the LORD. His plea sought Samuel's company more than God's pardon. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

26 COMMENTARY: Samuel said, I will not return with thee, for thou hast rejected the word of the LORD, and the LORD hath rejected thee from being king over Israel. The sentence stood. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

27 COMMENTARY: As Samuel turned to go away, Saul laid hold upon the skirt of his mantle, and it rent. The torn mantle acted the torn kingdom. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

28 COMMENTARY: Samuel said, The LORD hath rent the kingdom of Israel from thee this day, and hath given it to a neighbour of thine, that is better than thou. The rent cloth became the prophecy. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

29 COMMENTARY: And also the Strength of Israel will not lie nor repent, for he is not a man, that he should repent. Men are fickle and alter their minds, Henry notes, but with God it is not so: the Strength of Israel will not lie. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

30 COMMENTARY: Then he said, I have sinned: yet honour me now, I pray thee, before the elders of my people and before Israel, and turn again with me, that I may worship the LORD thy God. There were several signs of hypocrisy in Saul's repentance, Henry lists: he besought Samuel only, anxious to stand right in Samuel's opinion; he excused his fault even while confessing it, which is never the way of a true penitent; and all his care was to save his credit and preserve his interest in the people. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

31 COMMENTARY: So Samuel turned again after Saul, and Saul worshipped the LORD. The prophet granted the public worship, not a reversal of the sentence. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

32 COMMENTARY: Then said Samuel, Bring ye hither to me Agag king of the Amalekites, and Agag came unto him delicately, and said, Surely the bitterness of death is past. Many think the bitterness of death is past when it is not gone by, Henry warns; they put that evil day far from them, which is very near. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

33 COMMENTARY: Samuel said, As thy sword hath made women childless, so shall thy mother be childless among women, and Samuel hewed Agag in pieces before the LORD in Gilgal. Samuel called Agag to account for his own sins, following the example of his ancestors' cruelty, and justly was the righteous blood shed by Amalek required. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

34 COMMENTARY: Then Samuel went to Ramah, and Saul went up to his house to Gibeah of Saul. Prophet and king parted for their separate houses. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

35 COMMENTARY: Samuel came no more to see Saul until the day of his death; nevertheless Samuel mourned for Saul, and the LORD repented that he had made Saul king over Israel. Saul seemed unconcerned at the token of God's displeasure, Henry observes, yet Samuel mourned day and night for him — as Jerusalem was carnally secure while Christ wept over it. — Matthew Henry (Concise)
