Study Notes — 1 Samuel

Chapter 21

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: David came to Nob to Ahimelech the priest, and Ahimelech was afraid at meeting David, asking, Why art thou alone, and no man with thee? David in distress fled to the tabernacle of God, Henry notes; it is great comfort in a day of trouble to have a God to go to, to whom we may open our cases and from whom we may ask direction. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

2 COMMENTARY: David said to Ahimelech, The king hath commanded me a business, and said, Let no man know anything of the business, and I have appointed my servants to such and such a place. David told Ahimelech a gross untruth, Henry says plainly: Scripture does not conceal it, and we dare not justify it; it was ill done and proved of bad consequence, occasioning the death of the priests of the Lord. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

3 COMMENTARY: Now therefore what is under thine hand? Give me five loaves of bread, or what there is present. David asked the priest for bread and, next, for a sword — the fugitive's two needs. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

4 COMMENTARY: The priest answered, There is no common bread under mine hand, but there is hallowed bread, if the young men have kept themselves at least from women. Only the shewbread was available, and it came with a condition of cleanness. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

5 COMMENTARY: David answered that women had been kept from them about three days, and the vessels of the young men were holy, and the bread was in a manner common, though sanctified that day in the vessel. The priest's scruple was met with an assurance of cleanness. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

6 COMMENTARY: So the priest gave him hallowed bread, for there was no bread there but the shewbread taken from before the LORD to put hot bread in its place. Ahimelech supposed they might eat the shewbread, Henry notes, and the Son of David taught from it that mercy is to be preferred to sacrifice, and ritual observances must give way to moral duties. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

7 COMMENTARY: Now a certain man of Saul's servants was there that day, detained before the LORD: Doeg, an Edomite, chiefest of Saul's herdmen. Doeg set his foot as far within the tabernacle as David did, Henry observes; we little know with what hearts people come to God's house, nor what use they will make of pretended devotion — only God and the event can distinguish a David from a Doeg. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

8 COMMENTARY: David said to Ahimelech, Is there not here spear or sword? For I have neither brought my sword nor weapons, because the king's business required haste. The lie was extended to explain his weaponless state. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

9 COMMENTARY: The priest said, The sword of Goliath, whom thou slewest in the valley of Elah, is here wrapped in a cloth behind the ephod; if thou wilt take that, take it, for there is no other. And David said, There is none like that; give it me. The giant's sword, kept as a trophy behind the ephod, became the fugitive's weapon. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

10 COMMENTARY: David arose and fled that day for fear of Saul, and went to Achish king of Gath. God's persecuted people, Henry notes, have often found better usage from Philistines than from Israelites. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

11 COMMENTARY: Achish's servants said, Is not this David the king of the land? Did they not sing of him in dances, Saul hath slain his thousands, and David his ten thousands? The song that had angered Saul now endangered David in Gath. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

12 COMMENTARY: David laid up these words in his heart, and was sore afraid of Achish king of Gath. David had reason to put confidence in Achish, Henry says, yet he began to be afraid, and his conduct discovered wavering in his faith and courage. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

13 COMMENTARY: He changed his behaviour before them, feigned himself mad in their hands, scrabbled on the doors of the gate, and let his spittle fall down upon his beard. His conduct was degrading, Henry observes; David had great faith and courage, yet both failed him through fear and the weakness of his faith. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

14 COMMENTARY: Then said Achish to his servants, Lo, ye see the man is mad; wherefore then have ye brought him to me? The feigned madness worked on the king's contempt. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

15 COMMENTARY: Have I need of mad men, that ye have brought this fellow to play the mad man in my presence? Shall this fellow come into my house? The more simply we depend on God and obey him, Henry concludes, the more comfortably and surely we shall walk through this troublesome world; David's sorry shift is written for our warning, not for imitation, even in the greatest straits. — Matthew Henry (Concise)
