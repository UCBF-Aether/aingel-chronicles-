Study Notes — 1 Samuel

Chapter 27

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: David said in his heart, I shall now perish one day by the hand of Saul; there is nothing better for me than that I should speedily escape into the land of the Philistines, and Saul shall despair of seeking me any more in Israel, so shall I escape out of his hand. Unbelief is a sin that easily besets even good men, Henry observes, when without are fightings and within are fears; Lord, increase our faith! — Matthew Henry (Concise)

2 COMMENTARY: David arose and passed over with the six hundred men that were with him unto Achish son of Maoch, king of Gath. The fugitive crossed the border with his whole band. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

3 COMMENTARY: David dwelt with Achish at Gath, he and his men, every man with his household, even David with his two wives, Ahinoam the Jezreelitess and Abigail the Carmelitess, Nabal's wife. We may blush to think, Henry says, that the word of a Philistine should go further than the word of an Israelite, and that Gath should be a refuge for a good man when Israel's cities refused him safe abode. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

4 COMMENTARY: It was told Saul that David was fled to Gath, and he sought no more again for him. The flight across the border ended the hunt at last. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

5 COMMENTARY: David said to Achish, If I have now found grace in thine eyes, let them give me a place in some town in the country, that I may dwell there; for why should thy servant dwell in the royal city with thee? A separate town would free both guest and host from the court's watchfulness. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

6 COMMENTARY: Then Achish gave him Ziklag that day; wherefore Ziklag pertaineth unto the kings of Judah unto this day. David gained a comfortable settlement, Henry notes, not only at a distance from Gath but bordering upon Israel, where he might keep up correspondence with his own countrymen. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

7 COMMENTARY: The time that David dwelt in the country of the Philistines was a full year and four months. Sixteen months of exile in the enemy's land. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

8 COMMENTARY: David and his men went up and invaded the Geshurites, the Gezrites, and the Amalekites, for those nations were of old the inhabitants of the land, as thou goest to Shur, even unto Egypt. While David was in Philistine land, Henry notes, he attacked some remains of the devoted nations; the people he cut off were long before doomed to destruction. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

9 COMMENTARY: David smote the land and left neither man nor woman alive, and took away sheep, oxen, asses, camels, and apparel, and returned and came to Achish. It is often wisdom to shun public notice, Henry observes, but we must in no situation be idle; we must always try to do somewhat in God's cause. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

10 COMMENTARY: Achish said, Whither have ye made a road today? And David said, Against the south of Judah, and against the south of the Jerahmeelites, and against the south of the Kenites. David's answer pointed his raids at Judah's own neighbours, concealing their true targets. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

11 COMMENTARY: David saved neither man nor woman alive, to bring tidings to Gath, saying, Lest they should tell on us, saying, So did David, and so will be his manner all the while he dwelleth in the country of the Philistines. No witness survived to correct the story Achish was told. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

12 COMMENTARY: Achish believed David, saying, He hath made his people Israel utterly to abhor him; therefore he shall be my servant for ever. But an equivocation which serves the purpose of a lie, Henry warns, is as like to it as a hypocrite is to a profane person — only better in appearance, therefore more dangerous. Yet what a train of evils follows from unbelief, he adds: when we forget the Lord's past mercies and gracious assurances, we are overwhelmed with desponding fears and led to dishonourable methods; nothing so effectually establishes us as firm, unshaken dependence upon God's promises. — Matthew Henry (Concise)
