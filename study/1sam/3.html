Study Notes — 1 Samuel

Chapter 3

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: The child Samuel ministered before Eli, and the word of the LORD was precious in those days, with no open vision. God's word was rare, which makes the call that follows all the more significant. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

2 COMMENTARY: Eli was laid down in his place, his eyes grown dim so he could not see — the old priest in decline, while the young servant slept near the ark. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

3 COMMENTARY: Before the lamp of God went out in the temple where the ark was, Samuel lay down to sleep. The night setting, with the lamp still burning, marks the hour of God's visit. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

4 COMMENTARY: The LORD called Samuel, and he answered, Here am I. The call Divine grace designs to make effectual, Henry notes, will be repeated until it takes effect. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

5 COMMENTARY: Samuel ran to Eli, thinking Eli had called him, and Eli said, I called not; lie down again. Samuel's ready obedience mistook the voice, but it showed a servant's ear. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

6 COMMENTARY: The LORD called yet again, and Samuel again went to Eli, who again said he had not called. The repeated call patiently drew Samuel toward recognising God's voice. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

7 COMMENTARY: Samuel did not yet know the LORD, neither was the word of the LORD yet revealed to him. His ignorance was not unbelief but inexperience — he had not yet learned to distinguish God's voice. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

8 COMMENTARY: At the third call Eli perceived that the LORD had called the child. Though it was a disgrace to Eli that God's call passed him by to Samuel, he still told the boy how to meet it. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

9 COMMENTARY: Eli instructed Samuel to lie down and answer, Speak, LORD, for thy servant heareth. Good words should be put into children's mouths betimes, Henry observes, so they may be prepared to learn Divine things; and elders should assist the younger rising up, even those soon to be preferred before them. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

10 COMMENTARY: The LORD came and stood and called as before, Samuel, Samuel, and Samuel answered, Speak, for thy servant heareth. The call made effectual at last found a listening servant. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

11 COMMENTARY: God told Samuel he would do a thing in Israel at which both ears of everyone who heard it would tingle. The coming judgment on Eli's house would shock the whole nation. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

12 COMMENTARY: In that day God would perform against Eli all he had spoken concerning his house: when he began, he would also make an end. The sentence already pronounced would now be fully executed. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

13 COMMENTARY: God would judge Eli's house for ever for the iniquity he knew, because his sons made themselves vile and he restrained them not. Those who do not restrain the sins of others when it is in their power, Henry warns, make themselves partakers of the guilt. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

14 COMMENTARY: Therefore God swore the iniquity of Eli's house would not be purged with sacrifice nor offering for ever. The very sacrifices his sons had profaned could not atone for this settled, unrestrained wickedness. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

15 COMMENTARY: Samuel lay until morning and opened the doors of the house of the LORD, but feared to show Eli the vision. His first prophetic burden was a hard word for the man who had taught him to listen. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

16 COMMENTARY: Eli called Samuel, my son, and Samuel answered, Here am I — the same ready answer he had given all night, now to a summons he dreaded. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

17 COMMENTARY: Eli pressed him to hide nothing of what the LORD had said, under a solemn curse if he withheld any of it. Eli would hear the whole truth, however bitter. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

18 COMMENTARY: Samuel told him every whit and hid nothing, and Eli answered, It is the LORD: let him do what seemeth him good. Eli acknowledged the Lord's right to do as he saw good, assured he would do nothing wrong; Henry reads his meekness and patience as true repentance, accepting the punishment of his sin. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

19 COMMENTARY: Samuel grew, and the LORD was with him, and let none of his words fall to the ground. All increase in wisdom and grace, Henry notes, is owing to the presence of God with us. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

20 COMMENTARY: All Israel from Dan to Beersheba knew Samuel was established to be a prophet of the LORD. His unfailing word proved his calling to the whole land. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

21 COMMENTARY: The LORD appeared again in Shiloh, revealing himself to Samuel by the word of the LORD. God graciously repeats his visits to those who receive them aright, and early piety became Samuel's greatest honour. — Matthew Henry (Concise)
