Study Notes — 1 Samuel

Chapter 2

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Hannah's heart rejoiced not in Samuel but in the LORD, and her horn was exalted in his salvation. She looked beyond the gift and praised the Giver, rejoicing in expectation of God's coming salvation. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

2 COMMENTARY: There is none holy as the LORD, none beside him, no rock like our God. The song grounds all its reversals in God's unmatched holiness and strength. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

3 COMMENTARY: Let not arrogancy come out of the mouth, for the LORD is a God of knowledge and by him actions are weighed. Henry notes God respects not man's wisdom or fancied excellences, but judges truly. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

4 COMMENTARY: The bows of the mighty are broken, and those who stumbled are girded with strength. The strong are soon weakened and the weak soon strengthened when God pleases. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

5 COMMENTARY: The full hire themselves out for bread while the hungry cease, and the barren bears seven while she with many children waxes feeble. God reverses human plenty and want at his pleasure. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

6 COMMENTARY: The LORD kills and makes alive, brings down to the grave and brings up. Life and death themselves are in his hand, not in man's strength. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

7 COMMENTARY: The LORD makes poor and makes rich, brings low and lifts up. Are we poor, Henry asks, God made us poor, which is reason for contentment; are we rich, God made us rich, which is reason for thankful service and doing good with abundance. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

8 COMMENTARY: He raises the poor from the dust and the beggar from the dunghill to set them among princes and inherit the throne of glory. God chooses those the world counts foolish and lifts them to honour. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

9 COMMENTARY: He will keep the feet of his saints, and the wicked shall be silent in darkness, for by strength shall no man prevail. The subjects of Christ's kingdom will be safe, and its enemies ruined. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

10 COMMENTARY: The adversaries of the LORD shall be broken to pieces, he shall thunder from heaven, judge the ends of the earth, and give strength to his king and exalt the horn of his Anointed. Here, Henry notes, we first meet the name MESSIAH, his Anointed, and the song looks to the kingdom of Christ. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

11 COMMENTARY: Elkanah went home to Ramah, and the child Samuel ministered unto the LORD before Eli the priest. Devoted to the LORD in a special manner, Samuel was from a child employed about the sanctuary in the services he was capable of. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

12 COMMENTARY: The sons of Eli were sons of Belial who knew not the LORD. In sharp contrast to Samuel, the priests' own sons had no true knowledge of God. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

13 COMMENTARY: The priests' custom was that when any man offered sacrifice, the priest's servant came with a fleshhook while the flesh was seething. Eli had winked at abuses in the sanctuary service until they became customs. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

14 COMMENTARY: All that the fleshhook brought up the priest took for himself, and so they did in Shiloh to all Israel. Those who should have taught worshippers what was good instead solicited them to wickedness. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

15 COMMENTARY: Even before the fat was burnt, the servant demanded flesh to roast for the priest, refusing sodden flesh. Their offence was committed in the very offering of sacrifices for sins, which typified the atonement of the Saviour. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

16 COMMENTARY: If a worshipper asked that the fat first be burnt, the servant answered, Nay, but thou shalt give it me now, or else I will take it by force. Sins against the remedy itself, Henry warns, are most dangerous, treading under foot the blood of the covenant. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

17 COMMENTARY: The sin of the young men was very great before the LORD, for men abhorred the offering of the LORD. Their greed turned worshippers away from God's own appointed sacrifices. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

18 COMMENTARY: But Samuel ministered before the LORD, being a child girded with a linen ephod. As he served with a pious disposition of mind, it was counted ministering unto the LORD, and God would enable him to serve better. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

19 COMMENTARY: His mother made him a little coat and brought it year by year when she came up for the yearly sacrifice, a mother's continuing care for her lent child. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

20 COMMENTARY: Eli blessed Elkanah and his wife, praying the LORD would give them seed for the loan lent to the LORD, and they went home. Those who serve God as well as they can, Henry notes, receive a blessing from the Lord. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

21 COMMENTARY: The LORD visited Hannah, and she bore three sons and two daughters, while Samuel grew before the LORD. The lent child was repaid with a full household, and Samuel himself kept growing in God's presence. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

22 COMMENTARY: Eli, very old, heard all his sons did to Israel and how they lay with the women assembled at the door of the tabernacle. Eli shunned trouble and exertion, which led him to indulge his children without using parental authority to restrain them when young. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

23 COMMENTARY: Eli asked his sons why they did such things, since he heard of their evil dealings by all the people. His reproof, Henry observes, was far too mild and gentle for sins so public and scandalous. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

24 COMMENTARY: It is no good report, Eli said; ye make the LORD's people to transgress. In general, Henry warns, none are more abandoned than the degenerate children of godly persons once they break through restraints. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

25 COMMENTARY: If a man sins against another, the judge shall judge him, but if he sins against the LORD, who shall intreat for him? Yet they did not hearken, because the LORD would slay them — their sins against God himself left them without an intercessor. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

26 COMMENTARY: And the child Samuel grew on, and was in favour both with the LORD and also with men. Against the dark background of Eli's house, Samuel's steady growth stands out. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

27 COMMENTARY: A man of God came to Eli and recalled how the LORD had plainly appeared to his father's house in Egypt and chosen them for the priesthood. The coming sentence is set against this great, unmerited honour. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

28 COMMENTARY: God had chosen Aaron's house out of all Israel's tribes to offer on his altar, burn incense, and wear the ephod before him, and had given them the offerings of Israel. The priesthood was a gift, not a possession to abuse. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

29 COMMENTARY: Wherefore kick ye at my sacrifice and offering, God asked, and honourest thy sons above me, making yourselves fat with the chief of Israel's offerings? Those who allow their children in an evil way and do not restrain them, Henry says, in effect honour them more than God. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

30 COMMENTARY: Though God had said Eli's house should walk before him for ever, now the LORD said, be it far from me; those that honour me I will honour, and they that despise me shall be lightly esteemed. The promise was conditional on honouring God. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

31 COMMENTARY: The days would come when God would cut off the arm of Eli's house, so that no old man would remain in it. The house that had fattened itself on holy things would lose its strength and continuance. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

32 COMMENTARY: Eli would see an enemy in God's habitation amid all the wealth God gave Israel, and no old man would be in his house for ever. Even Israel's prosperity would not spare the judged house. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

33 COMMENTARY: The man of Eli's house not cut off from the altar would live to consume Eli's eyes and grieve his heart, and all the increase of his house would die in the flower of their age. The remnant would be a grief, not a comfort. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

34 COMMENTARY: The sign would be that Hophni and Phinehas would both die in one day. A single day's double death would prove the word true. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

35 COMMENTARY: God would raise up a faithful priest who would do according to his heart and mind, and build him a sure house, walking before God's anointed for ever. In the midst of the sentence against Eli's house, mercy is promised to Israel, and God's work shall never fall to the ground for want of hands. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

36 COMMENTARY: Everyone left of Eli's house would come crouching to the faithful priest for a piece of silver and a morsel of bread, begging to be put into a priest's office to eat bread. The once-fat house would be reduced to begging its bread. — Matthew Henry (Concise)
