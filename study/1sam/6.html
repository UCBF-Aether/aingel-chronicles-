Study Notes — 1 Samuel

Chapter 6

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: The ark of the LORD was in Philistine country seven months. Seven months the Philistines were punished by the ark's presence, Henry notes, because they would not send it home sooner; sinners lengthen their own miseries by refusing to part with their sins. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

2 COMMENTARY: The Philistines called for their priests and diviners, asking what to do with the ark and how to send it to its place. Their own religious experts were consulted to undo the trouble their capture had caused. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

3 COMMENTARY: The priests and diviners said, if you send away the ark, send it not empty but return a trespass offering, and then you shall be healed and know why God's hand was not removed. Even Philistine diviners understood that restitution, not mere removal, was required. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

4 COMMENTARY: Asked what trespass offering to return, they answered five golden emerods and five golden mice, according to the number of the Philistine lords — one for each lord, since the same plague was on all. The offering confessed the plague in golden images of it. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

5 COMMENTARY: They were to make images of their emerods and of the mice that marred the land, and give glory to the God of Israel, in case he would lighten his hand from them, their gods, and their land. Giving glory to God, not merely escaping pain, was the counsel. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

6 COMMENTARY: Why harden your hearts, they asked, as Egypt and Pharaoh hardened theirs, who let Israel go only after God had wrought wonderfully among them? How willing bad men are, Henry observes, to shift off their convictions and believe a judgment is mere chance, a rod with no voice to heed. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

7 COMMENTARY: Now make a new cart and take two milch kine that have never borne a yoke, tie them to the cart, and bring their calves home from them. The untrained, bereaved cows would be the test of God's hand. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

8 COMMENTARY: Take the ark and lay it on the cart, and put the golden jewels returned as a trespass offering in a coffer beside it, and send it away. The ark would travel unescorted, with its tribute beside it. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

9 COMMENTARY: And see: if it goes up by the way of its own coast to Bethshemesh, then the LORD has done this great evil; but if not, then it was chance that happened to them. The test was designed to distinguish God's hand from chance. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

10 COMMENTARY: The men did so, taking two milch kine, tying them to the cart, and shutting up their calves at home. Every natural pull of the cows would be toward home, to their calves. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

11 COMMENTARY: They laid the ark on the cart, and the coffer with the golden mice and the images of the emerods beside it. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

12 COMMENTARY: The kine took the straight way to Bethshemesh, going along the highway lowing as they went, turning neither right nor left, while the Philistine lords followed to the border. These two kine knew their Owner, Henry notes — the great Owner whom Hophni and Phinehas had not known — and God's providence serves its purposes even by brute creatures. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

13 COMMENTARY: The men of Bethshemesh were reaping wheat harvest in the valley, and when they lifted their eyes and saw the ark, they rejoiced to see it. Their joy at the ark's return was greater than the joy of harvest, Henry says, as the revival of holy ordinances after restraint and trouble is a matter of great joy. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

14 COMMENTARY: The cart came into the field of Joshua the Bethshemite and stood by a great stone, and they split the cart's wood and offered the kine as a burnt offering to the LORD. The cart that carried the ark became its own sacrifice. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

15 COMMENTARY: The Levites took down the ark and the coffer with its golden jewels and put them on the great stone, and the men of Bethshemesh offered burnt offerings and sacrifices that day. The return was met with proper priestly handling and worship. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

16 COMMENTARY: When the five Philistine lords had seen it, they returned to Ekron the same day. Their test was complete: the ark had gone home by God's hand, not by chance. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

17 COMMENTARY: These are the golden emerods returned as a trespass offering: one each for Ashdod, Gaza, Askelon, Gath, and Ekron. The record names each lord's city, confessing the plague city by city. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

18 COMMENTARY: And the golden mice, according to the number of all the Philistine cities belonging to the five lords, both fenced cities and country villages, as far as the great stone of Abel where the ark was set. The offering covered the whole land the mice had marred. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

19 COMMENTARY: He smote the men of Bethshemesh because they had looked into the ark, smiting fifty thousand and threescore and ten men, and the people lamented because the LORD had smitten many with a great slaughter. It is a great affront to God, Henry warns, for vain men to pry into secret things that belong not to them; God will not suffer his ark to be profaned, and those who will not reverently use the tokens of his grace shall feel his justice. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

20 COMMENTARY: The men of Bethshemesh said, Who is able to stand before this holy LORD God, and to whom shall he go up from us? Instead of asking how they might have peace with God and recover his favour, they desired to be rid of the ark — foolish men running from one extreme to the other. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

21 COMMENTARY: They sent messengers to Kirjathjearim, saying the Philistines had brought back the ark, come down and fetch it up to you. When God's word works terror on sinners' consciences, Henry observes, many instead of taking blame to themselves quarrel with the word and put it from them. — Matthew Henry (Concise)
