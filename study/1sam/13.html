Study Notes — 1 Samuel

Chapter 13

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Saul reigned one year, and when he had reigned two years over Israel, the events of this chapter took place. For above a year, Henry notes, he gave the Philistines time to prepare for war and to weaken and disarm Israel; when men are lifted up in self-sufficiency, they are often led into folly. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

2 COMMENTARY: Saul chose three thousand men of Israel: two thousand with him in Michmash and Mount Bethel, a thousand with Jonathan in Gibeah of Benjamin, and the rest he sent home. The small standing force was the army of a king who had let a year slip by. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

3 COMMENTARY: Jonathan smote the Philistine garrison in Geba, and Saul blew the trumpet throughout the land, saying, Let the Hebrews hear. The first blow against the occupiers was struck by the son, and proclaimed by the father. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

4 COMMENTARY: All Israel heard that Saul had smitten a garrison and that Israel was now had in abomination with the Philistines, and the people were called together after Saul to Gilgal. The chief advantages of the church's enemies, Henry observes, are derived from the misconduct of its professed friends. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

5 COMMENTARY: The Philistines gathered to fight Israel: thirty thousand chariots, six thousand horsemen, and people as the sand on the seashore, pitching in Michmash eastward from Bethaven. The response dwarfed anything Israel could field. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

6 COMMENTARY: When Israel saw they were in a strait and distressed, the people hid themselves in caves, thickets, rocks, high places, and pits. Terror scattered the nation before a sword was drawn. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

7 COMMENTARY: Some Hebrews went over Jordan to Gad and Gilead, while Saul remained in Gilgal with the people following him trembling. When Saul at length sounded the alarm, Henry notes, the people, dissatisfied with his management or terrified by the enemy, did not come to him or speedily deserted him. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

8 COMMENTARY: He tarried seven days, according to the set time Samuel had appointed, but Samuel came not to Gilgal, and the people were scattered from him. The appointed wait ran out while the army melted away. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

9 COMMENTARY: Saul said, Bring hither a burnt offering to me, and peace offerings, and he offered the burnt offering. Saul broke the order expressly given by Samuel, offering sacrifice himself though he was neither priest nor prophet. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

10 COMMENTARY: As soon as he had made an end of offering, behold, Samuel came, and Saul went out to meet and salute him. The prophet arrived the moment the disobedience was complete. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

11 COMMENTARY: Samuel said, What hast thou done? And Saul said, Because I saw the people scattered, and thou camest not within the appointed days, and the Philistines gathered at Michmash. When charged with disobedience, Henry observes, Saul justified himself and gave no sign of repentance. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

12 COMMENTARY: Therefore said I, the Philistines will come down upon me to Gilgal, and I have not made supplication unto the LORD: I forced myself therefore, and offered a burnt offering. He would have this act of disobedience pass for prudence and piety, Henry says, but men destitute of inward piety often lay great stress on outward performances of religion. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

13 COMMENTARY: Samuel said, Thou hast done foolishly; thou hast not kept the commandment of the LORD thy God, which he commanded thee, for now would the LORD have established thy kingdom upon Israel for ever. Samuel charged Saul with being an enemy to himself: those who disobey God's commandments do foolishly for themselves. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

14 COMMENTARY: But now thy kingdom shall not continue: the LORD hath sought him a man after his own heart, and commanded him to be captain over his people, because thou hast not kept what the LORD commanded thee. Men see only Saul's outward act, which seems small, Henry notes, but God saw unbelief and distrust of his providence, contempt of his authority, and rebellion against conscience; our disposition to obey or disobey is often proved by behaviour in things that appear small. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

15 COMMENTARY: Samuel arose and went up from Gilgal to Gibeah of Benjamin, and Saul numbered the people present with him, about six hundred men. The prophet left; the army had shrunk to six hundred. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

16 COMMENTARY: Saul, Jonathan, and the people with them abode in Gibeah of Benjamin, while the Philistines encamped in Michmash. The two camps faced each other across the pass. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

17 COMMENTARY: Spoilers came out of the Philistine camp in three companies, one turning toward Ophrah in the land of Shual. The raiders spread to strip the country while Israel watched, powerless. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

18 COMMENTARY: Another company turned the way to Bethhoron, and another to the border overlooking the valley of Zeboim toward the wilderness. Three roads, three raids, one defenceless land. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

19 COMMENTARY: Now there was no smith found throughout all Israel, for the Philistines said, Lest the Hebrews make them swords or spears. See how politic the Philistines were, Henry observes: they not only prevented Israel from making weapons but obliged them to depend on their enemies even for husbandry tools. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

20 COMMENTARY: All the Israelites went down to the Philistines to sharpen every man his share, coulter, axe, and mattock. The farmer's tools had to be carried to the enemy's forge. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

21 COMMENTARY: Yet they had a file for the mattocks, coulters, forks, axes, and to sharpen the goads. Even the sharpening was grudging and costly. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

22 COMMENTARY: So in the day of battle there was neither sword nor spear found in the hand of any of the people with Saul and Jonathan, except with Saul and Jonathan themselves. Miserable is a guilty, defenceless nation, Henry says — much more those destitute of the whole armour of God. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

23 COMMENTARY: The garrison of the Philistines went out to the passage of Michmash. The enemy held the pass, and the stage was set for Jonathan's exploit. — Matthew Henry (Concise)
