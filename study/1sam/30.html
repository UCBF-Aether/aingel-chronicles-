Study Notes — 1 Samuel

Chapter 30

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: When David and his men came to Ziklag on the third day, the Amalekites had invaded the south and Ziklag, smitten Ziklag, and burned it with fire. When we go abroad in the way of duty, Henry says, we may comfortably hope God will take care of our families in our absence — but David returned to find his home in ashes. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

2 COMMENTARY: They had taken the women captives that were therein, slaying none, great or small, but carrying them away and going on their way. The captives were taken alive, which left room for recovery. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

3 COMMENTARY: So David and his men came to the city, and behold, it was burned with fire, and their wives, sons, and daughters were taken captives. If, when we come off a journey, we find our abode in peace and not laid waste, Henry says, let the Lord be praised for it; David found the opposite. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

4 COMMENTARY: Then David and the people with him lifted up their voice and wept, until they had no more power to weep. The grief was spent to exhaustion. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

5 COMMENTARY: David's two wives were taken captives, Ahinoam the Jezreelitess, and Abigail the wife of Nabal the Carmelite. The captain's own household shared the common loss. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

6 COMMENTARY: David was greatly distressed, for the people spake of stoning him, because the soul of all the people was grieved, every man for his sons and daughters; but David encouraged himself in the LORD his God. David was brought thus low only just before he was raised to the throne, Henry notes; when things are at the worst with God's people, then they begin to mend. His men fretted at their loss and dispirited each other, but David set his graces to work and, by encouraging himself in God, kept his spirit calm. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

7 COMMENTARY: David said to Abiathar the priest, Ahimelech's son, I pray thee, bring me hither the ephod, and Abiathar brought the ephod to David. The distressed captain turned first to enquiry, not to revenge. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

8 COMMENTARY: David enquired whether he should pursue this troop and whether he should overtake them, and God answered, Pursue, for thou shalt surely overtake them and without fail recover all. If in all our ways, Henry says, even when there can be no doubt they are just, we acknowledge God, we may expect he will direct our steps, as he did David's. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

9 COMMENTARY: So David went, he and the six hundred men with him, and came to the brook Besor, where those left behind stayed. The pursuit began at once on the strength of the promise. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

10 COMMENTARY: But David pursued, he and four hundred men, for two hundred abode behind, too faint to go over the brook Besor. David, in tenderness to his men, Henry notes, would by no means urge them beyond their strength. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

11 COMMENTARY: They found an Egyptian in the field, brought him to David, gave him bread, and he did eat, and they made him drink water. The abandoned servant was fed before he was questioned. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

12 COMMENTARY: They gave him a piece of fig cake and two clusters of raisins, and when he had eaten, his spirit came again, for he had eaten no bread nor drunk water for three days and nights. Three days' hunger ended with figs and raisins. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

13 COMMENTARY: David asked the found man to whom he belonged and whence he was, and he answered that he was a young Egyptian, servant to an Amalekite, left behind by his master because he had fallen sick three days before. The poor Egyptian lad, scarcely alive, was made the means of a great deal of good to David, Henry observes. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

14 COMMENTARY: We made an invasion upon the south of the Cherethites, and upon Judah's coast, and upon the south of Caleb, and we burned Ziklag with fire. The servant's confession named Ziklag's burners. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

15 COMMENTARY: David said, Canst thou bring me down to this company? And he said, Swear unto me by God, that thou wilt neither kill me, nor deliver me into my master's hands, and I will bring thee down to this company. Those unworthy the name of true Israelites, Henry warns, shut up their compassion from persons in distress; David swore the servant's safety and gained his guide. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

16 COMMENTARY: When he had brought him down, behold, they were spread abroad upon all the earth, eating and drinking and dancing, because of all the great spoil taken from Philistia and Judah. Sinners are nearest to ruin, Henry says, when they cry, Peace and safety, and put the evil day far from them; nothing gives our spiritual enemies more advantage than sensuality and indulgence. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

17 COMMENTARY: David smote them from the twilight even unto the evening of the next day, and there escaped not a man of them, save four hundred young men who rode upon camels and fled. Eating, drinking, and dancing had been the soft and pleasant way, Henry notes, in which many have gone down to the congregation of the dead. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

18 COMMENTARY: David recovered all that the Amalekites had carried away, and David rescued his two wives. The promise — without fail recover all — began to hold. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

19 COMMENTARY: There was nothing lacking to them, neither small nor great, neither sons nor daughters, neither spoil, nor anything taken from them: David recovered all. The spoil was recovered and brought off, Henry says; nothing was lost, but a great deal gained. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

20 COMMENTARY: David took all the flocks and herds, which they drave before those other cattle, and said, This is David's spoil. The recovered herds became the spoil of the deliverer. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

21 COMMENTARY: David came to the two hundred men, too faint to follow, whom they had made to abide at the brook Besor, and they went forth to meet David and his people, and when David came near, he saluted them. The faint guard came out to greet the returning army. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

22 COMMENTARY: Then answered all the wicked men and men of Belial, of those that went with David, saying, Because they went not with us, we will not give them ought of the spoil recovered, save every man his wife and children, that they may lead them away and depart. Those are men of Belial indeed, Henry says, who delight in putting hardships upon their brethren and care not who is starved, so they may be fed to the full. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

23 COMMENTARY: Then said David, Ye shall not do so, my brethren, with that which the LORD hath given us, who hath preserved us, and delivered the company that came against us into our hand. What God gives us, Henry notes, he designs we should do good with; in distributing the spoil, David was just and kind. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

24 COMMENTARY: For who will hearken unto you in this matter? But as his part is that goeth down to the battle, so shall his part be that tarrieth by the stuff: they shall part alike. The rule of equal shares was laid down on the spot. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

25 COMMENTARY: It was so from that day forward, that he made it a statute and an ordinance for Israel unto this day. The battlefield ruling became Israel's standing law. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

26 COMMENTARY: When David came to Ziklag, he sent of the spoil unto the elders of Judah, even to his friends, saying, Behold a present for you of the spoil of the enemies of the LORD. David was generous and kind to all his friends, Henry observes; those who consider the Lord as the Giver of their abundance will dispose of it with fairness and liberality. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

27 COMMENTARY: To them which were in Bethel, and to them which were in south Ramoth, and to them which were in Jattir — the presents went town by town through Judah. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

28 COMMENTARY: And to them which were in Aroer, and to them which were in Siphmoth, and to them which were in Eshtemoa — the list of Judah's towns received their share. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

29 COMMENTARY: And to them which were in Rachal, and to them which were in the cities of the Jerahmeelites, and to them which were in the cities of the Kenites — the southern neighbours who had sheltered David were remembered. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

30 COMMENTARY: And to them which were in Hormah, and to them which were in Chorashan, and to them which were in Athach — the gifts reached the border towns. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

31 COMMENTARY: And to them which were in Hebron, and to all the places where David himself and his men were wont to haunt. Hebron, the city where David would soon be crowned, closed the list. — Matthew Henry (Concise)
