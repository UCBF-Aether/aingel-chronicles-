Study Notes — 1 Samuel

Chapter 1

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Elkanah of Ramathaim-zophim in Mount Ephraim opens the book, and Henry's chapter heading frames the whole book as the story of Eli's house, Samuel, Saul's rise and fall, and David's patient suffering as a pattern for believers. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

2 COMMENTARY: Elkanah had two wives, Peninnah with children and Hannah with none, setting up the household sorrow that drives the chapter. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

3 COMMENTARY: Elkanah went up yearly to worship and sacrifice at Shiloh, keeping up his attendance at God's altar despite the unhappy divisions in his family. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

4 COMMENTARY: At the sacrifice Elkanah gave portions to Peninnah and her children, the ordinary family share of the peace offering. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

5 COMMENTARY: Hannah received a worthy portion because Elkanah loved her, though the LORD had shut up her womb; Henry warns against abating just love to a relation for an infirmity that is their affliction, not their fault. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

6 COMMENTARY: Peninnah provoked Hannah sorely to make her fret over her childlessness. Henry calls it a base disposition to delight in grieving a sorrowful spirit, and says we ought to bear one another's burdens, not add to them. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

7 COMMENTARY: The provocation repeated year by year at the house of the LORD, until Hannah wept and could not eat. Those of a fretful spirit who lay provocations too much to heart, Henry notes, strip themselves of many comforts of life and godliness. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

8 COMMENTARY: Elkanah tenderly asked why Hannah wept and would not eat, offering himself as better to her than ten sons. Henry's counsel here is to notice our comforts, and look at what is for us as well as what is against us. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

9 COMMENTARY: After eating and drinking in Shiloh, Hannah rose to pray while Eli the priest sat by a post of the temple of the LORD. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

10 COMMENTARY: Hannah prayed in bitterness of soul and wept sore. Henry observes that she mingled tears with her prayers, trusting a God who knows the troubled soul. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

11 COMMENTARY: Hannah vowed that if God would look on her affliction and remember her, she would give the child to the LORD all his days. God gives us leave in prayer, Henry notes, not only to ask good things in general but to name the special good we most need. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

12 COMMENTARY: As Hannah continued praying before the LORD, Eli marked her mouth, watching a prayer he could not hear. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

13 COMMENTARY: Hannah spoke in her heart, only her lips moving, with no voice heard, so Eli thought her drunken. Her silent prayer testified her belief in God's knowledge of the heart and its desires. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

14 COMMENTARY: Eli rashly charged her with drunkenness and told her to put away her wine. Henry warns that it ill becomes us to be hasty in censuring others and to think people guilty while the matter is doubtful and unproved. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

15 COMMENTARY: Hannah answered meekly that she was a woman of a sorrowful spirit who had drunk neither wine nor strong drink, but had poured out her soul before the LORD. She did not retort the charge or upbraid Eli with his own sons' conduct. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

16 COMMENTARY: She asked not to be counted a daughter of Belial, explaining that she had spoken out of the abundance of her complaint and grief. When unjustly censured, Henry counsels, we need a double watch before the door of our lips not to return censure for censure. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

17 COMMENTARY: Eli acknowledged his mistake and blessed her: go in peace, and may the God of Israel grant your petition. Hannah thought it enough to clear herself, and Eli was willing to own his error. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

18 COMMENTARY: Hannah asked for grace in Eli's sight, then went her way, ate, and was sad no more. Having committed her case to God in prayer, and received Eli's prayer for her, prayer became heart's ease to her soul and smoothed her countenance. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

19 COMMENTARY: The family rose early, worshipped before the LORD, and returned to Ramah, where Elkanah knew Hannah his wife. They would not set out on their journey until they had worshipped God together. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

20 COMMENTARY: In due time Hannah conceived and bore a son, naming him Samuel, because she had asked him of the LORD. The name itself keeps the answer to prayer in view. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

21 COMMENTARY: Elkanah and all his house went up to offer the yearly sacrifice and his vow, keeping the household's regular worship even with a new child at home. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

22 COMMENTARY: Hannah stayed behind until the child was weaned, intending then to bring him to appear before the LORD. Henry notes God will have mercy and not sacrifice: those detained from public ordinances by nursing little children may take comfort here if they do that duty in a right spirit. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

23 COMMENTARY: Elkanah agreed she should tarry until the child was weaned, praying only that the LORD would establish his word. So Hannah nursed Samuel at home in preparation for giving him up. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

24 COMMENTARY: When Samuel was weaned, Hannah brought him to the house of the LORD in Shiloh with three bullocks, an ephah of flour, and a bottle of wine — a generous offering to accompany the gift of the child himself. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

25 COMMENTARY: They slew a bullock and brought the child to Eli, presenting Samuel in person to the priest who had once misjudged his mother. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

26 COMMENTARY: Hannah reminded Eli that she was the woman who had stood there praying to the LORD, identifying herself by that answered prayer. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

27 COMMENTARY: For this child I prayed, she said, and the LORD has given me my petition. Her grateful acknowledgment names the gift as an answer to prayer. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

28 COMMENTARY: Hannah lent Samuel to the LORD for as long as he lived, and he worshipped the LORD there. Whatever we give to God, Henry observes, is what we have first asked and received from him; and the child early showed true piety, a pattern for teaching little children to worship God young. — Matthew Henry (Concise)
