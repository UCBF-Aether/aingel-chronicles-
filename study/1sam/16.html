Study Notes — 1 Samuel

Chapter 16

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: The LORD said to Samuel, How long wilt thou mourn for Saul, seeing I have rejected him from reigning over Israel? Fill thine horn with oil, and go; I will send thee to Jesse the Bethlehemite, for I have provided me a king among his sons. Mourning for the rejected king was to give way to anointing his successor. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

2 COMMENTARY: Samuel feared that if Saul heard of the errand he would kill him, so the LORD told him to take a heifer and say he had come to sacrifice. Saul was grown very wicked, Henry notes, and God gave his prophet a true errand that concealed the anointing. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

3 COMMENTARY: Call Jesse to the sacrifice, God said, and I will show thee what thou shalt do, and thou shalt anoint unto me him whom I name unto thee. The choice would be revealed at the sacrifice, not before. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

4 COMMENTARY: Samuel did as the LORD spoke and came to Bethlehem, and the elders trembled at his coming and asked, Comest thou peaceably? It becomes us, Henry says, to stand in awe of God's messengers and to tremble at his word. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

5 COMMENTARY: He said, Peaceably: I am come to sacrifice unto the LORD; sanctify yourselves and come with me. And he sanctified Jesse and his sons and called them to the sacrifice. When our Lord Jesus came, Henry reflects, though men had reason to fear his errand was condemnation, he gave full assurance he came peaceably, for he came to sacrifice — and brought his offering with him. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

6 COMMENTARY: When they were come, Samuel looked on Eliab and said, Surely the LORD's anointed is before him. It was strange, Henry notes, that Samuel, so disappointed in Saul, whose countenance and stature had recommended him, should judge another man by that rule. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

7 COMMENTARY: But the LORD said, Look not on his countenance or the height of his stature, because I have refused him; for the LORD seeth not as man seeth: man looketh on the outward appearance, but the LORD looketh on the heart. We can tell how men look, Henry says, but God can tell what they are; he judges by the heart, valuing the faith, fear, and love planted there beyond human discernment. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

8 COMMENTARY: Jesse called Abinadab and made him pass before Samuel, and he said, Neither hath the LORD chosen this. The second son was refused as the first had been. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

9 COMMENTARY: Jesse made Shammah to pass by, and Samuel said, Neither hath the LORD chosen this. Stature and birth order were not God's measure. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

10 COMMENTARY: Jesse made seven of his sons to pass before Samuel, and Samuel said, The LORD hath not chosen these. All seven of the sons present were passed over. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

11 COMMENTARY: Samuel asked, Are here all thy children? And Jesse said, There remaineth yet the youngest, and behold, he keepeth the sheep, and Samuel said, Send and fetch him, for we will not sit down till he come. God does not favour children according to fond partiality, Henry observes, but often most honours and blesses those least regarded. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

12 COMMENTARY: He sent and brought him in: ruddy, of beautiful countenance, goodly to look to, and the LORD said, Arise, anoint him, for this is he. David was the youngest of Jesse's sons, Henry notes; his name signifies Beloved, and he was a type of God's beloved Son. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

13 COMMENTARY: Samuel took the horn of oil and anointed him in the midst of his brethren, and the Spirit of the LORD came upon David from that day forward, and Samuel rose up and went to Ramah. His anointing was not an empty ceremony, Henry says: a Divine power went with the instituted sign, and David found himself advanced in wisdom and courage with all the qualifications of a prince, though not advanced in outward circumstances. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

14 COMMENTARY: But the Spirit of the LORD departed from Saul, and an evil spirit from the LORD troubled him. Saul was made a terror to himself: if God and his grace do not rule us, Henry warns, sin and Satan will have possession of us. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

15 COMMENTARY: Saul's servants said, Behold now, an evil spirit from God troubleth thee. The court named the trouble plainly. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

16 COMMENTARY: Let our lord command thy servants to seek a man cunning in playing the harp, that when the evil spirit is upon thee, he shall play, and thou shalt be well. Music was proposed as the remedy for the troubled king. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

17 COMMENTARY: Saul said, Provide me now a man that can play well, and bring him to me. The king accepted the court's counsel. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

18 COMMENTARY: One servant answered, I have seen a son of Jesse the Bethlehemite, cunning in playing, a mighty valiant man, a man of war, prudent in matters, a comely person, and the LORD is with him. The description already joined the harpist and the warrior in one young man. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

19 COMMENTARY: Saul sent messengers to Jesse, saying, Send me David thy son, which is with the sheep. The shepherd was summoned from the flock to the court. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

20 COMMENTARY: Jesse took an ass laden with bread, a bottle of wine, and a kid, and sent them by David his son unto Saul. The father's gift accompanied the son's first coming to court. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

21 COMMENTARY: David came to Saul and stood before him, and Saul loved him greatly, and he became his armourbearer. The anointed shepherd now bore the rejected king's armour. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

22 COMMENTARY: Saul sent to Jesse, saying, Let David stand before me, for he hath found favour in my sight. The king kept at court the very youth God had chosen to replace him. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

23 COMMENTARY: When the evil spirit from God was upon Saul, David took a harp and played, so Saul was refreshed and well, and the evil spirit departed from him. It is a pity, Henry reflects, that music, which may serve the good temper of the mind, should ever be abused to support vanity; and music, diversions, company, or business may quiet a wounded conscience for a time, but nothing effects a real cure but the blood of Christ applied in faith and the sanctifying Spirit sealing the pardon. — Matthew Henry (Concise)
