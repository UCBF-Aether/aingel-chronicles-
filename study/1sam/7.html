Study Notes — 1 Samuel

Chapter 7

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: The men of Kirjathjearim fetched up the ark and brought it into Abinadab's house on the hill, sanctifying his son Eleazar to keep it. God will find a resting-place for his ark, Henry notes; if some thrust it from them, others' hearts shall be inclined to receive it. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

2 COMMENTARY: While the ark abode in Kirjathjearim the time was long, twenty years, and all the house of Israel lamented after the LORD. Twenty years passed before Israel cared for the want of the ark, and during this time Samuel laboured to revive true religion. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

3 COMMENTARY: Samuel spoke to all Israel: if you return to the LORD with all your hearts, put away the strange gods and Ashtaroth, prepare your hearts, and serve him only, and he will deliver you from the Philistines. This was one of the most effectual revivals of religion ever to take place in Israel, Henry observes, told in very few but expressive words. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

4 COMMENTARY: Then the children of Israel put away Baalim and Ashtaroth and served the LORD only. The revival bore fruit in a clean break with idols. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

5 COMMENTARY: Samuel said, Gather all Israel to Mizpeh, and I will pray for you unto the LORD. The people gathered for solemn repentance under their judge and intercessor. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

6 COMMENTARY: They gathered at Mizpeh, drew water and poured it out before the LORD, fasted, and said, We have sinned against the LORD, and Samuel judged Israel there. Pouring out water signified their humiliation and sorrow for sin; they poured out their hearts in repentance, free and full in confession, fixed in resolution to cast away all wrongdoing, giving glory to God and taking shame to themselves. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

7 COMMENTARY: When the Philistines heard Israel was gathered at Mizpeh, their lords went up against Israel, and Israel was afraid. When sinners begin to repent and reform, Henry warns, they must expect Satan to muster all his force against them to oppose and discourage them. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

8 COMMENTARY: Israel begged Samuel, Cease not to cry unto the LORD our God for us, that he will save us from the Philistines. What a comfort it is to believers, Henry notes, that our great Intercessor above never ceases and is never silent, always appearing in God's presence for us. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

9 COMMENTARY: Samuel took a sucking lamb and offered it wholly as a burnt offering, and cried to the LORD for Israel, and the LORD heard him. Samuel's sacrifice without his prayer would have been an empty shadow; God gave a gracious answer to the prayer. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

10 COMMENTARY: As Samuel offered the burnt offering, the Philistines drew near, but the LORD thundered with a great thunder that day upon them and discomfited them, and they were smitten before Israel. The thunder answered the prayer at the very moment of sacrifice. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

11 COMMENTARY: The men of Israel went out of Mizpeh and pursued the Philistines, smiting them until Bethcar. The pursued became the pursuers once God had scattered the enemy. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

12 COMMENTARY: Samuel took a stone, set it between Mizpeh and Shen, and called it Ebenezer, saying, Hitherto hath the LORD helped us. Samuel erected a memorial of the victory to God's glory and Israel's encouragement, and through successive generations the church has had cause to set up Ebeneezers for renewed deliverances. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

13 COMMENTARY: So the Philistines were subdued and came no more into Israel's coast, and the hand of the LORD was against them all the days of Samuel. Neither outward persecutions nor inward corruptions have prevailed against God's church, Henry notes, because hitherto the Lord has helped her, and he will help to the end. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

14 COMMENTARY: The cities the Philistines had taken, from Ekron to Gath, were restored to Israel, with their coasts delivered, and there was peace between Israel and the Amorites. The revival's victory recovered lost ground on every side. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

15 COMMENTARY: Samuel judged Israel all the days of his life. His judgeship, born in the revival, lasted to his death. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

16 COMMENTARY: He went year by year in circuit to Bethel, Gilgal, and Mizpeh, judging Israel in all those places. The circuit brought justice and worship to the people rather than waiting for them to come to him. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

17 COMMENTARY: His return was to Ramah, where his house was, and there he judged Israel and built an altar to the LORD. In this great revival, Henry observes, the ark was neither removed to Shiloh nor placed with the tabernacle elsewhere — a sign that the typical institutions' chief use was their meaning, and that repentance, faith, and love of God and man outweigh a lifeless service. — Matthew Henry (Concise)
