Study Notes — 1 Samuel

Chapter 4

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: The word of Samuel came to all Israel, and Israel went out against the Philistines, pitching beside Ebenezer while the Philistines pitched at Aphek. Samuel's word now guided the nation, even into a battle it would lose. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

2 COMMENTARY: The Philistines put themselves in array, and Israel was smitten, losing about four thousand men in the field. Sin, the accursed thing, was in the camp, Henry observes, and gave their enemies all the advantage they could wish for. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

3 COMMENTARY: The elders asked why the LORD had smitten them, and proposed fetching the ark from Shiloh to save them. They owned God's hand in their trouble, Henry notes, but instead of submitting they spoke angrily, unaware of any just provocation they had given him. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

4 COMMENTARY: The people sent to Shiloh for the ark of the covenant of the LORD of hosts, who dwells between the cherubim, and Eli's two sons, Hophni and Phinehas, came with it. They supposed they could oblige God to appear for them by bringing the ark into the camp. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

5 COMMENTARY: When the ark came into the camp, all Israel shouted so that the earth rang. Outward observance was clung to as if the ark itself, God's throne in the camp, would save them while the world and flesh sat on the throne of the heart. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

6 COMMENTARY: The Philistines heard the shout and asked what the great shout in the Hebrew camp meant, and learned that the ark of the LORD had come into the camp. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

7 COMMENTARY: The Philistines were afraid, saying God had come into the camp, and woe to them, for nothing like this had happened before. Even Israel's enemies recognised the ark's dread significance. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

8 COMMENTARY: Woe to us, they cried; who shall deliver us from these mighty Gods, the Gods who smote Egypt with all the plagues in the wilderness? Their fear recalled God's ancient judgments, though they did not know the God of Israel himself. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

9 COMMENTARY: Be strong and quit yourselves like men, the Philistines urged one another, lest they become servants to the Hebrews as the Hebrews had been to them. Fear stiffened them into desperate courage. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

10 COMMENTARY: The Philistines fought, Israel was smitten and fled every man to his tent, and the slaughter was very great — thirty thousand footmen fell. The ark in the camp did not save an unrepentant people. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

11 COMMENTARY: The ark of God was taken, and Eli's two sons, Hophni and Phinehas, were slain. The taking of the ark was a great judgment and a certain token of God's displeasure; let none think to shelter from God's wrath under the cloak of outward profession. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

12 COMMENTARY: A man of Benjamin ran from the army and came to Shiloh the same day with clothes rent and earth on his head, the signs of calamity. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

13 COMMENTARY: Eli sat by the wayside watching, for his heart trembled for the ark of God, and when the man came into the city the whole city cried out. The old judge's chief concern was not the army but the ark. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

14 COMMENTARY: Eli heard the noise of the crying and asked what the tumult meant, and the man came hastily to tell him. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

15 COMMENTARY: Eli was ninety-eight years old, his eyes dim so he could not see. Age and blindness frame his final hour, dependent on others' tidings. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

16 COMMENTARY: The man said he had fled that day from the army, and Eli asked, What is there done, my son? The question waited for the worst. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

17 COMMENTARY: The messenger reported that Israel had fled, there had been a great slaughter, Eli's two sons were dead, and the ark of God was taken. Each blow fell harder than the last. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

18 COMMENTARY: When mention was made of the ark, Eli fell backward from his seat by the gate, broke his neck, and died, for he was old and heavy, after judging Israel forty years. The defeat grieved him as judge, his sons' deaths touched him as father, but the ark struck him to the heart; Henry adds that a man may die miserably yet not die eternally. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

19 COMMENTARY: Phinehas' wife, near delivery, heard that the ark was taken and that her father-in-law and husband were dead, and she bowed herself and travailed, for her pains came upon her. The tidings brought on her labour. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

20 COMMENTARY: About the time of her death the women standing by said, Fear not, for thou hast born a son, but she answered not, neither did she regard it. Earthly joy could not reach a heart fixed on the lost ark. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

21 COMMENTARY: She named the child Ichabod, saying, The glory is departed from Israel, because the ark was taken and because of her father-in-law and husband. Her dying regret was for the loss of the ark and the departure of the glory from Israel. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

22 COMMENTARY: She repeated, The glory is departed from Israel, for the ark of God is taken. What pleasure can we take in creature comforts, Henry asks, if we want God's gracious presence? If God goes, the glory goes; yet though glory is withdrawn from one sinful nation after another, it shall never depart altogether. — Matthew Henry (Concise)
