Study Notes — 1 Samuel

Chapter 23

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: They told David, Behold, the Philistines fight against Keilah and rob the threshingfloors. When princes persecute God's people, Henry notes, let them expect vexation on all sides: if Saul fights against David, the Philistines fight against Saul's country. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

2 COMMENTARY: Therefore David enquired of the LORD, Shall I go and smite these Philistines? And the LORD said, Go, smite the Philistines, and save Keilah. David considered himself the protector of the land, Henry says; thus did the Saviour Jesus, and left us an example. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

3 COMMENTARY: David's men said, Behold, we be afraid here in Judah; how much more then if we come to Keilah against the Philistine armies? The fugitive band's fear was plain, and David did not dismiss it. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

4 COMMENTARY: Then David enquired of the LORD yet again, and the LORD answered, Arise, go down to Keilah, for I will deliver the Philistines into thine hand. The second enquiry confirmed the first with a promise. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

5 COMMENTARY: So David and his men went to Keilah, fought the Philistines, brought away their cattle, smote them with a great slaughter, and saved the inhabitants of Keilah. Those unlike David, Henry observes, sullenly decline to do good if they are not rewarded for services; David saved a city that would soon betray him. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

6 COMMENTARY: When Abiathar son of Ahimelech fled to David to Keilah, he came down with an ephod in his hand. The escaped priest brought the means of enquiring of God, and David would use it at once. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

7 COMMENTARY: It was told Saul that David was come to Keilah, and Saul said, God hath delivered him into mine hand, for he is shut in, by entering a town with gates and bars. The walled town that David had saved looked to Saul like a trap. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

8 COMMENTARY: Saul called all the people together to war, to go down to Keilah to besiege David and his men. The king mustered Israel against the man who had just saved one of its cities. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

9 COMMENTARY: David knew Saul secretly practised mischief against him, and said to Abiathar the priest, Bring hither the ephod. David applied to his great Protector for direction, Henry notes; no sooner was the ephod brought than he made use of it. We have the Scriptures in our hands, Henry says — let us take advice from them in doubtful cases: say, Bring hither the Bible. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

10 COMMENTARY: Then said David, O LORD God of Israel, thy servant hath certainly heard that Saul seeketh to come to Keilah, to destroy the city for my sake. David's address to God was very solemn and very particular, Henry observes, and God allows us to be so in our addresses: Lord, direct me in this matter, about which I am now at a loss. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

11 COMMENTARY: David asked whether the men of Keilah would deliver him up and whether Saul would come down, and the LORD answered that he would come down. The first question was answered directly. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

12 COMMENTARY: Then said David, Will the men of Keilah deliver me and my men into Saul's hand? And the LORD said, They will deliver thee up. Well might David complain, Henry says, that his enemies rewarded him evil for good, and that for his love they were his adversaries; Christ was used thus basely. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

13 COMMENTARY: Then David and his men, about six hundred, arose and departed out of Keilah, and went whithersoever they could go, and when Saul heard David was escaped, he forbore to go forth. God knows not only what will be, Henry notes, but what would be if it were not hindered, and therefore knows how to deliver the godly out of temptation. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

14 COMMENTARY: David abode in the wilderness in strongholds, and remained in a mountain in the wilderness of Ziph, and Saul sought him every day, but God delivered him not into his hand. David made no attempt against Saul, Henry observes; he kept God's way, waited God's time, and was content to secure himself in woods and wildernesses. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

15 COMMENTARY: David saw that Saul was come out to seek his life, and David was in the wilderness of Ziph in a wood. The hunt continued into the woods of Ziph. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

16 COMMENTARY: Jonathan Saul's son arose and went to David into the wood, and strengthened his hand in God. We find Jonathan comforting David, Henry says: as a pious friend, he directed him to God, the Foundation of his comfort. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

17 COMMENTARY: He said, Fear not, for Saul my father's hand shall not find thee, and thou shalt be king over Israel, and I shall be next unto thee, and that also Saul my father knoweth. As a self-denying friend, Henry notes, Jonathan took pleasure in the prospect of David's advancement to the throne. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

18 COMMENTARY: They two made a covenant before the LORD, and David abode in the wood, and Jonathan went to his house. As a constant friend, Henry observes, Jonathan renewed his friendship with him; our covenant with God should be often renewed, and therein our communion with him kept up. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

19 COMMENTARY: Then came the Ziphites to Saul to Gibeah, saying, Doth not David hide himself with us in strongholds in the wood, in the hill of Hachilah, south of Jeshimon? The men of Ziph betrayed the hiding place to the king. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

20 COMMENTARY: Now therefore, O king, come down according to all the desire of thy soul, and our part shall be to deliver him into the king's hand. The betrayers offered themselves as the king's hands. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

21 COMMENTARY: Saul said, Blessed be ye of the LORD, for ye have compassion on me. In the midst of his wickedness, Henry notes, Saul affected to speak the language of piety; such expressions, without suitable effects, can only amuse or deceive those who hear and use them. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

22 COMMENTARY: Go, prepare yet, and know and see his place where his haunt is, and who hath seen him there, for it is told me that he dealeth very subtilly. Saul demanded certainty before he moved. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

23 COMMENTARY: See therefore, and take knowledge of all the lurking places where he hideth himself, and come again to me with the certainty, and I will go with you, and if he be in the land, I will search him out throughout all the thousands of Judah. The net was to close over every thousand of Judah. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

24 COMMENTARY: They arose and went to Ziph before Saul, but David and his men were in the wilderness of Maon, in the plain south of Jeshimon. The betrayers went ahead, but David had already moved. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

25 COMMENTARY: Saul and his men went to seek him, and they told David, wherefore he came down into a rock and abode in the wilderness of Maon, and when Saul heard that, he pursued after David in Maon. The pursuit shifted to the rock country of Maon. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

26 COMMENTARY: Saul went on this side of the mountain, and David and his men on that side, and David made haste to get away for fear of Saul, for Saul and his men compassed David and his men round about to take them. This mountain was an emblem of Divine Providence coming between David and the destroyer, Henry observes. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

27 COMMENTARY: But there came a messenger unto Saul, saying, Haste thee, and come, for the Philistines have invaded the land. At the very moment the net closed, an invasion called the hunter away. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

28 COMMENTARY: Wherefore Saul returned from pursuing after David and went against the Philistines; therefore they called that place Selahammahlekoth. Sooner than his promise shall fail, Henry says, God will commission Philistines to effect our escape at the very moment when our case appears most desperate. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

29 COMMENTARY: David went up from thence and dwelt in strongholds at Engedi. The fugitive moved on to the cliffs above the Dead Sea, safe for another season. — Matthew Henry (Concise)
