Study Notes — 1 Samuel

Chapter 25

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Samuel died, and all Israel gathered, lamented him, and buried him in his house at Ramah, and David arose and went down to the wilderness of Paran. All Israel lamented Samuel, and they had reason, Henry says: he prayed daily for them, and those have hard hearts who can bury faithful ministers without grief. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

2 COMMENTARY: There was a man in Maon, whose possessions were in Carmel, very great, with three thousand sheep and a thousand goats, shearing his sheep in Carmel. We should not have heard of Nabal, Henry notes, if nothing had passed between him and David. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

3 COMMENTARY: The man's name was Nabal, and his wife Abigail, a woman of good understanding and beautiful countenance, but the man was churlish and evil in his doings, of the house of Caleb. Observe his name, Nabal, meaning a fool, Henry says; riches make men look great in the world's eye, but to one who takes right views, Nabal looked very mean. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

4 COMMENTARY: David heard in the wilderness that Nabal did shear his sheep. Shearing time was feast time, and the moment for the request. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

5 COMMENTARY: David sent ten young men, saying, Get you up to Carmel, go to Nabal, and greet him in my name. The greeting was courteous and the errand peaceful. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

6 COMMENTARY: Thus shall ye say to him that liveth in prosperity, Peace be both to thee, and peace to thine house, and peace unto all that thou hast. The blessing wished him peace in every direction. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

7 COMMENTARY: Now I have heard thou hast shearers; thy shepherds which were with us, we hurt them not, neither was there ought missing unto them, all the while they were in Carmel. David pleaded the kindness Nabal's shepherds had received, Henry notes; considering David's men were in distress, debt, and discontent, and provisions scarce, it was by good management they were kept from plundering. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

8 COMMENTARY: Ask thy young men, and they will show thee; wherefore let the young men find favour in thine eyes, for we come in a good day; give, I pray thee, whatsoever cometh to thine hand unto thy servants and thy son David. The request appealed to witnesses and to the good day of shearing. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

9 COMMENTARY: When David's young men came, they spoke to Nabal according to all those words in David's name, and ceased. The message was delivered exactly, and the messengers waited. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

10 COMMENTARY: Nabal answered with contempt, asking who David and the son of Jesse were, and saying many servants nowadays break away from their masters. He had no honour or honesty, Henry says, and was churlish, cross, ill-humoured, evil in his doings, hard and oppressive. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

11 COMMENTARY: Shall I then take my bread, my water, and my flesh killed for my shearers, and give it unto men whom I know not whence they be? Nabal went into a passion, Henry observes, as covetous men are apt to do when asked for anything, thinking thus to cover one sin with another, and by abusing the poor to excuse themselves from relieving them. But God will not thus be mocked. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

12 COMMENTARY: So David's young men turned their way, went again, and came and told David all those sayings. The refusal was reported word for word. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

13 COMMENTARY: David said unto his men, Gird ye on every man his sword, and they girded on, and David also girded on his sword, and about four hundred men went up after David, and two hundred abode by the stuff. David determined to destroy Nabal and all that belonged to him, Henry notes: is this thy voice, O David? Has he been so long in the school of affliction, where he should have learned patience, and yet is so passionate? — Matthew Henry (Concise)

14 COMMENTARY: But one of the young men told Abigail, Nabal's wife, saying, Behold, David sent messengers to salute our master, and he railed on them. A servant, not the master, saw the danger and spoke. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

15 COMMENTARY: But the men were very good unto us, and we were not hurt, neither missed we anything, as long as we were conversant with them in the fields. The servant's testimony confirmed David's claim in full. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

16 COMMENTARY: They were a wall unto us both by night and day, all the while we were with them keeping the sheep. David's men had been a living wall around Nabal's flocks. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

17 COMMENTARY: Now therefore know and consider what thou wilt do, for evil is determined against our master and all his household, for he is such a son of Belial that a man cannot speak to him. The household's only hope lay with Abigail. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

18 COMMENTARY: Then Abigail made haste and took two hundred loaves, two bottles of wine, five sheep ready dressed, five measures of parched corn, a hundred clusters of raisins, and two hundred cakes of figs, and laid them on asses. By a present Abigail atoned for Nabal's denial, Henry says; her behaviour was very submissive, and yielding pacifies great offences. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

19 COMMENTARY: She said to her servants, Go on before me; behold, I come after you. But she told not her husband Nabal. The gift went ahead, and the wife followed, without the husband's knowledge. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

20 COMMENTARY: As she rode on the ass and came down by the covert of the hill, behold, David and his men came down against her, and she met them. Wife and avenger met on the mountain road. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

21 COMMENTARY: Now David had said, Surely in vain have I kept all that this fellow hath in the wilderness, so that nothing was missed of all that pertained unto him, and he hath requited me evil for good. God is kind to the evil and unthankful, Henry asks, and why may not we be so? — Matthew Henry (Concise)

22 COMMENTARY: So and more also do God unto the enemies of David, if I leave of all that pertain to him by the morning light any that pisseth against the wall. The oath vowed the whole household's destruction by morning. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

23 COMMENTARY: When Abigail saw David, she hasted, lighted off the ass, fell before David on her face, and bowed herself to the ground. She put herself in the place of a penitent and a petitioner, Henry notes. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

24 COMMENTARY: She fell at his feet and said, Upon me, my lord, upon me let this iniquity be, and let thine handmaid speak in thine audience, and hear the words of thine handmaid. She took the iniquity on herself before she argued it. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

25 COMMENTARY: Let not my lord regard this man of Belial, even Nabal, for as his name is, so is he; Nabal is his name, and folly is with him; but I thine handmaid saw not the young men of my lord. She could not excuse her husband's conduct, Henry observes, and she depended not on her own reasonings but on God's grace to soften David. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

26 COMMENTARY: Now therefore, my lord, as the LORD liveth, and as thy soul liveth, seeing the LORD hath withholden thee from coming to shed blood and from avenging thyself with thine own hand, now let thine enemies, and they that seek evil to my lord, be as Nabal. She says it was below him to take vengeance on so weak and despicable an enemy as Nabal, Henry notes, who could do him no hurt as he had done him no kindness. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

27 COMMENTARY: And now this blessing which thine handmaid hath brought unto my lord, let it even be given unto the young men that follow my lord. The present was offered to the men, not to David's pride. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

28 COMMENTARY: I pray thee, forgive the trespass of thine handmaid, for the LORD will certainly make my lord a sure house, because my lord fighteth the battles of the LORD, and evil hath not been found in thee all thy days. She foretold the glorious end of David's troubles, Henry says: God will preserve thy life, therefore it becomes not thee unjustly to take away the lives of any, especially of the people of thy God. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

29 COMMENTARY: Yet a man is risen to pursue thee and seek thy soul, but the soul of my lord shall be bound in the bundle of life with the LORD thy God, and the souls of thine enemies, them shall he sling out, as out of the middle of a sling. The pursued king's life was bound up with God; his enemies' lives were sling-stones to be cast away. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

30 COMMENTARY: It shall come to pass, when the LORD shall have done to my lord according to all the good he hath spoken concerning thee, and shall have appointed thee ruler over Israel — Abigail looked past the present flight to the promised throne. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

31 COMMENTARY: That this shall be no grief unto thee, nor offence of heart unto my lord, either that thou hast shed blood causeless, or that my lord hath avenged himself; but when the LORD shall have dealt well with my lord, then remember thine handmaid. Abigail kept this argument for the last, Henry notes, as very powerful with so good a man: the less he indulged his passion, the more he consulted his peace and his conscience's repose. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

32 COMMENTARY: David said to Abigail, Blessed be the LORD God of Israel, which sent thee this day to meet me. David gave God thanks for sending him this happy check in a sinful way, Henry observes; whoever meets us with counsel, direction, comfort, caution, or seasonable reproof, we must see God sending them. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

33 COMMENTARY: And blessed be thy advice, and blessed be thou, which hast kept me this day from coming to shed blood and from avenging myself with mine own hand. Most people think it enough to take reproof patiently, Henry says, but few will take it thankfully, commend those who give it, and accept it as a favour; the nearer we are to committing sin, the greater the mercy of a seasonable restraint. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

34 COMMENTARY: For in very deed, as the LORD God of Israel liveth, which hath kept me back from hurting thee, except thou hadst hasted and come to meet me, surely there had not been left unto Nabal by the morning light any that pisseth against the wall. David owned how close he had come to the slaughter. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

35 COMMENTARY: So David received of her hand what she had brought him, and said, Go up in peace to thine house; see, I have hearkened to thy voice, and have accepted thy person. The gift was accepted, and the avenger became the protector. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

36 COMMENTARY: Abigail came to Nabal, and behold, he held a feast like the feast of a king, his heart merry, for he was very drunken, wherefore she told him nothing until the morning light. Sinners are often most secure when most in danger, Henry notes; he was very drunk, a sign he was Nabal, a fool who could not use plenty without abusing it. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

37 COMMENTARY: In the morning, when the wine was gone out of Nabal, his wife told him these things, and his heart died within him, and he became as a stone. His heart overnight merry with wine, next morning heavy as a stone, Henry reflects: so deceitful are carnal pleasures, so soon passes the laughter of the fool, and the end of that mirth is heaviness. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

38 COMMENTARY: About ten days after, the LORD smote Nabal, that he died. Worldly sorrow, mortified pride, and an affrighted conscience, Henry says, sometimes end the joys of the sensualist, but whatever the weapon, the Lord smites men with death when it pleases him. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

39 COMMENTARY: When David heard Nabal was dead, he said, Blessed be the LORD, that hath pleaded the cause of my reproach from Nabal's hand, and hath kept his servant from evil, for the LORD hath returned Nabal's wickedness upon his own head. And David sent and communed with Abigail, to take her to him to wife. David blessed God that he had been kept from killing Nabal. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

40 COMMENTARY: When David's servants came to Abigail to Carmel, saying, David sent us unto thee, to take thee to him to wife — the proposal followed the deliverance. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

41 COMMENTARY: She arose, bowed herself on her face to the earth, and said, Behold, let thine handmaid be a servant to wash the feet of the servants of my lord. Abigail believed David would be king, Henry notes, and greatly esteemed his pious and excellent character; she deemed the proposal honourable and advantageous, notwithstanding his present difficulties. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

42 COMMENTARY: Abigail hasted, arose, rode upon an ass, with five damsels after her, went after David's messengers, and became his wife. With great humility she consented, willing to share his trials; thus those who join themselves to Christ, Henry says, must be willing now to suffer with him, believing that hereafter they shall reign with him. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

43 COMMENTARY: David also took Ahinoam of Jezreel, and they were both of them his wives. The fugitive's household grew even in exile. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

44 COMMENTARY: But Saul had given Michal his daughter, David's wife, to Phalti son of Laish of Gallim. The king undid his own daughter's marriage while David married anew. — Matthew Henry (Concise)
