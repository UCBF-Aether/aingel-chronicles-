Study Notes — John

Chapter 3

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Nicodemus, a Pharisee and ruler of the Jews, came to Jesus by night, afraid or ashamed to be seen with him, as many are when religion is out of fashion. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: He confessed that Jesus was a teacher come from God, since no man could do such miracles unless God were with him. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: Jesus answered that unless a man is born again he cannot see the kingdom of God, for a new nature is the beginning of a new life. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: Nicodemus took the words literally, asking how a grown man could enter his mother's womb a second time, mistaking a spiritual birth for a bodily one. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: Jesus explained that the new birth is of water and of the Spirit, signified by baptism but wrought by the Spirit, without which no one enters the kingdom. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: That which is born of flesh is flesh, and that which is born of the Spirit is spirit, for a corrupt nature can only reproduce itself. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: Nicodemus was not to marvel at the necessity, for God's holiness, our depravity, and the happiness set before us make the change no strange thing. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: The wind blows where it pleases and its sound is heard though its path is unseen; so is everyone born of the Spirit, whose causes are hidden but whose effects are plain. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: Nicodemus still asked how these things could be, acknowledging his ignorance, which shows at least a desire to be better informed. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: Jesus answered that a master of Israel ought to know these things, for the Old Testament itself taught a circumcised heart and a new spirit. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: He spoke of what he knew and testified of what he had seen, yet his testimony was not received by those who make spiritual things strange to themselves. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: If earthly illustrations are not believed, heavenly things will be less so, and faith must receive what reason alone cannot prove. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: No man has ascended to heaven except he who came down from heaven, the Son of man who is in heaven, revealing the Father's will from above. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: As Moses lifted up the serpent in the wilderness, so must the Son of man be lifted up, that sinners stung by sin may look and live. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: Whoever believes in him shall not perish but have eternal life, for the remedy is applied simply by looking in faith to Christ crucified. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: God so loved the world that he gave his only begotten Son, a love so real and rich toward a worthless world that it should make us wonder. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: God sent his Son not to condemn the world but that the world through him might be saved, for there is no salvation in any other. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: Whoever believes in him is not condemned, though once a great sinner, because he is not dealt with according to what his sins deserve. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: This is the condemnation, that light came into the world and men loved darkness rather than light, because their deeds were evil. — Matthew Henry

20 COMMENTARY: Everyone who does evil hates the light and avoids it, lest his deeds should be reproved, for Christ is hated because sin is loved. — Matthew Henry

21 COMMENTARY: But whoever does truth comes to the light, desiring God's will to be known and done, that his deeds may be shown as wrought in God. — Matthew Henry

22 COMMENTARY: After this Jesus and his disciples came into the land of Judea, where he stayed and baptised, his ministry growing while John's waned. — Matthew Henry

23 COMMENTARY: John was also baptising at Aenon near Salim, because there was much water there, and people came and were baptised. — Matthew Henry

24 COMMENTARY: John had not yet been cast into prison, and his public work still ran alongside the beginning of Jesus' ministry. — Matthew Henry

25 COMMENTARY: A dispute arose between John's disciples and the Jews about purifying, the kind of dispute that easily turns into rivalry over who baptises. — Matthew Henry

26 COMMENTARY: John's disciples came to him complaining that the one he had witnessed to was now baptising and all men were going to him. — Matthew Henry

27 COMMENTARY: John answered that a man can receive nothing unless it is given him from heaven, and he was fully satisfied with the place and work assigned him. — Matthew Henry

28 COMMENTARY: He reminded them that he himself had said he was not the Christ but was sent before him, a forerunner, not the bridegroom. — Matthew Henry

29 COMMENTARY: He who has the bride is the bridegroom, and the friend of the bridegroom rejoices greatly at his voice, so John's joy was fulfilled in Christ's increase. — Matthew Henry

30 COMMENTARY: Christ must increase and John must decrease, for of his government and peace there will be no end while John's following lessens. — Matthew Henry

31 COMMENTARY: He who comes from above is above all, while he who is of the earth speaks of the earth, for John was a sinful, mortal man and Jesus came from heaven as the Son of God. — Matthew Henry

32 COMMENTARY: Jesus testifies what he has seen and heard, yet no man receives his testimony, so widespread is the refusal of plain truth. — Matthew Henry

33 COMMENTARY: But whoever receives his testimony sets his seal to this, that God is true, for Jesus' words are God's own words. — Matthew Henry

34 COMMENTARY: God gave the Spirit to Jesus not by measure, as to the prophets, but in all fullness, so his words carry divine authority. — Matthew Henry

35 COMMENTARY: The Father loves the Son and has given all things into his hand, placing the whole work of salvation under his authority. — Matthew Henry

36 COMMENTARY: Whoever believes on the Son has everlasting life, but whoever does not believe shall not see life, for the wrath of God abides on him. — Matthew Henry
