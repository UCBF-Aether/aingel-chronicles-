Study Notes — John

Chapter 2

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: On the third day there was a marriage in Cana of Galilee, and Jesus' mother was there, showing Christ's presence belongs at ordinary human joys. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: Jesus and his disciples were also invited, and those who would have Christ at their marriage must invite him by prayer. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: When the wine failed, Mary told Jesus of the want, humbly spreading the case before him and leaving the remedy to his pleasure. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: Jesus answered that his hour was not yet come, a reply of no disrespect, yet a standing witness against giving his mother undue honours. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: Mary told the servants to do whatever he said, and those who expect Christ's favours must observe his orders with ready obedience. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: Six stone waterpots stood there for Jewish purification, and the way of duty is the way to mercy: Christ works through ordinary means. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: Jesus told the servants to fill the pots with water, and they filled them to the brim, obeying without objecting to his methods. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: He then told them to draw out and carry some to the governor of the feast, trusting that Christ's word would make the water serve. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: The governor tasted water made wine and, not knowing its source, praised it as the best, for Christ's works commend themselves even to those who know not their Author. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: He remarked that men usually serve the good wine first and the worse afterward, but here the best had been kept until now, as Christ improves creature comforts into true comforts. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: This beginning of miracles at Cana manifested Jesus' glory, and his disciples believed on him, the sign confirming their faith. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: Afterward he went down to Capernaum with his mother, his brethren, and his disciples, and stayed there a few days. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: The Jewish Passover was at hand, and Jesus went up to Jerusalem, the first public work of his ministry awaiting him there. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: In the temple he found traders selling oxen, sheep, and doves, and money changers at work, a market made of God's house by covetous priests and rulers. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: Making a scourge of cords, he drove them all out with their sheep and oxen, poured out the changers' money, and overthrew their tables. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: To the dove sellers he said to take these things away and not make his Father's house a house of merchandise, for minds filled with worldly business profane worship. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: His disciples remembered the Scripture, that zeal for God's house had eaten him up, and understood his anger as holy zeal, not passion. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: The Jews demanded a sign for his authority to cleanse the temple, and Christ answered with a sign they would only understand later. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: He said, Destroy this temple, and in three days I will raise it up, foretelling his death by their malice and his resurrection by his own power. — Matthew Henry

20 COMMENTARY: They took him literally, objecting that the temple had taken forty-six years to build, for men mistake figurative Scripture by reading it only by the letter. — Matthew Henry

21 COMMENTARY: But Jesus spoke of the temple of his body, and the sign lay in his resurrection, not in a building of stone. — Matthew Henry

22 COMMENTARY: When he was risen, his disciples remembered what he had said and believed the Scripture and his word, for fulfilled prophecy unlocks the meaning of prophecy. — Matthew Henry

23 COMMENTARY: While he was in Jerusalem at the Passover, many believed in his name when they saw the miracles he did. — Matthew Henry

24 COMMENTARY: But Jesus did not commit himself to them, because he knew all men and discerned a faith resting only on wonders. — Matthew Henry

25 COMMENTARY: He needed no testimony about man, for he knew what was in man, trying the heart where others see only profession; beware of a dead, formal faith that cannot deceive the heart-searching God. — Matthew Henry
