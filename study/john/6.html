Study Notes — John

Chapter 6

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: After these things Jesus went over the Sea of Galilee, also called the Sea of Tiberias, withdrawing for a season from Judea. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: A great multitude followed him because they saw the miracles he did on the diseased, drawn by wonders more than by doctrine. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: Jesus went up into a mountain and sat there with his disciples, preparing to teach as well as to feed. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: The Passover was near, and the feast season swelled the crowds that sought him. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: Seeing a great company coming, Jesus asked Philip where bread should be bought for them to eat, testing his faith. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: He asked this to prove Philip, for he himself knew what he would do, and the question exposed the limits of human reckoning. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: Philip answered that two hundred pennyworth of bread would not suffice for each to take a little, counting only the cost. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: Andrew, Simon Peter's brother, pointed out a lad with five barley loaves and two small fishes, a small supply for so many. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: He added the obvious doubt, what are they among so many, for human provision always looks too little beside human need. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: Jesus told them to make the men sit down, and there was much grass in the place, so about five thousand men sat in order. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: Jesus took the loaves, gave thanks, and distributed them to the disciples, and the disciples to those seated, and likewise of the fishes as much as they would. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: When they were filled, he told his disciples to gather up the fragments that remained, that nothing be lost, for Christ's works are for use and not waste. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: They gathered twelve baskets full of fragments from the five barley loaves, more left over than they had begun with. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: Seeing the miracle, the men said this was of a truth that Prophet who should come into the world, acknowledging him as the expected Messiah. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: Perceiving they would come and take him by force to make him king, Jesus departed again into a mountain alone, refusing a worldly kingdom. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: When evening came, his disciples went down to the sea, returning to their ordinary course in his absence. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: They entered a ship toward Capernaum, and it was now dark, and Jesus had not yet come to them, so they were alone in the night. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: The sea arose by reason of a great wind, and disciples in the way of duty may still meet perils, for clouds often surround the children of light. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: Having rowed about twenty-five or thirty furlongs, they saw Jesus walking on the sea and drawing near the ship, and they were afraid, mistaking comfort for danger. — Matthew Henry

20 COMMENTARY: He said, It is I, be not afraid, the word that comforts saints as surely as it convicts sinners. — Matthew Henry

21 COMMENTARY: Then they willingly received him into the ship, and immediately the ship was at the land where they went, for with Christ aboard the shore is soon reached. — Matthew Henry

22 COMMENTARY: The next day the people on the other side saw there had been only one boat, and Jesus had not entered it with his disciples, yet his disciples had gone away alone. — Matthew Henry

23 COMMENTARY: Other boats had come from Tiberias near the place where they ate bread after the Lord had given thanks, and the crowd traced his movements by these signs. — Matthew Henry

24 COMMENTARY: When the people saw Jesus was not there, nor his disciples, they took shipping and came to Capernaum seeking him. — Matthew Henry

25 COMMENTARY: Finding him across the sea, they asked, Rabbi, when camest thou hither, curious about his coming rather than his teaching. — Matthew Henry

26 COMMENTARY: Jesus answered that they sought him not because they saw miracles, but because they ate of the loaves and were filled, seeking bread rather than the Bread. — Matthew Henry

27 COMMENTARY: He told them to labour not for meat that perishes, but for meat that endures to everlasting life, which the Son of man gives, for him the Father has sealed. — Matthew Henry

28 COMMENTARY: They asked what they should do to work the works of God, still thinking of works they could perform. — Matthew Henry

29 COMMENTARY: Jesus answered that the work of God is to believe on him whom God has sent, for constant exercise of faith is the most important and difficult part of obedience. — Matthew Henry

30 COMMENTARY: They asked what sign he would show that they might see and believe him, and what work he would do, demanding fresh proof after the feeding. — Matthew Henry

31 COMMENTARY: They cited the manna, saying their fathers ate manna in the desert, as written, He gave them bread from heaven to eat. — Matthew Henry

32 COMMENTARY: Jesus answered that Moses did not give them the true bread from heaven, but his Father gives the true bread from heaven. — Matthew Henry

33 COMMENTARY: For the bread of God is he who comes down from heaven and gives life to the world, not a food for the body only. — Matthew Henry

34 COMMENTARY: They said, Lord, evermore give us this bread, asking with earnestness, though still thinking of an earthly gift. — Matthew Henry

35 COMMENTARY: Jesus said, I am the bread of life; he who comes to me shall never hunger, and he who believes on me shall never thirst, for coming and believing are the same act. — Matthew Henry

36 COMMENTARY: But he said they had also seen him and yet did not believe, for men may acknowledge Christ as a prophet and still turn a deaf ear to him. — Matthew Henry

37 COMMENTARY: All that the Father gives him shall come to him, and whoever comes he will in no wise cast out, for the Father's will is that none given to the Son be lost. — Matthew Henry

38 COMMENTARY: For he came down from heaven not to do his own will, but the will of him who sent him. — Matthew Henry

39 COMMENTARY: This is the Father's will, that of all he has given him he should lose nothing, but raise it up at the last day. — Matthew Henry

40 COMMENTARY: And this is the will of him who sent him, that everyone who sees the Son and believes on him may have everlasting life, and he will raise him up at the last day. — Matthew Henry

41 COMMENTARY: The Jews murmured at him because he said he was the bread which came down from heaven, offended at a claim that required faith. — Matthew Henry

42 COMMENTARY: They said, Is not this Jesus, the son of Joseph, whose father and mother we know, and how then does he say he came down from heaven, judging by outward appearance. — Matthew Henry

43 COMMENTARY: Jesus answered, Murmur not among yourselves, for murmuring hardens the heart against the very doctrine that could save it. — Matthew Henry

44 COMMENTARY: No man can come to him unless the Father who sent him draws him, and he will raise him at the last day, for no one comes until divine grace has subdued his heart. — Matthew Henry

45 COMMENTARY: It is written in the prophets that they shall all be taught of God, and everyone who has heard and learned of the Father comes to Christ. — Matthew Henry

46 COMMENTARY: Not that any man has seen the Father, except he who is of God, for he has seen the Father, and the Jews must expect to be taught by his word and inward power. — Matthew Henry

47 COMMENTARY: Whoever believes on him has everlasting life, a present possession, not a distant hope. — Matthew Henry

48 COMMENTARY: He repeated, I am that bread of life, the true Bread for the salvation of souls, as bread is to the body. — Matthew Henry

49 COMMENTARY: Their fathers ate manna in the wilderness and are dead, for the advantage of manna was small, referring only to this life. — Matthew Henry

50 COMMENTARY: This is the bread which comes down from heaven, that a man may eat of it and not die. — Matthew Henry

51 COMMENTARY: He is the living bread which came down from heaven, and if any man eats of this bread he shall live forever, and the bread he will give is his flesh, given for the life of the world. — Matthew Henry

52 COMMENTARY: The Jews therefore strove among themselves, asking how this man could give them his flesh to eat, taking a spiritual saying in carnal terms. — Matthew Henry

53 COMMENTARY: Jesus answered that except they eat the flesh of the Son of man and drink his blood, they have no life in them, for eating and drinking mean believing in Christ crucified. — Matthew Henry

54 COMMENTARY: Whoever eats his flesh and drinks his blood has eternal life, and he will raise him up at the last day. — Matthew Henry

55 COMMENTARY: For his flesh is meat indeed, and his blood is drink indeed, true nourishment purchased by the breaking of his body and the shedding of his blood. — Matthew Henry

56 COMMENTARY: Whoever eats his flesh and drinks his blood dwells in him, and he in him, as members live by the head and branches by the root. — Matthew Henry

57 COMMENTARY: As the living Father sent him and he lives by the Father, so whoever eats him shall live by him, because he lives we shall live also. — Matthew Henry

58 COMMENTARY: This is the bread which came down from heaven, not as the fathers ate manna and are dead, for whoever eats this bread shall live forever. — Matthew Henry

59 COMMENTARY: These things he said in the synagogue as he taught in Capernaum, speaking openly where the people gathered. — Matthew Henry

60 COMMENTARY: Many of his disciples, hearing this, said this was a hard saying, and who could hear it, fastening on the difficulty rather than the promise. — Matthew Henry

61 COMMENTARY: When Jesus knew his disciples murmured at it, he asked whether this offended them, searching their hearts as he always did. — Matthew Henry

62 COMMENTARY: He asked what they would think if they saw the Son of man ascend where he was before, for his human nature, being joined to God, had truly come down from heaven. — Matthew Henry

63 COMMENTARY: It is the Spirit that quickens, the flesh profits nothing, and his words are spirit and life, for without the quickening Spirit all forms of religion are dead. — Matthew Henry

64 COMMENTARY: But there were some who did not believe, and Jesus knew from the beginning who believed not and who would betray him. — Matthew Henry

65 COMMENTARY: Therefore he said no man can come to him unless it is given him of the Father, for he alone can draw men to live by faith in him. — Matthew Henry

66 COMMENTARY: From that time many of his disciples went back and walked no more with him, for hard thoughts of Christ's words, admitted into the mind, end in drawing back. — Matthew Henry

67 COMMENTARY: Then Jesus said to the twelve, Will ye also go away, putting the searching question to those who remained. — Matthew Henry

68 COMMENTARY: Simon Peter answered, Lord, to whom shall we go, for thou hast the words of eternal life, and if we forsake Christ we forsake our own mercies. — Matthew Henry

69 COMMENTARY: And we believe and are sure that thou art that Christ, the Son of the living God, holding to first principles when others fell away. — Matthew Henry

70 COMMENTARY: Jesus answered, Have I not chosen you twelve, and one of you is a devil, warning them that nearness to Christ does not itself make a man true. — Matthew Henry

71 COMMENTARY: He spoke of Judas Iscariot, son of Simon, for he it was who would betray him, being one of the twelve. — Matthew Henry
