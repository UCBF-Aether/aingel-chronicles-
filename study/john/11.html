Study Notes — John

Chapter 11

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Now a certain man was sick, named Lazarus, of Bethany, the town of Mary and her sister Martha, a house where the fear of God was and his blessing rested. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: It was that Mary who anointed the Lord with ointment and wiped his feet with her hair, whose brother Lazarus was sick, and the family is named by its best-known act of love. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: Therefore his sisters sent to Jesus, saying, Lord, behold, he whom thou lovest is sick, applying to him on behalf of an afflicted friend, as we ought to do. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: When Jesus heard that, he said, This sickness is not unto death, but for the glory of God, that the Son of God might be glorified thereby, for sickness, loss, and disappointment are all for God's glory, and if God be glorified we ought to be satisfied. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: Now Jesus loved Martha, and her sister, and Lazarus, and those are most happy whom Jesus loves and by whom he is beloved. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: When he had heard therefore that he was sick, he abode two days still in the same place where he was, for God has gracious intentions even when he seems to delay, and deliverance only waits for the right time. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: Then after that he said to his disciples, Let us go into Judea again, for Christ never brings his people into danger but goes with them in it. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: His disciples said, Master, the Jews of late sought to stone thee, and goest thou thither again, for we are apt to think ourselves zealous for the Lord when we are only zealous for our own safety. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: Jesus answered, Are there not twelve hours in the day, and if any man walks in the day, he stumbles not, because he sees the light of this world, for a man has comfort while in the way of duty set by God's word and providence. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: But if a man walks in the night, he stumbles, because there is no light in him, for light in us is to moral actions what light about us is to natural ones. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: These things said he, and after that he said, Our friend Lazarus sleeps, but I go, that I may awake him out of sleep, for since we shall rise again, death to the believer is but sleep, a rest from labour. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: Then his disciples said, Lord, if he sleeps, he shall do well, thinking it needless for Christ to go and expose himself and them. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: However, Jesus spoke of his death, but they thought he had spoken of taking of rest in sleep, mistaking the figure for the fact. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: Then Jesus said to them plainly, Lazarus is dead, removing the figure with plain speech. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: And I am glad for your sakes that I was not there, to the intent you may believe, for the raising would perfect the faith of those who believed, and bring many to believe. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: Then Thomas, called Didymus, said to his fellow disciples, Let us also go, that we may die with him, and in difficult times Christians should encourage one another as Thomas did. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: Then when Jesus came, he found that he had lain in the grave four days already, so the case was past all human hope. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: Now Bethany was near unto Jerusalem, about fifteen furlongs off, close enough for many mourners to come. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: And many of the Jews came to Martha and Mary, to comfort them concerning their brother, for even a house of God's fear may become a house of mourning. — Matthew Henry

20 COMMENTARY: Then Martha, as soon as she heard Jesus was coming, went and met him, but Mary sat still in the house, for Martha went forth by faith, hope, and prayer to meet mercy coming toward her. — Matthew Henry

21 COMMENTARY: Then Martha said to Jesus, Lord, if thou hadst been here, my brother had not died, speaking her grief and her faith together. — Matthew Henry

22 COMMENTARY: But I know, she added, that even now, whatsoever thou wilt ask of God, God will give it thee, referring herself to God and leaving him to do as seems good. — Matthew Henry

23 COMMENTARY: Jesus said to her, Thy brother shall rise again, a promise meant to enlarge her expectations. — Matthew Henry

24 COMMENTARY: Martha said, I know he shall rise again in the resurrection at the last day, holding the general hope but not yet the present power. — Matthew Henry

25 COMMENTARY: Jesus said to her, I am the resurrection, and the life; he who believes in me, though he were dead, yet shall he live, for in every sense he is the Resurrection, the source, substance, first-fruits, and cause of it. — Matthew Henry

26 COMMENTARY: And whoever lives and believes in me shall never die, he said; believest thou this, putting the great truth to her as a personal question. — Matthew Henry

27 COMMENTARY: She said, Yea, Lord, I believe thou art the Christ, the Son of God, who should come into the world, confessing him fully. — Matthew Henry

28 COMMENTARY: And when she had said so, she went her way and called Mary her sister secretly, saying, The Master is come, and calls for thee, for when Christ comes he calls for us, in his word and ordinances. — Matthew Henry

29 COMMENTARY: As soon as Mary heard that, she arose quickly and came unto him, for those taught at his feet in peace may with comfort cast themselves at his feet in trouble. — Matthew Henry

30 COMMENTARY: Now Jesus was not yet come into the town, but was in the place where Martha met him. — Matthew Henry

31 COMMENTARY: The Jews then who were with her in the house and comforted her, when they saw Mary rise hastily and go out, followed her, saying, She goes unto the grave to weep there. — Matthew Henry

32 COMMENTARY: Then when Mary was come where Jesus was and saw him, she fell down at his feet, saying, Lord, if thou hadst been here, my brother had not died, repeating Martha's grief at his feet. — Matthew Henry

33 COMMENTARY: When Jesus therefore saw her weeping, and the Jews also weeping who came with her, he groaned in the spirit and was troubled, for in all the afflictions of believers he is afflicted. — Matthew Henry

34 COMMENTARY: And he said, Where have ye laid him, and they said, Lord, come and see, for his concern was shown by his kind inquiry after his friend's remains. — Matthew Henry

35 COMMENTARY: Jesus wept, a man of sorrows and acquainted with grief, whose tears of compassion resemble those he would have us shed, not for tales of distress but for real woe. — Matthew Henry

36 COMMENTARY: Then said the Jews, Behold how he loved him, reading his tears as love. — Matthew Henry

37 COMMENTARY: And some of them said, Could not this man, who opened the eyes of the blind, have caused that even this man should not have died, limiting his power by their unbelief. — Matthew Henry

38 COMMENTARY: Jesus therefore again groaning in himself came to the grave, which was a cave, and a stone lay upon it. — Matthew Henry

39 COMMENTARY: Jesus said, Take ye away the stone, and Martha, the sister of him that was dead, said, Lord, by this time he stinks, for he has been dead four days, for it is a good step toward raising a soul when the stone is taken away and prejudices are removed. — Matthew Henry

40 COMMENTARY: Jesus said to her, Said I not unto thee, that if thou wouldest believe, thou shouldest see the glory of God, for if we take Christ's word and rely on his power, we shall see God's glory. — Matthew Henry

41 COMMENTARY: Then they took away the stone from the place where the dead was laid, and Jesus lifted up his eyes and said, Father, I thank thee that thou hast heard me, teaching us by example to call God Father with humble reverence and holy boldness. — Matthew Henry

42 COMMENTARY: And I knew thou hearest me always, he said, but because of the people who stand by I said it, that they may believe thou hast sent me, for he prayed aloud that they might be convinced the Father had sent him. — Matthew Henry

43 COMMENTARY: And when he had thus spoken, he cried with a loud voice, Lazarus, come forth, a figure of the gospel call by which dead souls are brought out of the grave of sin, and of the archangel's trumpet at the last day. — Matthew Henry

44 COMMENTARY: And he who was dead came forth, bound hand and foot with graveclothes, and his face bound about with a napkin, and Jesus said, Loose him, and let him go, for the grave of sin is no place for those Christ has quickened, and they must lay aside every weight. — Matthew Henry

45 COMMENTARY: Then many of the Jews who came to Mary and had seen the things Jesus did, believed on him, for the raising brought many to faith. — Matthew Henry

46 COMMENTARY: But some of them went their ways to the Pharisees and told them what Jesus had done, carrying the news to his enemies. — Matthew Henry

47 COMMENTARY: Then the chief priests and Pharisees gathered a council and said, What do we, for this man does many miracles, a clear discovery of the madness and desperate enmity of man's heart. — Matthew Henry

48 COMMENTARY: If we let him thus alone, they said, all men will believe on him, and the Romans shall come and take away both our place and nation, for the calamity they sought to escape by sin was the very course to bring it on their heads. — Matthew Henry

49 COMMENTARY: And one of them, named Caiaphas, being high priest that same year, said, Ye know nothing at all, speaking with the arrogance of office. — Matthew Henry

50 COMMENTARY: Nor consider, he said, that it is expedient for us that one man should die for the people, and that the whole nation perish not, a counsel of worldly policy. — Matthew Henry

51 COMMENTARY: And this he spoke not of himself, but being high priest that year, he prophesied that Jesus should die for that nation, for words of prophecy in the mouth are not evidence of grace in the heart. — Matthew Henry

52 COMMENTARY: And not for that nation only, but that also he should gather together in one the children of God scattered abroad, for by dying he purchased them and the gift of the Holy Ghost, and his love should unite them. — Matthew Henry

53 COMMENTARY: Then from that day forth they took counsel together to put him to death, settling their enmity into a settled plot. — Matthew Henry

54 COMMENTARY: Jesus therefore walked no more openly among the Jews, but went thence unto a country near the wilderness, into a city called Ephraim, and there continued with his disciples. — Matthew Henry

55 COMMENTARY: And the Jews' Passover was near at hand, and many went out of the country up to Jerusalem before the Passover, to purify themselves, for before the gospel Passover we must renew repentance and solemnly prepare to meet God. — Matthew Henry

56 COMMENTARY: Then they sought for Jesus, and spoke among themselves as they stood in the temple, What think ye, that he will not come to the feast, wondering whether he would appear. — Matthew Henry

57 COMMENTARY: Now both the chief priests and Pharisees had given a commandment, that if any man knew where he were, he should show it, that they might take him, for no devices of man can alter the purposes of God, yet they plotted still. — Matthew Henry
