Study Notes — John

Chapter 5

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: After this there was a feast of the Jews, and Jesus went up to Jerusalem, where his next public work awaited him. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: By the sheep market there was a pool called Bethesda, having five porches, a place of resort for the sick of the city. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: In those porches lay a great multitude of impotent folk, blind, halt, and withered, waiting for the moving of the water, a picture of our natural impotence in spiritual things. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: An angel went down at a certain season and troubled the water, and whoever first stepped in was healed, teaching us not to let a season slip which may never return. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: A certain man had been infirm for thirty-eight years, long in affliction, yet God keeps account of how long his people suffer. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: When Jesus saw him and knew he had long been in that condition, he asked whether he would be made whole, singling this one out from the rest. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: The man answered that he had no one to put him into the pool when the water was troubled, and another stepped in before him, speaking of others' unkindness without peevish complaint. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: Jesus said, Rise, take up thy bed, and walk, a command that supposed no power in the man apart from Christ's grace, yet carried the power with it. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: Immediately the man was made whole, took up his bed, and walked, and the proof of spiritual cure is likewise our rising and walking. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: The Jews told the healed man it was the Sabbath and unlawful to carry his bed, turning a mercy into an occasion for dispute about the law. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: He answered that he who had made him whole had told him to take up his bed and walk, resting his obedience on the authority of his healer. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: They asked what man had told him to do so, caring more for the supposed breach of the Sabbath than for the miracle itself. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: The healed man did not know who it was, for Jesus had conveyed himself away in the multitude, seeking no display. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: Afterward Jesus found him in the temple and said, Behold, thou art made whole; sin no more, lest a worse thing come unto thee, for those eased of punishment are in danger of returning to sin. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: The man departed and told the Jews it was Jesus who had made him whole, reporting the fact plainly without malice. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: Therefore the Jews persecuted Jesus and sought to slay him, because he had done these things on the Sabbath day. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: Jesus answered, My Father worketh hitherto, and I work, declaring that he worked with and like his Father, as he saw good. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: Therefore the Jews sought the more to kill him, because he had not only broken the Sabbath in their eyes but called God his own Father, making himself equal with God. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: Jesus answered that the Son can do nothing of himself, but what he sees the Father do, for these he does likewise, in entire agreement with the Father. — Matthew Henry

20 COMMENTARY: For the Father loves the Son and shows him all things he himself does, and will show him greater works, that men may marvel. — Matthew Henry

21 COMMENTARY: As the Father raises the dead and quickens them, so the Son quickens whom he will, for the same divine power belongs to both. — Matthew Henry

22 COMMENTARY: For the Father judges no man, but has committed all judgment to the Son, purposely that all men might honour the Son as they honour the Father. — Matthew Henry

23 COMMENTARY: Whoever does not honour the Son does not honour the Father who sent him, whatever he may think or pretend. — Matthew Henry

24 COMMENTARY: Whoever hears Christ's word and believes on him who sent him has everlasting life and shall not come into condemnation, but is passed from death unto life. — Matthew Henry

25 COMMENTARY: The hour is coming, and now is, when the dead shall hear the voice of the Son of God and live, first the dead in sin raised to newness of life by the Spirit. — Matthew Henry

26 COMMENTARY: As the Father has life in himself, so has he given to the Son to have life in himself, the fountain of life dwelling in both. — Matthew Henry

27 COMMENTARY: He has also given the Son authority to execute judgment, because he is the Son of man, who knows all and can do all. — Matthew Henry

28 COMMENTARY: Marvel not at this, for the hour is coming when all in the graves shall hear his voice. — Matthew Henry

29 COMMENTARY: They shall come forth, those who have done good to the resurrection of life, and those who have done evil to the resurrection of damnation, for the office of Judge belongs to one of almighty power and knowledge. — Matthew Henry

30 COMMENTARY: Jesus said he could do nothing of himself, but as he heard he judged, and his judgment was just, because he sought not his own will but the Father's. — Matthew Henry

31 COMMENTARY: If he bore witness of himself alone, his witness would not be counted true, for the law required more than one witness. — Matthew Henry

32 COMMENTARY: There was another who bore witness of him, and Jesus knew that witness was true, pointing beyond himself to the Father's testimony. — Matthew Henry

33 COMMENTARY: The Jews had sent to John, and John bore witness unto the truth, a burning and shining light they had once welcomed for a season. — Matthew Henry

34 COMMENTARY: Jesus said he did not receive testimony from men, but spoke these things that they might be saved, using John's witness for their sake, not his own need. — Matthew Henry

35 COMMENTARY: John was a burning and shining light, and the Jews were willing for a season to rejoice in his light, yet they did not follow it to Christ. — Matthew Henry

36 COMMENTARY: But Jesus had greater witness than John's, for the works the Father gave him to finish bore witness that the Father had sent him. — Matthew Henry

37 COMMENTARY: The Father himself who sent him bore witness of him, yet the Jews had neither heard his voice nor seen his shape, for his word had no abiding place in their proud hearts. — Matthew Henry

38 COMMENTARY: They did not have his word abiding in them, because they did not believe him whom the Father had sent, according to ancient promises. — Matthew Henry

39 COMMENTARY: Jesus urged them to search the Scriptures, for in them they thought they had eternal life, and those Scriptures testified of him. — Matthew Henry

40 COMMENTARY: Yet they would not come to him that they might have life, for it is possible to be studious in the letter of Scripture and a stranger to its power. — Matthew Henry

41 COMMENTARY: Jesus said he received not honour from men, seeking no applause as the ground of his mission. — Matthew Henry

42 COMMENTARY: But he knew them, that they did not have the love of God in them, for they slighted Christ because they overvalued themselves. — Matthew Henry

43 COMMENTARY: He had come in his Father's name and they received him not, yet if another came in his own name they would receive him, for men welcome flatterers who seek their praise. — Matthew Henry

44 COMMENTARY: How could they believe, receiving honour from one another and not seeking the honour that comes from God only, when making human applause their idol? — Matthew Henry

45 COMMENTARY: Jesus said not to think he would accuse them to the Father, for Moses, in whom they trusted, was their accuser. — Matthew Henry

46 COMMENTARY: For had they believed Moses, they would have believed Christ, for Moses wrote of him, and Christ is the great subject of the Scriptures. — Matthew Henry

47 COMMENTARY: But if they did not believe Moses' writings, how should they believe Christ's words, since the same unbelief that rejected the law rejected the gospel it foretold. — Matthew Henry
