Study Notes — John

Chapter 10

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Verily, verily, whoever enters not by the door into the sheepfold, but climbs up some other way, is a thief and a robber, for the church is exposed to deceivers who will not come by Christ. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: But he who enters in by the door is the shepherd of the sheep, coming lawfully and openly to his own charge. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: To him the porter opens, and the sheep hear his voice, and he calls his own sheep by name and leads them out, for the great Shepherd knows all that are his. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: When he puts forth his own sheep, he goes before them, and the sheep follow him, for they know his voice, as Eastern shepherds went before their flocks to set them in the way. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: A stranger they will not follow, but will flee from him, for they know not the voice of strangers, and Christ's sheep are cautious and shy of those who would draw them to fancies about him. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: This parable Jesus spoke to them, but they understood not what things they were which he spoke, for many hear Christ's word and do not understand it because they will not. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: Then Jesus said again, Verily, verily, I am the door of the sheep, expounding one Scripture by another for those who would learn. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: All who ever came before him claiming the shepherd's place were thieves and robbers, but the sheep did not hear them, for true sheep discern a false voice. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: I am the door, he said; by me if any man enter in, he shall be saved, and shall go in and out and find pasture, for Christ is a door open for passage and communication, and the church's security is that he stands between it and its enemies. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: The thief comes only to steal, kill, and destroy, but Christ came that they might have life, and have it more abundantly. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: I am the good shepherd, he said; the good shepherd gives his life for the sheep, for careless shepherds hurt the flock by neglect, but Christ lays down his life for it. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: But he who is a hireling, and not the shepherd, whose own the sheep are not, sees the wolf coming and leaves the sheep and flees, and the wolf catches them and scatters the sheep. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: The hireling flees because he is a hireling and cares not for the sheep, for bad principles are the root of bad practices. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: I am the good shepherd, and know my sheep, and am known of mine, for the Lord knows whom he has chosen and is sure of them, and they know whom they have trusted. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: As the Father knows me, even so know I the Father, and I lay down my life for the sheep, for none could demand his life of him; he laid it down of himself for our redemption. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: And other sheep I have which are not of this fold, them also I must bring, and they shall hear my voice, and there shall be one fold and one shepherd, for his flock gathers beyond Israel. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: Therefore my Father loves me, because I lay down my life that I might take it again, for he offered himself to be both Saviour and Sacrifice. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: No man takes it from me, but I lay it down of myself; I have power to lay it down, and power to take it again, and this commandment I received of my Father, for his death was his own offering in the place and stead of men. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: There was a division again among the Jews for these sayings, for the same words divide hearers still. — Matthew Henry

20 COMMENTARY: Many said, He has a devil, and is mad; why hear you him, for Satan ruins many by putting them out of conceit with the word. — Matthew Henry

21 COMMENTARY: Others said, These are not the words of him who has a devil; can a devil open the eyes of the blind, arguing from his works against the slander. — Matthew Henry

22 COMMENTARY: And it was at Jerusalem the feast of the dedication, and it was winter, the season when Jesus walked in Solomon's porch. — Matthew Henry

23 COMMENTARY: And Jesus walked in the temple in Solomon's porch, where all who had anything to say to Christ might find him. — Matthew Henry

24 COMMENTARY: Then the Jews came round about him and said, How long dost thou make us to doubt; if thou be the Christ, tell us plainly, for they understood his meaning but could not form his words into a full charge. — Matthew Henry

25 COMMENTARY: Jesus answered, I told you, and you believed not; the works I do in my Father's name, they bear witness of me, for Christ would make us believe, and we make ourselves doubt. — Matthew Henry

26 COMMENTARY: But you believe not, because you are not of my sheep, as I said unto you, for unbelief marks those who are not his. — Matthew Henry

27 COMMENTARY: My sheep hear my voice, and I know them, and they follow me, the gracious disposition and happy state of his flock. — Matthew Henry

28 COMMENTARY: And I give unto them eternal life, and they shall never perish, neither shall any man pluck them out of my hand, for the Son is able to defend his sheep against all enemies. — Matthew Henry

29 COMMENTARY: My Father, who gave them to me, is greater than all, and no man is able to pluck them out of my Father's hand, for their safety rests on the Father's power as well as the Son's. — Matthew Henry

30 COMMENTARY: I and my Father are one, he said, claiming divine power and perfection equally with the Father. — Matthew Henry

31 COMMENTARY: Then the Jews took up stones again to stone him, for the claim of unity with the Father was, to them, blasphemy. — Matthew Henry

32 COMMENTARY: Jesus answered, Many good works have I shown you from my Father; for which of those works do you stone me, appealing to works of power and mercy that proclaimed him God blessed forever. — Matthew Henry

33 COMMENTARY: The Jews answered, For a good work we stone thee not, but for blasphemy, because thou, being a man, makest thyself God. — Matthew Henry

34 COMMENTARY: Jesus answered, Is it not written in your law, I said, Ye are gods, quoting their own Scriptures against their charge. — Matthew Henry

35 COMMENTARY: If he called them gods, unto whom the word of God came, and the Scripture cannot be broken, then the title in Scripture is not itself blasphemy. — Matthew Henry

36 COMMENTARY: Say you of him whom the Father has sanctified and sent into the world, Thou blasphemest, because I said, I am the Son of God, for whom the Father sends, he sanctifies, and the holy God employs none but such as he makes holy. — Matthew Henry

37 COMMENTARY: If I do not the works of my Father, believe me not, he said, letting his works be the test. — Matthew Henry

38 COMMENTARY: But if I do, though you believe not me, believe the works, that you may know and believe that the Father is in me, and I in him, for the Father was in the Son by divine power in his miracles, and the Son in the Father by knowledge of his whole mind. — Matthew Henry

39 COMMENTARY: Therefore they sought again to take him, but he escaped out of their hand, for no weapon formed against him shall prosper until his hour comes. — Matthew Henry

40 COMMENTARY: And he went away again beyond Jordan into the place where John at first baptised, and there he abode, withdrawing from their violence without fear. — Matthew Henry

41 COMMENTARY: And many resorted to him and said, John did no miracle, but all things that John spoke of this man were true, for John's witness stood confirmed by Christ's works. — Matthew Henry

42 COMMENTARY: And many believed on him there, for when Christ is known by faith in the heart, all Scripture says of him is found true. — Matthew Henry
