Study Notes — John

Chapter 4

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: When Jesus knew the Pharisees had heard he was making and baptising more disciples than John, he withdrew, preferring preaching to any rivalry over numbers. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: Jesus himself baptised not, but his disciples did, teaching that the benefit of the sacrament depends not on the hand that administers it. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: He therefore left Judea and departed again into Galilee, avoiding needless contention while his work went on. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: He must needs go through Samaria, taking the direct road where hatred between Jews and Samaritans made the journey itself a test of his mission. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: He came to Sychar, near the parcel of ground Jacob gave to Joseph, grounding his ministry in the old history of Israel. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: Jacob's well was there, and Jesus, wearied with his journey, sat by it at about the sixth hour, truly a man under the common fatigue of travellers. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: A woman of Samaria came to draw water, and Jesus asked her for a drink, showing no anger of his nation against hers. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: His disciples had gone into the city to buy food, leaving him alone to begin a conversation no Jew was expected to hold. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: The woman was surprised that a Jew would ask a Samaritan for a drink, since Jews had no dealings with Samaritans, yet moderate men on all sides are wondered at. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: Jesus answered that if she knew the gift of God and who spoke to her, she would have asked him, and he would have given her living water, the Spirit promised in the Old Testament. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: She took him literally, objecting that he had nothing to draw with and the well was deep, as carnal hearts look no higher than carnal means. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: She asked whether he was greater than Jacob, who gave the well and drank from it with his family and cattle, measuring him by the patriarch's gift. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: Jesus answered that whoever drinks of this well's water shall thirst again, for all earthly comforts yield only short satisfaction. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: But whoever drinks of the water he gives shall never thirst, for it becomes a well springing up into everlasting life, abundantly satisfying the soul. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: She answered with a carnal wish, asking for the water so she need not thirst or come to draw, ingeniously shifting conviction away from herself. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: Jesus then brought conviction home, telling her to go and call her husband, and so confronting her present state of life. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: She answered that she had no husband, and Jesus said she had answered truly, for the power of his word in searching secret things proved his divine authority. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: He told her she had had five husbands and the man she now had was not her husband, a severe reproof that she did not dispute. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: She then perceived he was a prophet, since only divine knowledge could lay bare her hidden life. — Matthew Henry

20 COMMENTARY: She turned the talk to the old dispute, saying their fathers worshipped on this mountain while the Jews said Jerusalem was the place. — Matthew Henry

21 COMMENTARY: Jesus answered that the hour was coming when worship would be tied to neither mountain nor Jerusalem, for such differences about place are passing away. — Matthew Henry

22 COMMENTARY: He added that the Samaritans worshipped what they did not know, while the Jews knew what they worshipped, for salvation was of the Jews and came to other nations through them. — Matthew Henry

23 COMMENTARY: But the hour was coming, and now was, when true worshippers would worship the Father in spirit and in truth, for the Father seeks such worshippers. — Matthew Henry

24 COMMENTARY: God is a Spirit, and those who worship him must worship in spirit and in truth, with fervent prayer and thanksgiving from an upright heart. — Matthew Henry

25 COMMENTARY: The woman said she knew Messiah was coming and would tell them all things, leaving the matter undecided until his coming. — Matthew Henry

26 COMMENTARY: Jesus answered plainly, I that speak unto thee am he, revealing himself more fully to this alien Samaritan woman than as yet to any of his disciples. — Matthew Henry

27 COMMENTARY: Upon this his disciples came and marvelled that he talked with a woman, yet they trusted that all Jesus said and did was well, and asked no question. — Matthew Henry

28 COMMENTARY: The woman left her waterpot and went into the city, so affected by his knowledge and his word that she became a messenger at once. — Matthew Henry

29 COMMENTARY: She urged the men to come and see a man who had told her all things she ever did, asking whether this was not the Christ. — Matthew Henry

30 COMMENTARY: They went out of the city and came to him, for those who would know Christ must meet him where he records his name. — Matthew Henry

31 COMMENTARY: Meanwhile his disciples urged him to eat, concerned for his bodily need after his journey. — Matthew Henry

32 COMMENTARY: He answered that he had meat to eat they knew not of, for doing God's will was his true refreshment. — Matthew Henry

33 COMMENTARY: The disciples wondered among themselves whether someone had brought him food, misunderstanding a spiritual saying in carnal terms. — Matthew Henry

34 COMMENTARY: Jesus explained that his meat was to do the will of him who sent him and to finish his work, with diligence and delight, as one who makes it his business. — Matthew Henry

35 COMMENTARY: He told them not to say there were yet four months to harvest, but to lift up their eyes, for the fields were already white, and gospel harvest-time is short and must be worked at once. — Matthew Henry

36 COMMENTARY: He who reaps receives wages and gathers fruit unto life eternal, so that sower and reaper rejoice together in one harvest. — Matthew Henry

37 COMMENTARY: Herein the saying is true, that one sows and another reaps, for the gospel harvest gathers the labour of many hands. — Matthew Henry

38 COMMENTARY: He sent them to reap where they had bestowed no labour, entering into the labours of prophets and of John before them. — Matthew Henry

39 COMMENTARY: Many Samaritans of that city believed on him because of the woman's testimony, for God sometimes uses very weak and unlikely instruments to begin a good work. — Matthew Henry

40 COMMENTARY: When the Samaritans came to him they besought him to stay, and he abode there two days, for those taught of God are truly desirous to learn more. — Matthew Henry

41 COMMENTARY: Many more believed because of his own word, their faith growing beyond second-hand report. — Matthew Henry

42 COMMENTARY: They told the woman they now believed not because of her saying alone, but because they had heard him themselves and knew this was indeed the Saviour of the world, not of the Jews only. — Matthew Henry

43 COMMENTARY: After two days he departed thence and went into Galilee, his work in Samaria done for that season. — Matthew Henry

44 COMMENTARY: For Jesus himself testified that a prophet has no honour in his own country, and he did not seek honour where familiarity bred contempt. — Matthew Henry

45 COMMENTARY: When he came into Galilee the Galileans received him, having seen all he did at Jerusalem at the feast, though their welcome still rested partly on wonders. — Matthew Henry

46 COMMENTARY: So Jesus came again into Cana, where he had made the water wine, and there a nobleman whose son was sick at Capernaum sought him. — Matthew Henry

47 COMMENTARY: When the nobleman heard Jesus had come out of Judea into Galilee, he went to him and besought him to come down and heal his son, who was at the point of death. — Matthew Henry

48 COMMENTARY: Jesus answered that except men see signs and wonders they will not believe, reproving a faith that demands wonders before it trusts. — Matthew Henry

49 COMMENTARY: The nobleman persisted, saying, Sir, come down ere my child die, and did not stop his request till he prevailed. — Matthew Henry

50 COMMENTARY: Jesus said, Go thy way, thy son liveth, and the man believed the word and went his way, showing the sincerity of his faith by obeying without seeing. — Matthew Henry

51 COMMENTARY: As he was going down his servants met him saying his son lived, for good news meets those who hope in God's word. — Matthew Henry

52 COMMENTARY: He asked the hour when the child began to amend, and they said the fever left him yesterday at the seventh hour. — Matthew Henry

53 COMMENTARY: The father knew it was the same hour Jesus had said, Thy son liveth, and he himself believed, and his whole house, for diligently comparing Christ's word with his works confirms faith. — Matthew Henry

54 COMMENTARY: This was the second miracle Jesus did when he came out of Judea into Galilee, a sign that the knowledge of Christ spreads through families and brings health and salvation. — Matthew Henry
