Study Notes — John

Chapter 8

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Jesus went unto the Mount of Olives, withdrawing at night after teaching in the temple. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: Early in the morning he came again into the temple, and all the people came to him, and he sat down and taught them. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: The scribes and Pharisees brought a woman taken in adultery and set her in the midst, using her shame as a trap. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: They said, Master, this woman was taken in adultery, in the very act, pressing the fact to force a verdict from him. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: They added that Moses in the law commanded such to be stoned, and asked what he said, hoping to set him against either Moses or Rome. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: They said this tempting him, that they might accuse him, but Jesus stooped down and wrote on the ground, as if he heard them not. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: When they continued asking, he lifted himself up and said, He that is without sin among you, let him first cast a stone at her, for those who judge others and do the same are self-condemned. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: Again he stooped down and wrote on the ground, leaving the sentence to work upon their consciences. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: Those who heard it, being convicted by their own conscience, went out one by one, beginning at the eldest, until Jesus was left alone with the woman. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: When Jesus had lifted himself up and saw none but the woman, he asked, Woman, where are thine accusers, and has no man condemned thee. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: She said, No man, Lord, and Jesus said, Neither do I condemn thee; go, and sin no more, for his great work was to bring sinners to repentance, not to destroy but to save, and forgiveness must prevail with us to leave sin. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: Then Jesus spoke again, saying, I am the light of the world; he who follows me shall not walk in darkness, but shall have the light of life, for one sun enlightens the whole world, and so does one Christ. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: The Pharisees said, Thou bearest record of thyself, and thy record is not true, objecting that self-witness proves nothing. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: Jesus answered that though he bore record of himself, his record was true, for he knew whence he came and whither he went, while they knew neither. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: You judge after the flesh, he said; I judge no man, for their judgment rested on outward appearance, not on truth. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: And yet if he judged, his judgment was true, for he was not alone, but he and the Father who sent him. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: It is written in their law that the testimony of two men is true, and Jesus met them on their own legal ground. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: He was one witness of himself, and the Father who sent him bore witness of him, so the law's requirement was met in a higher way. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: They asked, Where is thy Father, and Jesus answered that they knew neither him nor his Father, for if they had known him they would have known the Father also. — Matthew Henry

20 COMMENTARY: These words he spoke in the treasury as he taught in the temple, and no man laid hands on him, for his hour was not yet come, and enemies cannot hasten the time appointed by the Father. — Matthew Henry

21 COMMENTARY: Then Jesus said again, I go my way, and you shall seek me and shall die in your sins, for those who live and die in unbelief are forever undone. — Matthew Henry

22 COMMENTARY: The Jews asked, Will he kill himself, because he said, Whither I go, ye cannot come, misunderstanding a spiritual departure as self-destruction. — Matthew Henry

23 COMMENTARY: He said, You are from beneath, I am from above; you are of this world, I am not of this world, for his doctrine and kingdom would not suit their earthly taste. — Matthew Henry

24 COMMENTARY: Therefore he said they would die in their sins, for if they did not believe that he was he, they would die in their sins, and nothing but the doctrine of his grace can turn men from sin to God. — Matthew Henry

25 COMMENTARY: They asked, Who art thou, and Jesus answered, Even the same that I said unto you from the beginning, for he had declared himself plainly all along. — Matthew Henry

26 COMMENTARY: He had many things to say and judge of them, but he who sent him was true, and he spoke to the world what he had heard of the Father. — Matthew Henry

27 COMMENTARY: They understood not that he spoke to them of the Father, so blind were they to the very thing he was revealing. — Matthew Henry

28 COMMENTARY: Then Jesus said, When you have lifted up the Son of man, then you shall know that I am he, and that I do nothing of myself, but as the Father taught me, so I speak. — Matthew Henry

29 COMMENTARY: And he who sent him was with him, for the Father had not left him alone, because he always did those things that pleased him. — Matthew Henry

30 COMMENTARY: As he spoke these words, many believed on him, for such power attended his words that they were convinced and professed faith. — Matthew Henry

31 COMMENTARY: Then Jesus said to those Jews who believed on him, If you continue in my word, then you are my disciples indeed, for discipleship is shown by abiding, not by a moment's profession. — Matthew Henry

32 COMMENTARY: And you shall know the truth, and the truth shall make you free, for his teaching and Spirit show where hope and strength lie. — Matthew Henry

33 COMMENTARY: They answered, We are Abraham's seed and were never in bondage to any man, and how sayest thou, Ye shall be made free, feeling no grievance but bodily and worldly ones. — Matthew Henry

34 COMMENTARY: Jesus answered, Whoever commits sin is the servant of sin, for carnal hearts understand bondage to men but not bondage to sin. — Matthew Henry

35 COMMENTARY: And the servant abides not in the house forever, but the Son abides ever, so the slave's tenure is uncertain while the Son's is permanent. — Matthew Henry

36 COMMENTARY: If the Son therefore shall make you free, you shall be free indeed, for Christ has power to give real freedom, not the name of it. — Matthew Henry

37 COMMENTARY: I know you are Abraham's seed, he said, but you seek to kill me, because my word has no place in you, and where God's word has no place, no good is to be expected. — Matthew Henry

38 COMMENTARY: I speak what I have seen with my Father, he said, and you do what you have seen with your father, for each acts according to his true parentage. — Matthew Henry

39 COMMENTARY: They answered, Abraham is our father, and Jesus said, If you were Abraham's children, you would do the works of Abraham, for descent profits nothing without a contrary spirit's opposite. — Matthew Henry

40 COMMENTARY: But now you seek to kill me, a man who has told you the truth which he heard of God, and this Abraham did not do. — Matthew Henry

41 COMMENTARY: You do the deeds of your father, he said, and they answered, We are not born of fornication; we have one Father, even God, claiming God while rejecting his Son. — Matthew Henry

42 COMMENTARY: Jesus said, If God were your Father, you would love me, for I proceeded forth and came from God, neither came I of myself, but he sent me. — Matthew Henry

43 COMMENTARY: Why do you not understand my speech, he asked, even because you cannot hear my word, for their hearts were closed to it. — Matthew Henry

44 COMMENTARY: You are of your father the devil, he said, and the lusts of your father you will do, for the devil was a murderer from the beginning and abode not in the truth, being the author of all lies. — Matthew Henry

45 COMMENTARY: And because I tell you the truth, you believe me not, for truth itself was the offence. — Matthew Henry

46 COMMENTARY: Which of you convinces me of sin, he asked, and if I say the truth, why do you not believe me, for his enemies could find no fault in him but their own unbelief. — Matthew Henry

47 COMMENTARY: He who is of God hears God's words, he said; you therefore hear them not, because you are not of God. — Matthew Henry

48 COMMENTARY: Then the Jews answered, Say we not well that thou art a Samaritan, and hast a devil, reviling him when they could not answer him. — Matthew Henry

49 COMMENTARY: Jesus answered, I have not a devil, but I honour my Father, and you do dishonour me, disregarding men's applause as one dead to their praise. — Matthew Henry

50 COMMENTARY: And I seek not my own glory, he said; there is one who seeks and judges, for God will seek the honour of those who do not seek their own. — Matthew Henry

51 COMMENTARY: Verily, verily, I say unto you, if a man keep my saying, he shall never see death, for the believer's character is keeping Christ's sayings, and his privilege is never to see death forever. — Matthew Henry

52 COMMENTARY: Then the Jews said, Now we know thou hast a devil, for Abraham is dead, and the prophets, and yet thou sayest, If a man keep my saying, he shall never taste of death. — Matthew Henry

53 COMMENTARY: Art thou greater than our father Abraham, who is dead, they asked, and the prophets are dead; whom makest thou thyself. — Matthew Henry

54 COMMENTARY: Jesus answered, If I honour myself, my honour is nothing; it is my Father who honours me, of whom you say he is your God. — Matthew Henry

55 COMMENTARY: Yet you have not known him, he said, but I know him, and if I should say I know him not, I would be a liar like you, but I know him and keep his saying. — Matthew Henry

56 COMMENTARY: Your father Abraham rejoiced to see my day, he said, and he saw it and was glad, for Abraham discerned the dawn of the Sun of Righteousness and longed for its rising. — Matthew Henry

57 COMMENTARY: Then the Jews said, Thou art not yet fifty years old, and hast thou seen Abraham, measuring eternity by his age. — Matthew Henry

58 COMMENTARY: Jesus said, Verily, verily, before Abraham was, I AM, speaking Abraham a creature and himself the Creator, self-existent, the First and the Last, before all worlds. — Matthew Henry

59 COMMENTARY: Then they took up stones to cast at him, but Jesus hid himself and went out of the temple, going through the midst of them, for his hour was not yet come. — Matthew Henry
