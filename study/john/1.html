Study Notes — John

Chapter 1

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: The Son is called the Word because, as words reveal a mind, he reveals the Father's mind to the world, and his eternity proves he is God. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: The Word shared the Father's existence from the beginning, not as a creature made in time but as one coexistent with God. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: All things were made by him as Creator, not as a mere instrument, from the highest angel to the meanest creature. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: Life itself is in him, and that life is the light by which men live and understand. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: This true Light shines in a dark world, yet the darkness does not grasp or overcome it. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: John the Baptist was sent by God as a witness, not as the Light himself. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: His mission was to point men to the Light, so that through his testimony they might believe. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: He was not that Light himself, but was sent only to bear witness of that Light. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: Christ is the true Light, greater than any forerunner, who enlightens every person who comes into the world. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: He was in the world he had made, yet the world he made did not recognise its Maker. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: He came to his own people, yet his own did not receive him, because they would not part with their sins or have him reign over them. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: All who do receive him are given the right to become children of God, a standing received by faith rather than by birthright. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: This sonship comes not from blood, flesh, or human will, but from a new birth wrought by God. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: The Word became flesh and dwelt among us, so that his divine glory shone through the veil of his humanity, full of grace and truth. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: John testified that although Jesus came after him in public ministry, he ranked before him because he existed before him. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: From Christ's fullness believers receive grace upon grace, every spiritual blessing coming through him alone. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: The law was given through Moses, but grace and truth came through Jesus Christ, who supplies what the law cannot: pardon and strength. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: No one has seen God, but the only begotten Son, who is in the bosom of the Father, has declared him to us. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: When the Jews sent priests and Levites from Jerusalem to ask John who he was, his testimony began with a plain denial of being the Christ. — Matthew Henry

20 COMMENTARY: He confessed and denied not that he was not the Christ, refusing an honour that was not his. — Matthew Henry

21 COMMENTARY: Asked whether he was Elijah or that Prophet, he answered that he was neither in person, though he came in Elijah's spirit and power. — Matthew Henry

22 COMMENTARY: Pressed to give an answer to those who sent them, John was forced to state plainly who he was rather than leave them guessing. — Matthew Henry

23 COMMENTARY: He called himself only the voice crying in the wilderness, Make straight the way of the Lord, preparing the way rather than claiming the place of the Lord. — Matthew Henry

24 COMMENTARY: His questioners, being Pharisees, pressed him further, since they measured every teacher by party and office. — Matthew Henry

25 COMMENTARY: They asked why he baptised at all if he was neither Christ, Elijah, nor that Prophet, treating his baptism as an office he had no right to exercise. — Matthew Henry

26 COMMENTARY: John answered that he baptised with water only, while one stood among them whom they did not know, to whom even the meanest service would be too great an honour for him. — Matthew Henry

27 COMMENTARY: That one, coming after John, was preferred before him, and John counted himself unworthy to loose his shoe's latchet. — Matthew Henry

28 COMMENTARY: These things were done at Bethabara beyond Jordan, where John was baptising, a place remembered because testimony was publicly given there. — Matthew Henry

29 COMMENTARY: The next day John saw Jesus coming and proclaimed him the Lamb of God who takes away the sin of the world, the sacrifice pictured by the Passover lamb. — Matthew Henry

30 COMMENTARY: John explained that this was the one of whom he had said that a man coming after him was preferred before him, because he was before him. — Matthew Henry

31 COMMENTARY: John admitted he had not known Jesus by sight at first, but his baptism was appointed so that Christ might be revealed to Israel. — Matthew Henry

32 COMMENTARY: At Jesus' baptism John saw the Spirit descending like a dove and remaining on him, God's own witness to his Son. — Matthew Henry

33 COMMENTARY: The sign had been given to John beforehand: the one on whom the Spirit descends and remains is he who baptises with the Holy Ghost. — Matthew Henry

34 COMMENTARY: John therefore bore record that Jesus is the Son of God, the promised Messiah, and the end of all his testimony. — Matthew Henry

35 COMMENTARY: Again the next day John stood with two of his disciples, waiting for another opportunity to point men to Christ. — Matthew Henry

36 COMMENTARY: Looking upon Jesus as he walked, John repeated his witness, Behold the Lamb of God, taking every opportunity to lead people to Christ. — Matthew Henry

37 COMMENTARY: Hearing this, the two disciples followed Jesus, drawn by the argument that only he takes away sin. — Matthew Henry

38 COMMENTARY: Jesus turned and asked what they sought, a question every follower should ask of himself when he begins to follow Christ. — Matthew Henry

39 COMMENTARY: They answered by asking where he dwelt, and he invited them without delay to come and see, for now is the accepted time. — Matthew Henry

40 COMMENTARY: One of the two was Andrew, Simon Peter's brother, who had heard John speak and followed Jesus. — Matthew Henry

41 COMMENTARY: Andrew first found his own brother Simon and brought him to Jesus, as those who come to Christ labour for the spiritual welfare of their own kin. — Matthew Henry

42 COMMENTARY: He brought Simon to Jesus, who looked on him and gave him the name Cephas, a stone, marking the firm and constant character grace would make in him. — Matthew Henry

43 COMMENTARY: The next day Jesus would go forth into Galilee and found Philip, calling him to follow, showing that true Christianity is simply following Jesus. — Matthew Henry

44 COMMENTARY: Philip was of Bethsaida, the city of Andrew and Peter, and the call reached him among his own townsmen and kin. — Matthew Henry

45 COMMENTARY: Philip found Nathanael and told him of Jesus of Nazareth, the one of whom Moses and the prophets wrote. — Matthew Henry

46 COMMENTARY: Nathanael objected that no good could come out of Nazareth, a prejudice against a place that keeps many from the truth until they examine for themselves. — Matthew Henry

47 COMMENTARY: Jesus saw Nathanael coming and praised him as an Israelite indeed, in whom is no guile, a sincere and upright man rather than a dissembler. — Matthew Henry

48 COMMENTARY: Nathanael asked how Jesus knew him, and Jesus answered that he had seen him under the fig tree before Philip called him, knowing the secrets of his heart. — Matthew Henry

49 COMMENTARY: Convinced, Nathanael confessed Jesus as the Son of God and the King of Israel, moving from doubt to full confession. — Matthew Henry

50 COMMENTARY: Jesus told him that greater things than this private knowledge would follow, as faith that begins small is led on to more. — Matthew Henry

51 COMMENTARY: He promised that heaven would be opened and angels ascending and descending upon the Son of man, for through Christ heaven and earth are reconciled and united. — Matthew Henry
