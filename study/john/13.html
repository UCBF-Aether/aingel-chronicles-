Study Notes — John

Chapter 13

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Henry: his love for his own does not wear out at the hour of departure — the end of his earthly course is where that love is shown to hold, unseparated. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: And supper being ended, the devil having now put into the heart of Judas Iscariot, Simon's son, to betray him, a sin so exceedingly sinful, with so little temptation from the world and flesh, that it was plainly directly from Satan. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: Jesus, knowing the Father had given all things into his hands, and that he was come from God, and went to God, acted from the full knowledge of his dignity, not in spite of it. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: He rose from supper, laid aside his garments, took a towel, and girded himself, laying aside everything that would hinder the service he would do. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: After that he poured water into a basin, and began to wash the disciples' feet, and to wipe them with the towel wherewith he was girded, teaching us to think nothing below us wherein we may promote God's glory and our brethren's good. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: Then he came to Simon Peter, and Peter said, Lord, dost thou wash my feet, startled at the Master's lowliness. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: Jesus answered, What I do thou knowest not now, but thou shalt know hereafter, for our Lord does many things whose meaning his disciples do not for the present know, but shall know afterward. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: Peter said, Thou shalt never wash my feet, and Jesus answered, If I wash thee not, thou hast no part with me, for it is not humility but unbelief to put away the offers of the gospel as too good to be true, and only those spiritually washed by Christ have a part in him. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: Simon Peter said, Lord, not my feet only, but also my hands and my head, for he who truly desires sanctification desires it throughout, the whole man made pure. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: Jesus said, He who is washed needs not save to wash his feet, but is clean every whit, and you are clean, but not all, for the justified believer's daily care is to wash his feet, cleansing daily guilt and watching against defilement. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: For he knew who should betray him, therefore he said, You are not all clean, and when hypocrites are discovered, it should be no surprise or stumbling to us. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: Henry: the question opens the lesson — he asks what he has done so they will observe, not merely receive, the example he has set. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: Henry: they call him Master and Lord, and say well — the titles are owned, not flattered, and his example as Master and Lord is what authorises the command that follows. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: If I then, your Lord and Master, have washed your feet, you also ought to wash one another's feet, for duties are mutual, and we must both accept help from brethren and afford it. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: For I have given you an example, that you should do as I have done to you, and when we see our Master serving, we see how ill it becomes us to domineer. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: Verily, verily, the servant is not greater than his lord, neither he who is sent greater than he who sent him, so no disciple may refuse lowly service. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: If you know these things, happy are you if you do them, for knowledge without practice brings no blessedness. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: I speak not of you all, he said; I know whom I have chosen, but that the Scripture may be fulfilled, He that eats bread with me has lifted up his heel against me, for the prophecy of treachery may apply to all who meet God's mercies with ingratitude. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: Now I tell you before it comes, that when it comes to pass, you may believe that I am he, for foreknowledge confirms faith. — Matthew Henry

20 COMMENTARY: Verily, verily, he who receives whomsoever I send receives me, and he who receives me receives him who sent me, so the apostles' mission carries Christ's own authority. — Matthew Henry

21 COMMENTARY: When Jesus had thus said, he was troubled in spirit, and testified, and said, Verily, verily, one of you shall betray me, for our Lord had often spoken of his sufferings without such trouble as he now showed at Judas's treachery, and the sins of Christians are the grief of Christ. — Matthew Henry

22 COMMENTARY: Then the disciples looked one on another, doubting of whom he spoke, each fearing himself rather than accusing another. — Matthew Henry

23 COMMENTARY: Henry: one disciple leans on Jesus' bosom — the place of nearness given to the disciple Jesus loved, the same nearness Peter will use to ask his question. — Matthew Henry

24 COMMENTARY: Simon Peter therefore beckoned to him, that he should ask who it should be of whom he spoke, seeking the answer through the beloved disciple. — Matthew Henry

25 COMMENTARY: He then, lying on Jesus' breast, said, Lord, who is it, asking in the confidence of nearness. — Matthew Henry

26 COMMENTARY: The dipped sop identifies the traitor among those who shared his bread; Henry warns that the prophecy reaches beyond Judas to all who partake of God's mercies and meet them with ingratitude, lifting up the heel against him. — Matthew Henry

27 COMMENTARY: And after the sop Satan entered into him, and then Jesus said to him, That thou doest, do quickly, for those whose deeds are evil love darkness rather than light, and Judas went out as one weary of Jesus and his apostles. — Matthew Henry

28 COMMENTARY: Now no man at the table knew for what intent he spoke this unto him, for the sign was understood by none but John. — Matthew Henry

29 COMMENTARY: For some thought, because Judas had the bag, that Jesus had said to him, Buy those things we have need of against the feast, or that he should give something to the poor, a natural misreading of an ordinary errand. — Matthew Henry

30 COMMENTARY: Henry: Judas goes out weary of Jesus and his company, into the night — evil deeds choose darkness over light, and the hour fits the deed. — Matthew Henry

31 COMMENTARY: Therefore, when he was gone out, Jesus said, Now is the Son of man glorified, and God is glorified in him, for Christ had been glorified in many miracles, yet he spoke of his sufferings as a greater glory, since satisfaction was thereby made for the wrong done to God by man's sin. — Matthew Henry

32 COMMENTARY: If God be glorified in him, God shall also glorify him in himself, and shall straightway glorify him, for the cross and the crown belong together. — Matthew Henry

33 COMMENTARY: Little children, yet a little while I am with you, he said; you shall seek me, and as I said to the Jews, Whither I go, ye cannot come, so now I say to you, for we cannot yet follow him to heavenly happiness, but if we believe, we shall follow hereafter. — Matthew Henry

34 COMMENTARY: A new commandment I give unto you, that you love one another, as I have loved you, that you also love one another, for they were to love for Christ's sake and according to his example, seeking others' benefit as one body animated by one soul. — Matthew Henry

35 COMMENTARY: By this shall all men know you are my disciples, if you have love one to another, for if followers of Christ do not show love, they give cause to suspect their sincerity. — Matthew Henry

36 COMMENTARY: Simon Peter said, Lord, whither goest thou, and Jesus answered, Whither I go, thou canst not follow me now, but thou shalt follow me afterwards, for Peter overlooked brotherly love to ask about secret things that belong to God only. — Matthew Henry

37 COMMENTARY: Peter said, Lord, why cannot I follow thee now, I will lay down my life for thy sake, eager to know and to promise more than he could perform. — Matthew Henry

38 COMMENTARY: Jesus answered, Wilt thou lay down thy life for my sake, verily, verily, I say unto thee, The cock shall not crow, till thou hast denied me thrice, for Christ knows us better than we know ourselves, and will hide pride from those he loves by discovering them to themselves. — Matthew Henry
