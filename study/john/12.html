Study Notes — John

Chapter 12

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Then Jesus, six days before the Passover, came to Bethany, where Lazarus was, whom he had raised from the dead. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: There they made him a supper, and Martha served, but Lazarus was one of those who sat at table with him, for Martha still served, but now within hearing of Christ's gracious words. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: Then Mary took a pound of ointment of spikenard, very costly, and anointed the feet of Jesus, and wiped his feet with her hair, and the house was filled with the odour of the ointment, giving a token of love to him who had given real tokens of love to her family. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: Then one of his disciples, Judas Iscariot, Simon's son, who would betray him, objected to the gift. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: He asked why this ointment was not sold for three hundred pence and given to the poor, gilding a foul sin with a plausible pretence. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: This he said, not because he cared for the poor, but because he was a thief, and had the bag, and bore what was put therein, for the reigning love of money is heart-theft. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: Then Jesus said, Let her alone; against the day of my burying has she kept this, for opportunities are to be improved, and those first and most vigorously which are likely to be shortest. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: For the poor always you have with you, he said, but me you have not always, so the season for this service would soon pass. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: Much people of the Jews therefore knew he was there, and they came not for Jesus' sake only, but that they might see Lazarus also, whom he had raised from the dead. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: But the chief priests consulted that they might put Lazarus also to death, a wickedness, malice, and folly explicable only by desperate enmity against God. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: Because by reason of him many Jews went away and believed on Jesus, for the success of the gospel often angers wicked men as if they hoped for victory over the Almighty himself. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: On the next day much people who had come to the feast, when they heard Jesus was coming to Jerusalem, went forth to meet him. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: They took branches of palm trees and cried, Hosanna, Blessed is the King of Israel that comes in the name of the Lord, hailing him as King. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: And Jesus, when he had found a young ass, sat thereon, as it is written, fulfilling the prophecy of a humble King. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: Fear not, daughter of Sion, the prophecy said; behold, thy King comes, sitting on an ass's colt. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: These things his disciples understood not at first, but when Jesus was glorified, then they remembered these things were written of him and that they had done these things unto him, for many excellent things are not understood at first acquaintance with God. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: The people therefore who were with him when he called Lazarus out of his grave and raised him from the dead, bore record of the miracle. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: For this cause the people also met him, because they heard he had done this miracle, and the raising of Lazarus drew the crowds. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: The Pharisees therefore said among themselves, Perceive ye how ye prevail nothing; behold, the world is gone after him, confessing their failure in their own despite. — Matthew Henry

20 COMMENTARY: And there were certain Greeks among those who came up to worship at the feast, Gentiles seeking God at the Passover. — Matthew Henry

21 COMMENTARY: The same came therefore to Philip, who was of Bethsaida of Galilee, and desired him, saying, Sir, we would see Jesus, for in attendance on holy ordinances the great desire of the soul should be to see Jesus. — Matthew Henry

22 COMMENTARY: Philip came and told Andrew, and again Andrew and Philip told Jesus, bringing the Gentile request to him together. — Matthew Henry

23 COMMENTARY: And Jesus answered them, saying, The hour is come, that the Son of man should be glorified, for the calling of the Gentiles magnified the Redeemer. — Matthew Henry

24 COMMENTARY: Verily, verily, except a corn of wheat fall into the ground and die, it abides alone, but if it dies, it brings forth much fruit, for Christ might have kept his heavenly glory alone, but then no sinner could have been saved. — Matthew Henry

25 COMMENTARY: He who loves his life shall lose it, and he who hates his life in this world shall keep it unto life eternal, for we must be indifferent to trifling concerns to serve Christ willingly. — Matthew Henry

26 COMMENTARY: If any man serve me, let him follow me, he said, and where I am, there shall also my servant be, and if any man serve me, him will my Father honour. — Matthew Henry

27 COMMENTARY: Now is my soul troubled, he said, and what shall I say, Father, save me from this hour, for the sin of our souls troubled his soul when he undertook to make his soul an offering for sin, yet prayer against trouble may agree with patience under it. — Matthew Henry

28 COMMENTARY: Father, glorify thy name, he prayed, then came a voice from heaven, saying, I have both glorified it, and will glorify it again, as at his baptism and transfiguration. — Matthew Henry

29 COMMENTARY: The people therefore who stood by and heard it said it thundered, and others said, An angel spoke to him, hearing the sound without understanding the words. — Matthew Henry

30 COMMENTARY: Jesus answered, This voice came not because of me, but for your sakes, to confirm their faith. — Matthew Henry

31 COMMENTARY: Now is the judgment of this world, he said; now shall the prince of this world be cast out, for by his death he broke the power of death and cast out Satan as destroyer and deceiver. — Matthew Henry

32 COMMENTARY: And I, if I be lifted up from the earth, will draw all men unto me, for there is power in the death of Christ to draw souls to him, and he would draw men's hearts to heaven. — Matthew Henry

33 COMMENTARY: This he said, signifying what death he should die, namely, by lifting up on the cross. — Matthew Henry

34 COMMENTARY: The people answered, We have heard out of the law that Christ abides forever, and how sayest thou, The Son of man must be lifted up, and who is this Son of man, for they overlooked the prophecies of his sufferings. — Matthew Henry

35 COMMENTARY: Then Jesus said, Yet a little while is the light with you; walk while you have the light, lest darkness come upon you, for he who walks in darkness knows not whither he goes. — Matthew Henry

36 COMMENTARY: While you have light, believe in the light, that you may be the children of light, he said, and these things he spoke, and departed, and hid himself from them, for the light would not long continue with them. — Matthew Henry

37 COMMENTARY: But though he had done so many miracles before them, yet they believed not on him, finding a thousand objections to excuse unbelief. — Matthew Henry

38 COMMENTARY: That the saying of Isaiah the prophet might be fulfilled, which he spoke, Lord, who has believed our report, and to whom has the arm of the Lord been revealed. — Matthew Henry

39 COMMENTARY: Therefore they could not believe, because Isaiah said again, in the prophecy of judicial hardening. — Matthew Henry

40 COMMENTARY: He has blinded their eyes and hardened their heart, that they should not see with their eyes, nor understand with their heart, and be converted, and I should heal them, for conversion requires seeing Divine things truly, and God heals those who turn. — Matthew Henry

41 COMMENTARY: These things said Isaiah, when he saw his glory, and spoke of him, for Isaiah's vision was of Christ's glory. — Matthew Henry

42 COMMENTARY: Nevertheless among the chief rulers also many believed on him, but because of the Pharisees they did not confess him, lest they should be put out of the synagogue, for the power of the world smothers convictions from regard to men's applause. — Matthew Henry

43 COMMENTARY: For they loved the praise of men more than the praise of God, and love of praise makes a hypocrite when religion is in fashion and an apostate when it is in disgrace. — Matthew Henry

44 COMMENTARY: Jesus cried and said, He who believes on me, believes not on me, but on him who sent me, for faith in Christ is faith in the Father. — Matthew Henry

45 COMMENTARY: And he who sees me sees him who sent me, for beholding in Jesus the glory of the Father, we learn to obey, love, and trust him. — Matthew Henry

46 COMMENTARY: I am come a light into the world, he said, that whoever believes on me should not abide in darkness, and by daily looking to him we are freed from ignorance, error, sin, and misery. — Matthew Henry

47 COMMENTARY: And if any man hears my words and believes not, I judge him not, for I came not to judge the world, but to save the world, in his first coming. — Matthew Henry

48 COMMENTARY: He who rejects me and receives not my words has one who judges him, for the word I have spoken, the same shall judge him in the last day, and the same word seals the condemnation of all who despise it. — Matthew Henry

49 COMMENTARY: For I have not spoken of myself, he said, but the Father who sent me gave me a commandment, what I should say and what I should speak. — Matthew Henry

50 COMMENTARY: And I know his commandment is life everlasting, he said; whatsoever I speak therefore, even as the Father said unto me, so I speak. — Matthew Henry
