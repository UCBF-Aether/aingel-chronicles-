Study Notes — John

Chapter 18

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: When Jesus had spoken these words, he went forth with his disciples over the brook Cedron, where was a garden, into which he entered, and his disciples, for sin began in a garden, and in a garden the promised Seed entered into conflict with the old serpent. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: And Judas also, who betrayed him, knew the place, for Jesus often resorted thither with his disciples, and his familiar place became the place of betrayal. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: Judas then, having received a band of men and officers from the chief priests and Pharisees, came thither with lanterns and torches and weapons. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: Jesus therefore, knowing all things that should come upon him, went forth and said unto them, Whom seek ye, for when men would force him to a crown he withdrew, but when they came to force him to a cross he offered himself. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: They answered him, Jesus of Nazareth, and Jesus said unto them, I am he, and Judas also, who betrayed him, stood with them. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: As soon as he had said unto them, I am he, they went backward and fell to the ground, for he showed plainly what he could have done, and when he struck them down he could have struck them dead, but would not. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: Then he asked them again, Whom seek ye, and they said, Jesus of Nazareth, repeating their errand after being overthrown by his word. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: Jesus answered, I have told you that I am he; if therefore you seek me, let these go their way, for it must have been divine power that the officers let the disciples go quietly after resistance had been offered. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: That the saying might be fulfilled, which he spoke, Of them which thou gavest me have I lost none, for his care kept his own even in his arrest. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: Then Simon Peter, having a sword, drew it, and smote the high priest's servant, and cut off his right ear, and the servant's name was Malchus, for Peter acted by his own zeal rather than his Master's command. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: Then Jesus said to Peter, Put up thy sword into the sheath; the cup which my Father has given me, shall I not drink it, for it is but a cup, a small matter, given by a Father who has a father's authority and affection, and sufferings are gifts, not wrongs. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: Then the band and the captain and officers of the Jews took Jesus, and bound him, for we were bound with the cords of our iniquities, and Christ, made a sin-offering to free us from those bonds, himself submitted to be bound for us, and to his bonds we owe our liberty. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: And they led him away to Annas first, for he was father-in-law to Caiaphas, who was the high priest that same year. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: Now Caiaphas was he who gave counsel to the Jews, that it was expedient that one man should die for the people, the same worldly counsel now carried into action. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: And Simon Peter followed Jesus, and so did another disciple, and that disciple was known unto the high priest, and went in with Jesus into the palace of the high priest. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: But Peter stood at the door without, and then the other disciple, who was known unto the high priest, went out and spoke unto her who kept the door, and brought in Peter. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: Then the damsel who kept the door said unto Peter, Art not thou also one of this man's disciples, and he said, I am not, for the beginning of sin is as the letting forth of water, and one lie needs another to support it. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: And the servants and officers stood there, who had made a fire of coals, for it was cold, and they warmed themselves, and Peter stood with them, and warmed himself, lingering in the enemies' company. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: The high priest then asked Jesus of his disciples, and of his doctrine, seeking a charge from his teaching. — Matthew Henry

20 COMMENTARY: Jesus answered him, I spoke openly to the world; I ever taught in the synagogue, and in the temple, whither the Jews always resort, and in secret have I said nothing, for his doctrine may safely appeal to all who know it. — Matthew Henry

21 COMMENTARY: Why askest thou me, he said; ask them which heard me, what I have said unto them; behold, they know what I said, for those who judge in truth bear witness to his doctrine. — Matthew Henry

22 COMMENTARY: And when he had thus spoken, one of the officers who stood by struck Jesus with the palm of his hand, saying, Answerest thou the high priest so, for his enemies, while quarrelling with his truth, wilfully shut their eyes against it. — Matthew Henry

23 COMMENTARY: Jesus answered him, If I have spoken evil, bear witness of the evil, but if well, why smitest thou me, for our resentment of injuries must never be passionate, but reasoned, as he reasoned with the man who injured him. — Matthew Henry

24 COMMENTARY: Now Annas had sent him bound unto Caiaphas the high priest, passing him from one tribunal to another. — Matthew Henry

25 COMMENTARY: And Simon Peter stood and warmed himself, and they said therefore unto him, Art not thou also one of his disciples, and he denied it, and said, I am not, a second denial following the first. — Matthew Henry

26 COMMENTARY: One of the servants of the high priest, being his kinsman whose ear Peter cut off, said, Did not I see thee in the garden with him, pressing the fact Peter could not deny. — Matthew Henry

27 COMMENTARY: Peter then denied again, and immediately the cock crew, fulfilling Christ's warning and marking the depth of his fall. — Matthew Henry

28 COMMENTARY: Then they led Jesus from Caiaphas unto the hall of judgment, and it was early, and they themselves went not into the judgment hall, lest they should be defiled, but that they might eat the Passover, fearing the scandal of defilement more than the sin of murder. — Matthew Henry

29 COMMENTARY: Pilate then went out unto them, and said, What accusation bring ye against this man, for it was unjust to put to death one who had done so much good. — Matthew Henry

30 COMMENTARY: They answered and said unto him, If he were not a malefactor, we would not have delivered him up unto thee, offering no charge but their own verdict. — Matthew Henry

31 COMMENTARY: Then Pilate said unto them, Take ye him, and judge him according to your law, and the Jews said, It is not lawful for us to put any man to death, unwilling to bear the reproach of the sentence themselves. — Matthew Henry

32 COMMENTARY: That the saying of Jesus might be fulfilled, which he spoke, signifying what death he should die, for he had said he should be delivered to the Gentiles and put to death by them, and crucified, whereas the Jews would have stoned him, since crucifying was never used among the Jews. — Matthew Henry

33 COMMENTARY: Then Pilate entered into the judgment hall again, and called Jesus, and said unto him, Art thou the King of the Jews, asking whether he was the long-expected Messiah the Prince. — Matthew Henry

34 COMMENTARY: Jesus answered him, Sayest thou this thing of thyself, or did others tell it thee of me, answering with a question not for evasion, but that Pilate might consider what he did. — Matthew Henry

35 COMMENTARY: Pilate answered, Am I a Jew, thine own nation and the chief priests have delivered thee unto me; what hast thou done, for he never took upon himself any earthly power, nor were traitorous principles laid to him. — Matthew Henry

36 COMMENTARY: Jesus answered, My kingdom is not of this world; if my kingdom were of this world, then would my servants fight, that I should not be delivered to the Jews, but now is my kingdom not from hence, for its nature is not worldly, it is a kingdom within men, its riches and power spiritual, needing no force to maintain it, and opposing no kingdom but that of sin and Satan. — Matthew Henry

37 COMMENTARY: Pilate therefore said unto him, Art thou a king then, and Jesus answered, Thou sayest that I am a king; to this end was I born, and for this cause came I into the world, that I should bear witness unto the truth, and every one that is of the truth hears my voice, for when Christ said, I am the Truth, he said in effect, I am a King, conquering by the evidence of truth and ruling by its commanding power. — Matthew Henry

38 COMMENTARY: Pilate said unto him, What is truth, and when he had said this, he went out again unto the Jews, and said unto them, I find in him no fault at all, a good question put by one who had not patience to persevere in the search after truth, nor humility to receive it, yet a solemn declaration of Christ's innocence. — Matthew Henry

39 COMMENTARY: But you have a custom, Pilate said, that I should release unto you one at the Passover; will you therefore that I release unto you the King of the Jews, offering a compromise governed more by worldly wisdom than by justice. — Matthew Henry

40 COMMENTARY: Then they cried all again, saying, Not this man, but Barabbas, and now Barabbas was a robber, for sin is a robber, yet foolishly chosen by many rather than Christ, who would truly enrich us. — Matthew Henry
