Study Notes — John

Chapter 7

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Jesus walked in Galilee rather than Judea while his enemies there sought his life; Henry notes that even his favourers dared not openly avow him, while the counsel of his kinsmen aimed only at worldly advantage. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: Henry heads the chapter with the feast journey: the tabernacles feast is the public stage on which the brethren's counsel and the people's dispute now play out. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: Henry: the brethren, disgusted at no prospect of worldly advantage from him, counsel a move to Judea — advice that promotes present advantage, not God's work. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: Henry: the brethren's counsel is worldly through and through — ungodly advisers urging God's worker toward present advantage and public display, not toward the Father's hour. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: For neither did his brethren believe in him, and their counsel aimed only at worldly advantage, as ungodly men often counsel God's workers. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: Jesus said, My time is not yet come, but your time is always ready, for he acted by the Father's hour, not by human urging. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: Henry: the world cannot hate men who leave it alone, but it hates the witness who testifies that its works are evil — hatred follows testimony, and proves it. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: Henry: he will not go up at their bidding — his time is not yet full come, and no brother's timetable sets the hour of his going. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: When he had said these words, he abode still in Galilee, waiting his hour rather than following their timetable. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: Henry: he goes to the feast, but as it were in secret — no procession, no display, the opposite of the public showing his brethren pressed for. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: Henry: the people differ over him — some call him a good man, others a deceiver, and those who favour him dare not avow it openly while his accusers speak out. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: However, no man spoke openly of him for fear of the Jews, for those who favoured him dared not avow it, while his enemies spoke out. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: Henry: his discourse at the feast begins in the temple, mid-feast — teaching, not display, is the work he comes to do. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: The Jews marvelled, asking how this man knew letters, having never learned, for his doctrine carried an authority no school had given. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: Jesus answered, My doctrine is not mine, but his that sent me, for every faithful minister's doctrine comes from God's word through his Spirit. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: If any man will do God's will, he shall know of the doctrine, whether it is of God or men speak of themselves, for obedience opens the mind to truth. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: Henry: the mark of a true teacher is whose glory he seeks — his own, or his sender's; doctrine from God, not his own finding, is the test. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: Henry: the dispute turns on the law itself — men who boast Moses' law yet keep none of it, and who answer a healer with a plot to kill. — Matthew Henry

20 COMMENTARY: The people answered, Thou hast a devil; who goes about to kill thee, denying the very plot that was already forming. — Matthew Henry

21 COMMENTARY: Jesus answered, I have done one work, and you all marvel, referring to the healing at Bethesda that had provoked them. — Matthew Henry

22 COMMENTARY: Henry: their own rite answers them — circumcision given on the Sabbath shows the day's design embraces mercy-work, not mere outward rite. — Matthew Henry

23 COMMENTARY: If a man receives circumcision on the Sabbath so that the law of Moses is not broken, are you angry because he made a man altogether whole on the Sabbath, for restoring health agrees with the Sabbath's design. — Matthew Henry

24 COMMENTARY: Judge not according to appearance, but judge righteous judgment, deciding by worth and by the Spirit's gifts rather than by outward show. — Matthew Henry

25 COMMENTARY: Henry: the question comes from Jerusalem itself, the city where his life is sought — his presence there, sought and wondered at, is itself the dispute. — Matthew Henry

26 COMMENTARY: Henry: he speaks boldly while the rulers say nothing, and the silence itself raises the question — do they know this is the Christ? — Matthew Henry

27 COMMENTARY: However, they said, we know this man whence he is, but when Christ comes no man knows whence he is, resting their objection on his known origin. — Matthew Henry

28 COMMENTARY: Henry: they are in error about his origin — they know whence he is after the flesh, yet they know not the God who sent him, true to his promises. — Matthew Henry

29 COMMENTARY: But I know him, he said, for I am from him, and he has sent me, claiming a peculiar knowledge of God that provoked his hearers. — Matthew Henry

30 COMMENTARY: Then they sought to take him, but no man laid hands on him, because his hour was not yet come, for God can tie men's hands though he does not turn their hearts. — Matthew Henry

31 COMMENTARY: Henry: his discourses convince many he is the Messiah — yet conviction without courage to own it is the chapter's recurring weakness. — Matthew Henry

32 COMMENTARY: Henry: murmuring hardens into a mission — Pharisees and chief priests send officers to take him, turning talk into arrest. — Matthew Henry

33 COMMENTARY: Then Jesus said to them, Yet a little while am I with you, and then I go to him who sent me, a comfort to those weary of a hostile world, for they shall not be in it long. — Matthew Henry

34 COMMENTARY: You shall seek me and shall not find me, he said, and where I am, thither you cannot come, for the days of grace do not last forever. — Matthew Henry

35 COMMENTARY: Henry: their question turns scornful — whither will he go, to the dispersed, to teach Gentiles? — and the scorn itself shows how far their thoughts are from his. — Matthew Henry

36 COMMENTARY: Henry: they dispute a saying they cannot yet parse — such sayings are explained by the event, not by argument on the spot. — Matthew Henry

37 COMMENTARY: In the last day, that great day of the feast, Jesus stood and cried, saying, If any man thirst, let him come unto me and drink, alluding to the water poured out before the Lord. — Matthew Henry

38 COMMENTARY: He who believes on me, as the Scripture has said, out of his belly shall flow rivers of living water, for the Spirit in believers is a fountain of living, running water. — Matthew Henry

39 COMMENTARY: Henry: the Spirit is spoken of as not yet given, because Jesus is not yet glorified — the gift waits on the glorified Redeemer, from whom the streams will flow. — Matthew Henry

40 COMMENTARY: Many of the people, hearing this saying, said, Of a truth this is the Prophet, recognising the promised Prophet in his words. — Matthew Henry

41 COMMENTARY: Others said, This is the Christ, while some asked, Shall Christ come out of Galilee, letting a supposed origin blind them to the evidence. — Matthew Henry

42 COMMENTARY: Has not the Scripture said, they asked, that Christ comes of the seed of David and out of Bethlehem, the town where David was, arguing from prophecy without checking the fact. — Matthew Henry

43 COMMENTARY: So there was a division among the people because of him, for the same words that convinced some hardened others. — Matthew Henry

44 COMMENTARY: Henry: some would seize him, but no hand is laid on him — the staying of malice, sometimes unaccountable, is God's restraint until his hour. — Matthew Henry

45 COMMENTARY: Henry: the arrest party comes back without him, and their masters' question — why have ye not brought him? — opens the door to the officers' confession that follows. — Matthew Henry

46 COMMENTARY: The officers answered, Never man spoke like this man, confessing the wisdom, power, and grace with which Christ spoke. — Matthew Henry

47 COMMENTARY: Then the Pharisees answered, Are you also deceived, seeking to shame the officers back into obedience. — Matthew Henry

48 COMMENTARY: Have any of the rulers or of the Pharisees believed on him, they asked, making fashion and authority the test of truth. — Matthew Henry

49 COMMENTARY: But this people who do not know the law are cursed, they said, despising the common people whom God had chosen. — Matthew Henry

50 COMMENTARY: Henry: Nicodemus, once a timid night visitor, is brought forward to defeat his enemies' design — he pleads the law's own rule, that no man is judged before he is heard. — Matthew Henry

51 COMMENTARY: He asked, Does our law judge any man before it hears him and knows what he does, for even their own law required a hearing. — Matthew Henry

52 COMMENTARY: They answered, Art thou also of Galilee, search and look, for out of Galilee arises no prophet, answering law with contempt. — Matthew Henry

53 COMMENTARY: And every man went unto his own house, the council breaking up without laying hands on Jesus. — Matthew Henry

No note for verse(s) 11 — the public-domain sources consulted give no distinct explanation for these verses beyond the list itself, so nothing was invented for them.
