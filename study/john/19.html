Study Notes — John

Chapter 19

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Then Pilate therefore took Jesus, and scourged him, little thinking with what holy regard these sufferings would in after ages be thought upon by the best and greatest of men. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: Henry: the thorns and purple are scorn made visible — he comes forth willing to be exposed to it, and faith is bidden to behold him in it and love him. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: And they said, Hail, King of the Jews, and they smote him with their hands, for our Lord came forth willing to be exposed to their scorn, and it is good for everyone with faith to behold Christ in his sufferings. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: Pilate therefore went forth again, and said unto them, Behold, I bring him forth to you, that you may know that I find no fault in him, repeating his declaration of innocence. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: Henry: Pilate brings him forth crowned and robed and bids them behold the man — scorn on display, yet the sight faith is told to keep looking upon. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: When the chief priests therefore and officers saw him, they cried out, saying, Crucify him, crucify him, and Pilate said, Take ye him, and crucify him, for I find no fault in him, for their hatred sharpened their endeavours, and shall not our love quicken ours for him and his kingdom. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: The Jews answered him, We have a law, and by our law he ought to die, because he made himself the Son of God, at last stating the charge plainly. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: When Pilate therefore heard that saying, he was the more afraid, for even natural conscience makes men afraid of being found fighting against God, and Pilate thought Jesus might be some person above the common order. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: And he went again into the judgment hall, and said unto Jesus, Whence art thou, seeking the origin of one he feared to condemn. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: Henry: Pilate presses his power to crucify or release, yet already fears he may be fighting against God — the boast and the fear sit in the same man. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: Jesus answered, Thou couldest have no power at all against me, except it were given thee from above, therefore he that delivered me unto thee has the greater sin, for as our Lord suffered for the sins of both Jews and Gentiles, it was the counsel of Divine Wisdom that the Jews should first purpose his death and the Gentiles carry it into effect. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: And from thenceforth Pilate sought to release him, but the Jews cried out, saying, If thou let this man go, thou art not Caesar's friend; whosoever makes himself a king speaks against Caesar, for Pilate was willing to please all sides and governed more by worldly wisdom than by justice. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: And it was the preparation of the Passover, and about the sixth hour, and he said unto the Jews, Behold your King, presenting their King to a people who would reject him. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: But they cried out, Away with him, away with him, crucify him, and Pilate said, Shall I crucify your King, and the chief priests answered, We have no king but Caesar, for had not Christ been thus rejected of men, we had been forever rejected of God. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: Henry: the sentence is carried out at once — yet the leading away is for us, that we might escape, the sacrifice bound to the altar in our place. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: Bearing his cross to Golgotha, Christ is led forth for us that we might escape; Henry sees him nailed to the cross as a sacrifice bound to the altar, dying among criminals sacrificed to public justice rather than at the altar among sacrifices. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: Where they crucified him, and two other with him, on either side one, and Jesus in the midst, nailed to the cross as a Sacrifice bound to the altar, fulfilling Scripture, for he did not die among sacrifices at the altar, but among criminals sacrificed to public justice. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: Pilate would not let the chief priests alter the writing, which Henry ascribes to a secret power of God upon his heart, so that this statement of the Lord's character and authority might continue over the cross. — Matthew Henry

20 COMMENTARY: Henry: the title is set where many will read it, near the city, in Hebrew, Greek, and Latin — the statement of his character and authority is published, not hidden. — Matthew Henry

21 COMMENTARY: Henry: the priests press Pilate to alter the writing, but he will not gratify them — a secret power on his heart keeps the statement of Christ's character and authority standing. — Matthew Henry

22 COMMENTARY: Pilate answered, What I have written I have written, for a secret power of God upon his heart kept this statement of our Lord's character and authority from being altered. — Matthew Henry

23 COMMENTARY: Henry: the parting of his clothes and the lottery over the seamless coat are prophecy fulfilled by men acting for gain, not for Scripture. — Matthew Henry

24 COMMENTARY: Henry: the casting of lots over the coat is prophecy fulfilled by unwitting hands — all that was written shall be fulfilled, down to the last garment. — Matthew Henry

25 COMMENTARY: Now there stood by the cross of Jesus his mother, and his mother's sister, Mary the wife of Cleophas, and Mary Magdalene, faithful women at the cross when most had fled. — Matthew Henry

26 COMMENTARY: When Jesus therefore saw his mother, and the disciple standing by, whom he loved, he said unto his mother, Woman, behold thy son, tenderly providing for his mother at his death, for sometimes when God removes one comfort, he raises up another where we looked not for it. — Matthew Henry

27 COMMENTARY: Then he said to the disciple, Behold thy mother, and from that hour that disciple took her unto his own home, for Christ's example teaches all men to honour their parents in life and death and to provide for their wants. — Matthew Henry

28 COMMENTARY: Henry: knowing all is now accomplished, he speaks the thirst that fulfils Scripture — even the last bodily want is taken up into the finished work. — Matthew Henry

30 COMMENTARY: When Jesus therefore had received the vinegar, he said, It is finished, and he bowed his head, and gave up the ghost, for the counsels of the Father concerning his sufferings were now fulfilled, all types and prophecies accomplished, the ceremonial law abolished, an end made of transgression by everlasting righteousness, and the work of man's redemption completed, and his life was not taken by force, but freely given up. — Matthew Henry

31 COMMENTARY: Henry: the high day sets the clock — before the Sabbath, legs are to be broken and bodies removed, and that haste will itself prove, at the cross, that he is already dead. — Matthew Henry

32 COMMENTARY: Henry: the soldiers break the legs of the two crucified beside him — the grim work done on the pair, which makes the exception for Jesus, found already dead, the point of the passage. — Matthew Henry

33 COMMENTARY: But when they came to Jesus, and saw that he was dead already, they brake not his legs, for he died in less time than persons crucified commonly did, showing he had laid down his life of himself. — Matthew Henry

34 COMMENTARY: But one of the soldiers with a spear pierced his side, and forthwith came there out blood and water, signifying justification and sanctification, blood for atonement and water for purification, both flowing from the pierced side of our Redeemer. — Matthew Henry

35 COMMENTARY: And he that saw it bare record, and his record is true, and he knows that he says true, that you might believe, for the attestation was solemn, because there was something peculiar in it. — Matthew Henry

36 COMMENTARY: For these things were done, that the Scripture should be fulfilled, A bone of him shall not be broken, in Pilate's not allowing his legs to be broken, and there was a type of this in the paschal lamb. — Matthew Henry

37 COMMENTARY: And again another Scripture says, They shall look on him whom they pierced, for we should ever look to him whom by our sins we have pierced, who shed both water and blood that we might be justified and sanctified in his name. — Matthew Henry

38 COMMENTARY: And after this Joseph of Arimathea, being a disciple of Jesus, but secretly for fear of the Jews, besought Pilate that he might take away the body of Jesus, and Pilate gave him leave, and he came and took the body of Jesus, for disciples should openly own themselves, yet some who in lesser trials have been fearful, in greater have been courageous. — Matthew Henry

39 COMMENTARY: And there came also Nicodemus, who at the first came to Jesus by night, and brought a mixture of myrrh and aloes, about a hundred pound weight, for grace at first like a bruised reed may afterward resemble a strong cedar. — Matthew Henry

40 COMMENTARY: Then they took the body of Jesus, and wound it in linen clothes with the spices, as the manner of the Jews is to bury, and hereby these two rich men showed the value they had for Christ's person and doctrine, not lessened by the reproach of the cross. — Matthew Henry

41 COMMENTARY: Now in the place where he was crucified there was a garden, and in the garden a new sepulchre, wherein was never man yet laid, so it could not be said that it was not he, but some other, that rose. — Matthew Henry

42 COMMENTARY: There they laid Jesus therefore because of the Jews' preparation day, for the sepulchre was near at hand, and we must do our duty as the present day and opportunity are, leaving God to fulfil his promises in his own way and time, for here the Sun of Righteousness set for a while, to rise again in greater glory, and then to set no more. — Matthew Henry

No note for verse(s) 13, 29 — the public-domain sources consulted give no distinct explanation for these verses beyond the list itself, so nothing was invented for them.
