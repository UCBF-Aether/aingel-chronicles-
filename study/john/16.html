Study Notes — John

Chapter 16

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: These things have I spoken unto you, that you should not be offended, he said, for notice of trouble is given so terror will not surprise his disciples. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: They shall put you out of the synagogues, he said; yea, the time comes, that whoever kills you will think he does God service, for real enemies of God's service may pretend zeal for it, and villainy is not changed by putting God's name to it. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: And these things will they do unto you, because they have not known the Father, nor me, for ignorance of God is the root of persecution. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: But these things I have told you, that when the time shall come, you may remember I told you of them, and these things I said not at the beginning, because I was with you, for while he was with them to teach, guide, and comfort, they did not yet need this promise of the Spirit's presence. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: But now I go my way to him who sent me, he said, and none of you asks me, Whither goest thou, for sorrow had silenced their questions. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: But because I have said these things unto you, sorrow has filled your heart, for too great affection for this present life hinders joy in God, and melancholy Christians look only on the dark side of the cloud. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: Nevertheless I tell you the truth, he said; it is expedient for you that I go away, for if I go not away, the Comforter will not come unto you, but if I depart, I will send him unto you, for his bodily presence could be in only one place at a time, but his Spirit is everywhere. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: And when he is come, he will reprove the world of sin, and of righteousness, and of judgment, for convincing work is the Spirit's work, and none but he can do it effectually. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: Of sin, because they believe not on me, for the Spirit convinces of the fact, fault, folly, filth, fountain, and fruit of sin, proving all the world guilty before God. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: Of righteousness, because I go to my Father, and you see me no more, for Christ's ascension proves the ransom was accepted and righteousness finished, through which believers are justified. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: Of judgment, because the prince of this world is judged, for when Satan's power is broken, all will be well, and no other power can stand before Christ. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: I have yet many things to say unto you, but you cannot bear them now, for their capacity was not yet enlarged by the Spirit. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: However, when he, the Spirit of truth, is come, he will guide you into all truth, for he shall not speak of himself, but whatsoever he shall hear, that shall he speak, and he will show you things to come, for the Spirit is our Guide, not only to show the way but to go with us by continued aids. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: He shall glorify me, he said, for he shall receive of mine, and shall show it unto you, for all the gifts and graces of the Spirit, all apostolic preaching and writing, were to glorify Christ. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: All things that the Father has are mine, he said; therefore I said, he shall take of mine, and shall show it unto you, for Father and Son share all things. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: A little while, and you shall not see me, he said, and again, a little while, and you shall see me, because I go to the Father, for it is good to consider how near our seasons of grace are to an end, that we may improve them. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: Then some of his disciples said among themselves, What is this that he says unto us, A little while, and you shall not see me, and again, a little while, and you shall see me, and, Because I go to the Father, for the saying perplexed them. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: They said therefore, What is this that he says, A little while, for we cannot tell what he says, repeating the riddle to one another. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: Now Jesus knew they were desirous to ask him, and said unto them, Do you inquire among yourselves of that I said, A little while, and you shall not see me, and again, a little while, and you shall see me, answering their unspoken question. — Matthew Henry

20 COMMENTARY: Verily, verily, I say unto you, that you shall weep and lament, but the world shall rejoice, and you shall be sorrowful, but your sorrow shall be turned into joy, for their sorrows would soon be turned into joy. — Matthew Henry

21 COMMENTARY: A woman when she is in travail has sorrow, because her hour is come, but as soon as she is delivered of the child, she remembers no more the anguish, for joy that a man is born into the world, the very image of sorrow turned to joy. — Matthew Henry

22 COMMENTARY: And you now therefore have sorrow, he said, but I will see you again, and your heart shall rejoice, and your joy no man takes from you, for neither men nor devils, neither sufferings in life nor in death, can deprive believers of their joy in Christ. — Matthew Henry

23 COMMENTARY: And in that day you shall ask me nothing, he said; verily, verily, whatever you shall ask the Father in my name, he will give it you, for asking of the Father shows a sense of spiritual wants and conviction they are to be had from God only. — Matthew Henry

24 COMMENTARY: Hitherto you have asked nothing in my name, he said; ask, and you shall receive, that your joy may be full, for asking in Christ's name acknowledges our unworthiness and full dependence on him as our Righteousness. — Matthew Henry

25 COMMENTARY: These things have I spoken unto you in proverbs, he said, but the time comes, when I shall no more speak unto you in proverbs, but I shall show you plainly of the Father, for after his resurrection he intended plainly to teach them of the Father and the way to him through his intercession. — Matthew Henry

26 COMMENTARY: At that day you shall ask in my name, he said, and I say not unto you, that I will pray the Father for you, for the frequency of this command shows the great end of Christ's mediation: to impress us with our sinfulness and the merit and power of his death, whereby we have access to God. — Matthew Henry

27 COMMENTARY: For the Father himself loves you, because you have loved me, and have believed that I came out from God, for to address the Father in Christ's name and to address the Son as God in human nature reconciling the world are the same, as Father and Son are one. — Matthew Henry

28 COMMENTARY: I came forth from the Father, and am come into the world, he said; again, I leave the world, and go to the Father, a plain declaration of his coming from the Father and his return to him, God manifest in the flesh at his entrance and received up into glory at his departure. — Matthew Henry

29 COMMENTARY: His disciples said, Lo, now speakest thou plainly, and speakest no proverb, for by this saying they improved in knowledge. — Matthew Henry

30 COMMENTARY: Now are we sure thou knowest all things, they said, and needest not that any man should ask thee; by this we believe thou camest forth from God, for they improved also in faith, saying, Now are we sure, though they knew not their own weakness. — Matthew Henry

31 COMMENTARY: Jesus answered them, Do ye now believe, questioning a faith that would so soon be tested. — Matthew Henry

32 COMMENTARY: Behold, the hour comes, yea, is now come, that you shall be scattered, every man to his own, and shall leave me alone, and yet I am not alone, because the Father is with me, for the divine nature did not desert the human, but supported it and put comfort and value into his sufferings. — Matthew Henry

33 COMMENTARY: These things I have spoken unto you, that in me you might have peace, he said; in the world you shall have tribulation, but be of good cheer, I have overcome the world, for peace in Christ is the only true peace, through him we have peace with God and in our own minds, and while we think we stand, let us take heed lest we fall, watching and praying without ceasing. — Matthew Henry
