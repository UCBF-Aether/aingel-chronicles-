Study Notes — John

Chapter 9

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: As Jesus passed by, he saw a man blind from his birth, whom Christ saw even though the man could not see him. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: His disciples asked, Master, who did sin, this man or his parents, that he was born blind, assuming uncommon calamity must be a special punishment. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: Jesus answered, Neither has this man sinned nor his parents, but that the works of God should be made manifest in him, for calamities are sometimes for God's glory, not for punishment. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: I must work the works of him who sent me while it is day, he said; the night comes when no man can work, for our life is our day and we must not waste it. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: As long as I am in the world, I am the light of the world, and his healing would show the light he brings to those blind by nature. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: When he had spoken, he spat on the ground, made clay of the spittle, and anointed the eyes of the blind man, using means men would despise, for human reason cannot judge the Lord's methods. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: He said, Go, wash in the pool of Siloam, which is by interpretation, Sent, and he went and washed, and came seeing, for those who would be healed by Christ must be ruled by him. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: The neighbours and those who had seen him begging before said, Is not this he who sat and begged, wondering at so great a change. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: Some said, This is he; others said, He is like him; but he said, I am he, for grace makes the same person widely different in character, a monument to the Redeemer's glory. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: They asked, How were thine eyes opened, desiring the method of so wonderful a work. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: He answered, A man called Jesus made clay, anointed my eyes, and said, Go to Siloam and wash, and I went and washed, and I received sight, telling simply what was done. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: They asked, Where is he, and he said, I know not, for he had not yet learned who his healer was in full. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: They brought him who had been blind to the Pharisees, to have the miracle judged by authority. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: And it was the Sabbath day when Jesus made the clay and opened his eyes, a circumstance the Pharisees seized upon. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: Then again the Pharisees asked him how he had received his sight, and he said, He put clay upon my eyes, and I washed, and do see, repeating the plain fact. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: Therefore some Pharisees said, This man is not of God, because he keeps not the Sabbath day, while others said, How can a man who is a sinner do such miracles, and there was a division among them, for their zeal for mere rites consumed the substance of religion. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: They said to the blind man again, What sayest thou of him, that he has opened thine eyes, and he said, He is a prophet, growing in his confession. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: But the Jews did not believe concerning him, that he had been blind and received his sight, until they called the parents of him who had received his sight. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: They asked his parents, Is this your son, who you say was born blind, and how then does he now see, vainly hoping to disprove a notable miracle. — Matthew Henry

20 COMMENTARY: His parents answered, We know this is our son, and that he was born blind, affirming what they knew and no more. — Matthew Henry

21 COMMENTARY: But by what means he now sees we know not, they said, nor who opened his eyes; he is of age, ask him, for he shall speak for himself. — Matthew Henry

22 COMMENTARY: These words his parents spoke because they feared the Jews, for the Jews had agreed that if any man confessed Jesus to be Christ, he should be put out of the synagogue, and the fear of man brings a snare. — Matthew Henry

23 COMMENTARY: Therefore his parents said, He is of age, ask him, leaving the testimony to the man himself. — Matthew Henry

24 COMMENTARY: Then again they called the man who was blind and said, Give God the praise; we know that this man is a sinner, pressing him to deny his healer. — Matthew Henry

25 COMMENTARY: He answered, Whether he be a sinner or no, I know not; one thing I know, that whereas I was blind, now I see, for in grace we may not know how the change was wrought, but we can say, Whereas I was blind, now I see. — Matthew Henry

26 COMMENTARY: Then they asked him again, What did he to thee, and how opened he thine eyes, repeating the question to wear him down. — Matthew Henry

27 COMMENTARY: He answered, I have told you already, and you did not hear; wherefore would you hear it again, and will you also be his disciples, turning their interrogation back on them. — Matthew Henry

28 COMMENTARY: Then they reviled him, saying, Thou art his disciple, but we are Moses' disciples, as if discipleship to Moses excused rejecting Christ. — Matthew Henry

29 COMMENTARY: We know God spoke to Moses, they said; as for this fellow, we know not from whence he is, clinging to what they knew and refusing what was before their eyes. — Matthew Henry

30 COMMENTARY: The man answered, Why, herein is a marvellous thing, that you know not from whence he is, and yet he has opened my eyes, for the unlearned and simple-hearted draw proper inferences from the light, while the learned who desire otherwise never come to truth. — Matthew Henry

31 COMMENTARY: Now we know God hears not sinners, he argued, but if any man is a worshipper of God and does his will, him he hears. — Matthew Henry

32 COMMENTARY: Since the world began it was not heard that any man opened the eyes of one born blind, a work without precedent. — Matthew Henry

33 COMMENTARY: If this man were not of God, he could do nothing, he concluded, arguing strongly that Jesus was not a sinner but was of God. — Matthew Henry

34 COMMENTARY: They answered, Thou wast altogether born in sins, and dost thou teach us, and they cast him out, for the unbelief of those who enjoy means of conviction is indeed marvellous. — Matthew Henry

35 COMMENTARY: Jesus heard they had cast him out, and when he found him, he said, Dost thou believe on the Son of God, for Christ owns those who own him, and takes particular notice of sufferers for his cause. — Matthew Henry

36 COMMENTARY: He answered, Who is he, Lord, that I might believe on him, desiring to know the object of his new faith. — Matthew Henry

37 COMMENTARY: Jesus said, Thou hast both seen him, and it is he that talks with thee, graciously revealing himself to the man. — Matthew Henry

38 COMMENTARY: He said, Lord, I believe, and he worshipped him, for none but God is to be worshipped, so in worshipping Jesus he owned him to be God. — Matthew Henry

39 COMMENTARY: Jesus said, For judgment I am come into this world, that those who see not might see, and those who see might be made blind, for he gives sight to the spiritually blind and seals up those high in their own conceit. — Matthew Henry

40 COMMENTARY: Some Pharisees with him heard these words and said, Are we blind also, resenting the implication. — Matthew Henry

41 COMMENTARY: Jesus said, If you were blind, you should have no sin, but now you say, We see, therefore your sin remains, for the self-confident reject grace, so their guilt remains unpardoned and unbroken. — Matthew Henry
