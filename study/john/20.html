Study Notes — John

Chapter 20

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: The first day of the week Mary Magdalene came early, when it was yet dark, unto the sepulchre, and saw the stone taken away from the sepulchre, for if Christ gave his life a ransom and had not taken it again, it would not have appeared that his giving was accepted as satisfaction. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: Then she ran, and came to Simon Peter, and to the other disciple, whom Jesus loved, and said, They have taken away the Lord out of the sepulchre, and we know not where they have laid him, for it was a great trial to Mary that the body was gone, and weak believers often make that a complaint which is really ground of hope and joy. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: Peter therefore went forth, and that other disciple, and came to the sepulchre, for it is well when those most honoured with disciples' privileges are most active in disciples' duties. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: So they ran both together, and the other disciple outran Peter, and came first to the sepulchre, for the disciple whom Jesus loved in a special manner, and who therefore in a special manner loved Jesus, was foremost, and the love of Christ makes us abound in every duty. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: And he, stooping down and looking in, saw the linen clothes lying, yet went he not in, pausing at the threshold. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: Then Simon Peter, following him, went into the sepulchre, and saw the linen clothes lie, for he who was behind was Peter, who had denied Christ, and a sense of guilt hinders us in the service of God. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: And the napkin, which was about his head, was not lying with the linen clothes, but wrapped together in a place by itself, an orderly sign that the body had not been stolen in haste. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: Then went in also that other disciple, who came first to the sepulchre, and he saw, and believed, for sight of the empty tomb, with the clothes in order, kindled faith. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: For as yet they knew not the Scripture, that he must rise again from the dead, for their faith ran ahead of their understanding of prophecy. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: Then the disciples went away again unto their own home, leaving Mary still seeking. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: But Mary stood without at the sepulchre weeping, and as she wept, she stooped down and looked into the sepulchre, for we are likely to seek and find when we seek with affection and seek in tears. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: And she saw two angels in white sitting, the one at the head, and the other at the feet, where the body of Jesus had lain, yet a sight of angels and their smiles will not suffice without a sight of Jesus. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: And they said unto her, Woman, why weepest thou, and she said, Because they have taken away my Lord, and I know not where they have laid him, for many believers complain of clouds and darkness which are methods of grace for humbling their souls and endearing Christ to them. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: And when she had said this, she turned herself back, and saw Jesus standing, and knew not that it was Jesus, for Christ, in manifesting himself to those who seek him, often outdoes their expectations. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: Jesus said unto her, Woman, why weepest thou, whom seekest thou, and she, supposing him to be the gardener, said, Sir, if thou have borne him hence, tell me where thou hast laid him, and I will take him away, for her heart was in earnest to find Jesus. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: Jesus said unto her, Mary, and she turned herself, and said unto him, Rabboni, which is to say, Master, for Christ's way of making himself known to his people is by his word, applied to their souls, speaking to them in particular. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: Jesus said unto her, Touch me not, for I am not yet ascended to my Father, but go to my brethren, and say unto them, I ascend unto my Father, and your Father, and to my God, and your God, forbidding her to expect his bodily presence to continue as before, and teaching the relation to God from union with Christ, for Christ's Father is our Father, and our God is his God. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: Mary Magdalene came and told the disciples that she had seen the Lord, and that he had spoken these things unto her, for those who know the word of Christ should endeavour that others get good from their knowledge. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: Then the same day at evening, being the first day of the week, when the doors were shut where the disciples were assembled for fear of the Jews, came Jesus and stood in the midst, and said unto them, Peace be unto you, for this day was evidently set apart as the Christian Sabbath, in remembrance of his resurrection, and no doors can shut out Christ's presence from his disciples' private assemblies. — Matthew Henry

20 COMMENTARY: And when he had said this, he showed unto them his hands and his side, and then were the disciples glad, when they saw the Lord, for a sight of Christ will gladden the heart of a disciple at any time, and the more we see of Jesus, the more we shall rejoice. — Matthew Henry

21 COMMENTARY: Then Jesus said to them again, Peace be unto you; as my Father has sent me, even so send I you, giving them their mission in his peace. — Matthew Henry

22 COMMENTARY: And when he had said this, he breathed on them, and said, Receive ye the Holy Ghost, showing that their spiritual life and all their ability for their work would be derived from him and depend upon him, for every word of Christ received by faith comes accompanied by this divine breathing. — Matthew Henry

23 COMMENTARY: Whose soever sins ye remit, they are remitted unto them, and whose soever sins ye retain, they are retained, for Christ directed the apostles to declare the only method by which sin would be forgiven, not as a power to give judgment, but to declare the character of those whom God would accept or reject. — Matthew Henry

24 COMMENTARY: But Thomas, one of the twelve, called Didymus, was not with them when Jesus came, and absence from the assembly cost him the sight of the Lord. — Matthew Henry

25 COMMENTARY: The other disciples therefore said unto him, We have seen the Lord, but he said, Except I shall see in his hands the print of the nails, and put my finger into the print of the nails, and thrust my hand into his side, I will not believe, for Thomas limited the Holy One of Israel, when he would be convinced by his own method or not at all. — Matthew Henry

26 COMMENTARY: And after eight days again his disciples were within, and Thomas with them, and then came Jesus, the doors being shut, and stood in the midst, and said, Peace be unto you, for that one day in seven should be religiously observed was pointed out by Christ's meeting his disciples on that day once and again. — Matthew Henry

27 COMMENTARY: Then he said to Thomas, Reach hither thy finger, and behold my hands, and reach hither thy hand, and thrust it into my side, and be not faithless, but believing, for there is not an unbelieving word in our tongues, nor thought in our minds, but it is known to the Lord Jesus, and he was pleased to accommodate himself even to Thomas, rather than leave him in unbelief. — Matthew Henry

28 COMMENTARY: And Thomas answered and said unto him, My Lord and my God, speaking with affection, as one who took hold of Christ with all his might, and ashamed of his unbelief. — Matthew Henry

29 COMMENTARY: Jesus said unto him, Thomas, because thou hast seen me, thou hast believed; blessed are they that have not seen, and yet have believed, for if we are faithless, we are Christless and graceless, hopeless and joyless, but sound and sincere believers, though slow and weak, shall be graciously accepted. — Matthew Henry

30 COMMENTARY: And many other signs truly did Jesus in the presence of his disciples, which are not written in this book, for only a part of his works was recorded. — Matthew Henry

31 COMMENTARY: But these are written, that you might believe that Jesus is the Christ, the Son of God, and that believing you might have life through his name, for these were committed to writing that all might believe he was the promised Messiah, the Saviour of sinners, and the Son of God. — Matthew Henry
