Study Notes — Job

Chapter 32

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: So these three men ceased to answer Job, because he was righteous in his own eyes. JFB notes the prose opening and the reason: they could not prove him unrighteous. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: Then was kindled the wrath of Elihu the son of Barachel the Buzite, of the kindred of Ram, against Job, because he justified himself rather than God. JFB explains Elihu as God is Jehovah, foreshadowing Christ as messenger between God and Job, Barachel as God blesses, Buzite from Buz son of Nahor, and Ram as Aram, with Job more eager to vindicate himself than God. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: Also against his three friends was Elihu's wrath kindled, because they had found no answer, and yet had condemned Job. JFB notes that though silenced in argument, they still held their opinion. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: Now Elihu had waited till Job had spoken, because they were elder than he. JFB explains had spoken in Hebrew as in words, referring to his own long-ready reply, kept back in deference to the friends' seniority. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: When Elihu saw there was no answer in the mouth of these three men, then his wrath was kindled. The silence that ended the debate is what finally moves him to speak. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: And Elihu the son of Barachel the Buzite answered and said, I am young, and you are very old, wherefore I was afraid, and dared not show you my opinion. JFB notes poetry begins with I am young, and was afraid from a Hebrew root meaning to crawl. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: Elihu said, days should speak, and multitude of years should teach wisdom. JFB explains days as the aged, who by years should teach. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: But there is a spirit in man, and the inspiration of the Almighty gives them understanding. JFB notes Elihu claims inspiration as a divinely commissioned messenger, uncontradicted in chapter 42, and renders it as the spirit God puts in man and inspiration giving understanding, not mere years. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: Great men are not always wise, neither do the aged understand judgment. JFB renders great as old, with greater and less for older and younger, and judgment as what is right. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: Therefore Elihu says, hearken to me, I also will show my opinion. JFB renders it as I say, with opinion as knowledge. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: Behold, Elihu waited for the friends' words, gave ear to their reasons, whilst they searched out what to say. JFB notes Elihu was therefore present from the first, with reasons as understandings, the meaning intended by words, waiting until they should discover a suitable reply. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: Yea, Elihu attended unto them, and behold, there was none of them that convinced Job, or answered his words. The long listening ends in a verdict of failure. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: Lest the friends should say, we have found out wisdom, God thrusts him down, not man. JFB explains this ordering: lest they pride themselves on overcoming Job by wisdom, God alone can confute him, not man, and Elihu grounds his confutation not on sages' maxims but on special commission from God. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: Now Job has not directed his words against Elihu, neither will Elihu answer him with the friends' speeches. JFB notes Elihu is unprejudiced, since Job did not address him, and the friends' speeches were influenced by irritation. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: They were amazed, they answered no more, they left off speaking. JFB notes Elihu turns from the friends to Job, passing to the third person as in rebuke, with words taken from them. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: When Elihu had waited, for they spoke not, but stood still and answered no more. The waiting is over; silence from the friends is complete. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: Elihu says, I will answer also my part, I also will show my opinion. JFB renders my part as for my part, with opinion as knowledge. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

18 COMMENTARY: For Elihu is full of matter, and the spirit within him constrains him. JFB contrasts his fullness of words with the friends who have not a word more, with spirit as in chapter 33 and the constraining spirit of prophecy. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

19 COMMENTARY: Behold, Elihu's belly is as wine which has no vent, like new bottles ready to burst. JFB explains belly as bosom, from which Oriental speech seems to come, like new wine in fermentation without a vent, kept in new goatskin bottles, fitting the young Elihu against the old friends. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

20 COMMENTARY: Elihu will speak, that he may be refreshed, opening his lips and answering. JFB renders refreshed literally as that there may be air to me. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

21 COMMENTARY: Let Elihu not accept any man's person, neither give flattering titles unto man. JFB notes Elihu alludes to Job's complaint that the friends plead for God partially, accepting his person, and promises impartiality between God and Job, without flattery. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

22 COMMENTARY: For Elihu knows not to give flattering titles; in so doing his maker would soon take him away. JFB explains take away as punishment, the maker removing the flatterer. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
