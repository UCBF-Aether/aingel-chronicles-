Study Notes — Job

Chapter 28

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Surely there is a vein for silver, and a place for gold where they fine it. JFB explains vein as a mine from which metal is dug, and place for gold as where mined gold is refined, unlike river gold needing no refining. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: Iron is taken out of the earth, and brass is molten out of the stone. JFB notes brass is copper, since brass as mixed metal is modern; iron, less easily discovered, is fitly said to come from earth, since ore looks like mere earth. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: Man sets an end to darkness, and searches out all perfection, the stones of darkness and the shadow of death. JFB renders it as man making an end of darkness by exploring darkest depths with torches, carrying search to utmost perfection among stones in the darkest bowels of earth. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: The flood breaks out from the inhabitant, even the waters forgotten of the foot, dried up and gone away from men. JFB lists three mining hardships: a sudden stream breaking out beside the strange newcomer, miners hanging forgotten by the foot on ropes, and moving with uncertain step far from men. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: As for the earth, out of it comes bread, and under it is turned up as it were fire. JFB notes the fertile surface yields food, yet beneath it is turned up as with fire, whether mining fire or glowing precious stones. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: The stones of it are the place of sapphires, and it has dust of gold. JFB notes sapphires in alluvial soil near rocks and in gneiss, with dust of gold as gold dust or clods of gold. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: There is a path which no fowl knows, and which the vulture's eye has not seen. JFB explains fowl as the ravenous bird or eagle, sharpest-sighted, yet the miner penetrates by a way unseen by keenest birds. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: The lion's whelps have not trodden it, nor the fierce lion passed by it. JFB explains lion's whelps as sons of pride, the fiercest beasts, whose proud gait never treads the miner's path. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: Man puts forth his hand upon the rock, and overturns mountains by the roots. JFB notes rock as flint, cleaved by hand, with mountains undermined from their foundations. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: He cuts out rivers among the rocks, and his eye sees every precious thing. JFB explains cutting channels to drain hindering waters, so that once waters are gone he sees the precious things in the earth. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: He binds the floods from overflowing, and brings to light the thing that is hid. JFB renders it as restraining streams from weeping, the trickling subterranean rills that impede mining, so hidden things come to light. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: But where shall wisdom be found, and where is the place of understanding? JFB asks whether man can discover divine wisdom governing the world as he discovers earthly treasures: certainly not, since wisdom is conceived as a person distinct from God. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: Man knows not the price thereof, neither is it found in the land of the living. JFB notes man can fix no price on wisdom, as it is nowhere found in man's abode, implying both its worth and its unpurchasability. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: The depth says it is not in me, and the sea says it is not with me. Neither depth nor sea holds wisdom, though both hold treasure. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: It cannot be gotten for gold, neither shall silver be weighed for the price thereof. JFB notes the gold here is purest gold, from a root meaning shut up with care, and metals were weighed out before coining. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: It cannot be valued with the gold of Ophir, with the precious onyx or the sapphire. JFB notes Ophir gold as most precious, and onyx, from Greek for thumbnail, more valued formerly than now. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: The gold and the crystal cannot equal it, and the exchange of it shall not be for jewels of fine gold. JFB explains crystal as glass if then known, very costly, and jewels as vessels. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

18 COMMENTARY: No mention shall be made of coral or of pearls, for the price of wisdom is above rubies. JFB explains coral as red coral, pearls literally as what is frozen, probably crystal, and rubies as pearls, with the sanctuary stones symbolizing wisdom's light and perfection. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

19 COMMENTARY: The topaz of Ethiopia shall not equal it, neither shall it be valued with pure gold. JFB notes Ethiopia as Cush, either Ethiopia or southern Arabia near the Tigris. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

20 COMMENTARY: Whence then comes wisdom, and where is the place of understanding? JFB notes verse 12 repeated with great force: the question returns, unanswered by any treasure. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

21 COMMENTARY: Seeing it is hid from the eyes of all living, and kept close from the fowls of the air. JFB notes none can tell whence or where, and even birds, to whom heathen divination was assigned for keen sight and heavenward flight, have no insight into it. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

22 COMMENTARY: Destruction and death say, we have heard the fame thereof with our ears. JFB explains destruction and death as the abodes of the dead, who have only heard wisdom's report, not seen her, since in the dead regions her actings on nature are not seen. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

23 COMMENTARY: God understands the way thereof, and knows the place thereof. JFB notes God has wisdom and is himself wisdom. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

24 COMMENTARY: For God looks to the ends of the earth, and sees under the whole heaven. His sight covers all that is under heaven, where man's sight stops. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

25 COMMENTARY: To make the weight for the winds, and weigh the waters by measure. JFB notes God adjusted the weight of seemingly imponderable winds, lest too heavy or too light should injure, and measured waters fixing their bounds, with wisdom as counsellor. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

26 COMMENTARY: When God made a decree for the rain, and a way for the lightning of the thunder. JFB explains the decree regulating time, place, and quantity of rain, with a way through parted clouds for lightning. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

27 COMMENTARY: Then did God see wisdom, declare it, prepare it, and search it out. JFB explains declare as manifesting wisdom in his works, prepared as established her as governor, not created, since wisdom is from everlasting, and searched out as examining her works for governing adequacy. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

28 COMMENTARY: And unto man God said, behold, the fear of the Lord, that is wisdom, and to depart from evil is understanding. JFB renders it as but unto man: God's wisdom governs all things, while man's wisdom is fearing God and shunning evil, assured God's wisdom acts aright though its principle is not understood, as in afflicting the godly. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
