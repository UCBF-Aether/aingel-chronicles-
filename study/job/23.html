Study Notes — Job

Chapter 23

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Job answers Eliphaz a third time, appealing from his friends to God's just judgment. The chapter is Job's search for the God who seems withdrawn. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: Even today Job's complaint is bitter, and his stroke heavier than his groaning. JFB notes today may imply the debate ran more than one day, and the stroke is God's hand upon him, so heavy he cannot relieve himself adequately by groaning. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: Oh that Job knew where he might find God, that he might come even to his seat! JFB notes the same wish as before, with seat as a well-prepared throne. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: Job would order his cause before God and fill his mouth with arguments. JFB explains order as stating methodically, and filling as having abundance of arguments to adduce. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: Job would know the words God would answer him, and understand what he would say unto him. JFB notes the emphasis: it little matters what man says of him, if only he knows what God judges of him. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: Will God plead against Job with his great power? No, but he would put strength in him. JFB notes the objection: Job does not wish God to plead with omnipotence, but to attend to him as an ordinary judge, giving a patient hearing in divine knowledge of his innocence, with strength as in Jacob's wrestling. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: There the righteous might dispute with God, and Job would be delivered forever from his judge. JFB renders there as then, if God would attend: the result of the dispute would be acknowledgment as righteous, delivered from suspicion of guilt by his judge. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: Behold, Job goes forward, but God is not there, and backward, but he cannot perceive him. JFB explains forward and backward as east and west, Hebrew geographers facing sunrise, so before means east and behind west. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: On the left hand, where God works, Job cannot behold him, and he hides himself on the right hand so Job cannot see him. JFB renders left as north, where God's glorious works are especially seen, and right as south, the unexplored region then thought uninhabitable for heat, where God hides himself. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: But God knows the way that Job takes; when he has tried him, Job shall come forth as gold. JFB notes Job corrects himself: the omniscient One already knows his inward way, though for inscrutable cause he hides himself, and if he tries the cause Job shall come forth as gold. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: Job's foot has held his steps, his way he has kept and not declined. JFB explains held as fast by God's steps, the law as a way with God going before as guide, in whose footsteps Job must tread. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: Job has not gone back from the commandment of God's lips, esteeming his words more than his necessary food. JFB renders esteemed as laid up as treasure found, alluding to Eliphaz's counsel to lay up God's words, which Job says he has already done, more than his appointed portion of food. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: But God is in one mind, and who can turn him? What his soul desires, even that he does. JFB notes that notwithstanding Job's innocence, God is unaltered in purpose, with soul as will, and sovereignty: one great purpose, nothing haphazard. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: For God performs the thing appointed for Job, and many such things are with him. JFB notes God has yet many more such ills in store, though hidden in his breast. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: Therefore Job is troubled at God's presence; when he considers, he is afraid of him. JFB notes God's decrees, impossible to resist and leaving us in the dark about what comes next, are calculated to fill the mind with holy awe. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: For God makes Job's heart soft, and the Almighty troubles him. JFB renders soft as faint, melted courage. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: Because Job was not cut off before the darkness, nor has God covered the darkness from his face. JFB explains it as not taken away by death from the evil to come, and cut off in the Arabic sense as brought to the land of silence, his complaint hushed in death, while instead of covering the cloud of evil from his face, God covers him with it. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
