Study Notes — Job

Chapter 26

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Job answers Bildad, turning Bildad's majesty speech back on him with irony. The reply first exposes the friends' unhelpfulness, then outdoes them in describing God's greatness. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: How has Bildad helped him that is without power, and saved the arm that has no strength? JFB explains the negatives as designedly used for powerlessness: granting Job is powerlessness itself, how has Bildad helped such a one, with savest as supportest. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: How has Bildad counselled him that has no wisdom, and plentifully declared the thing as it is? JFB renders plentifully as abundantly wisdom, asking how Bildad's great pretensions to abundant wisdom have shown themselves. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: To whom has Bildad uttered words, and whose spirit came from him? JFB asks for whose instruction the words were meant, since Job knows God's omnipotence better than his instructor, and whose spirit is not God's but the borrowed sentiment of Eliphaz. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: Dead things are formed from under the waters, and the inhabitants thereof. JFB renders it as the souls of the dead trembling, with Rephaim from a root meaning weak, hence deceased, under the waters where the dead dwell, showing God's power reaches hell as well as heaven. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: Hell is naked before God, and destruction has no covering. JFB explains destruction as Abaddon, the abode of lost souls, with no covering from God's eyes. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: God stretches out the north over the empty place, and hangs the earth upon nothing. JFB notes the hint of the true theory of the earth: suspension in empty space, with the north as the highest part and the northern vault as a stretched canopy, consistent with the earth's globular form. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: God binds up the waters in his thick clouds, and the cloud is not rent under them. JFB compares the clouds to airy vessels which, though light, do not burst with the weight of water in them. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: God holds back the face of his throne, and spreads his cloud upon it. JFB renders it as encompassing or closing: God makes clouds a veil screening even the exterior of his throne from profane eyes, his agency everywhere yet himself invisible. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: God has compassed the waters with bounds, until the day and night come to an end. JFB renders it as drawing a circular bound round the waters, the horizon as a circle, indicating the earth's globular form, to the confines of light and darkness. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: The pillars of heaven tremble and are astonished at his reproof. JFB explains pillars poetically as mountains seeming to bear up the sky, astonished from terror, with reproof as thunder reverberating from cliff to cliff. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: God divides the sea with his power, and by his understanding smites through the proud. JFB explains divides as stilling or moving, perhaps at creation or the flood's assuaging, with the proud as the sea's pride. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: By his spirit God has garnished the heavens, and his hand has formed the crooked serpent. JFB explains the spirit as breath or wind dissipating clouds to revive the heavens, and the crooked serpent as the oblique, fleeing constellation, representing the splendour of all stars. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: Lo, these are parts of his ways, but how little a portion is heard of him? The thunder of his power who can understand? JFB renders parts as only the extreme boundaries, and the portion heard as a faint whisper against the fullness of thunder. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
