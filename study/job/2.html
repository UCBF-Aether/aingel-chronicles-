Study Notes — Job

Chapter 2

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Again there is a day when the sons of God present themselves, and Satan comes among them. JFB notes this is an appointed day for the angels to give account of their ministry, and Satan now has a special report to make about Job. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: The LORD asks Satan where he has come from, and Satan again answers that he has been going to and fro on the earth. The repetition frames a second, sharper trial. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: God again commends Job as perfect and upright, fearing God and shunning evil, and adds that he still holds fast his integrity although God was moved against him without cause. JFB explains integrity as completeness, the same word elsewhere rendered perfect. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: Satan answers with the proverb skin for skin: a man will trade any outward good for another, but life itself cannot be replaced. His sneer is that Job bore outward losses calmly only because his own person was untouched, and will give up even religion to save his life. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: Satan dares God to touch Job's bone and flesh, predicting Job will curse him to his face. The wager moves from property to the body itself. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: The LORD puts Job in Satan's hand but commands him to spare his life. JFB notes Satan's ingenuity in inflicting pain, and his knowledge of what the body can bear without vital injury. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: Satan strikes Job with sore boils from sole to crown. JFB explains the Hebrew as a singular burning sore, a universal inflammation, of the black leprous kind called elephantiasis, because the feet swell like an elephant's. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: Job takes a potsherd to scrape himself and sits among the ashes. JFB notes the potsherd was an instrument made for scratching, since the sore was too disgusting to touch, and sitting in ashes marks the deepest mourning and humility, as dust and ashes. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: Job's wife urges him to curse God and die. JFB reads curse as renounce: take farewell of God and so die, since, in her bitter view, no good is to be got from religion either here or hereafter. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: Job answers that she speaks as one of the foolish women speaks, and asks whether we should receive good from God and not also receive evil. JFB notes that sin and folly are allied in Scripture, and that receiving evil means bearing it willingly. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: Job's three friends — Eliphaz the Temanite, Bildad the Shuhite, and Zophar the Naamathite — hear of his evil and agree together to come mourn with him and comfort him. JFB traces Eliphaz to Idumea, where Teman in the south was famed for wisdom, and Bildad to Shuah, a son of Abraham and Keturah. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: Seeing Job from afar, the friends do not recognize him, lift up their voices and weep, tear their mantles, and sprinkle dust toward heaven. JFB notes the dust thrown upward so it falls on their heads is the deepest mourning. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: The friends sit with Job on the ground seven days and seven nights, and no one speaks a word, for his grief is very great. JFB notes seven days was the usual length of mourning, and that extraordinary griefs are too great for utterance, while ordinary griefs find vent in language. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
