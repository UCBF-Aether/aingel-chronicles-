Study Notes — Job

Chapter 25

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Bildad the Shuhite answers briefly, trying to show Job's rashness in wishing to come before God by arguments borrowed from Eliphaz. The short speech is the friends' last attempt before Job's long reply. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: Dominion and fear are with God, who makes peace in his high places. JFB explains dominion and fear as terror-inspiring power, with peace in high places implying power quelling all opposition on high and on earth. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: Is there any number of God's armies, and upon whom does not his light arise? JFB explains armies as angels and stars, countless, with his light rising upon all. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: How then can man be justified with God, or he born of a woman be clean? JFB refers back to Eliphaz's revelation and Job's own words on born of a woman: the question is the friends' standing challenge. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: Behold, even to the moon it shines not, and the stars are not pure in God's sight. JFB notes the moon answers to the heavens in Eliphaz's version, and stars to saints or angels: even the most dazzling objects, and angels of whom stars are emblems, are imperfect before the Creator, whose light and purity are not creaturely. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: How much less man, that is a worm, and the son of man, which is a worm? JFB notes two distinct Hebrew words: the first a worm bred in putridity, alluding to corruption, the second a crawling worm, implying weakness and grovelling. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
