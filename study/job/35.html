Study Notes — Job

Chapter 35

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Elihu spoke moreover, and said. This is Elihu's third speech, turning to Job's claim about the profit of righteousness. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: Thinkest thou this to be right, that thou saidst, my righteousness is more than God's? JFB renders it as I am righteous before God: Elihu means Job said so virtually, not in so many words. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: For thou saidst, what advantage will it be unto thee, and what profit shall I have if I be cleansed from my sin? JFB explains this as explanatory of verse 2: what advantage is thy integrity to thee, what profit more than if thou hadst sinned, words the wicked use which Job virtually sanctioned by saying they do not suffer for it. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: Elihu will answer Job, and his companions with him. JFB explains companions as those entertaining like sentiments with Job. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: Look unto the heavens, and see, and behold the clouds which are higher than thou. JFB notes Elihu, like Eliphaz, shows God is too exalted to be susceptible of benefit or hurt from men's righteousness or sin: the clouds are higher than Job, yet not as high as God's seat, so God has no inducement to injustice, and when he afflicts it must be for the good of the sufferer. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: If Job sins, what does he against God? Or if his transgressions be multiplied, what does he unto him? JFB renders it as how canst thou affect him, that can hurt him? — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: If Job be righteous, what gives he unto God, or what receives he of Job's hand? God gains nothing from man's righteousness, as the Psalms and Proverbs affirm. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: Job's wickedness may hurt a man as he is, and his righteousness may profit the son of man. It is themselves, not God, that men benefit by righteousness or hurt by sin. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: By reason of the multitude of oppressions the oppressed cry out by reason of the arm of the mighty. JFB notes Elihu states in Job's words the difficulty: the cries of the oppressed not being heard might lead man to think wrongs are not punished by God. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: But none says, where is God my maker, who gives songs in the night? JFB explains the reason: innocent sufferers often do not humbly seek God for succour, so pride is to blame for their ruin, and if the sufferer would apply to God with a humbled, penitent spirit, he would hear, with songs as joy at deliverance, and in the night as unexpectedly or in calamity. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: Who teaches us more than the beasts of the earth, and makes us wiser than the fowls of heaven? JFB notes man's spirit, which distinguishes him from the brute, is the strongest proof of God's beneficence, by which we may understand God is the Almighty helper of all sufferers who humbly seek him. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: There they cry, but none gives answer, because of the pride of evil men. JFB renders there as then, when none humbly casts himself on God: they cry proudly against God rather than humbly to God, so there can be no answer until pride gives place to humble, penitent prayer. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: Surely God will not hear vanity, neither will the Almighty regard it. JFB renders vanity as cries uttered in an unhumbled spirit, which applies in some degree to Job's cries, and still more to those of the wicked. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: Although Job says he shall not see God, yet judgment is before him, therefore let him trust in him. JFB explains judgment as thy cause, thy right, and trust as wait thou on him patiently until he take up thy cause, noting Elihu alludes to Job's words about not seeing God. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: But now, because it is not so, God has visited in his anger, yet he knows it not in great extremity. JFB explains that because Job waited not trustingly and patiently, God has visited, yet still has not taken severe cognizance of the great multitude of sins, so Job should not complain of being punished with undue severity. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: Therefore Job opens his mouth in vain, and multiplies words without knowledge. JFB calls this the apodosis to verse 15, with in vain as rashly. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
