Study Notes — Job

Chapter 18

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Bildad the Shuhite answers a second time, replying to Job's continued defence. The speech turns from doctrine to a catalogue of the wicked's doom. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: How long will it be ere you make an end of words? Mark, and afterwards we will speak. JFB explains Bildad charges the other friends with empty speeches, opposed to mark, coming to reason and considering intelligently. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: Why are we counted as beasts, and reputed vile in your sight? JFB explains beasts as alluding to Job's appeal to beasts in chapter 12, and vile as stubborn, answering the stupidity implied in the first clause. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: He tears himself in his anger: shall the earth be forsaken for him, and the rock removed out of its place? JFB notes Bildad turns to Job the self-tearer, arguing bitterly that unless Job is punished, the eternal order would be disturbed and the earth desolate through unavenged wickedness. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: Yea, the light of the wicked shall be put out, and the spark of his fire shall not shine. JFB notes God's decree is unalterable, and the fire alludes to Arabian hospitality, welcoming strangers to the tent fire: the ungodly shall be deprived even of that, his dwelling dark and desolate. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: The light shall be dark in his tabernacle, and his candle shall be put out with him. JFB explains the candle as the Eastern ceiling lamp kept burning all night, whose extinction images utter desolation. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: The steps of his strength shall be straitened, and his own counsel shall cast him down. JFB explains strong steps as firm health, straitened steps as inability to move at will, and his counsel as plans becoming the means of his fall. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: For he is cast into a net by his own feet, and he walks upon a snare. JFB renders it as letting himself go into the net, or walking on a covered pitfall that gives way beneath him. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: The gin shall take him by the heel, and the robber shall prevail against him. JFB explains the robber as the noose holding him fast, answering to the gin in the parallel clause. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: The snare is laid for him in the ground, and a trap for him in the way. The net, gin, snare, and trap multiply one image: the wicked's own path is mined against him. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: Terrors shall make him afraid on every side, and shall drive him to his feet. JFB notes the terrors of an evil conscience personified, pursuing him close at his heels like a conqueror scattering the enemy. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: His strength shall be hunger-bitten, and destruction shall be ready at his side. JFB gives the brief, bold Hebrew: his strength is hungry, with destruction as a great calamity close at hand. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: It shall devour the strength of his skin, and the firstborn of death shall devour his strength. JFB explains strength as members or branches, and the firstborn of death as the chiefest disease death ever engendered, a personification full of poetical horror. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: His confidence shall be rooted out of his tabernacle, and it shall bring him to the king of terrors. JFB explains confidence as all a father trusted for domestic happiness, rooted out suddenly, and brought slowly and solemnly to Death personified, the king of terrors. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: It shall dwell in his tabernacle because it is none of his, and brimstone shall be scattered upon his habitation. JFB explains terror shall haunt his house, now his no longer, with brimstone comparing Job's fire of God to Sodom's destruction. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: His roots shall be dried up beneath, and above shall his branch be cut off. JFB explains roots as himself, and branch as his children. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: His remembrance shall perish from the earth, and he shall have no name in the street. JFB renders street as field or meadow, where shepherds shall no more mention his name, a picture from nomadic life. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

18 COMMENTARY: He shall be driven from light into darkness, and chased out of the world. JFB explains light and darkness as existence and nonexistence. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

19 COMMENTARY: He shall neither have son nor nephew among his people, nor any remaining in his dwellings. JFB notes nephew is translated grandson elsewhere, but here means kinsman: no kin remains. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

20 COMMENTARY: They that come after him shall be astonished at his day, as they that went before were affrighted. JFB explains after and before as West and East, all peoples, seized with terror at his day of ruin. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

21 COMMENTARY: Surely such are the dwellings of the wicked, and this is the place of him that knows not God. The catalogue closes with its application: this is the end of the godless. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
