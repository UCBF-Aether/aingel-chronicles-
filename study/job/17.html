Study Notes — Job

Chapter 17

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Job's breath is corrupt, his days are extinct, the graves are ready for him. JFB notes corrupt breath is the result of elephantiasis, extinct days are an expiring light, and the plural graves heightens the force. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: Are there not mockers with Job, and does not his eye continue in their provocation? JFB renders it emphatically: had he only not to endure mockery amid their contentions, his eye would remain quiet, tarrying all night in undisturbed rest. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: Lay down now, put Job in a surety with God; who is he that will strike hands with him? JFB explains the pledge both litigants laid down before trial, and the surety as a presage of the Mediator, since only God himself could strike hands as security. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: God has hidden the friends' heart from understanding, therefore he shall not exalt them. JFB explains heart as intellect, and renders exalt as imperative: exalt them not, allow them not to conquer. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: He that speaks flattery to his friends, even the eyes of his children shall fail. JFB explains flattery as smoothness, then a prey divided by lot with a smooth stone, so the sense is delivering up a friend as prey, retorting the friends' reproach about Job's cut-off children. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: God has made Job a byword of the people, and aforetime he was as a tabret. JFB notes God is reverentially unnamed, byword as an execrated name implying superlative badness, and the tabret reading as being treated to his face as an object to spit upon. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: Job's eye is dim by reason of sorrow, and all his members are as a shadow. JFB notes members literally as figures, the individual forms of the body, opposed to a shadow without solidity. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: Upright men shall be astonished at this, and the innocent shall stir up himself against the hypocrite. JFB explains astonied as at unmerited sufferings, and the upright's sense of justice wounded and indignant at the wicked's prosperity, glancing at the false friends. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: The righteous also shall hold on his way, and he that has clean hands shall be stronger and stronger. JFB notes religious principle is heightened by misfortune, and the pious take fresh courage from suffering Job, like a warrior gaining courage in action. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: But as for you all, do you return, for Job cannot find one wise man among you. JFB renders return as recommencing their speech if they have anything really wise to advance, which Job doubts. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: Job's days are past, his purposes are broken off, even the thoughts of his heart. JFB explains broken off as threads cut from the loom, and thoughts as possessions, the feelings and fair hopes his heart once nourished. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: They change the night into day, and the light is short because of darkness. JFB explains the friends would persuade Job his misery will change into joy, which is impossible, and that any new light would be near only before the darkness of death. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: If Job waits for the grave as his house, and makes his bed in darkness, where is his hope? JFB renders it as waiting for Sheol as his house, with the apodosis at verse 15. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: Job has said to corruption, you are my father, and to the worm, you are my mother and sister. JFB notes this expresses most intimate connection: his diseased state makes him closely akin to grave and worm. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: And where is now Job's hope? As for his hope, who shall see it? JFB notes the question is who shall see fulfilled the hope of restoration the friends held out. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: They shall go down to the bars of the pit, when our rest together is in the dust. JFB explains his hopes shall be buried with him, the bars as the wastes of Sheol, and the rest as his and his hope's together — both expire together. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
