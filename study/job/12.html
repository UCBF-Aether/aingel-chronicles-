Study Notes — Job

Chapter 12

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Job answers Zophar, turning the friends' wisdom back on them with irony. The reply claims equal understanding and exposes their commonplaces. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: No doubt you are the people, and wisdom shall die with you. JFB calls this ironical, as if all the world's wisdom were concentrated in them and would expire when they expired. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: Job also has understanding, is not inferior to them, and knows such things as these. JFB explains not inferior as not vanquished in argument, and such things as commonplace maxims pompously adduced. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: Job is as one mocked of his neighbour, who calls upon God and God answers him, yet the just upright man is laughed to scorn. JFB notes the friends' unfounded accusations were a mockery, answering Zophar's word mockest, and renders neighbour as Job himself calling on God for a favourable answer. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: He who is ready to slip with his feet is as a lamp despised in the thought of him who is at ease. JFB gives the torch image: the secure traveller contemptuously throws aside the torch that guided his uncertain steps, as Job's friends now mock the helper they once gladly used. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: The tabernacles of robbers prosper, and they who provoke God are secure, into whose hand God brings abundantly. JFB notes fact opposes Zophar's theory that wickedness causes insecurity, and renders the last clause as those who make a god of their own hand, regarding might as their only rule. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: Ask the beasts and they shall teach you, and the fowls of the air shall tell you. JFB notes beasts, birds, fishes, and plants teach that the violent live most securely: the vulture more securely than the dove, the lion than the ox. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: Speak to the earth and it shall teach you, and the fishes of the sea shall declare it to you. JFB renders speak to the earth as the shrubs of the earth, joining the plant world to the animal witnesses. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: Who among all these does not know that the hand of the LORD has wrought this? JFB notes that in all these cases agency must be referred to Jehovah, and that this is the only undisputed passage in the poetical part where the name Jehovah occurs. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: In God's hand is the soul of every living thing and the breath of all mankind. JFB explains soul as animal life, and notes man is subjected to the same laws as the lower animals. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: Does not the ear try words, as the mouth tastes meat? JFB compares tasting meats with trying words: each chooses according to taste, and Job may borrow the ancients' proverbs that suit his case as the friends borrow those suiting theirs. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: With the ancient is wisdom, and in length of days understanding. JFB notes ancient means aged, and Job concedes the point only to turn it: with God, not merely with the aged, is true wisdom. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: With God is wisdom and strength, and he has counsel and understanding. JFB contrasts this with the ancient is wisdom: Job quotes an ancient saying suiting his argument, that with God is true wisdom, and man's part is to bow to his unchangeable decrees. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: If God breaks down, it cannot be built again; if he shuts up a man, there can be no opening. JFB notes Job refers to Zophar's shut up, now as God's absolute sovereignty. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: If God withholds the waters they dry up, and if he sends them out they overturn the earth. JFB probably sees an allusion to the flood, God's sovereign control over waters that bless or destroy. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: With God are strength and wisdom, and the deceived and the deceiver are his. Both sides of human deception fall under the same sovereign hand. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: God leads counsellors away spoiled, and makes judges fools. The wisest human counsel is stripped bare before him. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

18 COMMENTARY: God loosens the bond of kings and girds their loins with a girdle. JFB explains the bond as the authority with which kings bind subjects, replaced by the cord of captives instead of the royal girdle. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

19 COMMENTARY: God leads princes away spoiled and overthrows the mighty. JFB renders princes as priests, so even sacred ministers are not exempt from reversals, and the mighty as firm-rooted in power. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

20 COMMENTARY: God removes away the speech of the trusty and takes away the understanding of the aged. JFB renders the trusty as those secure in eloquence, and understanding as taste, the insight experience gives the aged. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

21 COMMENTARY: God pours contempt upon princes and weakens the strength of the mighty. JFB explains weakening as loosening the girdle: Orientals gird flowing garments for action, so loosening destroys their power in the people's eyes. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

22 COMMENTARY: God discovers deep things out of darkness and brings out to light the shadow of death. What is hidden from men is open before him. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

23 COMMENTARY: God increases the nations and destroys them, enlarges them and straitens them again. JFB notes straiteneth literally means leadeth in, that is, reduces. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

24 COMMENTARY: God takes away the heart of the chief of the people and causes them to wander in a wilderness where there is no way. JFB explains heart as intelligence, and the wilderness as figurative, not a reference to Israel's desert wanderings. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

25 COMMENTARY: They grope in the dark without light, and God makes them stagger like a drunken man. The loss of intelligence leaves leaders groping and reeling. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
