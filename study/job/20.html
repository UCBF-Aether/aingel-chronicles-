Study Notes — Job

Chapter 20

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Zophar the Naamathite answers a second time, provoked by Job's reply yet claiming calm reason. The speech is a second catalogue of the wicked's brief triumph and ruin. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: Therefore Zophar's thoughts cause him to answer, and for this he makes haste. JFB explains that the more excited he feels, the more his calm thoughts furnish his answer, notwithstanding his haste within. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: Zophar has heard the check of his reproach, and the spirit of his understanding causes him to answer. JFB explains the check as castigation intended as shame to him, and the spirit as his rational spirit answering in calm reason despite reproach urging hastiness. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: Know you not this of old, since man was placed upon earth, that the triumphing of the wicked is short? The appeal is to ancient knowledge, not new theory. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: The triumphing of the wicked is short, and the joy of the hypocrite but for a moment. JFB explains hypocrite literally as the ungodly, whose joy lasts only a moment. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: Though the wicked's excellency mounts up to the heavens, and his head reaches unto the clouds, yet he shall perish forever like his own dung. JFB cites the same towering pride as in Isaiah and Obadiah. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: Yet he shall perish forever like his own dung, and they who have seen him shall say where is he? JFB notes dung expresses disgust and lowest degradation against the sinner's haughtiness. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: He shall fly away as a dream and shall not be found, chased away as a vision of the night. The brief triumph vanishes like a dream on waking. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: The eye that saw him shall see him no more, neither shall his place behold him any more. JFB renders it as the eye following him but discerning him no more, a sharp looking that finds nothing. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: His children shall seek to please the poor, and his hands shall restore their goods. JFB explains this as atoning to the poor by restoring robbed property, or the children reduced to seeking favour from the very poor their father oppressed, with their hands restoring the poor's goods in righteous retribution. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: His bones are full of the sin of his youth, which shall lie down with him in the dust. JFB notes bones allude to Job's disease and his own words, and that sin so pervades his nature it accompanies him to the grave, never to be rid of. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: Though wickedness is sweet in his mouth, and he hides it under his tongue, sin's fascination is poison sweet to the taste but deadly to vital organs, prolonged by keeping the sweet morsel long in the mouth. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: Though he spares it and forsakes it not, but keeps it still within his mouth, the sweet morsel is kept only to turn bitter within. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: Yet his meat in his bowels is turned, and becomes the gall of asps within him. JFB explains turned as a total change into a disagreeable contrary, and gall as where the asp's poison was thought to lie. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: He has swallowed down riches, and shall vomit them up again; God shall cast them out of his belly. JFB notes he is forced to disgorge ill-gotten wealth. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: He shall suck the poison of asps, and the viper's tongue shall slay him. JFB notes it shall turn out he has sucked the poison all along. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: He shall not see the rivers, the floods, the brooks of honey and butter. JFB explains floods as plentiful streams, and honey and butter poured from jars as emblems of prosperity in the sultry East, now denied him. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

18 COMMENTARY: That which he laboured for he shall restore, and shall not swallow it down, and shall not rejoice in his substance. JFB gives the image of food taken away before swallowing, and restitution ending all enjoyment of ill-gotten gains. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

19 COMMENTARY: Because he oppressed and forsaken the poor, and violently taken away a house he did not build. JFB notes he ought to have espoused the poor's cause, but left them helpless and without shelter. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

20 COMMENTARY: Surely he shall not feel quietness in his belly, and shall not save of that which he desired. JFB renders it as inward parts knowing no rest from desires, with no inward peace, and not escaping with what he desired, alluding to Job stripped of all. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

21 COMMENTARY: There shall none of his meat be left, therefore no man shall look for his goods. JFB renders it as because his goods shall have no endurance, no one will look for them. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

22 COMMENTARY: In the fulness of his sufficiency he shall be in straits, and every hand of the wicked shall come upon him. JFB renders straitened as feeling straitened, with the whole hand of the miserable he oppressed coming upon him, the sense of his oppression now turning on him. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

23 COMMENTARY: When he is about to fill his belly, God shall cast the fury of his wrath upon him, and rain it upon him while he is eating. JFB explains the fiery rain as lightning, alluding to Job's misfortune, the opposite of refreshing desert rain. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

24 COMMENTARY: He shall flee from the iron weapon, and the bow of steel shall strike him through. JFB notes steel as brass, and that fleeing one danger he falls into a greater from the opposite quarter. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

25 COMMENTARY: It is drawn and comes out of the body, the glittering sword out of his gall, and terrors are upon him. JFB explains God drawing the sword so it passes right through the body, glittering like lightning, with gall as his life, inflicting a deadly wound. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

26 COMMENTARY: All darkness shall be hid in his secret places, and a fire not blown shall consume him, leaving evil in his tabernacle. JFB explains darkness as every calamity stored in God's secret treasures, and fire not blown as kindled by God, not man's hands, obliterating every trace. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

27 COMMENTARY: The heaven shall reveal his iniquity, and the earth shall rise up against him. JFB notes all creation is at enmity with him, proclaiming the guilt he would conceal. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

28 COMMENTARY: The increase of his house shall depart, and his goods shall flow away in the day of his wrath. JFB explains increase as prosperity, ill got and ill gone, flowing away like waters running dry in summer, using Job's own brook metaphor against him. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

29 COMMENTARY: This is the portion of a wicked man from God, and the heritage appointed unto him by God. JFB notes appointed is not chance but divine decree and settled principle. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
