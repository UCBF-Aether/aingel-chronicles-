Study Notes — Job

Chapter 33

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Wherefore, Job, Elihu prays him to hear his speeches and hearken to all his words. The address turns from the friends to Job alone. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: Behold, now Elihu has opened his mouth, and his tongue has spoken in his mouth. JFB renders mouth as palate, whereby the taste discerns: few, as Elihu, try their words with discrimination first and only say what is really good. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: Elihu's words shall be of the uprightness of his heart, and his lips shall utter knowledge clearly. JFB renders clearly as purely: sincerely, not distorting the truth through passion, as the friends did. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: The Spirit of God has made Elihu, and the breath of the Almighty has given him life. JFB notes the breath of the Almighty has inspired him, so he stands according to Job's wish in God's stead, a daysman or mediator between God and Job, designed as a type of Christ. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: If Job can answer Elihu, let him set his words in order before him and stand up. JFB notes images from a court of justice, alluding to Job's words about standing before God. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: Behold, Elihu is according to Job's wish in God's stead, also formed out of the clay. JFB explains formed as pressed together as a mass of clay by the potter, so that though acting as God's representative, he is but a creature like Job. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: Behold, Elihu's terror shall not make Job afraid, neither shall his hand be heavy upon him. JFB notes this alludes to Job's words asking that God's hand not lie heavy upon him. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: Surely Job has spoken in Elihu's hearing, and he has heard the voice of his words. JFB explains that Job had acknowledged sin in general, but the general spirit of his words was to maintain himself clean and charge God with injustice, going too far against the friends' false charge of hypocrisy. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: Job said, I am clean without transgression, innocent, with no iniquity in me. JFB renders clean as spotless, the claim Elihu takes up. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: Behold, God finds occasions against Job and counts him for his enemy. JFB renders occasions as enmities, the hostility Job charged upon God. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: God puts Job's feet in the stocks and marks all his paths. JFB notes marketh as narrowly watches. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: Behold, in this Job is not just, for God is greater than man. JFB explains it cannot be that God should jealously watch man, though spotless, as an enemy, for there must be sin in man, even though no hypocrite, which needs correction by suffering for the sufferer's good. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: Why does Job strive against God, for he gives not account of any of his matters? JFB renders his matters as ways: our part is not to strive with God but to submit, believing it right because he does it, not because we see all the reasons. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: For God speaks once, yea twice, yet man perceives it not. JFB renders it as man regards it not, or twice he repeats the warning if man gives no heed to the first: God tries what affliction will effect when man in prosperity has not heeded his will. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: In a dream, in a vision of the night, when deep sleep falls upon men, in slumberings upon the bed. JFB notes slumberings as light sleep opposed to deep sleep, and dreams and visions as among the ways God then spoke to man. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: Then God opens the ears of men and seals their instruction. JFB renders it literally as sealing their ears to himself by warnings, with the sureness and secrecy of a seal he reveals his warnings. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: That God may withdraw man from his purpose and hide pride from man. JFB explains purpose as work or business in a bad sense, and pride as an open pit which God hides or covers up, lest man fall into it. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

18 COMMENTARY: God keeps back man's soul from the pit, and his life from perishing by the sword. JFB explains soul as life, the pit as the grave and symbol of hell, and perishing by the sword as a violent death. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

19 COMMENTARY: Man is chastened also with pain upon his bed, and the multitude of his bones with strong pain. JFB notes the new thought: affliction is disciplinary for the good of the godly, with the perpetual contest of his bones as the never-resting fever in them. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

20 COMMENTARY: So that man's life abhors bread, and his soul dainty meat. JFB explains life as appetite, which ordinarily sustains life: the taking away of desire for food by sickness symbolizes the removal by affliction of lust for things which foster the spiritual fever of pride. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

21 COMMENTARY: His flesh is consumed away, that it cannot be seen, and his bones that were not seen stick out. JFB notes flesh once prominent can no more be seen, while bones once not seen now appear prominent. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

22 COMMENTARY: Yea, man's soul draws near unto the grave, and his life to the destroyers. JFB explains destroyers as angels of death commissioned by God to end man's life, or the death pains personified. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

23 COMMENTARY: If there be a messenger with him, an interpreter, one among a thousand, to show unto man his uprightness. JFB explains Elihu refers to himself as the divinely-sent messenger and interpreter to explain and vindicate God's righteousness, one rarely to be found, a type of Jesus Christ the Mediator. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

24 COMMENTARY: Then God is gracious unto him, and says, deliver him from going down to the pit, for I have found a ransom. JFB explains deliver literally as redeem, with ransom as a price paid for deliverance, receiving its full realization only in the eternal redemption found in Christ. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

25 COMMENTARY: His flesh shall be fresher than a child's, and he shall return to the days of his youth. JFB calls these the effects of restoration to God's favour: literally a temporal revival for Job, spiritually an eternal regeneration. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

26 COMMENTARY: He shall pray unto God, and he will be favourable unto him, and he shall see his face with joy, for God will render unto man his righteousness. JFB notes Job shall no longer pray in vain, and God shall no longer hide his face, restoring Job to justify God in his dealings. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

27 COMMENTARY: The restored man looks upon men and says, I have sinned and perverted that which was right, and it profited me not. JFB renders looketh as singeth joyfully before men, with perverted as making the straight crooked, as Job had misrepresented God's character, and profited as punishment not commensurate with sin. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

28 COMMENTARY: God will deliver the penitent's soul from going into the pit, and his life shall see the light. JFB renders it as he has delivered my soul and my life, a continuation of the penitent's testimony to the people. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

29 COMMENTARY: Lo, all these things God works oftentimes with man. JFB explains twice and thrice as alluding to the three ways: once by visions, secondly by afflictions, now thirdly by the messenger. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

30 COMMENTARY: To bring back man's soul from the pit, to be enlightened with the light of the living. God's repeated dealings aim at rescue into light. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

31 COMMENTARY: Mark well, O Job, hearken unto Elihu, hold thy peace, and he will speak. The appeal returns to direct address after the teaching. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

32 COMMENTARY: If Job has anything to say, let him answer Elihu, for Elihu desires to justify him. JFB explains justify as doing Job justice, and if he can, consistently with it, declaring him innocent. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

33 COMMENTARY: If not, let Job hearken unto Elihu, hold his peace, and Elihu shall teach him wisdom. JFB notes Elihu pauses here for a reply, then proceeds in the following chapter. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
