Study Notes — Job

Chapter 36

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Elihu also proceeded, and said. JFB notes this is Elihu's fourth speech, exceeding the ternary number of the others. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: Suffer Elihu a little, and he will show Job that he has yet to speak on God's behalf. JFB renders it literally as wait yet but a little for me, a formula of politeness. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: Elihu will fetch his knowledge from afar, and will ascribe righteousness to his Maker. JFB explains from afar as not trite commonplaces but drawn from God's mighty works, and ascribe righteousness as opposed to Job, who ascribed unrighteousness to God: in enquiring into God's ways one should presume they are all just. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: For truly Elihu's words shall not be false: he that is perfect in knowledge is with Job. JFB explains Elihu will not speak wickedly for God, as the friends did by unsound arguments, and renders the perfect one as a man of integrity in sentiments, namely Elihu himself, opposed to the dishonest reasonings of the friends. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: Behold, God is mighty, and despises not any: he is mighty in strength and wisdom. JFB renders it as strength of understanding: as mighty as God is, none is too low to be despised by him, for his might lies especially in strength of understanding, whereby he searches out the most minute things to give each his right. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: God preserves not the life of the wicked, but gives right to the poor. JFB explains right as espousing the cause of the afflicted. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: God withdraws not his eyes from the righteous, but with kings are they on the throne, yea, he establishes them for ever, and they are exalted. JFB notes God does not forsake the godly, as Job implied, but makes them sit on the throne as kings, true of believers in part already and fully hereafter. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: And if the righteous be bound in fetters, and be held in cords of affliction. JFB explains that if they be afflicted, it is no proof they are hypocrites, as the friends maintain, or that God disregards them, as Job asserts: God is thereby disciplining them and showing them their sins. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: Then God shows them their work, and their transgressions that they have exceeded. JFB renders work as transgression, and exceeded as behaved mightily, presumptuously or self-confidently. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: God opens also their ear to discipline, and commands that they return from iniquity. The affliction opens the ear to God's instruction, as in chapter 33. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: If they obey and serve God, they shall spend their days in prosperity, and their years in pleasures. JFB renders serve as worship, with God to be supplied. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: But if they obey not, they shall perish by the sword, and they shall die without knowledge. JFB renders without knowledge as on account of their foolishness. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: But the hypocrites in heart heap up wrath: they cry not when God binds them. JFB explains hypocrites as the ungodly, or a distinct class from the openly wicked, heaping up wrath of God against themselves instead of crying unto him when bound with fetters of affliction. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: They die in youth, and their life is among the unclean. JFB renders it as their life is ended as that of the unclean, prematurely and dishonourably, a warning that Job make not common cause with the wicked. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: God delivers the poor in his affliction, and opens their ears in oppression. JFB renders poor as the afflicted pious, whose ears are opened so as to be admonished in their straits to seek God penitently and so be delivered. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: Even so would God have removed Job out of the strait into a broad place, where there is no straitness, and that which should be set on his table should be full of fatness. JFB renders it as leading forth out of the jaws of a strait, with broad place expressing liberty and the well-supplied table the abundance of the prosperous. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: But Job has fulfilled the judgment of the wicked: judgment and justice take hold on him. JFB renders it as if thou art entirely filled with the judgment of the wicked, whether their guilt incurring judgment or the judgment pronounced on God by the guilty in misfortunes, then God's judgment and justice shall closely follow each other. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

18 COMMENTARY: Because there is wrath, beware lest God take Job away with his stroke: then a great ransom cannot deliver him. JFB explains beware lest the wrath of God, thy severe calamity, lead thee to scorn, and let not the great ransom of money seduce thee, as if thou couldst deliver thyself from wrath by it, noting even the ransom by Christ will be of no avail to wilful despisers. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

19 COMMENTARY: Will God esteem Job's riches? No, not gold, nor all the forces of strength. JFB renders forces of strength as resources of wealth. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

20 COMMENTARY: Desire not the night, when people are cut off in their place. JFB renders desire as pant for, noting Job had wished for death, and cut off literally as ascend, as corn cut and lifted upon the wagon: Job pants for death as desirable, but it is a night or region of darkness, and his fancied ascent will prove a descent. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

21 COMMENTARY: Take heed, regard not iniquity: for this Job has chosen rather than affliction. JFB renders regard as turn thyself to, and iniquity as presumptuous speaking against God, chosen rather than bearing affliction with pious patience: men think it an alleviation to complain against God, but this adds sin to sorrow. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

22 COMMENTARY: Behold, God exalts by his power: who teaches like him? JFB renders exalteth as doeth lofty things, showing his exalted power, and teacheth as God's might shown in his wisdom: he alone can teach, yet Job presumes to teach him, though God as sovereign explains not all his dealings. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

23 COMMENTARY: Who has enjoined God his way, or who can say, thou hast wrought iniquity? JFB notes Job dared to prescribe to God what he should do. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

24 COMMENTARY: Remember that Job magnify God's work, which men behold. JFB explains that instead of arraigning God, it should be Job's fixed principle to magnify God in his works, which all may see, convincing us that what we do not see is altogether wise and good. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

25 COMMENTARY: Every man may see it, and man may behold it afar off. JFB renders it as mortals behold it only from afar off, seeing but a small part. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

26 COMMENTARY: Behold, God is great, and we know him not, neither can the number of his years be searched out. JFB notes God's greatness in heaven and earth is a reason why Job should bow under his afflicting hand, and that we know him only in part. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

27 COMMENTARY: For God makes small the drops of water: they pour down rain according to the vapour thereof. JFB explains God draws up the drops of water from the earth below, and they pour down rain which is his vapour, condensed into drops which fall, not in a deluge but in drops of vapory rain, a fit prelude to the storm in which God appears. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

28 COMMENTARY: Which the clouds do drop and distil upon man abundantly. JFB renders abundantly literally as upon many men. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

29 COMMENTARY: Also can any understand the spreadings of the clouds, or the noise of his tabernacle? JFB explains spreadings as the canopy of thick clouds covering the heavens in a storm, and noise as crashing thunder, with God's tabernacle as his pavilion amid dark clouds. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

30 COMMENTARY: Behold, God spreads his light upon it, and covers the bottom of the sea. JFB explains light as lightning, spread over the vast mass of dark clouds in an instant, and covering the bottom or roots of the sea, so that in the storm the depths of ocean are laid bare and the light covers them. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

31 COMMENTARY: For by them God judges the people, and gives meat in abundance. JFB notes rain and lightnings are marvellous and not to be understood, yet necessary: by them he chastises on the one hand, and on the other gives food. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

32 COMMENTARY: With clouds God covers the light, and commands it not to shine by the cloud that comes between. JFB renders it as he covers both his hands with light, lightning, and gives it a command against his adversary, so that light has twofold effects: destructive lightning against the wicked, and genial light for good to his friends. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

33 COMMENTARY: The noise thereof shows concerning it, the cattle also concerning the vapour. JFB renders noise as he reveals it, announces concerning it, to his friend, and also to cattle and plants: as the genial effect of water in the growth of food is mentioned, so here that of light in cherishing cattle and plants. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
