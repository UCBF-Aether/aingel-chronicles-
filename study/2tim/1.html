Study Notes — 2 Timothy

Chapter 1

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Paul, an apostle of Jesus Christ by the will of God, according to the promise of life which is in Christ Jesus. The promise of eternal life to believers in Christ is the leading subject of ministers employed according to God's will, Henry notes. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

2 COMMENTARY: To Timothy, my dearly beloved son: Grace, mercy, and peace, from God the Father and Christ Jesus our Lord. The blessings named, Henry says, are the best we can ask for beloved friends: peace with God the Father and Christ Jesus our Lord. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

3 COMMENTARY: I thank God, whom I serve from my forefathers with pure conscience, that without ceasing I have remembrance of you in my prayers night and day. Whatever good we do, God must have the glory, Henry observes, and true believers in every age have the same religion in substance. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

4 COMMENTARY: Greatly desiring to see you, being mindful of your tears, that I may be filled with joy. The apostle's affection is personal: Timothy's tears at parting are remembered, and a meeting would fill Paul with joy even in prison. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

5 COMMENTARY: When I call to remembrance the unfeigned faith that is in you, which dwelt first in your grandmother Lois and your mother Eunice, and I am persuaded that in you also. Their faith is unfeigned and will stand trial as a living principle, Henry says, and pious women may take encouragement from Lois and Eunice, whose teaching made Timothy so excellent a minister. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

6 COMMENTARY: Wherefore I put you in remembrance that you stir up the gift of God, which is in you by the putting on of my hands. God has not given us the spirit of fear, Henry explains, but the spirit of power, courage, and resolution to meet difficulties, the spirit of love, and of a sound, quiet mind. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

7 COMMENTARY: For God has not given us the spirit of fear, but of power, and of love, and of a sound mind. The Holy Spirit is not the author of a timid or cowardly disposition or slavish fears, Henry stresses, but of power to bear afflictions well. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

8 COMMENTARY: Be not therefore ashamed of the testimony of our Lord, nor of me his prisoner, but be a partaker of the afflictions of the gospel according to the power of God. Those who cleave to the gospel need not be ashamed, Henry says, for the cause will bear them out, while its opposers shall be ashamed. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

9 COMMENTARY: Who has saved us and called us with a holy calling, not according to our works, but according to his own purpose and grace, given us in Christ Jesus before the world began. The call of the gospel is a holy call, making holy, Henry notes, and salvation is of free grace, given in God's purpose from all eternity in Christ, through whom alone all gifts come to sinful man. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

10 COMMENTARY: But is now made manifest by the appearing of our Saviour Jesus Christ, who has abolished death and brought life and immortality to light through the gospel. As there is so clear a prospect of eternal happiness by faith in him who is the Resurrection and the Life, Henry urges, let us give more diligence in making his salvation sure to our souls. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

11 COMMENTARY: Whereunto I am appointed a preacher, and an apostle, and a teacher of the Gentiles. Paul's threefold office names the work for which he now suffers, and the appointment itself is his authority. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

12 COMMENTARY: For which cause I also suffer these things; nevertheless I am not ashamed, for I know whom I have believed, and am persuaded that he is able to keep that which I have committed to him against that day. The apostle trusted his life, soul, and eternal interests to the Lord Jesus, Henry says, for no one else could deliver and secure his soul through life and death, and the hope of the lowest real Christian rests on the same foundation. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

13 COMMENTARY: Hold fast the form of sound words, which you have heard of me, in faith and love which is in Christ Jesus. It is not enough to assent to sound words, Henry warns, but we must love them, holding fast the substance of solid gospel truth. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

14 COMMENTARY: That good thing which was committed to you keep by the Holy Ghost which dwells in us. The Christian doctrine is a trust of unspeakable value, Henry says, committed to be preserved pure and entire, not by our own strength but by the Holy Spirit dwelling in us. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

15 COMMENTARY: This you know, that all they which are in Asia are turned away from me, of whom are Phygellus and Hermogenes. Desertion by whole provinces is named plainly, with two names, as the dark background against which one faithful man's constancy will shine. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

16 COMMENTARY: The Lord give mercy to the house of Onesiphorus, for he often refreshed me and was not ashamed of my chain. Onesiphorus refreshed Paul with letters, counsels, and comforts, Henry notes, and was not ashamed of his chain, while others fled from it. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

17 COMMENTARY: But when he was in Rome, he sought me out very diligently and found me. A good man will seek to do good, Henry observes, and Onesiphorus sought the prisoner until he found him, not waiting to be found. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

18 COMMENTARY: The Lord grant to him that he may find mercy of the Lord in that day, and in how many things he ministered to me at Ephesus, you know very well. The day of death and judgment is awful, Henry says, and if we would have mercy then, we must seek it now; the best we can ask for ourselves or friends is that we may find mercy of the Lord in that day. — Matthew Henry (Concise)
