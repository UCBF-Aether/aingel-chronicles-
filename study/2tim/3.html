Study Notes — 2 Timothy

Chapter 3

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: This know also, that in the last days perilous times shall come. Even in gospel times there would be perilous times, Henry says, from persecution without, but still more from corruptions within. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

2 COMMENTARY: For men shall be lovers of their own selves, covetous, boasters, proud, blasphemers, disobedient to parents, unthankful, unholy. Men love to gratify their own lusts more than to please God, Henry observes, and when every man is eager for what he can get and anxious to keep what he has, this makes men dangerous to one another; when men do not fear God, they will not regard man. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

3 COMMENTARY: Without natural affection, trucebreakers, false accusers, incontinent, fierce, despisers of those that are good. Times are perilous, Henry notes, when parents lack natural affection, when men have no rule over their own spirits, and when they despise what is good and to be honoured. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

4 COMMENTARY: Traitors, heady, highminded, lovers of pleasures more than lovers of God. God is to be loved above all, Henry says, but a carnal mind, full of enmity against him, prefers anything before him, especially carnal pleasure. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

5 COMMENTARY: Having a form of godliness, but denying the power thereof: from such turn away. A form of godliness is very different from the power, Henry stresses, and from such as are found to be hypocrites real Christians must withdraw. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

6 COMMENTARY: For of this sort are they which creep into houses, and lead captive silly women laden with sins, led away with divers lusts. Such artful men have been found within the outward church in every place and at all times, Henry observes, creeping by pretences and flatteries into the favour of those too easy of belief, ignorant, and fanciful. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

7 COMMENTARY: Ever learning, and never able to come to the knowledge of the truth. All must be ever learning to know the Lord, Henry says, but these follow every new notion, yet never seek the truth as it is in Jesus. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

8 COMMENTARY: Now as Jannes and Jambres withstood Moses, so do these also resist the truth: men of corrupt minds, reprobate concerning the faith. Like the Egyptian magicians, Henry notes, these were men of corrupt minds, prejudiced against the truth, and found quite without faith. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

9 COMMENTARY: But they shall proceed no further, for their folly shall be manifest to all men, as theirs also was. Though the spirit of error may be let loose for a time, Henry says, Satan can deceive the nations and the churches no further, and no longer, than God will permit. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

10 COMMENTARY: But you have fully known my doctrine, manner of life, purpose, faith, longsuffering, charity, patience. The more fully we know the doctrine of Christ as taught by the apostles, Henry says, the more closely we shall cleave to it. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

11 COMMENTARY: Persecutions, afflictions, which came to me at Antioch, at Iconium, at Lystra; what persecutions I endured: but out of them all the Lord delivered me. When we know the afflictions of believers only in part, they tempt us to decline the cause for which they suffer, Henry observes; known fully, they show the Lord's deliverance. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

12 COMMENTARY: Yea, and all that will live godly in Christ Jesus shall suffer persecution. A mere form of godliness is often allowed to pass, Henry notes, while open profession of the truth as it is in Jesus, and resolute attention to the duties of godliness, stir up the scorn and enmity of the world. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

13 COMMENTARY: But evil men and seducers shall wax worse and worse, deceiving, and being deceived. As good men by grace grow better, so bad men through Satan's craft and their own corruptions grow worse, Henry says, for the way of sin is downhill, and those who deceive others deceive themselves, as they will find at last to their cost. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

14 COMMENTARY: But continue in the things which you have learned and have been assured of, knowing of whom you have learned them. Those who would learn the things of God and be assured of them must know the Holy Scriptures, Henry says, for they are the divine revelation, and the age of children is the age to learn. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

15 COMMENTARY: And that from a child you have known the holy scriptures, which are able to make you wise to salvation through faith which is in Christ Jesus. The Bible is a sure guide to eternal life, Henry observes, and those who would get true learning must get it out of the Scriptures, not letting them lie neglected, seldom or never looked into. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

16 COMMENTARY: All scripture is given by inspiration of God, and is profitable for doctrine, for reproof, for correction, for instruction in righteousness. The prophets and apostles did not speak from themselves, Henry notes, but delivered what they received, and Scripture is of use to all, for all need to be taught, corrected, and reproved, with something suitable for every case. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

17 COMMENTARY: That the man of God may be perfect, thoroughly furnished to all good works. We best oppose error by promoting a solid knowledge of the word of truth, Henry concludes, and the greatest kindness we can do to children is to make them early to know the Bible, so that at last they gain the happiness therein promised by faith in Christ, the main subject of both Testaments. — Matthew Henry (Concise)
