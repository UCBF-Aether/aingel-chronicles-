Study Notes — 2 Timothy

Chapter 4

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: I charge you therefore before God, and the Lord Jesus Christ, who shall judge the quick and the dead at his appearing and his kingdom. The charge is laid before the Judge himself, whose appearing and kingdom give it all its weight. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

2 COMMENTARY: Preach the word; be instant in season, out of season; reprove, rebuke, exhort with all long suffering and doctrine. Those who love souls must be ever watchful, Henry says, venture and bear all the painful effects of faithfulness, and take every opportunity of making known the pure gospel. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

3 COMMENTARY: For the time will come when they will not endure sound doctrine, but after their own lusts shall they heap to themselves teachers, having itching ears. People turn away from the truth when they will not endure preaching that is searching, plain, and to the purpose, Henry notes, growing weary of the plain gospel and greedy of fables. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

4 COMMENTARY: And they shall turn away their ears from the truth, and shall be turned to fables. The itching ear turns from truth to fable, preferring what pleases to what searches. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

5 COMMENTARY: But watch in all things, endure afflictions, do the work of an evangelist, make full proof of your ministry. Against the turning away, Henry urges, the minister must watch in all things and endure afflictions, proving his ministry by doing its work fully. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

6 COMMENTARY: For I am now ready to be offered, and the time of my departure is at hand. The blood of the martyrs, though not a sacrifice of atonement, Henry says, was a sacrifice of acknowledgment to the grace of God and his truth, and death to a good man is his release from this world's imprisonment and his departure to another world's enjoyments. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

7 COMMENTARY: I have fought a good fight, I have finished my course, I have kept the faith. As a Christian and a minister, Henry notes, Paul had kept the faith, kept the doctrines of the gospel, and what comfort it will afford to speak so toward the end of our days. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

8 COMMENTARY: Henceforth there is laid up for me a crown of righteousness, which the Lord, the righteous judge, shall give me at that day, and not to me only, but to all them also that love his appearing. The crown of believers is purchased by the righteousness of Christ, Henry says; they have it not at present, yet it is sure, for it is laid up for them, and amid poverty, pain, and the agonies of death the believer may rejoice. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

9 COMMENTARY: Do your diligence to come shortly to me. The request is urgent, for the apostle's time is short and his need of Timothy's presence great. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

10 COMMENTARY: For Demas has forsaken me, having loved this present world, and is departed to Thessalonica; Crescens to Galatia, Titus to Dalmatia. The love of this world, Henry warns, is often the cause of turning back from the truths and ways of Jesus Christ, as Demas shows. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

11 COMMENTARY: Only Luke is with me. Take Mark, and bring him with you, for he is profitable to me for the ministry. Of the companions, only the physician remains, and Mark, once unprofitable, is now profitable for the ministry. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

12 COMMENTARY: And Tychicus have I sent to Ephesus. The faithful messenger is sent where the work needs him, even as Timothy is summoned to Rome. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

13 COMMENTARY: The cloak that I left at Troas with Carpus, when you come, bring with you, and the books, but especially the parchments. Paul was guided by divine inspiration, Henry observes, yet he would have his books; as long as we live we must still learn, and the apostles did not neglect human means for their own instruction. Let us thank God for so many writings of wise and pious men, and seek that by reading them our profiting may appear to all. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

14 COMMENTARY: Alexander the coppersmith did me much evil: the Lord reward him according to his works. There is as much danger from false brethren as from open enemies, Henry notes, and it is dangerous having to do with those who would be enemies to such a man as Paul. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

15 COMMENTARY: Of whom be you ware also, for he has greatly withstood our words. The warning is practical: a man who withstood the apostle's words will withstand Timothy's too. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

16 COMMENTARY: At my first answer no man stood with me, but all men forsook me: I pray God that it may not be laid to their charge. The Christians at Rome were forward to meet Paul at first, Henry notes, but when suffering with him seemed likely, all forsook him; God might justly be angry, but Paul prays God to forgive them. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

17 COMMENTARY: Notwithstanding the Lord stood with me, and strengthened me, that by me the preaching might be fully known, and that all the Gentiles might hear: and I was delivered out of the mouth of the lion. The apostle was delivered out of the mouth of the lion, that is, of Nero or one of his judges, Henry says, and if the Lord stands by us he will strengthen us in difficulties, his presence more than supplying every one's absence. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

18 COMMENTARY: And the Lord shall deliver me from every evil work, and will preserve me to his heavenly kingdom, to whom be glory for ever and ever. Amen. The same Lord who stood by him in the trial will preserve him to the heavenly kingdom, and to him belongs the glory. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

19 COMMENTARY: Salute Prisca and Aquila, and the household of Onesiphorus. The greetings reach back to old friends and to the house of the man who was not ashamed of Paul's chain. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

20 COMMENTARY: Erastus abode at Corinth, but Trophimus have I left at Miletum sick. The company is scattered by the work and by sickness, and the letter names them faithfully. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

21 COMMENTARY: Do your diligence to come before winter. Eubulus greets you, and Pudens, and Linus, and Claudia, and all the brethren. The last urgency is seasonal and practical: come before winter closes the roads, with greetings from the Roman brethren. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

22 COMMENTARY: The Lord Jesus Christ be with your spirit. Grace be with you. Amen. We need no more to make us happy, Henry concludes, than to have the Lord Jesus Christ with our spirits, for in him all spiritual blessings are summed up; it is the best prayer for friends, that he may be with their spirits to sanctify and save them, and at last receive them to himself. — Matthew Henry (Concise)
