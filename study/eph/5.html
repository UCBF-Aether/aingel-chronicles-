Study Notes — Ephesians

Chapter 5

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: As dear children forgiven by God, believers are to be followers, imitators, of God, resembling him especially in love and pardoning goodness. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: They are to walk in love as Christ loved them and gave himself as an offering and sacrifice to God, a sweet-smelling savor, in whose sacrifice his love triumphs. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: Fornication, all uncleanness, and covetousness must not even be named among saints, since such sins are wholly unbecoming to their profession. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: Neither filthiness, foolish talking, nor jesting is fitting; instead there should be giving of thanks, cheerfulness expressed as becomes Christians. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: No whoremonger, unclean person, or covetous man, who is an idolater, has any inheritance in the kingdom of Christ and of God, since a covetous man makes a god of his money. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: Let no one deceive them with vain words, for because of these things God's wrath comes on the children of disobedience, and light talk about sin must not be made light of. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: Therefore they must not be partakers with such people, since sharing in others' sins by commendation, counsel, consent, or concealment brings a share in their plagues. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: Once they were darkness, but now they are light in the Lord, so they must walk as children of light, grace having wrought a mighty change in their souls. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: The fruit of the Spirit, shown in all goodness, righteousness, and truth, is the evidence of that change from darkness to light. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: Children of light prove what is acceptable to the Lord, testing their conduct by his will rather than by the world's approval. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: They are to have no fellowship with the unfruitful works of darkness, but rather reprove them, since not reproving them is itself a kind of fellowship with them. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: It is a shame even to speak of the secret deeds of darkness, and a good man will be ashamed to name what wicked men are not ashamed to do. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: All things reproved are made manifest by the light, for whatever makes manifest is light, exposing sin as a breach of God's holy law. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: Hence the call: awake, sleeper, arise from the dead, and Christ shall give you light, as prophets and apostles called the dead in sin to arise. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: They must therefore walk circumspectly, not as fools but as wise, since care and caution are a remedy against sin. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: Redeeming the time, because the days are evil: time is a talent from God, misspent when not used according to his design, and lost time calls for doubled diligence. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: They must not be unwise, but understand what the will of the Lord is, since ignorance of duty and neglect of the soul show the greatest folly. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: They must not be drunk with wine, wherein is excess, but be filled with the Spirit, since drunkenness never goes alone and only ends in making sorrows more felt. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: Spirit-filled believers speak to themselves in psalms, hymns, and spiritual songs, singing and making melody in their hearts to the Lord, with reason to sing for joy. — Matthew Henry

20 COMMENTARY: They give thanks always for all things to God the Father in the name of the Lord Jesus Christ, never lacking matter for thanks even in trials. — Matthew Henry

21 COMMENTARY: They submit themselves to one another in the fear of God, each fulfilling duties to others for God's glory. — Matthew Henry

22 COMMENTARY: Wives are to submit to their own husbands as unto the Lord, a submission including honor and obedience from a principle of love. — Matthew Henry

23 COMMENTARY: The husband is the head of the wife, even as Christ is the head of the church, and he is the savior of the body, the pattern for headship. — Matthew Henry

24 COMMENTARY: As the church is subject to Christ, so wives are to be subject to their husbands in everything, within the Lord. — Matthew Henry

25 COMMENTARY: Husbands are to love their wives even as Christ loved the church and gave himself for it, a love sincere, pure, and constant despite her failures. — Matthew Henry

26 COMMENTARY: Christ gave himself that he might sanctify and cleanse the church with the washing of water by the word, delivering it from guilt, pollution, and dominion of sin. — Matthew Henry

27 COMMENTARY: His aim is to present the church to himself glorious, without spot or wrinkle, holy and without blemish, a perfection reached only in glory, though only the sanctified now will be glorified then. — Matthew Henry

28 COMMENTARY: So husbands ought to love their wives as their own bodies, since he who loves his wife loves himself. — Matthew Henry

29 COMMENTARY: No man ever hated his own flesh, but nourishes and cherishes it, even as the Lord cherishes the church. — Matthew Henry

30 COMMENTARY: For believers are members of Christ's body, of his flesh, and of his bones, joined to him in a real union. — Matthew Henry

31 COMMENTARY: For this cause a man shall leave father and mother and be joined to his wife, and the two shall be one flesh, the words of Adam spoken literally of marriage. — Matthew Henry

32 COMMENTARY: This is a great mystery, but Paul speaks concerning Christ and the church, marriage being a type of that union. — Matthew Henry

33 COMMENTARY: In sum, each husband is to love his wife as himself, and the wife is to reverence her husband, since all duties of marriage are included in unity and love. — Matthew Henry
