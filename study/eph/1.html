Study Notes — Ephesians

Chapter 1

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Paul presents himself as an apostle by God's will alone, writing to believers at Ephesus who are both set apart as saints and faithful in Christ. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: Grace and peace come only from God the Father and the Lord Jesus Christ, and Henry notes there is no peace without grace first received from them. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: God is praised for blessing believers with every spiritual blessing in the heavenly realm, all of it given in Christ rather than earned by them. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: Believers were chosen in Christ before the world began, so that holiness and blamelessness before God, not mere happiness, is the end of their election. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: That choice reaches its goal in adoption: by Jesus Christ believers are made God's children, according to the good pleasure of his will alone. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: The whole design aims at the praise of the glory of God's grace, by which believers are made accepted in the Beloved, not in themselves. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: In Christ believers already possess redemption through his blood, which Henry explains as forgiveness of sins given according to the riches of God's grace. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: That grace has abounded toward believers with all wisdom and prudence, God's wise ordering of the whole plan of redemption. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: God has now made known the mystery of his will, the once-hidden purpose of salvation through Christ, according to his own good pleasure. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: In the fullness of times God purposes to gather all things together in Christ, things in heaven and things on earth, under one head. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: In Christ believers have obtained an inheritance, predestined according to the purpose of him who works all things after the counsel of his own will. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: Jewish believers who first trusted in Christ exist to the praise of his glory, showing that God's promises came first to them. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: Gentile believers too, hearing the word of truth and believing, were sealed with the Holy Spirit of promise, marking them as God's own. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: The Spirit is the earnest, the first installment and pledge, of the inheritance until the final redemption of God's purchased possession, all to the praise of his glory. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: Paul had heard of their faith in the Lord Jesus and their love toward all the saints, and that report moves him to thanksgiving rather than to boasting about them. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: He never stops giving thanks for them and mentioning them in his prayers, since even the best Christians need to be prayed for. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: He prays that the Father of glory would give them the spirit of wisdom and revelation in the knowledge of him, since true knowledge of God is itself a gift. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: With enlightened understanding they would know the hope of their calling and the riches of the glory of God's inheritance in the saints. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: They would also know the exceeding greatness of God's power toward believers, the same mighty power that works in every conversion. — Matthew Henry

20 COMMENTARY: That power was displayed when God raised Christ from the dead and seated him at his right hand in the heavenly places. — Matthew Henry

21 COMMENTARY: Christ sits far above every principality, power, might, dominion, and every name named, in this age and in the age to come. — Matthew Henry

22 COMMENTARY: God has put all things under Christ's feet and given him to be head over all things to the church, so no rival power stands outside his rule. — Matthew Henry

23 COMMENTARY: The church is Christ's body, the fullness of him who fills all in all, supplied with every blessing from its head. — Matthew Henry
