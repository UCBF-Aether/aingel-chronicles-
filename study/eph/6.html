Study Notes — Ephesians

Chapter 6

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Children are to obey their parents in the Lord, for this is right, an obedience including inward reverence as well as outward acts, since parents hold authority from God and nature in subservience to God. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: Honor father and mother is the first commandment with promise, honor implying reverence, obedience, and relief and maintenance when needed. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: The promise is that it may be well with them and they may live long on the earth, outward prosperity ordinarily attending obedient children, though where it does not, it is made up with something better. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: Fathers must not provoke their children to wrath, but bring them up in the nurture and admonition of the Lord, governing with tenderness, convincing their judgments, and giving them a religious education rather than merely providing for them. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: Servants are to obey their earthly masters with fear and trembling, in singleness of heart, as unto Christ, reverencing those over them and serving faithfully, since civil service is not inconsistent with Christian liberty. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: That service must not be eye-service, as men-pleasers, but as servants of Christ doing God's will from the heart, conscientious when the master's eye is absent because their Master in heaven beholds them. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: It is to be done with good will, as to the Lord and not to men, since a steady regard to Christ makes service easy, pleasing, and acceptable to him. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: Faithful servants may trust God for their wages, knowing whatever good any man does he shall receive from the Lord, whether bond or free, since God rewards even the meanest drudgery done from duty. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: Masters are to do the same things to their servants, forbearing threatening, knowing their own Master is in heaven and there is no respect of persons with him, so masters and servants are fellow servants under Christ's impartial judgment. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: Finally, believers are to be strong in the Lord and in the power of his might, since spiritual strength and courage are necessary for warfare and all sufficiency is of God. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: They are to put on the whole armor of God, that they may stand against the wiles of the devil, aiming at all grace, since God both prepares and bestows this armor but believers must put it on by prayer and exercise. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: The wrestling is not against flesh and blood but against principalities, powers, rulers of this world's darkness, and spiritual wickedness in high places, a subtle, powerful, unseen enemy who assaults souls before they are aware. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: Therefore they are to take the whole armor of God, to withstand in the evil day, and having done all, to stand, resolving by God's grace not to yield, since resisting the devil makes him flee. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: Standing, they are to have loins girt with truth and the breastplate of righteousness, sincerity girding on all other graces, and Christ's righteousness, imputed and implanted, sheltering the heart. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: Their feet are to be shod with the preparation of the gospel of peace, a prepared and resolved frame of heart to adhere to the gospel, bringing peace with God, with oneself, and with others. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: Above all they take the shield of faith, with which they can quench all the fiery darts of the wicked, faith being all in all in temptation, receiving Christ and deriving grace from him as a universal defense. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: They take the helmet of salvation and the sword of the Spirit, which is the word of God, good hope of salvation guarding the head against despair, and Scripture, the Spirit's own sword, repelling temptation as Christ himself answered with what is written. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: Prayer buckles on all the armor: praying always with all prayer and supplication in the Spirit, watching with all perseverance and supplication for all saints, keeping constant times of prayer and a constant disposition to pray. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: Paul asks their prayers for himself, that utterance may be given him to open his mouth boldly and make known the mystery of the gospel. — Matthew Henry

20 COMMENTARY: He is an ambassador in bonds for that gospel, that he may speak boldly as he ought, his chains not silencing his embassy. — Matthew Henry

21 COMMENTARY: Tychicus, a beloved brother and faithful minister in the Lord, will make known to them all Paul's affairs, so they may know how he fares. — Matthew Henry

22 COMMENTARY: Paul sent Tychicus for this purpose, that they might know his affairs and that he might comfort their hearts. — Matthew Henry

23 COMMENTARY: Peace be to the brethren, and love with faith, from God the Father and the Lord Jesus Christ, the apostle's closing blessing on the church. — Matthew Henry

24 COMMENTARY: Grace be with all who love our Lord Jesus Christ in sincerity, Henry's final word resting on sincere, not pretended, love of Christ. — Matthew Henry
