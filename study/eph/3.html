Study Notes — Ephesians

Chapter 3

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Paul calls himself the prisoner of Jesus Christ for the Gentiles, suffering for preaching the truth yet under his Lord's special protection and care. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: He assumes they have heard of the stewardship of God's grace entrusted to him for their sake, since the gospel offers come from God's rich grace. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: By revelation God made the mystery known to Paul, as he had already written briefly, showing that this knowledge came by divine disclosure, not human discovery. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: Reading Paul's words, they can perceive his understanding in the mystery of Christ, the secret purpose of salvation now openly declared. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: In earlier ages this mystery was not made known to the sons of men as it is now revealed to the holy apostles and prophets by the Spirit. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: The heart of the mystery is that Gentiles are fellow heirs, members of the same body, and partakers of God's promise in Christ through the gospel. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: Paul was made a minister of this gospel according to the gift of God's grace, given to him by the effectual working of divine power. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: To Paul, who counts himself less than the least of all saints, was given the grace to preach among the Gentiles the unsearchable riches of Christ. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: His task is to make all see the fellowship of the mystery, hidden from the beginning in God who created all things by Jesus Christ. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: Through the church God's manifold wisdom is now made known to principalities and powers in heavenly places, while angels adore what self-wise men count foolishness. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: All this follows the eternal purpose God purposed in Christ Jesus our Lord, the same purpose behind both the first creation and the new creation of sinners. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: In Christ believers have boldness and access with confidence through faith in him, approaching God not timidly but as children. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: Paul therefore asks them not to faint at his tribulations for them, since his sufferings are their glory rather than a cause for discouragement. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: For this cause he bows his knees to the Father of our Lord Jesus Christ, turning from argument to prayer for spiritual blessings, the best blessings. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: From this Father the whole family in heaven and earth is named, one household gathered under one name. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: He prays they would be strengthened with might by God's Spirit in the inner man, according to the riches of his glory, since strength of soul is what the Christian life requires. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: That inner strength serves this end: that Christ may dwell in their hearts by faith, they being rooted and grounded in love. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: Rooted in love, they may be able with all saints to comprehend the breadth, length, depth, and height of Christ's love. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: They are to know that love which passes knowledge, and so be filled with all the fullness of God, a fullness that should satisfy where trifles cannot. — Matthew Henry

20 COMMENTARY: God is able to do exceeding abundantly above all we ask or think, according to the power that works in us, so prayer should expect more and ask for more. — Matthew Henry

21 COMMENTARY: To him be glory in the church by Christ Jesus throughout all ages, world without end, since the conversion of sinners and the comfort of believers are to his glory forever. — Matthew Henry
