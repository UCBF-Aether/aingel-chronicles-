Study Notes — Ephesians

Chapter 4

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Paul, the prisoner of the Lord, urges them to walk worthy of their calling, since nothing is pressed more earnestly in Scripture than a life becoming their profession. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: That worthy walk is marked by lowliness, meekness, longsuffering, and forbearing one another in love, humility being the opposite of pride. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: Believers are to endeavor to keep the unity of the Spirit in the bond of peace, one heart because they share one hope. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: There is one body and one Spirit, even as they are called in one hope of their calling, so division contradicts the very nature of their calling. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: One Lord, one faith, one baptism bind them together, since they believe the same great truths and were admitted by the same sign. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: One God and Father of all, above all, through all, and in them all, dwells in all believers as in his holy temple by his Spirit. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: To every believer grace is given according to the measure of Christ's gift, each receiving what Christ sees best to bestow for mutual help. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: When Christ ascended on high he led captivity captive and gave gifts to men, receiving that he might give, particularly the gift of the Holy Spirit. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: That he ascended implies he first descended into the lower parts of the earth, the same Lord humbling himself before he was exalted. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: He who descended is the same who ascended far above all heavens, that he might fill all things, so his exaltation is universal, not partial. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: He gave some to be apostles, some prophets, some evangelists, and some pastors and teachers, distributing gifts for the church's good. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: These gifts are for perfecting the saints, for the work of ministry, and for building up the body of Christ. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: The goal is that all come to unity of faith and knowledge of the Son of God, to a perfect man, to the measure of the stature of Christ's fullness, a perfection reached only in heaven. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: Then they will no longer be children tossed to and fro and carried about by every wind of doctrine, through the trickery of men lying in wait to deceive. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: Instead, speaking truth in love, they grow up into him in all things, who is the head, even Christ. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: From Christ the whole body, fitly joined and compacted by every joint, grows as each part works, and that growth tends to Christ's glory. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: Paul testifies in the Lord that they must no longer walk as other Gentiles walk, in the vanity of their minds, since the gospel draws a real line between nominal and true Christians. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: The unconverted Gentiles have darkened understanding and are alienated from God's life through ignorance and blindness of heart, sitting in darkness and loving it. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: Being past feeling, they have given themselves over to lasciviousness, working all uncleanness with greediness, sinful desires promising happiness but bringing misery. — Matthew Henry

20 COMMENTARY: Believers have not so learned Christ, whose truth appears in beauty and power when seen as it is in Jesus. — Matthew Henry

21 COMMENTARY: If they have truly heard him and been taught by him, as truth is in Jesus, their lives must show the difference. — Matthew Henry

22 COMMENTARY: They are to put off the old man, the corrupt former way of life, like a filthy garment, since deceitful lusts must be subdued and mortified. — Matthew Henry

23 COMMENTARY: They are to be renewed in the spirit of their minds, since shaking off corrupt principles is not enough without gracious ones in their place. — Matthew Henry

24 COMMENTARY: They are to put on the new man, created after God in righteousness and true holiness, a new nature directed by regenerating grace. — Matthew Henry

25 COMMENTARY: Putting away lying, each must speak truth with his neighbor, since God's people are children who dare not lie, and believers are members of one another. — Matthew Henry

26 COMMENTARY: They may be angry yet not sin, and must not let the sun go down on their wrath, since ungoverned passion gives place to the devil. — Matthew Henry

27 COMMENTARY: Neither give place to the devil: sin yielded to lets the enemy in, so the first motions of sin must be resisted, not consented to. — Matthew Henry

28 COMMENTARY: The thief must steal no more but labor honestly with his hands, not only to live honestly but to have something to give to those in need, since alms gained by robbery are hateful to God. — Matthew Henry

29 COMMENTARY: No corrupt communication should proceed from their mouths, but only what is good for edifying, ministering grace to hearers, since filthy words corrupt both speaker and hearer. — Matthew Henry

30 COMMENTARY: They must not grieve the Holy Spirit of God, by whom they are sealed unto the day of redemption, since the Spirit dwelling in them is the earnest of that day. — Matthew Henry

31 COMMENTARY: All bitterness, wrath, anger, clamor, evil speaking, and malice must be put away, since such corrupt passions grieve the Holy Spirit. — Matthew Henry

32 COMMENTARY: Instead they are to be kind, tenderhearted, and forgiving, even as God for Christ's sake has forgiven them, God's forgiveness being the pattern and motive for theirs. — Matthew Henry
