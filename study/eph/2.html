Study Notes — Ephesians

Chapter 2

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: God has quickened believers who were dead in trespasses and sins, since sin is the death of the soul and a dead soul has no desire for spiritual pleasures. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: In time past they walked according to the course of this world and the prince of the power of the air, a state of conformity to the world under Satan's rule in the hearts of men. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: All, whether prone to sensual or spiritual wickedness, once lived in the lusts of the flesh and were by nature children of disobedience and therefore children of wrath. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: But God, who is rich in mercy, acted from his great love toward them, God's eternal love being the fountain from which all his mercies flow. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: Even when they were dead in sins, God quickened them together with Christ, and Henry stresses the parenthesis: by grace ye are saved. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: God has raised them up together and made them sit together in heavenly places in Christ Jesus, sanctified souls raised above this world by his grace. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: He did this so that in ages to come he might show the exceeding riches of his grace in kindness toward them through Christ Jesus, encouraging later sinners to hope in the same grace. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: Salvation is by grace through faith, and that not of themselves: it is the gift of God, faith itself and conversion both given, not produced by man. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: It is not of works, lest any man should boast, since nothing done by us brings salvation about and all boasting is shut out. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: Believers are God's workmanship, created in Christ Jesus unto good works which God ordained beforehand, that they should glorify God by good conversation and perseverance in holiness. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: They are to remember that they were once Gentiles in the flesh, called Uncircumcision by those called the Circumcision, outsiders to Israel's privileges. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: At that time they were without Christ, aliens from the commonwealth of Israel, strangers from the covenants of promise, having no hope and without God, a sad description from which no one can remove himself. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: But now in Christ Jesus those who were far off are made near by the blood of Christ, since salvation comes by his sufferings and death, and God is a help at hand to his people. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: Christ himself is our peace, who has made both Jew and Gentile one and broken down the middle wall of partition between them, being the author, center, and substance of their peace with God and their union in one church. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: In his flesh he abolished the enmity, the law of commandments in ordinances, to make in himself of two one new man, so making peace. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: He did this to reconcile both to God in one body by the cross, having slain the enmity thereby, so that the cross ends the hostility it answers. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: He came and preached peace to those afar off and to those near, since through his person, sacrifice, and mediation sinners may draw near to God as Father. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: For through him both Jew and Gentile have access by one Spirit unto the Father, Christ purchasing leave to come and the Spirit giving a heart and strength to come. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: Therefore Gentile believers are no more strangers and foreigners, but fellow citizens with the saints and members of the household of God, the church being like a city and a house. — Matthew Henry

20 COMMENTARY: They are built upon the foundation of the apostles and prophets, Jesus Christ himself being the chief cornerstone, the church founded on the doctrine of Christ delivered by prophets and apostles. — Matthew Henry

21 COMMENTARY: In Christ the whole building, fitly framed together, grows into a holy temple in the Lord, each converted sinner becoming part of God's house. — Matthew Henry

22 COMMENTARY: In him they also are built together for a habitation of God through the Spirit, so believers should ask whether they are devoted as holy temples and habitations of God by the Spirit. — Matthew Henry
