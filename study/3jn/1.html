Study Notes — 3 John

Chapter 1

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: The elder writes to the wellbeloved Gaius, whom he loves in the truth. Henry notes John lays aside grander titles for the common pastoral title, and JFB stresses the personal emphasis: I, for my part, love him in the truth. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: John wishes Gaius prosperity and health even as his soul prospers. Henry calls soul-prosperity the greatest blessing on this side of heaven, and JFB notes bodily health is wished next after, not above, spiritual health. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: John rejoiced greatly when brethren came and testified of the truth in Gaius, even as he walks in the truth. Henry reads the testimony as the reality of his faith shown by loving deeds, and JFB notes the contrast with Diotrephes to come. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: John has no greater joy than to hear his children walk in truth. Henry calls walking in truth the best evidence of having the truth, and JFB notes children here means members of the church. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: Gaius does faithfully whatever he does to the brethren and to strangers. Henry praises a catholic spirit that welcomes all who bear Christ's image, and JFB notes the strangers were brethren unknown to him, which enhances the love shown. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: Those brethren bore witness of Gaius's charity before the church, and John urges him to bring them forward on their journey after a godly sort. Henry calls this doing common kindness in a manner worthy of God, and JFB explains it as giving provisions for the way. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: The brethren went forth for Christ's name's sake, taking nothing of the Gentiles. Henry stresses they carried a free gospel, and JFB notes they refused maintenance by choice, as Paul did at Corinth, so as not to burden infant Gentile churches. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: Fellow believers therefore ought to receive such workers, that they may be fellowhelpers to the truth. Henry argues those who cannot preach may still advance the gospel by receiving and helping those who do. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: John wrote to the church, but Diotrephes, who loves pre-eminence, receives them not. Henry names pride and ambition as the root, and JFB notes Diotrephes rejected the brethren John recommended, which was virtually rejecting John himself. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: If John comes, he will remember Diotrephes's deeds: prating with malicious words, refusing the brethren, forbidding others to receive them, and casting them out of the church. Henry warns it is worse to hinder good than merely to do none, and JFB notes the casting out was excommunication by a leading man's influence. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: Gaius is told to follow not evil but good, for he that does good is of God and he that does evil has not seen God. Henry reads doing good as evidence of birth from God, and JFB points the contrast: evil as in Diotrephes, good as in Demetrius. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: Demetrius has a good report of all men and of the truth itself, confirmed by John's own record. Henry calls a name in the gospel better than that of sons and daughters, and JFB notes the truth itself bears witness that his deeds match the gospel standard. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: John had many things to write but will not write with ink and pen. Henry observes many things are more proper for immediate communication than for letter, and JFB renders the sense as a wish not to write more. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: John trusts to see Gaius shortly and speak face to face, wishing him peace, sending friends' greetings, and asking him to greet the friends by name. Henry notes good Christians wish others the happiness they know themselves, and JFB explains peace as inward, fraternal, and supernal. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
