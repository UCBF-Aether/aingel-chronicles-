Study Notes — Psalms

Psalm 62

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Truly my soul waits upon God; from him comes my salvation: waiting truly — silently, solely — on God, whose salvation is the soul's supply. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: He only is my rock and my salvation; he is my defence; I shall not be greatly moved: only rock, only salvation, defence itself — and therefore not greatly moved, though shaken. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: How long will you imagine mischief against a man? You shall be slain, all of you, as a bowing wall and a tottering fence: the plotters, who imagine mischief against one man, are themselves like a bowing wall and tottering fence — ready to fall. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: They only consult to cast him down from his excellency; they delight in lies; they bless with their mouth but curse inwardly: consultation to cast down, delight in lies, blessing mouth with cursing heart — the conspiracy is defined. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: My soul, wait only upon God, for my expectation is from him: the soul is bidden again, now only upon God, for expectation is from him alone. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: He only is my rock and my salvation; he is my defence; I shall not be moved: the refrain returns stronger — now not greatly moved, but not moved at all. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: In God is my salvation and my glory; the rock of my strength, and my refuge, is in God: salvation and glory both in God, with rock of strength and refuge in him — glory is not in the excellency men would cast down. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: Trust in him at all times, you people; pour out your heart before him; God is a refuge for us: trust at all times, poured-out hearts, God as refuge for us — the personal psalm becomes the people's counsel. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: Surely men of low degree are vanity, and men of high degree are a lie; laid in the balance, they are altogether lighter than vanity: low men vanity, high men a lie, together lighter than vanity itself — human help weighs nothing. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: Trust not in oppression, and become not vain in robbery; if riches increase, set not your heart upon them: oppression, robbery, and increased riches are three false trusts, each forbidden to the heart. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: God has spoken once; twice have I heard this, that power belongs unto God: once spoken, twice heard — power belongs to God, heard and re-heard. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: Also unto you, O Lord, belongs mercy, for you render to every man according to his work: mercy also belongs to God, who renders to every man according to his work — power and mercy together govern the rendering. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
