Study Notes — Psalms

Psalm 44

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: We have heard with our ears, O God, our fathers have told us, what work you did in their days, in the times of old: heard history, told by fathers, is the psalm's foundation. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: How you drove out the heathen with your hand, and planted them; how you afflicted the people and cast them out: God's hand drove out, planted, afflicted, and cast out — the conquest was his act. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: For they got not the land by their own sword, neither did their own arm save them, but your right hand, and your arm, and the light of your countenance, because you had favour to them: sword and arm are denied, and right hand, arm, light of countenance, and favour are credited. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: You are my King, O God; command deliverances for Jacob: the King of the past is asked to command deliverances for Jacob now. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: Through you we will push down our enemies; through your name we will tread under those who rise against us: pushing and treading are done through God and his name, not through weapons. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: For I will not trust in my bow, neither shall my sword save me: bow and sword are explicitly distrusted as saviours. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: But you have saved us from our enemies, and put to shame those who hated us: past salvation and shaming of haters are the remembered pattern. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: In God we boast all the day long, and praise your name for ever: boasting in God all day, and praise for ever, are the response to remembered salvation. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: But you have cast off, and put us to shame, and go not forth with our armies: the turn is stark — casting off, shame, and God's absence from the armies replace the boasting. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: You make us turn back from the enemy, and those who hate us spoil for themselves: retreat and spoiling by haters follow God's casting off. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: You have given us like sheep appointed for meat, and scattered us among the heathen: sheep for slaughter and scattering among nations picture defeat as sacrifice and exile. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: You sell your people for nought, and do not increase your wealth by their price: selling for nothing — no profit even to the seller — stresses the worthlessness to which they have fallen in men's eyes. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: You make us a reproach to neighbours, a scorn and derision to those round about us: neighbours' reproach and the surrounding scorn complete the public shame. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: You make us a byword among the heathen, a shaking of the head among the people: a byword and a shaken head — they have become a proverb of ruin. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: My confusion is continually before me, and the shame of my face has covered me: confusion continually before him, shame covering his face — the shame is personal now. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: For the voice of him who reproaches and blasphemes, by reason of the enemy and avenger: reproach and blasphemy, from enemy and avenger, are the voices behind the shame. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: All this is come upon us, yet we have not forgotten you, neither dealt falsely in your covenant: despite all this, no forgetting and no false dealing with the covenant — innocence is claimed amid suffering. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

18 COMMENTARY: Our heart is not turned back, neither have our steps declined from your way: heart unturned and steps undeclined — inward and outward fidelity both stand. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

19 COMMENTARY: Though you have sore broken us in the place of dragons, and covered us with the shadow of death: broken in a dragons' place, covered with death's shadow — yet fidelity held. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

20 COMMENTARY: If we have forgotten the name of our God, or stretched out our hands to a strange god: the hypothetical is stated — forgetting God's name or reaching to a strange god would be the apostasy charged. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

21 COMMENTARY: Shall not God search this out? For he knows the secrets of the heart: God's searching and his knowledge of heart-secrets would expose any such apostasy — and none is found. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

22 COMMENTARY: Yea, for your sake we are killed all the day long; we are counted as sheep for the slaughter: suffering for God's sake, all day, as slaughter-sheep — Paul cites this of the church's sufferings. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

23 COMMENTARY: Awake, why do you sleep, O Lord? Arise, cast us not off for ever: God is called to awake from seeming sleep, to arise, and not to cast off for ever. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

24 COMMENTARY: Wherefore do you hide your face, and forget our affliction and oppression? The hiding of God's face and forgetting of affliction are the questions of a people who have kept covenant and still suffer. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

25 COMMENTARY: For our soul is bowed down to the dust; our belly cleaves to the earth: soul to dust, belly to earth — prostration is total. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

26 COMMENTARY: Arise for our help, and redeem us for your mercies' sake: the final plea is for arising, help, and redemption — grounded, as always, in mercies' sake, not in merit. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
