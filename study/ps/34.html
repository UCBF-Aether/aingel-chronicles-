Study Notes — Psalms

Psalm 34

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: I will bless the Lord at all times; his praise shall continually be in my mouth: praise at all times, not only in deliverance, and continually, not occasionally. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: My soul shall make her boast in the Lord; the humble shall hear and be glad: boasting in God, unlike boasting in self, gladdens the humble who hear it. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: O magnify the Lord with me, and let us exalt his name together: praise is invited to become shared — magnification is a company act. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: I sought the Lord, and he heard me, and delivered me from all my fears: seeking, hearing, and deliverance from all fears — not some — is the testimony. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: They looked to him and were lightened, and their faces were not ashamed: looking to God lightens the face, and shame is removed by the look. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: This poor man cried, and the Lord heard him, and saved him out of all his troubles: the poor man's cry, not his worth, moved God to save from all troubles. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: The angel of the Lord encamps round about those who fear him, and delivers them: a single angel encamping like an army pictures God's surrounding guard. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: O taste and see that the Lord is good; blessed is the man who trusts in him: goodness is to be tasted by experience, not merely heard of, and trust is the tasting. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: O fear the Lord, you his saints, for there is no want to those who fear him: fear of God and freedom from want are joined — reverence is provision. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: The young lions lack and suffer hunger, but those who seek the Lord shall not want any good thing: even the strongest hunters hunger, while seekers lack no good thing. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: Come, you children, hearken to me; I will teach you the fear of the Lord: the psalm turns teacher, and the lesson is the fear of the Lord. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: What man desires life, and loves many days, that he may see good? The question invites everyone who wants a good, long life to listen. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: Keep your tongue from evil, and your lips from speaking guile: the first lesson is the tongue — evil speech and guile must be kept, not merely regretted. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: Depart from evil, and do good; seek peace, and pursue it: the second lesson joins turning from evil to doing good, and seeking peace to pursuing it actively. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: The eyes of the Lord are upon the righteous, and his ears open to their cry: God's watch and hearing are the encouragement for the life just described. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: The face of the Lord is against evildoers, to cut off their remembrance from the earth: the same face that favours the righteous opposes the wicked, even to erasing their memory. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: The righteous cry, and the Lord hears, and delivers them out of all their troubles: the cry-hearing-deliverance pattern of verse 4 is repeated as a rule. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

18 COMMENTARY: The Lord is near to the brokenhearted, and saves those of a contrite spirit: nearness is promised precisely to the broken and contrite, not to the proud. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

19 COMMENTARY: Many are the afflictions of the righteous, but the Lord delivers him out of them all: afflictions are many — righteousness does not exempt — but deliverance is out of them all. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

20 COMMENTARY: He keeps all his bones; not one of them is broken: preservation extends to the bones, and John applies this to Christ, whose bones were not broken on the cross. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

21 COMMENTARY: Evil shall slay the wicked, and those who hate the righteous shall be desolate: evil itself becomes the wicked's executioner, and hatred of the righteous ends in desolation. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

22 COMMENTARY: The Lord redeems the soul of his servants, and none who trust in him shall be desolate: redemption of the soul is the final answer to desolation — the trusting are never desolate. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
