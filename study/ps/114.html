Psalm 114

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Henry insists time must not wear out the sense of mercy: Israel marched out with honour, not stolen away or driven out, from a barbarous people of strange language, having kept their own tongue pure as an earnest of deliverance. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

2 COMMENTARY: Henry reads Judah and Israel as God's sanctuary and dominion: he framed their civil and sacred constitution, set up his sanctuary with special tokens of his presence, and ruled as their King — and what is his sanctuary must submit to his laws, as Christ redeems us to bring us into God's service. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

3 COMMENTARY: Henry says the sea fled because it saw Judah as God's sanctuary and Israel his dominion: God was at the head of that people, so the waters must give way contrary to their nature when he speaks the word, for their rescue and their enemies' ruin. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

4 COMMENTARY: Henry treats Sinai's shaking as proof that the same power which fixed fluid waters also shakes stable mountains: before God the bulkiest hills are as manageable as rams and lambs to a shepherd, and their trembling should shame men's stupidity before his glory. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

5 COMMENTARY: Henry makes the psalmist put the question poetically to sea and Jordan, then answer it: the miracle was real, not by nature's power, merciful in kind — for Israel's salvation — and surprising, that nature's laws should be dispensed with to serve God's people. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

6 COMMENTARY: Henry asks the mountains the same question and answers it alike: they skipped at the presence of the Lord, and he who made hills skip can dissipate the strength of the proudest enemies and make them tremble. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

7 COMMENTARY: Henry bids the whole earth tremble at the presence of the God of Jacob, since the earth lies under the curse for man's sin; before that presence not only mountains but the earth itself may well tremble. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

8 COMMENTARY: Henry closes with provision matching protection: the almighty power that turned waters into a wall for Israel turned the flinty rock into a standing fountain, and that rock was Christ, a fountain of living waters from whom his Israel receives grace for grace. — Matthew Henry (Complete)
