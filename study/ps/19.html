Study Notes — Psalms

Psalm 19

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: The heavens declare God's glory, and the firmament shows his handiwork: creation is a constant, wordless sermon on its Maker. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: Day to day utters speech, and night to night shows knowledge: the succession of days and nights is a continuous teaching. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: There is no speech nor language where their voice is not heard: this universal testimony needs no translation, for it reaches every tongue. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: Their line goes out through all the earth, their words to the world's end; in them God sets a tabernacle for the sun: the heavens' witness is worldwide, and the sun's dwelling among them is its centrepiece. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: The sun is as a bridegroom from his chamber, rejoicing as a strong man to run a race: brightness, joy, and strength mark its daily course. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: Its going forth is from heaven's end, its circuit to the ends of it, and nothing is hidden from its heat: no corner escapes the sun, as none escapes God's witness. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: The law of the Lord is perfect, converting the soul; his testimony is sure, making wise the simple: from creation's witness David turns to Scripture, which does what nature cannot — converts and gives wisdom. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: The statutes of the Lord are right, rejoicing the heart; his commandment is pure, enlightening the eyes: rightness brings joy, purity brings light. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: The fear of the Lord is clean, enduring for ever; his judgments are true and righteous altogether: true religion is undefiled and lasting, and God's judgments are wholly just. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: They are more to be desired than gold, even much fine gold, and sweeter than honey and the honeycomb: value and delight together — Scripture is treasure and taste. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: Moreover by them God's servant is warned, and in keeping them there is great reward: warning and reward are the practical fruits of the word. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: Who can understand his errors? Cleanse me from secret faults: even the best reader of the law needs cleansing from sins he does not see. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: Keep back your servant also from presumptuous sins; let them not have dominion over me; then shall I be upright and innocent from the great transgression: wilful sin, once it rules, becomes the great transgression, and only God's restraint prevents it. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: Let the words of my mouth and the meditation of my heart be acceptable in your sight, O Lord, my strength and my redeemer: speech and inward thought together are offered to God, who is both strength and redeemer. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
