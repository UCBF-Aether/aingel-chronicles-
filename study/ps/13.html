Study Notes — Psalms

Psalm 13

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: How long will you forget me, Lord, for ever? How long will you hide your face? Repeated 'how long' voices the pain of felt desertion, not a charge that God has truly forgotten. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: How long shall I take counsel in my soul, with daily sorrow, while my enemy is exalted over me? Inward debate and outward triumph combine to weary the sufferer. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: Consider and hear me, Lord my God; lighten my eyes, lest I sleep the sleep of death: he asks for reviving light, for despair darkens toward death. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: Lest my enemy say, I have prevailed, and my troublers rejoice when I am moved: God's honour is pleaded — an enemy's boast would dishonour God's care. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: But I have trusted in your mercy, and my heart shall rejoice in your salvation: trust stated in the past tense steadies a present that still hurts. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: I will sing to the Lord because he has dealt bountifully with me: the psalm ends in song before the answer is seen, because mercy already received is ground enough for praise. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
