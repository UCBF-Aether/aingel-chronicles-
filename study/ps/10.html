Study Notes — Psalms

Psalm 10

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Why does the Lord stand afar off and hide himself in trouble? The psalm voices the believer's perplexity when God seems absent while the wicked prosper. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: The wicked in pride persecutes the poor; let him be taken in the devices he imagined, his own schemes becoming his snare. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: The wicked boasts of his heart's desire and blesses the covetous whom the Lord abhors: he praises the very greed God hates. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: Through pride of countenance he will not seek God; God is not in all his thoughts — practical atheism, living as if God did not see. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: His ways are always grievous, God's judgments far above out of his sight, and he puffs at all his enemies: prosperity hardens him into contempt. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: He says in his heart he shall not be moved and shall never be in adversity: security built on self-confidence is his delusion. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: His mouth is full of cursing, deceit, and fraud, with mischief and vanity under his tongue: speech betrays the heart that plots harm. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: He lurks in villages and secret places, murdering the innocent, his eyes set privily against the poor: oppression hides itself to strike safely. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: Like a lion in his den he lies in wait to catch the poor, drawing him into his net: the image is of patient, predatory cruelty. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: He crouches and humbles himself so the poor may fall by his strong ones: feigned lowliness is a hunting posture, not humility. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: He says in his heart God has forgotten, hides his face, and will never see: the wicked's theology is that God does not notice. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: Arise, O Lord, lift up your hand, and forget not the humble: the prayer answers verse 1 — the God who seems afar is called to act near. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: Why does the wicked contemn God, saying in his heart, You will not require it? Contempt rests on the false belief that sin goes unreckoned. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: But God has seen mischief and spite, to requite it with his hand; the poor commits himself to him, for he is the helper of the fatherless. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: Break the arm of the wicked and evil man, and seek out his wickedness till none is found: strip oppression of its power and expose it fully. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: The Lord is King for ever and ever, and the heathen perish out of his land: his reign outlasts every invader. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: The Lord has heard the desire of the humble, prepares their heart, and causes his ear to hear: God both forms the prayer and answers it. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

18 COMMENTARY: He judges the fatherless and oppressed, so that the man of the earth may no more oppress: justice for the defenceless is the end of the psalm, and the limit of human tyranny. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
