Study Notes — Psalms

Psalm 31

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: In you, Lord, I put my trust; let me never be ashamed; deliver me in your righteousness: trust, freedom from shame, and deliverance by God's righteousness open the psalm. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: Bow down your ear to me; deliver me speedily; be my strong rock, a house of defence to save me: a bowed ear, speedy deliverance, and a rock-house picture urgent, accessible refuge. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: For you are my rock and fortress; therefore for your name's sake lead me and guide me: God's nature and name are the reasons for leading, not David's worth. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: Pull me out of the net laid privily for me, for you are my strength: hidden nets need a stronger hand than the victim's to untangle them. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: Into your hand I commit my spirit; you have redeemed me, Lord God of truth: the words Christ uttered on the cross express a trust already complete — the spirit is deposited with the God of truth who redeems. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: I have hated those who regard lying vanities, but I trust in the Lord: hatred of idols and trust in God are two sides of one loyalty. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: I will be glad and rejoice in your mercy, for you have considered my trouble and known my soul in adversities: mercy that considers trouble and knows the soul in adversity is personal, not general. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: You have not shut me up into the enemy's hand, but set my feet in a large room: liberty replaces confinement, and the large room is the opposite of the net. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: Have mercy on me, Lord, for I am in trouble; my eye is consumed with grief, yea, my soul and my belly: grief reaches eye, soul, and body together. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: My life is spent with grief, my years with sighing; my strength fails because of my iniquity, and my bones are consumed: sorrow is here traced partly to his own sin, which weakens strength itself. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: I was a reproach among all my enemies, especially among neighbours, and a fear to acquaintances; those who saw me without fled from me: reproach spreads from enemies to neighbours, and even friends flee the sight of suffering. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: I am forgotten as a dead man out of mind, like a broken vessel: forgotten and broken — useless in men's account — is the depth of the humiliation. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: I have heard the slander of many; fear was on every side; while they took counsel together against me, they devised to take away my life: slander, encircling fear, and a plot on his life combine. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: But I trusted in you, Lord; I said, You are my God: the turn of the psalm is a single assertion of relation — You are my God — against all the preceding verses. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: My times are in your hand; deliver me from my enemies and persecutors: times, not merely this moment, are in God's hand, which is why deliverance can be asked with confidence. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: Make your face shine upon your servant; save me for your mercies' sake: the shining face is favour itself, and mercy, not merit, is its ground. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: Let me not be ashamed, Lord, for I have called on you; let the wicked be ashamed and silent in the grave: shame is prayed to fall where it belongs — on the wicked, in death's silence. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

18 COMMENTARY: Let lying lips be put to silence, which speak grievous things proudly and contemptuously against the righteous: the slander of verse 13 is answered by the silencing of the slanderers. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

19 COMMENTARY: Oh how great is your goodness, laid up for those who fear you, wrought for those who trust in you before the sons of men! Goodness is both stored up and publicly worked out, before men's eyes. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

20 COMMENTARY: You shall hide them in the secret of your presence from the pride of man, and keep them secretly in a pavilion from the strife of tongues: God's presence is a hiding-place from both pride and slanderous tongues. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

21 COMMENTARY: Blessed be the Lord, for he has shown me marvellous kindness in a strong city: kindness in a besieged, strong city — deliverance where escape looked impossible — is marvellous indeed. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

22 COMMENTARY: For I said in my haste, I am cut off from before your eyes; nevertheless you heard my supplications when I cried: hasty despair is corrected by heard prayer — feeling said cut off, fact said heard. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

23 COMMENTARY: O love the Lord, all his saints, for the Lord preserves the faithful and plentifully rewards the proud doer: preservation of the faithful and repayment of the proud are the two rules that justify love for God. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

24 COMMENTARY: Be of good courage, and he shall strengthen your heart, all you who hope in the Lord: the psalm ends as exhortation to all hopers — courage is not self-made but God-strengthened. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
