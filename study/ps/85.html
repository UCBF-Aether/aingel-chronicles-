Study Notes — Psalms

Psalm 85

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Favour to the land as God's own, smiling on it with distinguishing favours, is the spring-head of national good: possession of Canaan itself came by favour, and without its continuance ruin would have come many times. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

2 COMMENTARY: Forgiven iniquity and covered sin are paired with return from captivity: Henry notes the return counts as favour only when accompanied by pardon, since God covers sin wholly, not in part. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

3 COMMENTARY: Wrath taken away follows sin covered: God pacified when we are purified, anger turned by an intercessor standing in the gap, not stirred to consume. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

4 COMMENTARY: Turn us, O God of our salvation, and cause thine anger toward us to cease — past favour is the plea for present restoration. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

5 COMMENTARY: Anger forever and anger drawn to all generations are deprecations, not predictions: Henry contrasts impenitent sinners, for whom wrath is endless, with God's people, for whom God will not contend forever. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

6 COMMENTARY: Revival is prayed for as a second gathering, like the return from Babylon as life from the dead: God must revive his people with comforts spoken and deliverances wrought, as he once gave the captives some reviving in bondage. Henry draws the double end — that the people may rejoice, and that they may rejoice in God himself, who is both fountain of all mercies and centre of all joys. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

7 COMMENTARY: Shew us thy mercy, O Lord, and grant us thy salvation, is prayer for the manifestation of God's good-will: Show us thy mercy, O Lord! show thyself merciful to us; not only have mercy on us, but let us have the comfortable evidences of that mercy; let us know that thou hast mercy on us and mercy in store for us. And they pray that God would, graciously to them and gloriously to himself, appear on their behalf: Grant us thy salvation; grant it by thy promise, and then, no doubt, thou wilt work it by thy providence. The vessels of God's mercy are the heirs of his salvation; he shows mercy to those to whom he grants salvation; for salvation is of mere mercy. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

8 COMMENTARY: Watch-tower listening after prayer: God speaks peace to saints, but peace is spoken only to those who turn from sin, since backsliding after peace is egregious folly, and sin itself is the war against the peace God offers. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

9 COMMENTARY: Surely his salvation is nigh them that fear him, that glory may dwell in our land, is help at hand and honour secured: Surely his salvation is nigh, nigh to us, nigher than we think it is: it will soon be effected, how great soever our difficulties and distresses are, when God's time shall come, and that time is not far off. When the tale of bricks is doubled, then Moses comes. It is nigh to all who fear him; when trouble is nigh salvation is nigh, for God is a very present help in time of trouble to all who are his; whereas salvation is far from the wicked. This may fitly be applied to Christ the author of eternal salvation: it was the comfort of the Old-Testament saints that, though they lived not to see that redemption in Jerusalem which they waited for, yet they were sure it was nigh, and would be welcome, to all that fear God. And honour is secured: that glory may dwell in our land, that we may have the worship of God settled and established among us; for that is the glory of a land. When that goes, Ichabod — the glory has departed; when that stays glory dwells. This may refer to the Messiah, who was to be the glory of his people Israel, and who came and dwelt among them, for which reason their land is called Immanuel's land. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

10 COMMENTARY: Henry reads the meeting and kissing of the graces three ways at once: as reformation, where rulers and ruled alike are merciful, true, righteous and peaceable; as returned favour, where man's truth meets God's mercy and man's righteousness meets God's peace; and chiefly as the Messiah's undertaking, where salvation is so contrived that God may pardon sinners and be at peace with them without any wrong to his truth and righteousness. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

11 COMMENTARY: Truth springing from the earth is, in Henry's reading, truth grown in its proper soil, the hearts of men — the inward truth God desires, spread like grass over the earth — while righteousness looking down from heaven is God's mercy shining as the sun upon what it cherishes. Through the Mediator, heaven and earth meet again: God is just, and the justifier of those who believe in Jesus. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

12 COMMENTARY: The Lord gives what is good, everything he himself sees to be good for us, for all good comes from his goodness. Henry ties the land's increase to the graces going before it: when mercy, truth and righteousness govern hearts, seek first the kingdom and other things are added, and soul-prosperity either brings outward prosperity with it or sweetens the want of it. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

13 COMMENTARY: Righteousness shall go before him, and shall set us in the way of his steps, is a sure guidance in the good way: the righteousness of his promise which he has made to us, assuring us of happiness, and the righteousness of sanctification, that good work which he has wrought in us, these shall go before him to prepare his way, both to raise our expectations of his favour and to qualify us for it; and these shall go before us also, and be our guide to set us in the way of his steps, that is, to encourage our hopes and guide our practice, that we may go forth to meet him when he is coming towards us in ways of mercy. Christ, the sun of righteousness, shall bring us to God, and put us into the way that leads to him. John Baptist, a preacher of righteousness, shall go before Christ to prepare his way. Righteousness is a sure guide both in meeting God and in following him. — Matthew Henry (Complete)
