Study Notes — Psalms

Psalm 126

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Henry dates this to great surprising deliverance, most likely return from Babylon in Ezra's time — Babylon's captivity most remarkable, return typical of redemption by Christ, probably penned by Ezra or a prophet who came up first, Asaph singers returning (Ezra 2:41); Zion in captivity for degeneracy shall be turned when end answered, Cyrus proclaimed liberty for state reasons yet it was Lord's doing, captives sent not as dross to consume but gold to refine, sacred interests and public religion most valuable gain. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

2 COMMENTARY: Henry surprises with dream-likeness: sudden, too good to be true, questioning awake or vision as Peter in Acts 12:9, prevented with blessings before aware, like recovered health, life from dead, sudden ease from exquisite pain, new world — ecstasy scarcely within decency, mouth filled with laughter, tongue with singing, venting joy, glorifying God, noticing wonders; laughed-at now laugh, new song, laughter of joy in God not scorn of enemies; heathen spectators amazed, Jehovah done great things such as their gods cannot, honour extorted. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

3 COMMENTARY: Henry contrasts heathen spectators speaking as news, no part or lot, with God's people as sharers: with application — done great things for us, interested and advantaged, comfortable speaking of Christ's redemption as for us, who loved me and gave himself for me — and with affection — whereof we are glad, heathen amazed or angry, we glad; joy forbidden while whoring (Hos 9:1), now iniquity purged by captivity, sin taken away, God makes rejoice; repenting reforming people are rejoicing people; appearances great, God author, deliverances for us to rejoice in. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

4 COMMENTARY: Henry looks forward to mercies yet wanted: returned still in distress in own land (Neh 1:3), many yet in Babylon, rejoicing with trembling, grievances yet to redress — prayer for perfecting deliverance: ease returned of burdens groaned under, stir remaining to take benefit of liberty granted; beginnings of mercy encourage praying for completing, always matter for prayer even when furnished with praise, free and prosperous must not forget brethren in trouble; reunion welcome as streams in parched south, cold water to thirsty soul (Prov 25:25). — Matthew Henry (Complete)

5 COMMENTARY: Henry promises generally that all saints may comfort themselves: seedness of tears certainly ends in harvest of joy at last — suffering saints in tears often, greater share in calamities, but they sow in tears, doing duty of afflicted state, answering intentions of providences; weeping must not hinder sowing, when we suffer ill we must be doing well. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

6 COMMENTARY: Henry applies the promise to the single sower: he that goeth forth and weepeth, bearing precious seed, shall doubtless come again with rejoicing bringing sheaves — the weeping sower's faithful labour, continued through sorrow, assured of return in joy with the harvest his tears accompanied. — Matthew Henry (Complete)
