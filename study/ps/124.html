Study Notes — Psalms

Psalm 124

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Henry bids Israel say it: if the Lord had not been on our side — giving all glory to God, none to conquerors as usual — for the more desperate the disease, the more the Physician's skill appears in the cure, and happy the people whose God is Jehovah all-sufficient. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

2 COMMENTARY: Henry repeats for confirmation and attention: again and again the Lord appeared on their side when men rose up — God taking part, espousing cause, very present help nigh at hand, with us and among us as commander-in-chief, and if Jehovah himself had not undertaken, enemies would have overpowered us. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

3 COMMENTARY: Henry pictures wrath kindled as fire ready to consume, proud persecutors daring to swallow alive: men, creatures of our kind from whom humanity is expected, yet thirsting for blood — Absalom for his father's, a woman drunk with saints' blood — wrath cruel, anger outrageous, no less than destruction serving displeasure. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

4 COMMENTARY: Henry dwells on deluge similitude with ascents: waters overwhelming, stream going over souls — lives, comforts, all dear — as low grounds by land-flood or sands by spring-tide, God sometimes suffering enemies to prevail very far that his power appear more illustrious in deliverance. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

5 COMMENTARY: Henry names them proud waters: the same overwhelming stream repeated in ascent, proud enemies in pride persecuting the poor, whose prevailing is permitted only to magnify the rescue that follows. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

6 COMMENTARY: Henry blesses the Lord for narrow escape like lamb from jaws: God author of all deliverances must have glory, robbed if thanks are not returned — and enemies had no power but what was given from above, could not be prey unless God gave up, therefore rescued because he would not suffer ruin. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

7 COMMENTARY: Henry escapes like a sparrow from fowlers' snare: subtle, spiteful enemies laying snares to bring into sin and trouble, sometimes seemingly prevailing, God's people as unable as weak birds — then God's time to appear when all friends fail, breaking snare and turning counsel to foolishness, as Isaac saved ready for sacrifice, Jehovah-jireh in the mount seen. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

8 COMMENTARY: Henry trusts public help as personal help was trusted: our help is in the name of the Lord, as my help was in Psalm 121 — comfort that Israel's God made the world, therefore will have a church in the world and secure it in greatest danger, so church's friends putting confidence in him shall not be put to confusion. — Matthew Henry (Complete)
