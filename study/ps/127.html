Study Notes — Psalms

Psalm 127

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Henry's family-psalm for Solomon — house to build, city to keep, seed to raise — directs him to look up to God and depend on providence, without which wisdom, care, industry would not serve; except Lord build house, by providence and blessing, builders labour in vain though ever so ingenious, as Babel builders in defiance or Hiel under curse, pride or oppression foundations God does not build, best-laid project fails unless God crown it; except Lord keep city from fire and enemies, watchmen neither slumbering nor sleeping wake in vain, guards slain or city betrayed by accidents no sentinel can obviate. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

2 COMMENTARY: Henry calls vain the eager pursuit: rise early, sit up late, rob sleep to pay cares, eat bread of sorrows — all days in darkness (Eccl 5:17), care embittering comforts, lives burden, all to get money, all in vain except God prosper, riches not always to men of understanding (Eccl 9:11); beloved have minds easy without ado, Solomon Jedidiah beloved given sleep and kingdom while Absalom wheedled and Adonijah stirred in vain; inordinate care vain and fruitless, sleep God's gift — safe (Ps 4:8), sweet (Jer 31:25-26), quietness and contentment, present enjoyment and comfortable expectation; keep in love of God, easy whether little or much. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

3 COMMENTARY: Henry counts children God's gift: withheld, God withholds (Gen 30:2); given, God gives (Gen 33:5); to us what he makes them, comforts or crosses; heritage and reward, blessings not burdens, he that sends mouths sends meat if trusted — Obed-edom eight sons for entertaining ark (1 Chr 26:5); heritage for the Lord as well as from him, my children borne unto me (Ezek 16:20), most honour and comfort when accounted to him for a generation; Solomon multiplying wives contrary to law read of but one son — desire children as heritage must receive in way he pleases, lawful marriage to one wife (Mal 2:15). — Matthew Henry (Complete)

4 COMMENTARY: Henry arms the mighty man: arrows in hand of one who knows to use them for safety and advantage, so children of youth — born to parents young, strongest and most healthful, grown to serve by time parents need service, or children themselves young — instruments of much good fortifying family against enemies; arrows in hand with prudence may be directed aright to mark, God's glory and service of generation, but afterwards gone abroad are arrows out of hand too late to bend, too often proving arrows in heart, constant grief bringing godly parents' gray hairs with sorrow to grave. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

5 COMMENTARY: Henry blesses quiver full of arrows of different sizes, all of use one time or other, children of different capacities and inclinations several ways serviceable: he that has numerous issue may boldly speak with enemy in gate in judgment, in battle need not fear having so many good seconds, zealous, faithful, in vigour of youth (1 Sam 2:4-5) — happy is the man so furnished, not ashamed but speaking with enemies in the gate. — Matthew Henry (Complete)
