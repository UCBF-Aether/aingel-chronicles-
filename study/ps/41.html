Study Notes — Psalms

Psalm 41

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Blessed is he who considers the poor; the Lord will deliver him in time of trouble: consideration of the poor is blessed, and its reward is deliverance in trouble. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: The Lord will preserve him and keep him alive; he shall be blessed upon the earth, and you will not deliver him to the will of his enemies: preservation, life, earthly blessing, and safety from enemies' will are the fourfold reward. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: The Lord will strengthen him on the bed of languishing; you will make all his bed in his sickness: God himself tends the sickbed, making the bed of the languishing. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: I said, Lord, be merciful to me; heal my soul, for I have sinned against you: mercy and healing of the soul are asked, with sin against God confessed as the sickness. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: My enemies speak evil of me: When shall he die, and his name perish? Enemies wish not only death but the perishing of his name. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: And if he comes to see me, he speaks vanity; his heart gathers iniquity to itself; when he goes abroad, he tells it: the visiting enemy is a spy — vanity spoken, iniquity gathered, gossip spread abroad. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: All who hate me whisper together against me; against me they devise my hurt: whispering together is conspiracy, and its object is his hurt. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: An evil disease, say they, cleaves fast to him; and now that he lies he shall rise up no more: they pronounce his disease incurable and his lying down final. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: Yea, my own familiar friend, in whom I trusted, who ate of my bread, has lifted up his heel against me: the trusted bread-eating friend's lifted heel is the deepest treachery — the words Jesus applies to Judas. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: But you, O Lord, be merciful to me, and raise me up, that I may requite them: mercy and raising up are asked, with requital — just repayment — as the stated end. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: By this I know that you favour me, because my enemy does not triumph over me: the enemy's failure to triumph is the sign of God's favour. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: And as for me, you uphold me in my integrity, and set me before your face for ever: upheld integrity ends in God's face for ever — presence as the final reward. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: Blessed be the Lord God of Israel from everlasting to everlasting. Amen, and Amen: the first book of Psalms closes in blessing, from everlasting to everlasting, with a double Amen. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
