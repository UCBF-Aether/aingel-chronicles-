Study Notes — Psalms

Psalm 35

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Plead my cause, O Lord, with those who strive with me; fight against those who fight against me: God is asked to act as advocate and champion in a lawsuit become warfare. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: Take hold of shield and buckler, and stand up for my help: God is pictured arming himself for his servant's defence. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: Draw out the spear, and stop the way against my persecutors; say to my soul, I am your salvation: the spear blocks the pursuers' road, and God's own word to the soul is salvation itself. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: Let them be confounded and put to shame who seek after my soul; let them be turned back and brought to confusion who devise my hurt: shame and confusion are prayed on the soul-seekers, reversing their intent. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: Let them be as chaff before the wind, and let the angel of the Lord chase them: chaff driven by wind, with God's angel driving, pictures helpless, pursued wickedness. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: Let their way be dark and slippery, and let the angel of the Lord persecute them: darkness and slipperiness make the pursuers' own road their ruin. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: For without cause they hid for me their net in a pit, which without cause they dug for my soul: causelessness — twice stressed — is the injustice pleaded. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: Let destruction come upon him at unawares, and let his own hidden net catch himself; into that very destruction let him fall: the trapper caught in his own trap is the psalm's justice. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: And my soul shall be joyful in the Lord, and rejoice in his salvation: joy in the Lord, not in revenge, is the stated fruit of deliverance. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: All my bones shall say, Lord, who is like you, who delivers the poor from him too strong for him, the poor and needy from him who spoils him? The whole body joins the praise, for deliverance of the weak from the strong has no parallel. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: False witnesses rose up; they laid to my charge things I knew not: the charge is falsehood itself — accusations about things unknown to the accused. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: They rewarded me evil for good, to the spoiling of my soul: good repaid with evil, to the soul's spoiling, is the deepest ingratitude. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: But as for me, when they were sick, my clothing was sackcloth; I humbled my soul with fasting, and my prayer returned into my own bosom: he had mourned and fasted for these very enemies in their sickness, and his prayer, unanswered by them, returned to bless himself. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: I behaved as though he had been my friend or brother; I bowed down heavily, as one mourning for his mother: friendship, brotherhood, and a son's mourning for his mother measure his past sympathy. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: But in my adversity they rejoiced and gathered together; the abjects gathered against me, and I knew it not; they tore me and ceased not: his fall became their feast, and even base men joined the tearing without ceasing. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: With hypocritical mockers in feasts, they gnashed on me with their teeth: feast mockery and gnashing teeth picture contempt turned savage. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: Lord, how long will you look on? Rescue my soul from their destructions, my darling from the lions: the 'how long' of a watcher God, and the precious life threatened by lions, plead for rescue. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

18 COMMENTARY: I will give you thanks in the great congregation, and praise you among much people: deliverance will be thanked publicly, not privately alone. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

19 COMMENTARY: Let not my enemies wrongfully rejoice over me, nor let those who hate me without cause wink with the eye: wrongful joy and the secret wink of conspiracy are both prayed against. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

20 COMMENTARY: For they speak not peace, but devise deceitful matters against those quiet in the land: their speech is not peace but plots against the quiet — the peaceable are their target. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

21 COMMENTARY: Yea, they opened their mouth wide against me, and said, Aha, aha, our eye has seen it: the wide mouth and the exultant 'Aha' claim a witnessed triumph. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

22 COMMENTARY: This you have seen, O Lord; keep not silence; O Lord, be not far from me: against their claimed seeing stands God's true seeing — and his silence must end. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

23 COMMENTARY: Stir yourself, and awake to my judgment, even to my cause, my God and my Lord: God is called to wake, as it were, to the judgment of his servant's cause. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

24 COMMENTARY: Judge me, O Lord my God, according to your righteousness, and let them not rejoice over me: judgment by God's righteousness, not by the enemies' slander, is the plea. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

25 COMMENTARY: Let them not say in their hearts, Ah, so would we have it; let them not say, We have swallowed him up: the enemies' inward boast and their claim to have swallowed him are both forbidden by the prayer. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

26 COMMENTARY: Let them be ashamed and brought to confusion together who rejoice at my hurt; let them be clothed with shame and dishonour who magnify themselves against me: joint shame and clothing in dishonour answer joint rejoicing and self-magnifying. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

27 COMMENTARY: Let them shout for joy and be glad who favour my righteous cause; let them say continually, Let the Lord be magnified, who has pleasure in the prosperity of his servant: the friends' joy is to magnify God, whose pleasure is his servant's prosperity. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

28 COMMENTARY: And my tongue shall speak of your righteousness and of your praise all the day long: the psalm ends where praise must — in a tongue speaking God's righteousness and praise all day. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
