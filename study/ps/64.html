Study Notes — Psalms

Psalm 64

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Hear my voice, O God, in my prayer; preserve my life from fear of the enemy: hearing is asked, and preservation from fear — not only from the enemy, but from fear of him. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: Hide me from the secret counsel of the wicked, from the insurrection of workers of iniquity: secret counsel and insurrection — hidden plotting and open rising — both need hiding from God. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: Who whet their tongue like a sword, and bend their bows to shoot their arrows, even bitter words: tongue whetted as sword, bows bent for bitter words — speech is the weapon, bitter words the arrows. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: That they may shoot in secret at the perfect; suddenly do they shoot at him, and fear not: secret shooting at the perfect, suddenly, without fear — the upright is ambushed, not confronted. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: They encourage themselves in an evil matter; they commune of laying snares privily; they say, Who shall see them? Self-encouragement in evil, communing over secret snares, and 'Who shall see them?' — conspiracy comforts itself with invisibility. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: They search out iniquities; they accomplish a diligent search; both the inward thought of every one of them, and the heart, is deep: diligent searching out of iniquities, deep inward thought and heart — the plot is deep because the heart is deep. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: But God shall shoot at them with an arrow; suddenly shall they be wounded: God's arrow answers theirs — suddenly wounded, as suddenly they shot. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: So they shall make their own tongue to fall upon themselves; all who see them shall flee away: their own tongue falls on themselves, and onlookers flee — the weapon recoils, and the sight appals. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: And all men shall fear, and shall declare the work of God, for they shall wisely consider of his doing: universal fear, declaration of God's work, wise consideration of his doing — judgment teaches all men. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: The righteous shall be glad in the Lord, and shall trust in him, and all the upright in heart shall glory: gladness, trust, and glory for the righteous and upright in heart — the psalm ends in the joy of those the plotters shot at. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
