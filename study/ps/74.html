Study Notes — Psalms

Psalm 74

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: O God, why have you cast us off for ever? Why does your anger smoke against the sheep of your pasture? Casting off for ever questioned, anger smoking against pasture sheep — the ruined temple's lament opens in bewilderment. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: Remember your congregation, which you have purchased of old, the rod of your inheritance, which you have redeemed, this Mount Zion, wherein you have dwelt: congregation purchased of old, inheritance's rod redeemed, Zion dwelt in — ancient purchase and dwelling are pleaded. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: Lift up your feet unto the perpetual desolations, even all that the enemy has done wickedly in the sanctuary: feet lifted to perpetual desolations, all the enemy's wicked doing in the sanctuary — God is bidden come and see the ruin. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: Your enemies roar in the midst of your congregations; they set up their ensigns for signs: roaring enemies in the congregations, ensigns set up for signs — the sanctuary is occupied by hostile standards. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: A man was famous according as he had lifted up axes upon the thick trees: fame for axe-work on thick trees — the destroyers' earlier boasting is recalled. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: But now they break down the carved work thereof at once with axes and hammers: carved work broken at once with axes and hammers — the sanctuary's art is smashed. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: They have cast fire into your sanctuary, they have defiled by casting down the dwelling place of your name to the ground: fire cast into the sanctuary, the Name's dwelling cast to the ground and defiled — the fire completes the desecration. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: They said in their hearts, Let us destroy them together; they have burned up all the synagogues of God in the land: 'destroy them together' in their hearts, all God's synagogues burned in the land — the destruction is total and intended. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: We see not our signs; there is no more any prophet; neither is there among us any that knows how long: no signs seen, no prophet, none knowing how long — the silence of heaven matches the ruin on earth. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: O God, how long shall the adversary reproach, and shall the enemy blaspheme your name for ever? The question turns from ruin to God's name. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: Why do you withdraw your hand, even your right hand? Pluck it out of your bosom: withdrawn hand, right hand in the bosom — God is bidden pluck out the hand he seems to hide. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: For God is my King of old, working salvation in the midst of the earth: God as King of old, working salvation in earth's midst — ancient kingship grounds present hope. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: You did divide the sea by your strength; you broke the heads of the dragons in the waters: sea divided by strength, dragons' heads broken in waters — the Exodus victory over chaos-monsters. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: You broke the heads of leviathan in pieces, and gave him to be meat to the people inhabiting the wilderness: leviathan's heads broken, given as meat to wilderness people — the sea-monster becomes food for God's people. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: You did cleave the fountain and the flood; you dried up mighty rivers: fountain and flood cleft, mighty rivers dried — water miracles in wilderness and Jordan. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: The day is yours, the night also is yours; you have prepared the light and the sun: day and night God's, light and sun prepared by him — creation's order is his. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: You have set all the borders of the earth; you have made summer and winter: earth's borders set, summer and winter made — seasons and boundaries are God's appointments. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

18 COMMENTARY: Remember this, that the enemy has reproached, O Lord, and that the foolish people have blasphemed your name: remember the enemy's reproach and the foolish people's blasphemy of God's name — the plea returns to the name. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

19 COMMENTARY: O deliver not the soul of your turtledove unto the multitude of the wicked; forget not the congregation of your poor for ever: turtledove soul not delivered to the wicked multitude, poor congregation not forgotten for ever — the defenceless dove is God's people. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

20 COMMENTARY: Have respect unto the covenant, for the dark places of the earth are full of the habitations of cruelty: respect to the covenant is prayed, for dark places are full of cruelty's habitations — covenant is the argument against universal cruelty. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

21 COMMENTARY: O let not the oppressed return ashamed; let the poor and needy praise your name: oppressed not returning ashamed, poor and needy praising God's name — shame turned to praise is the prayed outcome. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

22 COMMENTARY: Arise, O God, plead your own cause; remember how the foolish man reproaches you daily: arising, pleading his own cause, remembering daily reproach by the foolish — God's own cause is now the plea. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

23 COMMENTARY: Forget not the voice of your enemies; the tumult of those who rise up against you increases continually: enemies' voice unforgotten, tumult of risers increasing continually — the psalm ends with rising tumult still unanswered, and the prayer still standing. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
