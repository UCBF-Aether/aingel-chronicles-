Study Notes — Psalms

Psalm 57

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Be merciful to me, O God, be merciful to me, for my soul trusts in you; yea, in the shadow of your wings will I make my refuge, until these calamities be overpast: mercy twice asked, trust as its ground, and wings' shadow as refuge until calamities pass — the title sets this in the cave, fleeing Saul. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: I will cry to God Most High, to God who performs all things for me: crying to the Most High, who performs all things for his servant — performance, not mere promise, is God's character. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: He shall send from heaven and save me from the reproach of him who would swallow me up; God shall send forth his mercy and his truth: heaven-sent salvation from swallowing reproach, by mercy and truth sent forth together. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: My soul is among lions, and I lie even among them set on fire, even the sons of men, whose teeth are spears and arrows, and their tongue a sharp sword: lions, fire-set men, teeth as spears and arrows, tongue as sword — the cave's danger is human and bestial at once. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: Be exalted, O God, above the heavens; let your glory be above all the earth: the refrain lifts the prayer above the cave — exaltation above heavens, glory above earth. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: They have prepared a net for my steps; my soul is bowed down; they have dug a pit before me, into the midst whereof they are fallen themselves: net for steps, bowed soul, dug pit — and the diggers fallen into their own pit. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: My heart is fixed, O God, my heart is fixed; I will sing and give praise: a fixed heart, twice stated, issues in singing and praise. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: Awake up, my glory; awake, psaltery and harp; I myself will awake early: glory, psaltery, and harp are bidden awake, and he himself will awake early — praise rises before dawn. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: I will praise you, O Lord, among the people; I will sing to you among the nations: praise among peoples and song among nations — the cave's song goes worldwide. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: For your mercy is great unto the heavens, and your truth unto the clouds: mercy to the heavens, truth to the clouds — the measures of praise match God's attributes. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: Be exalted, O God, above the heavens; let your glory be above all the earth: the refrain closes the psalm as it broke its middle — exaltation and glory above all. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
