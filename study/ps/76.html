Study Notes — Psalms

Psalm 76

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: God made himself known in Judah as he was not known among other nations, so that where he is known his name is great. Henry calls this distinguishing favour the honour and privilege of his people. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: In Salem and Zion God kept his tabernacle and dwelling-place, receiving his people's homage and issuing his orders by his oracles. His presence by his ordinances was the glory of the people. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: There, in answer to prayer and in fulfilment of promise, God broke every weapon of war — bow, shield, sword and battle itself. Public success owes as much to what is done in the church as to what is done in the camp. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: God is more glorious than the mountains of prey — the proud, oppressive powers that live by destroying. Their glory is to destroy; his is to deliver. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: The stouthearted invaders were spoiled and left helpless, sleeping the sleep of sinners that wakes to shame. Bold men were cowed, and strong men could not so much as find their hands. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: At the rebuke of the God of Jacob chariot and horse alike were cast into a dead sleep. God did but speak the word, and the destroying angel left their war-gear unformidable. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: God alone is to be feared, for who can stand before him once he is angry? His majesty is to be reverenced, his sovereignty submitted to, and his justice dreaded by offenders. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: God caused judgment to be heard from heaven, and the earth feared and was still, as silence is made by proclamation when the court sits. When God speaks judgment, reverent silence is the fitting response. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: God arose to judgment to save all the meek of the earth — the quiet in the land who bear wrongs but do none. Though meekness exposes them to injury, God will appear and plead their cause. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: Even the wrath of man is made to praise God, as far as he permits it to break forth, and what will not turn to his praise he restrains. He sets bounds to human rage as he does to the raging sea. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: All who approach God are to vow and pay — to bind themselves to him as loyal subjects and live up to the bond. Their presents are their prayers, praises, and above all their hearts. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: God cuts off the spirit of princes, slipping it off as easily as a flower from its stalk, and is terrible to the kings of the earth. Since there is no contending with God, submission is both wisdom and duty. — Matthew Henry
