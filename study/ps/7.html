Study Notes — Psalms

Psalm 7

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: David puts his trust in the Lord his God and asks to be saved and delivered from all who persecute him; trust is the psalm's foundation. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: Without deliverance his soul would be torn like a lion's prey, rent in pieces with none to rescue; the danger is mortal, not slight. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: He submits to examination: if he has done this wrong, if iniquity is in his hands, let the charge stand. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: If he has rewarded evil to a friend at peace with him, or plundered an enemy without cause, he accepts the verdict against himself. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: Then let the enemy pursue, overtake, tread down his life, and lay his honour in the dust: innocence is asserted by inviting its own punishment if false. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: He calls on God to arise in anger and lift himself against his enemies' rage, awake for the judgment God has commanded. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: The congregation of peoples shall compass God about, and for their sakes he returns on high: public vindication will teach the nations. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: The Lord judges the peoples; David asks to be judged by his righteousness and integrity in this cause — not a claim of sinlessness, but of innocence toward his accusers. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: Let the wickedness of the wicked end, but establish the just, for the righteous God tries hearts and reins, the inward thoughts and affections. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: His defence is of God, who saves the upright in heart: protection belongs to God, and its recipients are the sincere. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: God judges the righteous — vindicates them — and is angry with the wicked every day: divine anger is not a passing mood but a settled opposition to evil. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: If the wicked will not turn, God whets his sword, bends his bow, and makes it ready: judgment is prepared, not improvised. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: He has prepared instruments of death and ordains his arrows against persecutors: the weapons of wrath are aimed, not random. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: The wicked travails with iniquity, conceives mischief, and brings forth falsehood: sin is pictured as a painful, productive labour ending in lies. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: He digs a pit and falls into the ditch he made: his own trap becomes his ruin. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: His mischief returns on his own head, and his violence descends on his own pate: retribution follows the exact path of the wrong. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: David ends in praise to the Lord according to his righteousness, singing to the name of the Lord most high: judgment rightly understood issues in worship. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
