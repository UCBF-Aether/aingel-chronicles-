Psalm 122

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Henry welcomes the invitation as kindness, not affront: God wills concert worship, many joining in public ordinances beyond house worship, not forsaking assembling — and we must stir one another, let us go together, not do you go and pray for us, for slow and backward souls sharpen one another as iron sharpens iron, and rejoicers in God rejoice in calls to wait on him. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

2 COMMENTARY: Henry triumphs in the prospect: feet standing within Jerusalem's gates makes amends for tedious journey fatigue, desirable even as servants standing by or doorkeepers — for now there is a resting-place for the ark, and where it is there will we be. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

3 COMMENTARY: Henry praises the beautiful, compact city: houses contiguous, streets fair and spacious, uniform, strengthening one another, Jebusites driven out and wholly God's people's — a type of the gospel church compact in holy love and communion, all as one city. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

4 COMMENTARY: Henry gathers tribes as one man to the holy city: in obedience they go up to receive instruction from the testimony of Israel, consulting God's oracle, and to ascribe glory by thanksgiving — for if God speaks by his word, we answer by thanksgivings, and that is our errand in public worship. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

5 COMMENTARY: Henry loves the royal city for thrones of judgment: justice administered by a man after God's own heart secured civil interests as well as ecclesiastical, happy courts like Westminster Hall — and goodly neighbours, testimony directing thrones and thrones protecting testimony, befriending one another. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

6 COMMENTARY: Henry calls all to pray for Jerusalem's peace out of love, not custom: welfare, uniting inhabitants and preservation from enemies, desired because in its peace we have peace, earnestly prayed as God's gift to be enquired of — and lovers prosper, for God loves Zion's gates and will love those loving them, souls prospering by ordinances dearly loved. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

7 COMMENTARY: Henry puts words in mouths for all within walls and for palaces: peace in fortifications never attacked or never taken, effectual security — and prosperity for princes at the helm, for if they prosper it is well for public, and poorer sort must pray for palaces rather than envy them. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

8 COMMENTARY: Henry resolves personal prayer for brethren and companions: now tribes cheerfully resort and Jerusalem settled as God's name-place, he will say peace be within thee, not leaving prayer to priests, prophets or people while he fights and rules — for brethren in worship have knit his heart to them as true-hearted Israelites. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

9 COMMENTARY: Henry seconds prayer with endeavour for God's house: to utmost power seek thy good, for activity for public good proves sincerity of prayer — not merely because Jerusalem was his own city and family interests lodged there, but from warm regard to saints' communion and ordinances, affections set to God's house, which makes concern for public welfare right. — Matthew Henry (Complete)
