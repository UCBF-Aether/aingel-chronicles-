Psalm 52

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Why do you boast yourself in mischief, O mighty man? The goodness of God endures continually: boasting in mischief is questioned, and God's continual goodness — against Doeg's brief cruelty — is the answer. The occasion, as the title records, is Doeg the Edomite's betrayal of Ahimelech's house. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: The tongue devises mischiefs, like a sharp razor, working deceitfully: a tongue like a sharp razor cuts with deceit — Doeg's tongue cut down the priests. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: You love evil more than good, and lying rather than to speak righteousness: love of evil over good, and lying over righteous speech, is the character exposed. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: You love all devouring words, O deceitful tongue: devouring words are loved by the deceitful tongue — speech that swallows others whole. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: God shall likewise destroy you for ever, take you away, pluck you out of your dwelling place, and root you out of the land of the living: destruction for ever, taking away, plucking out, rooting out — the mighty man's end mirrors his uprooting of others. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: The righteous also shall see, and fear, and shall laugh at him: seeing, fearing, and laughing — reverent fear at God's judgment joined to laughter at folly's end. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: Lo, this is the man that made not God his strength, but trusted in the abundance of his riches, and strengthened himself in his wickedness: the epitaph is stated — not God as strength, but riches trusted, and wickedness as strength. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: But I am like a green olive tree in the house of God; I trust in the mercy of God for ever and ever: against the uprooted mighty man stands the green olive in God's house, trusting mercy for ever. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: I will praise you for ever, because you have done it, and I will wait on your name, for it is good before your saints: praise for ever for what God has done, and waiting on his good name before his saints, close the psalm. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
