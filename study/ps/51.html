Study Notes — Psalms

Psalm 51

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Have mercy upon me, O God, according to your lovingkindness; according to the multitude of your tender mercies blot out my transgressions: mercy according to lovingkindness, blotting according to multiplied tender mercies — the measures of pardon match the multitude of sin. This is David's psalm after Nathan came concerning Bathsheba, as Gill's introduction records. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: Wash me thoroughly from my iniquity, and cleanse me from my sin: thorough washing and cleansing are asked, for sin long lain upon him, as Gill notes, needs much washing. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: For I acknowledge my transgressions, and my sin is ever before me: acknowledgment before God and man, with sin ever before his face, is the penitent's state. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: Against you, you only, have I sinned, and done this evil in your sight, that you might be justified when you speak, and clear when you judge: all sin against creatures is chiefly against God the lawgiver, and confession justifies God's sentence. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: Behold, I was shapen in iniquity, and in sin did my mother conceive me: original sin is confessed — shapen and conceived in it, before any act of his own. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: Behold, you desire truth in the inward parts, and in the hidden part you shall make me to know wisdom: God desires truth inwardly, and wisdom is to be known in the hidden part — the opposite of his secret sin. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: Purge me with hyssop, and I shall be clean; wash me, and I shall be whiter than snow: hyssop, the cleansing herb of the law, and washing whiter than snow picture complete purification. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: Make me to hear joy and gladness, that the bones which you have broken may rejoice: broken bones — the frame crushed by conviction — shall rejoice at hearing joy and gladness again. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: Hide your face from my sins, and blot out all my iniquities: God's face hidden from sins, and all iniquities blotted, are prayed for together. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: Create in me a clean heart, O God, and renew a right spirit within me: creation, not repair, is asked — a clean heart created and a right spirit renewed. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: Cast me not away from your presence, and take not your holy spirit from me: casting away from presence and taking of the Holy Spirit are the feared losses, remembered in Saul's fall. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: Restore to me the joy of your salvation, and uphold me with your free spirit: joy of salvation restored, and a free, willing spirit upholding — joy and willingness return together. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: Then will I teach transgressors your ways, and sinners shall be converted to you: restored, he will teach transgressors, and sinners shall turn — his fall becomes others' instruction. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: Deliver me from bloodguiltiness, O God, you God of my salvation, and my tongue shall sing aloud of your righteousness: bloodguiltiness — Uriah's blood — is named, and deliverance from it will make his tongue sing God's righteousness aloud. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: O Lord, open my lips, and my mouth shall show forth your praise: opened lips are God's work before praise is man's. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: For you desire not sacrifice, else would I give it; you delight not in burnt offering: sacrifice and burnt offering, even if given, are not what God delights in from an unbroken heart. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: The sacrifices of God are a broken spirit; a broken and contrite heart, O God, you will not despise: a broken spirit and contrite heart are God's sacrifices — and these he will not despise. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

18 COMMENTARY: Do good in your good pleasure unto Zion; build the walls of Jerusalem: the penitent's prayer widens to Zion and Jerusalem's walls — personal pardon and public welfare together. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

19 COMMENTARY: Then shall you be pleased with the sacrifices of righteousness, with burnt offering and whole burnt offering; then shall they offer bullocks upon your altar: once the heart is right, sacrifices of righteousness please God again, and bullocks are offered on a restored altar. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
