Psalm 11

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: In the Lord David puts his trust, and therefore he rejects the counsel to flee like a bird to the mountain: flight urged by fear would betray the trust he professes. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: The wicked bend the bow and ready the arrow to shoot privily at the upright in heart: persecution is secret as well as violent. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: If the foundations are destroyed, what can the righteous do? When law, order, and public justice seem overthrown, human help looks hopeless — which is why the next verse lifts the eye to heaven. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: The Lord is in his holy temple, his throne is in heaven, and his eyes behold and try the children of men: earthly foundations may shake, the heavenly throne does not. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: The Lord tries the righteous, but the wicked and the lover of violence his soul hates: trial of the godly is fatherly; hatred of the violent is settled. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: Upon the wicked he shall rain snares, fire and brimstone, and a horrible tempest — the portion of their cup: Sodom's fate is the measure of what rebellion drinks. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: For the righteous Lord loves righteousness, and his countenance beholds the upright: the godly are seen, approved, and kept by the God whose nature is just. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
