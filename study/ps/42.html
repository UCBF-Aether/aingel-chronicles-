Study Notes — Psalms

Psalm 42

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: As the hart pants after the water brooks, so pants my soul after you, O God: thirst after water pictures thirst after God — a bodily need for a spiritual one. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: My soul thirsts for God, for the living God; when shall I come and appear before God? Thirst is for the living God, and the question is when he may appear before him in worship. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: My tears have been my meat day and night, while they continually say to me, Where is your God? Tears replace food, day and night, and the taunt 'Where is your God?' is the salt in them. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: When I remember these things, I pour out my soul in me, for I had gone with the multitude, I went with them to the house of God, with joy and praise, with a multitude keeping holyday: memory of festal processions to God's house pours out his soul in present exile. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: Why are you cast down, O my soul, and why disquieted in me? Hope in God, for I shall yet praise him for the help of his countenance: the soul is preached to — cast down and disquieted, yet bidden to hope and to future praise. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: O my God, my soul is cast down within me; therefore I will remember you from the land of Jordan, and of the Hermonites, from the hill Mizar: from exile's far places — Jordan, Hermon, little Mizar — he remembers God, and remembrance steadies the cast-down soul. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: Deep calls to deep at the noise of your waterspouts; all your waves and billows are gone over me: waterspout answers waterspout, wave upon wave — God's own waters overwhelm him. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: Yet the Lord will command his lovingkindness in the daytime, and in the night his song shall be with me, and my prayer to the God of my life: daytime lovingkindness commanded, night song given, prayer to the God of his life — the pattern holds through the flood. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: I will say to God my rock, Why have you forgotten me? Why go I mourning because of the enemy's oppression? The rock is questioned — forgotten, mourning, oppressed — yet still called rock. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: As with a sword in my bones, my enemies reproach me, while they say daily to me, Where is your God? The taunt cuts like a sword in the bones, daily repeated. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: Why are you cast down, O my soul, and why disquieted within me? Hope in God, for I shall yet praise him, who is the health of my countenance, and my God: the refrain returns, now with God as the health of his countenance — the face that was downcast shall be healed by praise. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
