Study Notes — Psalms

Psalm 33

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Rejoice in the Lord, you righteous, for praise is comely for the upright: praise fits the upright as a garment fits its wearer — it is becoming, not forced. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: Praise the Lord with harp; sing to him with the psaltery and an instrument of ten strings: instruments are named because praise should use the best skill and fullest sound available. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: Sing to him a new song; play skilfully with a loud noise: new mercies deserve new songs, played with skill and with joyful loudness. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: For the word of the Lord is right, and all his works are done in truth: the ground of praise is God's right word and truthful works. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: He loves righteousness and judgment; the earth is full of the goodness of the Lord: love of justice and a goodness that fills the earth are the character praised. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: By the word of the Lord the heavens were made, and all their host by the breath of his mouth: creation by word alone — no tools, no struggle — is the proof of his power. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: He gathers the waters of the sea as a heap, and lays up the depth in storehouses: the seas, which threaten chaos, are stored like goods in a warehouse under God's control. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: Let all the earth fear the Lord; let all the world's inhabitants stand in awe of him: the Creator's power commands universal fear and awe. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: For he spoke, and it was done; he commanded, and it stood fast: speech and standing fast — the word creates and sustains in one act. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: The Lord brings the counsel of the heathen to nought, and makes the devices of the people of none effect: human counsels, however grand, dissolve before God's. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: The counsel of the Lord stands for ever, the thoughts of his heart to all generations: against vanishing human plans stands a counsel that outlives generations. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: Blessed is the nation whose God is the Lord, and the people he has chosen for his own inheritance: national blessedness is not size or wealth but having the Lord as God and being his chosen inheritance. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: The Lord looks from heaven and beholds all the sons of men: his gaze is universal — no man is beneath his notice. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: From the place of his habitation he looks upon all the earth's inhabitants: heaven is not distance but vantage for seeing all. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: He fashions their hearts alike, and considers all their works: the one Fashioner of hearts knows every heart he made and weighs every work. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: No king is saved by the multitude of a host, and a mighty man is not delivered by much strength: armies and strength fail as saviours, however multiplied. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: A horse is a vain thing for safety, neither shall it deliver any by its great strength: the war-horse, the ancient tank, is vanity as a saviour. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

18 COMMENTARY: Behold, the eye of the Lord is upon those who fear him, upon those who hope in his mercy: the same eye that sees all rests with special care on the fearing and hoping. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

19 COMMENTARY: To deliver their soul from death, and keep them alive in famine: God's eye has a purpose — deliverance from death and preservation in famine. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

20 COMMENTARY: Our soul waits for the Lord; he is our help and shield: waiting is the people's posture, and God is both help and shield in the waiting. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

21 COMMENTARY: For our heart shall rejoice in him, because we have trusted in his holy name: joy follows trust, and the name trusted is holy. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

22 COMMENTARY: Let your mercy, Lord, be upon us, according as we hope in you: the closing prayer measures mercy by hope — as hope rises, so may mercy rest on us. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
