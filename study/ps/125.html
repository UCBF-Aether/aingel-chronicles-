Study Notes — Psalms

Psalm 125

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Henry sums this psalm Isaiah 3:10-11: well with righteous, ill with wicked, blessing and curse set before us; trusters are Mount Zion — church built on a rock gates of hell shall not prevail, firm by providence much more by promise, not removed from integrity or confidence, abiding for ever in grace earnest of glory. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

2 COMMENTARY: Henry shelters Jerusalem by round mountains breaking winds and barring enemies, such a defence is God's providence: Lord round about his people on every side, no gap in hedge where seeking enemy can enter (Job 1:10), henceforth even for ever — mountains may moulder, covenant cannot break (Isa 54:10), full accomplishment in heaven as pillars going no more out. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

3 COMMENTARY: Henry allows rod of wicked power and anger may fall on persons, estates, liberties, families, names — anything of lot, only not souls — but promises it shall not rest: God cuts work short in righteousness, makes way of escape with temptation, lest over-long trial tempt righteous to put hands to iniquity, grow weary, distrust promise; God proportions trials to strength and strength to trials, for elect's sake days shortened. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

4 COMMENTARY: Henry prays for sincere and constant: Do good, O Lord, to those that are good — supplication for all saints in faith, those who do well shall be well dealt with, as they should be shall be as they would be provided upright in heart, really as good as they seem; with upright God shows upright; not perfect sinless spotless, but sincere honest; promises should quicken prayers. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

5 COMMENTARY: Henry predicts, not prays, ruin of hypocrites and deserters turning aside for fear of rod to crooked indirect ways or growing worse and more daring: God sends away, casts out, leads forth with workers of iniquity — portion with worst sinners, doom same as lifelong walkers and more grievous, led as prisoners to execution, former righteousness not mentioned; but peace upon Israel. — Matthew Henry (Complete)
