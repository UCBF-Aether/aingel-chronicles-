Psalm 120

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Henry records deliverance by prayer from lying lips: distressed by flatterers smiling and kissing while aiming to smite under the fifth rib, and by forged accusations blackening names and endangering lives — the lot of innocent and excellent, David typing Christ — he cried to God who holds hearts and can bridle tongues, and was heard, enemies baffled at last. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

2 COMMENTARY: Henry prays deliverance of soul from lying lips and deceitful tongues: having prayed earnestly to be kept from lying himself and hating it heartily, he may with more confidence pray to be kept from being belied by others and from its ill consequences, appealing to him with power over bad men's consciences. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

3 COMMENTARY: Henry addresses the false tongue itself for conscience-awakening: consider what shall be given thee by the righteous Judge — surely sinners durst not act so if they knew the end thereof, for God will preserve his people from this mischievous generation and reckon with enemies. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

4 COMMENTARY: Henry threatens sharp arrows of the Almighty and juniper coals: wrath flying swiftly like arrows, striking ere aware, piercing strongest armour and hardest heart, with vehement heat keeping fire very long even when seeming out — the portion of the false tongue, for all loving and making a lie share the lake burning eternally. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

5 COMMENTARY: Henry laments ill neighbourhood in Mesech and Kedar figures: not literally dwelling so far off, but among rude, barbarous, litigious people like those shepherds — forced from God's ordinances, sojourning prolonged from his resting-place, a good man never at home banished from means of grace and communion. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

6 COMMENTARY: Henry counts long dwelling with peace-haters a grievous burden: cast and kept among those he hopes to be for ever separated from, like Lot in Sodom, vexatious and quarrelsome neighbours making the closet psalm's complaint — yet he will not love peace less for seeking it in vain. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

7 COMMENTARY: Henry ends with the climax ascent, I am for peace: though he speaks peace, they are for war, and for his love they are adversaries, as Christ's enemies stoned him for good words and works — so we must not think it strange, nor be overcome of evil, but still try to overcome evil with good. — Matthew Henry (Complete)
