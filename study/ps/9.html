Study Notes — Psalms

Psalm 9

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: David praises with his whole heart and shows forth all God's marvellous works: praise is wholehearted and specific, recounting deeds, not vague feeling. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: He is glad and rejoices in God, singing praise to the name of the Most High: joy and song belong together in worship. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: His enemies are turned back, falling and perishing at God's presence: their defeat is ascribed to God's appearing, not to David's sword alone. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: God has maintained David's right and cause, sitting on the throne judging right: the Judge has heard the case and decided justly. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: The heathen are rebuked, the wicked destroyed, their name put out for ever: judgment erases even the memory of proud power. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: The enemy's destructions are ended perpetually, their cities destroyed, their memorial perished: what they built to last is forgotten. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: But the Lord endures for ever and has prepared his throne for judgment: human empires pass, the divine throne does not. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: He judges the world in righteousness and ministers judgment in uprightness: the standard is God's own character, applied without partiality. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: The Lord is a refuge for the oppressed, a refuge in times of trouble: the afflicted have somewhere to flee, and it is a person, not a place. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: Those who know God's name will trust him, for he has not forsaken those who seek him: knowledge of God, proved by experience, breeds trust. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: Sing praises to the Lord who dwells in Zion and declare his doings among the people: worship in Zion must become witness to the nations. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: When God makes inquisition for blood he remembers the afflicted and does not forget the cry of the humble: murder and oppression do not escape his account. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: David now pleads for mercy in his own trouble from haters, asking the God who lifts him from the gates of death to consider him. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: That he may show forth God's praise in the gates of Zion and rejoice in salvation: deliverance is sought so that it may be publicly praised. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: The heathen sink in the pit they made, their own foot taken in the hidden net: their secret devices trap themselves. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: The Lord is known by the judgment he executes, and the wicked is snared in his own hands' work: God's character becomes visible in retribution. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: The wicked shall be turned into hell, and all nations that forget God: forgetfulness of God is itself the road to ruin. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

18 COMMENTARY: Yet the needy shall not always be forgotten, nor the poor's expectation perish for ever: delay is not abandonment. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

19 COMMENTARY: Arise, O Lord, let not man prevail; let the heathen be judged in God's sight, that power may be measured against justice. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

20 COMMENTARY: Put them in fear, that the nations may know themselves to be but men: the lesson of judgment is humility — proud nations are mortal after all. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
