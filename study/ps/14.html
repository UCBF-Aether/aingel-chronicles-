Psalm 14

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: The fool says in his heart, There is no God: this is not reasoned atheism but a practical denial in the heart, and it issues in corrupt, abominable works, with none doing good. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: The Lord looks down from heaven on the children of men, to see if any understand and seek God: God searches, and the search exposes the universal failure. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: All have gone aside and together become filthy; none does good, no, not one: Paul cites this passage in Romans to prove the common guilt of mankind. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: Have workers of iniquity no knowledge, who eat up God's people as bread and do not call on the Lord? Oppression of the godly and neglect of prayer go together, and both betray ignorance of God. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: There they were in great fear, for God is in the generation of the righteous: persecutors who feared no man are seized by fear when they perceive God with his people. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: You have shamed the counsel of the poor, because the Lord is his refuge: mocking the poor's trust is futile, for their refuge is God himself. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: Oh that salvation for Israel were come out of Zion! When the Lord brings back his people's captivity, Jacob shall rejoice and Israel be glad: the psalm ends longing for the deliverance that God alone brings from Zion. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
