Study Notes — Psalms

Psalm 46

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: God is our refuge and strength, a very present help in trouble: refuge, strength, and a help very present — not distant — in trouble are the threefold ground of fearlessness. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: Therefore we will not fear, though the earth be removed, and though the mountains be carried into the midst of the sea: even earth's removal and mountains in the sea cannot move those whose refuge is God. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: Though the waters roar and be troubled, though the mountains shake with the swelling thereof: roaring, troubled waters and shaking mountains complete the picture of cosmic upheaval met without fear. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: There is a river, whose streams shall make glad the city of God, the holy place of the tabernacles of the Most High: against the roaring sea stands a gladdening river — God's quiet provision for his city. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: God is in the midst of her; she shall not be moved; God shall help her, and that right early: God's indwelling presence makes the city unmovable, and his help comes early, at dawn. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: The heathen raged, the kingdoms were moved; he uttered his voice, the earth melted: raging nations and moved kingdoms dissolve at a single utterance — the earth melts before his voice. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: The Lord of hosts is with us; the God of Jacob is our refuge: the refrain names him Lord of hosts, with us, and God of Jacob, our refuge — covenant and armies together. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: Come, behold the works of the Lord, what desolations he has made in the earth: the invitation is to behold desolations — God's judgments on the earth are to be seen, not hidden. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: He makes wars to cease to the end of the earth; he breaks the bow, cuts the spear in sunder, and burns the chariot in the fire: war's weapons are broken, cut, and burned — peace is made by disarming, to earth's end. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: Be still, and know that I am God; I will be exalted among the heathen, I will be exalted in the earth: stillness is commanded as the way to know God, whose exaltation among nations and in the earth is certain. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: The Lord of hosts is with us; the God of Jacob is our refuge: the refrain returns, closing the psalm as it steadied its middle — presence and refuge are the last words. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
