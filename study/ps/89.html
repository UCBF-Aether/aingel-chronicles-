Study Notes — Psalms

Psalm 89

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Singing mercies forever in the worst of times is Henry's discipline: complaints turned into thanksgivings, because mercies are inexhaustible and truth inviolable even when dark providences cannot be reconciled with them. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

2 COMMENTARY: Mercy built up forever and faithfulness established in heavens picture a house still rising and a promise above earthly change: heavens may be clouded by earthly vapours but cannot be touched or changed. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

3 COMMENTARY: The covenant is made with David as God's chosen and his servant, and Henry draws two comforts from that naming: God is not changeable to recede from his own choice, nor unrighteous to cast off one who served him. It was confirmed with an oath, and its blessings were entailed on the family — seed established for ever and throne built up to all generations — a perpetuity that finds its accomplishment only in Christ. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

4 COMMENTARY: Thy seed will I establish for ever, and build up thy throne to all generations, is the perpetuity of the covenant: two things encourage the psalmist to build his faith on this covenant — first, the ratification of it; it was confirmed with an oath: the Lord has sworn, and he will not repent. Second, the perpetuity of it; the blessings of the covenant were not only secured to David himself, but were entailed on his family; it was promised that his family should continue — Thy seed will I establish for ever, so that David shall not want a son to reign — and that it should continue a royal family — I will build up thy throne to all generations, to all the generations of time. This has its accomplishment only in Christ, of the seed of David, who lives for ever, to whom God has given the throne of his father David, and of the increase of whose government and peace there shall be no end. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

5 COMMENTARY: Heavens praising wonders and saints praising faithfulness are two choirs: angels, best acquainted with God's works, admire them most, while the congregation owes its all to the faithfulness it praises. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

6 COMMENTARY: None in heaven comparable, none among mighty sons likened: nobles may be princes' peers, but between God and angels there is no parity to challenge reverence due to him alone. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

7 COMMENTARY: Greatly feared in the assembly and reverenced by all about him guard against familiarity breeding contempt: solemn assembly adds awe to secret approach, and acceptable service requires reverence and godly fear. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

8 COMMENTARY: Who is a strong Lord like God? No angel and no earthly potentate is comparable, with an arm or a voice like his. Henry reads the faithfulness round about him two ways: his attending angels are all faithful, and in everything he does, on all sides, he approves himself faithful to his word — unlike men, who are often least careful to keep their word precisely when most able to break it, while God can do everything and yet will never do an unjust thing. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

9 COMMENTARY: Thou rulest the raging of the sea: when the waves thereof arise, thou stillest them, is the command God has of the most ungovernable creatures: thou rulest the raging of the sea, than which nothing is more frightful or threatening, nor more out of the power of man to give check to; it can swell no higher, roll no further, beat no harder, continue no longer, nor do any more hurt, than God suffers it. When the waves thereof arise thou canst immediately hush them asleep, still them, and make them quiet, and turn the storm into a calm. This coming in here as an act of omnipotence, what manner of man then was the Lord Jesus, whom the winds and seas obeyed? — Matthew Henry (Complete)

10 COMMENTARY: Rahab broken as one slain and enemies scattered with a strong arm recall Egypt as the proud enemy type: Henry notes God sometimes scatters rather than slays outright, sending enemies abroad as monuments of justice. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

11 COMMENTARY: Heavens, earth, world and fulness founded by God make ownership the ground of trust: the founder claims tenements and tenants alike, so fear of man has no footing against such a proprietor. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

12 COMMENTARY: The north and the south thou hast created them: Tabor and Hermon shall rejoice in thy name, is God's incontestable property in all the creatures of the upper and lower world: he specifies, first, the remotest parts of the world, the north and south, the countries that lie under the two poles, which are uninhabited and little known: Thou hast created them, and therefore knowest them, takest care of them, and hast tributes of praise from them. The north is said to be hung over the empty place; yet what fulness there is there God is the owner of it. Second, the highest parts of the world. He mentions the two highest hills in Canaan — Tabor and Hermon, one lying to the west, the other to the east; these shall rejoice in thy name, for they are under the care of his providence, and they produce offerings for his altar. The little hills are said to rejoice in their own fruitfulness. Tabor is commonly supposed to be that high mountain in Galilee on the top of which Christ was transfigured; and then indeed it might be said to rejoice in that voice which was there heard, This is my beloved Son. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

13 COMMENTARY: God's arm is mighty and strong both to save his people and to destroy their enemies, and none can either resist its force or bear its weight. His right hand is high to reach the highest, even those who set their nests among the stars, and it is exalted in what he has done, having signalized his power in thousands of instances. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

14 COMMENTARY: Righteousness and judgment are the habitation of God's throne: none of his dictates or decrees ever varied from equity and wisdom, and his counsels from eternity and their consequences to eternity are all justice and judgment. Mercy and truth go before his face as harbingers — mercy in promising, truth in performing, truth in being as good as his word and mercy in being better. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

15 COMMENTARY: Blessed is the people that know the joyful sound: they shall walk, O Lord, in the light of thy countenance, is the blessedness of the Israel of God: glorious discoveries are made to them, and glad tidings of good brought to them; they hear, they know, the joyful sound. This may allude, first, to the shout of a victorious army, the shout of a king. Israel have the tokens of God's presence with them in their wars; the sound of the going in the top of the mulberry-trees was indeed a joyful sound; and they often returned making the earth ring with their songs of triumph; these were joyful sounds. Or, second, to the sound that was made over the sacrifices and on the solemn feast-day. This was the happiness of Israel, that they had among them the free and open profession of God's holy religion, and abundance of joy in their sacrifices. Or, third, to the sound of the jubilee-trumpet; a joyful sound it was to servants and debtors, to whom it proclaimed release. The gospel is indeed a joyful sound, a sound of victory, of liberty, of communion with God, and the sound of abundance of rain; blessed are the people that hear it, and know it, and bid it welcome. And special tokens of God's favour are granted them: they shall walk, O Lord! in the light of thy countenance; they shall govern themselves by thy directions, shall be guided by the eye; and they shall delight themselves in thy consolations. They shall have the favour of God; they shall know that they have it, and it shall be continual matter of joy and rejoicing to them. They shall go through all the exercises of a holy life under the powerful influences of God's lovingkindness, which shall make their duty pleasant to them and make them sincere in it, aiming at this, as their end, to be accepted of the Lord. We then walk in the light of the Lord when we fetch all our comforts from God's favour and are very careful to keep ourselves in his love. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

16 COMMENTARY: Rejoicing all the day in God's name rests on being exalted in his righteousness, not in any righteousness of our own: Christ's righteousness is a clothing both for dignity and for defence, lifting his people out of danger and into honour. Those who rejoice in Christ have enough to counterbalance grievances and silence griefs, so their joy is full and constant, and it is their duty to rejoice evermore. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

17 COMMENTARY: God himself is the glory of his people's strength — he is their strength, and it is their glory that he is so, and what they glory in. In his favour their horn is exalted, and the horn denotes beauty, plenty and power: those made accepted in the Beloved enjoy the greatest preferment men are capable of in this world, to be God's favourites. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

18 COMMENTARY: Our shield is of the Lord and our king is from the Holy One of Israel: if God be our ruler he will be our defender, and who then can harm us? Henry takes it both ways — God himself erected Israel's bulwarks and nominated her king, and he was himself a wall of fire round about them, the Holy One who is author and centre of their holy religion. Christ is that Holy One, born King of the Jews, and in nothing was Israel more blessed. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

19 COMMENTARY: Vision spoken to the Holy One is Nathan's vision first, then all prophets concerning Christ: help laid on one mighty and chosen from the people means help treasured in him for fallen man, not merely help given to him. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

20 COMMENTARY: God found David his servant and anointed him: a king of God's own choosing, mighty and fit for business, chosen out of the people, not out of princes but out of shepherds, found out, exalted, given help, and anointed. Henry applies it especially to Christ — mighty to save to the uttermost, Son of God, compassionate to the tempted, chosen from among men, bone of our bone, provided as a Saviour of God's own finding, and anointed with the Spirit without measure, Messiah and Christ. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

21 COMMENTARY: Hand established and arm strengthening promise commission matched with forces: Christ's hard work and hard usage meet a God who undertakes to strengthen, and no good work miscarries in such a hand. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

22 COMMENTARY: The enemy shall not exact upon him, nor the son of wickedness afflict him, because the peace-breaker who set himself against the Peacemaker could only reach to bruise his heel. Satan and death thought to gain advantage when Christ became surety for our debt, but once he satisfied the demands of God's justice they could not exact upon him — the prince of this world came, but had nothing in him. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

23 COMMENTARY: But I will beat down his foes before his face, and plague them that hate him, is the chosen one's victory over his enemies, that they should not encroach upon him: I will bend down his foes before his face; the prince of this world shall be cast out, principalities and powers spoiled, and he shall be the death of death itself, and the destruction of the grave. Some apply this to the ruin which God brought upon the Jewish nation, that persecuted Christ and put him to death. But all Christ's enemies, who hate him and will not have him to reign over them, shall be brought forth and slain before him. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

24 COMMENTARY: But my faithfulness and my mercy shall be with him: and in my name shall his horn be exalted, is the chosen one as the great trustee of the covenant between God and men, that God would be gracious and true to us: my faithfulness and my mercy shall be with him. They were with David; God continued merciful to him, and so approved himself faithful. They were with Christ; God made good all his promises to him. But that is not all; God's mercy to us, and his faithfulness to us, are with Christ; he is not only pleased with him, but with us in him; and it is in him that all the promises of God are yea and amen. So that if any poor sinners hope for benefit by the faithfulness and mercy of God, let them know it is with Christ; it is lodged in his hand, and to him they must apply for it. My mercy will I keep for him, to be disposed of by him, for evermore; in the channel of Christ's mediation all the streams of divine goodness will for ever run. Therefore it is the mercy of our Lord Jesus Christ which we look for unto eternal life; and, as the mercy of God flows to us through him, so the promise of God is, through him, firm to us: my covenant shall stand fast with him, both the covenant of redemption made with him and the covenant of grace made with us in him. The new covenant is therefore always new, and firmly established, because it is lodged in the hands of a Mediator. And this redounds to the everlasting honour of the Lord Jesus, that to him the great cause between God and man is entirely referred and the Father has committed all judgment to him, that all men might honour him; therefore it is here said, In my name shall his horn be exalted; this shall be his glory, that God's name is in him, and that he acts in God's name. As the Father gave me commandment, so I do. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

25 COMMENTARY: Hand in sea and right hand in rivers enlarge David's borders in type and the Messiah's in full: Henry hears the kingdoms of the world becoming the Lord's and his Christ's, with isles waiting for his law. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

26 COMMENTARY: He shall cry, Thou art my Father, and own God as his God and the rock of his salvation. Henry is sure Christ did so in the days of his flesh, in agony and on the cross — holy Father, righteous Father, Father forgive them, into thy hands I commend my spirit — looking to God as one he perfectly obeyed, who would bear him up and bear him out, making him more than a conqueror, a complete Saviour who endured the cross despising the shame, knowing he should be justified and glorified. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

27 COMMENTARY: Made firstborn and higher than the kings of the earth is, Henry insists, Christ's prerogative alone: firstborn of every creature and heir of all things, with all power given him in heaven and earth, far higher and more honourable than earthly kings, for he is King of kings, with angels, authorities and powers made subject to him. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

28 COMMENTARY: Mercy kept for him for evermore means God's mercy to us is lodged in Christ's hand, to be disposed of by him, and in the channel of his mediation all streams of divine goodness will for ever run — it is the mercy of our Lord Jesus Christ we look for unto eternal life. Likewise the covenant stands fast with him, both the covenant of redemption made with him and the covenant of grace made with us in him, always new and firmly established because it stands on a Mediator. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

29 COMMENTARY: Seed enduring for ever and throne as the days of heaven is read two ways: for David, successors who continued in imperial dignity as long as Judah was a kingdom, never passing to another family, until the Son of David came whose throne endures for ever; for Christ, his spiritual seed, all believers, the children God gave him, who shall endure in a glorified state when time and days are no more, his kingdom of grace through all ages and of glory to endless ages. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

30 COMMENTARY: If his children forsake God's law, Henry puts the case plainly so we will not be offended when it becomes fact: there will be much amiss in Christ's subjects, spots that are the spots of God's children, corruptions in the bowels of the church breaking out. The case is stated precisely so it can be reconciled with the stability of the covenant and we can be assured of that notwithstanding. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

31 COMMENTARY: Breaking statutes and not keeping commandments is the commission side of the same failure — forsaking the law by omissions in verse 30, breaking statutes by commissions here. Henry's point is that the children may profane and pollute God's statutes, yet that does not license the conclusion that God will profane his covenant; the same word is turned back on the promise in verse 33. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

32 COMMENTARY: Visiting transgression with the rod and iniquity with stripes is correction, not destruction: a rod, not an axe or sword, the rod of men used in correcting children, yielding the peaceable fruit of righteousness. It is a rod in God's own hand — wise, knowing what he does, gracious, doing what is best — and never felt except when there is great need, when God's honour must be vindicated and his people humbled. Being related to Christ does not excuse them from account; 'you only have I known, therefore I will punish you.' — Matthew Henry (Complete)

33 COMMENTARY: Nevertheless, lovingkindness shall not be totally and finally taken away, nor faithfulness fail: notwithstanding provocations, the covenant shall not be broken. Henry's key sentence is that afflictions are not only consistent with covenant-love but flow from it — the seed may be cast down but is not cast off, chastened but not disinherited — and favour continues both for Christ's sake, in whom mercy is laid up, and for the covenant's sake, since God will not break what he swore. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

34 COMMENTARY: My covenant will I not break, nor alter the thing that is gone out of my lips, is the continuance of Christ's kingdom made certain by the inviolable promise and oath of God, notwithstanding the sins of his subjects: Nevertheless, my kindness will I not totally and finally take from him. Notwithstanding their provocations, yet my covenant shall not be broken. Note, afflictions are not only consistent with covenant-love, but to the people of God they flow from it. Though David's seed be chastened, it does not follow that they are disinherited; they may be cast down, but they are not cast off. God's favour is continued to his people, first, for Christ's sake; in him the mercy is laid up for us, and God says, I will not take it from him, I will not lie unto David. We are unworthy, but he is worthy. Second, for the covenant's sake: my faithfulness shall not fail, my covenant will I not break. It was supposed that they had broken God's statutes, profaned and polluted them, so the word signifies; But, says God, I will not break, I will not profane and pollute, my covenant; it is the same word. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

35 COMMENTARY: Sworn once by his holiness, God will not lie unto David: in swearing by his holiness, that darling attribute, he swore by himself, for he will as soon cease to be as be otherwise than holy. His swearing once is enough; he needs not swear again, for his word and oath are two immutable things — and as Christ was made priest by an oath, so he was made king by an oath, both unchangeable. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

36 COMMENTARY: Seed established for ever and throne as the sun before him is secured by the sun and moon as faithful witnesses in heaven of the Creator's wisdom, power and goodness, continuing while time lasts, of which they are the measurers. Christ's seed shall be established for ever as lights of the world while the world stands, and when it is at an end, as lights shining in the firmament of the Father. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

37 COMMENTARY: It shall be established for ever as the moon, and as a faithful witness in heaven, is the church's continuance as long as sun and moon endure: that which is said and sworn is that God will have a church in the world as long as sun and moon endure. The sun and moon are faithful witnesses in heaven of the wisdom, power, and goodness of the Creator, and shall continue while time lasts, which they are the measurers of; but the seed of Christ shall be established for ever, as lights of the world while the world stands, to shine in it, and, when it is at an end, they shall be established lights shining in the firmament of the Father. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

38 COMMENTARY: But thou hast cast off and abhorred, thou hast been wroth with thine anointed — the psalm now turns to present ruin against past promise. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

39 COMMENTARY: Covenant made void and crown profaned to the ground are the complaint's sharpest edge: Henry learns from it how sin stains noble families, and how easily promised honour is mistaken for external glory, a mistake inexcusable once Christ says his kingdom is not of this world. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

40 COMMENTARY: Thou hast broken down all his hedges; thou hast brought his strong holds to ruin, is the house of David exposed and made a prey to all the neighbours, who insulted over that ancient and honourable family: thou hast broken down all his hedges — all those things that were a defence to them, and particularly that hedge of protection which they thought God's covenant and promise had made about them — and thou hast made even his strong-holds a ruin, so that they were rather a reproach to them than any shelter; and then, all that pass by the way spoil him and make an easy prey of him. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

41 COMMENTARY: All that pass by the way spoil him: he is a reproach to his neighbours, is the insolent talk of the enemies over the fallen house: he is a reproach to his neighbours, who triumph in his fall from so great a degree of honour. Nay, every one helps forward the calamity. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

42 COMMENTARY: Thou hast set up the right hand of his adversaries; thou hast made all his enemies to rejoice, is God permitting the enemies' power: thou hast set up the right hand of his adversaries, not only given them power, but inclined them to turn their power this way. If the enemies of the church lift up their hand against it, we must see God setting up their hand; for they could have no power unless it were given them from above. But, when God does permit them to do mischief to his church, it pleases them: thou hast made all his enemies to rejoice; and this is for thy glory, that those who hate thee should have the pleasure to see the tears and troubles of those that love thee. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

43 COMMENTARY: The edge of the sword turned back and the king not made to stand in battle is disability from God himself: the sword made blunt so it cannot do execution, and worse, the edge of his spirit taken off and his courage gone. Henry's sober doctrine is that the spirit of men is what the Father and former of spirits makes them, and no one stands with strength or resolution further than God upholds him — sad for the church when those who should stand up for it cannot stand. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

44 COMMENTARY: Glory made to cease and throne cast to the ground is honour lost and laid in the dust: the sacred crown cast down to be trampled, not only a king dethroned but a period put to the kingdom. Thrones and crowns are tottering things, often laid in the dust — but against that, Henry sets a crown of glory reserved for Christ's spiritual seed which fadeth not away. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

45 COMMENTARY: The days of his youth hast thou shortened: thou hast covered him with shame, is the house upon the brink of an inglorious exit: the days of his youth hast thou shortened; it is ready to be cut off, like a young man in the flower of his age. This seems to intimate that the psalm was penned in Rehoboam's time, when the house of David was but in the days of its youth, and yet waxed old and began to decay already. Thus it was covered with shame, and it was turned very much to its reproach that a family which, in the first and second reign, looked so great, and made such a figure, should, in the third, dwindle and look so little as the house of David did in Rehoboam's time. But it may be applied to the captivity in Babylon, which, in comparison with what was expected, was but the day of the youth of that kingdom. However, the kings then had remarkably the days of their youth shortened, for it was in the days of their youth, when they were about thirty years old, that Jehoiachin and Zedekiah were carried captives to Babylon. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

46 COMMENTARY: How long wilt thou hide thyself, for ever, and shall thy wrath burn like fire? What grieved them most was that God himself, as one displeased, did not appear by his prophets to comfort them nor by his providences to deliver them, but kept them long in the dark, as if in eternal night. Henry sharpens the plea: God not only hid himself but seemed set against them, and the question — shall wrath never be put out, what is hell but wrath burning for ever — is asked of the lot of God's anointed. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

47 COMMENTARY: Remember how short my time is, and wherefore hast thou made all men in vain? Henry reads it both as truth and as temptation: if the ancient lovingkindnesses relating to another life are forgotten, man with his noble powers and vast designs would indeed seem made in vain — but God would not make man in vain, so the plea returns to those lovingkindnesses. Short lives and long afflictions do not make men vain if they still glorify God, keep communion with him and get to heaven; those who neither serve nor enjoy him are made vain by themselves, not by God, who made all things for himself. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

48 COMMENTARY: What man is he that liveth, and shall not see death? shall he deliver his soul from the hand of the grave? is the plea of the universality and unavoidableness of death: What man — what strong man, so the word is — is he that liveth and shall not see death? The king himself, of the house of David, is not exempted from the sentence, from the stroke. Lord, since he is under a fatal necessity of dying, let not his whole life be made thus miserable. Shall he deliver his soul from the hand of the grave? No, he shall not when his time has come. Let him not therefore be delivered into the hand of the grave by the miseries of a dying life, till his time shall come. We must learn here that death is the end of all men; our eyes must shortly be closed to see death; there is no discharge from that war, nor will any bail be taken to save us from the prison of the grave. It concerns us therefore to make sure a happiness on the other side of death and the grave, that, when we fail, we may be received into everlasting habitations. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

49 COMMENTARY: Where are thy former lovingkindnesses, sworn to David in thy truth? The plea turns on God's unchangeableness: wilt thou fail of doing what thou promised, undo what thou hast done, art thou not still the same — why then may we not have the benefit of the former sure mercies of David? God's unchangeableness and faithfulness assure us he will not cast off those he has chosen and covenanted with. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

50 COMMENTARY: Remember, Lord, the reproach of thy servants; how I do bear in my bosom the reproach of all the mighty people, is the last plea, taken from the insolence of the enemies and the indignity done to God's anointed: Remember, Lord, the reproach, and let it be rolled away from us and returned upon our enemies. First, they were God's servants that were reproached, and the abuses done to them reflected upon their master, especially since it was for serving him that they were reproached. Second, the reproach cast upon God's servants was a very grievous burden to all that were concerned for the honour of God: I bear in my bosom the reproach of all the mighty people, and am even overwhelmed with it; it is what I lay much to heart and can scarcely keep up my spirits under the weight of. Third, they are thy enemies who do thus reproach us; and wilt thou not appear against them as such? Fourth, they have reproached the footsteps of thy anointed. They reflected upon all the steps which the king had taken in the course of his administration, tracked him in all his motions, that they might make invidious remarks upon everything he had said and done. Or, if we may apply it to Christ, the Lord's Messiah, they reproached the Jews with his footsteps, the slowness of his coming. They have reproached the delays of the Messiah; so Dr. Hammond. They called him, He that should come; but, because he had not yet come, because he did not now come to deliver them out of the hands of their enemies, when they had none to deliver them, they told them he would never come, they must give over looking for him. The scoffers of the latter days do, in like manner, reproach the footsteps of the Messiah when they ask, Where is the promise of his coming? The reproaching of the footsteps of the anointed some refer to the serpent's bruising the heel of the seed of the woman, or to the sufferings of Christ's followers, who tread in his footsteps, and are reproached for his name's sake. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

51 COMMENTARY: Enemies reproaching the footsteps of God's anointed tracked the king in all his motions to make invidious remarks on everything he said and did, and the reproach borne in the bosom was a grievous burden, overwhelming the psalmist. Henry applies it to Christ as well: they reproached the Jews with the Messiah's footsteps, the slowness of his coming — 'he that should come' had not come to deliver them, so they said he would never come — just as latter-day scoffers ask where the promise of his coming is, and just as the serpent bruised the heel of the seed of the woman. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

52 COMMENTARY: Blessed be the Lord for evermore. Amen, and Amen — praise closes even a psalm of complaint. — Matthew Henry (Complete)
