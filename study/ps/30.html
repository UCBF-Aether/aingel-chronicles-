Study Notes — Psalms

Psalm 30

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: I will extol you, Lord, for you have lifted me up and not made my foes rejoice over me: exaltation of God follows lifting from the pit, and the foes' disappointed joy is part of the praise. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: Lord my God, I cried to you, and you healed me: prayer and healing are joined as cry and answer, whether the sickness was of body or of soul. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: Lord, you brought up my soul from the grave and kept me alive, that I should not go down to the pit: near-death becomes a resurrection in miniature, credited wholly to God. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: Sing to the Lord, you his saints, and give thanks at the remembrance of his holiness: the saints' song is tuned to God's holiness, the attribute that makes his mercy wonderful. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: His anger endures but a moment, in his favour is life; weeping may endure for a night, but joy comes in the morning: God's anger is brief, his favour is life itself, and sorrow has a morning appointed. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: In my prosperity I said, I shall never be moved: prosperity bred the false security that trouble then exposed. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: Lord, by your favour you made my mountain stand strong; you hid your face, and I was troubled: strength was favour, not nature, and the hiding of God's face troubled him more than any enemy. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: I cried to you, Lord, and made supplication: the troubled man returns to the same prayer that healed him before. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: What profit is there in my blood, when I go down to the pit? Shall the dust praise you or declare your truth? He pleads that a dead man cannot praise — let him live, that praise may continue. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: Hear, Lord, and have mercy on me; Lord, be my helper: hearing, mercy, and help are asked together, as one gift. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: You have turned my mourning into dancing, put off my sackcloth, and girded me with gladness: the exchange is total — mourning garments replaced by gladness itself. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: To the end that my glory may sing praise to you and not be silent; Lord my God, I will give thanks to you for ever: the purpose of deliverance is endless praise, and the psalm vows it. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
