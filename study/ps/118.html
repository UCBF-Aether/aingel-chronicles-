Study Notes — Psalms

Psalm 118

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Henry says David's heart was full of God's goodness — he loved to think and speak of it, soliciting praise for God and comfort for others — for God is good not only in himself but good to you, his mercy running in never-failing streams parallel with eternity's longest line. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

2 COMMENTARY: Henry calls Israel to confess from its own experience what it was called to trust in Psalm 115: priests and people alike join one thankful song, owning they have had experience of mercy all their days and confide in it for everlasting good. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

3 COMMENTARY: Henry extends the same call to Aaron's house: priests, like people, must own God's goodness and join the song, for the thanksgivings of all who truly fear the Lord please him as much as Israel's or Aaron's. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

4 COMMENTARY: Henry includes all who fear the Lord, Jews and proselytes: if they can say no more, let them say his mercy endures for ever, encouraging themselves to trust him by what they have experienced. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

5 COMMENTARY: Henry says God answered with enlargements, doing more than David could ask — enlarging his heart in prayer and giving more largely than desired — setting him in a large place with room to bestir, enjoy and thrive, the more comfortable for coming out of distress. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

6 COMMENTARY: Henry grounds fearlessness in God's partisanship: the Lord is on my side as a righteous God espousing a righteous cause, so if we are for him he is for us; man is a depending creature of limited, subordinate power, and can do nothing but what God permits or turns to good. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

7 COMMENTARY: Henry reads the Lord taking his part with his helpers as God being all in all to them and to David, without whom neither he nor any friend could help him — and from this he is sure he shall see his desire upon haters, their designs defeated. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

8 COMMENTARY: Henry judges trust wiser, more comfortable and safer, with more reason and better speed, than confidence in man: devotion to God's guidance and government is better security than all kings undertaking to protect us. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

9 COMMENTARY: Henry repeats the point for princes: even the highest potentates are a poorer security than trust in the Lord, whose wisdom, power and goodness are entire dependence. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

10 COMMENTARY: Henry pictures adjacent nations — Philistines, Moabites, Syrians, Ammonites — confederate to cut off succours from newly enthroned David, yet in the Lord's name he will destroy them, going forth depending on his strength, by his warrant, for his glory, as against Goliath and as Christ triumphed over darkness. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

11 COMMENTARY: Henry stresses the repetition: compassed about, yea compassed about — enemies virulent, violent and for a time prevalent, rallying when disordered and pushing their design, yet still to be destroyed in the Lord's name. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

12 COMMENTARY: Henry likens enemies to bees and thorn-fire: numerous, noisy and vexatious, they sting to their own destruction like the bee losing life with its sting, and blaze furiously a while then go out, unable to do the mischief threatened — fury and fool's laughter alike to be pitied, not feared. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

13 COMMENTARY: Henry distinguishes thrust from chastening: the enemy thrust sore, Satan tempting to cast us from excellency, that we might fall into sin and ruin, yet the Lord helped David keep his feet and ground — without that help spiritual enemies would long have ruined us. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

14 COMMENTARY: Henry makes the Lord both strength and song: without him we are weak and sad, on him we stay for doing and suffering and solace ourselves expressing joy and easing grief; God is sometimes strength when not song, but when both, he has become not only Saviour but salvation itself. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

15 COMMENTARY: Henry hears rejoicing in the tabernacles of the righteous — mean, movable dwellings, no continuing city — yet more comfortable than wicked palaces, for where religion rules there is salvation, cause for continual joy, and the voice of rejoicing in daily family praise. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

16 COMMENTARY: Henry exalts God's right hand doing valiantly and being exalted, itself exalting David: divine power acts vigorously and victoriously, with spirit as well as strength, and when it does valiantly for salvation it ought to be exalted in praise. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

17 COMMENTARY: Henry makes life's purpose declaration: I shall not die by enemies' hands but live a monument of mercy and power, making it life's business to praise God, for it is not worth living for any other purpose than to declare his works for his honour and others' encouragement. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

18 COMMENTARY: Henry again distinguishes chastening from abandonment: men thrust for destruction, God chastens with a Father's love for instruction and holiness, and though he seems to turn men to destruction he says Return — chastened, not killed, as dying yet living, in Paul's phrase. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

19 COMMENTARY: Henry seeks humble admission: temple gates are gates of righteousness, shut against the uncircumcised, so those entering communion must become suitors, and once admitted go in to praise — our business within God's gates, longing till heaven's gates open for endless praise. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

20 COMMENTARY: Henry sees the gate opened on knocking: this is the Lord's appointed gate, into which the righteous shall enter — and some understand Christ himself, the way, the door of the sheep, by whom alone the righteous enter into God's righteousness. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

21 COMMENTARY: Henry hears thanks for a favour already granted in prospect: distant saints praising God because he heard Old Testament prayers for Messiah's coming and would be their salvation. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

22 COMMENTARY: Henry applies the rejected stone principally to Christ, with David's preferment as shadow: Saul's court refused the stone, as Jewish rulers refused to own Messiah or build faith on him, trampling and stumbling at him — disgrace to Christ, ruin to rejecters, for rejecters of Christ are rejected of God. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

23 COMMENTARY: Henry marvels at God's hand in both humiliation and exaltation: the rejected stone becomes headstone of the corner, chief foundation uniting Jew and Gentile and chief top-stone completing the building, pre-eminent as author and finisher of faith — the Lord's doing, product of his counsel, wonderful as his name. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

24 COMMENTARY: Henry solemnizes the day the Lord has made: the whole gospel dispensation, the accepted time and day of salvation, a continual feast kept with joy — or fitly the Christian sabbath, made holy in remembrance of resurrection, when the rejected stone began exaltation, a day to rejoice in as his honour and our advantage. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

25 COMMENTARY: Henry hears hosanna as a loyal inauguration shout, Vivat rex: save me, let this Saviour be my ruler and protection, give me interest in his kingdom's prosperity, victory over lusts, grace conquering and to conquer — and preserve him, send his gospel prospering to pull down strongholds. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

26 COMMENTARY: Henry gives priests their part: bless the coming Prince — blessed is he that cometh in the name of the Lord, he that was to come and is to come again, commissioned to do God's will — welcome him into hearts, bless his ministers, and bless the people out of the Lord's house with spiritual blessings in heavenly places. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

27 COMMENTARY: Henry pairs privilege and duty: God the Lord has shown us light — knowledge of himself, lifted countenance, prospect of everlasting light — and calls for ourselves as living sacrifices, bound with cords to the altar's horns in prayer and praise, hearts fixed and not starting back. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

28 COMMENTARY: Henry closes with personal exaltation: Thou art my God, on whom I depend and to whom I am devoted, who ownest me and art owned by me, therefore I will praise thee and exalt thee in my heart and others'. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

29 COMMENTARY: Henry ends as he began, for God's glory is Alpha and Omega of all addresses: give thanks because in Christ God is good to man, his mercy endures for ever, the covenant made sure and everlasting — fit close to a prophecy of Christ, echoed by angels' glory in the highest and our hosannas. — Matthew Henry (Complete)
