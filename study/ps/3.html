Psalm 3

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: David, fleeing Absalom, counts his troubles increased: many rise against him, and the danger is personal and pressing. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: His enemies add taunts to threats, saying God will not help him — the hardest trial, when foes attack faith itself. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: But God is his shield, his glory, and the lifter of his head: defence, honour, and restored courage are all found in the Lord. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: His cry was heard from God's holy hill; past answers to prayer become the ground of present trust. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: He slept and woke sustained by the Lord: ordinary rest, in danger, is itself a testimony to God's keeping. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: Therefore ten thousands massed around him cannot terrify him; numbers count for nothing against God's presence. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: He prays for God to arise and save, recalling past blows struck against his enemies — the cheek smitten and the teeth of the wicked broken, their power to bite removed. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: Salvation belongs to the Lord alone, and his blessing rests on his people: the psalm ends where it must, in a general truth wider than David's own case. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
