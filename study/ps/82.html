Study Notes — Psalms

Psalm 82

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: God stands in the congregation of the mighty and judges among the gods — rulers sit in his court, not above it. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

2 COMMENTARY: How long will ye judge unjustly and accept the persons of the wicked? Partial justice is itself indicted. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

3 COMMENTARY: Magistrates are charged to use entrusted power for those who cannot pay for it: the poor, who have no money to make friends or fee counsel, and the fatherless, who have lost the guides of their youth. Henry makes the point sharp — men called gods must be fathers of the fatherless, as Job was, and a poor man's honest cause must suffer no prejudice from his poverty, however great his adversary. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

4 COMMENTARY: Deliver the poor and needy, rid them out of the hand of the wicked, is the magistrate's charge to rescue: they are to rescue those who have already fallen into the hands of oppressors and deliver them — rid them out of the hand of the wicked; avenge them of their adversary. These are clients whom there is nothing to be got by, no pay for serving them, no interest by obliging them; yet these are those whom judges and magistrates must concern themselves for, whose comfort they must consult and whose cause they must espouse. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

5 COMMENTARY: Ignorance here is willed, not innocent: judges who will not understand walk on in darkness because interest, not reason or justice, governs them, and public miscarriage becomes public mischief, with foundations out of course. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

6 COMMENTARY: I have said, Ye are gods, and all of you children of the Most High — their office was godlike in authority, not in nature. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

7 COMMENTARY: But ye shall die like men and fall like one of the princes; high office does not exempt from death and judgment. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

8 COMMENTARY: Reasoning with proud oppressors has proved useless, so the psalmist appeals upward: God must take to himself his great power and judge the earth. Henry reads this two ways at once — in providence, God has reserved power to himself and overrules wicked rulers, and in the Messiah, Christ shall inherit all nations and set these disorders right at his coming. — Matthew Henry (Complete)
