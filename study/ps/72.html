Study Notes — Psalms

Psalm 72

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Give the king your judgments, O God, and your righteousness unto the king's son: judgments and righteousness for the king and the king's son are prayed for — Solomon's accession psalm, and, in its fullest sense, Christ's reign. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: He shall judge your people with righteousness, and your poor with judgment: righteous judgment of the people, judgment for the poor — the king's first work is justice for the lowly. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: The mountains shall bring peace to the people, and the little hills, by righteousness: mountains and little hills bringing peace by righteousness — the whole land's fruitfulness follows just rule. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: He shall judge the poor of the people, he shall save the children of the needy, and shall break in pieces the oppressor: judging the poor, saving needy children, breaking the oppressor — the threefold work of the just king. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: They shall fear you as long as the sun and moon endure, throughout all generations: fear of God as long as sun and moon endure, through all generations — the reign's duration is cosmic. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: He shall come down like rain upon the mown grass, as showers that water the earth: rain on mown grass, showers watering earth — the king's coming is gentle, refreshing, and life-giving. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: In his days shall the righteous flourish, and abundance of peace so long as the moon endures: righteous flourishing, abundance of peace while the moon endures — prosperity and peace are the reign's climate. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: He shall have dominion also from sea to sea, and from the river unto the ends of the earth: sea-to-sea dominion, river to earth's ends — the kingdom is universal. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: They who dwell in the wilderness shall bow before him, and his enemies shall lick the dust: wilderness dwellers bowing, enemies licking dust — submission reaches the wildest and the proudest. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: The kings of Tarshish and of the isles shall bring presents; the kings of Sheba and Seba shall offer gifts: Tarshish and isles, Sheba and Seba bringing presents and gifts — distant kings pay tribute. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: Yea, all kings shall fall down before him; all nations shall serve him: all kings falling down, all nations serving — the tribute becomes universal worship and service. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: For he shall deliver the needy when he cries, the poor also, and him who has no helper: delivering the crying needy, the poor, the helperless — the reason for universal service is mercy to the helpless. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: He shall spare the poor and needy, and shall save the souls of the needy: sparing and saving the souls of the poor and needy — mercy reaches the soul, not only the body. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: He shall redeem their soul from deceit and violence, and precious shall their blood be in his sight: redemption from deceit and violence, blood precious in his sight — the oppressed are valued, not expendable. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: And he shall live, and to him shall be given of the gold of Sheba; prayer also shall be made for him continually, and daily shall he be praised: living, Sheba's gold given, continual prayer, daily praise — the king's life is sustained by gifts, prayer, and praise. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: There shall be a handful of corn in the earth upon the top of the mountains; the fruit thereof shall shake like Lebanon, and they of the city shall flourish like grass of the earth: a handful of corn on mountain tops fruiting like Lebanon, city dwellers flourishing like grass — abundance from small beginnings, under the just king. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: His name shall endure for ever; his name shall be continued as long as the sun, and men shall be blessed in him; all nations shall call him blessed: name enduring for ever, continued as long as the sun, men blessed in him, all nations calling him blessed — the Abrahamic promise fulfilled in the King. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

18 COMMENTARY: Blessed be the Lord God, the God of Israel, who only does wondrous things: the doxology blesses the Lord God of Israel, who only does wonders — the second book of Psalms closes here. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

19 COMMENTARY: And blessed be his glorious name for ever, and let the whole earth be filled with his glory; Amen, and Amen: glorious name blessed for ever, whole earth filled with glory, double Amen. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

20 COMMENTARY: The prayers of David the son of Jesse are ended: the colophon notes David's prayers ended — the second book closes with David's name, though later psalms bear his name too, as collected and ordered. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
