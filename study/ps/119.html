Study Notes — Psalms

Psalm 119

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Henry marks the happy as those who make God's will the rule of their whole conversation, walking within the law's hedges and pressing forward in its paths by rule, never at adventures, and undefiled — habitually sincere, really as good as they seem, rowing the way they look. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

2 COMMENTARY: Henry says the blessed keep God's testimonies as a treasure and covenant deposit, as the apple of the eye, carrying their comfort to another world and leaving the knowledge to those after them — and seek him with a single eye and whole heart, for a divided heart is faulty. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

3 COMMENTARY: Henry distinguishes clogging sin from drawing-out sin: the happy do no iniquity as a practice or trade, not allowing themselves in any sin as servants of sin, though conscious of much iniquity that clogs them in God's ways. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

4 COMMENTARY: Henry warns against the tempter's liberty lie, as with our first parents questioning Hath God said: we are bound to keep God's precepts diligently, making religion our rule and our business, carefully and constantly, at our peril. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

5 COMMENTARY: Henry turns command into prayer: O that my ways were directed — not only events ordered as furtherance, but hearts guided by the Spirit so eyes behold statutes and hearts keep them; the good man's desire exactly agrees with God's will, even our sanctification. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

6 COMMENTARY: Henry makes respect to all commandments the good man's character: eyeing each command as copy, conforming to it, sorry where short, regarding prohibitions and precepts, inward and outward, pleasant and hazardous alike — and such shall not be ashamed, having confidence, credit and clearness of conscience. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

7 COMMENTARY: Henry pictures David still pressing forward as a scholar in Christ's school, aiming to be a head-scholar mighty in Scripture, coveting learning not for name or speculation but to give God glory — for praise with uprightness of heart, inward and aiming at his glory in conversation as in devotion, is what God accepts. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

8 COMMENTARY: Henry joins learning to keeping and to dependence: we cannot keep statutes unless we learn them, nor learn them in vain without keeping them, in full resolution by grace — and good men are undone if God forsakes them, for then the tempter is too hard; desertion must never be total and final, for that is hell. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

9 COMMENTARY: Henry asks for the next generation: a young man's way, polluted by original corruption and youthful lusts, is cleansed by taking heed according to God's word — making it his rule, examining his way by it as touchstone and chart, for ruin lies in living at large by no rule or by false rules. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

10 COMMENTARY: Henry pleads sought grace against wandering: David sought God as oracle, happiness and God, with his whole heart, only and diligently, and God never says Seek in vain; the best are sensible of aptness to wander, and the more pleasure found in keeping commands, the more earnest the prayer for preventing grace. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

11 COMMENTARY: Henry treasures the word in the heart, not merely house, hand or head where enemies or failing memory may take it: laid up ready for use and valued, it answers every temptation as our Master did, It is written, opposing precepts to sin's dominion, promises to its allurements, threatenings to its menaces. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

12 COMMENTARY: Henry intermixes praise with prayer: Blessed art thou, O Lord, infinitely happy in himself yet pleased to reckon himself honoured by our service — so teach me thy statutes, one drop from the fountain of blessedness, that I may know how to bless the blessed God and be blessed in him. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

13 COMMENTARY: Henry looks back with comfort: David declared all God's judgments with his lips, not only as king and prophet but in common discourse, out of abundance of heart, hiding God's word for others and bringing forth good things — and pleads good use of knowledge for increase, for to him that has shall be given. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

14 COMMENTARY: Henry rejoices in the way of commandments as in all riches: not only speculations and histories but precepts and the path of serious godliness, a constant even course of obedience, where true religion holds the unsearchable riches of Christ and the soul may take its ease. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

15 COMMENTARY: Henry meditates and respects: not only discoursing to others to show knowledge, but communing with his own heart and digesting what he declared, keeping God's ways always in eye as a traveller his road, so that good thoughts produce good works. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

16 COMMENTARY: Henry resolves on perpetual delight and remembrance: not for a season but still and for ever delighting in statutes, more than court pleasures, camp honours, sword or harp — for when law is written in the heart duty becomes delight, and meditators who delight are in no great danger of forgetting. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

17 COMMENTARY: Henry owes life itself to bounty: God's bounty gave life and continues it with supports and comforts, for in strict justice we perish; life is a choice mercy as opportunity to obey God where few glorify him, and to transmit his word to those after. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

18 COMMENTARY: Henry finds wondrous things in the law — strange, excellent, long hidden from the wise yet revealed to babes, and more in the gospel where Christ is all — but we are by nature blind till grace opens eyes, and even the enlightened must pray to see more clearly, as men first seen as trees walking. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

19 COMMENTARY: Henry confesses pilgrim status: heaven is home, earth but inn, and even David, established and served by strangers, calls himself a stranger — therefore needing commandments as guide, guard, companion and comforter, preparing him for removal hence. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

20 COMMENTARY: Henry pleads importunate, constant longing: the soul wholly employed in longing for God's judgments, broken if it wants the word's direction, converse and comfort — not now and then in a good humour, but as habitual hunger for necessary food. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

21 COMMENTARY: Henry traces wilful sin to pride: the proud magnify themselves above others and against God, setting their wills against his, erring in judgment into contrary principles and then in practice — and God resists them, cursing those who throw off the law, rebuking them here as earnest of worse, which makes saints cleave closer to the word. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

22 COMMENTARY: Henry rolls reproach off by prayer: neither greatness nor goodness secured David from libel, and hard words grieve a tender spirit and may drive or discourage duty — so appeal to God who holds hearts and tongues, can silence lying lips, and will clear righteousness as light; and plead keeping testimonies despite jeers for well-doing. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

23 COMMENTARY: Henry answers princes' council-room slander by meditation: as a deaf man he heard not, going on in duty, for the word spoke for him and comforted him — and those with pleasure in communion with God may easily despise censures, even of princes, as David typed Christ vilified by princes of this world. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

24 COMMENTARY: Henry makes testimonies both delight and counsellors: when affliction saddened, the word comforted above court, camp, city or country pleasures; when it perplexed, statutes counselled patient bearing and committing the cause to God — more safety than a multitude of counsellors, delight and counsel going together. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

25 COMMENTARY: Henry complains of a soul cleaving to dust — inclination to world and body and deadness to holy duties, like Paul's body of death, or affliction bringing him to death's dust — and begs quickening by providence into affairs and by grace into affections, for God's words are spirit and life and his word is guide and plea in every prayer. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

26 COMMENTARY: Henry prizes intimacy: David opened his very heart, declared his ways, wants, burdens and sins ingenuously, and God heard patiently and took cognizance — unspeakable comfort — and he asks not for visions or news of his lot, but teach me thy statutes, that acknowledging God in all ways he may have steps directed. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

27 COMMENTARY: Henry seeks methodical understanding to talk well of wonders: give a clear, distinct knowledge of the way of precepts, so he may speak with assurance and purpose of God's wondrous works of providence and especially redeeming love, walking intelligently in that way. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

28 COMMENTARY: Henry melts and asks strength: heaviness makes the soul melt like a wasting candle, penitent for sin or patient under affliction, and he begs strength in the soul according to the word, the bread of life, to do duties, resist temptations and bear burdens, as thy days so thy strength. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

29 COMMENTARY: Henry dreads the way of lying under difficulty: conscious of cheating Ahimelech and Achish in straits, he prays God prevent pious fraud and necessary self-defence excuses settling into deceit — and begs the law graciously, not merely transcribed by hand as kings must, but written in the heart, a charter of privileges and unspeakable gift. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

30 COMMENTARY: Henry chooses deliberately: the way of serious godliness is the way of truth, founded on eternal truth and the only true way to happiness, chosen not from ignorance of another but because no better, safer way is known, though narrow. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

31 COMMENTARY: Henry lays judgments before him as copy and model: as a learner lays his copy to write by and a workman his platform to work exactly, so the word must be in the eye by actual regard on all occasions, as well as in the heart by habitual conformity, that walking be accurate and by rule. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

32 COMMENTARY: Henry runs when the heart is enlarged: the choosing Christian sticks to testimonies with unchanged affection through trials and may expect not to be put to shame — and when God by his Spirit gives wisdom, sheds abroad love and puts gladness in the heart, that joy becomes wheels to obedience, pressing forward and redeeming time. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

33 COMMENTARY: Henry begs God himself as teacher despite prophets, priests and his own learning, for none teaches like him: teach not notions or language but the way of statutes applied to self, the word behind saying This is the way, walk in it — promising constancy to the end as surest proof of sincerity. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

34 COMMENTARY: Henry asks understanding as God of grace, not merely nature's faculties: an enlightened, sanctified understanding to use powers about things belonging to peace, since revelation in the word needs wisdom in the heart — and then obedience will be cordial, with the whole heart, for that way the whole man goes. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

35 COMMENTARY: Henry prays God to bow the will as well as enlighten the head, for God works both to will and to do: make me able, strengthening for every good work by his Spirit causing us to walk in statutes, and make me willing, subduing natural aversion — duty done with delight when the heart is inclined. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

36 COMMENTARY: Henry prays against covetousness, the sin opposed to all testimonies, root of much sin: that love of God may be rooted in, love of the world must be rooted out, for world-friendship is enmity with God — and God deals not by compulsion but draws with cords of a man toward good and away from evil. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

37 COMMENTARY: Henry asks restraining and constraining grace together: turn eyes from beholding vanity — honours, pleasures, profits whose prospect infects the heart and alienates from God, covenanting with eyes as Job did — and quicken me in thy way to redeem time and press forward, for gazing travellers rid no ground. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

38 COMMENTARY: Henry defines the good man as God's servant devoted to his fear, subject to law, employed in work, governed in affections by fear of God — and such may boldly pray God establish his word, fulfilling promises in due time and assuring meanwhile; what God promised we must pray for, neither more nor less. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

39 COMMENTARY: Henry appeals from men's censures to God's good judgments: conscious of giving occasion for blasphemy, he prays God who holds hearts and tongues prevent reproach falling on religion through his side, for God's throne judgments are right and kind to the wronged, and it is small to be judged by man's judgment while the Lord judges. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

40 COMMENTARY: Henry professes longing that grows with tasting: not only loving and delighting in what is attained, but earnestly desiring to know more and do better, pressing toward perfection — and prays quickening in righteousness, for where God wrought desire he will satisfy it, and where he wrought to will he will work to do. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

41 COMMENTARY: Henry builds hope on two pillars: God's mercy alone, not merit, and God's promise — let salvation come, temporal hastened from distress and eternal hastened by qualifications and pledges, for eternal life is expected as the mercy of our Lord Jesus Christ. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

42 COMMENTARY: Henry expects an answer for reproachers: when God saves those who trusted him, he silences those who shamed the counsel of the poor as if trust would deceive, and at completed salvation reproaches will be for ever silenced, proving it was not in vain to trust God. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

43 COMMENTARY: Henry begs the tongue of the learned never be taken utterly: wisdom and courage to use knowledge for others, bring forth things new and old, and profess faith when called — for sometimes the word is not ready, yet enough must remain for duty, lest support and defence depart. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

44 COMMENTARY: Henry resolves continual, everlasting keeping: with word in heart and mouth he shall stand complete in God's whole will, served in constant course every day and all day, never weary of well-doing, and transmitted from generation to generation as promised, word not departing from seed's seed. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

45 COMMENTARY: Henry promises liberty from seeking precepts: desiring to know and do duty and consulting the word, he will walk free from evil's fetters and free to good willingly, not by constraint — for sin's service is perfect slavery and God's service perfect liberty. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

46 COMMENTARY: Henry makes boldness before kings a fruit of love for testimonies: whether kings were judges like Saul and Achish or companions after the crown, David would speak of testimonies as council, guards, crown and all, never ashamed nor complaisant, like the three children before Nebuchadnezzar. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

47 COMMENTARY: Henry delights in commandments in conversing and conforming: never so well pleased with himself as when doing what pleases God, for the more delight in God's service the nearer perfection aimed at. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

48 COMMENTARY: Henry lifts hands and meditates: vehement desire laying hold lest commandments be missed, close application putting forth all strength to practise, not merely praise — and thoughtful contriving how to observe best, applying mind and hands together as true love's proof. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

49 COMMENTARY: Henry pleads promise and wrought hope together: remember thy word unto thy servant, for God wise will perfect purpose and faithful will perform promise — and God by grace wrought the hope itself, raising expectations of great things, so he who worked faith will work according to faith and not disappoint. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

50 COMMENTARY: Henry finds comfort in quickening: the word made him alive when dead in sin, lively when dead in duty, quickened to good when backward and in good when cold — and therefore comforts in affliction, for only those experiencing its quickening power may apply its comforts, and if it makes holy it makes easy under all events. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

51 COMMENTARY: Henry endures derision without declining: proud scorners ridiculed prayer as cant, seriousness as mopishness, strictness as needless preciseness, yet David had not so much as declined — if this be to be vile, I will be yet more vile — for the traveller goes on though dogs bark, and little can be borne for Christ by one who cannot bear a hard word. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

52 COMMENTARY: Henry comforts himself with judgments of old: reproach bore cheerfully because it was for God's sake, with no occasion save in the matter of his God, and remembrance of God's providences in mercy to his people and justice against persecutors — for he is still the same. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

53 COMMENTARY: Henry is horrified not by designs against himself but by open forsaking of law: wilful, avowed sin frightens him with dishonour to God, gratification to Satan, mischief to souls, and ruin to God's kingdom — sin a monstrous horrible thing in sanctified eyes. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

54 COMMENTARY: Henry sings statutes in pilgrimage: this world is inn and tabernacle, even David's palace only pilgrimage-house, and God's statutes were his songs, familiar as accustomed tunes, putting gladness in heart more than viol-chanting — so the afflicted may sing away sorrow over them. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

55 COMMENTARY: Henry remembers God's name in the night: on a waking bed, in affliction's night, calling to mind the discovery God made of himself in his word, his memorial to all generations — and remembrance so prefixed to law influences observance all day. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

56 COMMENTARY: Henry counts obedience its own wages: keeping thy precepts brought this comfort — either all comfortable things, for religion turns to good account and gainers own it, or the comfort of keeping itself, for a heart to obey is a most valuable reward, and the fruit-bearing branch is made more fruitful. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

57 COMMENTARY: Henry takes God as portion and prince: others' portion is in this life, wealth and honours, but the sanctified take the Lord for inheritance and cup, nothing less satisfying — and having said he would keep God's words, he must abide by it, putting himself often in mind of the promise. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

58 COMMENTARY: Henry entreats favour with the whole heart: knowing he forfeited it, unworthy yet undone without it, happy only if obtained — not demanded as debt but begged as suppliant for reconciliation, acceptance and smile, forgiveness past and grace future, pleaded on the promise according to thy word. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

59 COMMENTARY: Henry traces conversion to fixed thought: thinking beforehand to walk surely and afterwards reflecting, as embroiderers covering flaws or accountants reckoning what do I owe, what am I worth — not on wealth but on ways, for what we do follows into another world when what we have is left. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

60 COMMENTARY: Henry demands haste without demur: penitent reflections must produce pious resolutions, striking while iron is hot, not deferring like Felix to a more convenient season, but setting about duty today while it is called today, morning after nightly reflection. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

61 COMMENTARY: Henry holds fast when robbed: bands of wicked confederates spoiled David — Saul his effects, Absalom his palace, Amalekites Ziklag — yet like Job stripped by Chaldeans and Sabeans he had not forgotten the law, for no care or grief should drive God's word from mind, nor trouble make us think worse of God's ways. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

62 COMMENTARY: Henry rises at midnight to thank God for righteous judgments, not merely favours: great thoughts kept him awake and refreshed instead of sleep, secret devotion in bed-chamber as well as public courts, reverently rising in cold and dark lest affections cool, husbanding sleepless time for prayer. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

63 COMMENTARY: Henry loves saints for God's sake in them: not chiefly as firm friends to his interest, but as fearing God and keeping precepts, honouring him and supporting his kingdom — and he was companion of all such, king associating with poorest subjects in ordinances, sympathy, familiar converse and counsel. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

64 COMMENTARY: Henry argues from earth full of mercy: as heaven is full of glory, earth is full of pity and bounty, even where God has no homage and even inferior creatures taste goodness — so will he not feed his child with spiritual food, bread of life, as he feeds young ravens? He who leaves no bird unfed leaves no child untaught. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

65 COMMENTARY: Henry acknowledges well-dealing according to word: however God dealt, he dealt better than deserved, all in love for good, beyond expectation, never using a servant hardly — and favours look best compared with promise, flowing from that fountain. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

66 COMMENTARY: Henry prays for good judgment and knowledge, a good taste to discern differing things, truth from falsehood, good from evil, as ear tries words as mouth tastes meat: many have knowledge with little judgment, but both fortify against Satan's snares and furnish for God's service — pleaded on believing and consenting to commandments. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

67 COMMENTARY: Henry learns prosperity's danger and affliction's cure: in peace and plenty, easy and at home in the world, he went astray, prosperity breeding conceit, indulgence, forgetfulness and deafness to reproof — but sanctified affliction humbled, softened, opened ear to discipline, and like the prodigal brought him to himself and to his Father, now keeping the word. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

68 COMMENTARY: Henry praises fountain from streams: God art good and doest good, streams so numerous, full and strong to all creatures that the fountain in himself must be inexhaustible — and begs the good of teaching: instruct, incline and enable, that he may be good and do good, encouraged because God good and upright teaches sinners his way. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

69 COMMENTARY: Henry bears forged lies patiently and wholly: proud enviers invented stories with no colour to blemish his eclipsing reputation, yet he will not render railing for railing but sit silently, going on in duty with constancy — let them say what they will, I will keep thy precepts with my whole heart. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

70 COMMENTARY: Henry refuses envy of fat hearts: the proud at ease, full of world wealth and pleasures, are senseless, secure, past feeling of word or rod, sensual and voluptuous — but David delights in the law and builds security on promises, with communion pleasure infinitely preferable to carnal delights. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

71 COMMENTARY: Henry calls affliction good by experience: best saints are afflicted, waters of a full cup wrung out, while proud live in pomp — yet it was good for David, teaching many lessons and duties, recovering him from wanderings and making him learn statutes he had not learned in ease. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

72 COMMENTARY: Henry values law above lost wealth: affliction's profit counterbalances loss, for the law learned by it was better than all gold and silver lost — David with little Scripture yet valuing it as God's mouth-revelation shames us with complete Testaments accounted strange, and riches yielding poorer pleasures, maintenance and inheritance than the word. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

73 COMMENTARY: Henry adores Maker and begs new making: hands that made and fashioned this fearfully wonderful being must by grace make anew, for sin made us unable for service and indisposed for enjoyment — and God recovers his interest by giving understanding, the door by which he enters and gains the soul. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

74 COMMENTARY: Henry shares hope's joy: having hoped in the word and not been disappointed — a hope making not ashamed, present satisfaction and fruition at last — other fearers of God will be glad to see him relieved, for one child's comforts should be another's joy, and good people are glad to see one another, especially the eminent in hope. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

75 COMMENTARY: Henry owns affliction just and faithful: judgments are righteousness itself, less than deserved, God holy and just in all government — and afflictions are in the covenant, intended for good pursuant to salvation's design; easier to own in general than in one's own case, but David subscribes, even my afflictions are just and kind. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

76 COMMENTARY: Henry begs merciful kindness for comfort according to promise: claiming nothing as due, supports must come from mere mercy to misery and want — let evidence and effects come, working relief, for gracious souls fetch all comfort from a gracious God, fountain of happiness, and in his favour is life, in his frowns death. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

77 COMMENTARY: Henry begs tender mercies that he may live: revived, life made sweet, for no satisfaction exists under displeasure without tokens of favour — pleaded on promise, kindness promised because promised, and on delight in law and rejoicing hope, for those delighting in law may depend on favour that shall certainly make them happy. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

78 COMMENTARY: Henry slights causeless perversity: peevish dealers misconstruing all he said and did, for his love his adversaries, yet causeless reproach like causeless curse does not hurt and should not move — pray they be ashamed to repentance or ruin, and meanwhile meditate in precepts and entertain himself with them. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

79 COMMENTARY: Henry values saints' turning: not chiefly siding or arms, but love, prayer and association — especially intelligent knowers of testimonies, good heads as well as hearts, whose conversation edifies; after Uriah's sin fearers grew strange, so he prays, let them turn to me again. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

80 COMMENTARY: Henry prays for a sound heart, not rotten or deceitful form: hearty and constant religion, soul in health, acquainted with power not form — dreading hypocrisy's shame here if repented or hereafter if not, falling into scandal or quite off, and longing to come boldly to grace and lift face without spot at the great day. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

81 COMMENTARY: Henry faints yet hopes: soul fainting and eyes failing for salvation according to word, not creatures of fancy but objects of faith, as Old Testament saints fainted to see Messiah's salvation at distance stretching eyes — yet hope in the word holds, for but for hope the heart would break, vision for appointed time speaking at end and not lying. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

82 COMMENTARY: Henry cries When wilt thou comfort me: eyes failed for God's word while salvation and comfort long deferred, ready to faint in expectation — yet keep eye on that salvation and take up with nothing short, for promised salvation is secured by word certainly fulfilled in season. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

83 COMMENTARY: Henry, a smoke-blackened bottle, forgets not: affliction wasted ruddy freshness, dried, parched, shrivelled like leathern bottle in smoke, a despised broken vessel of no pleasure — yet duty not driven out, for no care or grief must crowd the word out, neither drinking nor weeping forgetting law, in prosperity or adversity. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

84 COMMENTARY: Henry asks judgment on persecutors without personal vengeance: not power to avenge or malice, but God taking vengeance belonging to him, repaying as throne-judge, the great day Enoch foretold — pleaded on long continuance of trouble, days numbered and wearing, looking and praying for that day's hastening. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

85 COMMENTARY: Henry exposes pit-diggers: proud persecutors, unjust and spiteful, deliberate, subtle as serpents, industrious hunters laying secret snares — and enemies to God himself, for pits against his anointed break law forbidding devising evil to neighbours, and even pit-digging causing mischief must answer for it. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

86 COMMENTARY: Henry anchors suffering faith: however it be, all thy commandments are faithful, so whatever is lost for observance is not lost by it — men false, high and low untrustworthy, but commandments reliable; true religion worth anything is worth everything, therefore worth suffering for. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

87 COMMENTARY: Henry refuses frightened forsaking: persecutors aimed to frighten him from God's ways and almost consumed him on earth, yet the bush in which God is burns without being burnt up — he would sooner forsake all dear in this world, sooner life, than forsake the word and its comfort. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

88 COMMENTARY: Henry depends on grace for perseverance: constant design to keep testimony as rule, confidence and portion whatever is lost, yet otherwise he should not keep them — quicken me after thy lovingkindness, for God's good work in us is surest token of his good-will, grace purely fruit of favour. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

89 COMMENTARY: Henry settles the word in heaven against earthly revolutions: God himself unchanging, word by which heavens were made settled in secret counsel immovable as brass mountains, revealed will as firm as secret will, no word falling to ground. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

90 COMMENTARY: Henry proves perpetuity by nature's constancy: faithfulness unto all generations, promises not antiquated by time, earth established and abiding despite bowels, seas and atmosphere, heavens and earth continuing in assigned posts according to ordinances, as Noah's promise keeps day, night, summer, winter steady. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

91 COMMENTARY: Henry makes all creatures servants and asks if man alone rebels: day and night, heaven and earth continue to this day according to ordinances, doing God's will and setting forth glory in their places — shall man be only revolter, only unprofitable burden of earth? — Matthew Henry (Complete)

92 COMMENTARY: Henry survives by delight: ready to perish not so much by death as despair, cut off from God's sight, yet kept in possession of his soul and close to God — because law had been delight formerly, evidence of integrity, and was delight now, fountains of living water when creature cisterns broke, a pleasant companion in solitude and sorrow. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

93 COMMENTARY: Henry resolves never to forget precepts because they quickened him: best evidence of love is never forgetting, never casting off religion on any occasion — and quickening by experience, spiritual life begun, maintained, excited and comforted, wrought by grace through word, not ministers prophesying to dry bones alone; good affections are best help for bad memories, impressions remaining though expressions be lost. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

94 COMMENTARY: Henry claims humbly, I am thine, not thou art mine: devoted and owned in covenant, pleading not his own qualification but God's propriety — I am not my own nor the world's — proved by seeking precepts carefully, and improved into save me from sin and ruin, for sincere self-givers may be sure of protection to the heavenly kingdom. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

95 COMMENTARY: Henry considers testimonies while wicked wait to destroy: cruel, crafty, confident enemies expecting prey, yet while they contrive destruction he dwells in thought on testimonies securing salvation — for testimonies support us when considered and dwelt upon. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

96 COMMENTARY: Henry sees an end of all creature perfection and breadth in commandment: Goliath strongest overcome, Asahel swiftest overtaken, Ahithophel wisest befooled, Absalom fairest deformed — perfection scanty, defective and short-lived, glory as flower of grass — but God's commandment exceedingly broad, reaching all cases and times, sanctifying wholly, promise extending to every burden, want and grievance. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

97 COMMENTARY: Henry protests love with holy vehemency: O how love I thy law — affection he wonders at, considering corruption and worldly temptations, grace having wrought it — loving not promises only but law itself, delighting in it after the inner man, with meditation all the day as unexceptionable evidence. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

98 COMMENTARY: Henry makes commandments constant companions and outwitting wisdom: shepherd and courtier alike got learning from God as author and word as means, commandments ever in mind and eye, meditation as business management — so he baffled enemies' designs, securing God as friend and preserving peace enemies would rob. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

99 COMMENTARY: Henry outstrips teachers by meditation: whether blaming prescribers proved wrong in event, or priests neglecting law for honours and formalities, or youthful teachers whose foundation he built on to teach them — meditation preaches to ourselves, revealing our own hearts teachers cannot, and written word outguides all doctors and fathers. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

100 COMMENTARY: Henry outdoes ancients by keeping precepts: young like Elihu against very old, or against former days' tradition, his keeping taught more than multitude of years — for the written word is a surer guide to heaven than all learning handed down. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

101 COMMENTARY: Henry refrains feet universally: checking and drawing back on entering temptation, though way broad, green, pleasant and crowded, foreseeing its end — abstaining as evidence of conscientious aim to keep the word and as means of keeping it in duties, for guilt or by-ways rob comfort and boldness before God. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

102 COMMENTARY: Henry credits constancy to divine teaching: not departing from judgments, choosing no other rule nor wilfully deviating, constant adherence in trying times evidencing integrity — because instructions were divine and grace enabled receiving them; only those taught of God continue to the end in what they learned. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

103 COMMENTARY: Henry tastes sweetness beyond honey: a spiritual taste, inward savour by experience we cannot give others — we have heard him ourselves — sweeter than any sense gratification, satisfaction in divine will and grace no pleasure comparable to. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

104 COMMENTARY: Henry gains head and heart from precepts: understanding to discern truth from falsehood, good from evil, in his own conduct and advising others — and therefore hatred of every false way, not merely refraining but antipathy and dread, for sin's way deceives and ruins though seeming right, and more understanding roots hatred deeper and furnishes answers to temptation. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

105 COMMENTARY: Henry uses the word as lamp for feet, not only light for eyes: discovering God and ourselves, showing what is amiss and dangerous, directing work and way in a dark world, kept burning with Spirit's oil like sanctuary lamps and Israel's pillar — guiding choice of way and each particular step, neither false way nor false step in the right way. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

106 COMMENTARY: Henry binds himself by oath to righteous judgments: commands as dictates of infinite wisdom, consonant to eternal equity, already binding by divine precept, now bound again by lifted hand and allegiance-fealty, appealing to God and owning curse if unperformed — vows to be remembered and performed, for honest men are as good as word, and non-performance hurts unspeakably. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

107 COMMENTARY: Henry, afflicted very much in spirit, with fightings without and fears within — often best saints' lot, think it not strange — begs quickening according to word: made lively and cheerful, quickened by afflictions to diligence, delivered as life from dead, pleading promise as he resolved to perform his own promises to God. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

108 COMMENTARY: Henry prays acceptance and assistance together: freewill offerings of mouth, prayers and praises, calves and fruit of lips, offered abundantly and cheerfully as spiritual priests, willing mind accepted — and teach me thy judgments, for nothing offered is acceptable unless God instructs its doing, grace in us as earnest as favour toward us. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

109 COMMENTARY: Henry holds life in hand yet not law forgotten: a step from death, as all men exposed and life slipping like sand, as soldier jeoparding life in high places, as man after God's heart always delivered to death — yet in no danger of losing religion, constant to God and duty amid hourly jeopardy. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

110 COMMENTARY: Henry keeps religion amid snares: wicked laid treacherous snares for his life, as Saul often did, yet he had not erred from precepts — and hope stands, for grace helping hitherto will not fail but still prevent wanderings, and law dwelling richly is a well of living water amid cares for safety. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

111 COMMENTARY: Henry portions himself in testimonies for ever: present delight evidences best things chosen, covenant everlasting taken as heritage no earthly heritage matches, saints accepting it, living upon it, content with little of this world — for it takes a good man's heart to see portion in promise, not possessions. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

112 COMMENTARY: Henry inclines heart to statutes with consent and constancy: blessings of testimonies require bonds of statutes, comfort only in duty's way, done with full complacency conquering aversion, and always, in every day's duty, to the end without weariness — following the Lord fully. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

113 COMMENTARY: Henry hates vain thoughts in his own heart: thoughts are words to God, and vain thoughts, lightly made of, are sinful and hurtful, diverting from good and opening door to all evil — he could not claim freedom from them, but hated, countenanced not, kept them under; and more love for law gives more mastery and watchfulness against them. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

114 COMMENTARY: Henry hides in God as hiding-place and shield: Saul's close places and war-shield prefigure God preserving from danger and in danger, life from death and soul from sin — by faith retiring and reposing in secret, opposing his power to enemies' might as shield quenching fiery darts, safe and easy hoping in the word that assures kindness. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

115 COMMENTARY: Henry resolves saintly and soldierly, then dismisses bad company: I will keep commandments of my God, singular though all desert, God commanding nothing but for good — and depart, ye evildoers, for bad company hinders holy life; choose no wicked companions, be not intimate, do not as they do nor as they would have us do. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

116 COMMENTARY: Henry begs sustaining grace twice: unable by own strength to go on, in danger of falling into sin and tiring in well-doing, he pleads promise hoped in — if unperformed he would be ashamed as fool for credulity, yet hope in God's word cannot make ashamed — and need, for without upholding he falls into sin, death and hell, but supported lives, spiritual life earnest of eternal. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

117 COMMENTARY: Henry grounds holy security on divine supports: hold me up and I shall be safe, out of danger and fear of danger — and then, in that strength, respect statutes continually, never turning eyes or feet aside, employing and delighting in them with diligence and pleasure. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

118 COMMENTARY: Henry sees errants trodden down for deceit as falsehood: departure from statutes is fatal error, wicked of earth minding earthly treasures and pleasures, strangers to heaven — God brings them to shameful ruin as footstool, resisting proud, while their self-deceiving false rules and hypocritical, crafty projects the God of truth hates and will punish. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

119 COMMENTARY: Henry learns from dross judgments: wicked mingled like dross with good metal must be separated, worthless scum no more comparable to righteous than dross to fine gold, put away at last into everlasting fire and sometimes here by church censure, magistrate's sword or God's judgments — and seeing word fulfilled in judgment increases love for testimonies warning from destroyer's paths. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

120 COMMENTARY: Henry trembles instead of insulting the fallen: flesh trembles for fear of God, reverencing terrible majesty — who can stand before this holy Lord? — and fearing lest he offend and become obnoxious, for good men need terror's restraint, especially when judgment begins at God's house and hypocrites are put away as dross. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

121 COMMENTARY: Henry appeals as witness and judge: he had done judgment and justice, rendering all their due as king and privately, no evil in his hand before Saul — honesty best policy and rejoicing in evil day — and begs God as surety for good against oppressors he cannot answer himself, Christ our surety with God and providence surety against the world, only let it be for good as wisdom sees best. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

122 COMMENTARY: Henry, uniquely without a law-word in this verse as Henry notes, still rests under suretyship: he asks God be surety for good, undertaking against those running him down, for he cannot make his part good himself — and with God as friend, proud oppressors shall not have their will, persecuted better than persecutors at last. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

123 COMMENTARY: Henry waits with failing eyes: oppressed, eyes toward heaven's help long looked for, salvation coming slower than expected, ready sometimes to despair because it tarries — yet deceiving ourselves if we look elsewhere, he keeps eyes on the word of righteousness that will make him easy. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

124 COMMENTARY: Henry begs instruction as mercy and as servant's due: in affliction and failing eyes, desire more to be told duty than expectation, led into precepts rather than prophecies — for if God who gave statutes does not teach, we never learn; and servant at a loss may expect his master to teach him to do work well, as those resolving to do God's will shall know testimonies. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

125 COMMENTARY: Henry asks renewed understanding as God's prerogative: without it testimonies cannot be known, and those knowing most still desire more, never thinking they know enough — pleaded on mercy, best plea of best saints who deserve no favour and claim none as debt, and on service, for taught servants serve well. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

126 COMMENTARY: Henry complains daring impiety and desires God's working: presumptuous sinners do not merely transgress but make void law, cancelling obligation, saying Who is the Lord, repealing God's laws to enact lusts — and God's time to work is when vice is most daring and iniquity full, yet also time for every one to work for the Lord in his place for sinking religion. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

127 COMMENTARY: Henry loves commandments above fine gold: gold dazzles most men, who venture soul, God and all to get and keep it, but word enriches soul toward God, does what gold cannot, and stands when worldly wealth fails — loved because esteemed right, reasonable, just, bearing God's image, no fault found. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

128 COMMENTARY: Henry hates every false way as love's fruit: sin's way directly contrary to right precepts is false, deceiving and ruining, and those consenting law is good and delighting after inner man will not be reconciled to it. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

129 COMMENTARY: Henry admires and adheres: testimonies wonderful in discoveries of God, Christ and another world, majesty of style, purity of matter, harmony of parts, effects for conviction and comfort — and therefore soul keeps them as inestimable treasure deposited innermost and uppermost, as tables in ark, promising great things from them. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

130 COMMENTARY: Henry finds entrance giving light to the simple: word intended to give understanding for travels through this world, outward ordinary means of Spirit's enlightening — even first verses give surprising, satisfying discoveries of universe's origin, first principles and milk for babes bring great light, and plain way to heaven wayfaring fools shall not err in. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

131 COMMENTARY: Henry pants like one stifled for fresh air: longing for commandments in forced absence from ordinances, greedily sucking word as new-born babes milk when enjoying them — gracious longings unaccountable to strangers, strong and earnest toward God and remembrance of his name. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

132 COMMENTARY: Henry asks only a good look, neighbour's fare: look graciously, smiles and light of countenance, cognizance of affairs, sweetness and gifts of mercy — not operations of hand, only face — pleaded as one loving God's name, desiring no better than dealings reserved for lovers, eye-hath-not-seen mercies, truly and eternally happy. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

133 COMMENTARY: Henry prays direction and deliverance equally earnest: order my steps in thy word, every step under grace's guidance, motions kept within bounds and carried in prescribed paths, not trifling — and let no iniquity gain consent or lead captive, dominion dreaded and deprecated, answered by promise, sin shall not have dominion over you. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

134 COMMENTARY: Henry seeks quiet life to serve better: deliver from oppression of man, whose power God controls and limits, that enemies' hands loose bonds for cheerfuller, enlarged keeping — not that he would not keep under oppression, but temporal blessings may be expected when desired with serving God better in eye. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

135 COMMENTARY: Henry, king glorying in servant title, is ambitious of Master's favour and solicitous for Master's work: asks not corn, wine, silver or gold, but make thy face shine, accepted and knowing it, comfort in cloudy days though world frowns — and teach me statutes, praying as earnestly for grace as comfort, for hidden face follows careless keeping, and wisdom qualifies for favour's return. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

136 COMMENTARY: Henry weeps rivers because others keep not law: great sorrow, gracious heart with weeping eye conformed to Christ, man of sorrows — pleading qualification for comfort as mourner in Zion who shall be comforted, grieving not chiefly his own wrongs but dishonour done to God by law-breaking. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

137 COMMENTARY: Henry confesses threefold righteousness: God's nature infinite rectitude, nothing wanting or amiss, will eternal rule of equity; his government never wronging any creature, promises, threatenings and executions upright, every word pure and true to merits; and his commands exceedingly righteous and faithful, requiring us act like him, just to all and true to engagements. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

138 COMMENTARY: Henry repeats that testimonies commanded with sovereign authority are righteousness and faithfulness itself: what we practise is righteous, what we believe faithful — necessary conviction for faith and obedience. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

139 COMMENTARY: Henry's zeal consumes him over forgotten words: enemies heard God's words yet willingly cast them behind backs, forgetting at bottom of all wickedness and enmity to God's people — and David, in league offensive and defensive with religion, felt indignation preying on spirits like Christ's zeal, constraining him to do more in religion himself: the worse others, the better we should be. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

140 COMMENTARY: Henry loves word for its purity: every servant of God loves it as revealing Master's will and directing Master's work, warm attachment wherever grace is — and love is evidence of love to God when loved for purity, bearing holiness' image, commanding purity, refined from corrupt mixture, refining us from worldliness and fleshly-mindedness. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

141 COMMENTARY: Henry, small and despised yet not forgetting: man after God's heart, honoured by King of kings, yet low in own and others' account, for excellency often exposes to scorn — and poor yet pious, refusing to throw off religion though it brings contempt, for contempt tries constancy, and low condition most needs precepts' support. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

142 COMMENTARY: Henry binds reason and subject together: word is everlasting righteousness, rule of judgment consonant to eternal counsels, determining everlasting state, reverenced as standard — and law is truth, double obligation twisted as cords: truth ruling understanding, law binding will, God's truth a law and law truth, inseparable. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

143 COMMENTARY: Henry raises constancy higher in trouble and anguish: trouble without, anguish within surprising, seizing, holding — yet commandments are delights, mouth not put out of taste, peace and pleasure calamities cannot deprive, sweetest enjoyment of word's variety often in trouble, as in 2 Corinthians. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

144 COMMENTARY: Henry infers understanding and life from everlasting righteousness: testimonies unalterable, admitted in power an abiding well of living water — meditate on equity and eternity, pray not for further revelation but further understanding of what is revealed, and then live for ever, eternally happy and presently comforted in prospect, for life eternal is knowing God. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

145 COMMENTARY: Henry prays inwardly and importunately: not boasting or trusting merit, but comforted to have taken appointed way — heart going with prayer, lip-labour alone lost, crying with whole heart, all soul's powers exerted, directed to Father, seeking comprehensive salvation, rescue and happiness, and looking after prayers to see what becomes of them. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

146 COMMENTARY: Henry binds purpose to petition: I will keep thy statutes by grace, for turning ear from law forfeits answer of peace — and pleads it humbly, save me from sins, corruptions, temptations and hindrances, not for ease but opportunity to serve God more cheerfully. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

147 COMMENTARY: Henry prevents dawn by hope: crying before morning, encouraged to instant prayer though answer delayed, vision for appointed time speaking at end and not lying — hope in word that will not fail, beginning day with God while mind fresh, first thoughts keeping fear all day. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

148 COMMENTARY: Henry redeems night-watches by meditation: awaking from first sleep, he would meditate and pray rather than turn again, esteeming God's words more than necessary repose as Job esteemed them more than food — for reading alone will not serve, meditation teaches God's own language and what to pray as we ought, and throne of grace is never unseasonable, for Israel's God never slumbers. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

149 COMMENTARY: Henry begs quickening in little with much: Lord, hear my voice, shall I obtain gracious audience? — quicken me, stir habits of grace into act, vigorous, lively, cheerful — depending on lovingkindness, good God being good to hopers in mercy, and on judgment, wisdom knowing need and promise securing mercy by new covenant's tenor. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

150 COMMENTARY: Henry contrasts nearness: mischief-followers industrious, letting slip no opportunity, impious and far from law, out of reach of its convictions, drawing nigh at his heels, a step from death — yet God brings his people into imminent peril, as Jacob, that like him they may wrestle for a blessing. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

151 COMMENTARY: Henry assures nearness beats nearness: they draw nigh to destroy, but thou art near, O Lord, to save — mightier and nearer, ready to help, never far to seek, within our call — and all commandments are truth, inviolably true and infallibly performed, beyond enemies' power to defeat promises. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

152 COMMENTARY: Henry confirms founded-for-ever testimonies: covenant commanded to a thousand generations, firm as truth itself, framed for perpetuity, well ordered and sure, every iota standing when heaven and earth pass — known of old from youth by grace persuading his heart and providence fulfilling beyond expectation, owned faithful by all who ever dealt with God. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

153 COMMENTARY: Henry prays pity, power and advocacy together: consider affliction, take all circumstances into thought, not sitting unconcerned — God never unmindful, yet will have us put him in remembrance and spread the case, leaving it to compassionate wisdom in his time and way — and deliver, as promised, with submission and regard to glory that we may serve better. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

154 COMMENTARY: Henry pleads cause and quickening by word: plead my cause as patron and advocate against many mighty adversaries, clearing integrity and silencing false accusations, for if God does not plead his people's cause, who will? — and quicken weak, drooping spirit till deliverance wrought, according to promise, for closer cleaving to word as rule and stay gives more assurance of due deliverance. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

155 COMMENTARY: Henry dooms the unseeking: wicked do not merely disobey but do not so much as seek statutes, not acquainting themselves, desiring duty, or endeavouring — resolved to live at large in heart's way — so salvation is far, temporal deliverance groundless, eternal certainly far, thrust away by thrusting Saviour away, further the longer sin persists, while damnation slumbers not and Judge stands at door. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

156 COMMENTARY: Henry admires tender mercies great and many, a fountain never exhausted, rich to all who call: God good notwithstanding wicked misery, mercies sufficient to have saved them had they not despised riches — and begs reviving grace according to judgments, new covenant's tenor or God's manner with lovers of his name. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

157 COMMENTARY: Henry stands amid many persecutors: Saul's abused authority drawing multitudes, public person with many enemies yet many friends to set against them, type of Christ and church whose persecutors are very many — yet not declining, knowing while adhering God is for him, no matter who against, steady duty fearing none. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

158 COMMENTARY: Henry grieves at transgressors, not for vexation but provocation: looking abroad at open sinners numerous, daring, impudent, industriously snaring unstable souls, dishonouring God, serving Satan, debauching world, ruining souls — grief to all regarding God's glory and mankind's welfare, hating sin truly as transgression of God's word. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

159 COMMENTARY: Henry appeals to love, not performance: consider how I love thy precepts, thou knowest all things — not consider how I fulfil them, for in many things he came short — but love as principle making obedience pleasing and pleasant, and begs quickening according to lovingkindness, not merit, for to that we owe lives, nay it is better than life itself. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

160 COMMENTARY: Henry comforts with word true from beginning and enduring for ever: always faithful hitherto, never failing any venturing, church built on this rock from its beginning, validity not gained by lapse like governments by prescription, but true at beginning — and faithful to end because righteous, every judgment unalterable, perpetual, adjusting everlasting doom. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

161 COMMENTARY: Henry stands in awe of word above princes: persecuted without cause as traitor, bidden serve other gods, princes with sword and law's colour doing hurt with reputation — sad power from God used against him — yet heart stands in awe of God's word, authority of precepts and terror of threatenings, resolving to please God though all the world frowns, obeying God rather than men. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

162 COMMENTARY: Henry rejoices as one finding great spoil: pleasure in reading, hearing, meditating, every meeting agreeable, rejoicing God discovered his mind and Israel blessed with light while nations sat in darkness — and more reverence brings more joy, spoil supposing victory through opposition, word making us more than conquerors, unspeakable gainers by plunder. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

163 COMMENTARY: Henry fixes leading affections aright: rooted antipathy to lying taken for all sin — treachery with God, cheat upon ourselves, hypocrisy, false doctrine, breach of faith, commerce or conversation lies doubly hated, for a lying tongue and false witness are among seven things the Lord hates — and rooted affection to law, loved for its truth, beauty of truth revealing deformity of lie. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

164 COMMENTARY: Henry praises seven times a day without complaints jostling praises out of tune: very frequently, not weekly or twice daily, at every meal and occasion, in everything giving thanks, subject never exhausted and affections never tired — praising righteous judgments, precepts just and good, promises and threatenings performed in providence, even afflictions if grace gets good by them. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

165 COMMENTARY: Henry gives lovers of law great peace and no offence: holy serenity, abundant satisfaction in duty and reflecting on it, work of righteousness as peace world can neither give nor take, troubles without yet peace within — and holy security, no event an invincible temptation or intolerable affliction, integrity held and tranquillity preserved, everything working for good and reconciled, without needless scruples or offence at brethren. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

166 COMMENTARY: Henry joins end and rule, never to be put asunder: eye on God's favour as end, hoping temporal and eternal salvation as happiness of God's preparing, promising and being-with, raised above world and borne under burdens — and eye on word as rule, conscience conforming in everything; hope without commandment-keeping is groundless, sincere endeavour ought to keep hope, and livelier hope makes livelier obedience. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

167 COMMENTARY: Henry witnesses soul-keeping and superlative love: loving testimonies exceedingly, better than wealth and pleasure, victorious love subduing lusts and extirpating carnal affections — and soul keeping them, heart-work not bodily exercise, soul sanctified, delivered into word's mould, employed glorifying God in spirit, keeping precepts by obedience and promises by reliance. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

168 COMMENTARY: Henry lives under God's eye: therefore kept precepts because by faith he saw God's eye always upon him, every step known and strictly observed, seeing and accepting well-done, seeing and displeased with amiss — consideration that should make us very careful in everything, as walking before God. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

169 COMMENTARY: Henry cries and supplicates as beggar: prayer as cry denoting fervency, supplication denoting humility, concerned it come near before God, lifted by faith and fervency, no guilt interposing, graciously received — with deep unworthiness and fear of miscarrying, access only because Christ approached as advocate. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

170 COMMENTARY: Henry asks wisdom and rescue according to word: understanding as wisdom of prudent to understand his way, know God, self and duty — and deliverance, with temptation a way to escape — directed and limited by promise, asking no other understanding or deliverance than promised, and expecting God to be as good as his word. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

171 COMMENTARY: Henry promises lips uttering praise when taught: teaching taken for granted after importunate, Jacob-like wrestling not letting go without spiritual blessing — taught of God have foundation of all spiritual blessings and earnest of eternal, know how to praise when understanding, heart and lips are opened, and pray for grace aiming at God's glory; learned nothing to purpose without learning praise. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

172 COMMENTARY: Henry speaks word because commandments are righteousness itself: knowing it with satisfaction as rule and standard, tongue shall not only utter praise to God's glory but discourse for others' instruction, full heart speaking, desiring others filled — more sight of righteousness makes more industry to bring others under rule, word governing discourse against sinful speaking and silence, and feeding many with grace. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

173 COMMENTARY: Henry pleads chosen precepts for helping hand: own hands insufficient, no creature helping to purpose, hand that made him must help — and choice as pledge, religion serious and deliberate, taken on trial as knowing no better, not by chance; choosers may depend on helping hand in services and sufferings. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

174 COMMENTARY: Henry longs for salvation while delighting in law: heart upon heaven despite throne's worldly courts saying it is good to be here, looking further to eternal salvation all saints long for — and law as greatest delight, cheerful obedience begging help to carry on, hopes increasing delight. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

175 COMMENTARY: Henry asks life to praise and judgments to help praise: prolonged life not to serve country or provide for family chiefly, but to praise God in land of living, doing honour in conflict — soul living as sanctified and comforted, fitted by grace and peace — and ordinances and providences, both God's judgments, furthering glorifying, matter of praise and fitting for work. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

176 COMMENTARY: Henry closes penitent, believing and obedient: gone astray wandering like lost sheep, as unconverted sinners and weak unsteady saints, apt to wander and unapt to find way, losing green pastures' comfort and exposing to mischiefs — yet seek thy servant as good shepherd seeks wandering sheep, David once tender shepherd himself, own me for thine with thy mark, send after me by word, conscience and providences, find me for God never seeks in vain — though strayed, not wickedly departed, not forgetting commandments, so wanderers mindful of duty may humbly commit to grace's care, living and dying repenting and praying. — Matthew Henry (Complete)
