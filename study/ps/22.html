Study Notes — Psalms

Psalm 22

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: My God, my God, why have you forsaken me? Why so far from helping and from the words of my roaring? The cry of dereliction, uttered by Christ on the cross, opens the great psalm of his sufferings; Henry and JFB read David's sorrows here as the shadow of Messiah's. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: He cries by day and is not heard, and by night and is not silent: prayer continues without answer, which is the sharpest part of the trial. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: But God is holy, inhabiting the praises of Israel: even in forsakenness, God's holiness and his dwelling among his people's praise are confessed. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: The fathers trusted and were delivered: past generations' deliverances are recalled as a pattern the sufferer longs to share. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: They cried and were delivered, trusted and were not confounded: the pattern is repeated to sharpen the contrast with his own case. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: But I am a worm and no man, a reproach of men, despised of the people: the sufferer is debased below humanity in men's eyes. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: All who see him laugh him to scorn, shooting out the lip and shaking the head: mockery takes the exact gestures the Gospels record at the cross. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: He trusted the Lord to deliver him; let him deliver him, seeing he delighted in him: enemies turn his very trust into a taunt. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: But God took him from the womb and made him hope upon his mother's breasts: God's care from birth is the oldest ground of trust. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: He was cast upon God from the womb; God is his God from his mother's belly: a lifelong relation is pleaded against a present sense of abandonment. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: Be not far from me, for trouble is near and there is none to help: nearness of trouble and absence of helpers make God's nearness the only plea. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: Many bulls compass him, strong bulls of Bashan beset him round: powerful, brutal enemies surround him like well-fed, fierce cattle. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: They gape on him with their mouths, as a ravening, roaring lion: the enemy is both gaping maw and roaring predator. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: He is poured out like water, his bones out of joint, his heart like wax melted within: the body dissolves under suffering, and the heart fails. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: His strength is dried like a potsherd, his tongue cleaves to his jaws, and he is brought into the dust of death: thirst and exhaustion mark the approach of death itself. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: Dogs compass him, the assembly of the wicked encloses him, and they pierce his hands and feet: the piercing, centuries before crucifixion, is the psalm's most literal prophecy of the cross. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: He can tell all his bones, while they look and stare on him: emaciation and the gaze of onlookers complete the public shame. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

18 COMMENTARY: They part his garments among them and cast lots on his vesture: the division of clothing, fulfilled at the cross, is named as the enemies' last indignity. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

19 COMMENTARY: But be not far from me, O Lord, my strength; haste to help me: the prayer returns, now addressed to God as strength itself. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

20 COMMENTARY: Deliver my soul from the sword, my darling — his precious life — from the power of the dog: the sword and the dog name violent and contemptuous death. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

21 COMMENTARY: Save me from the lion's mouth, for you have heard me from the horns of the unicorns: deliverance is already heard, and the psalm turns toward praise. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

22 COMMENTARY: I will declare your name to my brethren, and praise you in the midst of the congregation: the delivered sufferer becomes the preacher of God's name to his brethren. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

23 COMMENTARY: You who fear the Lord, praise him; all the seed of Jacob, glorify him; all the seed of Israel, fear him: the call to praise widens to the whole people. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

24 COMMENTARY: For he has not despised nor abhorred the affliction of the afflicted, nor hidden his face, but heard when he cried: the forsakenness of verse 1 is answered — God did hear. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

25 COMMENTARY: My praise shall be of you in the great congregation; I will pay my vows before those who fear him: public praise and kept vows follow deliverance. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

26 COMMENTARY: The meek shall eat and be satisfied, and praise the Lord who seek him; your heart shall live for ever: the thank-offering becomes a feast for the humble, with life for ever as its promise. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

27 COMMENTARY: All the ends of the world shall remember and turn to the Lord, and all kindreds of nations worship before him: the sufferer's deliverance draws the nations to repentance and worship. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

28 COMMENTARY: For the kingdom is the Lord's, and he governs among the nations: universal worship rests on universal kingship. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

29 COMMENTARY: All the fat of the earth shall eat and worship, and all going down to dust shall bow before him, and none can keep alive his own soul: rich and dying alike must bow, for life is not in human keeping. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

30 COMMENTARY: A seed shall serve him, accounted to the Lord for a generation: the kingdom continues in a posterity reckoned as God's own. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

31 COMMENTARY: They shall come and declare his righteousness to a people yet to be born, that he has done this: the gospel of what God has done is preached to the unborn — the psalm ends in mission. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
