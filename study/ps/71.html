Study Notes — Psalms

Psalm 71

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: In you, O Lord, I put my trust; let me never be put to confusion: trust and freedom from confusion open the old man's psalm. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: Deliver me in your righteousness, and cause me to escape; incline your ear to me, and save me: deliverance in God's righteousness, escape, an inclined ear, salvation — the prayer is fourfold. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: Be my strong habitation, whereunto I may continually resort; you have given commandment to save me, for you are my rock and fortress: a strong habitation for continual resort, commanded salvation, rock and fortress — God's command makes the refuge sure. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: Deliver me, O my God, out of the hand of the wicked, out of the hand of the unrighteous and cruel man: wicked, unrighteous, cruel hands are the threefold enemy from whom deliverance is asked. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: For you are my hope, O Lord God; you are my trust from my youth: hope and trust from youth — a lifelong relation is pleaded in old age. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: By you have I been held up from the womb; you are he who took me out of my mother's bowels; my praise shall be continually of you: held up from the womb, taken from his mother's bowels, praise continually — life itself has been God's holding. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: I am as a wonder unto many, but you are my strong refuge: a wonder to many — a prodigy of suffering or deliverance — yet God his strong refuge. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: Let my mouth be filled with your praise and with your honour all the day: mouth filled with praise and honour all day — the wonder's answer is continual praise. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: Cast me not off in the time of old age; forsake me not when my strength fails: casting off in old age, forsaking when strength fails, are the specific fears of the aged saint. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: For my enemies speak against me, and they who lay wait for my soul take counsel together: enemies speaking, soul-waiters counselling together — the plot is verbal and concerted. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: Saying, God has forsaken him; persecute and take him, for there is none to deliver him: 'God has forsaken him' is the enemies' theology, and 'none to deliver' their strategy. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: O God, be not far from me; O my God, make haste for my help: nearness and haste are prayed against the enemies' claim of forsakenness. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: Let them be confounded and consumed who are adversaries to my soul; let them be covered with reproach and dishonour who seek my hurt: confounding, consuming, covering with reproach and dishonour — the adversaries' end mirrors their intent. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: But I will hope continually, and will yet praise you more and more: continual hope and ever-increasing praise answer the plot — hope grows, praise grows. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: My mouth shall show forth your righteousness and your salvation all the day, for I know not the numbers thereof: righteousness and salvation shown all day, unnumbered — the theme is too great to count. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: I will go in the strength of the Lord God; I will make mention of your righteousness, even of yours only: going in the Lord's strength, mentioning God's righteousness only — the old man's strength is borrowed, his theme is God's alone. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: O God, you have taught me from my youth, and hitherto have I declared your wondrous works: taught from youth, declaring wondrous works hitherto — a lifetime of teaching issued in a lifetime of testimony. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

18 COMMENTARY: Now also when I am old and greyheaded, O God, forsake me not, until I have shown your strength unto this generation, and your power to every one that is to come: old and greyheaded, yet not forsaken until he has shown God's strength to this generation and power to the coming one — old age has a commission. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

19 COMMENTARY: Your righteousness also, O God, is very high, who has done great things; O God, who is like unto you! Very high righteousness, great things done, none like God — the doxology rises from the testimony. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

20 COMMENTARY: You, who have shown me great and sore troubles, shall quicken me again, and shall bring me up again from the depths of the earth: great and sore troubles shown by God, quickening again, brought up from earth's depths — the God who troubled will revive. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

21 COMMENTARY: You shall increase my greatness, and comfort me on every side: increased greatness, comfort on every side — revival is enlargement, not mere restoration. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

22 COMMENTARY: I will also praise you with the psaltery, even your truth, O my God; unto you will I sing with the harp, O Holy One of Israel: psaltery praise of God's truth, harp song to the Holy One of Israel — instruments join the old man's praise. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

23 COMMENTARY: My lips shall greatly rejoice when I sing unto you, and my soul, which you have redeemed: lips greatly rejoicing, redeemed soul singing — praise is both outward and inward. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

24 COMMENTARY: My tongue also shall talk of your righteousness all the day long, for they are confounded, for they are brought unto shame, that seek my hurt: tongue talking righteousness all day, because hurt-seekers are confounded and shamed — the psalm ends in the enemies' shame and the saint's unceasing speech. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
