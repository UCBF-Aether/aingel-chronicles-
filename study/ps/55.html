Study Notes — Psalms

Psalm 55

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Give ear to my prayer, O God, and hide not yourself from my supplication: the psalm opens begging an open ear and an unhidden God. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: Attend to me and hear me; I mourn in my complaint and make a noise: mourning complaint made aloud — grief is noisy, not stoic. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: Because of the voice of the enemy, because of the oppression of the wicked, for they cast iniquity upon me, and in wrath they hate me: the enemy's voice, the wicked's oppression, iniquity cast on him, and wrathful hatred are the causes of the noise. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: My heart is sore pained within me, and the terrors of death are fallen upon me: inward pain and death's terrors falling — the heart itself is the battlefield. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: Fearfulness and trembling are come upon me, and horror has overwhelmed me: fear, trembling, and overwhelming horror complete the portrait of a man undone by dread. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: And I said, Oh that I had wings like a dove! For then would I fly away and be at rest: the dove's wings are wished for — flight and rest, not victory, seem the only hope. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: Lo, then would I wander far off, and remain in the wilderness: far wandering and wilderness dwelling are the imagined refuge. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: I would hasten my escape from the windy storm and tempest: hastened escape from storm and tempest — the psalmist's troubles are weather, and he would outrun them. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: Destroy, O Lord, and divide their tongues, for I have seen violence and strife in the city: dividing tongues — as at Babel — answers the violence and strife seen in the city. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: Day and night they go about it upon the walls thereof; mischief also and sorrow are in the midst of it: day-and-night patrols of mischief and sorrow within the city walls — evil keeps watch. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: Wickedness is in the midst thereof; deceit and guile depart not from her streets: wickedness at the centre, deceit and guile never leaving the streets — the city is corrupt at heart and in public. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: For it was not an enemy that reproached me, then I could have borne it; neither was it he who hated me that magnified himself against me, then I would have hidden myself: an enemy's reproach could be borne, a hater's magnifying could be fled — but this is worse. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: But it was you, a man my equal, my guide, and my acquaintance: the betrayer was his equal, guide, and acquaintance — Ahithophel, in David's case, and the pattern of Judas. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: We took sweet counsel together, and walked to the house of God in company: sweet counsel and company to God's house make the betrayal bitter — shared worship, broken trust. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: Let death seize upon them, and let them go down quick into hell, for wickedness is in their dwellings and among them: sudden death and a quick descent to hell are prayed on those whose dwellings and company are wickedness. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: As for me, I will call upon God, and the Lord shall save me: against their fate stands his resolve — calling on God, and the Lord saving. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: Evening, and morning, and at noon, will I pray and cry aloud, and he shall hear my voice: thrice-daily prayer, cried aloud, with heard voice as its promise. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

18 COMMENTARY: He has delivered my soul in peace from the battle against me, for there were many with me: deliverance in peace from battle, with many on his side — God's host, or friends, outnumbering the foe. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

19 COMMENTARY: God shall hear and afflict them, even he who abides of old; because they have no changes, therefore they fear not God: the abiding God hears and afflicts the changeless, who fear not God precisely because nothing changes for them. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

20 COMMENTARY: He has put forth his hands against such as are at peace with him; he has broken his covenant: hands against the peaceable and a broken covenant describe the betrayer again — Ahithophel against David. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

21 COMMENTARY: The words of his mouth were smoother than butter, but war was in his heart; his words were softer than oil, yet they were drawn swords: butter-smooth, oil-soft words hide war in the heart and drawn swords in the mouth. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

22 COMMENTARY: Cast your burden upon the Lord, and he shall sustain you; he shall never suffer the righteous to be moved: the famous counsel — cast the burden, be sustained, never moved — is the psalm's practical centre. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

23 COMMENTARY: But you, O God, shall bring them down into the pit of destruction; bloody and deceitful men shall not live out half their days; but I will trust in you: the pit of destruction and half-lived days await the bloody and deceitful, while the psalmist's last word is trust. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
