Study Notes — Psalms

Psalm 121

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Henry lifts eyes to hills, or above hills: shall help come from hills' strength, princes like hills filling earth? No — in vain is salvation hoped for from hills — or lift eyes beyond instruments to God who makes them what they are, and to special presence on Moriah and Zion, temple, ark and oracle, whence help comes from word, prayer and secret of tabernacle. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

2 COMMENTARY: Henry lays all help in God: help desired is what he sends, expected in his own way and time — if he does not help no creature can, if he does none can hinder — and encourages confidence because he made heaven and earth from nothing by a word, sovereign of both hosts, able to use or restrain them for his people's succour. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

3 COMMENTARY: Henry promises feet kept: God will not suffer foot moved, providing saints not tempted above ability, not falling into sin though near it, nor into trouble though undermined by fraud or overthrown by force, nor frightened as when slipping — for he keeps feet of saints. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

4 COMMENTARY: Henry makes Israel's keeper every believer's keeper: wakeful, watchful, never weary, not so much as slumbering — the flock's shepherd is each sheep's shepherd, not one little one perishing, same wisdom, power and promises for church in general and particular believer. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

5 COMMENTARY: Henry calls God keeper and shade: infinite wisdom contriving and infinite power working safety, God himself guardian though angels are charged — and shade close as shadow to body, sheltering from scorching heat like a great rock in weary land, under which they sit with delight and assurance. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

6 COMMENTARY: Henry shields day and night: sun shall not smite with heat by day nor moon with cold and moisture by night, as wilderness pillar of cloud screened heat and fire diffused warmth against Jacob's drought by day and frost by night — figuratively, neither open assaults visible as sunbeams nor secret treacheries insinuating as night cold shall hurt. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

7 COMMENTARY: Henry preserves from all evil and especially the soul: evil of sin and trouble, preventing feared evil, sanctifying, removing or lightening felt evil, keeping from doing evil so affliction has no evil in it — and soul peculiarly cared for, not defiled by sin, disturbed by affliction, or perishing eternally, for soul is the man and all souls are his. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

8 COMMENTARY: Henry preserves going out and coming in for evermore: in all journeys outward or homeward, affairs at home and abroad, beginning and conclusion, life and death — going out to labour in morning of days and coming home to rest when evening of old age calls — a protection for life never out of date, guide even unto death, then hidden in grave and heaven, Spirit abiding for ever. — Matthew Henry (Complete)
