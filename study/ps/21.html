Study Notes — Psalms

Psalm 21

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: The king joys in God's strength and greatly rejoices in his salvation: the thanksgiving of Psalm 20 becomes the joy of Psalm 21. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: God has given him his heart's desire and not withheld the request of his lips: prayer answered is the ground of the joy. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: God prevents him — goes before him — with blessings of goodness, setting a crown of pure gold on his head: honour comes as a gift before it is earned. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: He asked life, and God gave length of days for ever and ever: for David this is long life and dynasty; in its fullest sense, as Henry notes, it points to the King who lives for ever. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: His glory is great in God's salvation, with honour and majesty laid upon him: the king's glory is borrowed from God's saving work. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: God has made him most blessed for ever and exceeding glad with his countenance: blessing and gladness flow from God's face, not from the crown alone. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: For the king trusts in the Lord, and through the mercy of the Most High he shall not be moved: trust and mercy, not armies, are his stability. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: God's hand shall find out all his enemies, and his right hand those who hate him: no enemy escapes the King's reach. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: He shall make them as a fiery oven in the time of his anger; the Lord shall swallow them up in wrath, and fire devour them: judgment is pictured as an oven that consumes wholly. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: Their fruit and seed shall be destroyed from the earth and from among men: the line of rebellion is cut off, not merely its present army. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: For they intended evil against God and imagined a device they cannot perform: intention against God fails because it exceeds human power. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: Therefore they shall turn their back when God makes ready his arrows against their face: the archery of judgment drives them to flight. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: Be exalted, Lord, in your own strength; so will we sing and praise your power: the psalm ends in worship of the strength that saved the king. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
