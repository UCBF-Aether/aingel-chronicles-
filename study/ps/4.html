Study Notes — Psalms

Psalm 4

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: David appeals to the God of his righteousness, who enlarged him in past distress; former deliverance is his plea for present mercy and a hearing. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: He turns on the sons of men who turn his glory into shame, loving vanity and seeking falsehood — preferring empty honours and lies to truth. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: The godly are set apart by God for himself, and therefore God will hear when they call; separation to God carries the promise of audience. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: Stand in awe and sin not: Henry urges trembling reverence, and quiet self-examination on one's bed, rather than answering slander with sin. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: Offer right sacrifices and trust the Lord: outward worship is worthless without the inward trust it should express. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: While many ask who will show them any good, David asks for the light of God's countenance — his favour is the true good, beyond all earthly plenty. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: God puts gladness in his heart greater than harvest joy: inward, God-given gladness outlasts the increase of corn and wine. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: In peace he lies down and sleeps, for the Lord alone makes him dwell in safety; safety is not in walls or armies but in God's keeping. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
