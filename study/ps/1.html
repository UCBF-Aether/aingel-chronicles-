Study Notes — Psalms

Psalm 1

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Blessedness begins negatively: the godly man refuses the counsel, the path, and finally the settled company of the ungodly, for sin deepens by degrees from listening to sitting down with scorners. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: Positively, his delight is in God's law, and he meditates on it constantly; Henry stresses that meditation is close, applied thinking, not a passing glance. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: Such a man is like a tree planted by water: rooted where grace supplies him, he bears fruit in season, his profession does not wither, and his lawful work prospers in soul-prosperity. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: The ungodly are the reverse: light, worthless chaff with no substance, easily driven by every wind of temptation and finally blown away by God's wrath. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: Therefore they cannot stand in judgment nor keep their place among the righteous; their exclusion is the consequence of the character described in verse 4. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: The Lord knows — approves and watches over — the way of the righteous, while the way of the ungodly ends in ruin; the two ways, and their two ends, are the whole psalm in miniature. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
