Psalm 79

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Henry opens with the church's complaint as a dishonour case: invasion is not only ruin for Jerusalem but defilement of God's inheritance and temple, which is why the prayer pleads his name rather than Israel's record. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

2 COMMENTARY: The dead bodies of God's servants were left as meat for birds and beasts, unburied in dishonour. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

3 COMMENTARY: Blood shed like water with none to bury turns slaughter into public shame: the unburied dead are the visible sign of a city laid waste. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

4 COMMENTARY: Reproach, scorn, and derision from neighbours make suffering social: God's people bear mockery from round about, not only violence from invaders. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

5 COMMENTARY: How long anger and jealousy burning like fire ask whether displeasure will be endless: Henry hears a plea distinguishing God's people from impenitent sinners, for whom wrath is indeed forever. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

6 COMMENTARY: It prays God to pour out his wrath instead on heathen kingdoms that have not known him or called on his name. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

7 COMMENTARY: Henry counts this crime alone as enough: the heathen did not merely disturb Jacob but devoured him, not merely encroach on his dwelling place but lay it waste by plundering and depopulating it. That is the weight of the plea, since whoever touches God's people touches the apple of his eye, and the prayer for wrath is in effect a prophecy of judgment on ungodliness. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

8 COMMENTARY: Former iniquities are pleaded against as an entail to be cut off by repentance: pardon blots out, and tender mercy must speedily prevent ruin for a people brought very low, since mercy, not merit, is the only hope left. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

9 COMMENTARY: Deliverance and purging are sought for the glory of the name, not for Israel's record: Henry insists pardon is the foundation and sweetness of deliverance, so prayer for sin's removal must outrank prayer for affliction's removal. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

10 COMMENTARY: Lest the heathen ask where their God is, the psalm prays that vengeance for shed blood be known among the heathen in their sight. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

11 COMMENTARY: Prisoners' sighs, silent because they dare not be heard, are recommended to divine pity and power: man's extremity is God's opportunity, and greatness of power must preserve those appointed to die. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

12 COMMENTARY: Sevenfold reproach returned into the bosom is prayed only where God's own name was blasphemed: Henry distinguishes reproach against us, left to forgiveness, from reproach against God, which may be prayed against as prophecy. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

13 COMMENTARY: Thanksgiving forever and praise to all generations close the psalm as a vow, not a mood: a people formed to show forth praise argue that if they are cut off, the tribute of praise itself ceases. — Matthew Henry (Complete)
