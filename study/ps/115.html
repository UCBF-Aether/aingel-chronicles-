Study Notes — Psalms

Psalm 115

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Henry excludes boasting for ever: no opinion of our own merits may enter prayer or praise, for all good we do is by grace and all good we have is mere mercy, so every crown is cast at the throne — and even unanswered prayer must end, whatever becomes of us, unto thy name give glory. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

2 COMMENTARY: Henry hears the heathen taunt as both complaint and plea: why should they ask where God is, when he is everywhere by providence and nigh by promise — and why is Israel brought so low as to give the taunt colour, unless God appear to vindicate his own name? — Matthew Henry (Complete)

3 COMMENTARY: Henry answers directly: our God is in the heavens, where heathen gods never were, and his power fills the lower world, for he has done whatsoever he pleased by sovereign counsel; ask where he is and he is at the beginning and end of everything, not far from any of us. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

4 COMMENTARY: Henry calls idols senseless things made of silver and gold dug poor and dirty from a mine — proper stuff for money, not for gods — the work of men's hands and vain imaginations, so that the argument is irrefragable: the workmen made it, therefore it is not God. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

5 COMMENTARY: Henry notes the idols' painted completeness mocks them: mouths and eyes were carved well enough, yet no life could be put in, so they speak not in answer to consulters — the crafty priest must speak for them, as Baal's image had no voice that answered. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

6 COMMENTARY: Henry adds ears that hear no prayer however loud, and noses that smell no incense however strong or sweet: the image is dead after pretended consecration exactly as before, with not so much as a breath of life. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

7 COMMENTARY: Henry completes the jest: hands that handle no gifts and can bestow none, feet that cannot stir a step for relief, and not even a mutter through the throat — fitter for a toy shop than a temple, for children to play with than for men to pray to. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

8 COMMENTARY: Henry infers the worshippers' sottishness: makers of images may be ingenious, but makers of gods are senseless like them, blind to God's invisible things in creation and deaf to day and night declaring his glory, sinking deeper into sense until God gives them up to a reprobate mind. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

9 COMMENTARY: Henry exhorts Israel as a body and each Israelite in private concerns to trust the living God, leaving God to dispose of all for the best; folly trusts dead images, wisdom trusts him who is a help to forward good and a shield against evil. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

10 COMMENTARY: Henry turns to Aaron's house: priests, most maligned and struck at, are under God's particular care and ought to be examples of cheerful confidence and faithful adherence in the worst of times. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

11 COMMENTARY: Henry includes the proselytes who fear the Lord though not of Israel's seed: he will not fail nor forsake them, for wherever there is awful fear of God there may be cheerful faith, and those who reverence his word may rely on it. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

12 COMMENTARY: Henry grounds trust in experience: the Lord has been mindful of us constantly and remarkably — of our case, wants, prayers, promises and covenant — and all our comforts flow from his thoughts toward us, though we have forgotten him. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

13 COMMENTARY: Henry extends the blessing beyond Israel and Aaron to all who fear the Lord in every nation, small and great, young and old, poor and figured, weak and strong in grace: the greatest need his blessing and it shall not be denied to the meanest that fear him. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

14 COMMENTARY: Henry reads increase as God's own blessing: increase in number, wealth and honour, but especially spiritual increase — knowledge, wisdom, grace, holiness and joy, constant and more and more till perfection, and hereditary, you in your children. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

15 COMMENTARY: Henry says the blessed of the Lord have encouragement enough to trust him, for their blesser made heaven and earth: his blessings are free, needing nothing himself, and rich, having all things at command for those who fear and trust him. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

16 COMMENTARY: Henry contrasts high glory with large goodness: the heaven of heavens is the Lord's own palace of light and bliss, yet the earth he has given to men for meat, drink and lodging — still his in chief, let out as a vineyard whose rents and account he will require. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

17 COMMENTARY: Henry distinguishes soul from body: faithful souls delivered from the flesh still praise God in perfect light, but the dead body cannot praise him here, death ending our glorifying service in this world of trial, and the grave being darkness and silence with no work or device. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

18 COMMENTARY: Henry closes with resolve born of that limit: others are dead and their service ended, so we will do the more to fill the gap, and we ourselves must shortly go to silence, so while we live we will bless the Lord, engage others, and praise him when we are gone, for evermore. — Matthew Henry (Complete)
