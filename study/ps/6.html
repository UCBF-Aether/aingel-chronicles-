Study Notes — Psalms

Psalm 6

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: David prays not to be rebuked in anger nor chastened in hot displeasure: he accepts correction but begs that it come in mercy, not wrath. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: Weakness is his plea: he is feeble, his bones vexed, and he asks for mercy and healing from the Lord who wounds and heals. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: His soul is sore vexed, and he cries, how long — the question of a sufferer who trusts God yet feels the delay. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: He asks the Lord to return, deliver his soul, and save him for mercies' sake: the ground of the plea is God's mercy, not David's merit. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: In death there is no remembrance and no thanks in the grave, in the sense of this life's public praise; David pleads that a life spared may still praise God on earth. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: Weary with groaning, he waters his bed with tears all night: grief is unhidden before God, and night makes it heavier. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: His eye is consumed with grief and aged by his enemies: sorrow shows in the body, and enemies aggravate it. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: Workers of iniquity are told to depart, for the Lord has heard his weeping: the turn of the psalm is from lament to assurance. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: The Lord has heard his supplication and will receive his prayer: hearing is repeated because certainty has replaced despair. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: His enemies shall be ashamed and sore vexed, turned back suddenly: the shame they planned for him falls on them, and swiftly. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
