Study Notes — Psalms

Psalm 69

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Save me, O God, for the waters are come in unto my soul: waters in unto the soul — drowning is the image of the whole psalm, applied by the New Testament to Christ's sufferings. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: I sink in deep mire, where there is no standing; I am come into deep waters, where the floods overflow me: sinking mire without standing, deep waters overflowing — no footing, no escape. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: I am weary of my crying; my throat is dried; my eyes fail while I wait for my God: weary crying, dried throat, failing eyes while waiting for God — prayer itself exhausts. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: They who hate me without a cause are more than the hairs of my head; they who would destroy me, being my enemies wrongfully, are mighty; then I restored that which I took not away: causeless haters outnumbering hairs, mighty wrongful enemies, and restoring what he never took — innocence made to pay. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: O God, you know my foolishness, and my sins are not hidden from you: foolishness known, sins unhidden — the sufferer confesses before God even while innocent toward his enemies. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: Let not those who wait on you, O Lord God of hosts, be ashamed for my sake; let not those who seek you be confounded for my sake, O God of Israel: waiters and seekers must not be shamed or confounded for his sake — his fate affects theirs. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: Because for your sake I have borne reproach; shame has covered my face: reproach borne for God's sake, shame covering his face — suffering for God, not for crime. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: I am become a stranger unto my brethren, and an alien unto my mother's children: stranger to brethren, alien to mother's children — the nearest become the farthest. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: For the zeal of your house has eaten me up, and the reproaches of those who reproached you are fallen upon me: house-zeal eating him up, reproaches against God falling on him — John applies this to Christ cleansing the temple. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: When I wept, and chastened my soul with fasting, that was to my reproach: weeping and fasting chastening became reproach — even penitence was mocked. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: I made sackcloth also my garment, and I became a proverb to them: sackcloth garment, proverb to them — mourning dress became a byword. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: They who sit in the gate speak against me, and I was the song of the drunkards: gate-sitters speaking against him, drunkards' song about him — high and low alike mock. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: But as for me, my prayer is unto you, O Lord, in an acceptable time; O God, in the multitude of your mercy hear me, in the truth of your salvation: prayer in an acceptable time, multitude of mercy, truth of salvation — the turn to prayer is timed by God's favour. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: Deliver me out of the mire, and let me not sink; let me be delivered from those who hate me, and out of the deep waters: deliverance from mire, from sinking, from haters, from deep waters — the drowning image is prayed against point by point. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: Let not the waterflood overflow me, neither let the deep swallow me up, and let not the pit shut her mouth upon me: waterflood not overflowing, deep not swallowing, pit not shutting her mouth — three drowning deaths are forbidden by prayer. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: Hear me, O Lord, for your lovingkindness is good; turn unto me according to the multitude of your tender mercies: hearing is asked because lovingkindness is good, and turning according to multiplied tender mercies. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: And hide not your face from your servant, for I am in trouble; hear me speedily: face unhidden, trouble confessed, speedy hearing asked. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

18 COMMENTARY: Draw nigh unto my soul, and redeem it; deliver me because of my enemies: nearness to the soul, redemption, deliverance because of enemies — God must come close to rescue. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

19 COMMENTARY: You have known my reproach, and my shame, and my dishonour; my adversaries are all before you: reproach, shame, dishonour known, adversaries all before God — nothing is hidden from the Judge. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

20 COMMENTARY: Reproach has broken my heart, and I am full of heaviness, and I looked for some to take pity, but there was none, and for comforters, but I found none: broken heart, full heaviness, pity sought and none, comforters sought and none — utter human desertion. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

21 COMMENTARY: They gave me also gall for my meat, and in my thirst they gave me vinegar to drink: gall for meat, vinegar for thirst — the Gospels record this literally at the cross. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

22 COMMENTARY: Let their table become a snare before them, and that which should have been for their welfare, let it become a trap: table to snare, welfare to trap — their own provision becomes their punishment. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

23 COMMENTARY: Let their eyes be darkened, that they see not, and make their loins continually to shake: darkened eyes, shaking loins — blindness and weakness as judgment. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

24 COMMENTARY: Pour out your indignation upon them, and let your wrathful anger take hold of them: poured indignation, wrathful anger taking hold — judgment as a flood poured out. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

25 COMMENTARY: Let their habitation be desolate, and let none dwell in their tents: desolate habitation, tents uninhabited — the persecutors' homes emptied. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

26 COMMENTARY: For they persecute him whom you have smitten, and they talk to the grief of those whom you have wounded: persecuting the God-smitten, talking to the wounded's grief — they add human cruelty to divine chastening. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

27 COMMENTARY: Add iniquity unto their iniquity, and let them not come into your righteousness: iniquity added to iniquity, exclusion from God's righteousness — the imprecation leaves them to their chosen course. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

28 COMMENTARY: Let them be blotted out of the book of the living, and not be written with the righteous: blotted from life's book, unwritten with the righteous — exclusion from the living and the just together. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

29 COMMENTARY: But I am poor and sorrowful; let your salvation, O God, set me up on high: poor and sorrowful, yet salvation setting him on high — the turn from imprecation to hope. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

30 COMMENTARY: I will praise the name of God with a song, and will magnify him with thanksgiving: song-praise and thanksgiving-magnifying are the response to salvation on high. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

31 COMMENTARY: This also shall please the Lord better than an ox or bullock that has horns and hoofs: song and thanksgiving please God better than horned, hoofed ox or bullock — praise outranks sacrifice. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

32 COMMENTARY: The humble shall see this, and be glad, and your heart shall live that seek God: humble seeing and glad, seekers' hearts living — the praise becomes others' life. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

33 COMMENTARY: For the Lord hears the poor, and despises not his prisoners: hearing the poor, not despising prisoners — God's ear is toward the low and bound. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

34 COMMENTARY: Let the heaven and earth praise him, the seas, and every thing that moves therein: heaven, earth, seas, and all moving things are bidden praise — creation joins the delivered man's song. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

35 COMMENTARY: For God will save Zion, and will build the cities of Judah, that they may dwell there, and have it in possession: Zion saved, Judah's cities built, dwelling and possession — deliverance becomes settlement. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

36 COMMENTARY: The seed also of his servants shall inherit it, and they who love his name shall dwell therein: servants' seed inheriting, name-lovers dwelling — the psalm ends in inheritance for the next generation. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
