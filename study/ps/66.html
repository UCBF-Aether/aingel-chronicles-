Study Notes — Psalms

Psalm 66

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Make a joyful noise unto God, all you lands: joyful noise from all lands opens the psalm of corporate thanksgiving. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: Sing forth the honour of his name; make his praise glorious: singing the honour of his name, making praise glorious — praise itself is to be glorious. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: Say unto God, How terrible are you in your works! Through the greatness of your power shall your enemies submit themselves unto you: terrible works and great power make enemies submit — the confession is addressed to God himself. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: All the earth shall worship you, and shall sing unto you; they shall sing to your name: all-earth worship and song to God's name — the psalm's horizon is worldwide. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: Come and see the works of God; he is terrible in his doing toward the children of men: come and see — the invitation is to inspect God's terrible doings toward men. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: He turned the sea into dry land; they went through the flood on foot; there did we rejoice in him: sea to dry land, flood crossed on foot, rejoicing there in him — the Exodus is the exhibit. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: He rules by his power for ever; his eyes behold the nations; let not the rebellious exalt themselves: everlasting rule, eyes on the nations, no exalting for the rebellious. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: O bless our God, you people, and make the voice of his praise to be heard: blessing God, with praise's voice heard — the peoples are bidden to be audible. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: Which holds our soul in life, and suffers not our feet to be moved: soul held in life, feet unmoved — preservation is both inward and outward. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: For you, O God, have proved us; you have tried us, as silver is tried: proving and trying as silver — affliction is assay, not abandonment. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: You brought us into the net; you laid affliction upon our loins: net and loins' affliction are ascribed to God — he, not chance, brought the trial. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: You have caused men to ride over our heads; we went through fire and through water; but you brought us out into a wealthy place: men riding over heads, fire and water passed through, then a wealthy place — the sequence is trial, then abundance, all under God's bringing. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: I will go into your house with burnt offerings; I will pay you my vows: house-entry with burnt offerings, vows paid — worship follows deliverance. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: Which my lips have uttered, and my mouth has spoken, when I was in trouble: lips' utterances in trouble are now honoured in the house. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: I will offer unto you burnt sacrifices of fatlings, with the incense of rams; I will offer bullocks with goats: fatlings, rams' incense, bullocks with goats — the offering is costly and complete. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: Come and hear, all you that fear God, and I will declare what he has done for my soul: come and hear, you God-fearers — the testimony turns personal, what God did for my soul. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: I cried unto him with my mouth, and he was extolled with my tongue: crying with the mouth, extolling with the tongue — prayer and praise are one act. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

18 COMMENTARY: If I regard iniquity in my heart, the Lord will not hear me: regarded iniquity in the heart blocks hearing — the condition of answered prayer is stated plainly. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

19 COMMENTARY: But verily God has heard me; he has attended to the voice of my prayer: verily heard, attended to the voice of prayer — the condition met, the answer given. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

20 COMMENTARY: Blessed be God, which has not turned away my prayer, nor his mercy from me: prayer unturned, mercy unremoved — the psalm ends in blessing for both. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
