Study Notes — Psalms

Psalm 101

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Singing of mercy and judgment is, first, singing of God's mercy and judgment in providence toward David since he was anointed king, during which he met many a rebuke and much hardship on the one hand and many wonderful deliverances and favours on the other. God's providences concerning his people are commonly mixed — mercy and judgment, set over against each other like April-days, showers and sunshine: mercy in the return of the ark, judgment in the death of Uzza. When God exercises us with such a mixture it is our duty to sing, and sing unto him, of both, suitably affected with both and making suitable acknowledgments for both — neither the laughter of a prosperous condition nor the tears of an afflicted condition must put us out of tune for sacred songs. Or it may be understood of David's own mercy and judgment, which he covenants to make conscience of in his place: to do justly and love mercy is the sum of our duty, and family mercies and family afflictions are both calls to family religion — and David put his vow into a song that he might the better keep it in mind, repeat it frequently, and communicate it to others and preserve it in his family as a pattern to his sons and successors. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

2 COMMENTARY: Behaving wisely in a perfect way and walking within the house with a perfect heart join rule and sincerity: the law is the perfect way, the heart must not dissemble, and the prayer for God's coming follows, since David behaved wisely and the Lord was with him. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

3 COMMENTARY: I will set no wicked thing before mine eyes: I hate the work of them that turn aside; it shall not cleave to me, is David's particular resolution to practise no evil himself: I will set no wicked thing before my eyes — I will not design nor aim at anything but what is for the glory of God and the public welfare. He will never have it in his eye to enrich himself by impoverishing his subjects, or enlarge his own prerogative by encroaching on their property. In all our worldly business we must see that what we set our eyes upon be right and good and not any forbidden fruit, and that we never seek that which we cannot have without sin. It is the character of a good man that he shuts his eyes from seeing evil. Nay, I hate the work of those that turn aside from the paths of equity, not only I avoid it, but I abhor it; it shall not cleave to me. If any blot of injustice should come on my hands, it shall be washed off quickly. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

4 COMMENTARY: A froward heart departing from him is David's resolution not to keep bad servants nor employ vicious men about him, lest by countenancing them he harden them in wickedness and encourage others to do likewise, lest by conversing with them and admitting them into his other servants' company he spread the infection of sin in his family, and lest by confiding in them and putting them in power he hinder everything good, since those who hated to be reformed would certainly hinder it. He names not gross sinners like drunkards, adulterers, murderers or blasphemers, whom he was in no danger of admitting, but those whose sins were less scandalous and no less dangerous: spiteful, malicious people, ill-natured, bearing a grudge a great while and caring not what mischief they do to those they have a pique against. A froward heart, one that delights to be cross and perverse, shall depart as not fit for society, whose bond is love, and David will have no acquaintance or conversation with such a wicked person, for a little of the leaven of malice and wickedness will leaven the whole lump. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

5 COMMENTARY: Whoso privily slandereth his neighbour, him will I cut off: him that hath an high look and a proud heart will not I suffer, is David's guard against slanderers and the haughty: second, with slanderers, and those who take a pleasure in wounding their neighbour's reputation secretly: whoso privily slanders his neighbour, either raises or spreads false stories, to the prejudice of his good name, him will I cut off from my family and court. Many endeavour to raise themselves into the favour of princes by unjust representations of persons and things, which they think will please their prince. If a ruler hearken to lies, all his servants are wicked. But David will not only not hearken to them, but will prevent the preferment of those that hope thus to curry favour with him: he will punish not only him that falsely accuses another in open court, but him that privily slanders another. I wish David had remembered this vow in the case of Mephibosheth and Ziba. Third, with haughty, conceited, ambitious people: none do more mischief in a family, in a court, in a church, for only by pride comes contention: therefore him that has a high look and a proud heart will I not suffer — I will have no patience with those that are still grasping at all preferments, for it is certain that they do not aim at doing good, but only at aggrandizing themselves and their families. God resists the proud, and so will David. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

6 COMMENTARY: Mine eyes shall be upon the faithful of the land, that they may dwell with me: he that walketh in a perfect way, he shall serve me, is David's resolution to put those in trust under him that were honest and good: my eyes shall be upon the faithful in the land. In choosing his servants and ministers of state he kept to the land of Israel and would not employ foreigners; none shall be preferred but true-born Israelites, and those such as were Israelites indeed, the faithful in the land — for even in that land there were those that were unfaithful. These faithful ones his eyes shall be upon, to discover them and find them out: for they were modest, did not crowd into the city to court preferment, but lived retired in the land, in the country, out of the way of it. Those are commonly most fit for places of honour and trust that are least fond of them; and therefore wise princes will spy out such in their recesses and privacies, and take them to dwell with them and act under them. He that walks in a perfect way, that makes conscience of what he says and does, shall serve me. The kingdom must be searched for honest men to make courtiers of; and, if any man is better than another, he must be preferred. This was a good resolution of David's; but either he did not keep to it or else his judgment was imposed upon when he made Ahithophel his right hand. It should be the care and endeavour of all masters of families, for their own sakes and their children's, to take such servants into their families as they have reason to hope fear God. The Son of David has his eyes upon the faithful in the land; his secret is with them, and they shall dwell with him. Saul chose servants for their goodliness, but David for their goodness. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

7 COMMENTARY: He that worketh deceit shall not dwell within my house: he that telleth lies shall not tarry in my sight, is David's guard against false deceitful people, that scruple not to tell lies, or commit frauds: he that worketh deceit, though he may insinuate himself into my family, yet, as soon as he is discovered, shall not dwell within my house. Some great men know how to serve their own purposes by such as are skilful to deceive, and they are fit tools for them to work by; but David will make use of no such persons as agents for him: he that tells lies shall not tarry in my sight, but shall be expelled the house with indignation. Herein David was a man after God's own heart, for a proud look and a lying tongue are things which God hates; and he was also a type of Christ, who will, in the great day, banish from his presence all that love and make a lie. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

8 COMMENTARY: Early destroying all the wicked of the land extends David's zeal to the reformation of city and country as well as court: all discovered and convicted shall have the law's course against them, and he would do his utmost to destroy all the wicked so that none notoriously wicked might be left, doing it early, losing no time and sparing no pains, forward and zealous in promoting reformation of manners and suppression of vice — for those must rise betimes that will do anything to purpose in the work. His aim was not only securing his own government and the country's peace but the honour of God in the purity of his church, cutting off all wicked doers from the city of the Lord — not Jerusalem only, but the whole land, as the gospel-church is — for it is the city's interest to be purged from wicked doers, who both blemish and weaken it, and the day is coming when the Son of David shall cut off all wicked doers from the new Jerusalem, into which nothing that does iniquity shall enter. — Matthew Henry (Complete)
