Psalm 123

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Henry lifts eyes to God dwelling in heavens, where glory manifests as King in court, prospect and power beholding calamities and able to send salvation — and to throne where injured innocency appeals, even when enemies ask Where is now your God, for our God is in the heavens. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

2 COMMENTARY: Henry waits as servants on a master's hand in six ways: directing hand appointing work, supplying hand giving daily bread and grace sufficient, assisting hand for strength, protecting hand righting wronged servants, correcting hand humbly submitted to without fleeing as Hagar did, and rewarding hand — true Christians looking to God as rewarder, not hypocrites to the world's hand — until mercy comes. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

3 COMMENTARY: Henry sues mercy without prescribing or pleading merit: have mercy upon us, repeated, for with men tender mercies are cruel and mockings cruel, but with the Lord there is mercy, sovereign remedy for whatever troubles the church, all we need to relieve and make easy. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

4 COMMENTARY: Henry is surfeited with contempt: souls exceedingly filled, enemies never jeering enough, a sword in bones — reproached though eyes are up to God, peaceable saints abused, excellent ones undervalued, by ease-living epicures serving bellies and proud defying God, magnifying themselves by vilifying — yet scorn has ever been God's people's lot, Ishmael mocking Isaac, and mercy with God comforts barbarously used names. — Matthew Henry (Complete)
