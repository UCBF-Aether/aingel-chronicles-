Psalm 73

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Truly God is good to Israel, even to such as are of a clean heart: the thesis is stated first — God is good to Israel, to the clean in heart — before the doubt that follows. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: But as for me, my feet were almost gone; my steps had well nigh slipped: almost gone feet, well-nigh slipped steps — the psalmist's faith nearly fell. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: For I was envious at the foolish, when I saw the prosperity of the wicked: envy at the foolish, at the wicked's prosperity, was the slipping cause. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: For there are no bands in their death, but their strength is firm: no bands — no pangs — in their death, strength firm — the wicked die easy and strong, it seems. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: They are not in trouble as other men, neither are they plagued like other men: no common trouble, no common plague — exemption from ordinary suffering is their lot. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: Therefore pride compasses them about as a chain; violence covers them as a garment: pride as a chain, violence as a garment — prosperity dresses them in pride and violence. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: Their eyes stand out with fatness; they have more than heart could wish: eyes standing out with fatness, more than heart could wish — excess beyond desire. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: They are corrupt, and speak wickedly concerning oppression; they speak loftily: corrupt, wicked speech about oppression, lofty speech — their talk matches their pride. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: They set their mouth against the heavens, and their tongue walks through the earth: mouth against heaven, tongue walking through earth — blasphemy above, oppression below, in one speech. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: Therefore his people return hither, and waters of a full cup are wrung out to them: God's people return hither — to this perplexity — and drink a full cup wrung out, of sorrow or of the wicked's teaching. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: And they say, How does God know? And is there knowledge in the Most High? 'How does God know?' and 'Is there knowledge in the Most High?' — the wicked's denial of divine knowledge is quoted. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: Behold, these are the ungodly, who prosper in the world; they increase in riches: behold the ungodly prospering in the world, increasing in riches — the fact that caused the envy, stated as conclusion. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: Verily I have cleansed my heart in vain, and washed my hands in innocency: cleansing heart and washing hands in innocency seem vain — the psalmist's bitter inference. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: For all the day long have I been plagued, and chastened every morning: plagued all day, chastened every morning — the godly man's lot, against the wicked's ease. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: If I say, I will speak thus, behold, I should offend against the generation of your children: speaking thus would offend God's children — loyalty to the generation restrains his doubt from becoming public unbelief. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: When I thought to know this, it was too painful for me: thinking to know this was too painful — reason alone could not solve it. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: Until I went into the sanctuary of God; then understood I their end: the sanctuary is the turning point — entering God's presence, he understood their end. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

18 COMMENTARY: Surely you did set them in slippery places; you cast them down into destruction: slippery places set, casting down into destruction — prosperity itself was the slippery ground. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

19 COMMENTARY: How are they brought into desolation, as in a moment! They are utterly consumed with terrors: desolation in a moment, utterly consumed with terrors — suddenness and completeness mark their fall. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

20 COMMENTARY: As a dream when one awakes, so, O Lord, when you awake, you shall despise their image: as a dream on waking, so God on awaking despises their image — their glory was a dream. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

21 COMMENTARY: Thus my heart was grieved, and I was pricked in my reins: grieved heart, pricked reins — the envy had wounded him inwardly, and he now sees it. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

22 COMMENTARY: So foolish was I, and ignorant; I was as a beast before you: foolish, ignorant, beast-like before God — the confession of his envy as brutishness. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

23 COMMENTARY: Nevertheless I am continually with you; you have held me by my right hand: nevertheless — despite beast-like folly — continually with God, held by the right hand. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

24 COMMENTARY: You shall guide me with your counsel, and afterward receive me to glory: guided by counsel now, received to glory afterward — guidance and glory are the two ends of God's holding. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

25 COMMENTARY: Whom have I in heaven but you? And there is none upon earth that I desire beside you: none in heaven but God, none on earth desired beside him — the psalm's climax of exclusive desire. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

26 COMMENTARY: My flesh and my heart fail, but God is the strength of my heart, and my portion for ever: failing flesh and heart, yet God as heart's strength and portion for ever — the answer to prosperity-envy is an everlasting portion. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

27 COMMENTARY: For, lo, they who are far from you shall perish; you have destroyed all them that go a whoring from you: far from God perishing, whoring from God destroyed — distance from God is itself ruin. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

28 COMMENTARY: But it is good for me to draw near to God; I have put my trust in the Lord God, that I may declare all your works: good to draw near, trust in the Lord God, declaring all his works — nearness, trust, and testimony close the psalm. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
