Study Notes — Psalms

Psalm 49

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Hear this, all you people; give ear, all inhabitants of the world: the audience is universal — all peoples and all the world's inhabitants are summoned. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: Both low and high, rich and poor, together: low and high, rich and poor together — no rank is exempt from the lesson. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: My mouth shall speak of wisdom, and the meditation of my heart shall be of understanding: mouth and heart agree — spoken wisdom from meditated understanding. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: I will incline my ear to a parable; I will open my dark saying upon the harp: a parable and a dark saying, opened on the harp, are the form of the wisdom — truth sung, not merely stated. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: Wherefore should I fear in the days of evil, when the iniquity of my heels shall compass me about? Fear in evil days, when heel-iniquity — sins dogging his steps, or supplanters at his heels — compasses him, is questioned as needless. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: They that trust in their wealth, and boast themselves in the multitude of their riches: wealth-trust and riches-boasting are the folly to be exposed. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: None of them can by any means redeem his brother, nor give to God a ransom for him: no wealth can redeem a brother or pay God a ransom — redemption is beyond purchase. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: For the redemption of their soul is precious, and it ceases for ever: the soul's redemption is so precious that the price ceases for ever — it cannot be paid. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: That he should still live for ever, and not see corruption: the unattainable aim is stated — to live for ever and never see corruption, which wealth cannot buy. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: For he sees that wise men die, likewise the fool and the brutish person perish, and leave their wealth to others: wise, fool, and brutish alike die and leave wealth to others — death levels wisdom and folly. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: Their inward thought is that their houses shall continue for ever, and their dwelling places to all generations; they call their lands after their own names: inwardly they dream of everlasting houses and named lands — a counterfeit eternity. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: Nevertheless man being in honour abides not; he is like the beasts that perish: honour without abiding makes man like perishing beasts. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: This their way is their folly, yet their posterity approve their sayings: the way is folly, yet posterity approves — folly is inherited as wisdom. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: Like sheep they are laid in the grave; death shall feed on them; and the upright shall have dominion over them in the morning; and their beauty shall consume in the grave from their dwelling: sheep in the grave, death as shepherd feeding on them, the upright ruling in the morning, beauty consumed — the reversal is total. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: But God will redeem my soul from the power of the grave, for he shall receive me: against wealth's failure stands God's redemption from the grave's power, and his receiving — the psalm's hope of life beyond death. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: Be not afraid when one is made rich, when the glory of his house is increased: fear at another's riches and increased house-glory is forbidden. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: For when he dies he shall carry nothing away; his glory shall not descend after him: death strips him — nothing carried, glory not descending. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

18 COMMENTARY: Though while he lived he blessed his soul, and men will praise you when you do well to yourself: self-blessing in life and men's praise for self-service are the rich man's portion, and it ends at death. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

19 COMMENTARY: He shall go to the generation of his fathers; they shall never see light: he joins his fathers' generation, who never see light — darkness is the end of the unredeemed rich. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

20 COMMENTARY: Man that is in honour, and understands not, is like the beasts that perish: the refrain returns with its key — honour without understanding is beast-like perishing. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
