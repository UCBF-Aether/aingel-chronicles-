Study Notes — Psalms

Psalm 2

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: The psalm opens with astonishment at the nations' futile rage: their plotting against God is as vain as imagining what can never stand. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: Earthly kings and rulers combine against the Lord and his Anointed, the Messiah; JFB notes this is fulfilled in the opposition to Christ. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: Their cry is for licence: they would break God's bands and cast off his cords, mistaking his government for bondage. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: God in heaven laughs, not in cruelty but in sovereign contempt for rebellion that cannot touch his throne. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: His laughter gives way to wrathful speech: he troubles his enemies with the displeasure their defiance has earned. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: Yet God's decree stands unmoved: his King is already set on Zion, his holy hill, whatever men resolve. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: The King declares the decree: the Lord calls him his Son, begotten this day — the eternal Sonship and the resurrection dignity that Henry and JFB both trace here. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: The Son need only ask, and the nations become his inheritance, to the ends of the earth; his kingdom is universal, not local. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: Rebellion will be broken with a rod of iron and dashed like a potter's vessel: judgment is certain, and resistance is brittle. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: Kings and judges are therefore counselled to be wise and instructed — to learn from the fate of all who fight God. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: True service joins fear with trembling joy: reverence without gloom, and gladness without presumption. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: To kiss the Son is to submit and honour him before wrath is kindled; those who trust in him, rather than in their own power, are blessed. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
