Study Notes — Psalms

Psalm 45

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: My heart is inditing a good matter; I speak of things made touching the king; my tongue is the pen of a ready writer: the heart overflows with a good theme — the King — and the tongue writes swiftly, as a ready scribe's pen. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: You are fairer than the children of men; grace is poured into your lips; therefore God has blessed you for ever: beauty and gracious speech mark the King, and God's everlasting blessing rests on him — the portrait Henry and JFB read as Christ, fairer than all men. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: Gird your sword upon your thigh, O most mighty, with your glory and majesty: the mighty King arms himself, and glory and majesty are his equipment, not mere ornament. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: And in your majesty ride prosperously because of truth and meekness and righteousness; and your right hand shall teach you terrible things: his prosperous ride is for truth, meekness, and righteousness, and his right hand will do terrible deeds against their enemies. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: Your arrows are sharp in the heart of the king's enemies, whereby the people fall under you: sharp arrows in enemies' hearts bring peoples into subjection — conviction and conquest together. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: Your throne, O God, is for ever and ever; the sceptre of your kingdom is a right sceptre: the King is addressed as God, his throne everlasting, his sceptre straight — Hebrews applies this to Christ. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: You love righteousness and hate wickedness; therefore God, your God, has anointed you with the oil of gladness above your fellows: love of righteousness and hatred of wickedness are the ground of an anointing with gladness above all fellows. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: All your garments smell of myrrh, aloes, and cassia, out of the ivory palaces, whereby they have made you glad: perfumed garments from ivory palaces picture the King's gladness and royal fragrance. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: Kings' daughters were among your honourable women; upon your right hand stood the queen in gold of Ophir: honourable women and a queen in Ophir gold attend the royal wedding. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: Hearken, O daughter, and consider, and incline your ear; forget also your own people and your father's house: the bride is bidden to listen, consider, and leave her former people and house — a new allegiance for a new marriage. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: So shall the king greatly desire your beauty, for he is your Lord; and worship him: the King's desire follows her forsaking, and her worship follows his lordship. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: And the daughter of Tyre shall be there with a gift; even the rich among the people shall entreat your favour: Tyre's daughter with gifts, and the rich entreating favour, show Gentiles drawn to the royal marriage. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: The king's daughter is all glorious within; her clothing is of wrought gold: inward glory and gold-wrought clothing together — the bride is glorious within before she is glorious without. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: She shall be brought to the king in raiment of needlework; the virgins her companions that follow her shall be brought to you: needlework raiment and virgin companions in procession mark the bridal entry. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: With gladness and rejoicing shall they be brought; they shall enter into the king's palace: gladness and rejoicing, not compulsion, bring them into the palace. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: Instead of your fathers shall be your children, whom you may make princes in all the earth: children replace fathers, and become princes in all the earth — the dynasty spreads worldwide. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: I will make your name to be remembered in all generations; therefore the people shall praise you for ever and ever: the King's name remembered through generations draws praise for ever — the psalm ends in the eternity it began to describe. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
