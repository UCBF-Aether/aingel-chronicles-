Study Notes — Psalms

Psalm 80

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Shepherd, flock-leader, and dweller between cherubim are titles for prayer: Henry reads the address as looking to a throne of grace, where petitions are received and directions given. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

2 COMMENTARY: Three tribes named are the squadron that marched next after the tabernacle: strength stirred up before them means deliverance shown in sight of all Israel. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

3 COMMENTARY: Turn us again, O God, and cause thy face to shine, and we shall be saved — the psalm's refrain of restoration. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

4 COMMENTARY: Anger against the prayer of his own people is the strangest grief in the psalm: Henry suggests either prayers asked amiss, with secret sin or doubting, or a seeming anger meant to try patience, as with the Canaanite woman. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

5 COMMENTARY: Bread of tears eaten day by day and tears drunk in great measure make sorrow a diet, not an episode: Henry notes many spend time in sorrow who shall spend eternity in joy. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

6 COMMENTARY: Made a strife to neighbours, with enemies laughing among themselves, turns divine frowning into public shame: a people become cheap prey, and strife is over who shall strip them. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

7 COMMENTARY: Again the refrain: turn us again, O God of hosts, cause thy face to shine, and we shall be saved. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

8 COMMENTARY: Vine brought from Egypt, heathen cast out, planting in their place: Henry's church is weak and unsightly yet spreading and fruitful, its root Christ and its branches believers. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

9 COMMENTARY: Room swept before it and deep root taken describe establishment in church and state so firm that neighbours' attempts could not pluck it up. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

10 COMMENTARY: Hills covered with shadow and boughs like goodly cedars show a land fully peopled and strong men raised, yet Henry notes the praise is for shadow and branches, not fruit: Israel was an empty vine. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

11 COMMENTARY: Boughs to the sea and branches to the river map dominion west, south, north, and east: conquest extended, while fruit, the one thing a vine exists for, is missing. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

12 COMMENTARY: Hedges broken down raise Henry's question of desertion: a plant of God's own planting is exposed, yet former favours are urged as pleas that he will not wholly cast off. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

13 COMMENTARY: Boar and wild beast ravaging only after the hedge falls teach restraint: until God withdrew protection, enemies could not pluck a leaf, so defence departed is the real calamity. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

14 COMMENTARY: Return, look down from heaven, behold and visit: heaven is at once place of prospect, of power, and of judgment, and the prayer refers its own case to God's compassionate consideration. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

15 COMMENTARY: Vineyard planted by the right hand and branch made strong for God's own praise: Henry hears branch as son, the stock preserved until the Branch himself should come, the true vine. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

16 COMMENTARY: It is burned with fire, it is cut down, they perish at the rebuke of thy countenance, is the deplorable state of Israel described: it is burnt with fire, it is cut down — the people are treated like thorns and briers, that are nigh unto cursing and whose end is to be burned, and no longer like vines that are protected and cherished. They perish not through the rage of the wild beast and the boar, but at the rebuke of thy countenance; that was it which they dreaded and to which they attributed all their calamities. It is well or ill with us according as we are under God's smiles or frowns. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

17 COMMENTARY: Hand upon the man of the right hand asks not only protection but ownership, strengthening, and success for the Davidic king as under-shepherd under the great Shepherd, an instrument in God's right hand. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

18 COMMENTARY: Not going back, quickening, and calling on the name form the closing bargain of the section: a people prepared for mercy ask to be kept from apostasy, so that prayer itself becomes the evidence of life. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

19 COMMENTARY: The full refrain closes the psalm: turn us again, O Lord God of hosts, cause thy face to shine, and we shall be saved. — Matthew Henry (Complete)
