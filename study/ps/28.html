Study Notes — Psalms

Psalm 28

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Unto you I will cry, O Lord my rock; be not silent to me, lest if you are silent I become like those who go down to the pit: God's silence would leave him as good as dead, so urgent is the need for an answer. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: Hear the voice of my supplications when I cry to you, lifting my hands toward your holy oracle: hands lifted toward the mercy-seat accompany the cry. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: Draw me not away with the wicked and workers of iniquity, who speak peace to neighbours but have mischief in their hearts: he prays not to share the fate of the peace-speaking, mischief-plotting wicked. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: Give them according to their deeds and the wickedness of their endeavours, after the work of their hands; render to them their desert: justice is asked to match deed for deed. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: Because they regard not the works of the Lord nor the operation of his hands, he shall destroy them and not build them up: contempt for God's works ends in being unbuilt — destroyed, not restored. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: Blessed be the Lord, because he has heard the voice of my supplications: the psalm turns, as often, from petition to blessing on the strength of heard prayer. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: The Lord is my strength and my shield; my heart trusted in him and I am helped; therefore my heart greatly rejoices, and with my song I will praise him: trust, help, joy, and song follow in order. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: The Lord is their strength, and the saving strength of his anointed: the help given to David is the people's help, and the anointed king's strength is theirs. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: Save your people, bless your inheritance, feed them also, and lift them up for ever: the prayer widens to shepherding — saving, blessing, feeding, and lifting the people for ever. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
