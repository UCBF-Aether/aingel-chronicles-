Study Notes — Psalms

Psalm 116

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Henry opens with love born of answered prayer: David had humbly and earnestly begged mercy, and God graciously accepted it, took cognizance of his case and granted an answer of peace, so love of complacency answers God's love of compassion. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

2 COMMENTARY: Henry reads the inclined ear as wonderful condescension — God laying his ear, as it were, to whispered groanings — and as encouragement to pray for life: having sped well despite unworthiness, why glean in any other field, but call upon him every day to the last day? — Matthew Henry (Complete)

3 COMMENTARY: Henry pictures sorrows likely to be his death compassing him on every side, pains of hell as terror of conscience from guilt, arresting him so he could not escape; without were fightings, within were fears, and melancholy sometimes creates its own trouble by indulging fancy. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

4 COMMENTARY: Henry calls this prayer the old and only remedy, not a last one, short but to the purpose and beggar-like: deliver my soul — save from death and from sin, which is what kills the soul — pleaded on God's graciousness itself. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

5 COMMENTARY: Henry testifies God is both righteous and gracious: righteous, doing no wrong in afflicting, and gracious in supporting and delivering; speak of God as we have found him, and have we ever found him otherwise than just and good? — Matthew Henry (Complete)

6 COMMENTARY: Henry says God preserves the simple — sincere and upright without guile, and also weak and helpless, unable to shift for themselves — because they commit themselves to him with no confidence in their own sufficiency; those under his protection shall be safe. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

7 COMMENTARY: Henry bids the soul return to its rest in God alone, not agitated by distrustful fears: God has dealt bountifully, providing for comfort and refreshment, so be satisfied — a word fit to close the eyes at night and at death, return to thy Noah, the ark of rest. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

8 COMMENTARY: Henry counts a threefold deliverance: soul from death, a life often forfeited yet lengthened as a miracle of mercy; eyes from tears, kept from being swallowed by over-much sorrow until God wipes all tears away; feet from falling into sin, misery or the grave, held by God's right hand. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

9 COMMENTARY: Henry makes walking before the Lord the devoted life: doing all in his presence and under his eye, in conformity and cheerful confidence, quickened by being spared in the land of the living — a land of mercy and opportunity, where a new life must be a new life indeed. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

10 COMMENTARY: Henry notes faith confessed with the mouth: David believed God's being, providence and promise — Samuel's assurance of sceptre for crook — and therefore spoke to God in prayer and praise, to his own soul, and to friends, though speaking cost him great affliction. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

11 COMMENTARY: Henry is honest about haste: under sore, long affliction David spoke rashly in consternation and flight, calling all men liars as friends deserted him, yet the best saints' faith is imperfect; what is spoken amiss in haste must by repentance be unsaid, and in the apostle's sense, let God be true and every man a liar. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

12 COMMENTARY: Henry hears a man sensible of many mercies and studious how to express gratitude: one benefit brings many to mind, and he asks what he shall render, not as if recompense were possible, but seeking something acceptable as acknowledgment of a grateful mind. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

13 COMMENTARY: Henry takes the cup of salvation as drink-offering in memory of deliverance, and also as whatever cup God puts in his hand — affliction sanctified to salvation, as Christ's own cup, or consolation tasted as God's love — together with calling on the Lord again, for God counts his people's prayers an honour, not a trouble. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

14 COMMENTARY: Henry insists vows are debts to be paid presently, not delayed like sorry debtors, and publicly in the presence of all his people — not for ostentation, but unashamed, inviting others to join. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

15 COMMENTARY: Henry says saints' deaths are precious in God's sight: he will not gratify Saul or Absalom with David's death, often prevents it when but a step away, orders its circumstances for the best, and will make killers pay dearly when inquisition is made for saints' blood — a comfort that should make us willing to die for Christ. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

16 COMMENTARY: Henry glories in servanthood over kingship: truly I am thy servant, by birth as son of God's handmaid and by redemption, since God loosed the bonds of death's sorrows — and the very bonds loosed shall tie him faster to his Master. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

17 COMMENTARY: Henry repeats the thank-offering and the call: thankful hearts express gratitude in sacrifices of piety and charity, first giving themselves as living sacrifices, and God is well pleased with doing good and communicating as accompaniment to thanks. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

18 COMMENTARY: Henry repeats the public payment of vows, now in the presence of all his people, showing he was not ashamed of God's service and that others might be invited to join him. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

19 COMMENTARY: Henry places that public praise in the courts of the Lord's house, in the midst of Jerusalem, amid a crowd of Israelites, that devotion might be brought into more reputation. — Matthew Henry (Complete)
