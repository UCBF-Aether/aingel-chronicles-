Study Notes — Psalms

Psalm 50

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: The mighty God, even the Lord, has spoken, and called the earth from the rising of the sun to the going down thereof: the Mighty God summons the whole earth, east to west, as witnesses to his judgment. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: Out of Zion, the perfection of beauty, God has shined: from Zion, beauty's perfection, God shines forth — judgment comes as light. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: Our God shall come, and shall not keep silence; a fire shall devour before him, and it shall be very tempestuous round about him: coming, not silence, with devouring fire and tempest — the Judge arrives in majesty. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: He shall call to the heavens from above, and to the earth, that he may judge his people: heaven and earth are called as witnesses for the judgment of his own people. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: Gather my saints together unto me, those that have made a covenant with me by sacrifice: saints are gathered as covenant-makers by sacrifice — judgment begins at God's own covenant people. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: And the heavens shall declare his righteousness, for God is judge himself: heaven declares the Judge's righteousness, for God himself is judge — no deputy, no appeal. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: Hear, O my people, and I will speak; O Israel, and I will testify against you; I am God, even your God: the testimony is against Israel itself, from God who is their God — relation makes the charge heavier. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: I will not reprove you for your sacrifices or burnt offerings, to have been continually before me: sacrifices are not the complaint — they were continually offered, and that is not the reproof. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: I will take no bullock out of your house, nor he-goats out of your folds: God needs no bullock or goat from them. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: For every beast of the forest is mine, and the cattle upon a thousand hills: forest beasts and cattle on a thousand hills are already his — sacrifice adds nothing to his wealth. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: I know all the fowls of the mountains, and the wild beasts of the field are mine: known fowls and owned wild beasts — God's knowledge and ownership cover all. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: If I were hungry, I would not tell you, for the world is mine, and the fullness thereof: even hunger — which God cannot know — would not make him dependent, for world and fullness are his. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: Will I eat the flesh of bulls, or drink the blood of goats? The question exposes the absurdity of imagining God fed by sacrifice. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: Offer unto God thanksgiving, and pay your vows unto the Most High: thanksgiving and paid vows are the sacrifices God does desire. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: And call upon me in the day of trouble; I will deliver you, and you shall glorify me: calling in trouble, deliverance, and glorifying God form the true sacrifice's sequence. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: But unto the wicked God says, What have you to do to declare my statutes, or that you should take my covenant in your mouth? The wicked's mouthing of statutes and covenant is challenged — profession without practice has no right to God's words. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: Seeing you hate instruction, and cast my words behind you: hatred of instruction and words cast behind the back explain the challenge. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

18 COMMENTARY: When you saw a thief, then you consented with him, and have been partaker with adulterers: consent with thieves and partnership with adulterers are the wicked's company. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

19 COMMENTARY: You give your mouth to evil, and your tongue frames deceit: mouth given to evil, tongue framing deceit — speech is the workshop of sin. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

20 COMMENTARY: You sit and speak against your brother; you slander your own mother's son: sitting slander against a brother, even a mother's son, is the wicked's household sin. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

21 COMMENTARY: These things you have done, and I kept silence; you thought that I was altogether such a one as yourself; but I will reprove you, and set them in order before your eyes: God's silence was misread as likeness to the sinner, but reproof will set sins in order before his eyes. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

22 COMMENTARY: Now consider this, you that forget God, lest I tear you in pieces, and there be none to deliver: forgetters of God are warned to consider, lest tearing come with no deliverer. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

23 COMMENTARY: Whoso offers praise glorifies me, and to him that orders his conversation aright will I show the salvation of God: praise glorifies God, and an ordered life is shown salvation — the psalm ends with the twofold true worship. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
