Study Notes — Psalms

Psalm 25

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Unto you, O Lord, I lift up my soul: the psalm begins with the whole self offered upward in prayer. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: O my God, I trust in you; let me not be ashamed, nor my enemies triumph over me: shame before enemies is the feared outcome, and trust is the plea against it. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: Let none who wait on you be ashamed; let the causeless transgressors be ashamed: waiting on God is never put to shame, while groundless treachery is. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: Show me your ways, Lord; teach me your paths: guidance is sought as a pupil seeks a teacher, not as a map alone. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: Lead me in your truth and teach me, for you are the God of my salvation; on you I wait all the day: truth, teaching, and waiting belong together, and salvation is the teacher's name. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: Remember your tender mercies and lovingkindnesses, for they have been ever of old: God's ancient mercies are pleaded against present need. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: Remember not the sins of my youth nor my transgressions; according to your mercy remember me for your goodness' sake: he asks God to remember mercy and forget sin — two opposite rememberings, both grounded in God's goodness. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: Good and upright is the Lord; therefore he teaches sinners in the way: God's goodness is the reason he teaches even sinners, rather than abandoning them. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: The meek he guides in judgment, and the meek he teaches his way: teachability — meekness — is the condition of guidance. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: All the paths of the Lord are mercy and truth to those who keep his covenant and testimonies: every road God takes with his covenant people is paved with mercy and truth. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: For your name's sake, Lord, pardon my iniquity, for it is great: the greatness of the sin becomes an argument for pardon, because pardon magnifies God's name. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: What man fears the Lord? Him God shall teach in the way he shall choose: the fear of God is the school, and God chooses the curriculum. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: His soul shall dwell at ease, and his seed shall inherit the earth: quiet of soul now, and inheritance afterwards, follow the fear of the Lord. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: The secret of the Lord is with those who fear him, and he will show them his covenant: intimacy — God's secret counsel — is given to the fearing, and it takes the form of covenant knowledge. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: My eyes are ever toward the Lord, for he shall pluck my feet out of the net: steady looking to God is the way out of the snare. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: Turn to me and have mercy on me, for I am desolate and afflicted: desolation and affliction together plead for God's turning. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: The troubles of my heart are enlarged; bring me out of my distresses: inward troubles grow, and only God's bringing-out suffices. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

18 COMMENTARY: Look on my affliction and pain, and forgive all my sins: affliction and sin are joined in one prayer, for forgiveness is the deepest relief of suffering. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

19 COMMENTARY: Consider my enemies, for they are many, and hate me with cruel hatred: many enemies and cruel hatred make the case urgent. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

20 COMMENTARY: Keep my soul and deliver me; let me not be ashamed, for I trust in you: keeping, deliverance, and freedom from shame are the threefold request of trust. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

21 COMMENTARY: Let integrity and uprightness preserve me, for I wait on you: the character he has kept becomes, under God, his preservation while he waits. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

22 COMMENTARY: Redeem Israel, O God, out of all his troubles: the personal prayer widens at last to the whole people, whose troubles are the psalmist's own. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
