Study Notes — Psalms

Psalm 5

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: David asks God to give ear to his words and consider his meditation: prayer includes both spoken words and the unspoken thoughts behind them. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: He addresses God as his King and his God, and resolves to pray to him alone; dependence is directed, not vague. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: Morning prayer orders the day: he directs his prayer to God early and looks up, expecting an answer as a watchman waits for dawn. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: God takes no pleasure in wickedness, and evil cannot dwell with him; his holiness sets the character of all who approach him. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: The foolish cannot stand in his sight, and he hates workers of iniquity: sin is not merely pitied, it is abhorred by a holy God. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: Liars shall be destroyed, and the bloody and deceitful man is abhorred by the Lord; falsehood and violence are named because they were David's enemies' weapons. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: By contrast David enters God's house in the multitude of his mercy, worshipping toward the temple in reverent fear; mercy, not merit, is his entrance. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: He asks to be led in God's righteousness because of his enemies, with the way made straight before him, so that he neither strays nor stumbles into their traps. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: His enemies' mouths have no faithfulness; inwardly they are wickedness, their throat an open grave, their tongue flattery — corruption that speaks fair and means harm. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: Their own counsels should bring them down, and their many transgressions cast them out, for they have rebelled against God himself, not merely against David. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: All who trust in God shall rejoice and shout for joy, because he defends them; those who love his name find their joy in him. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: The Lord blesses the righteous and compasses him with favour as with a shield: favour surrounds the godly on every side, as a shield covers the body. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
