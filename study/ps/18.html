Study Notes — Psalms

Psalm 18

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: I will love you, O Lord, my strength: the psalm of deliverance opens not with the deliverance but with love for the Deliverer. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: The Lord is his rock, fortress, deliverer, God, strength, buckler, horn of salvation, and high tower: every image names a different way God has proved a refuge. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: He calls on the Lord, worthy to be praised, and so shall be saved from his enemies: praise and prayer together are the way of salvation. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: The sorrows of death compassed him, and floods of ungodly men made him afraid: danger is pictured as drowning and as death closing in. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: The sorrows of hell compassed him, and the snares of death prevented him: death's nets were already laid before he could escape. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: In distress he called on the Lord and cried to his God; his voice was heard from the temple, and his cry came into God's ears: prayer reaches heaven even from the edge of the grave. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: Then the earth shook and trembled, and the hills' foundations moved, because God was wroth: creation itself answers God's anger at his servant's enemies. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: Smoke went up from his nostrils, fire from his mouth devoured, and coals were kindled: the language of a volcano pictures wrath too great for literal speech. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: He bowed the heavens and came down, with darkness under his feet: God descends in storm-dark majesty to intervene. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: He rode upon a cherub and flew on the wings of the wind: swift, sovereign movement — the cherubim are his chariot, the wind his wings. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: He made darkness his secret place, his pavilion dark waters and thick clouds: God's hiding is not absence but unapproachable majesty. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: At the brightness before him his thick clouds passed, with hailstones and coals of fire: light breaks through the storm as judgment breaks on the foe. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: The Lord thundered in the heavens, and the Highest gave his voice, with hailstones and coals of fire: thunder is God's voice in the storm of deliverance. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: He sent out arrows and scattered them, shot out lightnings and discomfited them: the storm itself becomes God's artillery. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: Then the channels of waters were seen, and the world's foundations discovered at God's rebuke, at the blast of his nostrils: the sea-bed is laid bare by a word. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: He sent from above, took him, and drew him out of many waters: rescue is God's hand reaching down into the flood. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: He delivered him from his strong enemy and from haters too strong for him: the enemy's strength is the very reason God, not David, gets the glory. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

18 COMMENTARY: They prevented him in the day of calamity, but the Lord was his stay: enemies struck first, yet God held him up. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

19 COMMENTARY: He brought him into a large place and delivered him because he delighted in him: liberty replaces straits, and delight, not desert, is the ground. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

20 COMMENTARY: The Lord rewarded him according to his righteousness and the cleanness of his hands: in this cause — his dealing with Saul — David's conduct was upright, and God vindicated it. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

21 COMMENTARY: For he kept the ways of the Lord and did not wickedly depart from his God: perseverance in God's ways is the evidence pleaded. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

22 COMMENTARY: All God's judgments were before him, and he did not put away his statutes: God's law was his constant rule, not an occasional reference. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

23 COMMENTARY: He was upright before God and kept himself from his iniquity: watchfulness against his own besetting sin is part of uprightness. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

24 COMMENTARY: Therefore the Lord recompensed him according to his righteousness and the cleanness of his hands in God's eyesight: the reward is measured by God's sight, not man's praise. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

25 COMMENTARY: With the merciful God shows himself merciful, and with the upright, upright: God deals with men according to the character they show toward him and others. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

26 COMMENTARY: With the pure he shows himself pure, and with the froward, froward: the crooked find God's ways crossing theirs, because they will not walk straight. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

27 COMMENTARY: For God saves the afflicted people but brings down high looks: humility is lifted, pride is lowered, as a fixed rule of his government. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

28 COMMENTARY: God lights his candle, and the Lord enlightens his darkness: the lamp of life and hope, nearly out, is rekindled by God. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

29 COMMENTARY: By God he runs through a troop and leaps over a wall: feats of war are ascribed to divine strength, not to David's prowess alone. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

30 COMMENTARY: As for God, his way is perfect, his word is tried, and he is a buckler to all who trust in him: perfection of way, purity of word, and protection of the trusting belong together. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

31 COMMENTARY: Who is God save the Lord, or who is a rock save our God? The question excludes every rival; only the Lord is God and rock. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

32 COMMENTARY: It is God who girds him with strength and makes his way perfect: equipment for the way and the way itself are both God's work. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

33 COMMENTARY: He makes his feet like hinds' feet and sets him on his high places: swiftness and sure footing on dangerous heights are God's gifts. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

34 COMMENTARY: He teaches his hands to war, so that a bow of steel is broken by his arms: skill and strength in battle are taught, not merely given. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

35 COMMENTARY: God gives him the shield of salvation, his right hand holds him up, and his gentleness makes him great: even gentleness is a weapon of divine upholding, and it enlarges rather than diminishes. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

36 COMMENTARY: God enlarges his steps under him, so his feet do not slip: room to move and firm footing come together in God's keeping. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

37 COMMENTARY: He pursues his enemies, overtakes them, and does not turn back till they are consumed: the victory is complete because God sustained the pursuit. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

38 COMMENTARY: He wounds them so they cannot rise; they fall under his feet: the enemy's power to rise again is broken. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

39 COMMENTARY: God girds him with strength for battle and subdues under him those who rise against him: strength and subjugation are both God's acts. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

40 COMMENTARY: God gives him the necks of his enemies, that he may destroy those who hate him: the enemy is put wholly at his disposal. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

41 COMMENTARY: They cried, but none saved them, even to the Lord, but he did not answer: prayer from enemies in extremity, without repentance, finds no deliverer. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

42 COMMENTARY: Then he beats them small as dust before the wind and casts them out as dirt in the streets: total, contemptible defeat is the end of proud resistance. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

43 COMMENTARY: God delivers him from the strivings of the people and makes him head of the heathen; a people he has not known shall serve him: domestic strife ends in wider rule, as Gentiles submit to David's throne — a shadow of Christ's kingdom. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

44 COMMENTARY: As soon as they hear of him they obey; strangers submit themselves: obedience follows report, without needing to see. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

45 COMMENTARY: Strangers fade away and are afraid out of their close places: hidden resistance withers before established power. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

46 COMMENTARY: The Lord lives, blessed be his rock, and let the God of his salvation be exalted: the psalm turns from history to doxology — the living God deserves exaltation. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

47 COMMENTARY: It is God who avenges him and subdues the people under him: vengeance is left to God, who executes it justly. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

48 COMMENTARY: He delivers him from his enemies, lifts him above those who rise against him, and delivers him from the violent man: repeated deliverance, rising above opposition, is the sum of the psalm. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

49 COMMENTARY: Therefore he gives thanks among the heathen and sings praises to God's name: deliverance known at home is proclaimed abroad. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

50 COMMENTARY: Great deliverance God gives to his king, showing mercy to his anointed, to David and his seed for evermore: the promise outlives David, reaching to his seed for ever — the covenant that points to Christ. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
