Study Notes — Psalms

Psalm 70

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Make haste, O God, to deliver me; make haste to help me, O Lord: haste twice asked — deliverance and help, both urgent, from God and Lord together. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: Let them be ashamed and confounded that seek after my soul; let them be turned backward, and put to confusion, that desire my hurt: shame, confusion, backward turning for soul-seekers and hurt-desirers. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: Let them be turned back for a reward of their shame that say, Aha, aha: turning back as shame's reward answers the mocking 'Aha, aha'. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: Let all those who seek you rejoice and be glad in you, and let such as love your salvation say continually, Let God be magnified: seekers' joy and gladness, lovers' continual 'Let God be magnified' — the counterpart to the mockers. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: But I am poor and needy; make haste unto me, O God; you are my help and my deliverer; O Lord, make no tarrying: poor and needy, haste asked, help and deliverer named, no tarrying — the psalm, a shortened Psalm 40 close, ends in urgent trust. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
