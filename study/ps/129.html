Psalm 129

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Henry hears church in several ages speaking as one old gray-headed person remembering former days: Israel oppressed more than any people, speckled bird pecked at by all round (Jer 12:9); troubles brought on selves by sins, God punished for them, yet neighbours hated for peculiarity of covenant and singularities of religion; God's people always had many enemies, church from infancy frequently afflicted, Israel's youth Egypt or Judges, gospel-church youth ten persecutions primitive church groaned under; probably penned in Babylon captivity or about return. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

2 COMMENTARY: Henry repeats affliction from youth with triumph: enemies' projects defeated, afflicted in hopes to ruin but not gained point, many storms weathered, shocks and brunts borne, yet in being — ship lived at sea tossed with tempests, waves and billows over it, because Christ built church upon rock and gates of hell have not prevailed nor ever shall. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

3 COMMENTARY: Henry ploughs barbarously: enemies tore as husbandman tears ground with plough-share, to pull to pieces and get all they could, wear out saints of Most High as ground tilled quite out of heart; God permitting intended people's good, fallow broken up to sow seeds of grace and reap harvest of good fruit, enemies meant not so nor hearts thought so (Isa 10:7), made long furrows never knew when to have done, aiming at nothing less than destruction; furrows also understood as stripes, cutters on back, cruel scourgings and mockings, scourge of tongue (Heb 11:36), fulfilled in Christ giving back to smiters (Isa 50:6), or desolations, Zion ploughed as field (Mic 3:12). — Matthew Henry (Complete)

4 COMMENTARY: Henry cuts cords: enemies' power broken — God cut gears, traces, spoiled ploughing, cut scourges spoiled lashing, cut bands of union combining them, cut bands of captivity holding God's people; many ways disabling wicked men from mischief designed and shaming counsels; Lord righteous either way — righteous suffering Israel afflicted, God just in all brought upon them though enemies unjust (Neh 9:33), and righteous not suffering Israel ruined, promised preserve people to himself and will be as good as word, righteous reckoning with persecutors rendering recompence (2 Thess 1:6). — Matthew Henry (Complete)

5 COMMENTARY: Henry concludes as Deborah (Judg 5:31), So let all thy enemies perish, O Lord: many hate Zion, Zion's God, worship, worshippers, antipathy to religion and religious people, seek ruin of both, do what they can that God may not have church in world; pray attempts frustrated, confounded and turned back with shame as unable to bring enterprise and expectation to pass — let them be confounded as much as they shall be. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

6 COMMENTARY: Henry withers enemies as grass upon house-top while God's people flourish as loaded palm or green fruitful olive: as men not to be feared, made as grass (Isa 51:12), as Zion's enemies certainly marked for ruin looked on with contempt as housetop grass little short sour good for nothing; perishes quickly, withers before maturity having no root, higher place pride more exposed to scorching sun sooner withers, withers before plucked up, enemies wither of themselves stay not till rooted out by judgments; of no use, unprofitable burdens of earth, attempts never ripen or come to head, harvest heap in day of grief (Isa 17:11). — Matthew Henry (Complete)

7 COMMENTARY: Henry leaves mower's hand unfilled and bosom empty: housetop grass harvest so scant no mower fills hand nor binder bosom — enemies' designs yield nothing, unlike true harvest labour rewarded, their gathering a jest not a crop. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

8 COMMENTARY: Henry withholds blessing from reapers: ancient laudable custom to salute strangers and travellers and particularly pray prosperity of harvest-labourers, Boaz to reapers Lord be with you (Ruth 2:4), acknowledging providence, testifying good-will, commending industry, accepted as pious ejaculation from devout upright heart — but religious expressions sacred must never be used in light ludicrous actions, mowing housetop grass would be jest, reverent will not prostitute devout salutation forms; dangerous to wish church's enemies God speed in designs, partakers of evil deeds (2 John 1:11); none blessing implies all wise good cry shame and beg God defeat them, woe to those with saints' prayers against them, I cursed his habitation (Job 5:3). — Matthew Henry (Complete)
