Study Notes — Psalms

Psalm 29

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Give unto the Lord, O you mighty, give unto the Lord glory and strength: the mighty — whether angels or princes — are bidden to ascribe glory and strength to God rather than claim it. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: Give unto the Lord the glory due to his name; worship the Lord in the beauty of holiness: glory is due, not optional, and worship's beauty is holiness itself. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: The voice of the Lord is upon the waters; the God of glory thunders; the Lord is upon many waters: the storm begins over the sea, and God's voice is the thunder. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: The voice of the Lord is powerful; the voice of the Lord is full of majesty: power and majesty are the two qualities of that voice. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: The voice of the Lord breaks the cedars, yea, breaks the cedars of Lebanon: the mightiest trees fall before a voice — strength is no match for God's word. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: He makes them skip like a calf, Lebanon and Sirion like a young unicorn: mountains themselves leap under the storm's force. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: The voice of the Lord divides the flames of fire: lightning is forked by God's voice, as if the fire obeyed his division. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: The voice of the Lord shakes the wilderness; the Lord shakes the wilderness of Kadesh: the storm moves inland, shaking even the barren south. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: The voice of the Lord makes the hinds calve and discovers the forests, and in his temple everyone speaks of his glory: the storm's terror in nature becomes glory spoken in the temple — fear turns to worship. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: The Lord sits upon the flood; yea, the Lord sits King for ever: above the flood-waters, the symbol of chaos, God sits enthroned as everlasting King. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: The Lord will give strength to his people; the Lord will bless his people with peace: the God of the storm gives his own people strength, and his last word to them is peace. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
