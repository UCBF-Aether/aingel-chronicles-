Study Notes — Psalms

Psalm 59

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Deliver me from my enemies, O my God; defend me from those who rise up against me: deliverance and defence are asked together — the title recalls Saul's men watching David's house to kill him. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: Deliver me from the workers of iniquity, and save me from bloody men: workers of iniquity and bloody men are the two classes of enemy. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: For, lo, they lie in wait for my soul; the mighty are gathered against me, not for my transgression, nor for my sin, O Lord: lying in wait, mighty gathered, yet not for his transgression or sin — innocence is pleaded. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: They run and prepare themselves without my fault; awake to help me, and behold: running and preparing without his fault — God is bidden awake, help, and behold. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: You therefore, O Lord God of hosts, the God of Israel, awake to visit all the heathen; be not merciful to any wicked transgressors: Lord of hosts and God of Israel is bidden awake to visit the heathen, with no mercy to wicked transgressors. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: They return at evening; they make a noise like a dog, and go round about the city: evening return, dog-like noise, circling the city — the enemies prowl like scavenger dogs. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: Behold, they belch out with their mouth; swords are in their lips, for who, say they, does hear? Belched speech, swords in lips, and the question 'who hears?' — they count on God's deafness. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: But you, O Lord, shall laugh at them; you shall have all the heathen in derision: God's laughter and derision answer their question — he hears, and he scorns their scorn. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: Because of his strength will I wait upon you, for God is my defence: because of the enemy's strength — or, as some read, God's strength — he waits on God, his defence. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: The God of my mercy shall prevent me; God shall let me see my desire upon my enemies: mercy going before him, and sight of his desire on enemies, are promised. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: Slay them not, lest my people forget; scatter them by your power, and bring them down, O Lord our shield: not slaying but scattering — a lasting, visible example lest the people forget — by power, brought down by the Lord their shield. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: For the sin of their mouth and the words of their lips let them even be taken in their pride, and for cursing and lying which they speak: mouth-sin, lip-words, pride, cursing, and lying are the snare that takes them. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: Consume them in wrath, consume them, that they may not be, and let them know that God rules in Jacob unto the ends of the earth: consuming in wrath, twice prayed, ends in knowledge — God rules in Jacob to earth's ends. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: And at evening let them return, and let them make a noise like a dog, and go round about the city: the evening dog-noise returns as refrain — the enemies still prowl, but now as a picture of futility. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: Let them wander up and down for meat, and grudge if they be not satisfied: wandering for meat, grudging unsatisfied — hunger without satisfaction is their lot. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: But I will sing of your power; yea, I will sing aloud of your mercy in the morning, for you have been my defence and refuge in the day of my trouble: against their evening prowling stands his morning song of power and mercy, for God was defence and refuge in trouble's day. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: Unto you, O my strength, will I sing, for God is my defence, and the God of my mercy: to God his strength he sings, for God is defence and God of mercy — the psalm ends in the two names of its trust. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
