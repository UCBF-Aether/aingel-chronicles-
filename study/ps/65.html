Study Notes — Psalms

Psalm 65

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Praise waits for you, O God, in Zion, and unto you shall the vow be performed: praise waiting in Zion, vows performed — worship in God's house is both expectation and payment. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: O you who hear prayer, unto you shall all flesh come: the Hearer of prayer draws all flesh — prayer is universal in its invitation. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: Iniquities prevail against me; as for our transgressions, you shall purge them away: prevailing iniquities, personal and collective, are answered by God's purging away. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: Blessed is the man whom you choose, and cause to approach unto you, that he may dwell in your courts; we shall be satisfied with the goodness of your house, even of your holy temple: chosen, caused to approach, dwelling in courts, satisfied with the house's goodness — approach is God's gift, and satisfaction its fruit. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: By terrible things in righteousness will you answer us, O God of our salvation, who are the confidence of all the ends of the earth, and of them afar off upon the sea: terrible things in righteousness are God's answers, and he is confidence to earth's ends and distant seas. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: Which by his strength sets fast the mountains, being girded with power: mountains set fast by strength, girded with power — creation's firmness is God's might. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: Which stills the noise of the seas, the noise of their waves, and the tumult of the people: stilled seas, stilled waves, stilled tumult of peoples — nature's and nations' noise alike obey. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: They also who dwell in the uttermost parts are afraid at your tokens; you make the outgoings of morning and evening to rejoice: uttermost dwellers fear at God's tokens, yet morning and evening outgoings rejoice — fear and joy meet in God's signs. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: You visit the earth and water it; you greatly enrich it with the river of God, which is full of water; you prepare them corn, when you have so provided for it: visiting, watering, enriching with God's full river, preparing corn — providence is personal visitation. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: You water the ridges abundantly, settle the furrows, make it soft with showers, bless the springing thereof: ridges watered, furrows settled, earth softened, springing blessed — the husbandry is God's own handiwork. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: You crown the year with your goodness, and your paths drop fatness: the year crowned with goodness, paths dropping fatness — the year's end wears goodness as a crown. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: They drop upon the pastures of the wilderness, and the little hills rejoice on every side: fatness drops on wilderness pastures, little hills rejoice on every side — even the wild and low places share the bounty. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: The pastures are clothed with flocks, the valleys also are covered over with corn; they shout for joy, they also sing: flocks clothing pastures, corn covering valleys, shouting and singing — creation itself joins the praise of verse 1. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
