Study Notes — Psalms

Psalm 37

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Fret not yourself because of evildoers, nor be envious against workers of iniquity: fretting and envy are forbidden as the first temptations when the wicked prosper. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: For they shall soon be cut down like grass, and wither as the green herb: their prosperity is grass — green today, cut tomorrow. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: Trust in the Lord and do good; so shall you dwell in the land, and verily you shall be fed: trust and doing good, not fretting, are the way to dwelling and being fed. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: Delight yourself also in the Lord, and he shall give you the desires of your heart: delight in God reshapes desires, and then God grants them. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: Commit your way to the Lord; trust also in him, and he shall bring it to pass: committing the way and trusting are the human parts; bringing it to pass is God's. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: He shall bring forth your righteousness as the light, and your judgment as the noonday: vindication will shine like light at noon, plain to all. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: Rest in the Lord and wait patiently for him; fret not because of him who prospers in his way, because of the man who brings wicked devices to pass: rest and patient waiting answer the prospering schemer. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: Cease from anger and forsake wrath; fret not yourself in any wise to do evil: anger and wrath must be dropped, lest fretting itself become doing evil. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: For evildoers shall be cut off, but those who wait on the Lord shall inherit the earth: cutting off and inheriting are the two opposite ends. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: For yet a little while, and the wicked shall not be; you shall diligently consider his place, and it shall not be: in a little while the wicked's very place is empty. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: But the meek shall inherit the earth, and delight themselves in abundance of peace: the meek — not the schemers — inherit, and their portion is peace in abundance. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: The wicked plots against the just, and gnashes on him with his teeth: plotting and gnashing show hatred that is both scheming and savage. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: The Lord shall laugh at him, for he sees that his day is coming: God's laughter is not cruelty but the certainty that the plotter's day is fixed. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: The wicked draw the sword and bend the bow, to cast down the poor and needy, and slay the upright in conversation: weapons are drawn against the poor and the upright — the defenceless and the honest. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: Their sword shall enter into their own heart, and their bows shall be broken: the weapon turns on its owner, and the bow breaks in his hands. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: A little that a righteous man has is better than the riches of many wicked: a little with righteousness outweighs much with wickedness. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: For the arms of the wicked shall be broken, but the Lord upholds the righteous: broken arms against upholding hands — the contrast is complete. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

18 COMMENTARY: The Lord knows the days of the upright, and their inheritance shall be for ever: known days and an everlasting inheritance replace the grass that withers. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

19 COMMENTARY: They shall not be ashamed in the evil time, and in days of famine they shall be satisfied: evil times bring no shame to them, and famine brings satisfaction, not want. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

20 COMMENTARY: But the wicked shall perish, and the Lord's enemies shall be as the fat of lambs; they shall consume into smoke: like sacrificial fat they blaze briefly and vanish as smoke. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

21 COMMENTARY: The wicked borrows and pays not again, but the righteous shows mercy and gives: borrowing without repayment versus merciful giving marks the two characters in money. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

22 COMMENTARY: For those blessed of him shall inherit the earth, and those cursed of him shall be cut off: blessing and cursing, not wealth, decide inheritance and cutting off. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

23 COMMENTARY: The steps of a good man are ordered by the Lord, and he delights in his way: ordered steps are God's work, and God delights in the way he orders. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

24 COMMENTARY: Though he fall, he shall not be utterly cast down, for the Lord upholds him with his hand: falling is not casting down while God's hand holds. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

25 COMMENTARY: I have been young and now am old, yet I have not seen the righteous forsaken, nor his seed begging bread: a lifetime's observation — the righteous are not forsaken, nor their children beggars. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

26 COMMENTARY: He is ever merciful and lends, and his seed is blessed: continual mercy and lending mark the righteous, and blessing follows his seed. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

27 COMMENTARY: Depart from evil and do good, and dwell for evermore: turning and doing, together, are the way to dwelling for ever. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

28 COMMENTARY: For the Lord loves judgment and forsakes not his saints; they are preserved for ever, but the seed of the wicked shall be cut off: love of judgment guarantees preservation of saints and cutting off of the wicked's seed. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

29 COMMENTARY: The righteous shall inherit the land and dwell therein for ever: inheritance becomes everlasting dwelling. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

30 COMMENTARY: The mouth of the righteous speaks wisdom, and his tongue talks of judgment: wisdom and judgment in speech flow from the heart described in verse 31. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

31 COMMENTARY: The law of his God is in his heart; none of his steps shall slide: inward law steadies outward steps. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

32 COMMENTARY: The wicked watches the righteous and seeks to slay him: watching for a kill is the wicked's occupation. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

33 COMMENTARY: The Lord will not leave him in his hand, nor condemn him when he is judged: God neither abandons the righteous to the watcher nor condemns him in court. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

34 COMMENTARY: Wait on the Lord and keep his way, and he shall exalt you to inherit the land; when the wicked are cut off, you shall see it: waiting and keeping the way end in exaltation, witnessed with one's own eyes. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

35 COMMENTARY: I have seen the wicked in great power, spreading himself like a green bay tree: the wicked in full, green, spreading power is a sight David himself had seen. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

36 COMMENTARY: Yet he passed away, and lo, he was not; I sought him, but he could not be found: the green tree vanishes so completely that seeking finds nothing. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

37 COMMENTARY: Mark the perfect man and behold the upright, for the end of that man is peace: the perfect and upright end in peace — mark that end, not the wicked's green beginning. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

38 COMMENTARY: But transgressors shall be destroyed together; the end of the wicked shall be cut off: together destroyed, and cut off at the end — the opposite of peace. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

39 COMMENTARY: But the salvation of the righteous is of the Lord; he is their strength in trouble: salvation's source is the Lord, and he is strength precisely in trouble. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

40 COMMENTARY: And the Lord shall help them and deliver them; he shall deliver them from the wicked and save them, because they trust in him: help, deliverance, and salvation are repeated because trust is their ground — the psalm ends where it began, in trust against fretting. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
