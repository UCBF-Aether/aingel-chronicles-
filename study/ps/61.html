Study Notes — Psalms

Psalm 61

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Hear my cry, O God; attend unto my prayer: hearing and attending are asked for cry and prayer together. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: From the end of the earth will I cry to you, when my heart is overwhelmed; lead me to the rock that is higher than I: from earth's end, with overwhelmed heart, he cries to be led to a rock higher than himself — refuge above his reach. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: For you have been a shelter for me, and a strong tower from the enemy: past shelter and strong tower are the grounds for present leading to the rock. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: I will abide in your tabernacle for ever; I will trust in the covert of your wings: abiding for ever in God's tabernacle, trusting in the covert of his wings — dwelling and shelter together. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: For you, O God, have heard my vows; you have given me the heritage of those who fear your name: heard vows and a heritage given — the heritage of the God-fearing is his by God's gift. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: You will prolong the king's life, and his years as many generations: the king's prolonged life, years as generations, is prayed for — the king here is David, and in the fuller sense his everlasting Son. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: He shall abide before God for ever; O prepare mercy and truth, which may preserve him: abiding before God for ever, preserved by mercy and truth prepared for him. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: So will I sing praise to your name for ever, that I may daily perform my vows: singing praise for ever and daily performing vows — the psalm ends in the vow-keeping it began by recalling. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
