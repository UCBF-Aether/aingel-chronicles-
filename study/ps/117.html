Psalm 117

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Henry calls this short psalm sweet, and full of gospel: Paul quotes it as proof the gospel would be preached to and entertained by Gentiles, for in Judah only was God known for ages while nations praised wood and stone — but Christ takes down the partition-wall, the afar off are made nigh, and even former enemies become willing subjects, called by gospel tidings, ordinances and grace to praise him. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

2 COMMENTARY: Henry gives praise its matter: mercy and truth shine brightest in the gospel — mercy strong and mighty for pardon of mighty sins and a mighty salvation, prevailing above Gentile deserts, and truth enduring for ever as the promise to the fathers takes effect in believing Gentiles, the spiritual seed of Abraham; mercy is the fountain of comforts, truth the foundation of hopes. — Matthew Henry (Complete)
