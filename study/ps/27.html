Study Notes — Psalms

Psalm 27

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: The Lord is my light and my salvation; whom shall I fear? The Lord is the strength of my life; of whom shall I be afraid? Light, salvation, and strength in God answer every fear by name. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: When the wicked, my enemies and foes, came to eat up my flesh, they stumbled and fell: the devourers themselves fall, and their fall is the proof of God's defence. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: Though a host encamp against me, my heart shall not fear; though war rise against me, in this I will be confident: numbers and war cannot shake confidence rooted in verse 1. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: One thing I have desired and will seek: to dwell in the house of the Lord all my days, to behold the beauty of the Lord and enquire in his temple: a single desire — God's presence, beauty, and counsel — outranks deliverance itself. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: For in trouble he shall hide me in his pavilion, in the secret of his tabernacle hide me, and set me up on a rock: God's house is also God's hiding-place and high ground. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: Now my head shall be lifted above my enemies round about; therefore I will offer sacrifices of joy in his tabernacle and sing praises: lifted head leads to joyful sacrifice and song. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: Hear, Lord, when I cry with my voice; have mercy on me and answer me: the tone shifts from confidence to supplication, as it must in real prayer. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: When you said, Seek my face, my heart said, Your face, Lord, will I seek: God's command and the heart's answer meet — seeking is obedience, not bargaining. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: Hide not your face from me; do not put your servant away in anger; you have been my help; leave me not, nor forsake me, O God of my salvation: past help is pleaded against the fear of present abandonment. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: When my father and mother forsake me, then the Lord will take me up: even the surest human bonds may fail, and God's taking-up remains. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: Teach me your way, Lord, and lead me in a plain path, because of my enemies: a level, plain path is prayed for precisely because enemies watch for a stumble. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: Deliver me not over to the will of my enemies, for false witnesses rise against me, breathing out cruelty: false witness and cruelty are the enemies' weapons, and God's withholding is the only defence. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: I had fainted unless I had believed to see the goodness of the Lord in the land of the living: faith in seeing God's goodness in this life, not only the next, kept him from fainting. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: Wait on the Lord; be of good courage, and he shall strengthen your heart; wait, I say, on the Lord: the psalm ends by preaching to itself — waiting, courage, and a strengthened heart, with waiting repeated for emphasis. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
