Study Notes — Psalms

Psalm 39

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: I said, I will take heed to my ways, that I sin not with my tongue; I will keep my mouth with a bridle while the wicked is before me: the tongue is bridled precisely because the wicked watch for a slip. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: I was dumb with silence, held my peace even from good, and my sorrow was stirred: silence kept even from good speech stirred sorrow rather than easing it. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: My heart was hot within me; while I was musing the fire burned; then I spoke with my tongue: musing kindled fire, and fire broke silence — thought became speech. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: Lord, make me to know my end, and the measure of my days, what it is, that I may know how frail I am: the prayer for knowledge of his end is a prayer to know frailty. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: Behold, you have made my days as a handbreadth, and my age is as nothing before you; verily every man at his best state is altogether vanity: a handbreadth measures life, and even man's best state is vanity before God. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: Surely every man walks in a vain show; surely they are disquieted in vain; he heaps up riches and knows not who shall gather them: life as a show, disquiet in vain, and riches heaped for an unknown heir — vanity in three forms. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: And now, Lord, what wait I for? My hope is in you: from vanity he turns to the only wait worth waiting — hope in God. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: Deliver me from all my transgressions; make me not the reproach of the foolish: deliverance from transgressions, not from trouble alone, is prayed for, lest he become the fool's reproach. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: I was dumb, I opened not my mouth, because you did it: silence returns, now as submission — God did it, therefore he will not plead against it. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: Remove your stroke away from me; I am consumed by the blow of your hand: submission in verse 9 does not cancel the plea in verse 10 — he still asks the stroke's removal, for he is consumed. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: When you with rebukes correct man for iniquity, you make his beauty consume away like a moth; surely every man is vanity: rebuke eats beauty as a moth eats cloth, and the lesson repeats — every man is vanity. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: Hear my prayer, Lord, and give ear to my cry; hold not your peace at my tears, for I am a stranger with you, and a sojourner, as all my fathers were: tears are pleaded, and strangerhood with God — a guest, like his fathers — is the ground of welcome. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: O spare me, that I may recover strength, before I go hence and be no more: sparing is asked for recovery of strength before departure, for after going hence there is no more of this life's service. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
