Study Notes — Psalms

Psalm 32

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Blessed is he whose transgression is forgiven, whose sin is covered: blessedness here is not prosperity but pardon — transgression forgiven and sin covered are its two faces. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: Blessed is the man to whom the Lord imputes not iniquity, and in whose spirit there is no guile: non-imputation and a guileless spirit together describe the justified man, as Paul expounds in Romans. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: When I kept silence, my bones waxed old through my roaring all the day: unconfessed sin rotted him inwardly, and the roaring was pain without confession. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: Day and night your hand was heavy on me; my moisture turned into summer drought: God's heavy hand dried up his inward life like a summer drought. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: I acknowledged my sin to you, and did not hide my iniquity; I said, I will confess my transgressions to the Lord, and you forgave the iniquity of my sin: confession, resolved and spoken, met immediate forgiveness — the turning point of the psalm. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: For this shall every godly one pray to you in a time when you may be found; surely in floods of great waters they shall not come near him: David's experience becomes a pattern — pray while God may be found, and floods cannot reach the praying man. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: You are my hiding place; you preserve me from trouble and compass me about with songs of deliverance: God himself is the hiding place, and deliverance itself becomes song around him. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: I will instruct you and teach you in the way you shall go; I will guide you with my eye: God now speaks as teacher, guiding not by force but by the eye — a look, for the willing. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: Be not as the horse or mule, which have no understanding, whose mouth must be held with bit and bridle: the untaught need force; the godly should need only God's eye. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: Many sorrows shall be to the wicked, but he who trusts in the Lord, mercy shall compass him about: sorrows surround the wicked, mercy surrounds the trusting — the two compassings contrast. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: Be glad in the Lord and rejoice, you righteous, and shout for joy, all upright in heart: the psalm that began with pardon ends in gladness and shouting joy for the righteous. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
