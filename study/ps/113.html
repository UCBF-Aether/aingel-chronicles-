Study Notes — Psalms

Psalm 113

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Henry stresses the pressing, repeated call: praise is a necessary and excellent duty, pleasing to God, yet one we are backward to, so precept is added to precept — and those who taste its pleasure court others to share the work, for there is employment for all hearts and hands. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

2 COMMENTARY: Henry reads the blessing of the name as without end: not to die with us, but to run through all generations of time and on into the better world, each age taking up what the last laid down. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

3 COMMENTARY: Henry takes sunrise to sunset as the whole habitable world: God's name ought to be praised in every place where his wisdom, power and goodness appear, and he glimpses here the gospel age when the church spreads beyond Israel to all countries. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

4 COMMENTARY: Henry exalts God above every nation and its pomp: put all nations together and he is above them all, as the drop of the bucket, and his glory sits above the heavens themselves, above the angels who serve under his command. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

5 COMMENTARY: Henry turns the question into holy admiration: no prince, potentate or blessed spirit can equal or compare with him, for he dwells on high, sees all, rules all, and justly draws all praise to himself. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

6 COMMENTARY: Henry marvels that the God who needs nothing humbles himself to behold heaven and earth — to support angels, direct their motions and accept their praise, and much more to visit men and order their affairs; greatest of all, the Son came from heaven to earth to seek and save the lost. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

7 COMMENTARY: Henry sees God magnifying his own wisdom by raising the despicable on a sudden: Gideon from threshing, Saul from seeking asses, David from sheep, apostles from fishing — and Joseph from prison-dust — so that the excellency of the power is seen to be of God, and in Christ poor fallen men are lifted from the dunghill of sin. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

8 COMMENTARY: Henry reads the seating with princes as promotion that comes from God alone, sometimes rewarding long-tried piety and patience under poverty and disgrace, and wisely observed as a return of providence showing the loving-kindness of the Lord. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

9 COMMENTARY: Henry recalls Sarah, Rebekah, Rachel, Hannah and Elizabeth: God looks on the affliction of his handmaids and takes away reproach, giving the long-childless particular joy and particular thanksgiving — yet rejoice with trembling — and applies the figure to the Gentile gospel church, children of the desolate, who have reason to say, Praise ye the Lord. — Matthew Henry (Complete)
