Study Notes — Psalms

Psalm 77

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: The psalmist cried aloud to God, and God gave ear to him. Prayer in trouble is not wasted breath; it is heard before it is answered. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

2 COMMENTARY: In the day of trouble he sought the Lord through a sleepless, uncomforted night, his sore running and not ceasing. Grief that will not be comforted still turns to God rather than away from him. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

3 COMMENTARY: Remembering God troubled him, and his complaint overwhelmed his spirit. Even holy memory can sting when present favour seems withdrawn. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

4 COMMENTARY: God held his eyes waking, and his trouble left him speechless. Sleeplessness and silence here are the marks of a burden too heavy for words. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

5 COMMENTARY: He considered the days of old and the years of ancient times, setting former prosperity against present calamity. Henry warns that magnifying former days only feeds discontent with the present, rather than comforting it. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

6 COMMENTARY: He recalled his song in the night and communed with his own heart, searching his spirit diligently. Faith examines itself rather than merely feeling its pain. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

7 COMMENTARY: He asks whether the Lord will cast off for ever and be favourable no more. The question is fear speaking, not settled unbelief, and it is brought to God himself. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

8 COMMENTARY: He asks whether mercy is clean gone and promise failed for evermore. The fear is that God's goodness has an end, which the rest of the psalm answers by remembering. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

9 COMMENTARY: He asks whether God has forgotten to be gracious and shut up his tender mercies in anger. The psalm names the darkest suspicion so that it can be answered. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

10 COMMENTARY: He calls this his infirmity, and resolves instead to remember the years of the right hand of the Most High. The cure for his fear is not feeling better but remembering God's hand. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

11 COMMENTARY: He will remember the works of the Lord and his wonders of old. Deliberate remembrance replaces anxious questioning as the psalm turns. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

12 COMMENTARY: He will meditate on all God's work and talk of his doings. Meditation and speech together fix the mind on what God has done. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

13 COMMENTARY: God's way is in the sanctuary, and no God is so great as Israel's God. Holiness and greatness meet in the God who is approached in worship. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

14 COMMENTARY: God does wonders and declares his strength among the peoples. His power is not hidden; it is shown openly for the nations to see. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

15 COMMENTARY: With his arm God redeemed his people, the sons of Jacob and Joseph. Redemption is God's own act, by strength, for a particular people he had chosen. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

16 COMMENTARY: The waters saw God and were afraid, and the depths were troubled. Creation itself trembles before the God who commands the sea. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

17 COMMENTARY: Clouds poured out water, skies sent out sound, and God's arrows went abroad. The storm at the Red Sea is read as God's artillery in Israel's deliverance. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

18 COMMENTARY: Thunder sounded in heaven, lightning lightened the world, and the earth trembled and shook. God's coming in judgment and deliverance shakes all that stands. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

19 COMMENTARY: God's way is in the sea and his path in the great waters, and his footsteps are not known. He works by ways that cannot be tracked, yet they lead his people through. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

20 COMMENTARY: God led his people like a flock by the hand of Moses and Aaron. The same God who rules the storm shepherds his people through human leaders he appoints. — Matthew Henry (Complete)
