Study Notes — Psalms

Psalm 68

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Let God arise, let his enemies be scattered; let them also who hate him flee before him: the ark's marching cry — God arising scatters enemies and flees haters. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: As smoke is driven away, so drive them away; as wax melts before the fire, so let the wicked perish at the presence of God: smoke driven, wax melted — the wicked perish at God's presence as smoke and wax before wind and fire. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: But let the righteous be glad; let them rejoice before God; yea, let them exceedingly rejoice: against the wicked's perishing stands the righteous' gladness, rejoicing, exceeding rejoicing before God. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: Sing unto God, sing praises to his name; extol him who rides upon the heavens by his name JAH, and rejoice before him: singing, praising, extolling the heaven-rider by the name JAH, rejoicing before him. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: A father of the fatherless, and a judge of the widows, is God in his holy habitation: father to the fatherless, judge for widows, from his holy habitation — God's majesty is shown in care for the defenceless. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: God sets the solitary in families; he brings out those bound with chains; but the rebellious dwell in a dry land: solitary set in families, chained brought out, rebellious in a dry land — God's settling reverses loneliness and bondage. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: O God, when you went forth before your people, when you marched through the wilderness: God's going forth before his people, marching through wilderness — the Exodus march is recalled. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: The earth shook, the heavens also dropped at the presence of God; even Sinai itself was moved at the presence of God, the God of Israel: shaking earth, dropping heavens, moved Sinai at God's presence — the God of Israel's theophany. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: You, O God, did send a plentiful rain, whereby you did confirm your inheritance, when it was weary: plentiful rain confirming a weary inheritance — provision refreshes God's tired people. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: Your congregation has dwelt therein; you, O God, have prepared of your goodness for the poor: congregation dwelling in the rain-refreshed land, goodness prepared for the poor. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: The Lord gave the word; great was the company of those that published it: the Lord's word given, great the publishing company — women publishers of victory, in the Hebrew, proclaim the triumph. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: Kings of armies did flee apace, and she who tarried at home divided the spoil: fleeing kings, home-tarrying dividers of spoil — victory's spoil reaches even those who stayed home. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: Though you have lien among the pots, yet shall you be as the wings of a dove covered with silver, and her feathers with yellow gold: lying among pots — in servile drudgery — gives way to dove wings silvered and feathers gilded. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: When the Almighty scattered kings in it, it was white as snow in Salmon: the Almighty's scattering of kings leaves the land white as snow on Salmon — victory whitens the battlefield. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: The hill of God is as the hill of Bashan, an high hill as the hill of Bashan: God's hill is compared to Bashan's high hill — lofty, yet chosen. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: Why leap you, you high hills? This is the hill which God desires to dwell in; yea, the Lord will dwell in it for ever: high hills leaping in envy are answered — this hill God desires, and will dwell in for ever. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: The chariots of God are twenty thousand, even thousands of angels; the Lord is among them, as in Sinai, in the holy place: twenty thousand chariots, thousands of angels, the Lord among them as in Sinai, in the holy place — heaven's army attends God's dwelling. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

18 COMMENTARY: You have ascended on high, you have led captivity captive; you have received gifts for men, yea, for the rebellious also, that the Lord God might dwell among them: ascension on high, captivity led captive, gifts received for men, even the rebellious, that God might dwell among them — Paul applies this to Christ's ascension and gifts to men. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

19 COMMENTARY: Blessed be the Lord, who daily loads us with benefits, even the God of our salvation: daily loaded benefits from the God of salvation — blessing is daily, not occasional. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

20 COMMENTARY: He who is our God is the God of salvation, and unto God the Lord belong the issues from death: our God is the God of salvation, and death's issues — its goings-out — belong to the Lord. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

21 COMMENTARY: But God shall wound the head of his enemies, and the hairy scalp of such a one as goes on still in his trespasses: wounding the head and hairy scalp of the persistent trespasser — judgment strikes the head of continued rebellion. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

22 COMMENTARY: The Lord said, I will bring again from Bashan, I will bring my people again from the depths of the sea: bringing again from Bashan and from sea-depths — no exile, however deep, is beyond God's retrieval. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

23 COMMENTARY: That your foot may be dipped in the blood of your enemies, and the tongue of your dogs in the same: feet dipped in enemies' blood, dogs' tongues in the same — total defeat, in the psalm's martial language. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

24 COMMENTARY: They have seen your goings, O God, even the goings of my God, my King, in the sanctuary: seen goings of God, my God, my King, in the sanctuary — the procession into worship is itself a theophany. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

25 COMMENTARY: The singers went before, the players on instruments followed after; among them were the damsels playing with timbrels: singers before, players after, damsels with timbrels among them — the procession's order is musical. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

26 COMMENTARY: Bless ye God in the congregations, even the Lord, from the fountain of Israel: blessing God in congregations, from Israel's fountain — praise rises from the people's source. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

27 COMMENTARY: There is little Benjamin with their ruler, the princes of Judah and their council, the princes of Zebulun, and the princes of Naphtali: little Benjamin, Judah's princes, Zebulun's, Naphtali's — the tribes march in the procession, small and great together. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

28 COMMENTARY: Your God has commanded your strength; strengthen, O God, that which you have wrought for us: commanded strength is prayed to be strengthened — what God wrought, God must sustain. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

29 COMMENTARY: Because of your temple at Jerusalem shall kings bring presents unto you: because of the temple at Jerusalem, kings bring presents — worship in Zion draws the nations' gifts. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

30 COMMENTARY: Rebuke the company of spearmen, the multitude of the bulls, with the calves of the people, till every one submit himself with pieces of silver; scatter the people that delight in war: rebuking spearmen and bulls, submission with silver, scattering war-delighters — proud powers are humbled to tribute. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

31 COMMENTARY: Princes shall come out of Egypt; Ethiopia shall soon stretch out her hands unto God: Egyptian princes coming, Ethiopian hands stretched to God — the nations' conversion is foreseen. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

32 COMMENTARY: Sing unto God, you kingdoms of the earth; O sing praises unto the Lord: kingdoms of earth are bidden sing to God, sing praises to the Lord. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

33 COMMENTARY: To him who rides upon the heavens of heavens, which were of old; lo, he sends out his voice, and that a mighty voice: the rider on ancient heavens of heavens sends out a mighty voice — thunder as God's voice closes the praise. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

34 COMMENTARY: Ascribe strength unto God; his excellency is over Israel, and his strength is in the clouds: strength ascribed to God, excellency over Israel, strength in clouds — the ascription matches the psalm's whole vision. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

35 COMMENTARY: O God, you are terrible out of your holy places; the God of Israel is he who gives strength and power unto his people. Blessed be God: terrible from holy places, giver of strength and power to his people, blessed be God — terror and gift together end the psalm. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
