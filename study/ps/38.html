Study Notes — Psalms

Psalm 38

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: O Lord, rebuke me not in your wrath, neither chasten me in your hot displeasure: as in Psalm 6, he accepts chastening but prays it be not in wrath. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: For your arrows stick fast in me, and your hand presses me sore: arrows and a pressing hand picture affliction felt as God's own doing. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: There is no soundness in my flesh because of your anger, nor rest in my bones because of my sin: flesh and bones both suffer, and sin is named as the cause alongside anger. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: For my iniquities are gone over my head; as a heavy burden they are too heavy for me: sins are a flood over the head and a burden too heavy to bear. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: My wounds stink and are corrupt because of my foolishness: wounds left untended by folly fester — sin neglected corrupts. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: I am troubled; I am bowed down greatly; I go mourning all the day: trouble, bowing, and all-day mourning describe the body's posture under guilt. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: For my loins are filled with a loathsome disease, and there is no soundness in my flesh: disease fills the loins, and soundness is gone from the flesh. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: I am feeble and sore broken; I have roared by reason of the disquietness of my heart: feebleness, brokenness, and roaring from a disquieted heart — the cry is inward before it is outward. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: Lord, all my desire is before you, and my groaning is not hidden from you: desire and groaning are both open to God, who needs no informing. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: My heart pants, my strength fails; as for the light of my eyes, it also is gone from me: panting heart, failing strength, and lost eyesight mark the body's collapse. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: My lovers and friends stand aloof from my sore, and my kinsmen stand afar off: friends and kin keep their distance from suffering, as from a plague. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: They also who seek my life lay snares for me, and those who seek my hurt speak mischievous things and imagine deceits all the day: snares, mischievous speech, and all-day deceit add enemies to affliction. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: But I, as a deaf man, heard not, and was as a dumb man who opens not his mouth: deafness and dumbness are chosen — he will not answer slander with slander. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: Thus I was as a man who hears not, and in whose mouth are no reproofs: no reproof is offered, because the case is left to God. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: For in you, O Lord, do I hope; you will hear, O Lord my God: hope in God and God's hearing are the reason for silence before men. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: For I said, Hear me, lest otherwise they should rejoice over me; when my foot slips, they magnify themselves against me: he fears their rejoicing at his slip more than the slip itself. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: For I am ready to halt, and my sorrow is continually before me: readiness to fall and continual sorrow are the honest state confessed. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

18 COMMENTARY: For I will declare my iniquity; I will be sorry for my sin: declaration and sorrow for sin are the penitential answer to affliction. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

19 COMMENTARY: But my enemies are lively and strong, and those who hate me wrongfully are multiplied: lively, strong, multiplied, wrongful haters contrast with his own weakness. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

20 COMMENTARY: They also who render evil for good are my adversaries, because I follow the thing that is good: good repaid with evil, for the very reason of following good, is the adversaries' charge against themselves. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

21 COMMENTARY: Forsake me not, O Lord; O my God, be not far from me: the plea against forsaking and farness gathers the whole psalm. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

22 COMMENTARY: Make haste to help me, O Lord my salvation: haste is asked because salvation delayed feels like salvation denied — and God is named as his salvation itself. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
