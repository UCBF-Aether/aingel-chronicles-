Study Notes — Psalms

Psalm 17

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Hear the right, Lord, attend to my cry, and give ear to a prayer not from feigned lips: sincerity is pleaded before the merits of the cause. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: Let my sentence come from your presence, and let your eyes behold what is equal: David asks God, not men, to judge, because God's judgment is even-handed. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: God has proved his heart, visited him in the night, tried him and found nothing; he purposes that his mouth shall not transgress: night thoughts, the truest test, have not condemned him in this cause. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: Concerning men's works, by the word of God's lips he has kept himself from the paths of the destroyer: Scripture, not custom, kept him from violent ways. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: Hold up his goings in God's paths, that his footsteps slip not: past keeping becomes a prayer for continued upholding. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: He has called on God because God will hear him; incline your ear and hear his speech: confidence in God's hearing encourages prayer, and prayer expresses confidence. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: Show marvellous lovingkindness, O Saviour by your right hand of those who trust in you from those who rise against them: God's saving power is the refuge of the trusting. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: Keep me as the apple of the eye, hide me under the shadow of your wings: the tenderest, most guarded part of the body and the shelter of a bird's wings picture God's care. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: From the wicked who oppress him, from deadly enemies who compass him about: the danger is encircling and mortal. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: They are enclosed in their own fat, and speak proudly with their mouth: prosperity has made them insensible and arrogant. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: They have compassed his steps, setting their eyes bowing down to the earth: they watch for a fall, crouching to spring. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: Like a greedy lion and a young lion lurking in secret places: the enemy's patience makes him more, not less, dangerous. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: Arise, Lord, disappoint him, cast him down, and deliver my soul from the wicked, who is your sword: even the wicked are God's instrument, but God can stay his own sword. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: From men of the world, who have their portion in this life, whose belly is filled with hidden treasure, full of children, leaving substance to their babes: their whole portion is present, earthly, and inherited — and therefore short. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: As for me, I will behold your face in righteousness, and be satisfied when I awake with your likeness: against their earthly portion stands the vision of God, satisfying beyond waking life itself. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
