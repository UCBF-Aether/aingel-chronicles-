Study Notes — Titus

Chapter 2

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: In contrast to the false teachers, Titus must speak what fits sound doctrine. JFB notes the stress falls on doing: wandering curiosity is best cured by recalling believers to the holy life they ought to practise. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: Aged men are to be sober, dignified, self-controlled, and sound in faith, love, and patient endurance. Henry adds that the decays of age excuse no excess; comfort is to be sought in nearer communion with God. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: Aged women likewise are to behave as befits holiness, not as slanderers or slaves to much wine, but as teachers of good things. JFB notes this teaching was private, by example and counsel, not public preaching. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: The older women are to school the younger women in self-restraint and in love for their husbands and children. Titus, a young man, wisely teaches the younger women through the older women rather than directly. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: Young women are to be discreet, chaste, busy at home, kind, and submissive to their own husbands, so that God's word is not spoken evil of. Failures in household duty, Henry warns, greatly reproach Christianity. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: Young men likewise are to be exhorted to be sober-minded, self-restrained in pleasures and follies. Henry observes that more young people are ruined by pride than by any other sin. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: Titus himself must be a pattern of good works in everything, showing uncorruptness, dignity, and sincerity in his teaching. All teaching is useless, JFB notes, unless the teacher's example confirms his word. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: His speech must be sound and beyond condemnation, so that opponents are put to shame, having no evil thing to say. A blameless life stops the mouths of adversaries, which is itself a glory for a Christian. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: Servants are to obey their own masters and seek to please them well in all things, not answering back. JFB explains the frequent charge: Christian freedom was easily misunderstood as licence to forget one's station. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: They must not pilfer but show all good faithfulness, so that they adorn the doctrine of God our Saviour in everything. Even the lowest station, Henry notes, can honour the gospel, and service to an earthly master done for Christ serves him. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: The ground of all these duties is that God's grace bringing salvation has appeared to all people, of every rank. Grace has now been embodied and made to shine forth in Jesus, after long being hidden in God's counsel. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: That grace disciplines us: it teaches us to deny ungodliness and worldly lusts once for all, and to live soberly toward ourselves, righteously toward neighbours, and godly toward God in this present age. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: We live this way looking for the blessed hope, the glorious appearing of the great God and our Saviour Jesus Christ. JFB notes the single article joins God and Saviour as one Being, and that this hope is the antidote to worldly lusts. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: Christ gave himself for us to redeem us from all iniquity, not merely its penalty, and to purify a people peculiarly his own, zealous for good works. Redemption and sanctification, Henry stresses, go together. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: These things Titus must speak, exhort, and rebuke with all authority, and let no one despise him. Speaking with such vigour commands respect and leaves no one above the need of admonition. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
