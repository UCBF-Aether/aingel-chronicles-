Study Notes — Joshua

Chapter 10

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: When Adonizedek king of Jerusalem heard Joshua had taken Ai and utterly destroyed it as Jericho, and that Gibeon had made peace with Israel, he moved to war. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: The five kings feared greatly, because Gibeon was a great city, as one of the royal cities, greater than Ai, and all its men were mighty. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: Adonizedek sent to Hoham king of Hebron, Piram king of Jarmuth, Japhia king of Lachish, and Debir king of Eglon, summoning a confederacy. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: He asked them to come up and help him smite Gibeon, because it had made peace with Joshua and Israel, and Henry notes that when sinners leave Satan's service to make peace with God, former friends often become foes. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: Therefore the five Amorite kings, of Jerusalem, Hebron, Jarmuth, Lachish, and Eglon, gathered together, went up with all their hosts, and encamped before Gibeon and made war against it. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: The men of Gibeon sent to Joshua at Gilgal, saying, Slack not thy hand from thy servants, come up quickly, save us and help us, for all the Amorite kings of the mountains are gathered against us. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: Joshua ascended from Gilgal with all the people of war and all the mighty men of valour, refusing to forsake his new vassals, and Henry notes how much less shall our true Joshua fail those who trust in him. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: The Lord told Joshua to fear them not, for he had delivered them into his hand, and not a man of them would stand before him. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: Joshua came upon them suddenly, going up from Gilgal all night. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: The Lord discomfited them before Israel, slaying them with a great slaughter at Gibeon, and chasing them along the way to Bethhoron and to Azekah and Makkedah. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: As they fled before Israel in the going down to Bethhoron, the Lord cast down great stones from heaven upon them unto Azekah, and more died by the hailstones than Israel slew with the sword. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: Then Joshua spoke to the Lord in the day the Lord delivered up the Amorites before Israel, saying in the sight of Israel, Sun, stand thou still upon Gibeon, and thou, Moon, in the valley of Ajalon. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: The sun stood still and the moon stayed until the people had avenged themselves upon their enemies, as written in the book of Jasher, the sun standing still in the midst of heaven and hasting not to go down about a whole day. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: There was no day like that before or after, that the Lord hearkened to the voice of a man, for the Lord fought for Israel, and Henry notes the great faith of Joshua answered by God's power, asking, Is anything too hard for the Lord? — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: Joshua returned with all Israel to the camp at Gilgal. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: But the five kings fled and hid themselves in a cave at Makkedah. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: It was told Joshua that the five kings were found hid in a cave at Makkedah. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: Joshua commanded great stones rolled upon the mouth of the cave, with men set by it to keep them. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: Israel was not to stay but to pursue their enemies, smite the hindmost of them, and not suffer them to enter into their cities, for the Lord their God had delivered them into their hand. — Matthew Henry

20 COMMENTARY: When Joshua and Israel had made an end of slaying them with a very great slaughter till they were consumed, the rest entered into fenced cities. — Matthew Henry

21 COMMENTARY: All the people returned to Joshua at Makkedah in peace, and none moved his tongue against any of the children of Israel, showing their perfect safety. — Matthew Henry

22 COMMENTARY: Joshua commanded the mouth of the cave opened and the five kings brought out to him. — Matthew Henry

23 COMMENTARY: They brought forth the five kings, of Jerusalem, Hebron, Jarmuth, Lachish, and Eglon, out of the cave. — Matthew Henry

24 COMMENTARY: When the kings were brought out, Joshua called for all the men of Israel and told the captains of the men of war who went with him to put their feet upon the necks of these kings, and they did so. — Matthew Henry

25 COMMENTARY: Joshua told them to fear not nor be dismayed, but be strong and of good courage, for thus the Lord would do to all their enemies against whom they fought, a type of Christ's victories over the powers of darkness and of believers' victories through him. — Matthew Henry

26 COMMENTARY: Afterward Joshua smote the kings, slew them, and hanged them on five trees, where they hung until evening. — Matthew Henry

27 COMMENTARY: At the going down of the sun Joshua commanded their carcases taken down from the trees, cast into the cave where they had hid, and great stones laid upon the cave's mouth, remaining to this day. — Matthew Henry

28 COMMENTARY: That day Joshua took Makkedah and smote it and its king with the edge of the sword, utterly destroying all souls in it and leaving none remaining, doing to its king as to the king of Jericho. — Matthew Henry

29 COMMENTARY: Joshua passed from Makkedah with all Israel to Libnah and fought against it. — Matthew Henry

30 COMMENTARY: The Lord delivered Libnah and its king into Israel's hand, and Joshua smote it and all souls in it with the sword, leaving none remaining, doing to its king as to the king of Jericho. — Matthew Henry

31 COMMENTARY: Joshua passed from Libnah with all Israel to Lachish, encamped against it, and fought against it. — Matthew Henry

32 COMMENTARY: The Lord delivered Lachish into Israel's hand, which took it on the second day and smote it and all souls in it, as he had done to Libnah. — Matthew Henry

33 COMMENTARY: Horam king of Gezer came up to help Lachish, and Joshua smote him and his people until he had left him none remaining. — Matthew Henry

34 COMMENTARY: From Lachish Joshua passed to Eglon with all Israel, encamped against it, and fought against it. — Matthew Henry

35 COMMENTARY: They took Eglon that day and smote it and all souls in it with the sword, utterly destroying them as they had done to Lachish. — Matthew Henry

36 COMMENTARY: Joshua went up from Eglon with all Israel to Hebron and fought against it. — Matthew Henry

37 COMMENTARY: They took Hebron, smote it, its king, all its cities, and all souls in them with the sword, leaving none remaining, as he had done to Eglon. — Matthew Henry

38 COMMENTARY: Joshua returned with all Israel to Debir and fought against it. — Matthew Henry

39 COMMENTARY: He took Debir, its king, and all its cities, smiting them with the sword and utterly destroying all souls, leaving none remaining, as he had done to Hebron and Libnah and their kings. — Matthew Henry

40 COMMENTARY: So Joshua smote all the country of the hills, the south, the vale, and the springs, and all their kings, leaving none remaining but utterly destroying all that breathed, as the Lord God of Israel commanded. — Matthew Henry

41 COMMENTARY: Joshua smote them from Kadeshbarnea even to Gaza, and all the country of Goshen even to Gibeon. — Matthew Henry

42 COMMENTARY: All these kings and their land Joshua took at one time, because the Lord God of Israel fought for Israel, and Henry notes they could not have gotten the victory if God had not undertaken the battle. — Matthew Henry

43 COMMENTARY: Joshua returned with all Israel to the camp at Gilgal, having made speed in taking these cities through diligence and improving opportunities. — Matthew Henry
