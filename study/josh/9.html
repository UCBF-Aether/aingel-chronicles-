Study Notes — Joshua

Chapter 9

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: When all the kings west of Jordan, in the hills, valleys, coasts of the great sea, and Lebanon, the Hittite, Amorite, Canaanite, Perizzite, Hivite, and Jebusite, heard of Jericho and Ai, they gathered together. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: They gathered themselves together to fight with Joshua and Israel with one accord, and Henry notes that though often at enmity with each other, they united against Israel, their minds blinded and hearts hardened to their destruction. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: When the inhabitants of Gibeon heard what Joshua had done to Jericho and Ai, they were led not to war but to make peace. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: They worked wilily, making as if they had been ambassadors, taking old sacks upon their asses and old wine bottles, rent and bound up. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: They wore old shoes and clouted upon their feet and old garments, and all their bread was dry and mouldy. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: They went to Joshua at Gilgal and said to him and the men of Israel that they were come from a far country, asking to make a league with them. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: The men of Israel answered the Hivites that perhaps they dwelt among them, and asked how they could make a league with them. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: They said to Joshua, We are thy servants, and Joshua asked who they were and from whence they came. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: They answered they had come from a very far country because of the name of the Lord his God, having heard his fame and all he did in Egypt. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: They recounted all he did to Sihon king of Heshbon and Og king of Bashan beyond Jordan. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: Their elders and all their country had told them to take victuals and go meet Israel, saying, We are your servants, therefore now make ye a league with us. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: They showed bread taken hot from their houses on the day they came forth, which was now dry and mouldy. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: Their wine bottles, filled new, were now rent, and their garments and shoes were become old by reason of the very long journey, and Henry notes one lie brings on another, the way of that sin being especially downhill. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: The men took of their victuals but asked not counsel at the mouth of the Lord, and Henry notes we make more haste than good speed when we stay not to take God with us by word and prayer. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: Joshua made peace with them and a league to let them live, and the princes of the congregation sware unto them. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: At the end of three days after the league, Israel heard the Gibeonites were their neighbours dwelling among them. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: Israel journeyed and came to their cities on the third day: Gibeon, Chephirah, Beeroth, and Kirjathjearim. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: Israel smote them not, because the princes had sworn unto them by the Lord God of Israel, though all the congregation murmured against the princes. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: All the princes said they had sworn unto them by the Lord God of Israel, and therefore they could not touch them, since a citizen of Zion swears to his own hurt and changes not. — Matthew Henry

20 COMMENTARY: They resolved to let them live, lest wrath be upon them because of the oath they had sworn. — Matthew Henry

21 COMMENTARY: The princes said, Let them live, but let them be hewers of wood and drawers of water for all the congregation, as the princes had promised them. — Matthew Henry

22 COMMENTARY: Joshua called for them and asked why they had beguiled Israel, saying they dwelt very far off when they dwelt among them. — Matthew Henry

23 COMMENTARY: Now therefore they were cursed, and none of them would be freed from being bondmen, hewers of wood and drawers of water for the house of God. — Matthew Henry

24 COMMENTARY: They answered that it was certainly told them how the Lord had commanded Moses to give Israel all the land and destroy all its inhabitants, so they were sore afraid for their lives and did this thing. — Matthew Henry

25 COMMENTARY: Now they were in Joshua's hand, and he might do to them as seemed good and right, submitting themselves to the God of Israel, which implied forsaking their idolatries. — Matthew Henry

26 COMMENTARY: Joshua did so unto them, delivering them out of Israel's hand so that they slew them not. — Matthew Henry

27 COMMENTARY: That day Joshua made them hewers of wood and drawers of water for the congregation and for the altar of the Lord, even to this day, in the place he should choose, and Henry notes any work becomes honourable when done for the house of the Lord. — Matthew Henry
