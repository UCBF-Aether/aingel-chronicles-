Study Notes — Joshua

Chapter 5

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: When the Amorite and Canaanite kings heard the Lord had dried up Jordan before Israel until they passed over, their hearts melted and no spirit remained in them, and Henry notes how dreadful is the case of those who see God's wrath advancing without being able to escape it. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: At that time the Lord told Joshua to make sharp knives and circumcise again the children of Israel the second time. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: Joshua made sharp knives and circumcised Israel at the hill of the foreskins. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: The cause was that all the people who came out of Egypt and were circumcised had died in the wilderness, while those born in the wilderness had not been circumcised. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: All the people that came out were circumcised, but all born in the wilderness by the way had not been circumcised. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: Israel walked forty years in the wilderness until all the men of war who came out of Egypt were consumed, because they obeyed not the voice of the Lord. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: Their children, raised up in their stead, Joshua circumcised, for they were uncircumcised, having not been circumcised by the way. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: When they had done circumcising all the people, they abode in their places in the camp until they were whole. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: The Lord told Joshua that this day he had rolled away the reproach of Egypt from off them, and the place was called Gilgal, since circumcision owned them as the free-born children of God, having the seal of the covenant. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: Israel encamped in Gilgal and kept the passover on the fourteenth day of the month at even in the plains of Jericho, a solemn passover kept at the appointed time in defiance of the Canaanites round about. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: They ate of the old corn of the land on the morrow after the passover, unleavened cakes and parched corn in the selfsame day. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: The manna ceased on the morrow after they ate the old corn, and they had no more manna but ate of the fruit of Canaan that year, since it came just when they needed it and continued as long as they needed it. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: When Joshua was by Jericho he lifted up his eyes and saw a man over against him with his sword drawn in his hand, and Joshua went to him and asked whether he was for them or for their adversaries. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: The man answered that as captain of the host of the Lord he was now come, and Joshua fell on his face and worshipped, asking what his lord said to his servant. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: The captain of the Lord's host told Joshua to loose his shoe from off his foot, for the place whereon he stood was holy, and Joshua did so, and Henry notes this Man was the Son of God, who received divine honours no created angel would have accepted. — Matthew Henry
