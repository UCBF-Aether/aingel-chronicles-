Study Notes — Joshua

Chapter 1

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: After the death of Moses the Lord spoke to Joshua the son of Nun, Moses' minister, and Henry notes Joshua had been trained up under command, since those fittest to rule are those who have learned to obey. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: Moses being dead, Joshua was commanded to arise and go over Jordan with all the people to the land God gave Israel, and Henry notes that though Jordan's banks were overflowed and Joshua had no bridge or boats, he must believe God would open a way. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: Every place the sole of their foot tread upon was given to them, as God had said to Moses, fulfilling the promise made to their fathers. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: Their territory was to run from the wilderness and Lebanon to the great river Euphrates, all the land of the Hittites, and to the Great Sea toward the going down of the sun. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: No man would be able to stand before Joshua all his life, for as God was with Moses so he would be with him, and would not fail him nor forsake him. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: Joshua was to be strong and of good courage, for to this people he would divide as an inheritance the land sworn to their fathers. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: Only being strong and very courageous to observe all the law Moses commanded, turning neither to the right hand nor to the left, would Joshua prosper wherever he went, since no man's dignity sets him above the law of God. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: The book of the law was not to depart from Joshua's mouth, but he was to meditate in it day and night and observe to do all written in it, for Henry notes he must make the law his rule and not neglect the one thing needful amid worldly affairs. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: God himself commanded Joshua to be strong and of good courage, not afraid nor dismayed, for the Lord his God would be with him whithersoever he went, and in the way of duty there is reason to be strong and very bold. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: Joshua then commanded the officers of the people, accepting the government and addressing himself immediately to the business of it. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: The officers were to pass through the host commanding the people to prepare victuals, for within three days they would pass over Jordan to possess the land, and Henry notes we honour God's truth when we stagger not at his promise. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: Joshua spoke particularly to the Reubenites, the Gadites, and the half tribe of Manasseh, reminding them of their engagement. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: They were to remember Moses' word that the Lord had given them rest and given them this land on the east side of Jordan. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: Their wives, little ones, and cattle were to remain in the land Moses gave them, while all their mighty men of valour passed over armed before their brethren to help them. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: They were to help their brethren until the Lord gave them rest as well and they possessed the land, and then return to their own possession, since those given rest by providence ought to consider what service they may do their brethren. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: The people answered that all Joshua commanded them they would do, and whithersoever he sent them they would go, engaging to obey without murmuring or disputing. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: As they had hearkened to Moses in all things, so they would hearken to Joshua, praying only that the Lord his God would be with him as he was with Moses. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: Whoever rebelled against Joshua's commandment and would not hearken to his words would be put to death, and the people charged him only to be strong and of good courage, since the best we can ask for magistrates is God's presence with them. — Matthew Henry
