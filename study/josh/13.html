Study Notes — Joshua

Chapter 13

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Joshua was old and stricken in years, and the Lord told him he was old and stricken in years, and there remained yet very much land to be possessed, and Henry notes it is good for the old to be put in remembrance of being so, and to do quickly what must be done before death. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: This is the land that yet remained: all the borders of the Philistines and all Geshuri, the first named portion of the unconquered country. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: Now therefore Joshua was to divide this land for an inheritance to the nine tribes and the half tribe of Manasseh, since God considers the frame of his people and would not burden them above their strength, promising to make Israel masters of all countries yet unsubdued in his own time. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: With the half tribe of Manasseh, the Reubenites and Gadites had received their inheritance which Moses gave them beyond Jordan eastward. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: Nevertheless Israel expelled not the Geshurites nor the Maachathites, but they dwell among Israel to this day, a distinct failure within the eastern settlement. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: Only to the tribe of Levi Moses gave no inheritance, for the sacrifices of the Lord God of Israel made by fire are their inheritance, as God said to them, and Henry notes their maintenance must come from all the tribes, and happy are those who have the Lord himself for their inheritance. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: Moses gave to the tribe of Reuben inheritance according to their families, the first of the eastern allotments to be described. — Matthew Henry

22 COMMENTARY: Balaam also the son of Beor, the soothsayer, did Israel slay with the sword among them that were slain by them, a distinct judgment recorded within Reuben's portion. — Matthew Henry

24 COMMENTARY: Moses gave inheritance to the tribe of Gad, even to the children of Gad according to their families. — Matthew Henry

29 COMMENTARY: Moses gave inheritance to the half tribe of Manasseh, and this was the possession of the half tribe of Manasseh by their families. — Matthew Henry

32 COMMENTARY: These are the countries Moses distributed for inheritance in the plains of Moab on the other side of Jordan by Jericho eastward, closing the account of the eastern distribution. — Matthew Henry

33 COMMENTARY: But to the tribe of Levi Moses gave not any inheritance, for the Lord God of Israel was their inheritance, as he said unto them, a point Henry notes twice in this chapter, since ministers should show themselves indifferent about worldly interests. — Matthew Henry

No note for verse(s) 3, 4, 5, 6, 9, 10, 11, 12, 16, 17, 18, 19, 20, 21, 23, 25, 26, 27, 28, 30, 31 — the public-domain sources consulted give no distinct explanation for these verses beyond the list itself, so nothing was invented for them.
