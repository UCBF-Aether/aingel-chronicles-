Study Notes — Joshua

Chapter 2

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Joshua sent two men from Shittim to spy secretly and view the land and Jericho, and Henry notes faith in God's promises ought to encourage diligence in the use of proper means rather than do away with it. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: The king of Jericho was told that men of Israel had come in by night to search out the country, and providence directed the spies to Rahab's house. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: The king sent to Rahab demanding she bring forth the men who had entered her house to search out the country. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: Rahab took the two men and hid them, saying men had come to her but she did not know whence they were, and Henry notes that which seems most accidental is often overruled by divine providence to serve great ends. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: She said the men went out about the shutting of the gate when it was dark, and urged pursuit after them, while in fact she had hidden them on the roof. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: She had brought them up to the roof and hid them with stalks of flax laid in order there. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: The pursuers went after them toward Jordan to the fords, and the gate was shut as soon as they had gone out. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: Before the spies lay down, Rahab came up to them on the roof to speak with them. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: She said she knew the Lord had given Israel the land, and that their terror had fallen on Jericho's inhabitants, since she believed God's promises would certainly be fulfilled and his threatenings take effect. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: They had heard how the Lord dried up the Red Sea for Israel and what Israel had done to the Amorite kings Sihon and Og, whom they utterly destroyed. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: As soon as they heard these things their hearts melted and no spirit remained in any man, for the Lord their God is God in heaven above and in earth beneath. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: Rahab prayed the spies to swear by the Lord that since she had showed them kindness, they would show kindness to her father's house. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: She asked that they save alive her father, mother, brethren, sisters, and all theirs, and deliver their lives from death. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: The men answered that their life would be for hers if she did not utter their business, and when the Lord gave them the land they would deal kindly and truly with her. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: She let them down by a cord through the window, for her house was on the town wall, and she dwelt upon the wall. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: She advised them to go to the mountain and hide three days lest the pursuers meet them, and then return on their way. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: The men said they would be blameless of the oath she had made them swear, setting needful conditions, since those conscientious in keeping promises are cautious in making them. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: When they came into the land she was to bind the scarlet line in the window through which she let them down, and gather her father, mother, brethren, and all her father's household into her house. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: Whoever went out of her house doors into the street would bear his own blood, but whoever remained with her in the house would be safe, and their blood would be on the spies' heads if any hand were upon them. — Matthew Henry

20 COMMENTARY: If she uttered their business, they would be quit of the oath she had made them swear. — Matthew Henry

21 COMMENTARY: She agreed according to their words, sent them away, and bound the scarlet line in the window, and Henry notes the scarlet cord recalls the sinner's security under the atoning blood of Christ, as the blood on the doorpost at the passover. — Matthew Henry

22 COMMENTARY: The spies went to the mountain and abode there three days until the pursuers returned, having searched all the way without finding them. — Matthew Henry

23 COMMENTARY: The two men returned, descended from the mountain, passed over, and came to Joshua and told him all things that befell them. — Matthew Henry

24 COMMENTARY: They reported that truly the Lord had delivered all the land into their hands, for all its inhabitants fainted because of them, an encouraging report since the terrors of conscience that dismay the ungodly are forebodings of approaching destruction. — Matthew Henry
