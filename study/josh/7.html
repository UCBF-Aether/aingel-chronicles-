Study Notes — Joshua

Chapter 7

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Israel committed a trespass in the accursed thing, for Achan son of Carmi, son of Zabdi, son of Zerah, of Judah, took of it, and the anger of the Lord was kindled against Israel, since one man's sin may defile or disquiet many. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: Joshua sent men from Jericho to Ai beside Bethaven east of Bethel, telling them to go up and view the country. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: The men returned advising that not all the people need go up, but only about two or three thousand, since Ai's men were few, and Henry notes the easy conquest of Jericho had bred contempt of the enemy and sloth. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: About three thousand men went up, but they fled before the men of Ai. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: The men of Ai smote about thirty-six of them, chasing them from before the gate to Shebarim and smiting them in the going down, so the hearts of the people melted and became as water. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: Joshua rent his clothes and fell on his face before the ark of the Lord until eventide, with the elders of Israel putting dust upon their heads. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: Joshua pleaded, Alas, O Lord God, why have you brought this people over Jordan to deliver them into the hand of the Amorites to destroy them, wishing they had been content to dwell on the other side of Jordan. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: He asked what he should say when Israel turned their backs before their enemies, pleading with God in concern for God's honour more than for Israel's fate. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: He feared the Canaanites and all the land's inhabitants would hear of it, environ Israel, and cut off their name from the earth, and asked what God would do for his great name, the best plea that can be urged. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: The Lord told Joshua to get up, asking why he lay thus upon his face, awakening him to inquiry rather than mere lamentation. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: Israel had sinned and transgressed God's covenant, taking of the accursed thing, stealing, dissembling, and putting it among their own stuff. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: Therefore Israel could not stand before their enemies but turned their backs, because they were accursed, and God would no more be with them unless they destroyed the accursed thing from among them. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: Joshua was told to rise and sanctify the people against tomorrow, for the Lord said there was an accursed thing in the midst of Israel that must be removed before they could stand before their enemies. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: In the morning they were to be brought according to their tribes, and the tribe the Lord took would come by families, then by households, then man by man. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: He that was taken with the accursed thing was to be burnt with fire, he and all he had, because he had transgressed the covenant and wrought folly in Israel. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: Joshua rose early and brought Israel by tribes, and the tribe of Judah was taken. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: He brought the family of Judah, took the Zarhites, and brought the family of the Zarhites man by man, and Zabdi was taken. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: He brought Zabdi's household man by man, and Achan son of Carmi, son of Zabdi, son of Zerah, of Judah, was taken, showing God's unerring judgment distinguishing the guilty from the innocent. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: Joshua told Achan to give glory to the Lord God of Israel and make confession to him, telling him what he had done and hiding nothing. — Matthew Henry

20 COMMENTARY: Achan answered that indeed he had sinned against the Lord God of Israel, and thus and thus he had done. — Matthew Henry

21 COMMENTARY: When he saw among the spoils a goodly Babylonish garment, two hundred shekels of silver, and a wedge of gold of fifty shekels, he coveted them and took them, hiding them in the earth in his tent, and Henry notes his sin began in the eye, as Eve saw the forbidden fruit, and proceeded out of the heart. — Matthew Henry

22 COMMENTARY: Joshua sent messengers who ran to the tent, and behold, the spoil was hid in his tent, with the silver under it. — Matthew Henry

23 COMMENTARY: They took the things from the tent and brought them to Joshua and all Israel, laying them out before the Lord. — Matthew Henry

24 COMMENTARY: Joshua and all Israel took Achan, the silver, the garment, the wedge of gold, his sons and daughters, oxen, asses, sheep, tent, and all he had, to the valley of Achor. — Matthew Henry

25 COMMENTARY: Joshua asked why Achan had troubled Israel, saying the Lord would trouble him that day, and all Israel stoned him with stones and burned them with fire after stoning. — Matthew Henry

26 COMMENTARY: They raised over him a great heap of stones that remains to this day, and the Lord turned from the fierceness of his anger, so the place was called the valley of Achor, and Henry notes one sinner destroys much good, and Achan perished not alone in his sin. — Matthew Henry
