Study Notes — Joshua

Chapter 6

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Jericho was straitly shut up because of Israel, none going out or coming in, and Henry notes the city foolishly strengthened itself against the Almighty, hardening its heart to its own destruction. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: The Lord told Joshua he had given into his hand Jericho, its king, and its mighty men of valour, resolving that Israel should quickly be its master. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: All the men of war were to compass the city once, and do so six days, with no warlike preparations, since by this uncommon method the Lord honoured the ark and showed all victories were from him. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: Seven priests were to bear before the ark seven trumpets of rams' horns, and on the seventh day they were to compass the city seven times while the priests blew the trumpets. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: When the priests made a long blast and the people heard the trumpet, all the people were to shout with a great shout, and the wall of the city would fall down flat, and they were to ascend every man straight before him. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: Joshua called the priests and told them to take up the ark of the covenant, with seven priests bearing seven trumpets before the ark of the Lord. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: He told the people to pass on and compass the city, with the armed men passing on before the ark of the Lord, wherever the ark went the people attending it. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: When Joshua had spoken, the seven priests bearing trumpets passed on before the Lord and blew, and the ark of the covenant followed them. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: The armed men went before the priests blowing trumpets, and the rereward came after the ark, the priests going on and blowing. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: Joshua commanded the people not to shout or make any noise with their voice, nor let any word proceed from their mouth, until the day he told them to shout. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: So the ark of the Lord compassed the city once, and they came into the camp and lodged there, since promised deliverances must be expected in God's way and in his time. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: Joshua rose early in the morning, and the priests took up the ark of the Lord. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: Seven priests bearing trumpets before the ark went on continually blowing, with the armed men before them and the rereward after the ark. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: The second day they compassed the city once and returned to camp, and so they did six days, their faith and patience being proved and increased. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: On the seventh day they rose early at dawn and compassed the city seven times in the same manner, only on that day compassing it seven times. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: At the seventh time, when the priests blew the trumpets, Joshua told the people to shout, for the Lord had given them the city, and their shout was a shout of faith and of prayer. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: The city was to be accursed to the Lord, with all in it, except Rahab the harlot and all in her house, because she hid the messengers sent to spy. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: Israel was in any wise to keep from the accursed thing, lest they make themselves accursed and make the camp of Israel a curse and trouble it. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: All the silver, gold, and vessels of brass and iron were consecrated to the Lord and were to come into his treasury. — Matthew Henry

20 COMMENTARY: So the people shouted when the priests blew, and when they heard the trumpet the wall fell down flat and they went up into the city and took it. — Matthew Henry

21 COMMENTARY: They utterly destroyed all in the city, man and woman, young and old, ox, sheep, and ass, with the edge of the sword, since Jericho was a solemn sacrifice to God's justice upon those who had filled up the measure of their sins. — Matthew Henry

22 COMMENTARY: Joshua told the two spies to go into the harlot's house and bring out Rahab and all that she had, as they had sworn to her. — Matthew Henry

23 COMMENTARY: The young spies went in and brought out Rahab, her father, mother, brethren, and all she had, and left them without the camp of Israel, plucked as brands from the burning. — Matthew Henry

24 COMMENTARY: They burnt the city with fire and all in it, except the silver, gold, and vessels of brass and iron, which they put into the treasury of the Lord's house. — Matthew Henry

25 COMMENTARY: Joshua saved Rahab the harlot alive and her father's household and all she had, and she dwells in Israel to this day, because she hid the messengers sent to spy out Jericho. — Matthew Henry

26 COMMENTARY: Joshua adjured them, saying cursed before the Lord be the man who rises up and builds Jericho, laying its foundation in his firstborn and setting up its gates in his youngest son. — Matthew Henry

27 COMMENTARY: So the Lord was with Joshua, and his fame was noised throughout all the country, showing the weight of a divine curse, from which there is no getting free where it rests. — Matthew Henry
