Study Notes — Joshua

Chapter 17

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: There was also a lot for Manasseh, for he was Joseph's firstborn, to wit, for Machir firstborn of Manasseh, father of Gilead, who because he was a man of war had Gilead and Bashan. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: There was also a lot for the rest of Manasseh's children by their families, for Abiezer, Helek, Asriel, Shechem, Hepher, and Shemida, the male children of Manasseh son of Joseph by their families. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: But Zelophehad son of Hepher, son of Gilead, son of Machir, son of Manasseh, had no sons but daughters, Mahlah, Noah, Hoglah, Milcah, and Tirzah. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: They came near before Eleazar the priest, Joshua son of Nun, and the princes, saying the Lord commanded Moses to give them an inheritance among their brethren, and Joshua gave them an inheritance among their father's brethren according to the Lord's commandment. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: There fell ten portions to Manasseh, beside the land of Gilead and Bashan beyond Jordan. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: Because Manasseh's daughters had an inheritance among his sons, the rest of Manasseh's land was for his sons, and Henry notes the daughters reaped the benefit of their pious zeal and prudent forecast, as those who make sure of a place in the saints' inheritance in this world will have the comfort of it in the other. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: Manasseh had in Issachar and in Asher Bethshean and her towns, Ibleam and her towns, the inhabitants of Dor and her towns, Endor and her towns, Taanach and her towns, and Megiddo and her towns, even three countries. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: Yet Manasseh's children could not drive out the inhabitants of those cities, but the Canaanites would dwell in that land, since though the tribes should intermix to do good offices to one another as one Israel, they suffered the Canaanites to live among them against God's command, to serve their own ends. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: Yet when Israel waxed strong, they put the Canaanites to tribute, but did not utterly drive them out. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: Joseph's children spoke to Joshua, asking why he had given them but one lot and one portion for an inheritance, seeing they were a great people, as the Lord had blessed them hitherto. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: Joshua answered that if they were a great people, they should get up to the wood country and cut down for themselves there in the land of the Perizzites and giants, if mount Ephraim was too narrow for them. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: Joseph's children replied the hill was not enough for them, and all the Canaanites in the valley had chariots of iron, both in Bethshean and her towns and in the valley of Jezreel. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: Joshua spoke to the house of Joseph, to Ephraim and Manasseh, saying they were a great people with great power and should not have only one lot. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: But the mountain would be theirs, for it was a wood, and they should cut it down, and its outgoings would be theirs, and they should drive out the Canaanites though they had iron chariots and were strong, and Henry notes Joshua, as a public person, had no more regard for his own tribe than any other, governing without favour, and that there is more kindness in pointing out advantages within reach than in granting indulgences to sloth. — Matthew Henry

No note for verse(s) 7, 8, 9, 10 — the public-domain sources consulted give no distinct explanation for these verses beyond the list itself, so nothing was invented for them.
