Study Notes — Joshua

Chapter 20

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: The Lord also spoke to Joshua, giving the law concerning the cities of refuge now that Israel was settled in their inheritance. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: Joshua was to speak to Israel to appoint cities of refuge, of which the Lord had spoken by the hand of Moses. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: The slayer who killed any person unawares and unwittingly might flee thither, and those cities would be his refuge from the avenger of blood, typifying the relief the gospel provides for penitent sinners in Christ, to whom believers flee for refuge. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: When the fugitive stood at the entering of the gate of the city and declared his cause in the elders' ears, they were to take him into the city and give him a place to dwell among them. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: If the avenger of blood pursued him, they were not to deliver the slayer into his hand, because he smote his neighbour unwittingly and hated him not before. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: He was to dwell in that city until he stood before the congregation for judgment, and until the death of the high priest then in office, and then return to his own city and house from whence he fled. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: They appointed Kedesh in Galilee in mount Naphtali, Shechem in mount Ephraim, and Kirjatharba, which is Hebron, in the mountain of Judah, and Henry notes the names' significance for Christ our Refuge: Kedesh signifies holy, Shechem a shoulder, the government being upon his shoulder, and Hebron fellowship, believers being called into fellowship with Christ. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: On the other side of Jordan eastward they assigned Bezer in the wilderness on the plain from Reuben, Ramoth in Gilead from Gad, and Golan in Bashan from Manasseh, and Henry notes Bezer is a fortification, God our stronghold, Ramoth height or exaltation, to which we are called in Christ, and Golan joy or exultation, for in him the saints are justified and shall glory. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: These were the cities appointed for all Israel and for the stranger sojourning among them, that whoever killed any person unawares might flee thither and not die by the avenger of blood until he stood before the congregation, and Henry notes they stood so that a man might reach one in half a day from any part of the country, for God is ever a Refuge at hand, and they were all Levites' cities, so the fugitive had God's servants with him to instruct and pray for him. — Matthew Henry
