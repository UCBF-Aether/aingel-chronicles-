Study Notes — Joshua

Chapter 11

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Henry: a fresh confederacy is a fresh danger, and fresh dangers drive God's people back to fresh supports from his word — the trial is proportioned to the strength given for it. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: Henry: the wonders already wrought are meant to make Israel act vigorously themselves, not to sit still before a host counted like sand on the shore. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: The Lord told Joshua not to be afraid because of them, for tomorrow about this time he would deliver them all slain before Israel, and Joshua was to hough their horses and burn their chariots with fire. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: Henry: encouragement turns into action — Joshua falls on the confederacy at Merom, acting vigorously on the promise rather than waiting on the enemy's numbers. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: Joshua did to them as the Lord bade him, houghing their horses and burning their chariots with fire, showing self-denial in compliance with God's command, since things the carnal heart depends on are hurtful to the life of faith. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: Henry: Hazor's fall is a judgment whose measure is full — a people left to the pride and obstinacy of their hearts meet the vengeance they merited, with Israel as executioner by command. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: Henry: Hazor's utter destruction is the judgment of a people whose iniquity is full — vengeance merited, not mere conquest, and Israel the appointed executioner. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: Henry: the taking of cities and kings is execution of a command, not spoil of ambition — Israel acts under the command given to Moses, and the judgment is the Lord's. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: Henry: duty kept and promise performed answer each other — Joshua leaves nothing undone, and God's word and his works are found to agree together. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: Joshua's long war with all those kings is Henry's picture of the believer's warfare: tedious, sharp and difficult, with no lasting peace to be expected until death, yet a rest from war remains for the people of God. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: Henry: the cities would not seek peace, left to the pride and obstinacy of their hearts — Gibeon alone stands as the exception to a refusal shared by all the rest. — Matthew Henry

20 COMMENTARY: Henry: the hardening is a judgment of abandonment — restraints withdrawn, hearts left to their own pride and enmity, driving them to battle rather than to peace. — Matthew Henry

21 COMMENTARY: At that time Joshua came and cut off the Anakims from the mountains, from Hebron, Debir, Anab, all the mountains of Judah, and all the mountains of Israel, destroying them utterly with their cities. — Matthew Henry

22 COMMENTARY: None of the Anakims were left in Israel's land, only in Gaza, Gath, and Ashdod there remained, and Henry notes the sons of Anak need never be a terror to God's Israel, for their day to fall will come. — Matthew Henry

23 COMMENTARY: Henry: the land rests from war, not in peace with the Canaanites — a rest that foreshadows the rest remaining for God's people when their warfare is accomplished. — Matthew Henry

No note for verse(s) 2, 3, 5, 8, 13, 14, 16, 17 — the public-domain sources consulted give no distinct explanation for these verses beyond the list itself, so nothing was invented for them.
