Study Notes — Joshua

Chapter 8

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: The Lord told Joshua to fear not nor be dismayed, but take all the people of war and go up to Ai, for he had given into his hand the king of Ai, his people, his city, and his land, and Henry notes that once sin is faithfully put away we may look to hear from God to our comfort. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: He was to do to Ai and its king as to Jericho and its king, except that the spoil and cattle they could take for a prey, and he was to lay an ambush behind the city. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: Joshua arose with all the people of war to go up against Ai, choosing thirty thousand mighty men of valour and sending them away by night. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: He commanded them to lie in wait against the city behind it, not going very far from the city but ready. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: Joshua and all the people with him would approach the city, and when Ai's men came out against them as at first, they would flee before them. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: Ai's men would come out after them until they had drawn them from the city, supposing Israel fled before them as at first. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: Then the ambush was to rise up and seize the city, for the Lord their God would deliver it into their hand. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: When they had taken the city they were to set it on fire, according to the commandment of the Lord, and Joshua had commanded them accordingly. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: Joshua sent them forth, and they went to lie in ambush between Bethel and Ai, on the west side of Ai, while Joshua lodged that night among the people. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: Joshua rose early, numbered the people, and went up with the elders of Israel before the people to Ai. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: All the people of war with him went up, drew nigh, and came before the city, pitching on the north side, with a valley between them and Ai. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: He took about five thousand men and set them to lie in ambush between Bethel and Ai, on the west side of the city. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: When the people were set, the host north of the city and the liers in wait west of it, Joshua went that night into the midst of the valley. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: When the king of Ai saw it, he hasted and rose up early with his people to battle at a time appointed, before the plain, not knowing there were liers in wait against him behind the city. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: Joshua and all Israel made as if beaten before them and fled by the way of the wilderness, and Henry notes Joshua's prudence: those maintaining spiritual conflicts must not love their ease, and he never drew back till the work was done. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: All the people in Ai were called together to pursue after Israel, and they pursued Joshua and were drawn away from the city. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: Not a man was left in Ai or Bethel that went not out after Israel, and they left the city open and pursued after Israel. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: The Lord told Joshua to stretch out the spear in his hand toward Ai, for he would give it into his hand, and Joshua stretched out the spear. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: The ambush arose quickly, ran as soon as he stretched out his hand, entered the city, took it, and hasted to set it on fire. — Matthew Henry

20 COMMENTARY: When Ai's men looked behind them they saw the smoke of the city ascend to heaven, and they had no power to flee this way or that, while those who fled to the wilderness turned back on the pursuers. — Matthew Henry

21 COMMENTARY: When Joshua and all Israel saw the ambush had taken the city and its smoke ascended, they turned again and slew the men of Ai. — Matthew Henry

22 COMMENTARY: The ambush issued out of the city against them, so they were in the midst of Israel, some on this side and some on that, and Israel smote them until they let none remain or escape. — Matthew Henry

23 COMMENTARY: The king of Ai they took alive and brought him to Joshua. — Matthew Henry

24 COMMENTARY: When Israel had made an end of slaying Ai's inhabitants in the field and wilderness, all Israel returned to Ai and smote it with the edge of the sword. — Matthew Henry

25 COMMENTARY: All that fell that day, both men and women, were twelve thousand, even all the men of Ai. — Matthew Henry

26 COMMENTARY: Joshua drew not back his hand with the spear until he had utterly destroyed all the inhabitants of Ai. — Matthew Henry

27 COMMENTARY: Only the cattle and spoil of the city Israel took for a prey to themselves, according to the Lord's command to Joshua. — Matthew Henry

28 COMMENTARY: Joshua burnt Ai and made it a heap forever, a desolation to this day. — Matthew Henry

29 COMMENTARY: The king of Ai he hanged on a tree until eventide, and at sunset Joshua commanded his carcase taken down, cast at the entering of the gate, and a great heap of stones raised upon it that remains to this day, since God the righteous Judge had sentenced the Canaanites and Israel only executed his doom. — Matthew Henry

30 COMMENTARY: Then Joshua built an altar to the Lord God of Israel in mount Ebal, without delay and without caring for the unsettled state of Israel, confirming the covenant as appointed. — Matthew Henry

31 COMMENTARY: He built it as Moses commanded, as written in the book of the law, an altar of whole stones over which no man had lift up iron, offering burnt offerings and peace offerings upon it. — Matthew Henry

32 COMMENTARY: He wrote upon the stones a copy of the law of Moses, which he wrote in the presence of the children of Israel, since it is a great mercy to have God's law in writing in a known tongue, seen and read of all. — Matthew Henry

33 COMMENTARY: All Israel, elders, officers, and judges, stood on either side of the ark before the priests bearing the ark, half before Gerizim and half before Ebal, as Moses had commanded to bless the people, with the stranger and him born among them. — Matthew Henry

34 COMMENTARY: Afterward he read all the words of the law, the blessings and cursings, according to all written in the book of the law. — Matthew Henry

35 COMMENTARY: There was not a word of all Moses commanded which Joshua read not before all the congregation of Israel, with the women, little ones, and strangers conversant among them, since we must not defer covenanting with God until settled in the world. — Matthew Henry
