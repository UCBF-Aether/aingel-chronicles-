Study Notes — Joshua

Chapter 22

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Then Joshua called the Reubenites, Gadites, and half tribe of Manasseh. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: He said they had kept all Moses commanded them, and obeyed his voice in all he commanded them. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: They had not left their brethren these many days to this day, but had kept the charge of the commandment of the Lord their God. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: Now the Lord had given rest to their brethren as promised, therefore they were to return to their tents, to the land of their possession which Moses gave them beyond Jordan. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: But they were to take diligent heed to do the commandment and law Moses commanded: to love the Lord their God, walk in all his ways, keep his commandments, cleave unto him, and serve him with all heart and soul, since those who have the commandment have it in vain unless they do it. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: So Joshua blessed them and sent them away, and they went to their tents. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: Now to half Manasseh Moses had given possession in Bashan, but to the other half Joshua gave among their brethren on this side of Jordan westward, and when he sent them away he blessed them. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: He told them to return with much riches to their tents, with very much cattle, silver, gold, brass, iron, and very much raiment, and divide the spoil of their enemies with their brethren. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: Reuben, Gad, and half Manasseh returned and departed from Israel out of Shiloh in Canaan, to go to Gilead, to their possession whereof they were possessed according to the Lord's word by Moses. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: When they came to the borders of Jordan in Canaan, they built there a great altar to see to by Jordan. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: Israel heard that Reuben, Gad, and half Manasseh had built an altar over against Canaan, in the borders of Jordan, at the passage of Israel's children. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: When Israel heard of it, the whole congregation gathered at Shiloh to go up to war against them. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: Israel sent Phinehas son of Eleazar the priest to Reuben, Gad, and half Manasseh in Gilead. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: With him went ten princes, a prince of each chief house throughout Israel's tribes, every one head of his father's house among Israel's thousands. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: They came to Reuben, Gad, and half Manasseh in Gilead and spoke with them. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: They charged: What trespass is this ye have committed against the God of Israel, to turn away this day from following the Lord, in that ye have builded you an altar, that ye might rebel this day against the Lord? — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: They asked if the iniquity of Peor was too little for them, from which they were not cleansed to this day, although there was a plague in the Lord's congregation. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: They warned that if they must turn away this day from following the Lord, seeing they rebelled today, tomorrow he would be wroth with the whole congregation of Israel. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: Notwithstanding, if their land was unclean, they were welcome to pass over to the Lord's land, where his tabernacle dwelt, and take possession among them, but they were not to rebel against the Lord nor against Israel by building an altar beside the Lord's altar. — Matthew Henry

20 COMMENTARY: They recalled that Achan son of Zerah committed a trespass in the accursed thing, and wrath fell on all Israel's congregation, and that man perished not alone in his iniquity, and Henry notes the remembrance of former great sins should engage us to guard against the beginnings of sin, for the way of sin is downhill. — Matthew Henry

21 COMMENTARY: Then Reuben, Gad, and half Manasseh answered the heads of Israel's thousands, and Henry notes the tribes took their brethren's reproofs in good part, answering with solemnity and meekness. — Matthew Henry

22 COMMENTARY: They appealed: The Lord God of gods, the Lord God of gods, he knoweth, and Israel shall know, if it be in rebellion or transgression against the Lord, save us not this day. — Matthew Henry

23 COMMENTARY: If they had built an altar to turn from following the Lord, or to offer burnt offering, meat offering, or peace offerings thereon, let the Lord himself require it. — Matthew Henry

24 COMMENTARY: Rather they had done it for fear of this thing, saying, In time to come your children might speak to their children, saying, What have ye to do with the Lord God of Israel? — Matthew Henry

25 COMMENTARY: For the Lord had made Jordan a border between them and Reuben and Gad, so their children might say, Ye have no part in the Lord, and so make their children cease from fearing the Lord. — Matthew Henry

26 COMMENTARY: Therefore they said, Let us now prepare to build us an altar, not for burnt offering nor for sacrifice. — Matthew Henry

27 COMMENTARY: But that it might be a witness between them and Israel and their generations after them, that they might do the service of the Lord before him with burnt offerings, sacrifices, and peace offerings, so their children might not say they had no part in the Lord. — Matthew Henry

28 COMMENTARY: Therefore they said that when it should be said to them or their generations in time to come, they would say, Behold the pattern of the Lord's altar which their fathers made, not for burnt offerings nor sacrifices, but a witness between them and Israel. — Matthew Henry

29 COMMENTARY: God forbid they should rebel against the Lord and turn this day from following him, to build an altar for burnt offerings, meat offerings, or sacrifices, beside the altar of the Lord their God before his tabernacle. — Matthew Henry

30 COMMENTARY: When Phinehas and the princes and heads of Israel's thousands heard the words Reuben, Gad, and Manasseh answered, it pleased them. — Matthew Henry

31 COMMENTARY: Phinehas said to them, This day we perceive the Lord is among us, because ye have not committed this trespass against the Lord, and now ye have delivered Israel out of the Lord's hand. — Matthew Henry

32 COMMENTARY: Phinehas and the princes returned from Reuben, Gad, and Manasseh out of Gilead to Canaan to Israel, and brought them word again. — Matthew Henry

33 COMMENTARY: The thing pleased Israel, and they blessed God and did not intend to go up against them in battle to destroy the land wherein Reuben and Gad dwelt. — Matthew Henry

34 COMMENTARY: Reuben and Gad called the altar Ed, for it shall be a witness between us that the Lord is God, and Henry notes it is well there was on both sides a disposition to peace as well as zeal for God, since quarrels about religion, for want of wisdom and love, often prove the fiercest and hardest to make up. — Matthew Henry
