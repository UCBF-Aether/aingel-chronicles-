Study Notes — Joshua

Chapter 24

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Henry: the gathering is a solemn religious assembly, not a mere muster — elders, heads, judges, and officers presented before God, and Joshua, his days lengthened, has one further service: a last testimony. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: Henry: the sermon opens with doctrine — a history of what God did for their fathers, beginning where their fathers served other gods, so that grace, not merit, frames everything that follows. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: Henry: in the recital God is the actor — he took Abraham, led him through Canaan, and multiplied his seed: the history is God's doing, rehearsed as doctrine before any demand is made. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: Henry: the recital names God's distinguishing hand — Esau settled in Seir, Jacob's house taken down to Egypt — each line placed by providence, not by chance. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: Henry: Moses and Aaron are sent, Egypt is plagued, and the people are brought out — deliverance by appointed instruments, in God's own order, as the doctrine of the sermon. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: Henry: the fathers are brought out and pursued to the sea — the recital's deliverance stands at the water's edge, where pursuit ends and freedom begins. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: When they cried unto the Lord, he put darkness between them and the Egyptians, brought the sea upon them and covered them, and their eyes had seen what he had done in Egypt, and they dwelt in the wilderness a long season. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: He brought them into the land of the Amorites beyond Jordan, and they fought with Israel, but he delivered them into Israel's hand, and they possessed their land, and he destroyed them from before Israel. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: Henry: Balak's war and Balaam's hire enter the recital as a foiled curse — the enemy's weapon turned aside, before the blessing that follows is even named. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: Henry: God would not listen to Balaam, so blessing came where cursing was bought — deliverance by overruling a prophet's mouth, not by Israel's sword. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: They went over Jordan and came to Jericho, and Jericho's men fought against them, the Amorites, Perizzites, Canaanites, Hittites, Girgashites, Hivites, and Jebusites, and the Lord delivered them into their hand. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: Henry: the hornet goes before them — the victory is God's own instrument, not their sword or bow, and the recital says so plainly, lest Israel claim the work. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: Henry: the recital ends in sheer gift — land unlaboured, cities unbuilt, vineyards unplanted: everything received sets up the exhortation to gratitude that follows. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: Now therefore they were to fear the Lord and serve him in sincerity and truth, and put away the gods their fathers served on the other side of the flood and in Egypt, and serve the Lord, since the application of this history of God's mercies is an exhortation to serve God in gratitude, that his favour might be continued. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: If it seemed evil to them to serve the Lord, they were to choose that day whom they would serve, whether the gods their fathers served beyond the flood, or the gods of the Amorites in whose land they dwelt, but as for Joshua and his house, they would serve the Lord, and Henry notes it is essential that God's service be performed with a willing mind, for love is the only genuine principle of acceptable service. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: Henry: their answer is a full purpose of heart — 'God forbid' — the willing mind the covenant requires, not a form imposed as a task. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: For the Lord their God was he that brought them and their fathers up out of Egypt, from the house of bondage, and did those great signs in their sight, and preserved them in all the way wherein they went, and among all the people through whom they passed. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: Henry: their resolve follows the history — because God drove out the nations, they answer with service; and Joshua will test whether the purpose holds beyond words. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: Henry: Joshua checks their confidence — a holy and jealous God is not served by mere forms or self-sufficiency; acceptable service springs from love, in spirit and in truth, and purposes will be vain without it. — Matthew Henry

20 COMMENTARY: Henry: Joshua states the fatal consequence plainly — good received and then forsaken ends in hurt and consumption, and the blame rests on those who chose it. — Matthew Henry

21 COMMENTARY: Henry: they repeat their choice after the warning — a deliberate purpose, restated plainly, which Joshua will now bind by covenant. — Matthew Henry

22 COMMENTARY: Henry: the covenant binds them by their own mouths — they are made witnesses against themselves, binding their own choice by their own answer. — Matthew Henry

23 COMMENTARY: Henry: the demand reaches inward — strange gods put away, and the heart inclined: outward profession without inward turning is the form Joshua warns against. — Matthew Henry

24 COMMENTARY: Henry: their answer joins service to obedience — the Lord they will serve, and the voice they will obey: profession and practice spoken together. — Matthew Henry

25 COMMENTARY: Joshua's covenant at Shechem binds a deliberate choice: Henry insists acceptable service must spring from love and a willing mind, not mere form, and the statute and ordinance stand as the solemn bond of that choice. — Matthew Henry

26 COMMENTARY: Henry: the covenant is written and monumented — words in the book of the law, and a stone set up, so the agreement outlasts the voices that made it. — Matthew Henry

27 COMMENTARY: Henry: the stone becomes a witness that 'heard' the words — the very walls and trees before which purposes were uttered will testify if the people deny their God. — Matthew Henry

28 COMMENTARY: So Joshua let the people depart, every man unto his inheritance, taking his last leave of them in this affecting manner, since if they perished their blood would be upon their own heads. — Matthew Henry

29 COMMENTARY: Henry: the most useful men, having served their generation according to God's will, fall asleep one after another — Joshua's long life ends in service, not in reign. — Matthew Henry

30 COMMENTARY: Henry: he is buried in his own inheritance at Timnathserah — the servant rests in the portion he received, while the work passes to others. — Matthew Henry

31 COMMENTARY: Henry: service outlives the man — Israel serves through Joshua's days and the elders' days, while witnesses who had known God's works still live to carry the memory. — Matthew Henry

32 COMMENTARY: The bones of Joseph, which Israel brought up out of Egypt, they buried in Shechem, in a parcel of ground Jacob bought of Hamor's sons for a hundred pieces of silver, and it became the inheritance of Joseph's children, and Henry notes Joseph gave commandment concerning his bones, that they should not rest in their grave till Israel had rest in the land of promise. — Matthew Henry

33 COMMENTARY: Eleazar son of Aaron died, and they buried him in a hill pertaining to Phinehas his son, in mount Ephraim, and Henry notes the most useful men, having served their generation according to God's will, one after another fall asleep, but Jesus rose from the dead and saw no corruption. — Matthew Henry
