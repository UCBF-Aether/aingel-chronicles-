Study Notes — 2 John

Chapter 1

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: John writes as the elder, a title of age and office rather than of name, to the elect lady and her children, whom he loves in the truth. Henry reads the lady as a noble Christian matron whose household is known for holiness, and JFB notes that Christian love here rests on shared truth, not mere affection. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: That love is for the truth's sake, the truth which dwells in believers and shall be with them for ever. Henry stresses that true religion is not a passing mood but an abiding inward life, and JFB joins this verse to the love of verse 1. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: Grace, mercy, and peace are wished from God the Father and from Jesus Christ, the Son of the Father, in truth and love. JFB orders the three gifts: grace covers guilt, mercy relieves misery, and peace follows from both. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: John rejoiced greatly to find some of her children walking in truth, as commanded by the Father. Henry sees early godly training bearing fruit, and JFB notes John probably met them on a tour of superintendence. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: John beseeches rather than commands, asking mutual love under a commandment old from the beginning. Henry notes love will prevail where bare authority is slighted, and JFB adds that the command is old in its preaching yet new in its gospel ground, imitation of God who first loved us. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: Love is defined as walking after God's commandments, and the commandment itself is to walk in that love. Henry calls universal obedience the proof of sincere love, and JFB sums it up: love is the fulfilling of the law. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: Many deceivers have gone out who confess not that Jesus Christ has come in the flesh, and such a one is a deceiver and an antichrist. Henry warns that denying Christ's person undermines his glory and kingdom, and JFB notes the denial strikes at both the possibility and the actuality of the incarnation. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: Believers must look to themselves, lest they lose what has been wrought and miss a full reward. Henry warns that fair attainments in Christ's school can be lost at last, and JFB explains the full reward as fully consummated glory, proportioned to faithfulness. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: Whoever runs ahead and abides not in the doctrine of Christ has not God, but whoever abides in it has both Father and Son. Henry reads departure from gospel doctrine as departure from God himself, since that doctrine is the appointed guide to him. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: A teacher who brings not this doctrine is not to be received into the house nor wished God speed. Henry allows relief of pressing need but not encouragement for ill service, and JFB stresses the greeting forbidden is the token of Christian brotherhood, not mere courtesy. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: To bid such a teacher God speed is to partake in his evil deeds. Henry warns that favour, silence, contribution, or open defence can all share another's guilt, and JFB notes the Greek is having communion with his evil deeds. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: John defers much to a face-to-face visit, hoping their joy may be full, rather than writing with paper and ink. Henry observes some things are better spoken than written, and JFB notes the heart pours itself out more freely in person than by letter. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: The children of the elect sister greet the lady, closing the letter with a family salutation. Henry sees grace abounding toward the whole family, with two elect sisters and their children, and JFB notes the non-mention of the lady herself here has been taken to favour reading the lady as a church. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
