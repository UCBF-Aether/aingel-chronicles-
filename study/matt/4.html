Study Notes — Matthew

Chapter 4

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Immediately after his baptism, Jesus was led up by the Spirit from the low Jordan valley into the wilderness, probably the wild Judean desert, to be tempted. “Tempted” bears both senses: the Spirit conducted him there simply to have his faith tried, while the devil, as agent, aimed to seduce him from allegiance to God. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: He fasted forty days and forty nights, hunger unfelt during the whole period and coming on only at its close, as with Moses and Elijah for the same period. Mark and Luke show there was already a forty days' temptation before the three specific temptations, aimed at dislodging from his breast the consciousness of his Sonship proclaimed at baptism. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: The tempter came with a new scene: “If thou be the Son of God, command that these stones be made bread.” Hunger now keen, the taunt runs: born in a stable, hurried to Egypt, thirty obscure years in Galilee — is this befitting the Son of God? Satan aimed in every temptation to bring Christ to sin against God, here to despair of his Father's goodness and distrust his care. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: He answered, “It is written” (Deuteronomy 8:3): man shall not live by bread alone, but by every word proceeding out of God's mouth. Israel spent forty years in a waste wilderness divinely provided for, to prove support depends not on bread but on God's unfailing word of promise; as man, Christ would await divine supply rather than snatch unwarranted relief. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: The devil conducted him into the holy city, so called as the city of the Great King and seat of the temple, and set him on the pinnacle of the temple — a certain well-known projection, perhaps Herod's portico overhanging the Kidron ravine. Satan has no objection to holy places as the scene of his assaults, and high places are slippery places. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: “If thou be the Son of God, cast thyself down, for it is written, He shall give his angels charge concerning thee” (Psalm 91:11-12) — Satan himself with a Bible under his arm and a text in his mouth. He omits “to keep thee in all thy ways,” concealing that casting himself down would not be one of his ways of duty; having felt the power of God's word, the tempter now tries it from his own mouth. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: Jesus said, “It is written again” (Deuteronomy 6:16): thou shalt not tempt the Lord thy God. Preservation in danger is divinely pledged, but to create danger to put the promised security skeptically to the proof, or wantonly demand a display of it, would forfeit the right to expect preservation. Despair and presumption are the two dangerous extremes. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: Again the devil conducted him to an exceeding high mountain and showed him all the kingdoms of the world and the glory of them — Luke adding “in a moment of time,” suggesting the tempter was permitted preternaturally to extend Christ's range of vision for a moment and throw a glitter over the scene. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: “All these things will I give thee, for that is delivered unto me, and to whomsoever I will I give it, if thou wilt fall down and worship me” — the sole but monstrous condition, with no Scripture quoted now because none could support so blasphemous a claim. Satan's sway is real in the sense of men's voluntary subjection, but his offer was a deputed supremacy as his gift and for his ends; the mask of piety is thrown off and he stands as God's rival. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: Then Jesus said, “Get thee hence, Satan,” calling the tempter by his right name, knowledge till now concealed, and ordering him off — final evidence that Matthew's order is the true one. “For it is written” (Deuteronomy 6:13): thou shalt worship the Lord thy God, and him only shalt thou serve, “serve” being a word used only of religious service, and “only” added to bring out the prohibition emphatically. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: Then the devil left him — Luke says, when he had exhausted every mode of temptation, he departed “till a season.” Behold, angels came and ministered unto him, supplying food as they did to Elijah; having refused to claim their illegitimate ministration, with what deep joy would he accept their services sent unasked at the close of the temptation. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: When Jesus heard John was cast into prison — “delivered up” — he withdrew into Galilee. It is just with God to take the gospel from those that slight it, and Christ will not stay long where he is not welcome; yet his withdrawal opened the Galilean ministry. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: Leaving Nazareth — passing it by rather than making it his stated residence — he came and dwelt in Capernaum on the northwest shore of the Sea of Galilee, in the borders of Zebulun and Naphtali. Capernaum was chosen for its mixed population, its centrality, its convenience for crossing the lake, and because four or five of the Twelve lived there. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: This move fulfilled what was spoken by Isaiah the prophet, Isaiah 9:1-2. The Evangelist seizes the point of the prophecy: Messiah taking up his abode in those very regions and shedding his light upon them is the fulfilment, and if not thus fulfilled, it received no fulfilment at all. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: “The land of Zebulun, and the land of Naphtali, by the way of the sea, beyond Jordan, Galilee of the Gentiles” — Galilee so called as the frontier between the Holy Land and the external world, in the highway of northern invaders and in communication with Tyre's commerce. These northern tribes suffered the first deportation, and their distance from Jerusalem gave them their character for turbulence or independence. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: “The people which sat in darkness saw great light; and to them which sat in the region and shadow of death light is sprung up.” Those without Christ sit in darkness, in a contented posture, choosing it rather than light; when the gospel comes to any place or soul, it makes day there — light discovers and directs. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: From that time Jesus began to preach, “Repent, for the kingdom of heaven is at hand” — taking up the identical summons of his forerunner. The doctrine of repentance is right gospel doctrine: not only the austere John but the gracious Jesus preached it, and the kingdom's economy was only “at hand” until the cross and Pentecost opened the fountain for sin and uncleanness. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

18 COMMENTARY: Walking by the Sea of Galilee, he saw Simon called Peter and Andrew his brother casting a net, for they were fishers. He went not to Herod's court nor to Jerusalem among the chief priests, but to the sea among fishermen: Christ chooses the foolish things of the world to confound the wise, and diligence in an honest calling is no hindrance to a holy life. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

19 COMMENTARY: “Follow me, and I will make you fishers of men” — raising them from a lower to a higher fishing, as David was raised from a lower to a higher feeding. They had followed him before as common disciples; now they must leave their calling, for those who follow Christ aright must be ready at his command to part with all things. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

20 COMMENTARY: They straightway left their nets and followed him. This instance of the power of the Lord Jesus encourages dependence on his grace: he speaks, and it is done. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

21 COMMENTARY: Going on, he saw James son of Zebedee and John his brother in the ship with their father, mending their nets, and he called them. It is a happy thing to see children careful of their parents and dutiful, and good to be found doing when Christ comes. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

22 COMMENTARY: They immediately left the ship and their father and followed him — Mark adding that Zebedee was left in the ship with the hired servants, showing the family were in easy circumstances. JFB distinguishes this calling from John's earlier Judean call and from Luke's later miraculous-draught call: here the four are called separately in pairs, before any public Galilean appearance. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

23 COMMENTARY: Jesus went about all Galilee, teaching in their synagogues — houses of local worship, one wherever ten learned men resided, after which Christian congregations were modelled — and preaching the gospel of the kingdom, healing every sickness and every disease. His miracles confirmed his divine mission and were emblems of the healing power of his doctrine. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

24 COMMENTARY: His fame went throughout all Syria, and they brought him all sick people: those taken with divers diseases and torments, the demon-possessed, the lunatic or moon-struck, and the palsied — and he healed them. Three diseases are named by Henry as the greatest: palsy the greatest weakness of body, lunacy the greatest malady of mind, possession the greatest misery of both; yet none was too bad or too hard for Christ to heal with a word. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

25 COMMENTARY: Great multitudes followed him from Galilee, Decapolis — ten Greek-settled cities east of Jordan — Jerusalem, and beyond Jordan, Perea. The vast, varied concourse gives the reader some idea of the audience for the astonishing discourse of the next three chapters, the Sermon on the Mount. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
