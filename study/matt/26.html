Study Notes — Matthew

Chapter 26

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: And it came to pass, when Jesus had finished all these sayings, he said unto his disciples. Our Lord had often told of his sufferings as at a distance, now he speaks of them as at hand. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: Ye know that after two days is the feast of the passover, and the Son of man is betrayed to be crucified. Jesus, the true paschal Lamb, was to be sacrificed for us at that very time, and his death and resurrection rendered public. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: Then assembled together the chief priests, and the scribes, and the elders of the people, unto the palace of the high priest, who was called Caiaphas. At the same time the Jewish council consulted how they might put him to death secretly. But it pleased God to defeat their intention. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: And consulted that they might take Jesus by subtilty, and kill him. Subtilty, not open accusation: they knew the people would not bear a public seizure, so they plotted in secret. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: But they said, Not on the feast day, lest there be an uproar among the people. Even murderers observe the calendar: they feared the feast crowds more than they feared God. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: Now when Jesus was in Bethany, in the house of Simon the leper. The pouring ointment upon the head of Christ was a token of the highest respect. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: There came unto him a woman having an alabaster box of very precious ointment, and poured it on his head, as he sat at meat. Where there is true love in the heart to Jesus Christ, nothing will be thought too good to bestow upon him. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: But when his disciples saw it, they had indignation, saying, To what purpose is this waste? The more Christ's servants and their services are cavilled at, the more he manifests his acceptance. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: For this ointment might have been sold for much, and given to the poor. The objection wears the mask of charity: the poor are invoked to condemn a gift to Christ. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: When Jesus understood it, he said unto them, Why trouble ye the woman? for she hath wrought a good work upon me. Christ defends the woman against his own disciples: the work is good because its object is himself. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: For ye have the poor always with you; but me ye have not always. The poor remain for lifelong charity; his bodily presence does not. The anointing belongs to this unrepeatable hour. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: For in that she hath poured this ointment on my body, she did it for my burial. Unwittingly, her love anoints him for the grave before his enemies prepare it. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: Verily I say unto you, Wheresoever this gospel shall be preached in the whole world, there shall also this, that this woman hath done, be told for a memorial of her. This act of faith and love was so remarkable, that it would be reported, as a memorial of Mary's faith and love, to all future ages, and in all places where the gospel should be preached. This prophecy is fulfilled. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: Then one of the twelve, called Judas Iscariot, went unto the chief priests. There were but twelve called apostles, and one of them was like a devil; surely we must never expect any society to be quite pure on this side heaven. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: And said unto them, What will ye give me, and I will deliver him unto you? And they covenanted with him for thirty pieces of silver. The greater profession men make of religion, the greater opportunity they have of doing mischief, if their hearts be not right with God. Observe, that Christ's own disciple, who knew so well his doctrine and manner of his life, and was false to him, could not charge him with any thing criminal, though it would have served to justify his treachery. What did Judas want? Was not he welcome wherever his Master was? Did he not fare as Christ fared? It is not the lack, but the love of money, that is the root of all evil. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: And from that time he sought opportunity to betray him. After he had made that wicked bargain, Judas had time to repent, and to revoke it; but when lesser acts of dishonesty have hardened the conscience men do without hesitation that which is more shameful. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: Now the first day of the feast of unleavened bread the disciples came to Jesus, saying unto him, Where wilt thou that we prepare for thee to eat the passover? Observe, the place for their eating the passover was pointed out by Christ to the disciples. He knows those hidden ones who favour his cause, and will graciously visit all who are willing to receive him. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: And he said, Go into the city to such a man, and say unto him, The Master saith, My time is at hand; I will keep the passover at thy house with my disciples. The disciples did as Jesus had appointed. Those who would have Christ's presence in the gospel passover, must do what he says. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: And the disciples did as Jesus had appointed them; and they made ready the passover. Obedience without sight of the householder's face: they go on his word alone, and find all ready. — Matthew Henry

20 COMMENTARY: Now when the even was come, he sat down with the twelve. The evening meal gathers the twelve for the last time around one table. — Matthew Henry

21 COMMENTARY: And as they did eat, he said, Verily I say unto you, that one of you shall betray me. It well becomes the disciples of Christ always to be jealous over themselves, especially in trying times. We know not how strongly we may be tempted, nor how far God may leave us to ourselves, therefore we have reason not to be high-minded, but to fear. — Matthew Henry

22 COMMENTARY: And they were exceeding sorrowful, and began every one of them to say unto him, Lord, is it I? Heart-searching examination and fervent prayer are especially proper before the Lord's supper, that, as Christ our Passover is now sacrificed for us, we may keep this feast, renewing our repentance, our faith in his blood, and surrendering ourselves to his service. Each asks, “Is it I?” — none points at his brother. — Matthew Henry

23 COMMENTARY: And he answered and said, He that dippeth his hand with me in the dish, the same shall betray me. The traitor shares the dish: betrayal comes not from afar but from the table itself. — Matthew Henry

24 COMMENTARY: The Son of man goeth as it is written of him: but woe unto that man by whom the Son of man is betrayed! it had been good for that man if he had not been born. The going is written; the woe is deserved. Prophecy does not excuse the betrayer. — Matthew Henry

25 COMMENTARY: Then Judas, which betrayed him, answered and said, Master, is it I? He said unto him, Thou hast said. Judas asks last, with the traitor's question on his lips, and receives the answer that condemns him out of his own mouth. — Matthew Henry

26 COMMENTARY: And as they were eating, Jesus took bread, and blessed it, and brake it, and gave it to the disciples, and said, Take, eat; this is my body. This ordinance of the Lord's supper is to us the passover supper, by which we commemorate a much greater deliverance than that of Israel out of Egypt. Take, eat; accept of Christ as he is offered to you; receive the atonement, approve of it, submit to his grace and his government. Meat looked upon, be the dish ever so well garnished, will not nourish; it must be fed upon: so must the doctrine of Christ. This is my body; that is, spiritually, it signifies and represents his body. We partake of the sun, not by having the sun put into our hands, but the beams of it darted down upon us; so we partake of Christ by partaking of his grace, and the blessed fruits of the breaking of his body. — Matthew Henry

27 COMMENTARY: And he took the cup, and gave thanks, and gave it to them, saying, Drink ye all of it. The blood of Christ is signified and represented by the wine. He gave thanks, to teach us to look to God in every part of the ordinance. This cup he gave to the disciples with a command, Drink ye all of it. — Matthew Henry

28 COMMENTARY: For this is my blood of the new testament, which is shed for many for the remission of sins. The pardon of sin is that great blessing which is, in the Lord's supper, conferred on all true believers; it is the foundation of all other blessings. — Matthew Henry

29 COMMENTARY: But I say unto you, I will not drink henceforth of this fruit of the vine, until that day when I drink it new with you in my Father's kingdom. He takes leave of such communion; and assures them of a happy meeting again at last; “Until that day when I drink it new with you”, may be understood of the joys and glories of the future state, which the saints shall partake with the Lord Jesus. That will be the kingdom of his Father; the wine of consolation will there be always new. While we look at the outward signs of Christ's body broken and his blood shed for the remission of our sins, let us recollect that the feast cost him as much as though he had literally given his flesh to be eaten and his blood for us to drink. — Matthew Henry

30 COMMENTARY: And when they had sung an hymn, they went out into the mount of Olives. The supper ends in song, and the song leads out toward the garden: praise before passion. — Matthew Henry

31 COMMENTARY: Then saith Jesus unto them, All ye shall be offended because of me this night: for it is written, I will smite the shepherd, and the sheep of the flock shall be scattered abroad. Improper self-confidence, like that of Peter, is the first step to a fall. There is a proneness in all of us to be over-confident. But those fall soonest and foulest, who are the most confident in themselves. Those are least safe, who think themselves most secure. — Matthew Henry

32 COMMENTARY: But after I am risen again, I will go before you into Galilee. Even in foretelling their scattering, he appoints the gathering place: risen, he will go before them into Galilee. — Matthew Henry

33 COMMENTARY: Peter answered and said unto him, Though all men shall be offended because of thee, yet will I never be offended. Satan is active to lead such astray; they are most off their guard: God leaves them to themselves, to humble them. — Matthew Henry

34 COMMENTARY: Jesus said unto him, Verily I say unto thee, That this night, before the cock crow, thou shalt deny me thrice. The denial is dated to this very night, before the cock crows: no room is left for Peter's confidence to hide. — Matthew Henry

35 COMMENTARY: Peter said unto him, Though I should die with thee, yet will I not deny thee. Likewise also said all the disciples. All join Peter's boast, and all will share his flight: confidence is contagious, and so is the fall. — Matthew Henry

36 COMMENTARY: Then cometh Jesus with them unto a place called Gethsemane, and saith unto the disciples, Sit ye here, while I go and pray yonder. He who made atonement for the sins of mankind, submitted himself in a garden of suffering, to the will of God, from which man had revolted in a garden of pleasure. — Matthew Henry

37 COMMENTARY: And he took with him Peter and the two sons of Zebedee, and began to be sorrowful and very heavy. Christ took with him into that part of the garden where he suffered his agony, only those who had witnessed his glory in his transfiguration. Those are best prepared to suffer with Christ, who have by faith beheld his glory. — Matthew Henry

38 COMMENTARY: Then saith he unto them, My soul is exceeding sorrowful, even unto death: tarry ye here, and watch with me. The words used denote the most entire dejection, amazement, anguish, and horror of mind; the state of one surrounded with sorrows, overwhelmed with miseries, and almost swallowed up with terror and dismay. He now began to be sorrowful, and never ceased to be so till he said, It is finished. — Matthew Henry

39 COMMENTARY: And he went a little farther, and fell on his face, and prayed, saying, O my Father, if it be possible, let this cup pass from me: nevertheless not as I will, but as thou wilt. He prayed that, if possible, the cup might pass from him. But he also showed his perfect readiness to bear the load of his sufferings; he was willing to submit to all for our redemption and salvation. According to this example of Christ, we must drink of the bitterest cup which God puts into our hands; though nature struggle, it must submit. It should be more our care to get troubles sanctified, and our hearts satisfied under them, than to get them taken away. — Matthew Henry

40 COMMENTARY: And he cometh unto the disciples, and findeth them asleep, and saith unto Peter, What, could ye not watch with me one hour? It is well for us that our salvation is in the hand of One who neither slumbers nor sleeps. All are tempted, but we should be much afraid of entering into temptation. — Matthew Henry

41 COMMENTARY: Watch and pray, that ye enter not into temptation: the spirit indeed is willing, but the flesh is weak. To be secured from this, we should watch and pray, and continually look unto the Lord to hold us up that we may be safe. — Matthew Henry

42 COMMENTARY: He went away again the second time, and prayed, saying, O my Father, if this cup may not pass away from me, except I drink it, thy will be done. The second prayer surrenders the condition: if the cup may not pass except he drink it, thy will be done. — Matthew Henry

43 COMMENTARY: And he came and found them asleep again: for their eyes were heavy. The disciples sleep again, eyes heavy: weakness repeats itself even after rebuke. — Matthew Henry

44 COMMENTARY: And he left them, and went away again, and prayed the third time, saying the same words. The third prayer repeats the same words: perseverance in submission, not novelty in petition, marks his agony. — Matthew Henry

45 COMMENTARY: Then cometh he to his disciples, and saith unto them, Sleep on now, and take your rest: behold, the hour is at hand, and the Son of man is betrayed into the hands of sinners. Doubtless our Lord had a clear and full view of the sufferings he was to endure, yet he spoke with the greatest calmness till this time. Christ was a Surety, who undertook to be answerable for our sins. Accordingly he was made sin for us, and suffered for our sins, the Just for the unjust; and Scripture ascribes his heaviest sufferings to the hand of God. He had full knowledge of the infinite evil of sin, and of the immense extent of that guilt for which he was to atone; with awful views of the Divine justice and holiness, and the punishment deserved by the sins of men, such as no tongue can express, or mind conceive. At the same time, Christ suffered being tempted; probably horrible thoughts were suggested by Satan that tended to gloom and every dreadful conclusion: these would be the more hard to bear from his perfect holiness. And did the load of imputed guilt so weigh down the soul of Him of whom it is said, He upholdeth all things by the word of his power? into what misery then must those sink whose sins are left upon their own heads! How will those escape who neglect so great salvation? — Matthew Henry

46 COMMENTARY: Rise, let us be going: behold, he is at hand that doth betray me. The hour of prayer ends and the hour of betrayal begins; he rises to meet it, not to flee it. — Matthew Henry

47 COMMENTARY: And while he yet spake, lo, Judas, one of the twelve, came, and with him a great multitude with swords and staves, from the chief priests and elders of the people. No enemies are so much to be abhorred as those professed disciples that betray Christ with a kiss. — Matthew Henry

48 COMMENTARY: Now he that betrayed him gave them a sign, saying, Whomsoever I shall kiss, that same is he: hold him fast. The sign of love becomes the sign of arrest: the kiss, meant for affection, is hired for identification. — Matthew Henry

49 COMMENTARY: And forthwith he came to Jesus, and said, Hail, master; and kissed him. Forthwith — without hesitation, without delay: the bargain moves straight to the kiss. — Matthew Henry

50 COMMENTARY: And Jesus said unto him, Friend, wherefore art thou come? Then came they, and laid hands on Jesus, and took him. God has no need of our services, much less of our sins, to bring about his purposes. Though Christ was crucified through weakness, it was voluntary weakness; he submitted to death. If he had not been willing to suffer, they could not conquer him. — Matthew Henry

51 COMMENTARY: And, behold, one of them which were with Jesus stretched out his hand, and drew his sword, and struck a servant of the high priest's, and smote off his ear. The sword flashes once, in defence of the Lord who needs no defence, and wounds a servant in the dark. — Matthew Henry

52 COMMENTARY: Then said Jesus unto him, Put up again thy sword into his place: for all they that take the sword shall perish with the sword. The sword is sheathed by command: his kingdom is not defended by the sword, and those who take it perish by it. — Matthew Henry

53 COMMENTARY: Thinkest thou that I cannot now pray to my Father, and he shall presently give me more than twelve legions of angels? Twelve legions stand ready at a prayer; he does not pray for them, because he wills to suffer. — Matthew Henry

54 COMMENTARY: But how then shall the scriptures be fulfilled, that thus it must be? The Scriptures must be fulfilled, and thus it must be: his refusal of the angels is obedience to the written word. — Matthew Henry

55 COMMENTARY: In that same hour said Jesus to the multitudes, Are ye come out as against a thief with swords and staves for to take me? I sat daily with you teaching in the temple, and ye laid no hold on me. It was a great sin for those who had left all to follow Jesus; now to leave him for they knew not what. What folly, for fear of death to flee from Him, whom they knew and acknowledged to be the Fountain of life! Yet the multitudes come by night with swords against one who sat daily teaching openly in the temple. — Matthew Henry

56 COMMENTARY: But all this was done, that the scriptures of the prophets might be fulfilled. Then all the disciples forsook him, and fled. The Scriptures are fulfilled in the arrest, and in the flight: the shepherd smitten, the sheep scattered, as written. — Matthew Henry

57 COMMENTARY: And they that had laid hold on Jesus led him away to Caiaphas the high priest, where the scribes and the elders were assembled. Jesus was hurried into Jerusalem. It looks ill, and bodes worse, when those who are willing to be Christ's disciples, are not willing to be known to be so. Here began Peter's denying him: for to follow Christ afar off, is to begin to go back from him. — Matthew Henry

58 COMMENTARY: But Peter followed him afar off unto the high priest's palace, and went in, and sat with the servants, to see the end. Peter follows afar off — the distance itself is the beginning of denial — and sits with the servants to see the end. — Matthew Henry

59 COMMENTARY: Now the chief priests, and elders, and all the council, sought false witness against Jesus, to put him to death. It is more our concern to prepare for the end, whatever it may be, than curiously to ask what the end will be. The event is God's, but the duty is ours. Now the Scriptures were fulfilled, which said, False witnesses are risen up against me. — Matthew Henry

60 COMMENTARY: But found none: yea, though many false witnesses came, yet found they none. At the last came two false witnesses. Even false witnesses could not agree: the council's case collapses for want of two agreeing voices, until at last two are found. — Matthew Henry

61 COMMENTARY: And said, This fellow said, I am able to destroy the temple of God, and to build it in three days. The charge twists his words: the temple of his body becomes the temple of stone, and a prophecy becomes a threat. — Matthew Henry

62 COMMENTARY: And the high priest arose, and said unto him, Answerest thou nothing? what is it which these witness against thee? The high priest demands an answer to a charge that answers itself with distortion; the prisoner is pressed to plead against perjury. — Matthew Henry

63 COMMENTARY: But Jesus held his peace, And the high priest answered and said unto him, I adjure thee by the living God, that thou tell us whether thou be the Christ, the Son of God. Christ was accused, that we might not be condemned; and if at any time we suffer thus, let us remember we cannot expect to fare better than our Master. When Christ was made sin for us, he was silent, and left it to his blood to speak. — Matthew Henry

64 COMMENTARY: Jesus saith unto him, Thou hast said: nevertheless I say unto you, Hereafter shall ye see the Son of man sitting on the right hand of power, and coming in the clouds of heaven. Hitherto Jesus had seldom professed expressly to be the Christ, the Son of God; the tenor of his doctrine spoke it, and his miracles proved it; but now he would not omit to make an open confession of it. It would have looked like declining his sufferings. He thus confessed, as an example and encouragement to his followers, to confess him before men, whatever hazard they ran. — Matthew Henry

65 COMMENTARY: Then the high priest rent his clothes, saying, He hath spoken blasphemy; what further need have we of witnesses? behold, now ye have heard his blasphemy. The rent clothes perform horror at the truth: blasphemy is charged against the confession that is the very centre of the faith. — Matthew Henry

66 COMMENTARY: What think ye? They answered and said, He is guilty of death. The council's verdict is swift: guilty of death, on the ground of his own confession. — Matthew Henry

67 COMMENTARY: Then did they spit in his face, and buffeted him; and others smote him with the palms of their hands. Disdain, cruel mocking, and abhorrence, are the sure portion of the disciple as they were of the Master, from such as would buffet and deride the Lord of glory. These things were exactly foretold in the fiftieth chapter of Isaiah. — Matthew Henry

68 COMMENTARY: Saying, Prophesy unto us, thou Christ: Who is he that smote thee? The mockery demands prophecy from the blindfolded Prophet: who smote thee — as if the Christ who sees all could not see through their hands. — Matthew Henry

69 COMMENTARY: Now Peter sat without in the palace: and a damsel came unto him, saying, Thou also wast with Jesus of Galilee. Peter's sin is truly related, for the Scriptures deal faithfully. Bad company leads to sin: those who needlessly thrust themselves into it, may expect to be tempted and insnared, as Peter. — Matthew Henry

70 COMMENTARY: But he denied before them all, saying, I know not what thou sayest. They scarcely can come out of such company without guilt or grief, or both. It is a great fault to be shy of Christ; and to dissemble our knowledge of him, when we are called to own him, is, in effect, to deny him. — Matthew Henry

71 COMMENTARY: And when he was gone out into the porch, another maid saw him, and said unto them that were there, This fellow was also with Jesus of Nazareth. The second accusation finds him in the porch, retreating from the first: flight within the palace still leaves him exposed. — Matthew Henry

72 COMMENTARY: And again he denied with an oath, I do not know the man. The second denial adds an oath to the first denial's evasion: each denial grows stronger as the truth grows harder to bear. — Matthew Henry

73 COMMENTARY: And after a while came unto him they that stood by, and said to Peter, Surely thou also art one of them; for thy speech bewrayeth thee. Peter's sin was aggravated; but he fell into the sin by surprise, not as Judas, with design. — Matthew Henry

74 COMMENTARY: Then began he to curse and to swear, saying, I know not the man. And immediately the cock crew. But conscience should be to us as the crowing of the cock, to put us in mind of the sins we had forgotten. — Matthew Henry

75 COMMENTARY: And Peter remembered the word of Jesus, which said unto him, Before the cock crow, thou shalt deny me thrice. And he went out, and wept bitterly. Peter was thus left to fall, to abate his self-confidence, and render him more modest, humble, compassionate, and useful to others. The event has taught believers many things ever since, and if infidels, Pharisees, and hypocrites stumble at it or abuse it, it is at their peril. Little do we know how we should act in very difficult situations, if we were left to ourselves. Let him, therefore, that thinketh he standeth, take heed lest he fall; let us all distrust our own hearts, and rely wholly on the Lord. Peter wept bitterly. Sorrow for sin must not be slight, but great and deep. Peter, who wept so bitterly for denying Christ, never denied him again, but confessed him often in the face of danger. True repentance for any sin will be shown by the contrary grace and duty; that is a sign of our sorrowing not only bitterly, but sincerely. — Matthew Henry
