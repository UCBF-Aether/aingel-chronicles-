Study Notes — Matthew

Chapter 2

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Jesus was born in Bethlehem of Judea, about six miles southwest of Jerusalem, in the days of Herod the Great, an Edomite made king by Rome — the sceptre departing from Judah as a sign the Messiah was at hand. The wise men were Magi, a learned class from the east, come to Jerusalem as the Jewish metropolis. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: “Where is he that is born King of the Jews?” is the language of strangers, not of Jews, and the star is safest regarded as a luminous meteor appearing under special laws for a special purpose. Their coming “to worship him” means religious homage, not mere civil respect, as the whole narrative shows. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: Herod was troubled as seeing a danger to his own throne, his guilty conscience perhaps adding other grounds of fear. All Jerusalem was troubled with him, dreading both revolutionary commotion and Herod's rage — a dead faith that hates truths which interfere with ambition. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: Herod gathered the chief priests — the current and former high priests with heads of the priestly courses — and the scribes, once mere transcribers of the law, now its interpreters, lawyers and divines. Together with the elders they formed the Sanhedrim, and Herod demanded of them, as authorized interpreters, where Christ should be born. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: Their answer was prompt: in Bethlehem of Judea, for thus it is written by Micah. JFB calls it an involuntary testimony from the highest tribunal in the nation — the same tribunal that would at length condemn him to die. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: “Thou Bethlehem, art not the least among the princes of Judah” differs verbally from Micah yet agrees in substance: though little, Bethlehem is lifted from lowest to highest rank because out of it shall come a Governor. “Rule” is “feed,” kings pictured as shepherds of God's people Israel. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: Having the place from the Sanhedrim, Herod privately called the wise men to fix the other point, the date, inquiring precisely what time the star appeared. The unsuspecting strangers told him all, presuming the star's appearance would mark the child's age. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: Herod sent them to Bethlehem to search diligently for the child and bring him word, “that I may come and worship him also” — the cunning hypocrite's cover. Yet the royal mandate meanwhile served the strangers as a safe conduct on their journey. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: The star they had seen in the east, apparently vanished in the interval, went before them again and stood over where the child was — hardly possible except by a luminous meteor, and not very high. JFB notes the Jewish ecclesiastics who could name the birthplace never trooped to Bethlehem, and God overruled even that cold indifference lest the news reach the tyrant too soon. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: When they saw the star they rejoiced with exceeding great joy, language of exuberant transport. Henry compares it to the joy of those who, after a long night of temptation and desertion, receive the Spirit of adoption witnessing that they are children of God. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: They came into a house, not the stable, and saw the child with Mary his mother — the Babe named first, then the mother, Joseph unnoticed as merely head of the house. Falling down to worship was lofty spiritual homage, and their gifts were religious offerings; the gold at least would prove serviceable for the unexpected journey to Egypt. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: Warned of God in a dream not to return to Herod, they departed to their own country another way. It must have surprised them, just as they prepared to carry glad news to the “pious” king, but the Lord knew the bloody old tyrant better than to let them see his face again. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: The angel appeared to Joseph in a dream: arise, take the child and his mother — a form repeated, hinting Joseph was no more than the Child's guardian — and flee into Egypt. Egypt, near at hand, a Roman province independent of Herod and much inhabited by Jews, was an easy refuge; the house of bondage had returned to its first use as a house of refuge. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: Joseph arose the same night and departed into Egypt, without delay. Henry notes this was a trial of the faith of Joseph and Mary, and their faith, being tried, was found firm — a comfort to any in trouble with their infants, remembering Christ's own straits as an infant. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: He remained there until Herod's death, which followed not long after, of a horrible disease described by Josephus. Thus was fulfilled Hosea's word, “Out of Egypt have I called my son,” quoted from the Hebrew: Israel was God's son and first-born called out of Egypt, and now the Only-begotten Son is called out in the same pattern. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: Seeing he was mocked by the wise men — truly mocked by a Higher than they, for he that sits in heaven laughs at the crafty — Herod was exceeding wroth and sent hired murderers to slay all male children in Bethlehem and its environs, two years old and under, by the time he had diligently inquired. He took a large sweep not to miss his mark, but the Babe was gone; Bethlehem was so small that Josephus's silence on the massacre is no argument against it. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: Then was fulfilled what was spoken by Jeremiah the prophet, the quotation differing only verbally from its source. Matthew's fulfilment citations anchor each turn of the story in prophecy his Jewish readers would recognize. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

18 COMMENTARY: In Ramah a voice was heard, Rachel weeping for her children and refusing comfort because they are not. In Jeremiah the words concern the Babylonian captivity; Rachel, buried near Bethlehem, is pictured rising to lament first a bitter captivity and now a bloody death, her babes the ram caught in the thicket while Isaac escapes — until the Babe of Bethlehem himself becomes the Ram caught in another thicket. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

19 COMMENTARY: When Herod was dead — he who thought himself safe from a dreaded Rival, when it was the Child who was safe from him — the angel of the Lord appeared in a dream to Joseph in Egypt. JFB notes the same angel seems employed on all these high occasions, most likely Gabriel. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

20 COMMENTARY: Arise and go into the land of Israel, in its most general sense, the particular province not yet indicated — so Joseph and Mary, like Abraham, went out not knowing whither they went until further direction came. “They are dead which sought the young child's life” echoes the Exodus case of Moses, though only Herod is meant. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

21 COMMENTARY: Joseph arose and came into the land of Israel, plainly intending to return to Bethlehem of Judea to rear the Infant King in his own royal city, until the time should come for him to occupy Jerusalem, the city of the Great King. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

22 COMMENTARY: Hearing Archelaus reigned in Judea in place of his father Herod, Joseph was afraid to go there — Archelaus held Judea, Samaria and Idumea as ethnarch, and after ten years of tyranny was banished to Gaul. Warned in a dream, Joseph turned aside to Galilee under the less cruel Antipas, where secluded Nazareth lay far from the seat of government. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

23 COMMENTARY: He came and dwelt in Nazareth, a small town in Lower Galilee in Zebulun's territory, held in bad esteem — so that Christ would bear the reproach of being called a Nazarene. Henry adds the comfort: wherever Providence allots our habitation, we may glory in being called by his name, sure that if we suffer with him we shall be glorified with him. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
