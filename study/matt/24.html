Study Notes — Matthew

Chapter 24

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: And Jesus went out, and departed from the temple: and his disciples came to him for to shew him the buildings of the temple. Christ foretells the utter ruin and destruction coming upon the temple. A believing foresight of the defacing of all worldly glory will help to keep us from admiring it, and overvaluing it. The most beautiful body soon will be food for worms, and the most magnificent building a ruinous heap. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: And Jesus said unto them, See ye not all these things? verily I say unto you, There shall not be left here one stone upon another, that shall not be thrown down. See ye not all these things? It will do us good so to see them as to see through them, and see to the end of them. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: And as he sat upon the mount of Olives, the disciples came unto him privately, saying, Tell us, when shall these things be? and what shall be the sign of thy coming, and of the end of the world? Our Lord having gone with his disciples to the Mount of Olives, he set before them the order of the times concerning the Jews, till the destruction of Jerusalem; and as to men in general till the end of the world. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: And Jesus answered and said unto them, Take heed that no man deceive you. The disciples had asked concerning the times, When these things should be? Christ gave them no answer to that; but they had also asked, What shall be the sign? This question he answers fully. What Christ here said to his disciples tended more to promote caution than to satisfy their curiosity; more to prepare them for the events that should happen, than to give a distinct idea of the events. This is that good understanding of the times which all should covet, thence to infer what Israel ought to do. Our Saviour cautions his disciples to stand on their guard against false teachers. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: For many shall come in my name, saying, I am Christ; and shall deceive many. False Christs arise first in the catalogue of troubles, because deception, not merely disaster, is the Church's chief danger. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: And ye shall hear of wars and rumours of wars: see that ye be not troubled: for all these things must come to pass, but the end is not yet. He foretells wars and great commotions among nations. From the time that the Jews rejected Christ, and he left their house desolate, the sword never departed from them. See what comes of refusing the gospel. Those who will not hear the messengers of peace, shall be made to hear the messengers of war. But where the heart is fixed, trusting in God, it is kept in peace, and is not afraid. It is against the mind of Christ, that his people should have troubled hearts, even in troublous times. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: For nation shall rise against nation, and kingdom against kingdom: and there shall be famines, and pestilences, and earthquakes, in divers places. The commotions spread from wars of men to convulsions of nature, all in divers places, none of them yet the end. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: All these are the beginning of sorrows. When we look forward to the eternity of misery that is before the obstinate refusers of Christ and his gospel, we may truly say, The greatest earthly judgments are but the beginning of sorrows. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: Then shall they deliver you up to be afflicted, and shall kill you: and ye shall be hated of all nations for my name's sake. Persecution follows prophecy: the disciples themselves will be delivered up, afflicted, killed, and hated — not for their own faults but for his name. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: And then shall many be offended, and shall betray one another, and shall hate one another. Under pressure, many stumble: offence turns to betrayal, and betrayal to hatred among those who once stood together. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: And many false prophets shall rise, and shall deceive many. False teachers multiply in troubled times, when anxious hearts grasp at any voice that promises deliverance. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: And because iniquity shall abound, the love of many shall wax cold. Abounding iniquity chills love: as sin spreads, affection cools, even among those who once burned warm. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: But he that shall endure unto the end, the same shall be saved. It is comforting that some shall endure even to the end; endurance, not enthusiasm, is the mark of salvation. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: And this gospel of the kingdom shall be preached in all the world for a witness unto all nations; and then shall the end come. Our Lord foretells the preaching of the gospel in all the world. The end of the world shall not be till the gospel has done its work. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: When ye therefore shall see the abomination of desolation, spoken of by Daniel the prophet, stand in the holy place, (whoso readeth, let him understand:). Christ foretells the ruin coming upon the people of the Jews; and what he said here would be of use to his disciples, for their conduct and for their comfort. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: Then let them which be in Judaea flee into the mountains. If God opens a door of escape, we ought to make our escape, otherwise we do not trust God, but tempt him. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: Let him which is on the housetop not come down to take any thing out of his house. The flight must be immediate: no going back for goods, no delay for property, when desolation stands in the holy place. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: Neither let him which is in the field return back to take his clothes. The labourer in the field must flee as he stands; the coat left behind is a small loss against life. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: And woe unto them that are with child, and to them that give suck in those days! Those who cannot flee swiftly — mothers with infants — bear the heaviest woe in a flight that allows no delay. — Matthew Henry

20 COMMENTARY: But pray ye that your flight be not in the winter, neither on the sabbath day. It becomes Christ's disciples, in times of public trouble, to be much in prayer: that is never out of season, but in a special manner seasonable when we are distressed on every side. Though we must take what God sends, yet we may pray against sufferings; and it is very trying to a good man, to be taken by any work of necessity from the solemn service and worship of God on the sabbath day. — Matthew Henry

21 COMMENTARY: For then shall be great tribulation, such as was not since the beginning of the world to this time, no, nor ever shall be. The tribulation is without precedent and without successor: the greatest earthly judgments are but the beginning of sorrows, yet this one stands alone in its kind. — Matthew Henry

22 COMMENTARY: And except those days should be shortened, there should no flesh be saved: but for the elect's sake those days shall be shortened. But here is one word of comfort, that for the elect's sake these days shall be made shorter than their enemies designed, who would have cut all off, if God, who used these foes to serve his own purpose, had not set bounds to their wrath. — Matthew Henry

23 COMMENTARY: Then if any man shall say unto you, Lo, here is Christ, or there; believe it not. In the confusion of tribulation, rival claimants will point here and there; the warning is simple: believe it not. — Matthew Henry

24 COMMENTARY: For there shall arise false Christs, and false prophets, and shall shew great signs and wonders; insomuch that, if it were possible, they shall deceive the very elect. Signs and wonders are no sure seal: false Christs will show great ones, so great that, if it were possible, even the elect would be deceived — and it is not possible. — Matthew Henry

25 COMMENTARY: Behold, I have told you before. The forewarning is itself the safeguard: told beforehand, the disciples need not be taken by surprise. — Matthew Henry

26 COMMENTARY: Wherefore if they shall say unto you, Behold, he is in the desert; go not forth: behold, he is in the secret chambers; believe it not. Whether the claimant hides in the desert or in secret chambers, the answer is the same: go not forth, believe it not. — Matthew Henry

27 COMMENTARY: For as the lightning cometh out of the east, and shineth even unto the west; so shall also the coming of the Son of man be. Christ foretells the rapid spreading of the gospel in the world. It is plainly seen as the lightning. Christ preached his gospel openly; his coming will be no secret to be hunted out, but lightning across the whole heaven. — Matthew Henry

28 COMMENTARY: For wheresoever the carcase is, there will the eagles be gathered together. The Romans were like an eagle, and the ensign of their armies was an eagle. When a people, by their sin, make themselves as loathsome carcasses, nothing can be expected but that God should send enemies to destroy them. It is very applicable to the day of judgment, the coming of our Lord Jesus Christ in that day. — Matthew Henry

29 COMMENTARY: Immediately after the tribulation of those days shall the sun be darkened, and the moon shall not give her light, and the stars shall fall from heaven, and the powers of the heaven shall be shaken. Christ foretells his second coming. It is usual for prophets to speak of things as near and just at hand, to express the greatness and certainty of them. Concerning Christ's second coming, it is foretold that there shall be a great change, in order to the making all things new. — Matthew Henry

30 COMMENTARY: And then shall appear the sign of the Son of man in heaven: and then shall all the tribes of the earth mourn, and they shall see the Son of man coming in the clouds of heaven with power and great glory. Then they shall see the Son of man coming in the clouds. At his first coming, he was set for a sign that should be spoken against, but at his second coming, a sign that should be admired. Sooner or later, all sinners will be mourners; but repenting sinners look to Christ, and mourn after a godly sort; and those who sow in those tears shall shortly reap in joy. Impenitent sinners shall see him whom they have pierced, and, though they laugh now, shall mourn and weep in endless horror and despair. — Matthew Henry

31 COMMENTARY: And he shall send his angels with a great sound of a trumpet, and they shall gather together his elect from the four winds, from one end of heaven to the other. The elect of God are scattered abroad; there are some in all places, and all nations; but when that great gathering day comes, there shall not one of them be missing. Distance of place shall keep none out of heaven. — Matthew Henry

32 COMMENTARY: Now learn a parable of the fig tree; When his branch is yet tender, and putteth forth leaves, ye know that summer is nigh. The fig tree's tender branch and leaves are a calendar all can read: summer follows the sign, surely and soon. — Matthew Henry

33 COMMENTARY: So likewise ye, when ye shall see all these things, know that it is near, even at the doors. As leaves mean summer, so these signs mean the coming is near, even at the doors. — Matthew Henry

34 COMMENTARY: Verily I say unto you, This generation shall not pass, till all these things be fulfilled. Our Lord declares that the Jews should never cease to be a distinct people, until all things he had been predicting were fulfilled. His prophecy reaches to the day of final judgment; therefore he here foretells that Judah shall never cease to exist as a distinct people, so long as this world shall endure. — Matthew Henry

35 COMMENTARY: Heaven and earth shall pass away, but my words shall not pass away. Heaven and earth, the most abiding things we know, shall pass; his words shall not. The prophecy's certainty rests on the Speaker, not on the scene. — Matthew Henry

36 COMMENTARY: But of that day and hour knoweth no man, no, not the angels of heaven, but my Father only. After all the signs, the day and hour remain hidden: no man, no angel knows; the Father only. Curiosity about the date is therefore closed to every creature. — Matthew Henry

37 COMMENTARY: But as the days of Noe were, so shall also the coming of the Son of man be. Men of the world scheme and plan for generation upon generation here, but they plan not with reference to the overwhelming, approaching, and most certain event of Christ's second coming, which shall do away every human scheme, and set aside for ever all that God forbids. That will be as surprising a day, as the deluge to the old world. — Matthew Henry

38 COMMENTARY: For as in the days that were before the flood they were eating and drinking, marrying and giving in marriage, until the day that Noe entered into the ark. Apply this, first, to temporal judgments, particularly that which was then hastening upon the nation and people of the Jews. Secondly, to the eternal judgment. Christ here shows the state of the old world when the deluge came. They were secure and careless; they knew not, until the flood came; and they believed not. — Matthew Henry

39 COMMENTARY: And knew not until the flood came, and took them all away; so shall also the coming of the Son of man be. Did we know aright that all earthly things must shortly pass away, we should not set our eyes and hearts so much upon them as we do. The evil day is not the further off for men's putting it far from them. — Matthew Henry

40 COMMENTARY: Then shall two be in the field; the one shall be taken, and the other left. What words can more strongly describe the suddenness of our Saviour's coming! Men will be at their respective businesses, and suddenly the Lord of glory will appear. — Matthew Henry

41 COMMENTARY: Two women shall be grinding at the mill; the one shall be taken, and the other left. Women will be in their house employments, but in that moment every other work will be laid aside, and every heart will turn inward and say, It is the Lord! Am I prepared to meet him? Can I stand before him? And what, in fact, is the day of judgment to the whole world, but the day of death to every one? — Matthew Henry

42 COMMENTARY: Watch therefore: for ye know not what hour your Lord doth come. To watch for Christ's coming is to maintain that temper of mind which we would be willing that our Lord should find us in. We know we have but a little time to live, we cannot know that we have a long time to live; much less do we know the time fixed for the judgment. — Matthew Henry

43 COMMENTARY: But know this, that if the goodman of the house had known in what watch the thief would come, he would have watched, and would not have suffered his house to be broken up. The thief comes at an unknown watch; the householder who knew the watch would watch that watch — and the point is that no man knows it. — Matthew Henry

44 COMMENTARY: Therefore be ye also ready: for in such an hour as ye think not the Son of man cometh. Our Lord's coming will be happy to those that shall be found ready, but very dreadful to those that are not. — Matthew Henry

45 COMMENTARY: Who then is a faithful and wise servant, whom his lord hath made ruler over his household, to give them meat in due season? Watchfulness takes the shape of faithful stewardship: meat in due season, given by the servant set over the household. — Matthew Henry

46 COMMENTARY: Blessed is that servant, whom his lord when he cometh shall find so doing. Blessedness is found in being found doing: the servant at his post when the Lord arrives. — Matthew Henry

47 COMMENTARY: Verily I say unto you, That he shall make him ruler over all his goods. Faithfulness over the household is rewarded with rule over all the goods: small trust kept well becomes great trust. — Matthew Henry

48 COMMENTARY: But and if that evil servant shall say in his heart, My lord delayeth his coming. If a man, professing to be the servant of Christ, be an unbeliever, covetous, ambitious, or a lover of pleasure, he will be cut off. The evil servant's ruin begins in his heart, with the thought that his lord delays. — Matthew Henry

49 COMMENTARY: And shall begin to smite his fellowservants, and to eat and drink with the drunken. Delay in the heart becomes cruelty and carousing in the life: smiting fellow servants, and feasting with the drunken. — Matthew Henry

50 COMMENTARY: The lord of that servant shall come in a day when he looketh not for him, and in an hour that he is not aware of. The lord comes precisely when the evil servant looks not for him: unwatchfulness is surprised by the very coming it discounted. — Matthew Henry

51 COMMENTARY: And shall cut him asunder, and appoint him his portion with the hypocrites: there shall be weeping and gnashing of teeth. Those who choose the world for their portion in this life will have hell for their portion in the other life. May our Lord, when he cometh, pronounce us blessed, and present us to the Father, washed in his blood, purified by his Spirit, and fit to be partakers of the inheritance of the saints in light. — Matthew Henry
