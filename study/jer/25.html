Study Notes — Jeremiah

Chapter 25

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: The word that came to Jeremiah concerning all the people of Judah in the fourth year of Jehoiakim the son of Josiah king of Judah, that was the first year of Nebuchadrezzar king of Babylon. Henry notes the call to turn from evil ways to the worship and service of God, and for sinners to trust in Christ, and partake of his salvation, concerns all men: the word concerning all Judah, in Jehoiakim's fourth year and Nebuchadrezzar's first, concerns all. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

2 COMMENTARY: The which Jeremiah the prophet spoke unto all the people of Judah, and to all the inhabitants of Jerusalem, saying. Henry's word is spoken to all Judah and all Jerusalem's inhabitants: the call to turn concerns all men, and is spoken to all. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

3 COMMENTARY: From the thirteenth year of Josiah the son of Amon king of Judah, even unto this day, that is the three and twentieth year, the word of the Lord has come unto me, and I have spoken unto you, rising early and speaking; but ye have not hearkened. Henry notes God keeps an account how long we possess the means of grace, and the longer we have them, the heavier will our account be if we have not improved them: twenty-three years of rising early and speaking, unhearkened, is the heavy account. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

4 COMMENTARY: And the Lord has sent unto you all his servants the prophets, rising early and sending them; but ye have not hearkened, nor inclined your ear to hear. Henry notes rising early points out the earnest desire that this people should turn and live: all his servants the prophets, rising early and sent, unhearkened, with ears uninclined, show earnestness refused. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

5 COMMENTARY: They said: Turn ye again now every one from his evil way, and from the evil of your doings, and dwell in the land that the Lord has given unto you and to your fathers for ever and ever. Henry notes personal and particular reformation must be insisted on as necessary to a national deliverance, and every one must turn from his own evil way: turn every one from his evil way and evil doings, and dwell in the given land for ever. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

6 COMMENTARY: And go not after other gods to serve them, and to worship them, and provoke me not to anger with the works of your hands; and I will do you no hurt. Henry's turning is particular: go not after other gods to serve and worship them, nor provoke anger with works of hands, and God will do no hurt. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

7 COMMENTARY: Yet ye have not hearkened unto me, says the Lord; that ye might provoke me to anger with the works of your hands to your own hurt. Henry notes yet all was to no purpose: they would not take the right and only method to turn away the wrath of God, and so they provoked anger with works of hands to their own hurt. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

8 COMMENTARY: Therefore thus says the Lord of hosts: Because ye have not heard my words. Henry's therefore begins the sentence: because ye have not heard my words, the captivity's time is fixed, and the prophecy's fixing confirms and comforts. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

9 COMMENTARY: Behold, I will send and take all the families of the north, says the Lord, and Nebuchadrezzar the king of Babylon, my servant, and will bring them against this land, and against the inhabitants thereof, and against all these nations round about, and will utterly destroy them, and make them an astonishment, and an hissing, and perpetual desolations. Henry notes every threatening of the Scripture will certainly be accomplished: the north's families and Nebuchadrezzar brought against land, inhabitants, and round-about nations, to utter destruction, astonishment, hissing, and perpetual desolations. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

10 COMMENTARY: Moreover I will take from them the voice of mirth, and the voice of gladness, the voice of the bridegroom, and the voice of the bride, the sound of the millstones, and the light of the candle. Henry's desolation takes mirth, gladness, bridegroom, bride, millstones, and candle-light: the fixed captivity silences every voice of common life. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

11 COMMENTARY: And this whole land shall be a desolation, and an astonishment; and these nations shall serve the king of Babylon seventy years. Henry notes the fixing of the time during which the Jewish captivity should last would not only confirm the prophecy, but also comfort the people of God, and encourage faith and prayer: seventy years of service to Babylon, whole land desolate and astonished, is the fixed time. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

12 COMMENTARY: And it shall come to pass, when seventy years are accomplished, that I will punish the king of Babylon, and that nation, says the Lord, for their iniquity, and the land of the Chaldeans, and will make it perpetual desolations. Henry notes the ruin of Babylon is foretold: the rod will be thrown into the fire when the correcting work is done, and when the set time to favour Zion is come, Babylon shall be punished for their iniquity, as other nations have been punished for their sins. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

13 COMMENTARY: And I will bring upon that land all my words which I have pronounced against it, even all that is written in this book, which Jeremiah has prophesied against all the nations. Henry's all my words brought upon Babylon, even all written in this book against all nations, is the certainty of every threatening accomplished. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

14 COMMENTARY: For many nations and great kings shall serve themselves of them also: and I will recompense them according to their deeds, and according to the works of their hands. Henry's recompense according to deeds and works of hands falls on Babylon as on others: many nations and great kings shall serve themselves of Babylon, as Babylon served itself of them. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

15 COMMENTARY: For thus says the Lord God of Israel unto me: Take the wine cup of this fury at my hand, and cause all the nations, to whom I send thee, to drink it. Henry notes the evil and the good events of life are often represented in Scripture as cups: under this figure is represented the desolation then coming upon that part of the world, of which Nebuchadnezzar, who had just begun to reign and act, was to be the instrument, but this destroying sword would come from the hand of God, and the wine cup of fury is taken from that hand. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

16 COMMENTARY: And they shall drink, and be moved, and be mad, because of the sword that I will send among them. Henry notes the desolations the sword should make in all these kingdoms are represented by the consequences of excessive drinking: drink, be moved, and be mad, because of the sword sent among them, for drunkenness deprives men of reason, makes men as mad, takes health, and is a sin which is its own punishment. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

17 COMMENTARY: Then took I the cup at the Lord's hand, and made all the nations to drink, unto whom the Lord had sent me. Henry's prophet takes the cup and makes all nations drink: the cup in the vision is to be a sword in the accomplishment, and Jeremiah enforces the cup while Nebuchadnezzar wields the sword. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

18 COMMENTARY: To wit, Jerusalem, and the cities of Judah, and the kings thereof, and the princes thereof, to make them a desolation, an astonishment, an hissing, and a curse; as it is this day. Henry warns if God's judgements begin with backsliding professors, let not the wicked expect to escape: Jerusalem, Judah's cities, kings, and princes drink first, made desolation, astonishment, hissing, and curse. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

19 COMMENTARY: Pharaoh king of Egypt, and his servants, and his princes, and all his people. Henry's cup passes to Egypt: Pharaoh, servants, princes, and all his people drink the desolation's cup after Jerusalem. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

20 COMMENTARY: And all the mingled people, and all the kings of the land of Uz, and all the kings of the land of the Philistines, and Ashkelon, and Azzah, and Ekron, and the remnant of Ashdod. Henry's cup passes to the mingled people, Uz, Philistia, Ashkelon, Azzah, Ekron, and Ashdod's remnant: the desolation fills a nation with confusion, and no kingdom is passed over. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

21 COMMENTARY: Edom, and Moab, and the children of Ammon. Henry's cup passes to Edom, Moab, and Ammon: the neighbouring nations drink the same fury. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

22 COMMENTARY: And all the kings of Tyrus, and all the kings of Zidon, and the kings of the isles which are beyond the sea. Henry's cup passes to Tyre, Zidon, and the isles beyond the sea: the sword's desolation reaches the coastlands. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

23 COMMENTARY: Dedan, and Tema, and Buz, and all that are in the utmost corners. Henry's cup passes to Dedan, Tema, Buz, and the utmost corners: even the farthest dwellers drink. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

24 COMMENTARY: And all the kings of Arabia, and all the kings of the mingled people that dwell in the desert. Henry's cup passes to Arabia's kings and the desert's mingled people: the wilderness nations drink. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

25 COMMENTARY: And all the kings of Zimri, and all the kings of Elam, and all the kings of the Medes. Henry's cup passes to Zimri, Elam, and the Medes: the eastern kingdoms drink. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

26 COMMENTARY: And all the kings of the north, far and near, one with another, and all the kingdoms of the world, which are upon the face of the earth: and the king of Sheshach shall drink after them. Henry's cup passes to all northern kings, far and near, and all the world's kingdoms, with Sheshach drinking last: the desolation is universal, and Babylon itself drinks after them. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

27 COMMENTARY: Therefore thou shalt say unto them: Thus says the Lord of hosts, the God of Israel; Drink ye, and be drunken, and spue, and fall, and rise no more, because of the sword which I will send among you. Henry notes they will refuse to take the cup at thy hand, and will not believe Jeremiah, but he must tell them it is the word of the Lord of hosts, and it is in vain for them to struggle against almighty power: drink, be drunken, spue, fall, and rise no more, because of the sword. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

28 COMMENTARY: And it shall be, if they refuse to take the cup at thine hand to drink, then shalt thou say unto them: Thus says the Lord of hosts; Ye shall certainly drink. Henry's certainly-drink answers refusal: if they refuse the cup, they shall certainly drink, for it is in vain to struggle against almighty power. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

29 COMMENTARY: For, lo, I begin to bring evil on the city which is called by my name, and should ye be utterly unpunished? Ye shall not be unpunished: for I will call for a sword upon all the inhabitants of the earth, says the Lord of hosts. Henry's beginning at the city called by God's name is the warning: if judgements begin with backsliding professors, let not the wicked expect to escape, for a sword is called upon all the earth's inhabitants. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

30 COMMENTARY: Therefore prophesy thou against them all these words, and say unto them: The Lord shall roar from on high, and utter his voice from his holy habitation; he shall mightily roar upon his habitation; he shall give a shout, as they that tread the grapes, against all the inhabitants of the earth. Henry notes the Lord has just ground of controversy with every nation and every person, and he will execute judgement on all the wicked: who can avoid trembling when God speaks in displeasure? The roar from on high, from his holy habitation, mightily upon his habitation, with a grape-treaders' shout against all earth's inhabitants, is that displeasure. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

31 COMMENTARY: A noise shall come even to the ends of the earth; for the Lord has a controversy with the nations, he will plead with all flesh; he will give them that are wicked to the sword, says the Lord. Henry's noise to earth's ends is the controversy with the nations: pleading with all flesh, giving the wicked to the sword. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

32 COMMENTARY: Thus says the Lord of hosts: Behold, evil shall go forth from nation to nation, and a great whirlwind shall be raised up from the coasts of the earth. Henry notes the days are fully come, the time fixed in the divine counsels, which will make the nations wholly desolate: evil from nation to nation, and a great whirlwind from earth's coasts, is the fixed time come. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

33 COMMENTARY: And the slain of the Lord shall be at that day from one end of the earth even unto the other end of the earth: they shall not be lamented, neither gathered, nor buried; they shall be dung upon the ground. Henry's slain from end to end, unlamented, ungathered, unburied, dung upon the ground, are the wholly desolate nations. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

34 COMMENTARY: Howl, ye shepherds, and cry; and wallow yourselves in the ashes, ye principal of the flock: for the days of your slaughter and of your dispersions are accomplished; and ye shall fall like a pleasant vessel. Henry's howling shepherds, crying and wallowing in ashes, principal of the flock, with days of slaughter and dispersions accomplished, falling like a pleasant vessel, are the tender and delicate sharing the common calamity. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

35 COMMENTARY: And the shepherds shall have no way to flee, nor the principal of the flock to escape. Henry's shepherds with no way to flee, and principal of the flock with no escape, are the judgement executed on all the wicked, none avoiding trembling when God speaks in displeasure. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

36 COMMENTARY: A voice of the cry of the shepherds, and an howling of the principal of the flock, shall be heard: for the Lord has spoiled their pasture. Henry's cry and howling, heard because the Lord has spoiled their pasture, are the shepherds' desolation: even those who used to live in peace, and did nothing to provoke, shall not escape. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

37 COMMENTARY: And the peaceable habitations are cut down because of the fierce anger of the Lord. Henry's peaceable habitations cut down by fierce anger are the common calamity's reach: peaceable dwellers share it, for the Lord's controversy is with every nation and person. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

38 COMMENTARY: He has forsaken his covert, as the lion: for their land is desolate because of the fierceness of the oppressor, and because of his fierce anger. Henry closes: blessed be God, there is a peaceable habitation above, for all the sons of peace, and the Lord will preserve his church and all believers in all changes, for nothing can separate them from his love, even when the lion has forsaken his covert and the land is desolate by the oppressor's fierceness and fierce anger. — Matthew Henry (Concise)
