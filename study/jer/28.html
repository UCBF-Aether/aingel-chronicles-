Study Notes — Jeremiah

Chapter 28

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: And it came to pass the same year, in the beginning of the reign of Zedekiah king of Judah, in the fourth year, and in the fifth month, that Hananiah the son of Azur the prophet, which was of Gibeon, spoke unto me in the house of the Lord, in the presence of the priests and of all the people, saying. Henry notes Hananiah spoke a false prophecy: the same year, Zedekiah's beginning, fourth year, fifth month, Hananiah of Gibeon speaks in the Lord's house before priests and all the people, and the false prophecy begins publicly. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

2 COMMENTARY: Thus speaks the Lord of hosts, the God of Israel, saying: I have broken the yoke of the king of Babylon. Henry's false prophecy promises temporal mercies in God's name: I have broken the yoke of Babylon is a promise of earthly blessing without the spiritual mercies God always promises with earthly blessings. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

3 COMMENTARY: Within two full years will I bring again into this place all the vessels of the Lord's house, that Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon took away from this place, and carried them to Babylon. Henry's temporal promise is vessels brought again within two full years: here is not a word of good counsel urging the Jews to repent and return to God, only a dated return of things. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

4 COMMENTARY: And I will bring again to this place Jeconiah the son of Jehoiakim king of Judah, with all the captives of Judah, that went into Babylon, says the Lord: for I will break the yoke of the king of Babylon. Henry's promise of Jeconiah and all captives brought again, for the yoke broken, is peace and prosperity spoken without adding that they must not by wilful sin stop God's favours. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

5 COMMENTARY: Then the prophet Jeremiah said unto the prophet Hananiah in the presence of the priests, and in the presence of all the people that stood in the house of the Lord. Henry notes this was not the first time Jeremiah had prayed for the people, though he prophesied against them: Jeremiah answers Hananiah before priests and people, and his answer is both prayer and appeal. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

6 COMMENTARY: Even the prophet Jeremiah said: Amen: the Lord do so: the Lord perform thy words which thou hast prophesied, to bring again the vessels of the Lord's house, and all that is carried away captive, from Babylon into this place. Henry's Amen is Jeremiah's prayer for the people: the Lord do so, perform thy words, bring again vessels and captives from Babylon into this place, is the true prophet wishing the false word true. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

7 COMMENTARY: Nevertheless hear thou now this word that I speak in thine ears, and in the ears of all the people. Henry's nevertheless is the appeal to the event: hear now this word in thine ears and all the people's ears, for the event shall prove Hananiah's falsehood. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

8 COMMENTARY: The prophets that have been before me and before thee of old prophesied both against many countries, and against great kingdoms, of war, and of evil, and of pestilence. Henry notes those who do not declare the alarming as well as the encouraging parts of God's word, and call men to repentance, and faith, and holiness, tread in the steps of the false prophets: prophets of old prophesied war, evil, and pestilence against many countries and great kingdoms, and Jeremiah stands in that line. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

9 COMMENTARY: The prophet which prophesies of peace, when the word of the prophet shall come to pass, then shall the prophet be known, that the Lord has truly sent him. Henry's test is the event: the prophet who speaks only of peace and prosperity, without adding that they must not by wilful sin stop God's favours, will be proved a false prophet when the word comes not to pass, and known as truly sent only when it does. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

10 COMMENTARY: Then Hananiah the prophet took the yoke from off the prophet Jeremiah's neck, and broke it. Henry notes Hananiah is sentenced to die, and Jeremiah, when he has received direction from God, boldly tells him so, but not before he received that commission: Hananiah takes the yoke from Jeremiah's neck and breaks it, the false sign before the true sentence. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

11 COMMENTARY: And Hananiah spoke in the presence of all the people, saying: Thus says the Lord; Even so will I break the yoke of Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon from the neck of all nations within the space of two full years. And the prophet Jeremiah went his way. Henry notes those have much to answer for, who tell sinners that they shall have peace, though they harden their hearts in contempt of God's word: Hananiah breaks the yoke and promises all nations freed within two full years, and Jeremiah goes his way, giving up even his right and leaving the Lord to plead his cause. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

12 COMMENTARY: Then the word of the Lord came unto Jeremiah the prophet, after that Hananiah the prophet had broken the yoke from off the neck of the prophet Jeremiah, saying. Henry's commission comes after the broken yoke: the word of the Lord came to Jeremiah only then, and Jeremiah boldly tells Hananiah so, but not before he received that commission. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

13 COMMENTARY: Go and tell Hananiah, saying: Thus says the Lord; Thou hast broken the yokes of wood; but thou shalt make for them yokes of iron. Henry notes every attempt of ungodly men to make vain the purposes of God will add to their miseries: broken yokes of wood become yokes of iron, and the false sign worsens the servitude it promised to end. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

14 COMMENTARY: For thus says the Lord of hosts, the God of Israel; I have put a yoke of iron upon the neck of all these nations, that they may serve Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon; and they shall serve him: and I have given him the beasts of the field also. Henry's iron yoke is upon all nations, that they serve Nebuchadnezzar, beasts of the field given him also: the attempt to make vain God's purposes adds misery, not freedom. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

15 COMMENTARY: Then said the prophet Jeremiah unto Hananiah the prophet: Hear now, Hananiah; The Lord has not sent thee; but thou makest this people to trust in a lie. Henry's bold telling is hear now, Hananiah, the Lord has not sent thee, but thou makest this people trust in a lie: the servant of God must be gentle to all men, yet bold when commissioned, and the lie-trust is the false prophet's charge. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

16 COMMENTARY: Therefore thus says the Lord; Behold, I will cast thee from off the face of the earth: this year thou shalt die, because thou hast taught rebellion against the Lord. Henry's sentence is this year thou shalt die, because thou hast taught rebellion against the Lord: cast from the face of the earth for teaching rebellion, the deceiver's doom is read. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

17 COMMENTARY: So Hananiah the prophet died the same year in the seventh month. Henry's sentence fulfilled is Hananiah died the same year in the seventh month: the gospel of Christ encourages men to do works meet for repentance, but gives no encouragement to continue in sin, and the false prophet's death proves the false word. — Matthew Henry (Concise)
