Study Notes — Jeremiah

Chapter 51

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Henry compares Babylon's enemies to a destroying wind, brought out of God's treasuries, raised against those dwelling in the midst of the Chaldeans and incorporated with them. The Chaldeans rise up against God by falling down before idols, he notes, and God will raise up destroyers who will be too hard for those who contend with him. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

2 COMMENTARY: Henry compares the invaders to fanners who shall drive Babylon away as chaff is driven by the fan and empty her land. The Chaldeans had themselves been fanners to winnow God's people and empty them, he observes, and now they shall in like manner be despoiled and dispersed. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

3 COMMENTARY: Henry reads this as the ample commission to destroy: let the archers bend their bows against Babylon's archers, spare not her young men, and utterly destroy all her host. This may animate God's instruments, he says, because what he has devised shall surely prosper, for he himself will do it. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

4 COMMENTARY: Henry counts this among the marks of a universal destruction: the slain shall fall in great abundance throughout the land of the Chaldeans, with multitudes thrust through in her streets. God will make thorough work of it, performing what he has purposed and perfecting what he has begun. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

5 COMMENTARY: Henry stresses that though Israel's land was filled with sin against the Holy One of Israel, and God justly delivered them to their enemies, Israel is not forsaken and not cast off, though cast out. It does not follow, he notes, that a people who have broken God's laws are thrown out of covenant, for God's care and love will flourish again. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

6 COMMENTARY: Henry calls flight out of Babylon wisdom when ruin approaches: quit the city and retire into a remote corner, that you may save your lives and not be cut off in her iniquity. There is a fixed time for vengeance, he adds, and though it cannot be expected sooner than that time, then it will come, for the avenging of Israel is the vengeance of the LORD. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

7 COMMENTARY: Henry acknowledges Babylon's great pomp: a golden cup in the LORD's hand, a rich and glorious empire, a golden city and a head of gold, filled with all good things as a cup with wine. He had made the earth drunk with this cup, he explains, some intoxicated with her pleasures and debauched, others intoxicated with her terrors and destroyed. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

8 COMMENTARY: Henry calls Babylon's fall a certain and surprising destruction: she is as sure to fall as if fallen already, though when Jeremiah prophesied she was in the height of her power. It came suddenly, when they did not think of it, and was perfected in a little time, yet taking balm for her pain is vain, for she who would not be healed by God's word shall not be healed by his providence. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

9 COMMENTARY: Henry records two readings of the complaint: God's captive people endeavoured, as instructed, to convince the Babylonians of the folly of idolatry but could not, since they still doted on graven images, and so resolved to quit them; or hired auxiliaries declared they had done their best to save her to no purpose. Either way, her judgment reaches unto heaven, and it is vain to withstand or avert it. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

10 COMMENTARY: Henry says Zion's children shall triumph in particular: the LORD has brought forth their righteousness, appearing on their behalf against unjust dealers and giving redress, and showing himself reconciled to them as a righteous nation. Let it therefore be declared in Zion to his praise, he says, that others may join in praising him. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

11 COMMENTARY: Henry says God has raised up the spirit of the kings of the Medes, Darius and Cyrus, who come against Babylon by a divine instinct, for God's device is against Babylon to destroy it, and this is the vengeance of his temple. The call to make bright the arrows and gather the shields is ironic, he notes: in a long time of peace and security the arrows have grown rusty and the shields scattered. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

12 COMMENTARY: Henry reads the call to set up the standard, strengthen the watch and prepare ambushes as upbraiding irony: Babylon would be found very secure and remiss, needing to be thus quickened, and was in the midst of her revels when taken. Yet the methods God has devised shall prosper, for the LORD has both devised and done what he spoke against Babylon. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

13 COMMENTARY: Henry says Babylon seems well fenced, dwelling upon many waters that embarrass an enemy's march, and abundant in treasures, yet neither waters nor wealth shall secure her. Her end is come as the measure of her covetousness, he explains: it sets bounds to ambition and avarice that otherwise would be boundless, as God says to proud waves, Hitherto shall you come, and no further. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

14 COMMENTARY: Henry says it is the LORD of hosts who has sworn it, swearing by himself since he could swear by no greater, that he will fill Babylon with vast and incredible numbers of the enemy's forces, as with caterpillars. They need only lift up a shout against it, he adds, for that shout shall dispirit all the inhabitants and make them an easy prey. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

15 COMMENTARY: Henry says no one need doubt that he who determined to destroy Babylon can make his words good, for he is the God who made the world by his power. Nothing is too hard for him, he notes, and it is in his name that our help stands and on him our hope is built. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

16 COMMENTARY: Henry says God's providence is a continued creation, with wind and rain at his disposal: at his voice there is a multitude of waters in the heavens, fed by vapours ascending from the earth, and lightnings and rain, seeming contraries like fire and water, are produced together, while the wind is brought out of his treasuries. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

17 COMMENTARY: Henry says the idols opposing God's word are a mere sham and their worshippers brutish people: the molten image is falsehood, with no breath in it, and those who make such images are like unto them. When idols come to be visited, examined and enquired into, their reputation sinks and they appear to be nothing. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

18 COMMENTARY: Henry calls the idols vanity, the work of errors, which in the time of their visitation shall perish. Their reputation sinks on examination, he says, and they are shown to be nothing at all. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

19 COMMENTARY: Henry insists there is no comparison between the God of Israel and the gods of the heathen: the portion of Jacob is the former of all things and the LORD of hosts. There is a near relation between him and his people, he notes, for he is their portion and they are his, the lot of his inheritance, so he will do what is best for them. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

20 COMMENTARY: Henry says Babylon had been God's battle-axe and his weapons of war, tools in his hand with which he broke in pieces nations and kingdoms. Such havoc the Chaldeans made as instruments of his wrath, he observes, yet now Babylon itself must fall, for the rod will itself be thrown into the fire at last. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

21 COMMENTARY: Henry lists the instruments of kingdoms that Babylon broke: horses and chariots, which are so much the strength of kingdoms. With Babylon as his battle-axe, God broke these in pieces. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

22 COMMENTARY: Henry continues the catalogue: with Babylon God broke in pieces man and woman, old and young, the young man and the maid, with which kingdoms are replenished. None of these orders of society escaped the havoc of God's instrument. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

23 COMMENTARY: Henry completes the catalogue: the shepherd and his flock, the husbandman and his yoke of oxen, and captains and rulers, with which kingdoms are maintained and supplied. Yet, he stresses, being instrumental in executing God's judgments on others will not exempt Babylon from God's judgments. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

24 COMMENTARY: Henry says the LORD God of recompences, to whom vengeance belongs, will surely requite Babylon, rendering unto her all the evil she has done in Zion, and returning it in the sight of his people. They shall have the satisfaction, he notes, of seeing their cause pleaded with jealousy and plainly seeing the punishment as the recompense for wrong done to Zion. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

25 COMMENTARY: Henry calls Babylon a destroying mountain, very lofty and bulky, destroying all the earth as stones tumbled from high mountains spoil the grounds about them. God declares he is against her, will stretch out his hand, roll her down from her rocks, and leave her a burnt mountain, a heap of ashes, for those cannot stand long whom God is against. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

26 COMMENTARY: Henry says the finality of Babylon's ruin appears in this: no use shall be made even of her ruins, so execrable shall they be, for none shall take from her a stone for a corner or for foundations. This denotes, he adds, that nothing shall be left on which to ground any hopes of raising her into a kingdom again, for she shall be desolate forever. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

27 COMMENTARY: Henry reads the call to set up a standard and blow the trumpet as enlisting soldiers and animating them for Cyrus's expedition, with the nations preparing recruits and the kingdoms of Ararat, Minni and Ashkenaz sending in their quota of men. General officers are to be appointed and the cavalry to advance in great numbers, like caterpillars laying the country waste. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

28 COMMENTARY: Henry says the kings and captains must prepare nations against Babylon, for the service is great and there is occasion for many hands. The kings of the Medes, their captains, all their rulers and all the land of his dominion are called together for this work. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

29 COMMENTARY: Henry says the whole land shall tremble and sorrow in universal consternation, seeing both the irresistible arm and the irreversible counsel and decree of God against Babylon. Every purpose of the LORD shall be performed, making the land a desolation without an inhabitant, for it is his purpose, which shall be performed. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

30 COMMENTARY: Henry says God has taken away the Babylonians' strength and spirit, so that their mighty men forbear to fight and remain in their holds, not daring so much as to peep forth. Their might fails, they become as timorous as women, and the enemy without resistance burns their dwelling-places and breaks their bars. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

31 COMMENTARY: Henry explains the running posts: the king of Babylon, at a distance from where the attack was made, had notice only late, so couriers from several quarters met at court, like Job's messengers one upon the heels of another, to tell him his city was taken at one end, with nothing to obstruct the conquerors from quickly reaching the other. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

32 COMMENTARY: Henry says the messengers must tell the king that the enemy has seized the passes, the forts upon the river, and, having got over the river, has burned the reeds on the river side to alarm and terrify the city. The men of war are affrighted and throw down their arms, surrendering at discretion. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

33 COMMENTARY: Henry compares Babylon to a threshing-floor: God's people were long threshed there as sheaves in the floor, but now the time has come for her herself to be threshed, with her princes, great men and all her inhabitants beaten in their own land. By sin she is made meet to be a seat of war, and her people, like corn in harvest, are ripe for destruction. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

34 COMMENTARY: Henry records Zion's complaint against Nebuchadnezzar: he devoured her, crushed her, emptied her of all that was valuable, swallowed her up as a dragon or whale swallows little fish by shoals, filled his belly and his treasures with her delicates, and cast her out as a vessel in which there is no pleasure. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

35 COMMENTARY: Henry says Zion and Jerusalem shall appeal to God that the violence done to them and their flesh, and the blood of their people shed like water, be upon Babylon and the inhabitants of Chaldea, with the guilt required at their hands. Ruin, he notes, is not far off from those who lie under the guilt of wrong done to God's people. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

36 COMMENTARY: Henry says the righteous Judge answers the appeal: I will plead thy cause and take vengeance for thee, and every drop of Jerusalem's blood shall be accounted for with interest. Drying up her sea and springs points to the waters that secured and enriched Babylon being taken away, as Cyrus turned the Euphrates aside. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

37 COMMENTARY: Henry says Babylon shall become heaps, a dwelling-place for dragons, an astonishment and a hissing, without an inhabitant. Jerome, he notes, said that in his time, though the ruins of Babylon's walls were to be seen, the ground enclosed by them was a forest of wild beasts. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

38 COMMENTARY: Henry says the Babylonians in their revels shall roar together like lions and yell as lions' whelps, drinking confusion to Cyrus and his army with loud huzzas. They call it singing, he remarks, but in scripture language and in the language of sober men it is called yelling. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

39 COMMENTARY: Henry says that in their heat, when their heads are hot with hard drinking, God will make their feasts and give them the cup of the LORD's right hand, a cup of fury that makes them drunk to revel and sleep a perpetual sleep, never to wake. On that very night, in the midst of the jollity, Belshazzar was slain. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

40 COMMENTARY: Henry says the Babylonians shall be brought down like lambs to the slaughter, like rams with he-goats, in such great numbers and so easily that the enemies make no more of killing them than the butcher does of killing lambs. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

41 COMMENTARY: Henry records the lament over Sheshach: how is the praise of the whole earth surprised, and how is Babylon become an astonishment among the nations! That which was universally cried up, he observes, may fall into general contempt. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

42 COMMENTARY: Henry compares the invading strength to an irruption and inundation of waters: the sea has come up upon Babylon, and once it has broken through its bounds there is no fence against it, so she is covered with the multitude of its waves and overpowered by a numerous army. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

43 COMMENTARY: Henry says Babylon's cities shall become a desolation, a dry land and a wilderness, an uninhabited and uncultivated desert, wherein no man dwells and no son of man passes by. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

44 COMMENTARY: Henry says Bel, the principal idol the Babylonians worshipped, is by name marked for punishment: that great devourer, to whom abundance of sacrifices and rich spoils were dedicated, shall disgorge what he greedily regaled himself with, and God will bring forth out of his temple all the wealth laid up there. His altars shall be forsaken and none shall regard him any more, so the idol thought to be a wall to Babylon shall fall and fail them. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

45 COMMENTARY: Henry repeats the call to God's people to go out of the midst of Babylon and deliver every man his soul from the fierce anger of the LORD. It is their wisdom, he says, to get out of the midst of Babylon lest they be involved, if not in her ruins, yet in her fears. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

46 COMMENTARY: Henry explains the rumours as a gradual destruction foreseen in stages: a rumour shall come one year that Cyrus is making vast preparations for war, and after that, in another year, a rumour that his design is upon Babylon. The people are warned lest their hearts faint at these alarms, for they might have sought conditions of peace but were too proud and secure, and their hearts were hardened to their destruction. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

47 COMMENTARY: Henry says God will do judgment upon Babylon's graven images, in token that the whole land shall be confounded and all her slain shall fall, with the wounded groaning throughout the country. Though the invaders are themselves idolaters, he notes, they shall destroy the images and temples of Babylon's gods, as an earnest of the abolishing of all counterfeit deities. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

48 COMMENTARY: Henry says some shall rejoice in Babylon's fall, not as it is the misery of fellow-creatures, but as the manifestation of God's righteous judgment and as opening the way for the release of his captives. On these accounts, he says, heaven and earth and all in both shall sing for Babylon, for Babylon's ruin is Zion's praise. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

49 COMMENTARY: Henry says Cyrus shall measure to the Chaldeans the same that they measured to the Jews, so that every observer may discern God recompensing them for what they did against his people. Just as Babylon caused the slain of Israel to fall, slaying all without distinction, so at Babylon shall fall the slain of all the earth. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

50 COMMENTARY: Henry says it is not only wisdom to quit the city when ruin approaches, but duty to quit the country too when ruin is accomplished and the captives are set at liberty by the pulling down of the prison over their heads. Those who escaped the sword must go away and stand not still, hastening to their own country, however comfortably seated in Babylon, for this is not their rest, but Canaan is, and wherever they are they must remember the LORD and let Jerusalem come into their minds. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

51 COMMENTARY: Henry takes notice of the discouragement of the returning captives: reminded of Jerusalem, they cry that they are confounded, shame covers their faces, for strangers have come into the sanctuaries of the LORD's house. They cannot bear the thought of the sanctuary profaned and ruined by strangers. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

52 COMMENTARY: Henry answers that discouragement with God's triumph over Babylon's gods: God will do judgment upon her graven images, and through all her land the wounded shall groan, so that the reproach of Jerusalem will be forever rolled away. The believing prospect of Jerusalem's recovery, he notes, will keep us from being ashamed of Jerusalem's ruins. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

53 COMMENTARY: Henry says Babylon, thinking herself very safe and very great, will be deceived: though she should mount up to heaven and fortify the height of her strength, yet God will send spoilers against her who shall break through her strength and bring down her height. All her height and fortification will not do. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

54 COMMENTARY: Henry says there is a sound of a cry coming from Babylon, a great outcry lamenting the great destruction from the land of the Chaldeans. This is the voice of mourning because the LORD has destroyed the great voice of mirth that used to be heard in Babylon. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

55 COMMENTARY: Henry says the LORD has spoiled Babylon and destroyed out of her the great voice, the voice of the multitude and of mirth, so that when her waves roar like great waters, the noise of their voice is uttered only in lamentation. The cry from Babylon replaces the city's former great voice. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

56 COMMENTARY: Henry says when the spoiler comes upon Babylon her mighty men are immediately taken, their weapons fail them, and every one of their bows is broken and stands them in no stead. Their politics fail them too, he adds, for their princes and captains in council are made drunk, but the ground of it all is that the LORD God of recompences will surely requite. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

57 COMMENTARY: Henry says Babylon's princes, wise men, captains, rulers and mighty men shall be made drunk and sleep a perpetual sleep, never waking. Their counsellors, he explains, are as men intoxicated through stupidity or despair, forming no right notions, staggering in their counsels and falling out among themselves, until the wine of God's wrath lays them into a fatal lethargy. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

58 COMMENTARY: Henry says Babylon's broad walls shall be utterly broken and her high gates burned with fire, and the people labouring in the defence shall labour in vain in the fire. The walls, he notes, were some 200 cubits high and 50 broad, with stones laid in pitch scarcely separable, yet they shall be utterly broken and the defenders shall tire themselves to no good. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

59 COMMENTARY: Henry says this prophecy was sent to Babylon by the hand of Seraiah, who went with or on behalf of Zedekiah into Babylon in the fourth year of his reign. Seraiah was a quiet prince, a prince of rest, Henry observes: in honour and power, yet calm of temper, studying peace, keeping his master from rebelling, and no persecutor of God's prophets, so Jeremiah might safely entrust him with the errand. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

60 COMMENTARY: Henry says a copy of this prophecy was taken, it seems by Jeremiah himself, since Baruch his scribe is not mentioned here, and Jeremiah wrote in a book all the evil that should come upon Babylon. It is of great advantage, he notes, both to propagating and to perpetuating the word of God to have it written and copies taken. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

61 COMMENTARY: Henry says Seraiah is desired to read the words when he comes to Babylon and sees what a magnificent, large, strong, rich and well-fortified place it is, and is tempted to think it will stand forever. Then he shall read all these words to himself and his particular friends, that with an eye of faith they may see to the end of these threatening powers and comfort themselves in captivity. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

62 COMMENTARY: Henry says Seraiah must make a solemn protestation of the divine authority and unquestionable certainty of what he has read: O LORD, thou hast spoken against this place, to cut it off, so that none shall remain in it, neither man nor beast, but it shall be desolate forever. Though he sees Babylon flourishing, he must foresee it falling, believing that God who knows everything can do everything, and therefore neither envy its pomp nor fear its power. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

63 COMMENTARY: Henry says Seraiah must then tie a stone to the book and cast it into the midst of the Euphrates, as a confirming sign of the things contained in it. In the sign it was the stone that sunk the book, which otherwise would have swum, he notes, but in the thing signified it was rather the book that sunk the stone: the divine sentence passed upon Babylon sunk that city, which seemed as firm as a stone. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

64 COMMENTARY: Henry says Seraiah must declare, Thus shall Babylon sink and shall not rise from the evil God will bring upon her, and they shall be weary, succumbing under a load they shall never shake off. Those who sink under the weight of God's wrath and curse, he notes, sink irrecoverably, and the chapter's last words seal the vision: thus far are the words of Jeremiah, recorded last because this prophecy was to be last accomplished among his prophecies against the Gentiles. — Matthew Henry (Complete)
