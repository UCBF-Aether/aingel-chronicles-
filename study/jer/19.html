Study Notes — Jeremiah

Chapter 19

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Thus says the Lord: Go and get a potter's earthen bottle, and take of the ancients of the people, and of the ancients of the priests. Henry notes the prophet must give notice of ruin coming upon Judah and Jerusalem, and both rulers and ruled must attend to it: the bottle is got, and ancients of people and priests are taken as witnesses. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: And go forth unto the valley of the son of Hinnom, which is by the entry of the east gate, and proclaim there the words that I shall tell thee. Henry's notice is proclaimed in the valley of Hinnom by the east gate: the place of the sin becomes the place of the sentence. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: And say: Hear ye the word of the Lord, O kings of Judah, and inhabitants of Jerusalem; Thus says the Lord of hosts, the God of Israel: Behold, I will bring evil upon this place, the which whosoever hears, his ears shall tingle. Henry notes that place which holiness made the joy of the whole earth, sin made the reproach and shame of the whole earth: kings and inhabitants must hear an evil that makes ears tingle. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: Because they have forsaken me, and have estranged this place, and have burned incense in it unto other gods, whom neither they nor their fathers have known, nor the kings of Judah, and have filled this place with the blood of innocents. Henry's cause is forsaking, estranging, incense to unknown gods, and innocent blood filling the place: the ruin's reason is set out before the ruin. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: They have built also the high places of Baal, to burn their sons with fire for burnt offerings unto Baal, which I commanded not, nor spoke it, neither came it into my mind. Henry's high places of Baal, with sons burned for offerings God never commanded, never spoke, never thought, are the sin that made the holy place a reproach. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: Therefore, behold, the days come, says the Lord, that this place shall no more be called Tophet, nor The valley of the son of Hinnom, but The valley of slaughter. Henry's renaming is the sentence: Tophet, the valley of Hinnom, becomes the valley of slaughter, for the place of child-sacrifice becomes the place of slaughter. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: And I will make void the counsel of Judah and Jerusalem in this place; and I will cause them to fall by the sword before their enemies, and by the hands of them that seek their lives: and their carcases will I give to be meat for the fowls of the heaven, and for the beasts of the earth. Henry's voided counsel and sword-fall, with carcases meat for fowls and beasts, is the ruin of which the prophet must give notice: there is no fleeing from God's justice, but by fleeing to his mercy. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: And I will make this city desolate, and an hissing; every one that passes thereby shall be astonished and hiss because of all the plagues thereof. Henry's desolate city, an hissing, with every passer-by astonished and hissing at its plagues, is Jerusalem the joy of the earth become its reproach and shame. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: And I will cause them to eat the flesh of their sons and the flesh of their daughters, and they shall eat every one the flesh of his friend in the siege and straitness, wherewith their enemies, and they that seek their lives, shall straiten them. Henry's siege straitness, eating sons, daughters, and friends' flesh, is the ruin's extremity: no fleeing from justice except by fleeing to mercy, and mercy was refused. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: Then shalt thou break the bottle in the sight of the men that go with thee. Henry notes the potter's vessel, after it is hardened, can never be pieced again when it is broken: the bottle broken before the witnesses' eyes is Judah broken beyond human piecing. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: And shalt say unto them: Thus says the Lord of hosts: Even so will I break this people and this city, as one breaks a potter's vessel, that cannot be made whole again: and they shall bury them in Tophet, till there be no place to bury. Henry notes as the bottle was broken, so shall Judah and Jerusalem be broken by the Chaldeans: no human hand can repair it, but if they return to the Lord he will heal, and Tophet shall be filled with burials till no place remains. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: Thus will I do unto this place, says the Lord, and to the inhabitants thereof, and even make this city as Tophet. Henry notes as they filled Tophet with the slain sacrificed to their idols, so will God fill the whole city with the slain that shall fall as sacrifices to his justice: the city itself becomes Tophet. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: And the houses of Jerusalem, and the houses of the kings of Judah, shall be defiled as the place of Tophet, because of all the houses upon whose roofs they have burned incense unto all the host of heaven, and have poured out drink offerings unto other gods. Henry notes whatever men may think, God will appear as terrible against sin and sinners as the Scriptures state, nor shall the unbelief of men make his promise or his threatenings of no effect: houses defiled as Tophet, for rooftop incense to heaven's host and drink offerings to other gods. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: Then came Jeremiah from Tophet, whither the Lord had sent him to prophesy; and he stood in the court of the Lord's house, and said to all the people. Henry's prophet ratifies in the temple court what he signed in the valley: from Tophet to the Lord's house, the same word is spoken to all the people. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: Thus says the Lord of hosts, the God of Israel: Behold, I will bring upon this city and upon all her towns all the evil that I have pronounced against it, because they have hardened their necks, that they might not hear my words. Henry closes that the obstinacy of sinners in sinful ways is their own fault: if they are deaf to the word of God, it is because they have stopped their ears, and hardened necks bring all the pronounced evil upon city and towns. We have need to pray that God, by his grace, would deliver us from hardness of heart, and contempt of his word and commandments. — Matthew Henry
