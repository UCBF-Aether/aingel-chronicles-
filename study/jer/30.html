Study Notes — Jeremiah

Chapter 30

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: The word that came to Jeremiah from the Lord, saying. Henry notes Jeremiah is to write what God had spoken to him: the word that came to Jeremiah is to be written, and the very words are such as the Holy Ghost teaches. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

2 COMMENTARY: Thus speaks the Lord God of Israel, saying: Write thee all the words that I have spoken unto thee in a book. Henry's write thee all the words in a book is the order: these are the words God ordered to be written, and promises written by his order are truly his word. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

3 COMMENTARY: For, lo, the days come, says the Lord, that I will bring again the captivity of my people Israel and Judah, says the Lord: and I will cause them to return to the land that I gave to their fathers, and they shall possess it. Henry notes he must write a description of the trouble the people were now in, and were likely to be in, but a happy end should be put to these calamities: though the afflictions of the church may last long, they shall not last always, and the days come when Israel and Judah's captivity is brought again, returned to the fathers' land, and possessed. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

4 COMMENTARY: And these are the words that the Lord spoke concerning Israel and concerning Judah. Henry's these are the words concerning Israel and Judah are the written promise: the deliverance of the Jews from Babylon is pointed out in the prophecy, but the restoration and happy state of Israel and Judah, when converted to Christ their King, are foretold. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

5 COMMENTARY: For thus says the Lord; We have heard a voice of trembling, of fear, and not of peace. Henry's voice of trembling, fear, and not peace is the trouble described: the people were now in trouble and likely to be in more, and the written words begin with that trembling voice. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

6 COMMENTARY: Ask ye now, and see whether a man does travail with child? Wherefore do I see every man with his hands on his loins, as a woman in travail, and all faces are turned into paleness? Henry's every man with hands on loins, as a woman in travail, all faces paleness, is the trouble's picture: also the miseries of the nations before the coming of Christ are foretold in this travail. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

7 COMMENTARY: Alas! for that day is great, so that none is like it: it is even the time of Jacob's trouble; but he shall be saved out of it. Henry's alas, that day great, none like it, Jacob's trouble, but he shall be saved out of it, is the happy end put to calamity: afflictions may last long, but shall not last always. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

8 COMMENTARY: For it shall come to pass in that day, says the Lord of hosts, that I will break his yoke from off thy neck, and will burst thy bonds, and strangers shall no more serve themselves of him. Henry notes our gracious Lord pardons the sins of the believer, and breaks off the yoke of sin and Satan, that he may serve God without fear, in righteousness and true holiness before him all the remainder of his days, as the redeemed subject of Christ our King: in that day the yoke is broken from the neck, bonds burst, and strangers serve themselves of him no more. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

9 COMMENTARY: But they shall serve the Lord their God, and David their king, whom I will raise up unto them. Henry notes they shall obey, or hearken to the Messiah, the Christ, the Son of David, their King: they shall serve the Lord their God, and David their king raised up unto them, and all men must honour the Son as they honour the Father, and come into the service and worship of God by him. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

10 COMMENTARY: Therefore fear thou not, O my servant Jacob, says the Lord; neither be dismayed, O Israel: for, lo, I will save thee from afar, and thy seed from the land of their captivity; and Jacob shall return, and shall be in rest, and be quiet, and none shall make him afraid. Henry's fear not, Jacob, neither be dismayed, Israel, for lo, I will save thee from afar and thy seed from captivity, and Jacob shall return in rest and quiet, none making afraid, is the Jews restored again. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

11 COMMENTARY: For I am with thee, says the Lord, to save thee: though I make a full end of all nations whither I have scattered thee, yet I will not make a full end of thee: but I will correct thee in measure, and will not leave thee altogether unpunished. Henry's I am with thee to save thee, full end of all nations whither scattered, yet not a full end of thee, but correction in measure, is the happy end: the church's afflictions last long, but not always, and correction is measured, not endless. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

12 COMMENTARY: For thus says the Lord, Thy bruise is incurable, and thy wound is grievous. Henry asks when God is against a people, who will be for them? Who can be for them, so as to do them any kindness? Thy bruise incurable, thy wound grievous, is the case against which no helper stands. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

13 COMMENTARY: There is none to plead thy cause, that thou mayest be bound up: thou hast no healing medicines. Henry notes incurable griefs are owing to incurable lusts: none to plead thy cause that thou mayest be bound up, no healing medicines, is the grief no human pleading heals. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

14 COMMENTARY: All thy lovers have forgotten thee; they seek thee not; for I have wounded thee with the wound of an enemy, with the chastisement of a cruel one, for the multitude of thine iniquity; because thy sins were increased. Henry's all lovers forgotten, seeking not, for I wounded thee with an enemy's wound, cruel chastisement, for multitude of iniquity, sins increased, is the just wound: though the captives suffered justly, and could not help themselves, the Lord intended to appear for them. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

15 COMMENTARY: Why criest thou for thine affliction? Thy sorrow is incurable for the multitude of thine iniquity: because thy sins were increased, I have done these things unto thee. Henry's why criest thou for affliction, sorrow incurable for multitude of iniquity, sins increased, I have done these things, is the just cause stated: every effort to heal ourselves must prove fruitless, so long as we neglect the heavenly Advocate and sanctifying Spirit. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

16 COMMENTARY: Therefore all they that devour thee shall be devoured; and all thine adversaries, every one of them, shall go into captivity; and they that spoil thee shall be a spoil, and all that prey upon thee will I give for a prey. Henry notes the Lord intended to appear for them, and to punish their oppressors, and he will still do so: devourers devoured, adversaries into captivity, spoilers a spoil, preyers given for a prey, is the oppressors' punishment. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

17 COMMENTARY: For I will restore health unto thee, and I will heal thee of thy wounds, says the Lord; because they called thee an outcast, saying, This is Zion, whom no man seeks after. Henry notes the dealings of his grace with every true convert, and every returning backslider, are the same in effect as his proceedings to the Jews: I will restore health and heal wounds, because they called thee outcast, Zion whom no man seeks after, is grace healing the incurable. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

18 COMMENTARY: Thus says the Lord; Behold, I will bring again the captivity of Jacob's tents, and have mercy on his dwellingplaces; and the city shall be built upon her own heap, and the palace shall remain after the manner thereof. Henry notes we have here further intimations of the favour of God for them after the days of their calamity have expired: Jacob's tents' captivity brought again, mercy on dwellingplaces, city built upon her own heap, palace remaining after its manner, is favour after calamity. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

19 COMMENTARY: And out of them shall proceed thanksgiving and the voice of them that make merry: and I will multiply them, and they shall not be few; I will also glorify them, and they shall not be small. Henry's out of them thanksgiving and merry voices, multiplied not few, glorified not small, is the favour's fruit: after calamity, praise proceeds. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

20 COMMENTARY: Their children also shall be as aforetime, and their congregation shall be established before me, and I will punish all that oppress them. Henry's children as aforetime, congregation established before me, oppressors punished, is the settlement: the proper work and office of Christ, as Mediator, is to draw near unto God for us, as the High Priest of our profession, and his undertaking establishes the congregation. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

21 COMMENTARY: And their nobles shall be of themselves, and their governor shall proceed from the midst of them; and I will cause him to draw near, and he shall approach unto me: for who is this that engaged his heart to approach unto me? says the Lord. Henry notes his own undertaking, in compliance with his Father's will, and in compassion to fallen man, engaged him: Jesus Christ was, in all this, truly wonderful, and the governor from their midst, caused to draw near and approach, is the Mediator drawing near. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

22 COMMENTARY: And ye shall be my people, and I will be your God. Henry notes they shall be taken again into covenant with the Lord, according to the covenant made with their fathers: I will be your God is his good-will to us, which is the summary of that part of the covenant, and ye shall be my people is the covenant renewed. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

23 COMMENTARY: Behold, the whirlwind of the Lord goes forth with fury, a continuing whirlwind: it shall fall with pain upon the head of the wicked. Henry notes the wrath of God against the wicked is very terrible, like a whirlwind: the whirlwind goes forth with fury, continuing, falling with pain upon the wicked's head, is wrath's terror. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

24 COMMENTARY: The fierce anger of the Lord shall not return, until he has done it, and until he has performed the intents of his heart: in the latter days ye shall consider it. Henry notes the purposes of his wrath, as well as the purposes of his love, will all be fulfilled: God will comfort all that turn to him, but those who approach him must have their hearts engaged to do it with reverence, devotion, and faith, and fierce anger returns not until done and intents performed, considered in latter days. How will they escape who neglect so great salvation? — Matthew Henry (Concise)
