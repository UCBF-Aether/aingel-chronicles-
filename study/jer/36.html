Study Notes — Jeremiah

Chapter 36

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: And it came to pass in the fourth year of Jehoiakim the son of Josiah king of Judah, that this word came unto Jeremiah from the Lord, saying. Henry notes the writing of the Scriptures was by Divine appointment: in Jehoiakim's fourth year this word came unto Jeremiah, and Divine wisdom directed to writing as a proper means. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

2 COMMENTARY: Take thee a roll of a book, and write therein all the words that I have spoken unto thee against Israel, and against Judah, and against all the nations, from the day I spoke unto thee, from the days of Josiah, even unto this day. Henry's take a roll, write all words spoken against Israel, Judah, and all nations, from Josiah's days even unto this day, is the Divine appointment: if it failed, the house of Judah would be the more without excuse. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

3 COMMENTARY: It may be that the house of Judah will hear all the evil which I purpose to do unto them; that they may return every man from his evil way; that I may forgive their iniquity and their sin. Henry notes the Lord declares to sinners the evil he purposes to do against them, that they may hear, and fear, and return from their evil ways, and whenever any one makes this use of God's warnings, in dependence on his promised mercy, he will find the Lord ready to forgive his sins: it may be Judah will hear all purposed evil, return every man from evil way, that I may forgive iniquity and sin. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

4 COMMENTARY: Then Jeremiah called Baruch the son of Neriah: and Baruch wrote from the mouth of Jeremiah all the words of the Lord, which he had spoken unto him, upon a roll of a book. Henry's Jeremiah called Baruch, Neriah's son, and Baruch wrote from Jeremiah's mouth all the Lord's words upon a roll, is the writing done: Divine wisdom's proper means executed. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

5 COMMENTARY: And Jeremiah commanded Baruch, saying, I am shut up; I cannot go into the house of the Lord. Henry's Jeremiah commanded Baruch, I am shut up, I cannot go into the Lord's house, is the shut-up prophet's charge: the roll must go where the prophet cannot. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

6 COMMENTARY: Therefore go thou, and read in the roll, which thou hast written from my mouth, the words of the Lord in the ears of the people in the Lord's house upon the fasting day: and also thou shalt read them in the ears of all Judah that come out of their cities. Henry's therefore go thou, read in the roll written from my mouth, the Lord's words in the people's ears in the Lord's house upon the fasting day, and in all Judah's ears coming from cities, is the reading commanded: the written word must be heard. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

7 COMMENTARY: It may be they will present their supplication before the Lord, and will return every one from his evil way: for great is the anger and the fury that the Lord has pronounced against this people. Henry notes all others will be left without excuse, and the consideration that great is the anger God has pronounced against us for sin, should quicken both our prayers and our endeavours: it may be they will present supplication, return every one from evil way, for great is the anger and fury pronounced, is the supplication hoped. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

8 COMMENTARY: And Baruch the son of Neriah did according to all that Jeremiah the prophet commanded him, reading in the book the words of the Lord in the Lord's house. Henry's Baruch did according to all Jeremiah commanded, reading in the book the Lord's words in the Lord's house, is the obedience: the roll is read as commanded. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

9 COMMENTARY: And it came to pass in the fifth year of Jehoiakim the son of Josiah king of Judah, in the ninth month, that they proclaimed a fast before the Lord to all the people in Jerusalem, and to all the people that came from the cities of Judah unto Jerusalem. Henry notes shows of piety and devotion may be found even among those, who, though they keep up forms of godliness, are strangers and enemies to the power of it: in Jehoiakim's fifth year, ninth month, they proclaimed a fast before the Lord to all Jerusalem's people and all Judah's comers, is the fast's form. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

10 COMMENTARY: Then read Baruch in the book the words of Jeremiah in the house of the Lord, in the chamber of Gemariah the son of Shaphan the scribe, in the higher court, at the entry of the new gate of the Lord's house, in the ears of all the people. Henry's Baruch read in the book Jeremiah's words in the Lord's house, Gemariah's chamber, higher court, new gate entry, in all the people's ears, is the public reading: the roll is heard at the gate. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

11 COMMENTARY: When Michaiah the son of Gemariah, the son of Shaphan, had heard out of the book all the words of the Lord, he went down into the king's house, into the scribe's chamber: and, lo, all the princes sat there. Henry's Michaiah, Gemariah's son, Shaphan's son, heard out of the book all the Lord's words, went down into the king's house, scribe's chamber, lo, all princes sat there, is the hearing carried to princes. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

12 COMMENTARY: Then he went down into the king's house, into the scribe's chamber: and, lo, all the princes sat there, to wit, Elishama the scribe, and Delaiah the son of Shemaiah, and Elnathan the son of Achbor, and Gemariah the son of Shaphan, and Zedekiah the son of Hananiah, and all the princes. Henry's Elishama the scribe, Delaiah, Elnathan, Gemariah, Zedekiah, all princes sitting, is the princes assembled: the princes patiently attended the reading of the whole book. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

13 COMMENTARY: Then Michaiah declared unto them all the words that he had heard, when Baruch read the book in the ears of the people. Henry's Michaiah declared all words heard when Baruch read the book in the people's ears, is the declaring: the princes hear the whole book by declaration. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

14 COMMENTARY: Therefore all the princes sent Jehudi the son of Nethaniah, the son of Shelemiah, the son of Cushi, unto Baruch, saying, Bring the roll wherein thou hast read in the ears of the people, and come. So Baruch the son of Neriah took the roll in his hand, and came unto them. Henry's princes sent Jehudi to Baruch, bring the roll read in the people's ears, and come, so Baruch took the roll and came, is the roll fetched: the princes would hear it themselves. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

15 COMMENTARY: And they said unto him, Sit down now, and read it in our ears. So Baruch read it in their ears. Henry's sit down now, read it in our ears, so Baruch read in their ears, is the princes' reading: they patiently attended the reading of the whole book. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

16 COMMENTARY: Now it came to pass, when they had heard all the words, they were afraid both one and other, and said unto Baruch, We will surely tell the king of all these words. Henry notes they were in great fear: when they had heard all words, they were afraid both one and other, and said, we will surely tell the king of all these words, is fear and resolve to tell. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

17 COMMENTARY: And they asked Baruch, saying, Tell us now, How didst thou write all these words at his mouth? Henry's they asked Baruch, tell us now, how didst thou write all these words at his mouth, is the inquiry: convinced to the truth and importance of what they hear, they ask the writing's manner. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

18 COMMENTARY: Then Baruch answered them, He pronounced all these words unto me with his mouth, and I wrote them with ink in the book. Henry's Baruch answered, he pronounced all these words with his mouth, and I wrote them with ink in the book, is the answer: the roll is mouth-pronounced, ink-written. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

19 COMMENTARY: Then said the princes unto Baruch, Go, hide thee, thou and Jeremiah; and let no man know where ye be. Henry notes even those who are convinced to the truth and importance of what they hear, and are disposed to favour those who preach it, often have difficulties and reserves about their safety, interest, or preferment, so that they do not act according to their convictions, and try to get rid of what they find troublesome: go, hide thee, thou and Jeremiah, let no man know where ye be, is the princes' reserve, favouring yet hiding. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

20 COMMENTARY: And they went in to the king into the court, but they laid up the roll in the chamber of Elishama the scribe, and told all the words in the ears of the king. Henry notes those who despise the word of God will soon show, as this king did, that they hate it, and, like him, they would wish it destroyed: they went in to the king into the court, laid up the roll in Elishama's chamber, told all words in the king's ears, is the telling that meets hatred. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

21 COMMENTARY: So the king sent Jehudi to fetch the roll: and he took it out of Elishama the scribe's chamber. And Jehudi read it in the ears of the king, and in the ears of all the princes which stood beside the king. Henry's king sent Jehudi to fetch the roll, took it from Elishama's chamber, Jehudi read it in the king's ears and all standing princes' ears, is the king's hearing: the roll is read to the king. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

22 COMMENTARY: Now the king sat in the winterhouse in the ninth month: and there was a fire on the hearth burning before him. Henry's king sat in the winterhouse in the ninth month, fire on the hearth burning before him, is the scene: winterhouse, ninth month, hearth fire, the burning to come. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

23 COMMENTARY: And it came to pass, that when Jehudi had read three or four leaves, he cut it with the penknife, and cast it into the fire that was on the hearth, until all the roll was consumed in the fire that was on the hearth. Henry's when Jehudi had read three or four leaves, he cut it with the penknife, cast into the hearth fire, until all the roll was consumed, is the burning: those who despise the word will soon show they hate it, and would wish it destroyed. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

24 COMMENTARY: Yet they were not afraid, nor rent their garments, neither the king, nor any of his servants that heard all these words. Henry notes see what enmity there is against God in the carnal mind, and wonder at his patience: yet they were not afraid, nor rent garments, neither king nor servants hearing all words, is the carnal mind's enmity, unafraid. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

25 COMMENTARY: Nevertheless Elnathan and Delaiah and Gemariah had made intercession to the king that he would not burn the roll: but he would not hear them. Henry notes the princes showed some concern, till they saw how light the king made of it: nevertheless Elnathan, Delaiah, and Gemariah interceded that he would not burn the roll, but he would not hear them, is the concern overruled by lightness. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

26 COMMENTARY: But the king commanded Jerahmeel the son of Hammelech, and Seraiah the son of Azriel, and Shelemiah the son of Abdeel, to take Baruch the scribe and Jeremiah the prophet: but the Lord hid them. Henry's king commanded Jerahmeel, Seraiah, and Shelemiah to take Baruch and Jeremiah, but the Lord hid them, is the seizure failed: beware of making light of God's word, for the Lord hides his own. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

27 COMMENTARY: Then the word of the Lord came to Jeremiah, after that the king had burned the roll, and the words which Baruch wrote at the mouth of Jeremiah, saying. Henry's then the word came to Jeremiah, after the king had burned the roll and Baruch's mouth-written words, is the word after burning: the burned word returns. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

28 COMMENTARY: Take thee again another roll, and write in it all the former words that were in the first roll, which Jehoiakim the king of Judah has burned. Henry's take again another roll, write all former words that were in the first roll which Jehoiakim burned, is the rewriting: the despised word is rewritten. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

29 COMMENTARY: And thou shalt say to Jehoiakim king of Judah, Thus says the Lord; Thou hast burned this roll, saying, Why hast thou written therein, saying, The king of Babylon shall certainly come, and destroy this land, and shall cause man and beast to cease from thence? Henry's thou shalt say to Jehoiakim, thus says the Lord, thou hast burned this roll, saying, why hast thou written, Babylon's king shall certainly come, destroy this land, cause man and beast to cease, is the burner's charge: the king's why is answered. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

30 COMMENTARY: Therefore thus says the Lord of Jehoiakim king of Judah; He shall have none to sit upon the throne of David, and his dead body shall be cast out to the heat of the day, and to the frost of the night. Henry's therefore thus says the Lord of Jehoiakim, he shall have none to sit upon David's throne, dead body cast out to day's heat and night's frost, is the burner's doom: the word-burner loses throne and burial. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

31 COMMENTARY: And I will punish him and his seed and his servants for their iniquity; and I will bring upon them, and upon the inhabitants of Jerusalem, and upon the men of Judah, all the evil that I have pronounced against them; but they hearkened not. Henry's I will punish him, seed, servants for iniquity, bring upon them, Jerusalem's inhabitants, Judah's men, all evil pronounced, but they hearkened not, is the punishment: the unhearkened evil comes. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

32 COMMENTARY: Then took Jeremiah another roll, and gave it to Baruch the scribe, the son of Neriah; who wrote therein at the instruction of Jeremiah all the words of the book which Jehoiakim the king of Judah had burned in the fire: and there were added unto them many like words. Henry's Jeremiah took another roll, gave to Baruch, who wrote at Jeremiah's instruction all the burned book's words, and many like words added, is the word restored and enlarged: beware of making light of God's word, for it returns with additions. — Matthew Henry (Concise)
