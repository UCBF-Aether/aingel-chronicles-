Study Notes — Jeremiah

Chapter 21

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: The word which came unto Jeremiah from the Lord, when king Zedekiah sent unto him Pashur the son of Melchiah, and Zephaniah the son of Maaseiah the priest, saying. Henry notes when the siege had begun, Zedekiah sent to ask of Jeremiah respecting the event: in times of distress and danger, men often seek those to counsel and pray for them, whom, at other times, they despise and oppose. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

2 COMMENTARY: Enquire, I pray thee, of the Lord for us; for Nebuchadrezzar king of Babylon makes war against us; if so be that the Lord will deal with us according to all his wondrous works, that he may go up from us. Henry warns they only seek deliverance from punishment: Zedekiah asks enquiry for wondrous works to lift the siege, not repentance to remove the cause. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

3 COMMENTARY: Then said Jeremiah unto them: Thus shall ye say to Zedekiah. Henry's answer begins with the message itself: thus shall ye say to Zedekiah, for the king who sought counsel in distress shall hear counsel he despises. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

4 COMMENTARY: Thus says the Lord God of Israel: Behold, I will turn back the weapons of war that are in your hands, wherewith ye fight against the king of Babylon, and against the Chaldeans, which besiege you without the walls, and I will assemble them into the midst of this city. Henry warns that when professors continue in disobedience, presuming upon outward privileges, let them be told that the Lord will prosper his open enemies against them: weapons turned back and assembled into the city's midst is the Lord prospering Babylon. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

5 COMMENTARY: And I myself will fight against you with an outstretched hand and with a strong arm, even in anger, and in fury, and in great wrath. Henry's Lord himself fights against them, with outstretched hand and strong arm, in anger, fury, and great wrath: the open enemy prospers because God himself is against the city. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

6 COMMENTARY: And I will smite the inhabitants of this city, both man and beast: they shall die of a great pestilence. Henry's smiting falls on man and beast alike: a great pestilence in the besieged city is the Lord fighting against his own people. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

7 COMMENTARY: And afterward, says the Lord, I will deliver Zedekiah king of Judah, and his servants, and the people, and such as are left in this city, from the pestilence, from the sword, and from the famine, into the hand of Nebuchadrezzar king of Babylon, and into the hand of their enemies, and into the hand of those that seek their lives: and he shall smite them with the edge of the sword; he shall not spare them, neither have pity, nor have mercy. Henry notes as the king and his princes would not surrender, the people are exhorted to do so: Zedekiah, servants, and people left from pestilence, sword, and famine shall be delivered to Babylon without sparing, pity, or mercy. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

8 COMMENTARY: And unto this people thou shalt say: Thus says the Lord: Behold, I set before you the way of life, and the way of death. Henry notes no sinner on earth is left without a Refuge, who really desires one; but the way of life is humbling, it requires self-denial, and exposes to difficulties: the way of life and the way of death are set before the people even in the siege. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

9 COMMENTARY: He that abides in this city shall die by the sword, and by the famine, and by the pestilence: but he that goes out, and falls to the Chaldeans that besiege you, he shall live, and his life shall be unto him for a prey. Henry's way of life is surrender: abiding in the city is death by sword, famine, and pestilence, while going out and falling to the Chaldeans is life, though life as a prey. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

10 COMMENTARY: For I have set my face against this city for evil, and not for good, says the Lord: it shall be given into the hand of the king of Babylon, and he shall burn it with fire. Henry's face set for evil and not for good is the only way of deliverance: the city given to Babylon and burned with fire, unless the people surrender. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

11 COMMENTARY: And touching the house of the king of Judah, say: Hear ye the word of the Lord. Henry turns to the king's house: touching the house of the king of Judah, hear the word of the Lord, for the wickedness of the king and his household must be answered. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

12 COMMENTARY: O house of David, thus says the Lord: Execute judgement in the morning, and deliver him that is spoiled out of the hand of the oppressor, lest my fury go forth like fire, and burn that none can quench it, because of the evil of your doings. Henry notes the wickedness of the king and his family was the worse because of their relation to David: they were urged to act with justice, at once, lest the Lord's anger should be unquenchable, executing judgement in the morning and delivering the spoiled from the oppressor. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

13 COMMENTARY: Behold, I am against thee, O inhabitant of the valley, and rock of the plain, says the Lord; which say, Who shall come down against us? Or who shall enter into our houses? Henry asks: if God be for us, who can be against us? But if he be against us, who can do anything for us? The valley-dweller and rock of the plain, asking who shall come down or enter, shall learn that God himself is against them. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

14 COMMENTARY: But I will punish you according to the fruit of your doings, says the Lord: and I will kindle a fire in the forest thereof, and it shall devour all things round about it. Henry's punishment according to the fruit of doings is fire kindled in the forest, devouring all round about: the king's house, worse for its relation to David, is punished by its own fruit. — Matthew Henry (Concise)
