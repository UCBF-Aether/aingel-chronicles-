Study Notes — Jeremiah

Chapter 9

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Oh that Jeremiah's head were waters and his eyes a fountain of tears, that he might weep day and night for the slain of the daughter of his people. Henry notes Jeremiah wept much, yet wished he could weep more, that he might rouse the people to a due sense of the hand of God. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

2 COMMENTARY: Oh that Jeremiah had in the wilderness a lodging place of wayfaring men, that he might leave his people, for they are all adulterers, an assembly of treacherous men. Henry warns that even the desert, without communion with God through Christ Jesus and the influences of the Holy Spirit, must be a place for temptation and evil; while, with these blessings, we may live in holiness in crowded cities. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

3 COMMENTARY: They bend their tongues like their bow for lies, but are not valiant for the truth, proceeding from evil to evil, and they do not know the Lord. Henry observes the people accustomed their tongues to lies, bending them as a bow is bent for the shot. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

4 COMMENTARY: Take heed every one of his neighbour, and trust not in any brother, for every brother will utterly supplant, and every neighbour will walk with slanders. Henry notes they were so false that a brother could not be trusted. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

5 COMMENTARY: They deceive every one his neighbour and will not speak the truth, having taught their tongue to speak lies and wearied themselves to commit iniquity. Henry notes that in trading and bargaining they said anything for their own advantage, though they knew it to be false. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

6 COMMENTARY: Their habitation is in the midst of deceit; through deceit they refuse to know the Lord. Henry asks: where no knowledge of God is, what good can be expected? — Matthew Henry (Concise)

7 COMMENTARY: Therefore the Lord of hosts will melt them and try them, for how shall he do for the daughter of his people? Henry notes God marked their sin: the melting and trying is his measured response to a people steeped in lies. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

8 COMMENTARY: Their tongue is as an arrow shot out, speaking deceit: one speaks peaceably to his neighbour with his mouth, but in heart he lays his wait. Henry's image is the tongue as a weapon drawn and loosed for deceit, peaceable in speech and murderous in intent. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

9 COMMENTARY: Shall God not visit them for these things, and shall not his soul be avenged on such a nation as this? Henry's answer is that God marked their sin, and visitation must follow a nation whose every dealing is deceit. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

10 COMMENTARY: For the mountains Jeremiah will take up a weeping and wailing, and for the habitations of the wilderness a lamentation, because they are burned up, so that none can pass through. Henry notes God has many ways of turning a fruitful land into barrenness for the wickedness of those that dwell therein. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

11 COMMENTARY: God will make Jerusalem heaps and a den of dragons, and the cities of Judah desolate, without an inhabitant. Henry's correction ends in desolation: the city that would not know God is left without an inhabitant. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

12 COMMENTARY: Who is the wise man that may understand this, and who is he to whom the mouth of the Lord has spoken, that he may declare why the land perishes and is burned up like a wilderness that none passes through? Henry notes that in Zion the voice of joy and praise used to be heard while the people kept close to God, but sin has altered the sound: it is now the voice of lamentation. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

13 COMMENTARY: The Lord says it is because they have forsaken his law which he set before them, and have not obeyed his voice, neither walked therein. Henry warns that unhumbled hearts lament their calamity, but not their sin, which is the cause of it. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

14 COMMENTARY: Instead they have walked after the imagination of their own heart, and after Baalim, which their fathers taught them. Henry's cause of the lamentation is this walking after their own imagination and after Baalim, forsaking the law for idols. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

15 COMMENTARY: Therefore the Lord of hosts, the God of Israel, will feed this people with wormwood and give them water of gall to drink. Henry's captivity is bitter food and bitter drink: the affliction tastes of the sin that caused it. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

16 COMMENTARY: God will scatter them also among the heathen, whom neither they nor their fathers have known, and send a sword after them until he has consumed them. Henry notes the captives suffer in a foreign land, scattered among strangers with a sword at their heels. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

17 COMMENTARY: Thus says the Lord of hosts: Consider, and call for the mourning women, that they may come, and send for cunning women. Henry's call is to bewail in earnest: professional mourners are summoned because the whole land must learn to lament. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

18 COMMENTARY: Let them make haste and take up a wailing for us, that our eyes may run down with tears and our eyelids gush out with waters. Henry's wailing is abundant and unceasing, eyes running down with tears for a calamity too great for ordinary grief. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

19 COMMENTARY: For a voice of wailing is heard out of Zion: How are we spoiled! We are greatly confounded, because we have forsaken the land and cast out our dwellings. Henry notes sin altered Zion's sound from joy and praise to this voice of lamentation over spoiling and confounding. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

20 COMMENTARY: Yet hear the word of the Lord, O ye women, and let your ear receive the word of his mouth, and teach your daughters wailing and every one her neighbour lamentation. Henry urges: hearken to the word of the Lord, and mourn with godly sorrow; this alone can bring true comfort, and it can turn the heaviest afflictions into precious mercies. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

21 COMMENTARY: For death is come up into our windows and entered into our palaces, to cut off the children from without and the young men from the streets. Henry warns that though doors be shut ever so fast, death steals upon us: it enters the palaces of princes and great men, though stately, strongly built, and guarded, and nor are those abroad more safe, for death cuts off even the children from without and the young men from the streets. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

22 COMMENTARY: Speak, thus says the Lord: even the carcases of men shall fall as dung upon the open field, and as the handful after the harvestman, and none shall gather them. Henry's end of unrepented lamentation is unburied carcases: only godly sorrow, hearkening to the word of the Lord, can turn such affliction into mercy. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

23 COMMENTARY: Thus says the Lord: let not the wise man glory in his wisdom, neither the mighty man in his might, nor the rich man in his riches. Henry observes that in this world of sin and sorrow, ending soon in death and judgement, how foolish for men to glory in their knowledge, health, strength, or riches, or in anything which leaves them under the dominion of sin and the wrath of God, and of which an account must hereafter be rendered; it will but increase their misery. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

24 COMMENTARY: But let him that glories glory in this, that he understands and knows the Lord, who exercises lovingkindness, judgement, and righteousness in the earth, for in these things he delights. Henry notes those are the true Israel who worship God in the Spirit, rejoice in Christ Jesus, and have no confidence in the flesh; let us prize the distinction which comes from God and will last for ever, and seek it diligently. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

25 COMMENTARY: Behold, the days come when God will punish all them which are circumcised with the uncircumcised. Henry's distinction is not the fleshly mark: those who glory in the flesh shall be punished with the uncircumcised, for the true distinction comes from God alone. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

26 COMMENTARY: Egypt, Judah, Edom, Ammon, Moab, and all in the utmost corners dwelling in the wilderness are uncircumcised, and all the house of Israel are uncircumcised in heart. Henry's point is that the nations and Israel alike stand uncircumcised in heart; only those who worship God in the Spirit and have no confidence in the flesh are the true Israel. — Matthew Henry (Concise)
