Study Notes — Jeremiah

Chapter 26

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: In the beginning of the reign of Jehoiakim the son of Josiah king of Judah came this word from the Lord, saying. Henry notes God's ambassadors must not seek to please men, or to save themselves from harm: the word comes in Jehoiakim's beginning, and the ambassador must speak it whatever it costs. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

2 COMMENTARY: Thus says the Lord: Stand in the court of the Lord's house, and speak unto all the cities of Judah, which come to worship in the Lord's house, all the words that I command thee to speak unto them; diminish not a word. Henry's ambassador diminishes not a word: standing in the Lord's house court, speaking all commanded words to all Judah's worshipping cities, without pleasing men or saving himself. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

3 COMMENTARY: If so be they will hearken, and turn every man from his evil way, that I may repent me of the evil, which I purpose to do unto them because of the evil of their doings. Henry notes see how God waits to be gracious: if so be they hearken and turn every man from his evil way, God may repent of the purposed evil. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

4 COMMENTARY: And thou shalt say unto them: Thus says the Lord; If ye will not hearken to me, to walk in my law, which I have set before you. Henry's condition is hearkening to walk in the set law: if they will not hearken, the curse follows, for those who will not be subject to God's commands make themselves subject to God's curse. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

5 COMMENTARY: To hearken to the words of my servants the prophets, whom I sent unto you, both rising up early, and sending them, but ye have not hearkened. Henry's prophets, rising early and sent, unhearkened, are the voice refused: hearkening to them was hearkening to the law. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

6 COMMENTARY: Then will I make this house like Shiloh, and will make this city a curse to all the nations of the earth. Henry warns if they persisted in disobedience, it would ruin their city and temple: can anything else be expected? The house like Shiloh, the city a curse to all nations, is the ruin of persistence. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

7 COMMENTARY: So the priests and the prophets and all the people heard Jeremiah speaking these words in the house of the Lord. Henry's priests, prophets, and all the people heard the undiminished words in the Lord's house: hearing is the charge's beginning, and the threat's occasion. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

8 COMMENTARY: Now it came to pass, when Jeremiah had made an end of speaking all that the Lord had commanded him to speak unto all the people, that the priests and the prophets and all the people took him, saying, Thou shalt surely die. Henry notes the priests and prophets charged Jeremiah as deserving death, and bore false witness against him: made an end of speaking all commanded, he is taken with, Thou shalt surely die. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

9 COMMENTARY: Why hast thou prophesied in the name of the Lord, saying, This house shall be like Shiloh, and this city shall be desolate without an inhabitant? And all the people were gathered against Jeremiah in the house of the Lord. Henry's false witness is the charge itself: why prophesy house like Shiloh and city desolate, when the prophet only spoke the Lord's words? All the people gathered against him. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

10 COMMENTARY: When the princes of Judah heard these things, then they came up from the king's house unto the house of the Lord, and sat down in the entry of the new gate of the Lord's house. Henry notes the elders of Israel came to inquire into this matter: princes coming up from the king's house to sit in the new gate's entry are the inquiry begun. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

11 COMMENTARY: Then spoke the priests and the prophets unto the princes and to all the people, saying: This man is worthy to die; for he has prophesied against this city, as ye have heard with your own ears. Henry's priests and prophets speak the death-charge to princes and people: worthy to die, for prophesying against the city, as ye heard with your own ears. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

12 COMMENTARY: Then spoke Jeremiah unto all the princes and to all the people, saying: The Lord sent me to prophesy against this house and against this city all the words that ye have heard. Henry notes Jeremiah declares that the Lord sent him to prophesy thus: as long as ministers keep close to the word they have from God, they need not fear, and Jeremiah's defence is his sending. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

13 COMMENTARY: Therefore now amend your ways and your doings, and obey the voice of the Lord your God; and the Lord will repent him of the evil that he has pronounced against you. Henry notes those are very unjust who complain of ministers for preaching of hell and damnation, for it is from a desire to bring them to heaven and salvation: amend ways and doings, obey the voice, and the Lord will repent of the pronounced evil. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

14 COMMENTARY: As for me, behold, I am in your hand: do with me as seems good and meet unto you. Henry's prophet in their hand, to do as seems good and meet, is fearlessness from keeping close to God's word: he need not fear, for the word, not himself, is on trial. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

15 COMMENTARY: But know ye for certain, that if ye put me to death, ye shall surely bring innocent blood upon yourselves, and upon this city, and upon the inhabitants thereof: for of a truth the Lord has sent me unto you to speak all these words in your ears. Henry warns Jeremiah warns them of their danger if they go on against him: all men may know that to hurt, or put to death, or to show hatred to their faithful reprovers, will hasten and increase their own punishment, and innocent blood upon selves, city, and inhabitants is that hastened punishment. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

16 COMMENTARY: Then said the princes and all the people unto the priests and to the prophets: This man is not worthy to die: for he has spoken to us in the name of the Lord our God. Henry notes when secure sinners are threatened with taking away the Spirit of God, and the kingdom of God, it is what is warranted from the word of God: princes and people judge, This man is not worthy to die, for he spoke in the name of the Lord our God. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

17 COMMENTARY: Then rose up certain of the elders of the land, and spoke to all the assembly of the people, saying. Henry's elders rise to speak to all the assembly: the defence by the elders begins with precedent, that the word is warranted. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

18 COMMENTARY: Micah the Morasthite prophesied in the days of Hezekiah king of Judah, and spoke to all the people of Judah, saying: Thus says the Lord of hosts; Zion shall be plowed like a field, and Jerusalem shall become heaps, and the mountain of the house as the high places of a forest. Henry notes Hezekiah who protected Micah prospered: Micah prophesied Zion plowed, Jerusalem heaps, and the house's mountain as forest high places, in Hezekiah's days, and was protected. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

19 COMMENTARY: Did Hezekiah king of Judah and all Judah put him at all to death? Did he not fear the Lord, and beseech the Lord, and the Lord repented him of the evil which he had pronounced against them? Thus might we procure great evil against our souls. Henry's Hezekiah feared, besought, and the Lord repented of pronounced evil: putting Micah to death would have procured great evil against their own souls, and protecting him prospered. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

20 COMMENTARY: And there was also a man that prophesied in the name of the Lord, Urijah the son of Shemaiah of Kirjathjearim, who prophesied against this city and against this land according to all the words of Jeremiah. Henry notes the examples of bad men, and the bad consequences of their sins, should deter from what is evil: Urijah prophesied the same words against city and land, and his example is set beside Jeremiah's. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

21 COMMENTARY: And when Jehoiakim the king, with all his mighty men, and all the princes, heard his words, the king sought to put him to death: but when Urijah heard it, he was afraid, and fled, and went into Egypt. Henry asks: did Jehoiakim, who slew Urijah, prosper? The king sought his death, and Urijah fled afraid into Egypt, the bad example's first consequence. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

22 COMMENTARY: And Jehoiakim the king sent men into Egypt, namely, Elnathan the son of Achbor, and certain men with him into Egypt. Henry's Jehoiakim sends men into Egypt after the fled prophet: the bad king's pursuit crosses borders, and his example deters from evil. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

23 COMMENTARY: And they fetched forth Urijah out of Egypt, and brought him unto Jehoiakim the king; who slew him with the sword, and cast his dead body into the graves of the common people. Henry notes Urijah was faithful in delivering his message, but faulty in leaving his work: fetched from Egypt, slain with the sword, cast into common graves, and the Lord was pleased to permit him to lose his life, while Jeremiah was protected in danger. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

24 COMMENTARY: Nevertheless the hand of Ahikam the son of Shaphan was with Jeremiah, that they should not give him into the hand of the people to put him to death. Henry closes: those are safest who most simply trust in the Lord, whatever their outward circumstances may be, and that he has all men's hearts in his hands encourages us to trust him in the way of duty. He will honour and recompense those who show kindness to such as are persecuted for his sake, and Ahikam's hand with Jeremiah is that kindness, keeping him from the people's hand. — Matthew Henry (Concise)
