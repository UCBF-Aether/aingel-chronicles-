Study Notes — Jeremiah

Chapter 10

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Hear the word which the Lord speaks to you, O house of Israel. Henry opens by showing the glory of Israel's God and exposing the folly of idolaters: the word itself, not heathen custom, must teach Israel. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

2 COMMENTARY: Thus says the Lord: learn not the way of the heathen, and be not dismayed at the signs of heaven, for the heathen are dismayed at them. Henry warns that charms and other attempts to obtain supernatural help, or to pry into futurity, are copied from the wicked customs of the heathen. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

3 COMMENTARY: For the customs of the people are vain: one cuts a tree out of the forest, the work of the hands of the workman, with the axe. Henry calls such customs vanity: a god cut from the forest by a workman's axe is a doctrine of vanity, not a power. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

4 COMMENTARY: They deck it with silver and with gold, and fasten it with nails and hammers, that it move not. Henry's folly is plain: the idol must be decked and nailed fast, lest it fall, yet it cannot move itself. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

5 COMMENTARY: They are upright as the palm tree, but speak not: they must needs be borne, because they cannot go. Be not afraid of them, for they cannot do evil, neither also is it in them to do good. Henry urges us to stand in awe, and not dare provoke God, by giving that glory to another which is due to him alone: a god that must be carried can neither harm nor help. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

6 COMMENTARY: Forasmuch as there is none like unto thee, O Lord; thou art great, and thy name is great in might. Henry sets the glory of Israel's God against the idols: none is like him, and his name is great in might. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

7 COMMENTARY: Who would not fear thee, O King of nations? For to thee doth it appertain: forasmuch as among all the wise men of the nations, and in all their kingdoms, there is none like unto thee. Henry notes fear belongs to the King of nations alone, since among all wise men and kingdoms none is like him. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

8 COMMENTARY: But they are altogether brutish and foolish: the stock is a doctrine of vanities. Henry warns that all knowledge not from the word of God leads to doctrines of vanity, and idolaters, for all their learning, are brutish and foolish. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

9 COMMENTARY: Silver spread into plates is brought from Tarshish, and gold from Uphaz, the work of the workman, and of the hands of the founder: blue and purple is their clothing: they are all the work of cunning men. Henry's exposure is that the idol, for all its silver, gold, blue, and purple, remains the work of cunning men. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

10 COMMENTARY: But the Lord is the true God, he is the living God, and an everlasting king: at his wrath the earth shall tremble, and the nations shall not be able to abide his indignation. Henry contrasts the true, living, everlasting King with dead idols: at his wrath the earth trembles. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

11 COMMENTARY: Thus shall ye say unto them: the gods that have not made the heavens and the earth, even they shall perish from the earth, and from under these heavens. Henry's test is creation: gods that made nothing shall perish from the earth they did not make. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

12 COMMENTARY: He has made the earth by his power, he has established the world by his wisdom, and has stretched out the heavens by his discretion. Henry shows the glory of Israel's God as Maker: power made the earth, wisdom established the world, discretion stretched the heavens. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

13 COMMENTARY: When he utters his voice, there is a multitude of waters in the heavens, and he causes the vapours to ascend from the ends of the earth; he makes lightnings with rain, and brings forth the wind out of his treasures. Henry's God commands the waters, vapours, lightnings, and winds: the heavens obey the voice the idols cannot utter. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

14 COMMENTARY: Every man is brutish in his knowledge: every founder is confounded by the graven image: for his molten image is falsehood, and there is no breath in them. Henry notes the founder is confounded by his own work: the molten image is falsehood, and there is no breath in it. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

15 COMMENTARY: They are vanity, and the work of errors: in the time of their visitation they shall perish. Henry warns that in the time of visitation the vanities perish: the idols' day of reckoning exposes them as the work of errors. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

16 COMMENTARY: The portion of Jacob is not like them: for he is the former of all things; and Israel is the rod of his inheritance: the Lord of hosts is his name. Henry notes Israel's portion is unlike the idols, for he is the former of all things; and he is ready to forgive, and save all who repent and believe in the name of his Son Jesus Christ, for faith learns these blessed truths from the word of God. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

17 COMMENTARY: Gather up thy wares out of the land, O inhabitant of the fortress. Henry turns to the Jews who continued in their own land and felt secure: gathering up wares is the order to prepare for flight, since security in the fortress is false. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

18 COMMENTARY: For thus says the Lord: behold, I will sling out the inhabitants of the land at this once, and will distress them, that they may find it so. Henry warns that sooner or later sinners will find all things as the word of God has declared, and that its threatenings are not empty terrors. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

19 COMMENTARY: Woe is me for my hurt! My wound is grievous: but I said, truly this is a grief, and I must bear it. Henry contrasts submission, which will support the believer under every grief allotted to him, with sullen despair, under which nothing can render the load of divine vengeance easy to be borne. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

20 COMMENTARY: My tabernacle is spoiled, and all my cords are broken: my children are gone forth of me, and they are not: there is none to stretch forth my tent any more, and to set up my curtains. Henry notes the designs which men lay deep, and think well formed, are dashed to pieces in a moment, and events are often overruled so as to be quite contrary to what we intended and expected. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

21 COMMENTARY: For the pastors are become brutish, and have not sought the Lord: therefore they shall not prosper, and all their flocks shall be scattered. Henry warns those cannot expect to prosper who do not, by faith and prayer, take God with them in all their ways: unsought, the Lord leaves pastors brutish and flocks scattered. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

22 COMMENTARY: Behold, the noise of the bruit is come, and a great commotion out of the north country, to make the cities of Judah desolate, and a den of dragons. Henry notes the report of the enemy's approach was very dreadful: the bruit from the north is the desolation itself approaching. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

23 COMMENTARY: O Lord, I know that the way of man is not in himself: it is not in man that walks to direct his steps. Henry urges that if the Lord has directed our steps into the ways of peace and righteousness, let us entreat him to enable us to walk therein, since man cannot direct his own steps. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

24 COMMENTARY: O Lord, correct me, but with judgement; not in thine anger, lest thou bring me to nothing. Henry counsels: say not, Lord, do not correct me; but, Lord, do not correct me in anger. We may bear the smart of God's rod, but we cannot bear the weight of his wrath. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

25 COMMENTARY: Pour out thy fury upon the heathen that know thee not, and upon the families that call not on thy name: for they have eaten up Jacob, and devoured him, and consumed him, and have made his habitation desolate. Henry notes those who restrain prayer prove that they know not God, for those who know him will seek him and seek his favour; and if even severe corrections lead sinners to be convinced of wholesome truths, they will have abundant cause for gratitude, and will then humble themselves before the Lord. — Matthew Henry (Concise)
