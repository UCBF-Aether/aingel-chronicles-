Study Notes — Jeremiah

Chapter 34

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: The word which came unto Jeremiah from the Lord, when Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon, and all his army, and all the kingdoms of the earth of his dominion, and all the people, fought against Jerusalem, and against all the cities thereof, saying. Henry notes Zedekiah is told that the city shall be taken, and that he shall die a captive, but he shall die a natural death: the word when Nebuchadnezzar and all armies and kingdoms fought against Jerusalem and all cities is the taking foretold. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

2 COMMENTARY: Thus says the Lord, the God of Israel; Go and speak to Zedekiah king of Judah, and tell him, Thus says the Lord; Behold, I will give this city into the hand of the king of Babylon, and he shall burn it with fire. Henry's go and speak to Zedekiah, behold, I will give this city to Babylon's king, and he shall burn it with fire, is the taking told: the city shall be taken. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

3 COMMENTARY: And thou shalt not escape out of his hand, but shalt surely be taken, and delivered into his hand; and thine eyes shall behold the eyes of the king of Babylon, and he shall speak with thee mouth to mouth, and thou shalt go to Babylon. Henry's thou shalt not escape, surely taken, delivered into his hand, eyes beholding Babylon's king's eyes, speaking mouth to mouth, going to Babylon, is the captive's fate: he shall die a captive. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

4 COMMENTARY: Yet hear the word of the Lord, O Zedekiah king of Judah; Thus says the Lord of thee, Thou shalt not die by the sword. Henry's yet hear, O Zedekiah, thou shalt not die by the sword, is the natural death: he shall die a captive, but not by the sword. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

5 COMMENTARY: But thou shalt die in peace: and with the burnings of thy fathers, the former kings which were before thee, so shall they burn odours for thee; and they will lament thee, saying, Ah lord! for I have pronounced the word, says the Lord. Henry notes it is better to live and die penitent in a prison, than to live and die impenitent in a palace: thou shalt die in peace, with fathers' burnings, odours burned for thee, lamented, Ah lord, is the penitent prisoner's peace. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

6 COMMENTARY: Then Jeremiah the prophet spoke all these words unto Zedekiah king of Judah in Jerusalem. Henry's Jeremiah spoke all these words unto Zedekiah in Jerusalem is the telling done: the city taken, captive dying naturally, penitent in prison, is spoken to the king. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

7 COMMENTARY: When the king of Babylon's army fought against Jerusalem, and against all the cities of Judah that were left, against Lachish, and against Azekah: for these defenced cities remained of the cities of Judah. Henry's Babylon army fought against Jerusalem and all Judah's left cities, Lachish and Azekah, defenced cities remaining, is the siege's remnant: the taking foretold was already narrowing to these. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

8 COMMENTARY: This is the word that came unto Jeremiah from the Lord, after that the king Zedekiah had made a covenant with all the people which were at Jerusalem, to proclaim liberty unto them. Henry notes a Jew should not be held in servitude above seven years: this word after Zedekiah covenanted with all Jerusalem's people to proclaim liberty is the liberty covenant, and the seven-year law behind it. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

9 COMMENTARY: That every man should let his manservant, and every man his maidservant, being an Hebrew or an Hebrewess, go free; that none should serve himself of them, to wit, of a Jew his brother. Henry's every man let Hebrew manservant and maidservant go free, none serve himself of a Jew his brother, is the law: this law they and their fathers had broken. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

10 COMMENTARY: Now when all the princes, and all the people, which had entered into the covenant, heard that every one should let his manservant, and every one his maidservant, go free, that none should serve themselves of them any more, then they obeyed, and let them go. Henry's princes and people entered the covenant, heard every one let servants go free, none serve themselves any more, then obeyed and let them go, is the partial reformation: obeyed for a time. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

11 COMMENTARY: But afterward they turned, and caused the servants and the handmaids, whom they had let go free, to return, and brought them into subjection for servants and for handmaids. Henry notes when there was some hope that the siege was raised, they forced the servants they had released into their services again: afterward they turned, caused freed servants and handmaids to return, brought into subjection, is the turning back when hope rose. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

12 COMMENTARY: Therefore the word of the Lord came to Jeremiah from the Lord, saying. Henry's therefore the word came to Jeremiah is the reproof: the Jews reproved for compelling poor brethren to return to unlawful bondage begins here. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

13 COMMENTARY: Thus says the Lord, the God of Israel; I made a covenant with your fathers in the day that I brought them forth out of the land of Egypt, out of the house of bondmen, saying. Henry's I made a covenant with fathers in the day brought forth from Egypt, house of bondmen, is the covenant recalled: the Egypt deliverance covenant is the seven-year law's root. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

14 COMMENTARY: At the end of seven years let ye go every man his brother an Hebrew, which has been sold unto thee; and when he has served thee six years, thou shalt let him go free from thee: but your fathers hearkened not unto me, neither inclined their ear. Henry's at seven years' end let go every Hebrew brother sold, served six years, let go free, but fathers hearkened not, inclined not ear, is the broken law: they and their fathers had broken it. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

15 COMMENTARY: And ye were now turned, and had done right in my sight, in proclaiming liberty every man to his neighbour; and ye had made a covenant before me in the house which is called by my name. Henry notes those who think to cheat God by dissembled repentance and partial reformation put the greatest cheat upon their own souls: ye were now turned, done right in my sight, proclaiming liberty every man to neighbour, covenanted before me in the house called by my name, is the dissembled repentance, right for a moment. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

16 COMMENTARY: But ye turned and polluted my name, and caused every man his servant, and every man his handmaid, whom he had set at liberty, to return, and brought them into subjection, to be unto you for servants and for handmaids. Henry notes this shows that liberty to sin is really only liberty to have the sorest judgments: ye turned, polluted my name, caused freed servants and handmaids to return, brought into subjection, is the polluting turn. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

17 COMMENTARY: Therefore thus says the Lord; Ye have not hearkened unto me, in proclaiming liberty, every one to his brother, and every man to his neighbour: behold, I proclaim a liberty for you, says the Lord, to the sword, to the pestilence, and to the famine; and I will make you to be removed into all the kingdoms of the earth. Henry notes it is just with God to disappoint expectations of mercy, when we disappoint the expectations of duty: ye have not hearkened in proclaiming liberty to brother and neighbour, behold, I proclaim liberty for you to sword, pestilence, famine, removed into all kingdoms, is mercy disappointed for duty disappointed. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

18 COMMENTARY: And I will give the men that have transgressed my covenant, which have not performed the words of the covenant which they had made before me, when they cut the calf in twain, and passed between the parts thereof. Henry notes when reformation springs only from terror, it is seldom lasting: I will give the men that transgressed my covenant, not performed its words made before me, when they cut the calf in twain and passed between parts, is the terror-born covenant broken. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

19 COMMENTARY: The princes of Judah, and the princes of Jerusalem, the eunuchs, and the priests, and all the people of the land, which passed between the parts of the calf. Henry notes solemn vows thus entered into profane the ordinances of God, and the most forward to bind themselves by appeals to God are commonly most ready to break them: princes of Judah and Jerusalem, eunuchs, priests, all the land's people, passing between the calf's parts, are the forward binders. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

20 COMMENTARY: I will even give them into the hand of their enemies, and into the hand of them that seek their life: and their dead bodies shall be for meat unto the fowls of the heaven, and to the beasts of the earth. Henry's I will give them into enemies' hands, into seekers of life, dead bodies for heaven's fowls and earth's beasts, is the vow-breakers' doom: profaned ordinances bring sorest judgments. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

21 COMMENTARY: And Zedekiah king of Judah and his princes will I give into the hand of their enemies, and into the hand of them that seek their life, and into the hand of the king of Babylon's army, which are gone up from you. Henry's Zedekiah and princes given to enemies, life-seekers, Babylon's army gone up from you, is the king's share: the army hoped gone returns. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

22 COMMENTARY: Behold, I will command, says the Lord, and cause them to return to this city; and they shall fight against it, and take it, and burn it with fire: and I will make the cities of Judah a desolation without an inhabitant. Henry notes let us look to our hearts, that our repentance may be real, and take care that the law of God regulates our conduct: behold, I will command, cause them to return to this city, fight, take, burn with fire, make Judah's cities desolation without inhabitant, is the real repentance called for, and the desolation for the unreal. — Matthew Henry (Concise)
