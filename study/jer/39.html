Study Notes — Jeremiah

Chapter 39

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: In the ninth year of Zedekiah king of Judah, in the tenth month, came Nebuchadrezzar king of Babylon and all his army against Jerusalem, and they besieged it. Henry notes Jerusalem was so strong, that the inhabitants believed the enemy could never enter it: in Zedekiah's ninth year, tenth month, Nebuchadnezzar and all his army came against Jerusalem and besieged it, and strength believed impregnable is besieged. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

2 COMMENTARY: And in the eleventh year of Zedekiah, in the fourth month, the ninth day of the month, the city was broken up. Henry's in the eleventh year, fourth month, ninth day, the city was broken up, is the strong city entered: sin provoked God to withdraw his protection, and then it was as weak as other cities. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

3 COMMENTARY: And all the princes of the king of Babylon came in, and sat in the middle gate, even Nergalsharezer, Samgarnebo, Sarsechim, Rabsaris, Nergalsharezer, Rabmag, with all the residue of the princes of the king of Babylon. Henry's all Babylon's princes came in, sat in the middle gate, Nergalsharezer, Samgarnebo, Sarsechim, Rabsaris, Rabmag, with all residue princes, is the entry: the gate sat by conquerors. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

4 COMMENTARY: And it came to pass, that when Zedekiah the king of Judah saw them, and all the men of war, then they fled, and went forth out of the city by night, by the way of the king's garden, by the gate betwixt the two walls: and he went out the way of the plain. Henry's when Zedekiah saw them, and all men of war, then they fled, went forth by night, king's garden way, gate betwixt two walls, way of the plain, is the flight: observe the wonderful changes of Providence, how uncertain are earthly possessions. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

5 COMMENTARY: But the Chaldeans' army pursued after them, and overtook Zedekiah in the plains of Jericho: and when they had taken him, they brought him to Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon to Riblah in the land of Hamath, where he gave judgment upon him. Henry's Chaldeans pursued, overtook Zedekiah in Jericho's plains, took him, brought to Nebuchadnezzar at Riblah in Hamath, where he gave judgment, is the overtaking: those who will not believe God's words will be convinced by the event. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

6 COMMENTARY: Then the king of Babylon slew the sons of Zedekiah in Riblah before his eyes: also the king of Babylon slew all the nobles of Judah. Henry's Babylon's king slew Zedekiah's sons in Riblah before his eyes, also slew all Judah's nobles, is the judgment: see the just dealings of Providence. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

7 COMMENTARY: Moreover he put out Zedekiah's eyes, and bound him with chains, to carry him to Babylon. Henry notes Zedekiah had his eyes put out, so he was condemned to darkness who had shut his eyes against the clear light of God's word: moreover he put out Zedekiah's eyes, bound him with chains, to carry him to Babylon, is the darkness for shut eyes. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

8 COMMENTARY: And the Chaldeans burned the king's house, and the houses of the people, with fire, and brake down the walls of Jerusalem. Henry's Chaldeans burned the king's house and people's houses with fire, broke down Jerusalem's walls, is the burning: whether the Lord makes men poor or rich, nothing will profit them while they cleave to their sins. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

9 COMMENTARY: Then Nebuzaradan the captain of the guard carried away captive into Babylon the remnant of the people that remained in the city, and those that fell away, that fell to him, with the rest of the people that remained. Henry's Nebuzaradan carried away captive into Babylon the remnant remaining in the city, those fallen away, with the rest remaining, is the captivity: the strong city's remnant is carried. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

10 COMMENTARY: But Nebuzaradan the captain of the guard left of the poor of the people, which had nothing, in the land of Judah, and gave them vineyards and fields at the same time. Henry's Nebuzaradan left of the poor, which had nothing, in Judah's land, gave them vineyards and fields at the same time, is the poor left with fields: earthly possessions change, and the poor receive. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

11 COMMENTARY: Now Nebuchadrezzar king of Babylon gave charge concerning Jeremiah to Nebuzaradan the captain of the guard, saying. Henry notes the servants of God alone are prepared for all events, and they are delivered and comforted, while the wicked suffer: now Nebuchadnezzar gave charge concerning Jeremiah to Nebuzaradan, is the charge for the servant. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

12 COMMENTARY: Take him, and look well to him, and do him no harm; but do unto him even as he shall say unto thee. Henry's take him, look well to him, do him no harm, do unto him even as he shall say, is the charge's care: the Lord will raise them up friends, do them good, and perform all his promises. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

13 COMMENTARY: So Nebuzaradan the captain of the guard sent, and Nebushasban, Rabsaris, and Nergalsharezer, Rabmag, and all the king of Babylon's princes. Henry's Nebuzaradan sent, and Nebushasban, Rabsaris, Nergalsharezer, Rabmag, and all Babylon's princes, is the sending: the profane princes are sent for the prophet. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

14 COMMENTARY: Even they sent, and took Jeremiah out of the court of the prison, and committed him unto Gedaliah the son of Ahikam the son of Shaphan, that he should carry him home: so he dwelt among the people. Henry notes they often meet with more kindness from the profane, than from hypocritical professors of godliness: even they sent, took Jeremiah out of the prison court, committed him to Gedaliah, Ahikam's son, Shaphan's son, that he should carry him home, so he dwelt among the people, is the kindness from the profane. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

15 COMMENTARY: Now the word of the Lord came unto Jeremiah, while he was shut up in the court of the prison, saying. Henry notes here is a message to assure Ebedmelech of a recompence for his great kindness to Jeremiah: now the word came unto Jeremiah, while shut up in the prison court, is the message while shut up. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

16 COMMENTARY: Go and speak to Ebedmelech the Ethiopian, saying, Thus says the Lord of hosts, the God of Israel; Behold, I will bring my words upon this city for evil, and not for good; and they shall be accomplished in that day before thee. Henry's go and speak to Ebedmelech the Ethiopian, thus says the Lord, behold, I will bring my words upon this city for evil, not good, accomplished in that day before thee, is the evil accomplished before the kind man: the message assures recompence amid evil. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

17 COMMENTARY: But I will deliver thee in that day, says the Lord: and thou shalt not be given into the hand of the men of whom thou art afraid. Henry's but I will deliver thee in that day, thou shalt not be given into the hand of men of whom thou art afraid, is the deliverance: because thou hast put thy trust in me, says the Lord. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

18 COMMENTARY: For I will surely deliver thee, and thou shalt not fall by the sword, but thy life shall be for a prey unto thee: because thou hast put thy trust in me, says the Lord. Henry notes God recompenses men's services according to their principles, and those who trust God in the way of duty, as this good man did, will find that their hope shall not fail in times of the greatest danger: for I will surely deliver thee, thou shalt not fall by the sword, thy life for a prey, because thou hast put thy trust in me, is the trust's recompence. — Matthew Henry (Concise)
