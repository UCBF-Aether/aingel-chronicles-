Study Notes — Jeremiah

Chapter 20

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Now Pashur the son of Immer the priest, who was also chief governor in the house of the Lord, heard that Jeremiah prophesied these things. Henry's doom of Pashur begins with hearing: the chief governor in the Lord's house heard Jeremiah prophesy ruin, and the hearing provoked the smiting. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

2 COMMENTARY: Then Pashur smote Jeremiah the prophet, and put him in the stocks that were in the high gate of Benjamin, which was by the house of the Lord. Henry notes Pashur smote Jeremiah, and put him in the stocks: the prophet who gave notice of ruin is smitten and stocked in the high gate by the Lord's house. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

3 COMMENTARY: And it came to pass on the morrow, that Pashur brought forth Jeremiah out of the stocks. Then said Jeremiah unto him: The Lord has not called thy name Pashur, but Magormissabib. Henry notes Jeremiah was silent till God put a word into his mouth: brought forth on the morrow, he speaks the new name, Fear on every side, to confirm the word he preached. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

4 COMMENTARY: For thus says the Lord: Behold, I will make thee a terror to thyself, and to all thy friends: and they shall fall by the sword of their enemies, and thine eyes shall behold it: and I will give all Judah into the hand of the king of Babylon, and he shall carry them captive into Babylon, and shall slay them with the sword. Henry notes the name speaks a man not only in distress, but in despair; not only in danger, but in fear on every side: a terror to himself and to all his friends, with Judah given to Babylon before his eyes. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

5 COMMENTARY: Moreover I will deliver all the strength of this city, and all the labours thereof, and all the precious things thereof, and all the treasures of the kings of Judah will I give into the hand of their enemies, which shall spoil them, and take them, and carry them to Babylon. Henry's miserable man made a terror to himself sees strength, labours, precious things, and kings' treasures delivered to enemies, spoiled, taken, and carried to Babylon. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

6 COMMENTARY: And thou, Pashur, and all that dwell in thine house shall go into captivity: and thou shalt come to Babylon, and there thou shalt die, and shalt be buried there, thou, and all thy friends, to whom thou hast prophesied lies. Henry notes his friends shall fail him, and God lets him live miserably, that he may be a monument of divine justice: Pashur and his house, and all his friends to whom he prophesied lies, shall go to Babylon, die there, and be buried there. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

7 COMMENTARY: O Lord, thou hast deceived me, and I was deceived: thou art stronger than I, and hast prevailed: I am in derision daily, every one mocks me. Henry notes the prophet complains of the insult and injury he experienced, but verse 7 may be read, Thou hast persuaded me, and I was persuaded: thou wast stronger than I, and didst overpower me by the influence of thy Spirit upon me, and so Jeremiah, persuaded and prevailed upon, is in derision daily. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

8 COMMENTARY: For since I spoke, I cried out, I cried violence and spoil; because the word of the Lord was made a reproach unto me, and a derision, daily. Henry's complaint is the word made reproach and daily derision: since he spoke, crying violence and spoil, the word itself became his reproach. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

9 COMMENTARY: Then I said: I will not make mention of him, nor speak any more in his name. But his word was in mine heart as a burning fire shut up in my bones, and I was weary with forbearing, and I could not stay. Henry notes the prophet found the grace of God mighty in him to keep him to his business, notwithstanding the temptation he was in to throw it up: the word as burning fire shut up in bones would not let him stay silent. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

10 COMMENTARY: For I heard the defaming of many, fear on every side. Report, say they, and we will report it. All my familiars watched for my halting, saying, Peradventure he will be enticed, and we shall prevail against him, and we shall take our revenge on him. Henry's defaming many, fear on every side, with familiars watching for halting and plotting revenge, is the insult and injury the prophet complains of, yet grace keeps him to his business. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

11 COMMENTARY: But the Lord is with me as a mighty terrible one: therefore my persecutors shall stumble, and they shall not prevail: they shall be greatly ashamed; for they shall not prosper: their everlasting confusion shall never be forgotten. Henry notes whatever injuries are done to us, we must leave them to that God to whom vengeance belongs, and who has said, I will repay: the Lord with him as a mighty terrible one makes persecutors stumble, not prevail, ashamed and confused everlastingly. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

12 COMMENTARY: But, O Lord of hosts, that tries the righteous, and sees the reins and the heart, let me see thy vengeance on them: for unto thee have I opened my cause. Henry notes let the people of God open their cause before him, and he will enable them to see deliverance: Jeremiah opens his cause to the Lord who tries the righteous and sees reins and heart. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

13 COMMENTARY: Sing unto the Lord, praise ye the Lord: for he has delivered the soul of the poor from the hand of evildoers. Henry notes so full was he of the comfort of God's presence, the divine protection he was under, and the divine promise he had to depend upon, that he stirred up himself and others to give God the glory: sing and praise, for the poor soul is delivered from evildoers' hands. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

14 COMMENTARY: Cursed be the day wherein I was born: let not the day wherein my mother bare me be blessed. Henry notes when grace has the victory, it is good to be ashamed of our folly, to admire the goodness of God, and be warned to guard our spirits another time: see how strong the temptation was, over which the prophet got the victory by divine assistance, for he is angry that his first breath was not his last. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

15 COMMENTARY: Cursed be the man who brought tidings to my father, saying, A man child is born unto thee; making him very glad. Henry warns while we remember that these wishes are not recorded for us to utter the like, we may learn good lessons from them: the cursed messenger of glad tidings shows how foolish and unnatural are the thoughts and wishes of our hearts when we yield to discontent. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

16 COMMENTARY: And let that man be as the cities which the Lord overthrew, and repented not: and let him hear the cry in the morning, and the shouting at noontide. Henry notes see how much those who think they stand ought to take heed lest they fall, and to pray daily, Lead us not into temptation: the man as overthrown cities, hearing cry in the morning and shouting at noon, is the discontented wish of a standing prophet taking heed too late. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

17 COMMENTARY: Because he slew me not from the womb; or that my mother might have been my grave, and her womb to be always great with me. Henry notes how frail, changeable, and sinful is man: the wish that the womb had slain him, that his mother had been his grave, is frailty yielding to discontent. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

18 COMMENTARY: Wherefore came I forth out of the womb to see labour and sorrow, that my days should be consumed with shame? Henry closes: let us consider him who endured the contradiction of sinners against himself, lest we should be at any time weary and faint in our minds under our lesser trials, for the prophet's question, why came I forth to see labour and sorrow with days consumed in shame, is answered by considering Christ, who endured greater contradiction and fainted not. — Matthew Henry (Concise)
