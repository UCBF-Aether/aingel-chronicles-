Study Notes — Jeremiah

Chapter 50

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Henry notes Babylon's judgment is put last among the prophecies against the Gentiles because it was last accomplished, the king of Sheshach drinking last of the cup of fury. Babylon was the rod in God's hand for chastising the other nations, and now at length that rod shall be thrown into the fire. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

2 COMMENTARY: Henry says Babylon's destruction is spoken of as a thing done, to be published to the nations and not concealed, with the flag hung out on a day of triumph. Even Babylon's principal idols, Bel and Merodach, which the people protected with all care and expected protection from, shall be confounded and broken to pieces. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

3 COMMENTARY: Henry says the nation from the north is Media, lying north of Babylon, with Assyria the route by which Cyrus descended upon her, and God will find nations yet further north to come upon those who threatened evil from the north. Her land shall be made desolate, with man and beast departing. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

4 COMMENTARY: Henry promises first a return to God and then to the land: Israel and Judah together shall go weeping to seek the LORD their God. These tears, he notes, flow not from worldly sorrow as at the captivity, but from godly sorrow, tears of repentance for sin and of joy at the dawning of deliverance. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

5 COMMENTARY: Henry says they shall ask the way to Zion with their faces thitherward, resolving not to take up short of it, and shall join themselves to the LORD in a perpetual covenant never to be forgotten. In all true converts, he observes, there are both a sincere desire to attain the end and a constant care to keep in the way. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

6 COMMENTARY: Henry laments God's people as lost sheep, hurried from mountain to hill, who have forgotten their resting-place. Their misery was aggravated because their own shepherds, their princes and priests, led them astray from duty and so provoked God to turn them out of their land. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

7 COMMENTARY: Henry notes the devourers excused themselves by saying, We offend not, because they have sinned against the LORD, the habitation of justice and the hope of their fathers. That excuse, he says, aggravated Israel's sin and justified God, but did not justify the adversaries in what they did to them. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

8 COMMENTARY: Henry calls the people to hasten away as soon as the door of liberty opens, removing not merely out of Babylon's borders but out of its midst, and to be as the he-goats before the flocks. It is a graceful thing, he remarks, to be forward in a good work and set others a good example. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

9 COMMENTARY: Henry calls the invading army an assembly of great nations, the Medes and Persians with all their allies, regularly formed by the divine will and counsel for this execution. God will raise them up, incline and fit them for the service, and cause them to come up, so that their arrows, like those of a mighty expert man, shall none return in vain, for when God gives commission he gives success. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

10 COMMENTARY: Henry says Babylon's wealth shall be a rich and easy prey, and, strangely, all that spoil her shall be satisfied, having so much that even they themselves shall say they have enough. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

11 COMMENTARY: Henry says the Chaldeans rejoiced and were glad as destroyers of God's heritage, growing fat as the heifer at grass and bellowing as bulls on the spoils of Jerusalem. God does not afflict his people willingly, he notes, and takes it very ill if the instruments he employs afflict them willingly, so those who swallowed down riches must vomit them up again. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

12 COMMENTARY: Henry reads the mother confounded and ashamed as Babylon the mother-city, deserted by those who should have been her guards, and become the hindermost of the nations, a wilderness, a dry land and a desert. The great admirers of Babylon shall see it rendered very despicable. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

13 COMMENTARY: Henry says it is because of the wrath of the LORD that Babylon shall be wholly desolate and uninhabited, and everyone going by shall be astonished and hiss at her plagues. Instead of condoling with her, passers-by shall triumph in her fall. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

14 COMMENTARY: Henry says the archers are bidden to put themselves in array and shoot, sparing no arrows, because she has sinned against the LORD. Sin, he notes, makes men a mark for the arrows of God's judgments, and the injuries done to God's people are laid to Babylon's charge as a heinous crime. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

15 COMMENTARY: Henry says the shout against Babylon is the triumphant shout of those already sure of victory, for those whom God directs to shoot may shout, sure not to miss the mark. She has given her hand, surrendering tamely, her foundations have fallen and her walls are thrown down, and now vengeance may be taken as reprisal, doing unto her as she has done. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

16 COMMENTARY: Henry says not even the poor of the land shall be left in Babylon, as there were in Israel: the sower and the sickle-handler shall be cut off, so that harvest comes with no reapers and seed-time with no sower. Her hired auxiliary forces shall also desert, turning every one to his own people for fear of the oppressing sword, as mercenary men often do on the approach of danger. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

17 COMMENTARY: Henry pictures Israel as a scattered sheep worried by dogs and roared upon by lions: Assyria first devoured the ten tribes, and at last Nebuchadnezzar has broken Judah's bones. Those who pursue the sins of their predecessors, he notes, must expect to be pursued by their plagues. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

18 COMMENTARY: Henry says the king of Babylon must be punished as the king of Assyria was, for pursuing the same course. If they do as their predecessors did, he remarks, let them fare as their predecessors fared. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

19 COMMENTARY: Henry says God will return the captivity and bring Israel again to his own habitation, for the discontinuance of possession was not the destruction of their right to Canaan. They shall feed on Carmel and Bashan, the richest parts of the country, and their souls shall be satisfied, for those who seek first the kingdom of God shall have other things added to them. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

20 COMMENTARY: Henry calls pardon the root of all the rest: Israel's iniquity shall be sought for and there shall be none, and Judah's sins shall not be found, blotted out as a cloud, crossed out as a debt, cast into the depth of the sea. Deliverances out of trouble, he notes, are comforts indeed when they are fruits of the forgiveness of sin, and this pardon includes a thorough reformation of heart and life. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

21 COMMENTARY: Henry identifies Merathaim as the country of the Mardi, lying part in Assyria and part in Armenia, and Pekod as another country Cyrus took on his way to Babylon. The command to waste and utterly destroy after them, doing all God has commanded, matches the word of Cyrus, who shall perform all God's pleasure. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

22 COMMENTARY: Henry treats the sound of battle and of great destruction as the assurance of success for those who do what God commands: they shall accomplish what he threatens, and Babylon shall become a desolation. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

23 COMMENTARY: Henry calls Babylon the hammer of the whole earth, long beating down all nations far and near, and says it is time now that it be cut asunder and broken. The God of the whole earth, he notes, will break the hammer of the whole earth and assert the injured rights of nations. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

24 COMMENTARY: Henry says God has laid a snare for Babylon, and she is taken unaware because she has striven against the LORD, joining issue with him as in law or battle. Those who strive against the LORD, he remarks, will soon find themselves over-matched. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

25 COMMENTARY: Henry says the LORD has opened his armoury and brought forth the weapons of his indignation, with Media and Persia as God's armoury, whence he fetches Cyrus and his officers and armies. Great men, he notes, are but instruments which the great God makes use of to serve his own purposes. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

26 COMMENTARY: Henry says all shall come together against Babylon from the utmost border, for there will be both work and pay enough for them all, and distance of place must not hinder them. Her storehouses shall be opened, her wealth shovelled up as heaps of ruins, and she shall be destroyed utterly, showing how little account the great God makes of what men so much value. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

27 COMMENTARY: Henry reads the bullocks as Babylon's princes and great men, fat and bulky, who shall fall by the sword not as men of war on a bed of honour but as beasts by the butcher's hand. Woe unto them, he adds, for their day has come and they are not aware of it, which makes their case the more sad for the little sense they have of it. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

28 COMMENTARY: Henry says the voice of those who flee and escape out of Babylon declares in Zion the vengeance of the LORD, the vengeance of his temple. The burning of the temple and the carrying away of its vessels were the articles in the charge on which greater stress was laid than on Babylon's being the hammer of the earth, for whatever wrong is done to God's church will certainly be reckoned for. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

29 COMMENTARY: Henry says the archers are called together to camp against Babylon round about and let none escape, recompensing her according to her work and according to all she has done. She has been proud against the LORD, against the Holy One of Israel, insulting him in insulting over his people, and so has made him her enemy. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

30 COMMENTARY: Henry says Babylon's young men shall fall in the streets and all her men of war be cut off in that day which should have been her defence. God is against her, and none can stand before him when he is angry. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

31 COMMENTARY: Henry takes the address, O thou most proud, literally as pride itself, and says the pride of men's hearts sets God against them and ripens them apace for ruin. God resists the proud and will bring them down, for it is his glory to look upon the proud and abase them. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

32 COMMENTARY: Henry says the most proud shall stumble and fall, and none shall raise him up, falling not so much by others thrusting him down as by his own stumbling, since he holds his head so high he never looks under his feet. The fire God kindles in his cities shall devour all round about him, and who can help up those whom God will throw down? — Matthew Henry (Complete)

33 COMMENTARY: Henry notes Israel and Judah were oppressed together, the remnants of the ten tribes having mingled with the two tribes after the kingdoms of Assyria and Chaldea were united. Their captors held them fast and refused to let them go, and they were humble suppliants who could attempt nothing towards their own liberty. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

34 COMMENTARY: Henry calls this the people's comfort in distress: though they are weak, their Redeemer is strong, their Avenger who has a right to them and will make good his claim. The LORD of hosts shall thoroughly plead their cause, pleading it with jealousy and carrying it, that he may give rest to the land and disquiet Babylon's inhabitants. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

35 COMMENTARY: Henry says the Chaldeans had long been God's sword for executing judgment on the nations, but now, being as bad as any of them, a sword of justice is brought upon them. It falls on their princes, whose dignity, wealth and power shall not secure them, and on their wise men, whose learning and policy shall neither secure them nor stand the public in any stead. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

36 COMMENTARY: Henry calls the liars Babylon's soothsayers and astrologers, who cheated with prognostications of peace and prosperity, and says the sword shall make them dote and talk like fools. The sword upon her mighty men shall dismay them, for what stead will their hands stand them in when their hearts fail them? — Matthew Henry (Complete)

37 COMMENTARY: Henry says the sword upon horses and chariots means the invaders shall master all Babylon's warlike stores, seizing or destroying them, while the mingled foreign troops in her service become as weak and timorous as women. The sword upon her treasures, the sinews of war, means they shall be robbed and used by the enemy against her. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

38 COMMENTARY: Henry says the waters that secured Babylon shall be dried up, for Cyrus drew the Euphrates into so many channels that it became passable for his army to reach walls thought inaccessible. He adds that the land of graven images was mad upon its idols, doting on frightful scarecrow images, which he calls the maddest thing in the world, making a god of a creature. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

39 COMMENTARY: Henry says the fruitful country, its waters dried up, shall be turned to barrenness and inhabited no more by men but by wild beasts of the desert and islands and by owls. This was foretold concerning Babylon in Isaiah 13:19-22, that it shall be no more inhabited forever. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

40 COMMENTARY: Henry says Babylon shall become like Sodom and Gomorrah, so that no man shall abide there and no son of man dwell in it. As the Chaldeans had laid Edom waste, he notes, so they shall themselves be laid waste. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

41 COMMENTARY: Henry says the people from the north, the great nation and many kings from the coasts of the earth, shall put Babylon into the utmost confusion and consternation. The expressions for the invaders' terrors are those used before for the Chaldeans invading Judah, to intimate that Babylon shall be paid in her own coin. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

42 COMMENTARY: Henry describes the invaders holding bow and lance, cruel and without mercy, their voice roaring like the sea, riding on horses arrayed as a man for battle. God can find out such as shall be for terror and destruction to those who have been for terror and destruction to others. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

43 COMMENTARY: Henry notes the one difference from the earlier passage: there it is said, We have heard the fame thereof and our hands waxed feeble, but here the king of Babylon himself hears the report and his hands wax feeble. That proud and daring prince, he says, shall in his distress be as weak and dispirited as the meanest Israelites were in theirs. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

44 COMMENTARY: Henry says the lion coming up from the swelling of Jordan is the invader who shall tear and destroy and make Babylon's habitation desolate. These verses repeat the Edom prophecy of chapter 49:19-21 with necessary alterations, showing that though providence may seem unequal for a time, its retributions will be equal at last. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

45 COMMENTARY: Henry says the counsel and purposes of the LORD against Babylon are settled, and surely the least of the flock shall draw them out and make their habitations desolate. When thou shalt make an end to spoil, he notes, thou shalt be spoiled. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

46 COMMENTARY: Henry says the desolation of Babylon shall be so astonishing that at the noise of her taking the earth is moved and the cry is heard among the nations. All the nations about shall be terrified by it. — Matthew Henry (Complete)
