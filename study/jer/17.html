Study Notes — Jeremiah

Chapter 17

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: The sin of Judah is written with a pen of iron, and with the point of a diamond: it is graven upon the table of their heart, and upon the horns of your altars. Henry notes the sins which men commit make little impression on their minds, yet every sin is marked in the book of God: they are all so graven upon the table of the heart that they will all be remembered by the conscience. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

2 COMMENTARY: Whilst their children remember their altars and their groves by the green trees upon the high hills. Henry notes that which is graven in the heart will become plain in the life: men's actions show the desires and purposes of their hearts, and children remembering altars and groves by green trees show the idolatry graven in Judah. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

3 COMMENTARY: O my mountain in the field, I will give thy substance and all thy treasures to the spoil, and thy high places for sin, throughout all thy borders. Henry's spoil is the fatal consequence of idolatry: substance, treasures, and high places given to spoil for sin throughout all borders. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

4 COMMENTARY: And thou, even thyself, shalt discontinue from thine heritage that I gave thee; and I will cause thee to serve thine enemies in the land which thou knowest not: for ye have kindled a fire in mine anger, which shall burn for ever. Henry urges what need we have to humble ourselves before God, who are so vile in his sight, and to depend on his mercy and grace, begging him to search and prove us, not to suffer us to be deceived by our own hearts, but to create in us a clean and holy nature by his Spirit: discontinuance from heritage and service to enemies in an unknown land is the fire kindled by idolatry. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

5 COMMENTARY: Thus says the Lord: Cursed be the man that trusts in man, and makes flesh his arm, and whose heart departs from the Lord. Henry notes he who puts confidence in man shall be like the heath in a desert, a naked tree, a sorry shrub, the product of barren ground, useless and worthless: those who trust to their own righteousness and strength, and think they can do without Christ, make flesh their arm, and their souls cannot prosper in graces or comforts. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

6 COMMENTARY: For he shall be like the heath in the desert, and shall not see when good comes; but shall inhabit the parched places in the wilderness, in a salt land and not inhabited. Henry's heath in the desert, in parched places and salt land, not seeing when good comes, is the cursed man who trusts in man. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

7 COMMENTARY: Blessed is the man that trusts in the Lord, and whose hope the Lord is. Henry notes those who make God their Hope shall flourish like a tree always green, whose leaf does not wither: blessed is the man whose hope the Lord is. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

8 COMMENTARY: For he shall be as a tree planted by the waters, and that spreads out her roots by the river, and shall not see when heat comes, but her leaf shall be green; and shall not be careful in the year of drought, neither shall cease from yielding fruit. Henry notes they shall be fixed in peace and satisfaction of mind, and shall not be anxious in a year of drought: those who make God their Hope have enough in him to make up the want of all creature-comforts, and shall not cease from yielding fruit in holiness and good works. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

9 COMMENTARY: The heart is deceitful above all things, and desperately wicked: who can know it? Henry notes the heart, the conscience of man, in his corrupt and fallen state, is deceitful above all things: it calls evil good, and good evil, and cries peace to those to whom it does not belong. Herein the heart is desperately wicked; it is deadly, it is desperate. The case is bad indeed, if the conscience, which should set right the errors of other faculties, is a leader in the delusion. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

10 COMMENTARY: I the Lord search the heart, I try the reins, even to give every man according to his ways, and according to the fruit of his doings. Henry notes we cannot know our own hearts, nor what they will do in an hour of temptation, and much less can we know the hearts of others, or depend upon them; but whatever wickedness there is in the heart, God sees it, for men may be imposed upon, but God cannot be deceived, and he gives every man according to his ways. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

11 COMMENTARY: As the partridge sits on eggs, and hatches them not; so he that gets riches, and not by right, shall leave them in the midst of his days, and at his end shall be a fool. Henry notes he that gets riches, and not by right, though he may make them his hope, never shall have joy of them: this shows what vexation it is to a worldly man at death, that he must leave his riches behind, but though the wealth will not follow to another world, guilt will, and everlasting torment. The rich man takes pains to get an estate, and sits brooding upon it, but never has any satisfaction in it; by sinful courses it comes to nothing. Let us be wise in time; what we get, let us get it honestly, and what we have, use it charitably, that we may be wise for eternity. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

12 COMMENTARY: A glorious high throne from the beginning is the place of our sanctuary. Henry notes the prophet acknowledges the favour of God in setting up religion: there is fulness of comfort in God, overflowing, ever-flowing fulness, like a fountain, always fresh and clear, like spring-water, while the pleasures of sin are puddle-waters, and the glorious high throne from the beginning is the place of sanctuary. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

13 COMMENTARY: O Lord, the hope of Israel, all that forsake thee shall be ashamed, and they that depart from me shall be written in the earth, because they have forsaken the Lord, the fountain of living waters. Henry's fountain is the Lord himself: forsaking the fountain of living waters, they shall be ashamed and written in the earth, while the fulness of comfort in God flows fresh and clear like spring-water. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

14 COMMENTARY: Heal me, O Lord, and I shall be healed; save me, and I shall be saved: for thou art my praise. Henry notes he prays to God for healing, saving mercy: whatever wounds or diseases we find to be in our hearts and consciences, let us apply to the Lord to heal us, to save us, that our souls may praise his name, for his hands can bind up the troubled conscience, and heal the broken heart, and cure the worst diseases of our nature. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

15 COMMENTARY: Behold, they say unto me, Where is the word of the Lord? Let it come now. Henry notes the malice of the prophet's enemies: they taunt, Where is the word of the Lord, let it come now, scorning the word whose coming they should fear. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

16 COMMENTARY: As for me, I have not hastened from being a pastor to follow thee: neither have I desired the woeful day; thou knowest: that which came out of my lips was right before thee. Henry notes he appeals to God concerning his faithful discharge of the office to which he was called: he humbly begs that God would own and protect him in the work to which he had plainly called him, not having hastened from being a pastor, nor desired the woeful day. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

17 COMMENTARY: Be not a terror unto me: thou art my hope in the day of evil. Henry's hope in the day of evil is God himself: be not a terror to the prophet who appealed for healing and protection, for thou art his hope when evil comes. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

18 COMMENTARY: Let them be confounded that persecute me, but let not me be confounded: let them be dismayed, but let not me be dismayed: bring upon them the day of evil, and destroy them with double destruction. Henry's prayer against persecutors is the prophet's appeal owned: confounded persecutors, undismayed prophet, and double destruction on the day of evil. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

19 COMMENTARY: Thus said the Lord unto me: Go and stand in the gate of the children of the people, whereby the kings of Judah come in, and by the which they go out, and in all the gates of Jerusalem. Henry notes the prophet was to lay before the rulers and the people of Judah the command to keep holy the sabbath day: standing in the people's gate, and in all Jerusalem's gates, he lays the command before kings and people alike. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

20 COMMENTARY: And say unto them: Hear ye the word of the Lord, ye kings of Judah, and all Judah, and all the inhabitants of Jerusalem, that enter in by these gates. Henry's command is to kings, all Judah, and all Jerusalem's inhabitants: hear the word of the Lord concerning the sabbath, and strictly observe the fourth command. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

21 COMMENTARY: Thus says the Lord: Take heed to yourselves, and bear no burden on the sabbath day, nor bring it in by the gates of Jerusalem. Henry warns to take heed, and watch against the profanation of the sabbath: no burden borne on the sabbath, nor brought in by Jerusalem's gates. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

22 COMMENTARY: Neither carry forth a burden out of your houses on the sabbath day, neither do ye any work, but hallow ye the sabbath day, as I commanded your fathers. Henry notes it is a day of rest, and must not be made a day of labour, unless in cases of necessity: let not the soul be burdened with the cares of this world on sabbath days, but hallow the day as commanded to the fathers. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

23 COMMENTARY: But they obeyed not, neither inclined their ear, but made their neck stiff, that they might not hear, nor receive instruction. Henry's test is the sabbath: the degree of strictness with which this ordinance is observed, or the neglect shown towards it, is a good test to find the state of spiritual religion in any land, and stiff necks that will not hear show a land's religion neglected. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

24 COMMENTARY: And it shall come to pass, if ye diligently hearken unto me, says the Lord, to bring in no burden through the gates of this city on the sabbath day, but hallow the sabbath day, to do no work therein. Henry notes if they obeyed this word, their prosperity should be restored: diligent hearkening, no burden through the gates, and a hallowed sabbath are the condition of restored prosperity. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

25 COMMENTARY: Then shall there enter into the gates of this city kings and princes sitting upon the throne of David, riding in chariots and on horses, they, and their princes, the men of Judah, and the inhabitants of Jerusalem: and this city shall remain for ever. Henry's restored prosperity is a city remaining for ever, with kings and princes on David's throne entering its gates: the streams of religion run deep or shallow, according as the banks of the sabbath are kept up or neglected. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

26 COMMENTARY: And they shall come from the cities of Judah, and from the places about Jerusalem, and from the land of Benjamin, and from the plain, and from the mountains, and from the south, bringing burnt offerings, and sacrifices, and meat offerings, and incense, and bringing sacrifices of praise, unto the house of the Lord. Henry's sabbath-keeping land brings offerings from every quarter to the house of the Lord: let all, by their own example and by attention to their families, strive to check the evil of profanation, that national prosperity may be preserved, and, above all, that souls may be saved. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

27 COMMENTARY: But if ye will not hearken unto me to hallow the sabbath day, and not to bear a burden, even entering in at the gates of Jerusalem on the sabbath day; then will I kindle a fire in the gates thereof, and it shall devour the palaces of Jerusalem, and it shall not be quenched. Henry's neglected sabbath ends in unquenchable fire: kindled in the gates, devouring Jerusalem's palaces, never quenched, because the banks of the sabbath were neglected and the streams of religion ran shallow. — Matthew Henry (Concise)
