Study Notes — Jeremiah

Chapter 22

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Thus says the Lord: Go down to the house of the king of Judah, and speak there this word. Henry notes justice is recommended, and destruction threatened in case of disobedience: the prophet is sent down to the king's house itself to speak the word there. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

2 COMMENTARY: And say: Hear the word of the Lord, O king of Judah, that sits upon the throne of David, thou, and thy servants, and thy people that enter in by these gates. Henry notes the king of Judah is spoken to, as sitting upon the throne of David, the man after God's own heart: let him follow his example, that he may have the benefit of the promises made to him, and with him his servants and people who enter by these gates. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

3 COMMENTARY: Thus says the Lord: Execute ye judgement and righteousness, and deliver the spoiled out of the hand of the oppressor: and do no wrong, do no violence to the stranger, the fatherless, nor the widow, neither shed innocent blood in this place. Henry notes the way to preserve a government is to do the duty of it: execute judgement and righteousness, deliver the spoiled from the oppressor, and do no wrong or violence to stranger, fatherless, widow, nor shed innocent blood. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

4 COMMENTARY: For if ye do this thing indeed, then shall there enter in by the gates of this house kings sitting upon the throne of David, riding in chariots and on horses, he, and his servants, and his people. Henry's preserved government is a succession of kings on David's throne entering by the gates, riding in chariots and on horses, with servants and people: doing the duty preserves the dignity. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

5 COMMENTARY: But if ye will not hear these words, I swear by myself, says the Lord, that this house shall become a desolation. Henry warns sin will be the ruin of the houses of princes, as well as of meaner men: if they will not hear, the house sworn by God himself shall become a desolation. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

6 COMMENTARY: For thus says the Lord unto the king's house of Judah: Thou art Gilead unto me, and the head of Lebanon: yet surely I will make thee a wilderness, and cities which are not inhabited. Henry's Gilead and head of Lebanon, made wilderness and uninhabited cities, show how little earthly grandeur is to be depended upon: the king's house, for all its height, becomes wilderness. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

7 COMMENTARY: And I will prepare destroyers against thee, every one with his weapons: and they shall cut down thy choice cedars, and cast them into the fire. Henry asks who can contend with destroyers of God's preparing? Every one with his weapons, cutting choice cedars and casting them into fire, are destroyers prepared by God himself. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

8 COMMENTARY: And many nations shall pass by this city, and they shall say every man to his neighbour, Wherefore has the Lord done thus unto this great city? Henry notes God destroys neither persons, cities, nor nations, except for sin: many nations passing by shall ask why the Lord did thus to this great city, and the answer shall be sin. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

9 COMMENTARY: Then they shall answer: Because they have forsaken the covenant of the Lord their God, and worshipped other gods, and served them. Henry notes even in this world he often makes it plain for what crimes he sends punishment, and it will be clear at the day of judgement: the answer is forsaking the covenant, worshipping and serving other gods. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

10 COMMENTARY: Weep ye not for the dead, neither bemoan him: but weep sore for him that goes away: for he shall return no more, nor see his native country. Henry notes here is a sentence of death upon two kings, the wicked sons of a very pious father: Josiah was prevented from seeing the evil to come in this world, and removed to see the good to come in the other world, therefore weep not for him, but for his son Shallum, who is likely to live and die a wretched captive. Dying saints may be justly envied, while living sinners are justly pitied. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

11 COMMENTARY: For thus says the Lord touching Shallum the son of Josiah king of Judah, which reigned instead of Josiah his father, which went forth out of this place: He shall not return thither any more. Henry's Shallum shall not return: gone forth out of this place, he shall return no more, and the wretched captive's sentence is fixed. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

12 COMMENTARY: But he shall die in the place whither they have led him captive, and shall see this land no more. Henry's captive dies where he was led captive, and sees this land no more: the sentence of death upon the king is exile without return. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

13 COMMENTARY: Woe unto him that builds his house by unrighteousness, and his chambers by wrong; that uses his neighbour's service without wages, and gives him not for his work. Henry notes here also is the doom of Jehoiakim: he built his houses by unrighteousness, with money gotten unjustly, and he defrauded his workmen of their wages. God notices the wrong done by the greatest to poor servants and labourers, and will repay those in justice, who will not, in justice, pay those whom they employ. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

14 COMMENTARY: That says: I will build me a wide house and large chambers, and cuts him out windows; and it is cieled with cedar, and painted with vermilion. Henry notes no doubt it is lawful for princes and great men to build, beautify, and furnish houses; but those who enlarge their houses, and make them sumptuous, need carefully to watch against the workings of vain-glory: the wide house, large chambers, cedar ceiling, and vermilion paint are vain-glory built by wrong. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

15 COMMENTARY: Shalt thou reign, because thou closest thyself in cedar? Did not thy father eat and drink, and do judgement and justice, and then it was well with him? Henry notes the children who despise their parents' old fashions commonly come short of their real excellences: Jehoiakim knew that his father found the way of duty to be the way of comfort, yet he would not tread in his steps, and cedar-closing is no reigning. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

16 COMMENTARY: He judged the cause of the poor and needy; then it was well with him: was not this to know me? Says the Lord. Henry's father judged the poor and needy, and it was well with him, for this was to know the Lord: knowing God is judging the poor's cause, not building wide houses. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

17 COMMENTARY: But thine eyes and thine heart are not but for thy covetousness, and for to shed innocent blood, and for oppression, and for violence, to do it. Henry notes covetousness, which is the root of all evil, was at the bottom of all: eyes and heart for covetousness, innocent blood, oppression, and violence, and Jehoiakim was unjust, and made no conscience of shedding innocent blood. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

18 COMMENTARY: Therefore thus says the Lord concerning Jehoiakim the son of Josiah king of Judah: They shall not lament for him, saying, Ah my brother! Or, Ah sister! They shall not lament for him, saying, Ah lord! Or, Ah his glory! Henry notes he shall die unlamented, hateful for oppression and cruelty: no brother, sister, lord, or glory lamented for him. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

19 COMMENTARY: He shall be buried with the burial of an ass, drawn and cast forth beyond the gates of Jerusalem. Henry's unlamented king is buried with an ass's burial, drawn and cast forth beyond Jerusalem's gates: the greatest of men must look upon the meanest as their neighbours, and be just to them accordingly, or die as beasts. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

20 COMMENTARY: Go up to Lebanon, and cry; and lift up thy voice in Bashan, and cry from the passages: for all thy lovers are destroyed. Henry notes the Jewish state is described under a threefold character: very haughty in a day of peace and safety, very fearful on alarm of trouble, very much cast down under pressure of trouble, and now all lovers destroyed, with cries from Lebanon, Bashan, and the passages. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

21 COMMENTARY: I spoke unto thee in thy prosperity; but thou saidst, I will not hear. This has been thy manner from thy youth, that thou obeyedst not my voice. Henry notes many never are ashamed of their sins till brought by them to the last extremity: spoken to in prosperity, answering I will not hear, from youth obeying not his voice, is the manner that ends in extremity. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

22 COMMENTARY: The wind shall eat up all thy pastors, and thy lovers shall go into captivity: surely then shalt thou be ashamed and confounded for all thy wickedness. Henry's wind-eaten pastors and captive lovers bring shame and confounding for all wickedness: ashamed at last, but only at the extremity. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

23 COMMENTARY: O inhabitant of Lebanon, that makes thy nest in the cedars, how gracious shalt thou be when pangs come upon thee, the pain as of a woman in travail! Henry's cedar-nest dweller, haughty in peace, shall be gracious indeed when pangs come as a woman in travail: the nest in the cedars does not spare the pangs. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

24 COMMENTARY: As I live, says the Lord, though Coniah the son of Jehoiakim king of Judah were the signet upon my right hand, yet would I pluck thee thence. Henry warns those that think themselves as signets on God's right hand must not be secure, but fear lest they should be plucked thence: even the signet on the right hand shall be plucked off. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

25 COMMENTARY: And I will give thee into the hand of them that seek thy life, and into the hand of them whose face thou fearest, even into the hand of Nebuchadrezzar king of Babylon, and into the hand of the Chaldeans. Henry's king and his family shall be carried to Babylon: given into the hand of life-seekers and feared faces, Babylon and the Chaldeans. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

26 COMMENTARY: And I will cast thee out, and thy mother that bare thee, into another country, where ye were not born; and there shall ye die. Henry notes we know where we were born, but where we shall die we know not; it is enough that our God knows: cast out with his mother into another country, where they were not born, there shall they die, and the king shall close his days in bondage. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

27 COMMENTARY: But to the land whereunto they desire to return, thither shall they not return. Henry's desired land is denied: to the land they desire to return, thither shall they not return, for the king's bondage has no return. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

28 COMMENTARY: Is this man Coniah a despised broken idol? Is he a vessel wherein is no pleasure? Wherefore are they cast out, he and his seed, and are cast into a land which they know not? Henry notes the Jewish king shall be despised: time was when he was delighted in, but all those in whom God has no pleasure, some time or other, will be so lowered, that men will have no pleasure in them, and Coniah becomes a despised broken idol, a vessel wherein is no pleasure. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

29 COMMENTARY: O earth, earth, earth, hear the word of the Lord. Henry's triple call summons earth itself to hear: O earth, earth, earth, hear the word of the Lord, for the doom of the royal family is spoken to all the earth. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

30 COMMENTARY: Thus says the Lord: Write ye this man childless, a man that shall not prosper in his days: for no man of his seed shall prosper, sitting upon the throne of David, and ruling any more in Judah. Henry closes: whoever are childless, it is the Lord that writes them so, and those who take no care to do good in their days cannot expect to prosper. How little is earthly grandeur to be depended upon, or flourishing families to be rejoiced in! But those who hear the voice of Christ, and follow him, have eternal life, and shall never perish, neither shall any enemy pluck them out of his almighty hands. — Matthew Henry (Concise)
