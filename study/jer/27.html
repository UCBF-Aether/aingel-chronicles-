Study Notes — Jeremiah

Chapter 27

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: In the beginning of the reign of Jehoiakim the son of Josiah king of Judah came this word unto Jeremiah from the Lord, saying. Henry notes Jeremiah is to prepare a sign that all the neighbouring countries would be made subject to the king of Babylon: the word in Jehoiakim's beginning prepares the yoke-sign. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

2 COMMENTARY: Thus says the Lord to me: Make thee bonds and yokes, and put them upon thy neck. Henry's sign is bonds and yokes put upon his own neck: the prophet wears the subjection he preaches, that all neighbouring countries may see it. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

3 COMMENTARY: And send them to the king of Edom, and to the king of Moab, and to the king of the Ammonites, and to the king of Tyrus, and to the king of Zidon, by the hand of the messengers which come to Jerusalem unto Zedekiah king of Judah. Henry's yokes are sent to Edom, Moab, Ammon, Tyre, and Zidon by messengers come to Zedekiah: the sign goes to every neighbouring king. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

4 COMMENTARY: And command them to say unto their masters: Thus says the Lord of hosts, the God of Israel; Thus shall ye say unto your masters. Henry's command to say unto masters is God's asserted right: the Lord of hosts, God of Israel, speaks to every master through the yoke. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

5 COMMENTARY: I have made the earth, the man and the beast that are upon the ground, by my great power and by my outstretched arm, and have given it unto whom it seemed meet unto me. Henry notes God asserts his right to dispose of kingdoms as he pleases: whatever any have of the good things of this world, it is what God sees fit to give, and we should therefore be content, for the Maker of earth, man, and beast gives it to whom it seems meet to him. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

6 COMMENTARY: And now have I given all these lands into the hand of Nebuchadnezzar the king of Babylon, my servant; and the beasts of the field have I given him also to serve him. Henry notes the things of this world are not the best things, for the Lord often gives the largest share to bad men, and dominion is not founded in grace: all lands, and beasts of the field, are given to Nebuchadnezzar, God's servant for this work. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

7 COMMENTARY: And all nations shall serve him, and his son, and his son's son, until the very time of his land come: and then many nations and great kings shall serve themselves of him. Henry's service lasts son and son's son, until his land's time comes, when many nations and great kings serve themselves of him: dominion given is dominion limited. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

8 COMMENTARY: And it shall come to pass, that the nation and kingdom which will not serve the same Nebuchadnezzar the king of Babylon, and that will not put their neck under the yoke of the king of Babylon, that nation will I punish, says the Lord, with the sword, and with the famine, and with the pestilence, until I have consumed them by his hand. Henry notes those who will not serve the God who made them shall justly be made to serve their enemies that seek to ruin them: the unyoked nation is punished with sword, famine, and pestilence until consumed by Babylon's hand. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

9 COMMENTARY: Therefore hearken not ye to your prophets, nor to your diviners, nor to your dreamers, nor to your enchanters, nor to your sorcerers, which speak unto you, saying, Ye shall not serve the king of Babylon. Henry urges them to prevent their destruction by submission: hearken not to prophets, diviners, dreamers, enchanters, and sorcerers who promise no service to Babylon, for their promise is destruction's road. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

10 COMMENTARY: For they prophesy a lie unto you, to remove you far from your land; and that I should drive you out, and ye should perish. Henry's lying prophets prophesy removal far from land, driving out, and perishing: the lie that promises liberty delivers exile. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

11 COMMENTARY: But the nations that bring their neck under the yoke of the king of Babylon, and serve him, those will I let remain still in their own land, says the Lord; and they shall till it, and dwell therein. Henry notes a meek spirit, by quiet submission to the hardest turns of providence, makes the best of what is bad: many persons may escape destroying providences by submitting to humbling providences, and the yoked nations remain, till, and dwell in their own land. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

12 COMMENTARY: I spoke also to Zedekiah king of Judah according to all these words, saying: Bring your necks under the yoke of the king of Babylon, and serve him and his people, and live. Henry notes Jeremiah persuades the king of Judah to surrender to the king of Babylon: bring necks under the yoke, serve him and his people, and live, for it is better to take up a light cross in our way than to pull a heavier on our own heads. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

13 COMMENTARY: Why will ye die, thou and thy people, by the sword, by the famine, and by the pestilence, as the Lord has spoken against the nation that will not serve the king of Babylon? Henry asks: is it their wisdom to submit to the heavy iron yoke of a cruel tyrant, that they may secure their lives; and is it not much more our wisdom to submit to the pleasant and easy yoke of our Lord and Master, Jesus Christ, that we may secure our souls? Why die by sword, famine, and pestilence, when submission is life? — Matthew Henry (Concise)

14 COMMENTARY: Therefore hearken not unto the words of the prophets that speak unto you, saying, Ye shall not serve the king of Babylon: for they prophesy a lie unto you. Henry's hearken-not is repeated for Zedekiah: the prophets promising no service prophesy a lie, and the lie is death. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

15 COMMENTARY: For I have not sent them, says the Lord, yet they prophesy a lie in my name; that I might drive you out, and that ye might perish, ye, and the prophets that prophesy unto you. Henry warns those who encourage sinners to go on in sinful ways will perish with them: unsent prophets prophesying lies in God's name drive king and prophets alike to perishing. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

16 COMMENTARY: Also I spoke to the priests and to all this people, saying: Thus says the Lord; Hearken not to the words of your prophets that prophesy unto you, saying, Behold, the vessels of the Lord's house shall now shortly be brought again from Babylon: for they prophesy a lie unto you. Henry's priests and people hear the same hearken-not: the vessels shortly brought again from Babylon is a lie prophesied to them. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

17 COMMENTARY: Hearken not unto them; serve the king of Babylon, and live: wherefore should this city be laid waste? Henry asks why should they die the second death, infinitely worse than that by sword and famine, when they may submit and live? Hearken not, serve Babylon, and live, for wherefore should this city be laid waste? — Matthew Henry (Concise)

18 COMMENTARY: But if they be prophets, and if the word of the Lord be with them, let them now make intercession to the Lord of hosts, that the vessels which are left in the house of the Lord, and in the house of the king of Judah, and at Jerusalem, go not to Babylon. Henry's test is intercession: if they be prophets with the Lord's word, let them intercede that the left vessels go not to Babylon, for true prophets pray, and liars only promise. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

19 COMMENTARY: For thus says the Lord of hosts concerning the pillars, and concerning the sea, and concerning the bases, and concerning the residue of the vessels that remain in this city. Henry notes Jeremiah assures them that the brazen vessels should go after the golden ones: pillars, sea, bases, and residue vessels remaining in the city are named for Babylon. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

20 COMMENTARY: Which Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon took not, when he carried away captive Jeconiah the son of Jehoiakim king of Judah, from Jerusalem to Babylon, and all the nobles of Judah and Jerusalem. Henry's vessels not taken at Jeconiah's captivity, with all Judah and Jerusalem's nobles, are the residue now named: what Babylon left then, Babylon takes now. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

21 COMMENTARY: Yea, thus says the Lord of hosts, the God of Israel, concerning the vessels that remain in the house of the Lord, and in the house of the king of Judah and of Jerusalem. Henry's all shall be carried to Babylon: vessels remaining in the Lord's house, the king's house, and Jerusalem are all for Babylon. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

22 COMMENTARY: They shall be carried to Babylon, and there shall they be until the day that I visit them, says the Lord; then will I bring them up, and restore them to this place. Henry concludes with a gracious promise, that the time would come when they should be brought back: though the return of the prosperity of the church does not come in our time, we must not despair, for it will come in God's time, and the vessels carried to Babylon shall be visited, brought up, and restored to this place. — Matthew Henry (Concise)
