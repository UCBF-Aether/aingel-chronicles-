Study Notes — Jeremiah

Chapter 42

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Henry notes they came near, which intimates that hitherto they had kept at a distance from the prophet and been shy of him. Though brands plucked out of the fire, they had not returned to the Lord, and their contempt of God and his prophets remained the sin that most easily beset them; now, to serve a turn, Jeremiah is sought out by captains and people alike. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

2 COMMENTARY: Henry notes they express themselves wonderfully well: with great respect to the prophet, applying humbly as petitioners while intimating their own unworthiness, and with a great opinion of his interest in heaven, asking him to pray for those who know not how to pray for themselves. They speak of themselves as objects of compassion, a remnant but a few of many, easily swallowed up, and beg direction in their plunge. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

3 COMMENTARY: Henry notes their request is that the Lord take their ruin into his thoughts and show them the way wherein they may walk and the thing they may do for their own safety. In every difficult, doubtful case, he says, our eye must be up to God for direction, and we may still pray in faith to be guided by wisdom and the hints of providence. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

4 COMMENTARY: Henry notes Jeremiah faithfully promises to pray for direction and to deliver whatever message God sends just as he receives it, without adding, altering, or diminishing. Ministers may hence learn to pray conscientiously for those who desire their prayers, as Samuel did when slighted, and to advise as near as they can to the mind of God, keeping back nothing profitable whether pleasing or not. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

5 COMMENTARY: Henry notes they appeal to the Lord as a true and faithful witness, with the impudence to appeal to God concerning their sincerity while at the same time they dissembled. Those who expect the benefit of good ministers' prayers, he says, must conscientiously hearken to their preaching and be governed by it as far as it agrees with the mind of God. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

6 COMMENTARY: Henry notes their promise is an implicit, universal obedience: whether the command seem good or evil to them, they will believe that if God commands it, it is certainly good, and must not be disputed but done. It rests on a good consideration, that it may be well with them, intimating their conviction that on no other terms could they expect it to be well. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

7 COMMENTARY: Henry notes the answer did not come immediately, not till ten days after, holding them long in suspense, perhaps to punish their hypocrisy or to show that Jeremiah did not speak of himself, since he could not speak when he would but must wait for instructions. It teaches us to continue waiting upon God for direction: the vision is for an appointed time, and at the end it shall speak. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

8 COMMENTARY: Henry notes that when the word came, Jeremiah delivered it publicly to Johanan, all the captains, and all the people from the least to the greatest, fully and faithfully as he had promised, keeping nothing back. If he had directed them by his own prudence the case was so difficult he might not have known what to advise; what he advises is what the Lord the God of Israel says. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

9 COMMENTARY: Henry notes Jeremiah opens with the authority of the God of Israel, unto whom they sent him to present their supplication, so that they were bound in honour and duty to observe it. The message that follows is not the prophet's own counsel but the answer of the God they themselves had consulted. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

10 COMMENTARY: Henry notes God would have them still abide in this land, counting it a mercy and a duty to stay where their brethren were forced out, and he persuades them as a friend rather than commanding as a prince. His tender concern appears in, I repent me of the evil: not that he changed his mind, but he was ready to change his way and return in mercy, building and planting them as a phoenix-kingdom rising out of the ashes of the last. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

11 COMMENTARY: Henry notes this answers their fear of the king of Babylon, a man of great might and little mercy whose will is a law, lest he avenge Gedaliah's death on them though they witnessed against it. Be not afraid, for that fear will bring a snare, and I am with you: if God be for you to save you, who can be against you to hurt you? — Matthew Henry (Complete)

12 COMMENTARY: Henry notes God will show mercies unto them, so that the king of Babylon himself shall have mercy upon them and cause them to return to their own land and habitations. Whatever kindness men do us we must attribute to God's kindness, he says: God has made that our duty which is really our privilege, and obedience will be its own recompence; do not forsake your own mercies for lying vanities. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

13 COMMENTARY: Henry notes the sin supposed here is their resolve, We will not dwell in this land, not though God himself undertake their protection, nor continue in it in obedience to the voice of the Lord. He may say what he pleases, they say in effect, but we will do what we please: a refusal that turns a conditional warning into an accusation. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

14 COMMENTARY: Henry notes their reason for Egypt is that there they shall see no war, nor hear the trumpet, nor have hunger of bread. It is folly to quit our place, especially the holy land, because we meet trouble in it, he says, but greater folly to think that by changing our place we can escape the judgments of God, since evil pursues sinners in every way of disobedience and is escaped only by returning to allegiance. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

15 COMMENTARY: Henry notes the sentence is pronounced in God's name upon the remnant of Judah, who think that because they are a remnant they must be spared of course and indulged in their own humour. If they wholly set their faces to enter Egypt and obstinately resolve to sojourn there though God opposes them by word and providence, then they must take what follows. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

16 COMMENTARY: Henry notes the very judgments that frightened them shall pursue them into Egypt: the sword they feared shall overtake them there, and the famine they dreaded shall follow close after them. The evils we think to escape by sin, he says, we certainly and inevitably run ourselves upon. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

17 COMMENTARY: Henry notes all who set their faces to sojourn in Egypt shall die by sword, famine, and pestilence, with none remaining or escaping the evil God will bring upon them. Those impatient of common calamities who think to avoid them by changing place will find they do not better themselves: all our removes in this world are but from one wilderness to another. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

18 COMMENTARY: Henry notes they shall meet the second part of Jerusalem's desolations in Egypt: as God's anger and fury were poured forth upon Jerusalem, so they shall be poured forth upon them there, leaving them an execration, an astonishment, a curse, and a reproach, never to see this place again. Those who have by sin made God their enemy will find him a consuming fire wherever they go. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

19 COMMENTARY: Henry notes the conclusion of the whole matter is a solemn protest that the message has been faithfully delivered: Go not into Egypt, for to do so disobeys God's command, and what has been said will be a witness against them. They cannot now plead ignorance of the mind of God, since they have been plainly admonished this day. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

20 COMMENTARY: Henry notes God charges them with base dissimulation in their application for divine direction: they professed one thing and intended another, promising what they never meant to perform. They used deceit against their own souls, for those who think to put a cheat upon God will prove in the end to have put a damning cheat upon themselves. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

21 COMMENTARY: Henry notes Jeremiah declares the message this day, yet they have not obeyed the voice of the Lord nor anything for which he was sent to them. God, who knew their hearts, was already aware they were determined to go contrary to his command, probably discovering it in their countenance and secret mutterings before the discourse had finished. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

22 COMMENTARY: Henry notes the doom is now ratified absolutely: they shall die by sword, famine, and pestilence in the very place whither they desire to go and sojourn. God's threatenings may be vilified but cannot be nullified by human unbelief, for there is no place privileged from divine arrests, and what our hearts are most set upon often proves afflictive, and sometimes fatal. — Matthew Henry (Complete)
