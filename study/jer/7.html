Study Notes — Jeremiah

Chapter 7

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: The word that came to Jeremiah from the Lord opens the temple sermon. Henry frames it: no observances, professions, or supposed revelations will profit if men do not amend their ways and doings. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: Jeremiah must stand in the gate of the Lord's house and proclaim the word to all Judah entering to worship. Henry notes the sermon meets worshippers at the gate, where their confidence in the temple is strongest. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: Amend your ways and your doings, and God will cause you to dwell in this place. Henry stresses none can claim an interest in free salvation while allowing themselves in known sin or living in neglect of known duty. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: Trust not in lying words, saying, 'The temple of the Lord, The temple of the Lord, The temple of the Lord, are these.' Henry observes they thought the temple they profaned would be their protection, but a profaned temple protects no one. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: If ye thoroughly amend your ways and doings, and thoroughly execute judgment between a man and his neighbour, then the promise follows. Henry's condition is thoroughness: amendment must reach both ways and doings, not profession only. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: If ye oppress not the stranger, the fatherless, and the widow, shed not innocent blood in this place, and walk not after other gods to your hurt, then God will cause you to dwell in the land given to their fathers for ever. Henry notes the amendment is concrete: justice to the helpless, innocent blood unstained, and no gods followed to their own hurt. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: Then will God cause them to dwell in this place, in the land given to their fathers for ever and ever. Henry holds out the promise as the fruit of amendment: dwelling follows doing, not saying. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: Behold, ye trust in lying words that cannot profit. Henry warns that observances without amendment cannot profit: lying words are exactly what temple confidence without obedience becomes. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: Will ye steal, murder, commit adultery, swear falsely, burn incense to Baal, and walk after other gods whom ye know not, and then claim deliverance? Henry names the poisonous sentiment: continuing in sin because grace has abounded, or that grace may abound, makes Christ the minister of sin. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: And come and stand before God in this house called by his name, saying, 'We are delivered to do all these abominations?' Henry answers with the cross rightly understood: the Son of God gave himself for transgressions to show the excellence of the divine law and the evil of sin, the most effectual remedy to such sentiments. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: Is this house called by God's name become a den of robbers in your eyes? Behold, even God has seen it. Henry warns never to think wickedness can be done without suffering for it: the den is seen, whatever the worshippers claim. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: Go now to God's place in Shiloh, where he set his name at the first, and see what he did to it for the wickedness of Israel. Henry points to Shiloh as the precedent: a place where God's name once dwelt was not spared when its people were wicked. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: Because ye have done all these works, and God spoke, rising up early, but ye heard not, and called, but ye answered not, therefore judgment follows. Henry notes the aggravation: early and repeated speech met with deaf ears and silent mouths. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: God will do to this house called by his name, wherein they trust, and to the place given to them and their fathers, as he has done to Shiloh. Henry's point is that trust in the house cannot save the house when its people will not amend. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: God will cast them out of his sight, as he has cast out all their brethren, even the whole seed of Ephraim. Henry notes the casting out of Ephraim is the pattern: Judah will follow the same road if she follows the same sins. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: Therefore pray not for this people, neither lift up cry nor prayer nor intercession, for God will not hear. Henry's section closes the first movement: when amendment is refused, even intercession is stayed until repentance comes. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: Seest thou not what they do in the cities of Judah and in the streets of Jerusalem? Henry turns to the evidence: the idolatry is public, in cities and streets, not hidden. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: The children gather wood, the fathers kindle the fire, and the women knead dough to make cakes to the queen of heaven and pour drink offerings to other gods, to provoke God to anger. Henry notes the Jews took pride in showing zeal for their idols, whole families enlisted, and bids us learn earnestness in God's service even from this bad example. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: Do they provoke God to anger? Do they not provoke themselves to the confusion of their own faces? Henry observes the direct tendency of the sin is malice against God, but it will hurt themselves: idolatry confounds its own makers. — Matthew Henry

20 COMMENTARY: God's anger and fury shall be poured out upon this place, upon man, beast, trees of the field, and fruit of the ground, burning unquenched. Henry warns there is no escaping: God's wrath is fire unquenchable, poured out on the whole place the idolatry filled. — Matthew Henry

21 COMMENTARY: Put your burnt offerings unto your sacrifices, and eat flesh, says the Lord of hosts. Henry introduces the next section: since sacrifice without obedience is rejected, the offerings may as well be eaten as common flesh. — Matthew Henry

22 COMMENTARY: God did not speak to their fathers nor command them, in the day he brought them out of Egypt, concerning burnt offerings or sacrifices. Henry shows obedience was required first: what God commanded was to hearken diligently to the voice of the Lord. — Matthew Henry

23 COMMENTARY: This thing God commanded: 'Obey my voice, and I will be your God, and ye shall be my people; walk in all the ways I have commanded you, that it may be well unto you.' Henry calls the promise very encouraging: let God's will be your rule, and his favour shall be your happiness. — Matthew Henry

24 COMMENTARY: But they hearkened not, nor inclined their ear, but walked in the counsels and imagination of their evil heart, going backward and not forward. Henry notes God was displeased with disobedience: they walked by their own counsels instead of his voice. — Matthew Henry

25 COMMENTARY: Since the day their fathers came out of Egypt, God has sent all his servants the prophets, daily rising up early and sending them. Henry stresses the persistence of the sending: prophet after prophet, morning after morning. — Matthew Henry

26 COMMENTARY: Yet they hearkened not, nor inclined their ear, but hardened their neck, doing worse than their fathers. Henry observes each generation hardened further: disobedience grew, it did not lessen. — Matthew Henry

27 COMMENTARY: Jeremiah shall speak all these words to them, but they will not hearken; he shall call, but they will not answer. Henry's section leaves the prophet with a hard commission: speak faithfully to ears that will not hear. — Matthew Henry

28 COMMENTARY: Thou shalt say to them: 'This is a nation that obeys not the voice of the Lord their God, nor receives correction; truth is perished and cut off from their mouth.' Henry concludes that we understand the gospel as little as the Jews understood the law if we think even Christ's sacrifice lessens our obligation to obey. — Matthew Henry

29 COMMENTARY: Cut off thine hair, O Jerusalem, and cast it away, taking up a lamentation on high places, for the Lord has rejected and forsaken the generation of his wrath. Henry reads the shorn hair as a token both of sorrow and of slavery: Jerusalem must be degraded and separated from God, as she had been separated to him. — Matthew Henry

30 COMMENTARY: The children of Judah have done evil in God's sight, setting their abominations in the house called by his name, to pollute it. Henry notes the heart is the place where God has chosen to put his name, but if sin has the innermost and uppermost place there, we pollute the temple of the Lord. — Matthew Henry

31 COMMENTARY: They have built the high places of Tophet in the valley of the son of Hinnom, to burn their sons and daughters in the fire, which God commanded not, neither came it into his heart. Henry's section makes the destruction terrible in proportion: the place of their sin becomes the place of their slain. — Matthew Henry

32 COMMENTARY: The days come when it shall no more be called Tophet, nor the valley of the son of Hinnom, but the valley of slaughter, for they shall bury in Tophet till there be no place. Henry notes evil pursues sinners even after death: the burial ground itself becomes their monument. — Matthew Henry

33 COMMENTARY: The carcasses of this people shall be meat for fowls of heaven and beasts of the earth, and none shall fray them away. Henry stresses the completeness of the desolation: even burial is denied, and the unburied dead feed the creatures. — Matthew Henry

34 COMMENTARY: God will cause to cease from Judah's cities and Jerusalem's streets the voice of mirth and gladness, the voice of bridegroom and bride, for the land shall be desolate. Henry closes that those who will not by grace be cured of vain mirth shall by justice be deprived of all mirth. — Matthew Henry
