Study Notes — Jeremiah

Chapter 12

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Righteous art thou, O Lord, when I plead with thee: yet let me talk with thee of thy judgements: Wherefore doth the way of the wicked prosper? Wherefore are all they happy that deal very treacherously? Henry counsels that when we are most in the dark concerning God's dispensations, we must keep up right thoughts of God, believing that he never did the least wrong to any of his creatures, and abide by this first principle: the Lord is righteous. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

2 COMMENTARY: Thou hast planted them, yea, they have taken root: they grow, yea, they bring forth fruit: thou art near in their mouth, and far from their reins. Henry notes the God with whom we have to do knows how our hearts are toward him: he knows both the guile of the hypocrite, near in mouth but far in heart, and the sincerity of the upright. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

3 COMMENTARY: But thou, O Lord, knowest me: thou hast seen me, and tried mine heart toward thee: pull them out like sheep for the slaughter, and prepare them for the day of slaughter. Henry notes divine judgements would pull the wicked out of their pasture as sheep for the slaughter: tried and known by God, Jeremiah asks that the planted wicked be pulled out. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

4 COMMENTARY: How long shall the land mourn, and the herbs of every field wither, for the wickedness of them that dwell therein? The beasts are consumed, and the birds; because they said, He shall not see our last end. Henry observes this fruitful land was turned into barrenness for the wickedness of those that dwelt therein: the land mourns and the herbs wither because of its dwellers. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

5 COMMENTARY: If thou hast run with the footmen, and they have wearied thee, then how canst thou contend with horses? And if in the land of peace, wherein thou trustedst, they wearied thee, then how wilt thou do in the swelling of Jordan? Henry notes the Lord reproved the prophet: the opposition of the men of Anathoth was not so formidable as what he must expect from the rulers of Judah, so footmen wearying him warns of horses to come. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

6 COMMENTARY: For even thy brethren, and the house of thy father, even they have dealt treacherously with thee; yea, they have called a multitude after thee: believe them not, though they speak fair words unto thee. Henry warns our grief that there should be so much evil is often mixed with peevishness on account of the trials it occasions us: even brethren and father's house deal treacherously, and fair words from them are not to be believed. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

7 COMMENTARY: I have forsaken mine house, I have left mine heritage; I have given the dearly beloved of my soul into the hand of her enemies. Henry notes God's people had been the dearly-beloved of his soul, precious in his sight, but they acted so that he gave them up to their enemies: the forsaken house is the beloved given over. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

8 COMMENTARY: Mine heritage is unto me as a lion in the forest; it crieth out against me: therefore have I hated it. Henry's heritage turned lion: the people cried out against God, and so he gave them up, for they had made themselves hateful by their own roaring. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

9 COMMENTARY: Mine heritage is unto me as a speckled bird, the birds round about are against her; come ye, assemble all the beasts of the field, come to devour. Henry observes many professing churches become like speckled birds, presenting a mixture of religion and the world, with its vain fashions, pursuits, and pollutions; God's people are as men wondered at, as a speckled bird, but this people had by their own folly made themselves so, and the beasts and birds are called to prey upon them. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

10 COMMENTARY: Many pastors have destroyed my vineyard, they have trodden my portion under foot, they have made my pleasant portion a desolate wilderness. Henry's pastors destroyed the vineyard and trod the portion under foot: the pleasant portion became a wilderness by its own shepherds. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

11 COMMENTARY: They have made it desolate, and being desolate it mourneth unto me; the whole land is made desolate, because no man layeth it to heart. Henry warns that until the judgements were actually inflicted, none of the people would lay the warning to heart: the desolate land mourns because no man laid it to heart. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

12 COMMENTARY: The spoilers are come upon all high places through the wilderness: for the sword of the Lord shall devour from the one end of the land even to the other end of the land: no flesh shall have peace. Henry warns that when God's hand is lifted up, and men will not see, they shall be made to feel: the sword devours from end to end, and no flesh has peace. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

13 COMMENTARY: They have sown wheat, but shall reap thorns: they have put themselves to pain, but shall not profit: and they shall be ashamed of your revenues because of the fierce anger of the Lord. Henry notes silver and gold shall not profit in the day of the Lord's anger, and the efforts of sinners to escape misery, without repentance and works answerable thereto, will end in confusion: wheat sown reaps thorns, and pain profits nothing. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

14 COMMENTARY: Thus says the Lord against all mine evil neighbours, that touch the inheritance which I have caused my people Israel to inherit: behold, I will pluck them out of their land, and pluck out the house of Judah from among them. Henry notes the Lord would plead the cause of his people against their evil neighbours: the neighbours who touched the inheritance shall themselves be plucked out. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

15 COMMENTARY: And it shall come to pass, after that I have plucked them out I will return, and have compassion on them, and will bring them again, every man to his heritage, and every man to his land. Henry notes the Lord would afterwards show mercy to those nations, when they should learn true religion: plucked out, they shall yet be returned in compassion to heritage and land. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

16 COMMENTARY: And it shall come to pass, if they will diligently learn the ways of my people, to swear by my name, The Lord liveth; as they taught my people to swear by Baal; then shall they be built in the midst of my people. Henry observes this seems to look forward to the times when the fulness of the Gentiles shall come in: those who would have their lot with God's people, and a last end like theirs, must learn their ways, and walk in them, even swearing by the Lord's name as once they taught Baal's. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

17 COMMENTARY: But if they will not obey, I will utterly pluck up and destroy that nation, says the Lord. Henry's condition stands: those who will not learn God's ways and walk in them shall be utterly plucked up and destroyed, for the lot with God's people is learned, not claimed. — Matthew Henry (Concise)
