Study Notes — Jeremiah

Chapter 38

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Then Shephatiah the son of Mattan, and Gedaliah the son of Pashur, and Jucal the son of Shelemiah, and Pashur the son of Malchiah, heard the words that Jeremiah had spoken unto all the people, saying. Henry notes Jeremiah went on in his plain preaching: Shephatiah, Gedaliah, Jucal, and Pashur heard the words Jeremiah spoke unto all the people, and plain preaching is heard by princes. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

2 COMMENTARY: Thus says the Lord, He that remains in this city shall die by the sword, by the famine, and by the pestilence: but he that goes forth to the Chaldeans shall live; for he shall have his life for a prey, and shall live. Henry's he that remains shall die by sword, famine, pestilence, but he that goes forth to Chaldeans shall live, life for a prey, is the plain preaching: the wicked look upon faithful ministers as enemies, because they show what enemies the wicked are to themselves while impenitent. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

3 COMMENTARY: Thus says the Lord, This city shall surely be given into the hand of the king of Babylon's army, which shall take it. Henry's this city shall surely be given into Babylon's king's army's hand, which shall take it, is the plain preaching continued: the city given is the message princes hate. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

4 COMMENTARY: Therefore the princes said unto the king, We beseech thee, let this man be put to death: for thus he weakens the hands of the men of war that remain in this city, and the hands of all the people, in speaking such words unto them: for this man seeks not the welfare of this people, but the hurt. Henry notes the princes went on in their malice: therefore princes said, let this man be put to death, for he weakens war men's hands and all people's hands, speaking such words, seeking not welfare but hurt, is malice calling preaching hurt. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

5 COMMENTARY: Then Zedekiah the king said, Behold, he is in your hand: for the king is not he that can do any thing against you. Henry's Zedekiah said, behold, he is in your hand, for the king is not he that can do anything against you, is the king yielding: the prophet is handed to malice. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

6 COMMENTARY: Then took they Jeremiah, and cast him into the dungeon of Malchiah the son of Hammelech, that was in the court of the prison: and they let down Jeremiah with cords. And in the dungeon there was no water, but mire: so Jeremiah sunk in the mire. Henry notes Jeremiah was put into a dungeon, and many of God's faithful witnesses have been privately made away in prisons: they took Jeremiah, cast into Malchiah's dungeon in the prison court, let down with cords, no water but mire, sunk in mire, is the dungeon. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

7 COMMENTARY: Now when Ebedmelech the Ethiopian, one of the eunuchs which was in the king's house, heard that they had put Jeremiah in the dungeon; the princes sitting then in the gate of Benjamin. Henry's Ebedmelech the Ethiopian, one of the king's house eunuchs, heard they had put Jeremiah in the dungeon, princes sitting then in Benjamin's gate, is the friend hearing: see how God can raise up friends for his people in distress. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

8 COMMENTARY: Ebedmelech went forth out of the king's house, and spoke to the king saying. Henry's Ebedmelech went forth out of the king's house, and spoke to the king, is the bold appearance: let this encourage us to appear boldly for God. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

9 COMMENTARY: My lord the king, these men have done evil in all that they have done to Jeremiah the prophet, whom they have cast into the dungeon; and he is like to die for hunger in the place where he is: for there is no more bread in the city. Henry notes Ebedmelech was an Ethiopian, yet he spoke to the king faithfully, These men have done ill in all they have done to Jeremiah: my lord the king, these men have done evil in all done to Jeremiah, cast into dungeon, like to die for hunger, no more bread in the city, is the faithful speech. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

10 COMMENTARY: Then the king commanded Ebedmelech the Ethiopian, saying, Take from hence thirty men with thee, and take up Jeremiah the prophet out of the dungeon, before he die. Henry's king commanded Ebedmelech, take thirty men, take up Jeremiah out of the dungeon before he die, is the release ordered: orders were given for the prophet's release. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

11 COMMENTARY: So Ebedmelech took the men with him, and went into the house of the king under the treasury, and took thence old cast clouts and old rotten rags, and let them down by cords into the dungeon to Jeremiah. Henry notes special notice is taken of his tenderness for Jeremiah: Ebedmelech took the men, went into the king's house under the treasury, took old cast clouts and rotten rags, let them down by cords into the dungeon, is the tenderness prepared. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

12 COMMENTARY: And Ebedmelech the Ethiopian said unto Jeremiah, Put now these old cast clouts and rotten rags under thine armholes under the cords. And Jeremiah did so. Henry's Ebedmelech said, put now these old cast clouts and rotten rags under armholes under cords, and Jeremiah did so, is the tenderness used: the Lord's children are conformed to his example. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

13 COMMENTARY: So they drew up Jeremiah with cords, and took him up out of the dungeon: and Jeremiah remained in the court of the prison. Henry notes Ebedmelech saw him drawn up: so they drew up Jeremiah with cords, took him up out of the dungeon, and Jeremiah remained in the prison court, is the drawing up seen. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

14 COMMENTARY: Then Zedekiah the king sent, and took Jeremiah the prophet unto him into the third entry that is in the house of the Lord: and the king said unto Jeremiah, I will ask thee a thing; hide nothing from me. Henry notes Jeremiah was not forward to repeat the warnings, which seemed only to endanger his own life, and to add to the king's guilt: Zedekiah sent and took Jeremiah into the third entry in the Lord's house, I will ask thee a thing, hide nothing from me, is the private conference begun. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

15 COMMENTARY: Then Jeremiah said unto Zedekiah, If I declare it unto thee, wilt thou not surely put me to death? and if I give thee counsel, wilt thou not hearken unto me? Henry's Jeremiah said, if I declare it, wilt thou not surely put me to death, and if I give counsel, wilt thou not hearken, is the not-forward prophet: warnings seem only to endanger life and add guilt. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

16 COMMENTARY: So Zedekiah the king swore secretly unto Jeremiah, saying, As the Lord lives, that made us this soul, I will not put thee to death, neither will I give thee into the hand of these men that seek thy life. Henry's Zedekiah swore secretly, as the Lord lives, that made us this soul, I will not put thee to death, neither give thee into life-seekers' hands, is the oath: the king swears the prophet safe. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

17 COMMENTARY: Then said Jeremiah unto Zedekiah, Thus says the Lord, the God of hosts, the God of Israel; If thou wilt assuredly go forth unto the king of Babylon's princes, then thy soul shall live, and this city shall not be burned with fire; and thou shalt live, and thine house. Henry notes he asked whether he feared to do the will of God: Jeremiah said, thus says the Lord, if thou wilt assuredly go forth to Babylon's princes, then thy soul shall live, city not burned, thou shalt live, and thine house, is the counsel, fearing God's will. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

18 COMMENTARY: But if thou wilt not go forth to the king of Babylon's princes, then shall this city be given into the hand of the Chaldeans, and they shall burn it with fire, and thou shalt not escape out of their hand. Henry's but if thou wilt not go forth to Babylon's princes, then city given to Chaldeans, burned with fire, thou shalt not escape, is the refusal's end: the less men fear God, the more they fear men. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

19 COMMENTARY: And Zedekiah the king said unto Jeremiah, I am afraid of the Jews that are fallen to the Chaldeans, lest they deliver me into their hand, and they mock me. Henry notes the less men fear God, the more they fear men: Zedekiah said, I am afraid of the fallen Jews, lest they deliver me into their hand, and they mock me, is fear of men over fear of God. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

20 COMMENTARY: But Jeremiah said, They shall not deliver thee. Obey, I beseech thee, the voice of the Lord, which I speak unto thee: so it shall be well unto thee, and thy soul shall live. Henry's Jeremiah said, they shall not deliver thee, obey, I beseech thee, the Lord's voice which I speak, so it shall be well, thy soul shall live, is the assurance: often they dare not act according to their own judgments and consciences, but obedience is well. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

21 COMMENTARY: But if thou refuse to go forth, this is the word that the Lord has shewed me. Henry's but if thou refuse to go forth, this is the word the Lord has shewed me, is the shewed word: refusal's vision follows. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

22 COMMENTARY: And, behold, all the women that are left in the king of Judah's house shall be brought forth to the king of Babylon's princes, and those women shall say, Thy friends have set thee on, and have prevailed against thee: thy feet are sunk in the mire, and they are turned away back. Henry's behold, all women left in Judah's king's house brought forth to Babylon's princes, saying, thy friends set thee on and prevailed, thy feet sunk in mire, turned away back, is the refusal's shame: friends prevailing, feet in mire, is the king's end pictured. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

23 COMMENTARY: So they shall bring out all thy wives and thy children to the Chaldeans: and thou shalt not escape out of their hand, but shalt be taken by the hand of the king of Babylon: and thou shalt cause this city to be burned with fire. Henry's so they shall bring out all wives and children to Chaldeans, thou shalt not escape, taken by Babylon's king's hand, thou shalt cause this city burned with fire, is the refusal's doom: the king causes the burning he feared. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

24 COMMENTARY: Then said Zedekiah unto Jeremiah, Let no man know of these words, and thou shalt not die. Henry's Zedekiah said, let no man know of these words, and thou shalt not die, is the secrecy: the conference is to be kept private. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

25 COMMENTARY: But if the princes hear that I have talked with thee, and they come unto thee, and say unto thee, Declare unto us now what thou hast said unto the king, and what the king hath said unto thee; hide it not from us, and we will not put thee to death. Henry's but if princes hear I talked with thee, and come, declare what thou saidst to the king and king to thee, hide not, we will not put thee to death, is the princes' threat: the secret is threatened. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

26 COMMENTARY: Then thou shalt say unto them, I presented my supplication before the king, that he would not cause me to return to Jonathan's house, to die there. Henry's then thou shalt say, I presented my supplication before the king, that he would not cause me to return to Jonathan's house to die there, is the answer commanded: the supplication is the cover. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

27 COMMENTARY: Then came all the princes unto Jeremiah, and asked him: and he told them according to all these words that the king had commanded him. So they left off speaking with him; for the matter was not perceived. Henry's then all princes came, asked him, he told according to all words the king commanded, so they left off speaking, for the matter was not perceived, is the conference kept: the children of Satan to their master, the Lord's children to his example, and the secret holds. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

28 COMMENTARY: So Jeremiah abode in the court of the prison until the day that Jerusalem was taken: and he was there when Jerusalem was taken. Henry's so Jeremiah abode in the prison court until the day Jerusalem was taken, and he was there when taken, is the abiding: the prophet remains in the court until the city's taking. — Matthew Henry (Concise)
