Study Notes — Jeremiah

Chapter 1

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Jeremiah was a priest from Anathoth, a town in Benjamin a few miles north of Jerusalem allotted to the Kohathites. JFB notes the stigma on that priestly family after Abiathar, and that God chooses weak and despised things to confound the mighty. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown; Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: The word came to Jeremiah in the thirteenth year of Josiah. JFB explains that Jehoahaz and Jehoiachin are omitted from the title because each reigned only three months, and that the first and last kings of a prophet's ministry are the ones usually named. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: The word came also in the days of Jehoiakim, unto the end of the eleventh year of Zedekiah and the carrying away of Jerusalem in the fifth month. JFB notes these opening verses were probably prefixed by Jeremiah when he collected his prophecies for his countrymen to take to Babylon, and identifies the fifth month with the month of Jerusalem's fall in 2 Kings 25:8. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: The word of the LORD came to Jeremiah, marking the start of his prophetic office. JFB notes this inscription was doubtless made by Jeremiah himself when he collected his prophecies. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: Before Jeremiah was formed, God knew him, meaning he approved him as his chosen instrument, and sanctified him, meaning he separated him to the prophetic office. JFB stresses this is predestination in God's secret counsel, not a claim that Jeremiah was cleansed from original sin at that point, and that his commission reached the nations as well as Judah. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: Jeremiah pleaded that he could not speak, for he was a child. JFB takes him to have been under twenty-five, with child here meaning young man, and notes that such reluctance in true prophets shows they did not seize the office from self-deceiving fanaticism, as false prophets did. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: God refused the excuse of youth: Jeremiah must go to all to whom he is sent and speak whatever he is commanded. JFB reads the sending in a hostile sense, to all against whom he is sent, and notes that Jeremiah, naturally timid, was moulded by the Spirit to the courage the task required without losing his own character. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: Jeremiah must not fear their faces, for God is with him to deliver him. Henry draws the lesson that the fear of God is the best remedy against the fear of man, and that it is better to have all men as enemies than God. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown; Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: The LORD touched Jeremiah's mouth and put his words there, a symbolic act showing God would give him utterance despite his inability to speak. Gill compares it to the seraph touching Isaiah's lips, and notes the mouth was now sanctified to the LORD's service, so Jeremiah would speak God's words and not his own. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: Jeremiah was set over nations and kingdoms, not as a prince but as a prophet with oversight of them, to foretell their destruction or restoration. JFB explains that prophets are said to do what they foretell, since God's word is his instrument, and that building and planting, restoration on repentance, follow the words of uprooting because denunciation came first. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: Jeremiah saw a rod of an almond tree. JFB explains the almond is literally the wakeful tree, flowering earliest in the year, and so a symbol of God's early and speedy execution of his purpose. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: God confirmed the vision: he will hasten his word to perform it. JFB renders it, I will be wakeful as to my word, playing on the almond's name, so the vision promises swift fulfilment, not delay. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: In a second vision Jeremiah saw a seething pot with its face toward the north. JFB explains the pot, kept boiling by a flame blown under it, as an eastern symbol of raging war, and the northern side as the quarter from which the invasion would pour south upon Judah. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: Out of the north an evil shall break forth upon all the inhabitants of the land. JFB renders break forth as disclose itself, and identifies the evil with the Chaldeans, who would come upon Judah by the northern route since they could not cross the desert. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: God will call all the families of the kingdoms of the north, and they will come and set their thrones at the gates of Jerusalem. JFB takes families as the tribes and clans making up Babylon's kingdoms, and the thrones at the gates as the conquerors setting up their tribunal where justice was administered. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: God will utter his judgments against Judah for forsaking him, burning incense to other gods and worshipping the works of their own hands. JFB notes that utter means pronounce, and that the judicial sentences of the invaders would in effect be the judgments of God. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: Jeremiah must gird up his loins, resolutely preparing for his task like a man tucking up flowing robes for active work, and must not be dismayed at their faces. JFB notes dismayed and confound are the same Hebrew word, to break: if he breaks before them, God will let him be broken before them. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

18 COMMENTARY: God makes Jeremiah a defenced city, an iron pillar and brazen walls against kings, princes, priests and people. JFB explains the plural walls as abundant strength, and the people of the land as the general masses as distinct from rulers and priests. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

19 COMMENTARY: They will fight against Jeremiah but not prevail, for God is with him to deliver him. Henry closes the chapter on this promise of divine protection: the whole counsel of God must be declared, and those who are sure God is with them need not fear whoever stands against them. — Matthew Henry
