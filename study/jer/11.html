Study Notes — Jeremiah

Chapter 11

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: The word that came to Jeremiah from the Lord, saying. Henry's reproof begins with a commission: the word came to Jeremiah so that the disobedient Jews might be reproved by God's own charge, not the prophet's invention. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

2 COMMENTARY: Hear the words of this covenant, and speak unto the men of Judah, and to the inhabitants of Jerusalem. Henry notes that in general men will hearken to those who speak of doctrines, promises, and privileges; but when duties are mentioned, they will not bend their ear, and this covenant is duty spoken plainly. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

3 COMMENTARY: And say unto them, thus says the Lord God of Israel: Cursed be the man that obeys not the words of this covenant. Henry warns God never promised to bestow blessings on his rational creatures while they persist in wilful disobedience: the curse falls on disobedience to the covenant's words. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

4 COMMENTARY: Which I commanded your fathers in the day that I brought them forth out of the land of Egypt, from the iron furnace, saying, Obey my voice, and do them, according to all which I command you: so shall ye be my people, and I will be your God. Henry's condition is obedience: pardon and acceptance are promised freely to all believers, but no man can be saved who does not obey the command of God to repent, to believe in Christ, to separate from sin and the world, and to choose self-denial and newness of life. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

5 COMMENTARY: That I may perform the oath which I have sworn unto your fathers, to give them a land flowing with milk and honey, as it is this day. Then answered I, and said, So be it, O Lord. Henry notes the oath's fulfilment rides on obedience: the land flowing with milk and honey is the blessing promised to a people who obey, and Jeremiah answers, So be it. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

6 COMMENTARY: Then the Lord said unto me, Proclaim all these words in the cities of Judah, and in the streets of Jerusalem, saying, Hear ye the words of this covenant, and do them. Henry stresses doing, not merely hearing: the words must be proclaimed in cities and streets, and men who hearken to promises but not duties will not bend their ear to this. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

7 COMMENTARY: For I earnestly protested unto your fathers in the day that I brought them up out of the land of Egypt, even unto this day, rising early and protesting, saying, Obey my voice. Henry's protest was earnest and early, day after day from Egypt to this day, yet the people persisted in wilful disobedience. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

8 COMMENTARY: Yet they obeyed not, nor inclined their ear, but walked every one in the imagination of their evil heart: therefore I will bring upon them all the words of this covenant, which I commanded them to do; but they did them not. Henry's judgement follows the pattern: they would not bend their ear to duties, so all the words of the covenant, curses included, are brought upon them. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

9 COMMENTARY: And the Lord said unto me, A conspiracy is found among the men of Judah, and among the inhabitants of Jerusalem. Henry's reproof deepens into conspiracy: the disobedience is not scattered failure but a found conspiracy among Judah and Jerusalem together. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

10 COMMENTARY: They are turned back to the iniquities of their forefathers, which refused to hear my words; and they went after other gods to serve them: the house of Israel and the house of Judah have broken my covenant which I made with their fathers. Henry notes the return to forefathers' iniquities: refusing to hear, going after other gods, and breaking the covenant made with their fathers. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

11 COMMENTARY: Therefore thus says the Lord: behold, I will bring evil upon them, which they shall not be able to escape; and though they shall cry unto me, I will not hearken unto them. Henry warns evil pursues sinners and entangles them in snares, out of which they cannot free themselves: the evil brought upon them cannot be escaped, and their cry will not be heard. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

12 COMMENTARY: Then shall the cities of Judah and inhabitants of Jerusalem go, and cry unto the gods unto whom they offer incense: but they shall not save them at all in the time of their trouble. Henry notes that now, in their distress, their many gods and many altars stand them in no stead. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

13 COMMENTARY: For according to the number of thy cities were thy gods, O Judah; and according to the number of the streets of Jerusalem have ye set up altars to that shameful thing, even altars to burn incense unto Baal. Henry's many gods and many altars, one per city and one per street, prove of no use in trouble: multitude of idols is no multitude of saviours. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

14 COMMENTARY: Therefore pray not thou for this people, neither lift up a cry or prayer for them: for I will not hear them in the time that they cry unto me for their trouble. Henry warns those whose own prayers will not be heard cannot expect benefit from the prayers of others: even Jeremiah's intercession is barred when their own cry is refused. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

15 COMMENTARY: What has my beloved to do in mine house, seeing she has wrought lewdness with many, and the holy flesh is passed from thee? When thou doest evil, then thou rejoicest. Henry notes their profession of religion shall prove of no use: when trouble came upon them, they made this their confidence, but God has rejected it, and his altar shall yield them no satisfaction. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

16 COMMENTARY: The Lord called thy name, A green olive tree, fair, and of goodly fruit: with the noise of a great tumult he has kindled fire upon it, and the branches of it are broken. Henry warns the remembrance of God's former favours to them shall be no comfort under troubles: the fair green olive tree is set on fire, and its branches broken, for all its former fruit. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

17 COMMENTARY: For the Lord of hosts, that planted thee, has pronounced evil against thee, for the evil of the house of Israel and of the house of Judah, which they have done against themselves to provoke me to anger in offering incense unto Baal. Henry's summary is that every sin against the Lord is a sin against ourselves, and so it will be found sooner or later: the planter pronounces evil against his own planting, because they provoked him with Baal. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

18 COMMENTARY: And the Lord has given me knowledge of it, and I know it: then thou shewedst me their doings. Henry notes God knows all the secret designs of his and his people's enemies, and can, when he pleases, make them known: Jeremiah knows their doings only because the Lord showed them to him. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

19 COMMENTARY: But I was like a lamb or an ox that is brought to the slaughter; and I knew not that they had devised devices against me, saying, Let us destroy the tree with the fruit thereof, and let us cut him off from the land of the living, that his name may be remembered no more. Henry tells that those of Jeremiah's own city plotted how they might cause his death: they thought to end his days, but he outlived most of his enemies; they thought to blast his memory, but it lives to this day, and will be blessed while time lasts. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

20 COMMENTARY: But, O Lord of hosts, that judgest righteously, that triest the reins and the heart, let me see thy vengeance on them: for unto thee have I revealed my cause. Henry notes God's justice is a terror to the wicked, but a comfort to the godly: when we are wronged, we have a God to commit our cause to, and it is our duty to commit it to him. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

21 COMMENTARY: Therefore thus says the Lord of the men of Anathoth, that seek thy life, saying, Prophesy not in the name of the Lord, lest thou die by our hand. Henry's men of Anathoth sought the prophet's life for prophesying in the Lord's name: the threat of death by their hand is the price of faithful prophecy. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

22 COMMENTARY: Therefore thus says the Lord of hosts: behold, I will punish them: the young men shall die by the sword; their sons and their daughters shall die by famine. Henry warns God's justice is a terror to the wicked: the punishers of Anathoth shall themselves be punished, young men by sword, sons and daughters by famine. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

23 COMMENTARY: And there shall be no remnant of them: for I will bring evil upon the men of Anathoth, even the year of their visitation. Henry closes with patient continuance: we should look well to our own spirits, that we are not overcome with evil, but that by patient continuance in praying for our enemies, and in kindness to them, we may overcome evil with good, while God brings the year of visitation on the impenitent. — Matthew Henry (Concise)
