Study Notes — Jeremiah

Chapter 33

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Moreover the word of the Lord came unto Jeremiah the second time, while he was yet shut up in the court of the prison, saying. Henry notes those who expect to receive comforts from God must call upon him: moreover the word came the second time, while yet shut up in the prison court, and promises are given, not to do away, but to quicken and encourage prayer. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

2 COMMENTARY: Thus says the Lord the maker thereof, the Lord that formed it, to establish it; the Lord is his name. Henry's thus says the Lord the maker, the Lord that formed it, to establish it, the Lord his name, is the promise-giver: these promises lead us to the gospel of Christ, and in that God has revealed truth to direct us, and peace to make us easy. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

3 COMMENTARY: Call unto me, and I will answer thee, and shew thee great and mighty things, which thou knowest not. Henry's call unto me, and I will answer thee, and shew thee great and mighty things which thou knowest not, is the comfort's condition: those who expect comforts must call upon him, and promises quicken and encourage prayer. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

4 COMMENTARY: For thus says the Lord, the God of Israel, concerning the houses of this city, and concerning the houses of the kings of Judah, which are thrown down by the mounts, and by the sword. Henry's houses of this city, and houses of Judah's kings, thrown down by mounts and sword, are the desolation the promise answers: the city thrown down is the place of restoration. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

5 COMMENTARY: They come to fight with the Chaldeans, but it is to fill them with the dead bodies of men, whom I have slain in mine anger and in my fury, and for all whose wickedness I have hid my face from this city. Henry's they come to fight Chaldeans, but fill them with dead bodies slain in anger and fury, for wickedness hiding my face from this city, is the wrath: many are led to perceive the real difference between the people of God and the world around them, and to fear the Divine wrath. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

6 COMMENTARY: Behold, I will bring it health and cure, and I will cure them, and will reveal unto them the abundance of peace and truth. Henry's behold, I will bring health and cure, cure them, reveal abundance of peace and truth, is the promise: in the gospel of Christ God has revealed truth to direct us, and peace to make us easy. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

7 COMMENTARY: And I will cause the captivity of Judah and the captivity of Israel to return, and will build them, as at the first. Henry's I will cause Judah and Israel's captivity to return, and build them as at the first, is the rebuilding: the people long in sorrow shall again be filled with joy. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

8 COMMENTARY: And I will cleanse them from all their iniquity, whereby they have sinned against me; and I will pardon all their iniquities, whereby they have sinned, and whereby they have transgressed against me. Henry notes all who by sanctifying grace are cleansed from the filth of sin, by pardoning mercy are freed from the guilt: I will cleanse from all iniquity sinned against me, pardon all iniquities sinned and transgressed, is cleansing and pardon. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

9 COMMENTARY: And it shall be to me a name of joy, a praise and an honour before all the nations of the earth, which shall hear all the good that I do unto them: and they shall fear and tremble for all the goodness and for all the prosperity that I procure unto it. Henry notes when sinners are thus justified, washed, and sanctified in the name of the Lord Jesus, and by the Holy Spirit, they are enabled to walk before God in peace and purity: it shall be to me a name of joy, praise, honour before all nations hearing all good done, fearing and trembling for goodness and prosperity procured, is the justified people walking in peace and purity. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

10 COMMENTARY: Thus says the Lord; Again there shall be heard in this place, which ye say shall be desolate without man and without beast, even in the cities of Judah, and in the streets of Jerusalem, that are desolate, without man, and without inhabitant, and without beast. Henry's again there shall be heard in this place, which ye say desolate without man and beast, in Judah's cities and Jerusalem's desolate streets, is the joy promised: the people long in sorrow shall again be filled with joy, heard again in desolation. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

11 COMMENTARY: The voice of joy, and the voice of gladness, the voice of the bridegroom, and the voice of the bride, the voice of them that shall say, Praise the Lord of hosts: for the Lord is good; for his mercy endures for ever: and of them that shall bring the sacrifice of praise into the house of the Lord. For I will cause to return the captivity of the land, as at the first, says the Lord. Henry's voice of joy and gladness, bridegroom and bride, praise the Lord of hosts, for the Lord is good, mercy endures for ever, sacrifice of praise brought into the Lord's house, captivity returned as at first, is the joy's voice: where the Lord gives righteousness and peace, he will give all needful supplies for temporal wants, and all we have will be comforts, as sanctified by the word and by prayer. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

12 COMMENTARY: Thus says the Lord of hosts; Again in this place, which is desolate without man and without beast, and in all the cities thereof, shall be an habitation of shepherds causing their flocks to lie down. Henry's again in this desolate place without man and beast, in all cities, habitation of shepherds causing flocks to lie down, is the supply: righteousness and peace bring needful temporal supplies. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

13 COMMENTARY: In the cities of the mountains, in the cities of the vale, and in the cities of the south, and in the land of Benjamin, and in the places about Jerusalem, and in the cities of Judah, shall the flocks pass again under the hands of him that tells them, says the Lord. Henry's mountains, vale, south, Benjamin, about Jerusalem, Judah's cities, flocks passing again under the teller's hands, is the counting restored: all we have will be comforts, sanctified by word and prayer. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

14 COMMENTARY: Behold, the days come, says the Lord, that I will perform that good thing which I have promised unto the house of Israel and to the house of Judah. Henry notes to crown the blessings God has in store, here is a promise of the Messiah: behold, the days come, I will perform that good thing promised to Israel and Judah, is the crowning promise. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

15 COMMENTARY: In those days, and at that time, will I cause the Branch of righteousness to grow up unto David; and he shall execute judgment and righteousness in the land. Henry notes he imparts righteousness to his church, for he is made of God to us righteousness, and believers are made the righteousness of God in him: in those days, at that time, I will cause the Branch of righteousness to grow up unto David, executing judgment and righteousness in the land, is the Branch imparting righteousness. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

16 COMMENTARY: In those days shall Judah be saved, and Jerusalem shall dwell safely: and this is the name wherewith she shall be called, The Lord our righteousness. Henry notes Christ is our Lord God, our righteousness, our sanctification, and our redemption: in those days Judah saved, Jerusalem dwelling safely, name wherewith called, The Lord our righteousness, is Christ our righteousness. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

17 COMMENTARY: For thus says the Lord; David shall never want a man to sit upon the throne of the house of Israel. Henry notes his kingdom is an everlasting kingdom: for thus says the Lord, David shall never want a man to sit upon Israel's throne, is the everlasting kingdom's throne. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

18 COMMENTARY: Neither shall the priests the Levites want a man before me to offer burnt offerings, and to kindle meat offerings, and to do sacrifice continually. Henry notes the covenant of priesthood shall be secured: neither shall Levitical priests want a man before me to offer burnt offerings, kindle meat offerings, do sacrifice continually, is the priesthood secured. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

19 COMMENTARY: And the word of the Lord came unto Jeremiah, saying. Henry's word of the Lord came unto Jeremiah is the covenant's confirmation: the priesthood and kingdom promises are confirmed by a further word. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

20 COMMENTARY: Thus says the Lord; If ye can break my covenant of the day, and my covenant of the night, and that there should not be day and night in their season. Henry notes but in this world prosperity and adversity succeed each other, as light and darkness, day and night: if ye can break my day covenant and night covenant, that there should not be day and night in season, is the day-night surety, succeeding as light and darkness. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

21 COMMENTARY: Then may also my covenant be broken with David my servant, that he should not have a son to reign upon his throne; and with the Levites the priests, my ministers. Henry's then may also my covenant be broken with David my servant, that he should not have a son to reign, and with Levitical priests my ministers, is the conditional impossibility: only breaking day and night breaks David and Levi. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

22 COMMENTARY: As the host of heaven cannot be numbered, neither the sand of the sea measured: so will I multiply the seed of David my servant, and the Levites that minister unto me. Henry notes all true believers are a holy priesthood, a royal priesthood, they offer up spiritual sacrifices, acceptable to God, themselves, in the first place, as living sacrifices: as heaven's host unnumbered, sea's sand unmeasured, so will I multiply David's seed and ministering Levites, is the multiplied priesthood. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

23 COMMENTARY: Moreover the word of the Lord came to Jeremiah, saying. Henry's moreover the word came to Jeremiah is the further word: the covenant's confirmation continues. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

24 COMMENTARY: Considerest thou not what this people have spoken, saying, The two families which the Lord has chosen, he has even cast them off? thus they have despised my people, that they should be no more a nation before them. Henry notes let us not despise the families which were of old the chosen people of God, though for a time they seem to be cast off: considerest thou not what this people spoke, the two families the Lord chose, he cast off, thus despised my people, no more a nation before them, is the despising to be refused. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

25 COMMENTARY: Thus says the Lord; If my covenant be not with day and night, and if I have not appointed the ordinances of heaven and earth. Henry's if my covenant be not with day and night, if I have not appointed heaven and earth's ordinances, is the covenant repeated: the day-night ordinances stand, so the chosen stand. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

26 COMMENTARY: Then will I cast away the seed of Jacob and David my servant, so that I will not take any of his seed to be rulers over the seed of Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob: for I will cause their captivity to return, and have mercy on them. Henry notes the promises of that covenant shall have full accomplishment in the gospel Israel: in Galatians 6:16, all that walk according to the gospel rule, are made to be the Israel of God, on whom shall be peace and mercy, and then will I cast away Jacob and David's seed, not taking seed to rule Abraham, Isaac, Jacob's seed, for I will cause captivity to return and have mercy, is the mercy that prevents casting away. — Matthew Henry (Concise)
