Study Notes — Jeremiah

Chapter 6

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: O children of Benjamin, gather to flee out of Jerusalem, blow the trumpet in Tekoa, and set up a sign of fire in Bethhaccerem, for evil appears out of the north and great destruction. Henry warns it is vain to contend with God's judgments: the Chaldean army shall break in upon Judah and in a little time devour all. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: The daughter of Zion is likened to a comely and delicate woman. Henry's section makes the delicacy the point: the more Judah indulges in the pleasures of this life, the more she unfits herself for its troubles, and the day is coming when the careless and secure in sinful ways will be visited. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: Shepherds with their flocks shall come against her, pitching tents round about and feeding each in his place. Henry notes the invaders are eager, not to fulfil God's counsels but to fill their own treasures, yet God thereby serves his own purposes. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: Prepare war against her; arise, let us go up at noon, though woe follows, for the day goes away and evening shadows stretch out. Henry calls it folly to trifle when eternal salvation must be worked out and its enemies fought: the noon assault leaves no time to trifle. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: Arise, let us go by night and destroy her palaces. Henry's warning stands: the siege presses day and night, and those secure in sinful ways will be visited without respite. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: Hew down trees and cast a mount against Jerusalem, for this is the city to be visited, wholly oppression in the midst of her. Henry notes the siege works are God's visitation upon a city whose whole midst is oppression. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: As a fountain casts out her waters, so she casts out her wickedness, with violence and spoil heard in her and grief and wounds continually before God. Henry compares the corrupt heart in its natural state to such a fountain: always flowing, yet always full of evil thoughts. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: Be thou instructed, O Jerusalem, lest God's soul depart from thee and he make thee desolate, a land not inhabited. Henry adds that the God of mercy is loth to depart even from a provoking people, and is earnest with them that repentance and reformation may prevent things from coming to extremity. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: They shall thoroughly glean the remnant of Israel as a vine, turning back the hand as a grapegatherer into the baskets. Henry's next section warns that when the Lord arises to take vengeance, no sinners of any age or rank, or of either sex, escape the gleaning. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: To whom shall Jeremiah speak and give warning that they may hear? Their ear is uncircumcised and they cannot hearken; the word of the Lord is a reproach to them and they have no delight in it. Henry notes they were set upon the world and wholly carried away by love of it, so warning finds no hearing. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: Jeremiah is full of the fury of the Lord and weary with holding in, and will pour it out upon children abroad and the assembly of young men, husband with wife, aged with him full of days. Henry stresses no rank escapes: vengeance takes every age and station together. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: Their houses shall be turned to others, with fields and wives together, for God will stretch out his hand upon the inhabitants of the land. Henry judges the sin by God's word and finds multitudes in every station given up to it, so that houses, fields, and families pass to strangers. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: From the least even to the greatest, every one is given to covetousness, and from prophet to priest every one deals falsely. Henry names covetousness as the universal sin: set upon the world, all ranks are carried away by love of it. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: They have healed the hurt of God's people slightly, saying, 'Peace, peace,' when there is no peace. Henry warns that flatterers in a sinful way are our worst and most dangerous enemies, healing slightly what needs deep cure. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: Were they ashamed when they committed abomination? Nay, they were not at all ashamed, neither could they blush, therefore they shall fall among those who fall and be cast down at God's visitation. Henry's point is shamelessness: unable to blush, they cannot repent. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: Stand in the ways and see, ask for the old paths, where the good way is, and walk therein, and find rest for souls; but they said, 'We will not walk therein.' Henry urges wisdom for souls: the way of godliness and righteousness, set forth by the written word, has always been the way God owned and blessed, and walking in it brings abundant recompense at journey's end. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: God set watchmen over them, saying, 'Hearken to the sound of the trumpet,' but they said, 'We will not hearken.' Henry concludes that if men will not obey God's voice and flee to his appointed Refuge, it will plainly appear at judgment that they are ruined because they reject God's word. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: Therefore hear, ye nations, and know, O congregation, what is among them. Henry's next section summons the nations as witnesses: God's professing people's sins make them an easy prey, and their rejection of outward services will be published abroad. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: Hear, O earth: God will bring evil upon this people, even the fruit of their thoughts, because they have not hearkened to his words nor his law but rejected it. Henry notes the evil is the fruit of their own thoughts: rejected words ripen into judgment. — Matthew Henry

20 COMMENTARY: To what purpose comes incense from Sheba and sweet cane from a far country? Their burnt offerings are not acceptable, nor sacrifices sweet to God. Henry explains that God rejects outward services as worthless to atone for sins: sacrifice and incense were to direct them to a Mediator, but offered to purchase a license to go on in sin, they provoke God. — Matthew Henry

21 COMMENTARY: God will lay stumblingblocks before this people, and fathers and sons together shall fall upon them, neighbour and friend perishing. Henry notes the sins of professing people make them an easy prey to their enemies, stumbling where they once walked securely. — Matthew Henry

22 COMMENTARY: Behold, a people comes from the north country, and a great nation shall be raised from the sides of the earth. Henry's section has the northern nation as the instrument: saints may rejoice in hope of mercies seen only in promise, but sinners must mourn for fear of judgments seen only in threatenings, and this nation is the threat made flesh. — Matthew Henry

23 COMMENTARY: They shall lay hold on bow and spear, cruel and without mercy, their voice roaring like the sea, riding on horses set in array as men for war against the daughter of Zion. Henry notes the invaders dare not be faced: Judah's sins make her an easy prey, and she dares not show herself. — Matthew Henry

24 COMMENTARY: We have heard the fame thereof, our hands wax feeble, anguish takes hold of us, and pain as of a woman in travail. Henry contrasts the two responses: saints rejoice in promised mercies, while sinners mourn at threatened judgments, and Zion's travail here is the mourning of fear. — Matthew Henry

25 COMMENTARY: Go not forth into the field, nor walk by the way, for the sword of the enemy and fear is on every side. Henry notes the prey is surrounded: the sins of God's professing people leave them nowhere to walk safely. — Matthew Henry

26 COMMENTARY: O daughter of God's people, gird with sackcloth and wallow in ashes, making mourning as for an only son, most bitter lamentation, for the spoiler shall suddenly come upon us. Henry's section calls for mourning proportioned to the judgment: sudden spoiling deserves the bitterness of losing an only son. — Matthew Henry

27 COMMENTARY: Jeremiah is set for a tower and a fortress among God's people, that he may know and try their way. Henry compares the prophet's testing work to assaying ore: the tower watches and proves what the people truly are. — Matthew Henry

28 COMMENTARY: They are all grievous revolters, walking with slanders, brass and iron, all corrupters. Henry calls them the worst of revolters: sinners soon become tempters, corrupting others as well as themselves. — Matthew Henry

29 COMMENTARY: The bellows are burned, the lead is consumed of the fire, the founder melts in vain, for the wicked are not plucked away. Henry notes the ore supposed to have good metal proves all dross: nothing prevails to part them from their sins. — Matthew Henry

30 COMMENTARY: Reprobate silver shall men call them, because the Lord has rejected them. Henry closes that when warnings, corrections, rebukes, and all means of grace leave men unrenewed, they will be left, as rejected of God, to everlasting misery; our prayer must be to be refined by the Lord as silver is refined. — Matthew Henry
