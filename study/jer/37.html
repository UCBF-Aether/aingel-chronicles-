Study Notes — Jeremiah

Chapter 37

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: And king Zedekiah the son of Josiah reigned instead of Coniah the son of Jehoiakim, whom Nebuchadrezzar king of Babylon made king in the land of Judah. Henry notes numbers witness the fatal effects of other men's sins, yet heedlessly step into their places, and follow the same destructive course: Zedekiah, Josiah's son, reigned instead of Coniah, whom Nebuchadnezzar made king in Judah, stepping into the predecessor's place. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

2 COMMENTARY: But neither he, nor his servants, nor the people of the land, did hearken unto the words of the Lord, which he spoke by the prophet Jeremiah. Henry's neither he, nor servants, nor land's people, hearkened unto the Lord's words spoken by Jeremiah, is the heedless course: witnessing fatal effects, yet following the same destructive course. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

3 COMMENTARY: And Zedekiah the king sent Jehucal the son of Shelemiah and Zephaniah the son of Maaseiah the priest to the prophet Jeremiah, saying, Pray now unto the Lord our God for us. Henry notes when in distress, we ought to desire the prayers of ministers and Christian friends: Zedekiah sent Jehucal and Zephaniah the priest to Jeremiah, pray now unto the Lord our God for us, is the distressed prayer desired. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

4 COMMENTARY: Now Jeremiah came in and went out among the people: for they had not put him into prison. Henry's Jeremiah came in and went out among the people, for they had not put him into prison, is the liberty before imprisonment: the prophet at liberty receives the king's messengers. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

5 COMMENTARY: Then Pharaoh's army was come forth out of Egypt: and when the Chaldeans that besieged Jerusalem heard tidings of them, they departed from Jerusalem. Henry notes it is common for those to desire to be prayed for, who will not be advised, yet sinners are often hardened by a pause in judgments: Pharaoh's army come forth from Egypt, Chaldeans besieging Jerusalem hearing tidings departed, is the pause in judgments. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

6 COMMENTARY: Then came the word of the Lord unto the prophet Jeremiah, saying. Henry's then came the word unto Jeremiah is the assurance: if God help us not, no creature can, and the word answers the pause. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

7 COMMENTARY: Thus says the Lord, the God of Israel; Thus shall ye say to the king of Judah, that sent you unto me to enquire of me; Behold, Pharaoh's army, which is come forth to help you, shall return to Egypt into their own land. Henry's thus shall ye say to Judah's king that sent you to enquire, behold, Pharaoh's army come forth to help you shall return to Egypt into their own land, is the help failing: if God help not, no creature can. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

8 COMMENTARY: And the Chaldeans shall come again, and fight against this city, and take it, and burn it with fire. Henry's Chaldeans shall come again, fight against this city, take it, burn it with fire, is the return: whatever instruments God has determined to use, they shall do the work, though they seem unlikely. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

9 COMMENTARY: Thus says the Lord; Deceive not yourselves, saying, The Chaldeans shall surely depart from us: for they shall not depart. Henry's deceive not yourselves, saying, Chaldeans shall surely depart, for they shall not depart, is the self-deception warned: sinners hardened by a pause must not call pause departure. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

10 COMMENTARY: For though ye had smitten the whole army of the Chaldeans that fight against you, and there remained but wounded men among them, yet should they rise up every man in his tent, and burn this city with fire. Henry's though ye smote the whole Chaldean army, and only wounded men remained, yet should they rise up every man in his tent, and burn this city with fire, is the unlikely instrument: whatever instruments God determined, they shall do the work, though they seem unlikely, even wounded men rising. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

11 COMMENTARY: And it came to pass, that when the army of the Chaldeans was broken up from Jerusalem for fear of Pharaoh's army. Henry notes there are times when it is the wisdom of good men to retire, to enter into their chambers, and to shut the doors: when the Chaldean army was broken up from Jerusalem for fear of Pharaoh's army, the retirement's occasion comes. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

12 COMMENTARY: Then Jeremiah went forth out of Jerusalem to go into the land of Benjamin, to separate himself thence in the midst of the people. Henry's Jeremiah went forth from Jerusalem to go into Benjamin's land, to separate himself thence in the midst of the people, is the wise retiring: entering chambers, shutting doors, in the pause. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

13 COMMENTARY: And when he was in the gate of Benjamin, a captain of the ward was there, whose name was Irijah, the son of Shelemiah, the son of Hananiah; and he took Jeremiah the prophet, saying, Thou fallest away to the Chaldeans. Henry's when he was in Benjamin's gate, Irijah, captain of the ward, took Jeremiah, saying, thou fallest away to Chaldeans, is the seizure as deserter: the retiring prophet is seized. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

14 COMMENTARY: Then said Jeremiah, It is false; I fall not away to the Chaldeans. But he hearkened not to him: so Irijah took Jeremiah, and brought him to the princes. Henry notes it is no new thing for the best friends of the church to be belied, as in the interests of her worst enemies: Jeremiah said, it is false, I fall not away to Chaldeans, but he hearkened not, so Irijah took Jeremiah to princes, is the belied friend. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

15 COMMENTARY: Wherefore the princes were wroth with Jeremiah, and smote him, and put him in prison in the house of Jonathan the scribe: for they had made that the prison. Henry's princes wroth, smote him, put him in prison in Jonathan the scribe's house, made that the prison, is the commitment: seized as deserter, committed to prison. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

16 COMMENTARY: When Jeremiah was entered into the dungeon, and into the cabins, and Jeremiah had remained there many days. Henry's Jeremiah entered into dungeon and cabins, remained there many days, is the prison's depth: the best friend belied sits many days in dungeon. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

17 COMMENTARY: Then Zedekiah the king sent, and took him out: and the king asked him secretly in his house, and said, Is there any word from the Lord? And Jeremiah said, There is: for, said he, thou shalt be delivered into the hand of the king of Babylon. Henry notes Jeremiah obtained mercy of the Lord to be faithful, and would not, to obtain mercy of man, be unfaithful to God or to his prince; he tells the king the whole truth: Zedekiah sent and took him out, asked secretly, is there any word from the Lord, Jeremiah said, there is, thou shalt be delivered into Babylon's king's hand, is the whole truth told. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

18 COMMENTARY: Moreover Jeremiah said unto king Zedekiah, What have I offended against thee, or against thy servants, or against this people, that ye have put me in prison? Henry notes when thus falsely accused, we may deny the charge, and commit our cause to Him who judges righteously: moreover Jeremiah said, what have I offended against thee, servants, or this people, that ye put me in prison, is the denied charge committed to the righteous Judge. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

19 COMMENTARY: Where are now your prophets which prophesied unto you, saying, The king of Babylon shall not come against you, nor against this land? Henry's where are now your prophets which prophesied, Babylon's king shall not come against you nor this land, is the false prophets' absence: when Jeremiah delivered God's message, he spoke with boldness, and the bold message exposes the missing prophets. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

20 COMMENTARY: Therefore hear now, I pray thee, O my lord the king: let my supplication, I pray thee, be accepted before thee; that thou cause me not to return to the house of Jonathan the scribe, lest I die there. Henry notes when Jeremiah delivered God's message, he spoke with boldness, but when he made his own request, he spoke submissively: a lion in God's cause must be a lamb in his own, and therefore hear now, I pray thee, O my lord the king, let my supplication be accepted, cause me not to return to Jonathan's house, lest I die there, is the lamb's request. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

21 COMMENTARY: Then Zedekiah the king commanded that they should commit Jeremiah into the court of the prison, and that they should give him daily a piece of bread out of the bakers' street, until all the bread in the city were spent. Thus Jeremiah remained in the court of the prison. Henry notes God gave Jeremiah favour in the eyes of the king: the Lord God can make even the cells of a prison become pastures to his people, and will raise up friends to provide for them, so that in the days of famine they shall be satisfied, and Zedekiah commanded Jeremiah committed into the prison court, daily bread from bakers' street until all city bread spent, thus Jeremiah remained in the prison court, is the pasture-cell with daily bread. — Matthew Henry (Concise)
