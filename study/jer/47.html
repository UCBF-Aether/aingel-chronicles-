Study Notes — Jeremiah

Chapter 47

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Henry notes the date is observable: this word came against the Philistines before Pharaoh smote Gaza, when they were in full strength and lustre, their cities in good condition with no peril foreseen by human probabilities, so Pharaoh's blow would be but an earnest and beginning of sorrows, and Henry recalls the Philistines had always been sworn enemies and dangerous near neighbours to Israel, humbled in David's time but grown considerable again until Nebuchadnezzar cut them off. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

2 COMMENTARY: Henry explains the waters rising out of the north as both multitudes of people and great threatening calamities: the Chaldean army shall overflow the small Philistine country like a deluge, a terrible storm out of the cold climate whence fair weather was said to come, so that all the inhabitants of the land shall howl and nothing but lamentation shall be heard. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

3 COMMENTARY: Henry notes that before killing begins, the very stamping of horses' hoofs and rattling of chariots and rumbling of wheels shall strike such terror that fathers shall not look back to their children for feebleness of hands, seeming void of natural affection, despairing of carrying them off and forgetting even those pieces of themselves. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

4 COMMENTARY: Henry stresses it is a day to spoil the Philistines because the LORD will spoil them, and those whom God will spoil must needs be spoiled, while Tyre and Zidon, strong wealthy cities that used to help the Philistines in a strait, shall themselves be involved in the common ruin with every helper that remains cut off, and the remnant of Caphtor, kin to the Philistines by Genesis 10, shall be spoiled with them. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

5 COMMENTARY: Henry explains baldness upon Gaza and Ashkelon cut off with the remnant of their valley either as invaders stripping them of all ornaments or as themselves made bald in token of extreme grief, with the fruitful valley's products spoiled and made prey, and notes the prophet's tender question, how long wilt thou cut thyself, marking a calamity not only cutting but long cutting. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

6 COMMENTARY: Henry reads the prophet's plea to the sword of the LORD to put itself into its scabbard, rest, and be still as expressing earnest desire to see an end of war, looking with compassion even upon the Philistines, and notes war is the sword of the LORD, with which he punishes his enemies' crimes and pleads his people's cause, yet once drawn it does not quickly find the scabbard again. — Matthew Henry (Complete)

7 COMMENTARY: Henry gives the answer stopping his own complaint: how can the sword be quiet when the LORD has given it a charge against Ashkelon and the sea shore and there appointed it, since every bullet has its charge from the LORD of hosts, commander-in-chief, and the sword cannot be sheathed until it has fulfilled its charge, as God's word and rod accomplish that for which he sends them. — Matthew Henry (Complete)
