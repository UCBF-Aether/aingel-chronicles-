Study Notes — Jeremiah

Chapter 3

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: The law's rule of divorce is cited: if a man puts away his wife and she joins another, her first husband shall never take her again, lest the land be greatly polluted. Henry notes Israel deserved to be abandoned utterly by that rule, yet God graciously says, 'yet return again to me,' for he has not tied himself by the laws he made for us and has none of man's peevish resentment. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: Lift up her eyes to the high places and see where she has not been lien with: in the ways she sat for idols, like the Arabian in the wilderness, polluting the land with whoredoms and wickedness. Henry notes they were insatiable in lusting after false worships, seeking opportunity and courting new gods, not only sinners themselves but tempters to others. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: The showers have been withheld and there is no latter rain, yet she has a whore's forehead and refuses to be ashamed. Henry observes God corrected gently, withholding only the latter rain as an intimation of continual dependence, but it had no effect, and those past shame are past hope. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: Wilt thou not from this time cry, 'My father, thou art the guide of my youth?' Henry explains the expected return: coming to God as a father to beg pardon like a returning prodigal, and as the guide and husband of their youth, covenanting to follow his guidance henceforward. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: Will he reserve his anger for ever, or keep it to the end? Behold, she has spoken and done evil things as she could. Henry notes repenting sinners may encourage themselves that though God chide, he will not always chide, yet he also records the hypocritical reading: crying 'My Father' while still doing evil as they can, and flattering themselves his anger will end while treasuring up wrath. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: In the days of Josiah, God asks whether Jeremiah has seen what backsliding Israel has done, going up on every high mountain and under every green tree to play the harlot. Henry dates the sermon to Josiah's reformation, in which the king was hearty but the people were not sincere, and notes Israel's idolatry ran downhill from the golden calves at Dan and Bethel. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: God said after all these things, 'Turn thou unto me,' but she returned not, and her treacherous sister Judah saw it. Henry notes God by his prophets invited Israel to repent, pressing serious piety and return to the true God's worship, yet they persisted, and God keeps account of how often he has called and been refused. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: When backsliding Israel committed adultery, God put her away and gave her a bill of divorce, yet treacherous sister Judah feared not but played the harlot also. Henry explains the divorce as God throwing Israel out of his protection, leaving them an easy prey, scattering their synagogues and schools of the prophets, and excluding further claim to the covenant. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: Through the lightness of her whoredom she defiled the land and committed adultery with stones and stocks. Henry charges Judah with heartily concurring under wicked kings like Manasseh and Amon, so that notorious whoredom made the whole country an abomination to God. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: Yet for all this, treacherous Judah has not turned to God with her whole heart, but feignedly. Henry notes the present case under Josiah: the people were forced to external compliance, keeping a solemn passover and renewing covenants, but their hearts were not right with God, and feigned return deceives no one. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: Backsliding Israel has justified herself more than treacherous Judah. Henry warns this comparative justification stands Israel in little stead, but aggravates Judah doubly: more was expected from her sacred profession, and she refused the warning of Israel's ruin, so that God's judgments on others, unheeded, aggravate her destruction. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: Proclaim toward the north: 'Return, thou backsliding Israel, and I will not cause mine anger to fall upon you, for I am merciful and will not keep anger for ever.' Henry notes the call looks north to the captive ten tribes to show God had not forgotten them, and to upbraid Judah, since backsliding Israel will sooner accept mercy than treacherous Judah. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: Only acknowledge thine iniquity: thou hast transgressed against the Lord, scattered thy ways to strangers under every green tree, and not obeyed his voice. Henry calls the terms of pardon easy and reasonable: confess the corruption of nature, the actual transgressions, their multitude, and above all the disobedience in them, for the sinfulness of sin is the worst thing in it. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: Turn, O backsliding children, for God is married unto them, and will take one of a city and two of a family and bring them to Zion. Henry notes that of the many backsliders few return, like gleanings of the vintage, yet not one of the few shall be lost: though there be but one in a city, God will find that one and bring him safely to Zion. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: God will give pastors according to his heart, who shall feed them with knowledge and understanding. Henry observes a gathered church must be governed, not left to range like wild beasts, and pastors after God's heart feed the flock with the word of God rather than feeding themselves and fleecing the flock. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: When they are multiplied in the land, they shall say no more, 'The ark of the covenant of the Lord,' nor shall it come to mind or be visited any more. Henry reads this as the ceremonial law set aside in the Messiah's kingdom: Christ, the truth of all those types, is the ark and mercy-seat, and true worshippers worship in spirit and in truth. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: At that time Jerusalem shall be called the throne of the Lord, and all nations shall be gathered to it, no more walking after the imagination of their evil heart. Henry notes two things make the gospel church famous: God's special residence and government there as the throne of his glory, government, and grace, and the accession of the Gentiles gathered to it. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: The house of Judah shall walk with the house of Israel, coming together out of the land of the north to the land given to their fathers. Henry sees the return under Cyrus as a type of Jews and Gentiles united in the gospel church, one sheepfold under one shepherd, with all enmities slain. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: God asks how he shall put them among the children and give them a pleasant land, a goodly heritage of the hosts of nations, and answers that they shall call him 'My father' and not turn away. Henry notes the difficulty is our utter unworthiness, and God's own answer supplies the Spirit of adoption crying 'Abba, Father' and the fear in their hearts that they never turn from him. — Matthew Henry

20 COMMENTARY: Surely as a wife treacherously departs from her husband, so Israel has dealt treacherously with God. Henry calls this treason, not mere unfaithfulness: joined to God by a marriage covenant, they broke it against one who had always dealt kindly and faithfully with them. — Matthew Henry

21 COMMENTARY: A voice is heard upon the high places, weeping and supplications, for Israel has perverted its way and forgotten the Lord its God. Henry notes sin is the perverting of the way, forgetting God lies at the bottom of all sin, and prayers and tears well become consciences that know they have perverted their way, rather than hearts fretting against the Lord. — Matthew Henry

22 COMMENTARY: Return, ye backsliding children, and God will heal your backslidings; the people answer, 'Behold, we come unto thee, for thou art the Lord our God.' Henry notes God calls them children in tenderness, foolish and froward yet his sons whom he corrects but will not disinherit, and promises healing by pardoning mercy, quieting peace, and renewing grace. — Matthew Henry

23 COMMENTARY: Truly in vain is salvation hoped for from the hills and the multitude of mountains; truly in the Lord our God is the salvation of Israel. Henry notes the returning people now disclaim all creature confidences: they worshipped idols on hills and mountains, but from God alone comes every good thing, even salvation itself. — Matthew Henry

24 COMMENTARY: Shame has devoured the labour of their fathers from their youth, their flocks, herds, sons, and daughters. Henry observes true penitents learn to call their beloved sin 'shame' and to charge all their mischiefs upon it: their idolatry both provoked desolating judgments and consumed their substance in sacrifices, even human sacrifices. — Matthew Henry

25 COMMENTARY: We lie down in our shame and our confusion covers us, for we have sinned against the Lord our God, we and our fathers, from our youth even to this day, and have not obeyed his voice. Henry notes both penal and penitential shame surround them, and the worst thing in sin is the affront put upon God by disobeying his voice. — Matthew Henry
