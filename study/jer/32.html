Study Notes — Jeremiah

Chapter 32

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: The word that came to Jeremiah from the Lord in the tenth year of Zedekiah king of Judah, which was the eighteenth year of Nebuchadrezzar. Henry notes Jeremiah, being in prison for his prophecy, purchased a piece of ground: the word in Zedekiah's tenth year, Nebuchadnezzar's eighteenth, comes to the imprisoned prophet. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

2 COMMENTARY: For then the king of Babylon's army besieged Jerusalem: and Jeremiah the prophet was shut up in the court of the prison, which was in the king of Judah's house. Henry's Babylon army besieging Jerusalem, Jeremiah shut up in the prison court in the king's house, is the prison for prophecy: the sign of purchase comes from confinement. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

3 COMMENTARY: For Zedekiah king of Judah had shut him up, saying, Wherefore dost thou prophesy, and say, Thus says the Lord, Behold, I will give this city into the hand of the king of Babylon, and he shall take it. Henry's Zedekiah shut him up, wherefore dost thou prophesy, thus says the Lord, I will give this city to Babylon's king, and he shall take it, is the prophecy that imprisoned him. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

4 COMMENTARY: And Zedekiah king of Judah shall not escape out of the hand of the Chaldeans, but shall surely be delivered into the hand of the king of Babylon, and shall speak with him mouth to mouth, and his eyes shall behold his eyes. Henry's Zedekiah shall not escape Chaldean hands, but surely delivered to Babylon's king, speaking mouth to mouth, eyes beholding eyes, is the king's own fate foretold. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

5 COMMENTARY: And he shall lead Zedekiah to Babylon, and there shall he be until I visit him, says the Lord: though ye fight with the Chaldeans, ye shall not prosper. Henry's he shall lead Zedekiah to Babylon, there until I visit him, though ye fight Chaldeans ye shall not prosper, is the siege's hopeless end. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

6 COMMENTARY: And Jeremiah said, The word of the Lord came unto me, saying. Henry's Jeremiah said, the word of the Lord came unto me, is the imprisoned prophet still receiving the word: prison does not silence the word. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

7 COMMENTARY: Behold, Hanameel the son of Shallum thine uncle shall come unto thee, saying, Buy thee my field that is in Anathoth: for the right of redemption is thine to buy it. Henry's Hanameel, Shallum's son, thine uncle, shall come, buy my Anathoth field, for redemption's right is thine, is the purchase commanded: it concerns ministers to make it appear that they believe what they preach to others. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

8 COMMENTARY: So Hanameel mine uncle's son came to me in the court of the prison according to the word of the Lord, and said unto me, Buy my field, I pray thee, that is in Anathoth, which is in the country of Benjamin: for the right of inheritance is thine, and the redemption is thine; buy it for thyself. Then I knew that this was the word of the Lord. Henry's Hanameel came in the prison court according to the Lord's word, buy my Anathoth field in Benjamin, inheritance and redemption thine, buy for thyself, then I knew this was the Lord's word, is faith recognizing the word in the deed. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

9 COMMENTARY: And I bought the field of Hanameel my uncle's son, that was in Anathoth, and weighed him the money, even seventeen shekels of silver. Henry's I bought the field, weighed seventeen shekels of silver, is the purchase done: it is good to manage even our worldly affairs in faith, to do common business with reference to the providence and promise of God. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

10 COMMENTARY: And I subscribed the evidence, and sealed it, and took witnesses, and weighed him the money in the balances. Henry's subscribed, sealed, witnesses taken, money weighed in balances, is the deed made sure: common business done with reference to providence and promise. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

11 COMMENTARY: So I took the evidence of the purchase, both that which was sealed according to the law and custom, and that which was open. Henry's evidence taken, sealed according to law and custom, and open, is the double deed: the purchase is witnessed both ways. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

12 COMMENTARY: And I gave the evidence of the purchase unto Baruch the son of Neriah, the son of Maaseiah, in the sight of Hanameel mine uncle's son, and in the presence of the witnesses that subscribed the book of the purchase, before all the Jews that sat in the court of the prison. Henry's evidence given to Baruch, Neriah's son, in Hanameel's sight, witnesses' presence, before all Jews sitting in the prison court, is the public pledge: the deed is delivered openly. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

13 COMMENTARY: And I charged Baruch before them, saying. Henry's I charged Baruch before them is the charge given: the deed's keeper is charged before witnesses. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

14 COMMENTARY: Thus says the Lord of hosts, the God of Israel; Take these evidences, this evidence of the purchase, both which is sealed, and this evidence which is open; and put them in an earthen vessel, that they may continue many days. Henry's take these evidences, sealed and open, put in an earthen vessel, that they may continue many days, is the preservation: the deed must outlast the siege. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

15 COMMENTARY: For thus says the Lord of hosts, the God of Israel; Houses and fields and vineyards shall be possessed again in this land. Henry's houses, fields, and vineyards shall be possessed again in this land, is the sign's meaning: though Jerusalem was besieged, and the whole country likely to be laid waste, yet the time would come, when houses, and fields, and vineyards, should be again possessed. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

16 COMMENTARY: Now when I had delivered the evidence of the purchase unto Baruch the son of Neriah, I prayed unto the Lord, saying. Henry notes Jeremiah adores the Lord and his infinite perfections: now when I had delivered the evidence to Baruch, I prayed, and the purchase leads to prayer. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

17 COMMENTARY: Ah Lord God! behold, thou hast made the heaven and the earth by thy great power and stretched out arm, and there is nothing too hard for thee. Henry notes when at any time we are perplexed about the methods of Providence, it is good for us to look to first principles: let us consider that God is the fountain of all being, power, and life, that with him no difficulty is such as cannot be overcome, and ah Lord God, thou madest heaven and earth by great power and stretched arm, nothing too hard for thee, is the first principle. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

18 COMMENTARY: Thou showest lovingkindness unto thousands, and recompensest the iniquity of the fathers into the bosom of their children after them: the Great, the Mighty God, the Lord of hosts, is his name. Henry notes he is a God of boundless mercy, and a God of strict justice: thou showest lovingkindness to thousands, recompensest fathers' iniquity into children's bosom, Great, Mighty God, Lord of hosts his name, is mercy and justice together. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

19 COMMENTARY: Great in counsel, and mighty in work: for thine eyes are open upon all the ways of the sons of men: to give every one according to his ways, and according to the fruit of his doings. Henry notes he directs every thing for the best: great in counsel, mighty in work, eyes open upon all men's ways, giving every one according to ways and fruit of doings, is providence directing all. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

20 COMMENTARY: Which hast set signs and wonders in the land of Egypt, even unto this day, and in Israel, and among other men; and hast made thee a name, as at this day. Henry's signs and wonders in Egypt even unto this day, in Israel, and among other men, made thee a name as at this day, is the adoration's history: the God of first principles set signs and wonders. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

21 COMMENTARY: And hast brought forth thy people Israel out of the land of Egypt with signs, and with wonders, and with a strong hand, and with a stretched out arm, and with great terror. Henry's brought forth Israel from Egypt with signs, wonders, strong hand, stretched arm, great terror, is the deliverance remembered in prayer. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

22 COMMENTARY: And hast given them this land, which thou didst swear to their fathers to give them, a land flowing with milk and honey. Henry's given them this land, sworn to fathers, flowing with milk and honey, is the gift recalled: the land now besieged was sworn and given. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

23 COMMENTARY: And they came in, and possessed it; but they obeyed not thy voice, neither walked in thy law; they have done nothing of all that thou commandedst them to do: therefore thou hast caused all this evil to come upon them. Henry notes Jeremiah owns that God was righteous in causing evil to come upon them: they came in and possessed, but obeyed not voice, walked not in law, did nothing commanded, therefore thou caused all this evil upon them, is righteousness owned. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

24 COMMENTARY: Behold the mounts, they are come unto the city to take it; and the city is given into the hand of the Chaldeans, that fight against it, because of the sword, and of the famine, and of the pestilence: and what thou hast spoken is come to pass; and, behold, thou seest it. Henry notes whatever trouble we are in, personal or public, we may comfort ourselves that the Lord sees it, and knows how to remedy it: behold mounts come to take the city, city given to fighting Chaldeans because of sword, famine, pestilence, what thou spokest is come to pass, behold thou seest it, is trouble seen by the Lord. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

25 COMMENTARY: And thou hast said unto me, O Lord God, Buy thee the field for money, and take witnesses; for the city is given into the hand of the Chaldeans. Henry notes we must not dispute God's will, but we may seek to know what it means: thou saidst unto me, buy the field for money, take witnesses, for the city is given to Chaldeans, is the perplexity laid before God, not disputed. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

26 COMMENTARY: Then came the word of the Lord unto Jeremiah, saying. Henry notes God's answer discovers the purposes of his wrath against that generation of the Jews, and the purposes of his grace concerning future generations: then came the word of the Lord unto Jeremiah, and the answer begins. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

27 COMMENTARY: Behold, I am the Lord, the God of all flesh: is there any thing too hard for me? Henry's behold, I am the Lord, God of all flesh, is there anything too hard for me, is the answer's first principle: with him no difficulty cannot be overcome. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

28 COMMENTARY: Therefore thus says the Lord; Behold, I will give this city into the hand of the Chaldeans, and into the hand of Nebuchadrezzar king of Babylon, and he shall take it. Henry's therefore, behold, I will give this city to Chaldeans and Nebuchadnezzar, and he shall take it, is wrath's purpose: it is sin, and nothing else, that ruins them. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

29 COMMENTARY: And the Chaldeans, that fight against this city, shall come and set fire on this city, and burn it with the houses, upon whose roofs they have offered incense unto Baal, and poured out drink offerings unto other gods, to provoke me to anger. Henry's Chaldeans shall come, set fire, burn city with houses whose roofs offered incense to Baal and drink offerings to other gods, to provoke anger, is sin ruining: the burning is provoked. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

30 COMMENTARY: For the children of Israel and the children of Judah have only done evil before me from their youth: for the children of Israel have only provoked me to anger with the work of their hands, says the Lord. Henry's Israel and Judah only done evil from youth, only provoked anger with works of hands, is the ruin's cause: sin, and nothing else. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

31 COMMENTARY: For this city has been to me as a provocation of mine anger and of my fury from the day that they built it even unto this day; that I should remove it from before my face. Henry's this city has been a provocation of anger and fury from the day built even unto this day, that I should remove it from before my face, is the long provocation: the city built became provocation. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

32 COMMENTARY: Because of all the evil of the children of Israel and of the children of Judah, which they have done to provoke me to anger, they, their kings, their princes, their priests, and their prophets, and the men of Judah, and the inhabitants of Jerusalem. Henry's because of all evil of Israel and Judah, to provoke anger, they, kings, princes, priests, prophets, men of Judah, inhabitants of Jerusalem, is the provokers named: all orders provoked. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

33 COMMENTARY: And they have turned unto me the back, and not the face: though I taught them, rising up early and teaching them, yet they have not hearkened to receive instruction. Henry's turned back, not face, though I taught rising early, yet not hearkened to receive instruction, is the refusal: teaching rose early, backs turned. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

34 COMMENTARY: But they set their abominations in the house, which is called by my name, to defile it. Henry's set abominations in the house called by my name, to defile it, is the defilement: sin in the named house ruins. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

35 COMMENTARY: And they built the high places of Baal, which are in the valley of the son of Hinnom, to cause their sons and their daughters to pass through the fire unto Molech; which I commanded them not, neither came it into my mind, that they should do this abomination, to cause Judah to sin. Henry's built Baal's high places in Hinnom's valley, to cause sons and daughters through fire to Molech, which I commanded not, neither came into my mind, to cause Judah to sin, is the abomination: the worst sin is uncommanded and unthought. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

36 COMMENTARY: And now therefore thus says the Lord, the God of Israel, concerning this city, whereof ye say, It shall be delivered into the hand of the king of Babylon by the sword, and by the famine, and by the pestilence. Henry notes this people were now at length brought to despair: and now therefore thus says the Lord concerning this city, whereof ye say, delivered to Babylon's king by sword, famine, pestilence, is the despair stated. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

37 COMMENTARY: Behold, I will gather them out of all countries, whither I have driven them in mine anger, and in my fury, and in great wrath; and I will bring them again unto this place, and I will cause them to dwell safely. Henry notes but God gives hope of mercy which he had in store for them hereafter: behold, I will gather from all countries driven in anger, fury, great wrath, bring again to this place, cause to dwell safely, is mercy in store. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

38 COMMENTARY: And they shall be my people, and I will be their God. Henry notes doubtless the promises are sure to all believers: God will own them for his, and he will prove himself theirs, and they shall be my people, I their God, is the owning. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

39 COMMENTARY: And I will give them one heart, and one way, that they may fear me for ever, for the good of them, and of their children after them. Henry notes he will give them a heart to fear him: all true Christians shall have a disposition to mutual love, and I will give one heart, one way, fear me for ever, for good of them and children after them, is the heart given. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

40 COMMENTARY: And I will make an everlasting covenant with them, that I will not turn away from them, to do them good; but I will put my fear in their hearts, that they shall not depart from me. Henry notes though they may have different views about lesser things, they shall all be one in the great things of God, in their views of the evil of sin, and the low estate of fallen man, the way of salvation through the Saviour, the nature of true holiness, the vanity of the world, and the importance of eternal things: I will make an everlasting covenant, not turn away to do good, put fear in hearts, not depart from me, is the oneness covenant. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

41 COMMENTARY: Yea, I will rejoice over them to do them good, and I will plant them in this land assuredly with my whole heart and with my whole soul. Henry notes whom God loves, he loves to the end: we have no reason to distrust God's faithfulness and constancy, but only our own hearts, and I will rejoice over them to do good, plant them in this land assuredly with whole heart and whole soul, is love to the end. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

42 COMMENTARY: For thus says the Lord; Like as I have brought all this great evil upon this people, so will I bring upon them all the good that I have promised them. Henry notes he will settle them again in Canaan, and these promises shall surely be performed: like as I brought all this great evil, so will I bring all the good promised, is the sure performance. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

43 COMMENTARY: And fields shall be bought in this land, whereof ye say, It is desolate without man or beast; it is given into the hand of the Chaldeans. Henry notes Jeremiah's purchase was the pledge of many a purchase that should be made after the captivity: fields shall be bought in this land, whereof ye say desolate without man or beast, given to Chaldeans, is the pledge multiplied. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

44 COMMENTARY: Men shall buy fields for money, and subscribe evidences, and seal them, and take witnesses in the land of Benjamin, and in the places about Jerusalem, and in the cities of Judah, and in the cities of the mountains, and in the cities of the valley, and in the cities of the south: for I will cause their captivity to return, says the Lord. Henry notes those inheritances are but faint resemblances of the possessions in the heavenly Canaan, which are kept for all who have God's fear in their hearts, and do not depart from him: let us then bear up under our trials, assured we shall obtain all the good he has promised us, and men shall buy fields for money, subscribe, seal, take witnesses in Benjamin, about Jerusalem, Judah's cities, mountains, valley, south, for I will cause captivity to return, is the buying resumed everywhere. — Matthew Henry (Concise)
