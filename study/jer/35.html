Study Notes — Jeremiah

Chapter 35

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: The word which came unto Jeremiah from the Lord in the days of Jehoiakim the son of Josiah king of Judah, saying. Henry notes Jonadab was famous for wisdom and piety, living nearly 300 years before: the word in Jehoiakim's days sets the Rechabite trial, and Jonadab's fame frames it. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

2 COMMENTARY: Go unto the house of the Rechabites, and speak unto them, and bring them into the house of the Lord, into one of the chambers, and give them wine to drink. Henry's go unto the Rechabites, speak, bring into the Lord's house, into a chamber, give wine to drink, is the trial set: Jonadab charged his posterity not to drink wine, and the trial offers it. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

3 COMMENTARY: Then I took Jaazaniah the son of Jeremiah, the son of Habaziniah, and his brethren, and all his sons, and the whole house of the Rechabites. Henry's Jaazaniah, Jeremiah's son, Habaziniah's son, brethren, sons, whole house taken, is the whole house brought: Jonadab's posterity observed his rules strictly. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

4 COMMENTARY: And I brought them into the house of the Lord, into the chamber of the sons of Hanan, the son of Igdaliah, a man of God, which was by the chamber of the princes, which was above the chamber of Maaseiah the son of Shallum, the keeper of the door. Henry's brought into the Lord's house, Hanan's sons' chamber, by princes' chamber, above Maaseiah's door-keeper chamber, is the public chamber: the trial is set where all may see. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

5 COMMENTARY: And I set before the sons of the house of the Rechabites pots full of wine, and cups, and I said unto them, Drink ye wine. Henry's pots full of wine and cups set before Rechabites' sons, drink ye wine, is the temptation: the consideration that we are strangers and pilgrims should oblige us to abstain from all fleshly lusts, and wine is set before strangers. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

6 COMMENTARY: But they said, We will drink no wine: for Jonadab the son of Rechab our father commanded us, saying, Ye shall drink no wine, neither ye, nor your sons for ever. Henry's we will drink no wine, for Jonadab our father commanded, ye shall drink no wine, neither ye nor sons for ever, is the strict observance: Jonadab charged his posterity not to drink wine, and they keep it. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

7 COMMENTARY: Neither shall ye build house, nor sow seed, nor plant vineyard, nor have any: but all your days ye shall dwell in tents; that ye may live many days in the land where ye be strangers. Henry notes he also appointed them to dwell in tents, or movable dwelling: this would teach them not to think of settling anywhere in this world, and to keep low would be the way to continue long in the land where they were strangers, and neither build house, sow seed, plant vineyard, nor have any, but dwell in tents all days, that ye may live many days where strangers, is that low keeping. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

8 COMMENTARY: Thus have we obeyed the voice of Jonadab the son of Rechab our father in all that he has charged us, to drink no wine all our days, we, our wives, our sons, nor our daughters. Henry notes humility and contentment are always the best policy, and men's surest protection: thus have we obeyed Jonadab's voice in all charged, to drink no wine all days, we, wives, sons, daughters, is humility and contentment kept. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

9 COMMENTARY: Nor to build houses for us to dwell in: neither have we vineyard, nor field, nor seed. Henry notes also, that they might not run into unlawful pleasures, they were to deny themselves even lawful delights: nor build houses to dwell in, neither vineyard, field, nor seed, is lawful delight denied. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

10 COMMENTARY: But we have dwelt in tents, and have obeyed, and done according to all that Jonadab our father commanded us. Henry notes let them have little to lose, and then losing times would be the less dreadful: let them sit loose to what they had, and then they might with less pain be stript of it, and we have dwelt in tents, obeyed, done according to all Jonadab commanded, is sitting loose. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

11 COMMENTARY: But it came to pass, when Nebuchadrezzar king of Babylon came up into the land, that we said, Come, and let us go to Jerusalem for fear of the army of the Chaldeans, and for fear of the army of the Syrians: so we dwell at Jerusalem. Henry notes Jonadab's posterity observed these rules strictly, only using proper means for their safety in a time of general suffering: when Nebuchadnezzar came up, we said, come, let us go to Jerusalem for fear of Chaldean and Syrian armies, so we dwell at Jerusalem, is proper means for safety, not rule-breaking. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

12 COMMENTARY: Then came the word of the Lord unto Jeremiah, saying. Henry notes the trial of the Rechabites' constancy was for a sign: then came the word unto Jeremiah, and the sign's application begins. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

13 COMMENTARY: Thus says the Lord of hosts, the God of Israel; Go and tell the men of Judah and the inhabitants of Jerusalem, Will ye not receive instruction to hearken to my words? says the Lord. Henry notes it made the disobedience of the Jews to God the more marked: go and tell Judah's men and Jerusalem's inhabitants, will ye not receive instruction to hearken to my words, is the marked disobedience asked. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

14 COMMENTARY: The words of Jonadab the son of Rechab, that he commanded his sons not to drink wine, are performed; for unto this day they drink none, but obey their father's commandment: notwithstanding I have spoken unto you, rising early and speaking; but ye hearkened not unto me. Henry notes the Rechabites were obedient to one who was but a man like themselves, and Jonadab never did for his seed what God has done for his people: Jonadab's words performed, unto this day they drink none, obeying father's commandment, notwithstanding I spoke rising early, but ye hearkened not, is man obeyed and God unhearkened. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

15 COMMENTARY: I have sent also unto you all my servants the prophets, rising up early and sending them, saying, Return ye now every man from his evil way, and amend your doings, and go not after other gods to serve them, and ye shall dwell in the land which I have given to you and to your fathers: but ye have not inclined your ear, nor hearkened unto me. Henry's I sent all my servants the prophets, rising early and sending, return every man from evil way, amend doings, go not after other gods, dwell in the land given to you and fathers, but ye inclined not ear, hearkened not, is the prophets' early sending refused. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

16 COMMENTARY: Because the sons of Jonadab the son of Rechab have performed the commandment of their father, which he commanded them; but this people has not hearkened unto me. Henry's because Jonadab's sons performed their father's commandment, but this people has not hearkened unto me, is the contrast: performed man-command against unhearkened God-command. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

17 COMMENTARY: Therefore thus says the Lord God of hosts, the God of Israel; Behold, I will bring upon Judah and upon all the inhabitants of Jerusalem all the evil that I have pronounced against them: because I have spoken unto them, but they have not heard; and I have called unto them, but they have not answered. Henry's therefore, behold, I will bring upon Judah and all Jerusalem's inhabitants all evil pronounced, because I spoke but they heard not, called but they answered not, is the judgment: disobedience marked brings pronounced evil. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

18 COMMENTARY: And Jeremiah said unto the house of the Rechabites, Thus says the Lord of hosts, the God of Israel; Because ye have obeyed the commandment of Jonadab your father, and kept all his precepts, and done according unto all that he has commanded you. Henry notes mercy is promised to the Rechabites: Jeremiah said unto the Rechabites, thus says the Lord, because ye obeyed Jonadab's commandment, kept all precepts, done according to all commanded, is the mercy promised for obedience. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

19 COMMENTARY: Therefore thus says the Lord of hosts, the God of Israel; Jonadab the son of Rechab shall not want a man to stand before me for ever. Henry notes we are not told respecting the performance of this promise, but doubtless it was performed, and travellers say the Rechabites may be found a separate people to this day: let us follow the counsels of our pious forefathers, and we shall find good in so doing, and Jonadab shall not want a man to stand before me for ever, is the promise whose performance is doubtless. — Matthew Henry (Concise)
