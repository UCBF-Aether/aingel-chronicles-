Study Notes — Leviticus

Chapter 7

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: This chapter continues the priestly duty laws for trespass offerings, which are most holy, with part consumed on the altar and part as priests' perquisite. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: The trespass offering is killed where the burnt offering is killed and its blood sprinkled round about the altar, under the same preliminary regulations as burnt offerings. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: All its fat is offered from it, the fat tail and the fat covering the entrails, as the fatty parts are consumed on the altar as in sin and peace offerings. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: The kidneys with their fat and the fatty lobe above the liver are removed with the rest of the fat portion, following the established fat pattern. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: The priest burns these fat portions on the altar as an offering made by fire, which is what makes it a trespass offering. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: Every male among the priests may eat the flesh in a holy place, as the common share of the priestly order who live upon these provisions and meet at a common table. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: The trespass offering follows one law with the sin offering, and the priest who makes atonement with it shall have it, as the officiating minister's fee. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: The officiating priest keeps for himself the skin of the burnt offering he has offered, since all flesh and fat are consumed and only the skin remains, a valuable perquisite thought to continue a patriarchal usage from Adam. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: Every meat offering baked in the oven, prepared in a covered pan, or in a pan belongs to the priest who offers it, as the officiating minister's exclusive fee. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: Every other meat offering, whether mixed with oil or dry, belongs to all Aaron's sons equally, as the common provision of the priestly order. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: The law of peace offerings now follows, with leavened bread offered besides the usual accompaniments, as thanksgiving bread common at feasts. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: For a thanksgiving peace offering, unleavened cakes mingled with oil, wafers anointed with oil, and cakes of fried flour are brought with the sacrifice, together with leavened bread as part of the festive thanksgiving. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: Leavened bread is offered with the sacrifice of thanksgiving peace offerings, because such bread is common at feasts and marks the offering as a thanksgiving meal. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: Of the whole oblation one cake from each kind is offered as a heave offering to the LORD, belonging to the priest who sprinkles the blood of the peace offerings, as the officiating minister's portion. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: The flesh of a thanksgiving peace offering shall be eaten the same day it is offered, with none left until morning, keeping the feast within the day of offering. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: A vow or voluntary peace offering may be eaten on the day of offering and on the day following, a wider window than the thanksgiving offering. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: If any flesh of the sacrifice remains until the third day, it shall be burnt with fire, a prohibition thought to prevent a superstitious notion that some virtue or holiness belonged to the kept flesh. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

18 COMMENTARY: Flesh eaten on the third day makes the sacrifice unacceptable to God and not profitable to the offerer, since the appointed time has been violated. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

19 COMMENTARY: Flesh that has touched any unclean thing shall not be eaten but burnt with fire, while flesh that is clean may be eaten by all who are clean, preserving the holiness of the shared feast. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

20 COMMENTARY: A person unclean who eats peace-offering flesh shall be cut off from his people, that is, excluded from Israelite privileges under a sentence of excommunication. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

21 COMMENTARY: Whoever touches an unclean thing, whether the uncleanness of man, an unclean beast, or any abominable unclean thing, some copies reading reptile, and then eats peace-offering flesh shall likewise be cut off. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

22 COMMENTARY: The prohibition of fat is now restated, as explained at Leviticus 3:17, that the reserved fat belongs to the LORD and is not to be eaten. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

23 COMMENTARY: No fat of ox, sheep, or goat shall be eaten, since these are the sacrificial animals whose fat is reserved for the altar. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

24 COMMENTARY: Fat of a beast that dies of itself or is torn may be used for other purposes, but shall in no wise be eaten, maintaining the distinction between altar fat and ordinary use. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

25 COMMENTARY: Whoever eats the fat of a beast offered by fire to the LORD shall be cut off from his people, because he has taken the LORD's portion. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

26 COMMENTARY: No manner of blood shall be eaten, whether of fowl or of beast, in any dwelling, the blood prohibition resting on reverence for the atoning blood, as at Leviticus 3:17. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

27 COMMENTARY: Whoever eats any blood shall be cut off from his people, under the same exclusion as for eating reserved fat or eating while unclean. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

28 COMMENTARY: The priests' portion from peace offerings is now prescribed, completing the summing up of priestly privileges and duties. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

29 COMMENTARY: The offerer himself must bring his peace offering to the LORD, with his own hands, in order to show that the sacrifice is voluntary. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

30 COMMENTARY: His own hands bring the offerings made by fire, the fat with the breast, so the breast may be waved as a wave offering before the LORD. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

31 COMMENTARY: The priest burns the fat on the altar, but the breast, having been waved to and fro as devoted to God, is given to Aaron and his sons generally. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

32 COMMENTARY: The right shoulder, or thigh, is given to the priest as a heave offering from the peace offerings, as the officiating priest's particular perquisite. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

33 COMMENTARY: The son of Aaron who offers the blood and the fat of the peace offering shall have the right thigh for his part, since he is the officiating minister. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

34 COMMENTARY: The wave breast and heave shoulder are taken from Israel's peace offerings and given to Aaron and his sons by a perpetual statute, the breast for the order generally and the shoulder for the officiant. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

35 COMMENTARY: This is the anointing portion of Aaron and his sons from the offerings made by fire, from the day Moses presented them to minister as priests, anointing here meaning office or dignity. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

36 COMMENTARY: The LORD commanded this portion to be given them by Israel on the day He anointed them, by a statute forever throughout their generations. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

37 COMMENTARY: These verses sum up the laws of burnt offering, meat offering, sin offering, trespass offering, consecrations, and peace offering, as the general law regulating priestly privileges and duties. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

38 COMMENTARY: All these laws were commanded by the LORD to Moses on Mount Sinai, on the day He commanded Israel to offer their offerings in the wilderness of Sinai. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
