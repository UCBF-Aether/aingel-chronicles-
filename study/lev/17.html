Study Notes — Leviticus

Chapter 17

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: The LORD speaks to Moses, giving the law that blood of beasts must be offered at the tabernacle door. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: The command is spoken to Aaron, his sons, and all Israel, so the whole people, not priests alone, are bound by the place and blood laws. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: Whoever kills an ox, lamb, or goat in or outside the camp must bring it to the tabernacle, since killing for food in the desert was almost always a feast eaten in company, like a peace offering, and public slaughter prevented private sacrifice to a favourite idol. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: If he does not bring it to the tabernacle door to offer it before the LORD, blood guilt is imputed to him, he has shed blood, and he shall be cut off, the law being observable only while encamped within accessible distance of the tabernacle. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: The purpose is that Israel bring sacrifices offered in the open field to the priest at the tabernacle door as peace offerings, the law being directed against Egyptian habits of field sacrifice. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: The priest sprinkles the blood on the LORD's altar at the tabernacle door and burns the fat for a sweet savour, the public rite replacing any private field offering. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: They shall no more offer sacrifices to devils, literally goats, after whom they have played the harlot, a reference to goat-symbolled Pan, Faunus, and Saturn worshipped licentiously in Egypt, especially at Mendes. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: Whatever man of Israel or stranger offers a burnt offering or sacrifice must bring it to the tabernacle, since before the law men worshipped wherever they pleased, but afterward religion could be acceptably performed only at the appointed place, as a preventive of idolatry at grove and field altars. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: If he does not bring it to the tabernacle door to offer it to the LORD, that man shall be cut off from his people, the place restriction applying to stranger as well as native. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: Whoever eats any blood, God will set His face against him and cut him off, God's face denoting anger, with death for a public crime and vengeance for a secret one, the rite targeted being the Zabian practice of eating flesh and sometimes blood where it was poured out to contract friendship with deities and seek visions. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: For the life of the flesh is in the blood, and God has given it on the altar to make atonement for souls, God as sovereign author and proprietor of nature reserving blood to Himself and allowing men only one use of it, in sacrifices. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: Therefore no one among Israel, nor any stranger dwelling among them, shall eat blood, the prohibition extending beyond natives to sojourners. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: Whoever hunts and catches an animal or bird that may be eaten shall pour out its blood and cover it with dust, unlike heathen sportsmen who poured blood as a libation to the god of the chase, Israel being debarred from superstitious uses by covering it. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: For it is the life of all flesh, its blood sustains its life, therefore Israel shall not eat the blood of any flesh, for the life of all flesh is its blood, and whoever eats it shall be cut off. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: Every person who eats what died of itself or was torn by beasts, native or stranger, shall wash his clothes, bathe in water, and be unclean until evening, then he shall be clean, the uncleanness lasting from discovery of the fault until evening. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: But if he does not wash his clothes or bathe his body, he shall bear his guilt, the washing being the appointed removal of the defilement, binding only on an Israelite in its dietary form. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
