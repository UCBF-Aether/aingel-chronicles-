Study Notes — Leviticus

Chapter 12

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: After laws about defilement from without, through unclean animals and carcasses, a series of statutes follows about impurity connected with persons themselves, traced through life's stages to death. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: A woman who has conceived and borne a male child is unclean seven days, as in the days of separation for her infirmity, the languor of menstruation, and everyone coming near her contracts a similar defilement. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: On the eighth day the child's foreskin is circumcised, at the end of the mother's week of seclusion, as ordered in Genesis 17. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: She then continues in the blood of her purification thirty-three days, touching no hallowed thing nor coming into the sanctuary, visitors being allowed to approach her though she is still excluded from public ordinances. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: If she bears a female child she is unclean two weeks and continues sixty-six days, a longer period taken as a symbolical remembrancer that woman was the introducer of sin and bore its heaviest punishment, a stigma removed by Christ. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: When her purifying days are fulfilled, for son or daughter, she brings a lamb of the first year for a burnt offering and a young pigeon or turtledove for a sin offering to the tabernacle door, on the forty-first day for a boy and the eighty-first for a girl. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: The priest offers them before the LORD and makes atonement for her, and she is clean from the flow of her blood, the burnt offering implying renewed surrender to God and the sin offering recalling the origin of sin and the child's fallen nature. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: If she cannot bring a lamb, she brings two turtledoves or two young pigeons, one for a burnt offering and one for a sin offering, and the priest makes atonement and she is clean, the offering Mary made, proving the humble condition of Jesus' family. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
