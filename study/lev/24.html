Study Notes — Leviticus

Chapter 24

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

2 COMMENTARY: Israel shall bring pure olive oil beaten, cold-drawn and of great purity, for the light, to cause lamps to burn continually, repeating Exodus 27. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: Without the veil, in the tabernacle, Aaron shall order the lamps from evening to morning continually, a perpetual statute, the priests' daily presence being necessary to clean and trim them. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: He shall order the lamps upon the pure candlestick, so called because of pure gold, continually, symbolical of the light ministers are to diffuse through the Church. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: Fine flour shall be taken and twelve cakes baked, two tenth deals, about thirteen and a half pounds, in each, for the showbread previously appointed in Exodus 25, baked by Levites with flour furnished by the people. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: They shall be set in two rows, six on a row, upon the pure table before the LORD, the twelve cakes corresponding to the tribes. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: Pure frankincense shall be put upon each row, that it may be on the bread for a memorial, an offering made by fire, the frankincense being burnt so the showbread is called an offering by fire. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: Every Sabbath he shall set it in order before the LORD continually, from Israel by an everlasting covenant, hot loaves replacing stale ones each week. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: It shall be Aaron's and his sons', eaten in a holy place, for it is most holy from offerings made by fire by a perpetual statute, stale loaves being eaten only by priests except in necessity. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: The son of an Israelite woman whose father was Egyptian goes out among Israel and strives with an Israelite in the camp, a mixed-multitude marriage connection not infrequent after the exodus. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: The Israelite woman's son blasphemes the LORD's name and curses, and is brought to Moses, his mother being Shelomith daughter of Dibri of Dan, the youth venting rage in Egyptian fashion, cursing the object of his opponent's reverence, the King and God of the Hebrews. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: He who cursed shall be brought forth without the camp, all who heard him laying hands on his head, and all the congregation stoning him, executions taking place outside the camp since flagrant offenders must be thrust out of a holy people, and hand-laying testifying publicly and devolving guilt on the criminal alone. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: He who blasphemes the LORD's name shall surely be put to death, all the congregation stoning him, stranger as well as native, since strangers joining the camp, though not circumcised, become amenable to the law, especially blasphemy, from which Jews later derived the practice of not uttering Yahweh's name. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: He who kills any man shall surely be put to death, these verses repeating social laws whose penalties are inflicted not by private parties but through judges. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

18 COMMENTARY: He who kills a beast shall make it good, beast for beast, the same judicial repetition governing property injury. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

19 COMMENTARY: If a man causes a blemish in his neighbour, as he has done so shall it be done to him, the lex talionis being administered by judges, not private vengeance. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

20 COMMENTARY: Breach for breach, eye for eye, tooth for tooth, as he has caused a blemish in a man, so shall it be done to him, the measure of judicial penalty matching the injury. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

21 COMMENTARY: He who kills a beast shall restore it, but he who kills a man shall be put to death, distinguishing property restoration from capital homicide. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

22 COMMENTARY: There shall be one manner of law for stranger and native, for the LORD is their God, the same judicial law applying to the mixed multitude as to Israel. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

23 COMMENTARY: Moses speaks to Israel, and they bring forth him who cursed out of the camp and stone him, doing as the LORD commanded Moses, stoning thereafter becoming the established punishment for blasphemy. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

No note for verse(s) 1, 12, 13, 15 — the public-domain sources consulted give no distinct explanation for these verses beyond the list itself, so nothing was invented for them.
