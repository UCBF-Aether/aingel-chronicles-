Study Notes — Leviticus

Chapter 13

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: The LORD speaks to Moses and Aaron, giving the priest, not an elder or house head, the examination of every suspected case, so the law serves ritual as well as sanitary ends. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: A man with a rising, scab, or bright spot like leprosy is brought to Aaron or his sons, since the disease, endemic in Egypt, had appeared extensively among Israel shortly after the exodus, seeded by bondage hardships. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: The priest looks on the plague, and if the hair in it is turned white and the plague appears deeper than the skin, it is leprosy and he pronounces him unclean, sending him out to a lazaretto. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: If the bright spot is white but not deeper than the skin and the hair is not white, the priest shuts him up seven days, doubtful symptoms requiring domestic confinement and a second examination. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: On the seventh day the priest looks again, and if the plague stays in its place and has not spread, he shuts him up seven more days, since a doubtful eruption needs a second week for its character to manifest. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: On the seventh day, if the plague is somewhat dark, faint or beginning to disappear, and has not spread, the priest pronounces him clean, and he washes his clothes, the detention itself having tainted him ceremonially. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: If the scab spreads much after he has been seen for his cleansing, he shall be seen again, since a doubtful case turning malignant appears as a perpetually enlarging dusky patch. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: If the priest sees the scab has spread in the skin, he pronounces it leprosy and him unclean, the spreading proving contagion and sentencing separation from family and friends. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: When the plague of leprosy is in a man, he shall be brought to the priest, the same priestly examination governing every confirmed case. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: If the priest sees a white rising that has turned the hair white with quick raw flesh in it, this bright white leprosy is the most malignant variety, marked by a glossy spreading scale on an elevated base depressed in the middle. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: It is an old leprosy in his skin, and the priest pronounces him unclean without shutting him up, since he is already unclean and temporary confinement is unnecessary. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: If leprosy breaks out abroad and covers all the skin from head to foot, wherever the priest looks, the case is governed by the whole-body rule rather than the local rising rule. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: If the priest sees the leprosy has covered all his flesh, he pronounces him clean, since a non-ulcerated whole-body spread has run its course and lost contagious power, and he is restored while dry scales remain. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: When raw flesh appears in him, he shall be unclean, because ulceration changes the whole-body case into an inveterate one. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: The priest sees the raw flesh and pronounces him unclean, for raw flesh is unclean, its purulent matter being able to infect others through absorbent vessels. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: If the raw flesh turns again and is changed to white, he shall come to the priest, the change reversing the ulcerated sign. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: The priest sees it turned white and pronounces him clean, for the plague is healed, as with the whole-body case that has exhausted itself. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

18 COMMENTARY: Flesh in whose skin there was a boil and is healed is a further affection that may tend to leprosy, though not a decided symptom alone. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

19 COMMENTARY: If in the boil's place there is a white rising or a bright spot, white and somewhat reddish, it shall be shown to the priest, as a possible leprous development from the boil. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

20 COMMENTARY: If the priest sees it deeper than the skin with hair turned white, he pronounces it leprosy broken out of the boil, and him unclean. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

21 COMMENTARY: If the priest sees no white hair, no depth below the skin, but some darkness, he shuts him up seven days, the doubtful boil case requiring confinement. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

22 COMMENTARY: If it spreads much in the skin, the priest pronounces him unclean, for it is a plague of leprosy spreading from the boil. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

23 COMMENTARY: If the bright spot stays in its place and has not spread, it is a burning boil, and the priest pronounces him clean. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

24 COMMENTARY: Flesh with a hot burning, a fiery inflammation or carbuncle, whose quick flesh has a white bright spot, somewhat reddish or white, is likewise an affection tending to leprosy. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

25 COMMENTARY: If the priest sees hair turned white in the bright spot and it appears deeper than the skin, it is leprosy broken out of the burning, and he pronounces him unclean. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

26 COMMENTARY: If the priest sees no white hair and it is no lower than the skin but somewhat dark, he shuts him up seven days, as with the doubtful boil. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

27 COMMENTARY: On the seventh day, if it spreads in the skin, the priest pronounces him unclean, for it is the plague of leprosy. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

28 COMMENTARY: If the spot stays and has not spread but is somewhat dark, it is a rising of the burning, and the priest pronounces him clean, for it is an inflammation of the burning. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

29 COMMENTARY: A man or woman with a plague on the head or beard is examined under the dry scall rule, where leprosy is deeper than the skin and the hair becomes thin and yellow. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

30 COMMENTARY: If the priest sees the scall deeper than the skin with thin yellow hair, he pronounces it a dry scall, even leprosy upon the head or beard, and the person unclean. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

31 COMMENTARY: If the priest sees the scall not deeper than the skin and no black hair in it, he shuts up the scall sufferer seven days. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

32 COMMENTARY: On the seventh day, if the scall has not spread, there is no yellow hair, and it is not deeper than the skin, the priest shuts him up seven more days. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

33 COMMENTARY: He shall be shaven, but the scall shall he not shave, and the priest shuts him up seven more days, isolating the scall for a further examination. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

34 COMMENTARY: On the seventh day, if the scall has not spread and is not deeper than the skin, the priest pronounces him clean, and he washes his clothes and is clean. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

35 COMMENTARY: If the scall spreads much in the skin after his cleansing, it is judged by spreading, as with other doubtful cases turned malignant. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

36 COMMENTARY: The priest looks, and if the scall has spread, he need not seek for yellow hair, for he is unclean. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

37 COMMENTARY: If the scall stays in his sight and black hair grows up in it, the scall is healed and he is clean, and the priest pronounces him clean. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

38 COMMENTARY: If a man or woman has bright spots, even white bright spots, in the skin, this modification is distinguished by a dull white colour and is entirely cutaneous, never injuring the constitution. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

39 COMMENTARY: If the priest sees the bright spots are darkish white, it is a freckled spot growing in the skin, and he is clean, since this common form is not contagious and needs no exclusion. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

40 COMMENTARY: A man whose hair falls off his head is bald, yet he is clean, baldness alone not being a decisive sign of leprosy. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

41 COMMENTARY: If his hair falls off from the forehead, he is forehead bald, yet he is clean, the same rule applying to the front of the head. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

42 COMMENTARY: If in the bald head or bald forehead there is a white reddish sore, it is leprosy sprung up there, judged by the sore in addition to the baldness. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

43 COMMENTARY: The priest looks on it, and if the rising of the sore is white reddish in the bald head or forehead, as leprosy appears in the skin, the baldness case becomes a leprosy case. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

44 COMMENTARY: He is a leprous man, unclean utterly, and the priest shall pronounce him utterly unclean, since his plague is in his head. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

45 COMMENTARY: The leper's clothes shall be rent, his head bare, and he shall put a covering upon his upper lip and cry, Unclean, unclean, all tokens of heavy calamity and mourning proclaiming that everyone must shun him. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

46 COMMENTARY: All the days the plague is in him he shall be defiled and dwell alone, without the camp, in a lazaretto by himself or with other lepers, a seclusion tantamount to banishment. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

47 COMMENTARY: The garment also in which the plague of leprosy is, whether woollen or linen, is now examined, since Hebrew dress consisted exclusively of these materials. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

48 COMMENTARY: Whether the plague is in the warp or woof of linen or woollen, or in a skin, it falls under the garment law, warp and woof here meaning the flax or wool before interweaving, or the skin in natural or artificial form. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

49 COMMENTARY: If the plague is greenish or reddish in garment or skin, that coloured stain is the first symptom exciting suspicion, analogous to the malignant leprosy on the body. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

50 COMMENTARY: The priest looks on the plague and shuts up the plagued thing seven days, the garment receiving the same rigid priestly inspection as a person. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

51 COMMENTARY: On the seventh day, if the plague has spread in the garment, it is a fretting, exasperated or malignant, leprosy, and he pronounces it unclean. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

52 COMMENTARY: He shall burn the garment, whether warp or woof, woollen or linen, or skin article, for it is a fretting leprosy and shall be burnt in the fire. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

53 COMMENTARY: If the priest sees the plague has not spread, he commands the plagued thing to be washed and shuts it up seven more days. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

54 COMMENTARY: After washing, the priest looks again and shuts it up seven days, the second experimental week deciding a non-spreading stain. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

55 COMMENTARY: If after washing the plague has not changed colour, though it has not spread, it is unclean, for it frets inward, corroding the fabric or leather, and it shall be burnt whether the spot is on the inside or outside. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

56 COMMENTARY: If the priest sees the plague is somewhat dark after washing, he rends it out of the garment or skin, cutting out the fading spot. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

57 COMMENTARY: If it appears still in the garment, it is a spreading plague, and he burns that in which the plague is, with fire. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

58 COMMENTARY: If the garment, warp, woof, or skin article is washed and the plague departs, it shall be washed a second time and shall be clean. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

59 COMMENTARY: This is the law of the plague of leprosy in woollen or linen garments, warp or woof, or skin articles, to pronounce them clean or unclean, a law whose destruction of polluted clothing was a sanitary precaution promoting cleanliness and allaying fear of infection. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
