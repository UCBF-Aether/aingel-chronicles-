Study Notes — Leviticus

Chapter 21

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: The LORD speaks to Moses, giving the priests' mourning law, whose design is to keep inviolate the purity and dignity of the sacred office. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: A priest shall not be defiled for the dead among his people, except for his kin nearest to him, his mother, father, son, daughter, and brother, since contact with a corpse debars a mourner from the tabernacle for a week, an inconvenience the whole order must avoid except for family affection or necessity. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: For his sister, a virgin near to him who has had no husband, he may be defiled, the exception being strictly confined to nearest kindred. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: But he shall not defile himself, being a chief man among his people, to profane himself, or, as others read it, being a husband, he shall not defile himself by a wife's obsequies beyond the kin exceptions. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: Priests shall not make baldness on their heads, shave the corners of their beards, or make cuttings in their flesh, a special injunction beyond the general law, that they may show moderation of grief, restrain passion to console others, and sorrow not as those without hope of resurrection. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: They shall be holy unto their God and not profane His name, for they offer the LORD's offerings made by fire, the bread of their God, therefore they shall be holy. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: They shall not take a wife who is a whore or profane, nor one put away from her husband, for he is holy unto his God, private individuals being allowed connections forbidden to priests, whose office requires unblemished sanctity in family as well as self. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: Therefore he shall sanctify the priest, for he offers the bread of God, and he shall be holy unto Israel, for the LORD who sanctifies them is holy. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: If a priest's daughter profanes herself by playing the whore, she profanes her father, and she shall be burnt with fire, departures in priests' families being visited with severer punishment than in others. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: He who is high priest among his brethren, on whose head anointing oil is poured and who is consecrated to put on garments, shall not uncover his head nor rend his clothes, the family-bereavement indulgence granted to common priests being denied to him. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: Neither shall he go in to any dead body, nor defile himself for his father or his mother, since his absence from the sanctuary for defilement removal could not be dispensed with, nor could he intercede unless ceremonially clean. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: Neither shall he go out of the sanctuary nor profane it, for the crown of his God's anointing oil is upon him, his high dignity demanding corresponding superiority in personal holiness. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: He shall take a wife in her virginity, a stringent rule upholding the dignity of his station and family, as later extended to Christian ministers' families. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: A widow, divorced woman, profane woman, or harlot he shall not take, but a virgin of his own people shall he take to wife, the high priest's marriage being the most restricted of all. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: Neither shall he profane his seed among his people, disgracing his house by a low marriage, for the LORD sanctifies him. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: The LORD speaks to Moses again, now concerning blemished priests. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: No man of Aaron's seed in generations who has any blemish shall approach to offer the bread of his God, the blemish rule governing all succeeding generations. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

18 COMMENTARY: A blind or lame man, or one with a flat nose or anything superfluous, shall not approach, any physical infirmity disturbing associations or exciting ridicule and detracting from the office's weight and authority. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

19 COMMENTARY: A man with a broken foot or broken hand shall not approach, under the same blemish rule. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

20 COMMENTARY: A crookbacked man, dwarf, one with a blemish in his eye, scurvy, scabbed, or broken stones, shall not approach, the list covering visible malformations. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

21 COMMENTARY: No descendant of Aaron with a blemish shall come near to offer the LORD's offerings made by fire, for he has a blemish and shall not offer the bread of his God, though he may be employed in inferior sanctuary duties. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

22 COMMENTARY: He may eat the bread of his God, both most holy and holy, the blemish barring office, not maintenance. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

23 COMMENTARY: Only he shall not go in unto the veil nor come near the altar, because he has a blemish, lest he profane the sanctuaries, for the LORD sanctifies them, the holiness required being external and relative, with typical reference to Christ's priesthood. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

24 COMMENTARY: Moses speaks this to Aaron, his sons, and all Israel, so the blemish and mourning laws are published to the whole congregation, not priests alone. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
