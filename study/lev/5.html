Study Notes — Leviticus

Chapter 5

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Hearing the voice of swearing means hearing the words of adjuration read in a Jewish court, where witnesses were adjured rather than sworn directly, and the offence here is neglecting an opportunity to lodge information one could give. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: A person who unknowingly touches an unclean thing, then neglects purification or joins religious service while defiled, may afterwards be convinced of the offence and so become guilty. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: The same principle covers touching the uncleanness of man: the defilement may be hidden at first, but once it is known to him he bears guilt for it. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: A rash oath is sworn without duly considering its nature and consequences, perhaps binding oneself to do wrong or neglecting a vow to do good, and only afterwards is the transgression recognised. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: Guilt must be met with voluntary confession from the impulse of conscience, before the matter comes to the knowledge of the world, since spontaneous confession releases the sinner from punishment a prior discovery might have brought. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: The trespass offering differs from a sin offering in being appointed for one who did evil unwittingly, doubted his own criminality, or stood in a special situation requiring such a sacrifice, and here it is a female lamb or kid. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: If he cannot bring a lamb, the law accepts two turtledoves or two young pigeons, one for a sin offering and the other for a burnt offering, grading the offering to his means. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: The priest offers the sin-offering bird first, wringing off its head from its neck without dividing it completely, following the graduated trespass scale. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: Blood of the sin offering is sprinkled on the side of the altar and the rest drained at its base, marking it as a sin offering within the graduated scale. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: The second bird is offered as a burnt offering according to the prescribed manner, and by this pair the priest makes atonement and the sin is forgiven. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: If even two birds are beyond his ability, the tenth part of an ephah of fine flour without oil or frankincense satisfies the law, so that poverty bars no one from atonement. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: The priest takes a handful of the flour as its memorial and burns it on the altar according to the offerings made by fire, the flour offering standing in the same graduated scale as lamb and birds. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: By this flour offering the priest makes atonement for sin in any of these matters, it is forgiven him, and the rest belongs to the priest as a meat offering. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: A further trespass law is introduced concerning the holy things of the LORD, moving from concealment, defilement, and rash oaths to sacrilege. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: Ignorant sacrilege, such as withholding full tithes or first-fruits or eating meats belonging to the priests alone, requires a ram without blemish valued in sanctuary shekels, as soon as the involuntary fraud is known. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: Along with the ram, restitution must be made for the harm done to the holy thing, with a fifth added and given to the priest, the amount being determined by the priest, and by this atonement he is forgiven. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: This case also concerns holy things but differs as a doubtful case, where conscience suspects wrong though the understanding is in doubt whether criminality has been committed, yet the person is still guilty and bears iniquity. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

18 COMMENTARY: For such a doubtful trespass he brings a ram without blemish at the priest's valuation, and the priest makes atonement for the ignorance in which he erred and did not know it, and he is forgiven. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

19 COMMENTARY: The rite is expressly a trespass offering, because he has certainly trespassed against the LORD, and these provisions are designed to impress responsibility to God and a salutary fear of secret wrong. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
