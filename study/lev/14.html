Study Notes — Leviticus

Chapter 14

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: The LORD speaks to Moses, giving the law for the leper's cleansing after the diagnostic laws of the previous chapter. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: The law of the leper in the day of his cleansing begins outside the camp, since a convalescent leper may not return to society immediately or at his own will. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: The priest goes out of the camp, looks, and if the plague is healed, gives a certificate of health, a priest skilled in diagnostics being deputed to attend such outcasts. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: Two clean birds are taken, literally little birds such as sparrows or pigeons, common and accessible in the desert, with cedar wood, scarlet, and hyssop, the cedar probably juniper from Sinai, bound with scarlet ribbon to hyssop. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: One bird is killed in an earthen vessel over running water, by an attendant rather than the priest since it is outside the camp, its blood mingled with spring water to increase the quantity for sprinkling. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: The living bird with the cedar, scarlet, and hyssop is dipped in the blood of the slain bird over running water, the living bird attached so its tail is moistened but not head or wings, that it may fly when loosed. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: The priest sprinkles the cleansed leper seven times, denoting complete purification, pronounces him clean with solemnity, and lets the living bird loose in the open field, in token of release from quarantine and uncleanness carried away. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: He who is cleansed washes his clothes, shaves off all his hair, washes himself, and is clean, then comes into the camp but tarries abroad out of his tent seven days, with further purifications through that week. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: On the seventh day he shaves all his hair, head, beard, and eyebrows, washes clothes and flesh, and is clean, the whole process being repeated before re-entry to his tent. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: On the eighth day he takes two he-lambs without blemish, one ewe-lamb of the first year, fine flour, and a log of oil, the purification not being complete until he appears daily at the tabernacle door with these offerings. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: The priest who makes him clean presents the man and his offerings before the LORD at the tabernacle door, as a newly restored patient repairs to offer thanksgiving, presented by the great Priest whose blood alone makes clean. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: The priest takes one he-lamb for a trespass offering with the log of oil and waves them before the LORD, the trespass offering being necessary for inherent sin or for defiling the camp before expulsion. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: He kills the lamb in the holy place where sin and burnt offerings are killed, for as the sin offering is the priest's, so is the trespass offering, most holy. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: The priest puts trespass blood on the cleansed man's right ear, right thumb, and right great toe, exactly as in the priests' consecration, marking hearing, doing, and walking. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: The priest takes some of the log of oil and pours it into his own left hand, preparing to anoint the cleansed man after the blood. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: He dips his right finger in the oil in his left hand and sprinkles it seven times before the LORD, the sevenfold sprinkling denoting complete purification. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: The priest puts oil from his hand on the right ear, thumb, and toe, upon the place of the trespass blood, and the rest upon his head, blood signifying forgiveness and oil embleming healing and sanctification. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

18 COMMENTARY: The remnant of oil in the priest's hand he pours upon the cleansed man's head, and makes atonement for him before the LORD. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

19 COMMENTARY: The priest offers the sin offering and makes atonement for him that is to be cleansed from his uncleanness, removing the ceremonial pollution that excluded him from religious ordinances. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

20 COMMENTARY: He then offers the burnt offering and meat offering on the altar, making atonement for him, and he shall be clean, the burnt offering having also the character of a thank offering for restoration. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

21 COMMENTARY: If he is poor and cannot get so much, he takes one lamb for a trespass offering to be waved, a tenth of flour mingled with oil, and a log of oil, a kind provision extending the privilege to poorer lepers, who are as publicly and completely cleansed. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

22 COMMENTARY: He also brings two turtledoves or young pigeons, such as he is able to get, one for a sin offering and the other for a burnt offering. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

23 COMMENTARY: On the eighth day he brings them to the priest at the tabernacle door before the LORD, as in the costlier offering. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

24 COMMENTARY: The priest takes the trespass lamb and the log of oil and waves them for a wave offering before the LORD, under the same poor man's scale. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

25 COMMENTARY: He kills the trespass lamb and puts its blood on the cleansed man's right ear, thumb, and toe, the same application as in the full offering. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

26 COMMENTARY: The priest pours some oil into his own left hand, as before. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

27 COMMENTARY: With his right finger he sprinkles oil from his left hand seven times before the LORD. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

28 COMMENTARY: He puts oil on the right ear, thumb, and toe, upon the place of the trespass blood, as in the full rite. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

29 COMMENTARY: The rest of the oil in his hand he puts upon the cleansed man's head, to make atonement for him before the LORD. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

30 COMMENTARY: He offers one of the turtledoves or young pigeons, such as he can get, as a sin offering or burnt offering according to the scale. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

31 COMMENTARY: Even such as he is able to get, one for a sin offering and the other for a burnt offering with the meat offering, the priest makes atonement for him before the LORD. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

32 COMMENTARY: This is the law for a leper in whom is the plague, who is not able to get the full offering for his cleansing. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

33 COMMENTARY: The LORD speaks to Moses and Aaron again, now concerning leprosy in a house. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

34 COMMENTARY: When they come into Canaan and the LORD puts leprosy in a house of their possession, this law is prospective, not operating until settlement in Canaan, and whether judicial or merely permitted in providence, it concerns a disease in the house itself. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

35 COMMENTARY: The owner comes and tells the priest that it seems to him there is a plague in the house, the first suspicion being reported to the priest. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

36 COMMENTARY: The priest commands the house to be emptied before he enters to see the plague, so all in the house is not made unclean, the furniture being removed on first suspicion. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

37 COMMENTARY: He looks on the plague, and if it is in the walls with hollow streaks, greenish or reddish, appearing lower than the wall, it is suspected leprosy, a mural efflorescence or mildew apt in damp situations and injurious to health and stability. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

38 COMMENTARY: The priest goes out to the door and shuts up the house seven days, following the same course and period as in human leprosy. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

39 COMMENTARY: On the seventh day he returns, and if the plague has spread in the walls, the infected parts are to be removed. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

40 COMMENTARY: He commands the stones with the plague to be taken away and cast into an unclean place outside the city, the rough unhewn stones being removed as with mural salt. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

41 COMMENTARY: He causes the house to be scraped within round about, and the dust scraped off to be poured out in an unclean place outside the city. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

42 COMMENTARY: Other stones are taken and put in place of those stones, and other mortar is taken and the house plastered, as an infected wall must be plastered anew if it is to remain. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

43 COMMENTARY: If the plague returns and breaks out in the house after the stones are taken out, the house scraped, and plastered, the return is judged as a new outbreak. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

44 COMMENTARY: The priest comes, looks, and if the plague has spread, it is a fretting leprosy in the house, and it is unclean. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

45 COMMENTARY: He breaks down the house, its stones, timber, and mortar, and carries them out of the city to an unclean place, and if the contagion may spread, the house is destroyed altogether. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

46 COMMENTARY: He who goes into the house all the while it is shut up shall be unclean until evening, sharing the house's exclusion. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

47 COMMENTARY: He who lies in the house or eats in it shall wash his clothes, the same washing rule as for contact with a leprous person or garment. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

48 COMMENTARY: If the priest comes and sees the plague has not spread after plastering, he pronounces the house clean, because the plague is healed, a solemn declaration that removes public apprehension and the owner's suspicion. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

49 COMMENTARY: To cleanse the house he takes two birds, cedar wood, scarlet, and hyssop, the very same rite as for a leprous person. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

50 COMMENTARY: One bird is killed in an earthen vessel over running water, as in the person's cleansing. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

51 COMMENTARY: He takes the cedar, hyssop, scarlet, and living bird, dips them in the slain bird's blood and running water, and sprinkles the house seven times. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

52 COMMENTARY: He cleanses the house with the bird's blood, running water, living bird, cedar, scarlet, and hyssop, the atonement for the house referring to its occupants' sins or to the ceremonial purification itself. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

53 COMMENTARY: He lets the living bird loose out of the city into the open fields and makes atonement for the house, and it shall be clean, as the leper's release from quarantine. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

54 COMMENTARY: This is the law for all manner of plague of leprosy, and scall, the whole diagnostic and cleansing law summed up. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

55 COMMENTARY: And for the leprosy of a garment, and of a house, the garment and house laws being included in the same summary. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

56 COMMENTARY: And for a rising, and for a scab, and for a bright spot, the skin signs of chapter 13 being included likewise. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

57 COMMENTARY: To teach when it is unclean and when it is clean, this is the law of leprosy, designed to foster religious fear and inward purity as well as sanitary care. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
