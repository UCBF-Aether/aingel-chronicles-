Study Notes — Leviticus

Chapter 16

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: The LORD speaks to Moses after Nadab and Abihu's death, a catastrophe that filled Aaron with apprehension lest guilt be entailed on his house, and this law secures the Aaronic order by due observance. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: Aaron shall not come at all times into the holy place within the veil, lest he die, for the LORD will appear in the cloud upon the mercy seat, and only the high priest may enter there, once a year, to inspire reverence for the most holy place. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: Aaron shall come with a young bullock for a sin offering and a ram for a burnt offering, victims brought alive but not sacrificed until the ceremonies between verses 3 and 11 are complete. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: He shall put on holy linen coat, breeches, girdle, and mitre, washing his flesh first, a plain linen dress like common Levites, since he atones for his own sins as a humble suppliant, highest and lowest being on a level as sinners. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: He shall take from Israel two kids of the goats for a sin offering and one ram for a burnt offering, the goats, though used differently, constituting only one offering. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: Aaron shall offer his bullock of the sin offering and make atonement for himself and his house, the priests, whose sins are judicially transferred by laying hands on the victim's head. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: He shall take the two goats and present them before the LORD at the tabernacle door, both presented before their disposal is decided. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: He shall cast lots on the two goats, one lot for the LORD and the other for the scapegoat, the lots being gold pieces inscribed for the LORD and for Azazel, shaken in an urn and placed on each goat's head. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: The goat on which the LORD's lot falls he shall bring and offer for a sin offering, as the people's sin offering. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: The goat on which the scapegoat lot falls shall be presented alive before the LORD to make atonement with him and to let him go into the wilderness, the two goats together forming one atonement. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: Aaron shall bring his bullock, make atonement for himself and his house, and kill it with his own hands, the first part of the service solemnising his mind and the people's by sacrifices for their sins. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: He shall take a censer full of burning coals from the altar and his hands full of sweet incense beaten small and bring it within the veil, amid the solemn attention and anxious prayers of the multitude. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: He shall put the incense on the fire before the LORD, that the cloud of incense may cover the mercy seat, that he die not, the fragrant smoke preventing presumptuous gazing at the LORD's throne and being the cloud in which He appears. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: He shall take the bullock's blood and sprinkle it with his finger once upon the mercy seat eastward, on the side next to himself, and seven times before the mercy seat, on the front of the ark. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: He shall kill the goat of the people's sin offering, bring its blood within the veil, and do with it as with the bullock's blood, sprinkling it upon and before the mercy seat. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: He shall make atonement for the holy place because of Israel's uncleanness and transgressions in all their sins, and so for the tabernacle remaining among them in their uncleanness, the whole tabernacle being stained by a guilty people's sins. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: No man shall be in the tabernacle when he goes in to make atonement until he comes out, having made atonement for himself, his household, and all Israel, the exclusion marking the priests' unworthiness and the impurities of their service. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

18 COMMENTARY: He shall go out to the altar before the LORD, make atonement for it, and put blood of the bullock and goat on its horns round about, the mingled blood showing priests and people equally need atonement. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

19 COMMENTARY: He shall sprinkle blood on the altar with his finger seven times, and cleanse and hallow it from Israel's uncleanness, the sanctuary thus purified so the LORD may continue to dwell among them. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

20 COMMENTARY: When he has finished reconciling holy place, tabernacle, and altar, he shall bring the live goat, already presented before the LORD. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

21 COMMENTARY: Aaron shall lay both hands on the live goat's head and confess over it all Israel's iniquities, transgressions, and sins, putting them on the goat, and send it away by a fit man into the wilderness, transferring them to the goat as substitute, a typical reference to Christ who bore away our sins. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

22 COMMENTARY: The goat shall bear all their iniquities to a land not inhabited, and he shall let it go in the wilderness, in early times loosed to escape, in Christ's time carried to a high rock twelve miles from Jerusalem and thrust over it. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

23 COMMENTARY: Aaron shall come into the tabernacle, put off the linen garments worn in the holy place, and leave them there, laying aside plain linen for the remaining service. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

24 COMMENTARY: He shall wash his flesh in water in the holy place, put on his pontifical garments, come forth, and offer his burnt offering and the people's burnt offering, making atonement for himself and the people, the two reserved rams. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

25 COMMENTARY: The fat of the sin offering he shall burn upon the altar, the altar portion of the sin offerings whose carcasses are dealt with outside. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

26 COMMENTARY: He who lets go the scapegoat shall wash his clothes, bathe his flesh in water, and afterward come into the camp, as with those who burn the sin-offering carcasses. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

27 COMMENTARY: The bullock and goat for sin offerings, whose blood was brought in to make atonement in the holy place, shall be carried forth without the camp, and their skins, flesh, and dung burnt in the fire, in conformity with the general sin-offering law. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

28 COMMENTARY: He who burns them shall wash his clothes, bathe his flesh in water, and afterward come into the camp. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

29 COMMENTARY: This shall be a perpetual statute, that in the seventh month, on the tenth day, they shall afflict their souls and do no work, whether native or sojourning stranger, this annual expiation being observed as a solemn fast and sabbath, with death for labour. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

30 COMMENTARY: For on that day the priest shall make atonement for you, to cleanse you, that you may be clean from all your sins before the LORD, the year's sins, irreverences, and impurities being expiated at once. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

31 COMMENTARY: It shall be a sabbath of rest to you, and you shall afflict your souls, by a perpetual statute, kept as a holy convocation. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

32 COMMENTARY: The priest who is anointed and consecrated to minister in his father's stead shall make the atonement and put on the linen clothes, the holy garments, so the office continues in succession. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

33 COMMENTARY: He shall make atonement for the holy sanctuary, the tabernacle, the altar, the priests, and all the people of the congregation, the whole order of atonement being repeated by each successor. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

34 COMMENTARY: This shall be an everlasting statute, to make atonement for Israel for all their sins once a year, and Aaron did as the LORD commanded Moses, the public reading of this chapter with Leviticus 23 producing salutary impressions of sin and duty. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
