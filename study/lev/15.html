Study Notes — Leviticus

Chapter 15

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: The LORD speaks to Moses and Aaron, giving further forms of personal uncleanness after the leprosy laws, to be governed by the same separation principle. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: When any man has a running issue out of his flesh, because of his issue he is unclean, whether from natural causes or disease, and the stringent rules that follow are calculated to prevent contagion and discourage licentious excess. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: His uncleanness is defined by the issue itself, whether his flesh runs with it or is stopped from it, so the condition, not merely the flow, defiles. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: Every bed he lies on and everything he sits on shall be unclean, the separation extending from the person to anything connected with him. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: Whoever touches his bed shall wash his clothes, bathe in water, and be unclean until evening, secondary contact requiring washing and a day's exclusion. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: He who sits on anything on which the man with the issue sat shall wash his clothes, bathe, and be unclean until evening, the sitting place carrying the defilement. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: He who touches the body of the man with the issue shall wash his clothes, bathe, and be unclean until evening, direct contact defiling as surely as contact with his bed. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: If the man with the issue spits on a clean person, that person shall wash his clothes, bathe, and be unclean until evening, the issue's moisture itself conveying defilement. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: Whatever saddle he rides upon shall be unclean, the riding seat being treated like a bed or sitting place. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: Whoever touches anything that was under him shall be unclean until evening, and whoever bears such things shall wash clothes, bathe, and be unclean until evening, bearing being a closer contact than touching. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: Whomever the man with the issue touches, if he has not rinsed his hands in water, shall wash clothes, bathe, and be unclean until evening, unwashed hands carrying the defilement to the touched person. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: An earthen vessel he touches shall be broken, since Israelite pottery, like Egyptian water jars, was unglazed and porous and liable to imbibe impure particles, while a wooden vessel shall be rinsed in water. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: When he is cleansed of his issue, he numbers seven days for his cleansing, washes his clothes, bathes his flesh in running water, and is clean, a week's probation like a leper's to make sure he is completely healed. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: On the eighth day he takes two turtledoves or two young pigeons and comes before the LORD to the tabernacle door and gives them to the priest, as the offering that ends the probation. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: The priest offers one for a sin offering and the other for a burnt offering, making atonement for him before the LORD for his issue, removing ceremonial defilement and typically pardoning sin. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: If a man's seed goes from him, he shall wash all his flesh in water and be unclean until evening, the same evening limit applying to this involuntary issue. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: Every garment and skin on which seed is shall be washed with water and be unclean until evening, the defilement extending to cloth and leather. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

18 COMMENTARY: A woman with whom a man lies with seed shall both bathe in water and be unclean until evening, the shared act sharing the evening uncleanness. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

19 COMMENTARY: If a woman has an issue of blood in her flesh, she shall be put apart seven days, and whoever touches her shall be unclean until evening, though this may be a natural affection, it was anciently considered contagious and typified moral impurity. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

20 COMMENTARY: Everything she lies upon in her separation shall be unclean, and everything she sits upon shall be unclean, the same bed and seat rule as for a man's issue. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

21 COMMENTARY: Whoever touches her bed shall wash his clothes, bathe in water, and be unclean until evening. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

22 COMMENTARY: Whoever touches anything she sat upon shall wash his clothes, bathe, and be unclean until evening. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

23 COMMENTARY: If it is on her bed or on anything she sits upon, when he touches it he shall be unclean until evening, indirect contact through her bed or seat still defiling. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

24 COMMENTARY: If any man lies with her and her flowers are upon him, he shall be unclean seven days, and all the bed he lies upon shall be unclean, sharing her full separation period. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

25 COMMENTARY: If a woman has an issue of blood many days out of her separation time, or beyond it, all the days of her unclean issue she shall be as in her separation days, the prolonged flow extending the separation. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

26 COMMENTARY: Every bed she lies on all the days of her issue shall be as the bed of her separation, and whatever she sits on shall be unclean as in her separation. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

27 COMMENTARY: Whoever touches those things shall be unclean, wash his clothes, bathe in water, and be unclean until evening, as with her ordinary separation. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

28 COMMENTARY: If she is cleansed of her issue, she counts seven days for herself, and after that she shall be clean, a week's probation like the man's issue. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

29 COMMENTARY: On the eighth day she takes two turtles or two young pigeons and brings them to the priest at the tabernacle door. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

30 COMMENTARY: The priest offers one for a sin offering and the other for a burnt offering, making atonement for her before the LORD for the issue of her uncleanness, the appointed expiation whose neglect would defile the tabernacle. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

31 COMMENTARY: Thus Israel shall be separated from their uncleanness, lest they die in it when they defile the tabernacle among them, God requiring complete purity from a people dwelling as priests in His presence, and debarring even involuntary impurities as disrespect to His majesty. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

32 COMMENTARY: This is the law for one who has an issue, and for him whose seed goes from him and is unclean thereby, the whole men's-issue law summed up. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

33 COMMENTARY: And for her who is sick of her flowers, and for one with an issue, man or woman, and for him who lies with her who is unclean, the women's law and the shared-lying law being summed up with the men's. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
