Study Notes — Leviticus

Chapter 2

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: The “meat offering” is a bloodless gift offering, “meat” here meaning food generally, here the flour of wheat. These voluntary oblations, offered alone, are a thankful acknowledgment of the bounty of Providence. Oil symbolises the influences of the Spirit, as frankincense symbolises prayer. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: The priest burns only a memorial portion, a part of the offering, with all the frankincense, as an offering made by fire of a sweet savour unto the LORD. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: The remnant goes to Aaron and his sons, and this appropriation to the priests distinguishes the meat offering from a burnt offering. They alone partake of it within the sacred precincts, as among the most holy things. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: An oven-baked offering is unleavened cakes of fine flour mingled with oil, or wafers anointed with oil. The Eastern oven is generally a circular hole in the floor, plastered inside, on which cakes are baked like pancakes. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: A pan-baked offering is fine flour unleavened, mingled with oil, baked on a thin plate of copper or iron over a slow fire, like a girdle for baking oatmeal cakes. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: The pan offering is parted in pieces and oil poured on it, as Eastern people commonly eat broken bread dipped in oil. The offering was prepared beforehand by the offerer, and the priest took it from his hands and burnt a portion on the altar. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: A fryingpan offering follows the same pattern: fine flour with oil, prepared by the offerer and presented for the priest to burn a memorial portion. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: Whatever its form, the meat offering is brought to the LORD, presented to the priest, and brought by him to the altar, following the same order as the offerings already described. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: The priest takes a memorial from the offering and burns it on the altar, an offering made by fire of a sweet savour unto the LORD, as in the unbaked offering. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: What is left of the offering is Aaron's and his sons', a thing most holy of the offerings made by fire, just as with the offering of fine flour. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: No meat offering may be made with leaven, and no leaven or honey may be burnt in a fire offering. Nothing sweet or sour was to be offered: leavened bread soon spoils in warm climates, and leaven was regarded as an emblem of hypocrisy or corruption, while some think the ban reflects leaven and honey's use in heathen rites. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: Firstfruits oblations are voluntary offerings from a person's increase, in which leaven and honey might be used. Though presented at the altar, they are not burnt for a sweet savour but assigned by God for the priests' use. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: Every meat offering must be seasoned with salt, the salt of the covenant never lacking. Salt, unlike leaven, preserves and does not putrefy, so it became an emblem of incorruption and purity and of a perpetual covenant, a perfect reconciliation and lasting friendship. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: A firstfruits meat offering of green ears of corn, dried by fire and beaten out of full ears, seems to be a voluntary offering before the harvest, the ears parched at the fire in the favourite Eastern way. It is an early tribute of pious thankfulness for the earth's increase, offered by the usual directions. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: Oil is put on the firstfruits offering and frankincense laid on it, as with the offering of fine flour, the oil symbolising the Spirit and the frankincense prayer. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: The priest burns the memorial of it, part of the beaten corn and part of the oil, with all the frankincense, an offering made by fire unto the LORD. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
