Study Notes — Leviticus

Chapter 26

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: They shall make no idols nor graven image, neither rear a standing image nor set up an image of stone to bow down to, for He is the LORD their God, the law being repeated against prevalent forms among neighbouring nations, Syrian and Canaanite pillars and Egyptian obelisks. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: They shall keep His sabbaths and reverence His sanctuary, for He is the LORD, Sabbath and sanctuary being frequently mentioned as antidotes to idolatry. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: If they walk in His statutes and keep His commandments and do them, the covenant promises a variety of blessings so long as they continue obedient, amply fulfilled in plenty, peace, a populous country, and victory. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: He will give rain in due season, and the land shall yield increase, and trees shall yield fruit, rain falling in Judea at seedtime in autumn and before harvest in spring. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: Their threshing shall reach unto the vintage, and vintage unto sowing time, and they shall eat bread to the full and dwell safely, no idle time between harvest and vintage, so great would be the increase. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: He will give peace in the land, and they shall lie down and none shall make them afraid, and He will rid evil beasts out of the land, neither shall the sword go through their land, the blessing being peace and safety. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: They shall chase their enemies, and they shall fall before them by the sword, the blessing of victory over enemies. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: Five of them shall chase a hundred, and a hundred shall put ten thousand to flight, and enemies shall fall by the sword, disproportionate victory marking covenant blessing. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: He will have respect unto them, and make them fruitful, and multiply them, and establish His covenant with them, the blessing of a populous country and confirmed covenant. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: They shall eat old store, and bring forth the old because of the new, their stock of old corn still unexhausted when the next harvest brings a new supply. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: He will set His tabernacle among them, and His soul shall not abhor them, God's dwelling among them being the crowning blessing. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: He will walk among them, and will be their God, and they shall be His people, the covenant formula of mutual belonging. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: He is the LORD their God, who brought them out of Egypt that they should not be bondmen, having broken the bands of their yoke and made them go upright, a metaphor for emancipation from Egyptian slavery. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: But if they will not hearken unto Him, and will not do all His commandments, then, in proportion to their great privileges, would be the extent of their national criminality and severity of punishment. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: If they despise His statutes or their soul abhors His judgments, so that they will not do all His commandments but break His covenant, the curse begins with despising and abhorring the law itself. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: He will appoint over them terror, consumption, and burning ague, consuming eyes and causing sorrow of heart, and they shall sow seed in vain, for enemies shall eat it, terror being the falling sickness, and ague uncertainly explained, even as jaundice or a scorching wind. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: He will set His face against them, and they shall be slain before enemies, they that hate them shall reign over them, and they shall flee when none pursues, reversing the blessing of chasing enemies. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

18 COMMENTARY: If they will not yet for all this hearken, He will punish them seven times more for their sins, that is, with far more severe and protracted calamities. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

19 COMMENTARY: He will break the pride of their power, and make heaven as iron and earth as brass, no figures better conveying severe and long-continued famine. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

20 COMMENTARY: Their strength shall be spent in vain, for land shall not yield increase, neither shall trees yield fruits, the famine making labour fruitless. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

21 COMMENTARY: If they walk contrary unto Him and will not hearken, He will bring seven times more plagues upon them according to their sins, the second sevenfold series intensifying the first. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

22 COMMENTARY: He will send wild beasts among them, robbing them of children, destroying cattle, making them few, and highways desolate, one of the four judgments, destroying trade, freedom, and safety on the roads. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

23 COMMENTARY: If they will not be reformed by these things but will walk contrary unto Him, the third series is conditioned on unreformed obstinacy. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

24 COMMENTARY: Then He will also walk contrary unto them and punish them yet seven times for their sins, God answering contrary walking with contrary judgment. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

25 COMMENTARY: He will bring a sword upon them avenging the quarrel of His covenant, and when gathered within cities He will send pestilence, and they shall be delivered into the enemy's hand, the sword being the vengeance of the covenant. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

26 COMMENTARY: When He has broken the staff of bread, ten women shall bake bread in one oven, delivering it by weight, and they shall eat and not be satisfied, scarcity so great that one oven suffices for ten families' usual baking, with bread rationed by weight. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

27 COMMENTARY: If they will not for all this hearken but walk contrary unto Him, the fourth series is conditioned on continued impenitence. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

28 COMMENTARY: Then He will walk contrary unto them also in fury, and He Himself will chastise them seven times for their sins, the final sevenfold chastisement being thorough and complete. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

29 COMMENTARY: They shall eat the flesh of their sons, and the flesh of their daughters shall they eat, a revolting picture actually exhibited at Samaria's siege, Jerusalem's siege by Nebuchadnezzar, and its destruction by the Romans. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

30 COMMENTARY: He will destroy their high places, cut down their images, and cast their carcases upon the carcases of their idols, and His soul shall abhor them, high places being consecrated enclosures on hilltops for idolatry, and images sun-chariots or conical pyramids on high places. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

31 COMMENTARY: He will make their cities waste, bring sanctuaries to desolation, and not smell the savour of sweet odours, the tabernacle and temple being desolated and sacrifices no longer accepted. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

32 COMMENTARY: He will bring the land into desolation, and enemies dwelling therein shall be astonished at it, desolation itself astonishing the occupiers. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

33 COMMENTARY: He will scatter them among the heathen and draw out a sword after them, and land shall be desolate and cities waste, as when the elite were removed into Assyria and placed in various parts of the kingdom. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

34 COMMENTARY: Then shall the land enjoy her sabbaths as long as it lies desolate and they are in enemies' land, even then shall the land rest and enjoy her sabbaths, a long arrear of sabbatic years accumulated through avarice and apostasy, seemingly seventy, matching the captivity's duration. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

35 COMMENTARY: As long as it lies desolate it shall rest, because it did not rest in their sabbaths when they dwelt upon it, the land finally taking the rest denied it. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

36 COMMENTARY: Upon those left alive He will send faintness into their hearts in enemies' lands, and the sound of a shaken leaf shall chase them, and they shall flee as fleeing from a sword and fall when none pursues, terror reversing the blessing of fearless rest. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

37 COMMENTARY: They shall fall one upon another as before a sword when none pursues, and they shall have no power to stand before enemies, panic without a pursuer marking the curse. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

38 COMMENTARY: They shall perish among the heathen, and the land of enemies shall eat them up, the ten tribes on removal into captivity never returning, all traces lost. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

39 COMMENTARY: They that are left shall pine away in their iniquity in enemies' lands, and also in the iniquities of their fathers shall they pine away with them, exile wasting them in accumulated iniquity. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

40 COMMENTARY: If they shall confess their iniquity and the iniquity of their fathers, with their trespass against Him, and that they have walked contrary unto Him, this passage holds out gracious promise of forgiveness and favour on repentance and restoration in memory of the fathers' covenant. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

41 COMMENTARY: And that He also has walked contrary unto them and brought them into enemies' land, if then their uncircumcised hearts be humbled and they accept the punishment of their iniquity, humbled hearts accepting punishment open the way to restoration. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

42 COMMENTARY: Then He will remember His covenant with Jacob, and also with Isaac, and also with Abraham, and He will remember the land, covenant memory with the patriarchs grounding restoration. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

43 COMMENTARY: The land also shall be left of them and shall enjoy her sabbaths while desolate without them, and they shall accept the punishment of their iniquity, because they despised His judgments and their soul abhorred His statutes, desolation and accepted punishment preceding restoration. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

44 COMMENTARY: Yet for all that, when they are in enemies' land, He will not cast them away, neither abhor them, to destroy them utterly and break His covenant with them, for He is the LORD their God, exile not annulling the covenant. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

45 COMMENTARY: But He will for their sakes remember the covenant of their ancestors, whom He brought out of Egypt in the sight of the heathen, that He might be their God, for He is the LORD, the exodus covenant remembered for their sakes. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

46 COMMENTARY: These are the statutes and judgments and laws which the LORD made between Him and Israel in Mount Sinai by the hand of Moses, some thinking the last chapter was originally placed after the twenty-fifth, others that the next chapter was added as an appendix after people resolved to dedicate themselves and possessions. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
