Study Notes — Leviticus

Chapter 23

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: The LORD speaks to Moses, giving the sundry feasts, times of assembling appointed by direct authority and proclaimed publicly. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: Israel shall proclaim the feasts of the LORD as holy convocations, literally times of assembling, announced by the joyful sound, evidences of divine wisdom subservient to religious knowledge and piety. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: Six days work shall be done, but the seventh is the sabbath of rest, a holy convocation, given precedence, observed in dwellings, at the tabernacle door where practicable, and later in synagogues. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: These are the feasts of the LORD to be proclaimed in their seasons, in March, May, and September, seasons chosen because winter journeys were impracticable and summer was busy with harvest, and to counteract Egyptian associations, mostly prospective until settlement in Canaan. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: In the fourteenth day of the first month at even is the LORD's passover, a perpetual memorial of redemption from Egypt with a typical reference to a greater redemption. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: On the fifteenth day of the same month is the feast of unleavened bread, seven days of unleavened eating, following the passover memorial. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: On the first day there shall be a holy convocation, with no servile work, though meat may be dressed, unlike the Sabbath's total work ban, the day being devoted to social devotion. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: Seven days they shall offer an offering made by fire, and on the seventh day a holy convocation with no servile work, in addition to ordinary daily sacrifices, with unleavened bread eaten in families all seven days. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: The LORD speaks to Moses again, now concerning first-fruits. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: When they come into the land and reap its harvest, they shall bring a sheaf of first-fruits to the priest, an omer of barley, the earliest grain, whose reaping begins the general harvest. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: The priest shall wave the sheaf before the LORD, to be accepted for them, on the morrow after the sabbath, the sixteenth of the first month, waving it toward the four points, burning part, and keeping the rest, an act of dependence on God's bounty sanctifying the whole harvest. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: On the day the sheaf is waved, they shall offer a he lamb without blemish of the first year for a burnt offering, joining a perfect victim to the first-fruits wave. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: Its meat offering shall be two tenth deals of fine flour mingled with oil, an offering by fire of sweet savour, with a drink offering of a fourth part of a hin of wine. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: They shall eat neither bread, parched corn, nor green ears until the selfsame day the offering is brought, until the wave-sheaf has sanctified the harvest, a statute forever in generations and dwellings. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: They shall count from the morrow after the sabbath, from the day the wave sheaf is brought, seven sabbaths complete, the first day of passover week being observed as a Sabbath. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: Even to the morrow after the seventh sabbath, fifty days, they shall offer a new meat offering, the forty-ninth day after first-fruits, or fiftieth including it, being Pentecost. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: Out of their habitations they shall bring two wave loaves of two tenth deals of fine flour baked with leaven, somewhat more than ten pounds each, the first-fruits of the finished harvest offered in the nation's name, leavened unlike passover bread, as gratitude for daily food rather than haste. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

18 COMMENTARY: With the bread they shall offer seven lambs without blemish, one young bullock, and two rams for a burnt offering, with meat and drink offerings by fire of sweet savour, the national offering completing the harvest. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

19 COMMENTARY: Then they shall sacrifice one kid of the goats for a sin offering and two lambs of the first year for peace offerings, joining sin and peace offerings to the harvest thanksgiving. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

20 COMMENTARY: The priest shall wave them with the bread of first-fruits as a wave offering before the LORD, with the two lambs, holy to the LORD for the priest, the wave loaves and lambs becoming the priest's portion. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

21 COMMENTARY: They shall proclaim on the selfsame day a holy convocation, doing no servile work, a statute forever, though the feast extended over a week, only the first day being held as a Sabbath, both for first-fruits and as a memorial of the law's giving. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

22 COMMENTARY: When reaping, they shall not make clean riddance of field corners nor gather gleanings, but leave them for poor and stranger, the law of chapter 19 repeated here as priests remind the people at first-fruits to unite piety to God with charity to the poor. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

23 COMMENTARY: The LORD speaks to Moses again, now concerning trumpets, atonement, and tabernacles. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

24 COMMENTARY: In the seventh month, on the first day, shall be a sabbath, a memorial of blowing of trumpets, a holy convocation, the first day of the ancient civil year, trumpets sounded thirty times to announce the new year and prepare for the approaching solemn feast. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

25 COMMENTARY: They shall offer an offering made by fire and do no servile work, the new-year day being religiously observed. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

26 COMMENTARY: The LORD speaks to Moses again, concerning the day of atonement. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

27 COMMENTARY: On the tenth day of the seventh month shall be a day of atonement, a holy convocation, afflicting souls and offering by fire, an unusual festival at which the year's sins are expiated, as in chapter 16. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

28 COMMENTARY: They shall do no work in that same day, for it is a day of atonement to make atonement for them before the LORD, the severest penalty attaching to its violation. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

29 COMMENTARY: Whatever soul is not afflicted in that same day shall be cut off from among his people, affliction being essential to the day's expiation. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

30 COMMENTARY: Whatever soul does any work in that same day, the same soul God will destroy from among his people, the work ban being absolute, unlike feast days permitting meat dressing. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

31 COMMENTARY: They shall do no manner of work, a statute forever throughout generations in all dwellings, the atonement Sabbath binding wherever Israel dwells. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

32 COMMENTARY: It shall be a sabbath of rest, afflicting souls from the evening of the ninth day to the evening of the tenth, celebrating the sabbath from even to even. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

33 COMMENTARY: The LORD speaks to Moses again, now concerning tabernacles. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

34 COMMENTARY: On the fifteenth day of the seventh month shall be the feast of tabernacles for seven days unto the LORD, the third great annual festival, lasting a week like the other two. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

35 COMMENTARY: On the first day shall be a holy convocation, with no servile work, the fifteenth day, end of September and beginning of October, being observed as a Sabbath. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

36 COMMENTARY: Seven days they shall offer an offering made by fire, and on the eighth day a holy convocation, offering by fire, with no servile work, it being a solemn assembly, the eighth day being the great day of the feast and the feast of ingathering after vintage. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

37 COMMENTARY: These are the feasts of the LORD, to be proclaimed holy convocations, offering by fire, burnt, meat, sacrifice, and drink offerings, every thing upon his day, each feast having its own daily offerings at the sanctuary. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

38 COMMENTARY: Beside the LORD's sabbaths, gifts, vows, and freewill offerings, the feast offerings are additional to ordinary Sabbaths and voluntary gifts. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

39 COMMENTARY: Also on the fifteenth day of the seventh month, when they have gathered the land's fruit, they shall keep a feast seven days, the first day a sabbath and the eighth day a sabbath, the ingathering feast marking the vintage's end. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

40 COMMENTARY: On the first day they shall take boughs of goodly trees, branches of palm, boughs of thick trees, and willows of the brook, and rejoice before the LORD seven days, solid branches building booths and lighter branches carried in triumphal procession singing psalms and crying Hosanna. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

41 COMMENTARY: They shall keep it a feast seven days in the year, a statute forever in generations, in the seventh month, the annual booth festival being perpetual. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

42 COMMENTARY: All that are Israelites born shall dwell in booths, erected on flat roofs, in streets or fields, in grateful commemoration of dwelling securely in booths in the wilderness. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

43 COMMENTARY: That generations may know that God made Israel dwell in booths when He brought them out of Egypt, for He is the LORD their God, the booths teaching each generation the wilderness sojourn. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

44 COMMENTARY: Moses declares unto Israel the feasts of the LORD, proclaiming the times of assembling publicly, as commanded. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
