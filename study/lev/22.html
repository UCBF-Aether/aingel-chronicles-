Study Notes — Leviticus

Chapter 22

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: The LORD speaks to Moses, giving the law of priests in their uncleanness and of blemish-free sacrifices. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: Aaron and his sons shall separate themselves, that is abstain, from holy things and not profane God's holy name in things Israel hallows, a careless use of consecrated things dishonouring God's name and worship. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: Whoever of Aaron's seed in generations goes to holy things with uncleanness upon him shall be cut off from God's presence, minute restrictions keeping priests on guard and preserving awe and submission, lest familiarity lead them to trifle with religion under cloak of office. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: A leper or one with a running issue shall not eat holy things until clean, and whoever touches anything unclean by the dead or a man whose seed goes from him is likewise barred, priests being under no greater license than the people. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: Whoever touches a creeping thing making him unclean, or a man from whom he takes uncleanness, whatever it is, is barred until cleansed, the chief temporary defilements being specified to prevent priestly license. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: The soul that touches any such shall be unclean until evening and shall not eat holy things unless he washes his flesh with water, washing being the outward sign of restoration to forfeited privileges, not the cleansing itself. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: When the sun is down he shall be clean and afterward eat holy things, because it is his food, the evening limit ending the exclusion. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: That which dies of itself or is torn with beasts he shall not eat to defile himself, nature revolting against such food, but an express precept being needed to show that meat with its blood is prohibited. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: They shall keep God's ordinance, lest they bear sin and die if they profane it, for the LORD sanctifies them, neglect of washing leaving them in uncleanness and cut off. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: No stranger shall eat of the holy thing, neither a priest's sojourner nor hired servant, the priests' portion being restricted to his own family. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: If the priest buys a soul with his money, or one born in his house, they shall eat of his meat, a bought or homeborn slave being a stated household member, unlike a temporary guest. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: If a priest's daughter is married to a stranger, she may not eat of the holy offerings, having left her father's household. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: But if a priest's daughter is a widow or divorced, childless, and returned to her father's house as in her youth, she shall eat of her father's meat, being reinstated, whereas a mother must find support elsewhere since her children have no priestly privilege; and no stranger shall eat thereof, the interdict repeated to show its stringency. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: If a man eats of the holy thing unwittingly, he shall add a fifth part to it and give it to the priest with the holy thing, a common Israelite unconsciously partaking of tithes or first-fruits restoring the amount plus a fine for the sanctuary. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: They shall not profane Israel's holy things offered to the LORD, whether priests defiling offerings by presenting them while unclean, or Israel defiling them by touching or reserving what is appointed to priests alone. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: Nor shall they suffer Israel to bear iniquity of trespass when they eat their holy things, for the LORD sanctifies them, the guilt falling through profanation of the priests' portion. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: The LORD speaks to Moses again, now concerning sacrifices without blemish. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

18 COMMENTARY: Whoever of Israel or strangers offers an oblation for vows or freewill offerings as a burnt offering must observe the blemish law, which applies to native and stranger alike. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

19 COMMENTARY: They shall offer at their own will, to their being accepted, a male without blemish of beeves, sheep, or goats, sacrifices being gifts to a King, and no imperfect victim fitting praise, conciliation, or atonement. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

20 COMMENTARY: Whatever has a blemish shall not be offered, for it shall not be acceptable, a perfect victim being required as a fitting instrument for gratitude, worship, prayer, confession, and atonement. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

21 COMMENTARY: Whoever offers a peace offering to accomplish a vow or a freewill offering in beeves or sheep, it shall be perfect to be accepted, with no blemish in it, symbolising whole dedication with sincere heart and pointing typically to Him without whom no sacrifice is acceptable. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

22 COMMENTARY: Blind, broken, maimed, wen, scurvy, or scabbed animals shall not be offered to the LORD nor made an offering by fire on the altar, each blemish disqualifying the gift to the King. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

23 COMMENTARY: A bullock or lamb with anything superfluous or lacking may be offered for a freewill offering but for a vow shall not be accepted, the rendering being that for either vow or freewill a blemished victim is not accepted, since without blemish is required. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

24 COMMENTARY: They shall not offer to the LORD what is bruised, crushed, broken, or cut, nor make any offering thereof in their land, the same perfection being required of every altar victim. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

25 COMMENTARY: Neither from a stranger's hand shall they offer God's bread of any of these, because their corruption is in them and blemishes in them, they shall not be accepted, a stranger's blemished gift being no better than a native's. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

26 COMMENTARY: The LORD speaks to Moses again, concerning the age and humanity rules for sacrifices. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

27 COMMENTARY: A bullock, sheep, or goat shall be seven days under the dam, and from the eighth day onward it shall be accepted for an offering by fire, animals not being perfect nor good for food until the eighth day, and offering them earlier would indicate contempt of religion. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

28 COMMENTARY: Whether cow or ewe, they shall not kill it and her young both in one day, a lesson of humanity and tenderness to the dam, securing sacrifices from appearance of unfeeling cruelty. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

29 COMMENTARY: When they offer a sacrifice of thanksgiving, they shall offer it at their own will, as a freewill gift to the King. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

30 COMMENTARY: On the same day it shall be eaten, leaving none until the morrow, the thanksgiving offering being confined to its day, unlike a freewill offering allowed a second day. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

31 COMMENTARY: Therefore they shall keep God's commandments and do them, for He is the LORD, the blemish and holiness laws ending in obedience. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

32 COMMENTARY: Neither shall they profane His holy name, but He will be hallowed among Israel, for He is the LORD who sanctifies them, careless use of holy things dishonouring the name that must be hallowed. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

33 COMMENTARY: He brought them out of Egypt to be their God, for He is the LORD, the exodus grounding the holiness and sacrifice laws in deliverance. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
