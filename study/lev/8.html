Study Notes — Leviticus

Chapter 8

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: The consecration now described in full detail had been ordered long before in Exodus 29, but is carried out only after the tabernacle is completed and the sacrifice laws are enacted. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: Aaron and his sons are taken with the garments, anointing oil, sin-offering bullock, two rams, and basket of unleavened bread, the full provision for the consecration ceremonial. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: All the congregation is gathered at the tabernacle door, so the people may be satisfied that Aaron's appointment is not a personal intrusion or a family arrangement between him and Moses. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: Moses does as commanded and the congregation gathers, the public assembly itself impressing the divine origin and authority of the priestly institution. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: Moses tells the congregation that this is what the LORD commanded to be done, framing the whole inauguration as divinely prescribed, not humanly devised. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: Moses washes Aaron and his sons wholly with water, whereas on ordinary occasions only hands and feet are washed, the entire ablution teaching inward purity and the obligation of sanctuary ministers to be holy. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: The coat, girdle, robe, and ephod with its curious girdle are put on Aaron, the splendour of the vestments producing respect for the ministry and the predominant linen inculcating unspotted righteousness. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: The breastplate is put on him with the Urim and Thummim in it, completing the high priest's vestments whose splendour and linen alike point to dignity and righteousness. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: The mitre is set on his head with the golden plate, the holy crown, on its front, the gorgeous tiara completing the official vestments intended to command respect. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: Moses anoints the tabernacle and all in it with the anointing oil and consecrates them, the anointing intimating that leaders in worship should have the unction of the Holy One in gifts and graces. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: Oil is sprinkled seven times on the altar and the altar, its utensils, the laver, and its base are anointed, consecrating the whole place of service. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: Oil is poured on Aaron's head and he is anointed to consecrate him, marking the high priest himself as set apart by the same unction as the sanctuary. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: Aaron's sons are brought, clothed in coats, girded with girdles, and given bonnets, sharing in the linen vestments that inculcate unspotted righteousness. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: The bullock for the sin offering is brought and Aaron and his sons lay hands on its head, a timely public confession of personal sin and transference of guilt to the typical victim. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: Moses kills the bullock, puts blood on the altar horns with his finger, purifies the altar, and pours the rest at its base, consecrating the altar to make atonement for it. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: The fat, the caul, and the kidneys are burnt on the altar, the altar portion of the sin offering, while the rest of the victim is dealt with outside. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: The bullock's skin, flesh, and dung are burnt with fire without the camp, as with a sin offering whose blood is carried into the sanctuary, it is not eaten. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

18 COMMENTARY: The ram for a burnt offering is brought, a token of the priests' entire dedication to the service of God after their sin has been confessed. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

19 COMMENTARY: Moses kills the ram and sprinkles its blood round about the altar, the burnt offering following the sin offering in the consecration order. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

20 COMMENTARY: The ram is cut into pieces, its head and fat laid in order, and its inwards and legs washed, preparing the whole burnt offering. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

21 COMMENTARY: Moses burns the whole ram on the altar as a burnt sacrifice of a sweet savour, the token of entire dedication being wholly consumed. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

22 COMMENTARY: The other ram, the ram of consecration, is brought after sin and burnt offerings, as their peace offering declaring pleasure in entering God's service. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

23 COMMENTARY: Moses kills the consecration ram and puts its blood on Aaron's right ear, right thumb, and right great toe, marking hearing, doing, and walking as consecrated. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

24 COMMENTARY: The same blood is put on Aaron's sons' right ears, thumbs, and toes, and the rest sprinkled round about the altar, bringing them into the same consecration. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

25 COMMENTARY: The fat, rump, inward fat, caul, kidneys, and right shoulder are taken, the altar and priestly portions of the consecration ram. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

26 COMMENTARY: From the basket of consecration, unleavened cakes, a cake of oiled bread, and a wafer are taken, joining bread to the peace offering of consecration. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

27 COMMENTARY: All these are put in Aaron's and his sons' hands and waved as a wave offering before the LORD, declaring their pleasure in being brought into close communion with God as sanctuary ministers. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

28 COMMENTARY: Moses takes them from their hands and burns them on the altar upon the burnt offering, as consecration offerings for a sweet savour made by fire. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

29 COMMENTARY: Moses takes the breast and waves it before the LORD as his part of the ram of consecration, the officiant receiving the wave portion. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

30 COMMENTARY: Moses sprinkles anointing oil and altar blood on Aaron, his garments, his sons, and their garments, consecrating persons and vestments together for service. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

31 COMMENTARY: Aaron and his sons boil the flesh at the tabernacle door and eat it there with the basket bread, as commanded, sharing the peace offering of their own consecration. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

32 COMMENTARY: What remains of flesh and bread shall be burnt with fire, so no part of the consecration meal is kept beyond its appointed use. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

33 COMMENTARY: They shall not go out of the tabernacle door for seven days, undergoing a week's probation in the court before entering the interior, with the same rites observed each day. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

34 COMMENTARY: As has been done this day, so the LORD has commanded to be done to make atonement for them, the whole consecration being a divinely appointed atonement. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

35 COMMENTARY: They must stay at the tabernacle door day and night for seven days and keep the LORD's charge, lest they die, the smallest breach forfeiting their lives. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

36 COMMENTARY: Aaron and his sons do all things the LORD commanded by Moses, completing the public consecration that vindicates the priesthood's divine origin. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
