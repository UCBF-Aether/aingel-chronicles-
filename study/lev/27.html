Study Notes — Leviticus

Chapter 27

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: The LORD speaks to Moses, giving the vow laws that close Leviticus, prescribing conditions for the voluntary duty of votive offerings. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: When a man makes a singular vow, persons shall be for the LORD by estimation, persons being accustomed at all times to present votive offerings from gratitude or deliverance from apprehended evil, and Moses being empowered to prescribe the conditions. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: The estimation shall be, for a male from twenty years old even unto sixty, fifty shekels of silver after the shekel of the sanctuary, mature age being rated highest as capable of greatest service. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: If it be a female, the estimation shall be thirty shekels, the female valuation being set below the male's at the same age. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: If from five years old even unto twenty, the estimation shall be for a male twenty shekels and for a female ten, young people being rated less because not so serviceable. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: If from a month old even unto five years, the estimation shall be for a male five shekels of silver and for a female three, infants being devotable by parents before birth but not offered nor redeemed till a month after birth. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: If from sixty years old and above, if a male, the estimation shall be fifteen shekels, and for a female ten, old people being valued below the young but above children. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: But if he is poorer than the estimation, then he shall present himself before the priest, and the priest shall value him according to his ability that vowed, the poor in no case being freed from payment, to prevent rash vows, but rated according to means, with priestly discretion to reduce. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: If it is a beast whereof men bring an offering unto the LORD, all that he gives of such unto the LORD shall be holy, a clean beast after vowing being neither usable for common purposes nor exchangeable, but sacrificed. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: He shall not alter it nor change it, a good for a bad or a bad for a good, and if he changes beast for beast, then it and the exchange thereof shall be holy, the vow binding both animals. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: If it is any unclean beast of which they do not offer a sacrifice unto the LORD, then he shall present the beast before the priest, an ass or camel, for instance, being appropriated to the priest at estimated value. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: The priest shall value it, whether good or bad, and as the priest values it, so shall it stand, the priest's estimation fixing the redemption price. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: But if he will at all redeem it, then he shall add a fifth part thereof unto the estimation, the unclean beast being redeemable by the vower on payment of value plus a fifth. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: When a man sanctifies his house to be holy unto the LORD, then the priest shall estimate it, whether good or bad, and as the priest estimates it, so shall it stand, the house being valued and sold with proceeds dedicated to the sanctuary. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: If he who sanctified it will redeem his house, then he shall add the fifth part of the money of the estimation unto it, and it shall be his, redemption on second thought costing value plus a fifth. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: If a man sanctifies unto the LORD some part of a field of his possession, then the estimation shall be according to the seed thereof, a homer of barley seed valued at fifty shekels of silver, the field's value being reckoned by its sowing capacity. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: If he sanctifies his field from the year of jubilee, according to the estimation it shall stand, a field sanctified at jubilee bearing the full valuation. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

18 COMMENTARY: But if he sanctifies his field after the jubilee, then the priest shall reckon the money according to the years remaining even unto the jubilee, and it shall be abated from the estimation, later sanctification costing only the remaining years' proportion. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

19 COMMENTARY: If he who sanctified the field will in any wise redeem it, then he shall add the fifth part of the money of the estimation unto it, and it shall be assured to him, redemption of a hereditary field costing value plus a fifth. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

20 COMMENTARY: If he will not redeem the field, or if he has sold the field to another man, it shall not be redeemed any more, an unredeemed hereditary field passing beyond redemption once sold to another. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

21 COMMENTARY: But the field, when it goes out in the jubilee, shall be holy unto the LORD, as a field devoted, the possession thereof shall be the priest's, a vowed hereditary estate not reverting to the owner but remaining attached in perpetuity to the sanctuary, to oblige redemption or kinsman redemption lest a patrimonial inheritance leave a family. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

22 COMMENTARY: If a man sanctifies unto the LORD a field which he has bought, which is not of the fields of his possession, acquired property in land being treated differently from hereditary estate. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

23 COMMENTARY: Then the priest shall reckon unto him the worth of the estimation, even unto the year of jubilee, and he shall give the estimation in that day, as a holy thing unto the LORD, the purchased field's value being reckoned to jubilee and paid at once. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

24 COMMENTARY: In the year of jubilee the field shall return unto him of whom it was bought, even to him to whom the possession of the land did belong, acquired land, if not redeemed, returning to the donor at jubilee, unlike hereditary land. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

25 COMMENTARY: All estimations shall be according to the shekel of the sanctuary, twenty gerahs shall be the shekel, the sanctuary standard fixing every valuation. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

26 COMMENTARY: Only the firstling of the beasts, which should be the LORD's firstling, no man shall sanctify it, whether ox or sheep, it is the LORD's, clean firstlings being consecrated by universal standing law and therefore not devotable. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

27 COMMENTARY: If it is of an unclean beast, then he shall redeem it according to the estimation, and shall add a fifth part unto it, or if it is not redeemed, then it shall be sold according to the estimation, unclean firstlings following the unclean-beast rule. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

28 COMMENTARY: Notwithstanding, no devoted thing that a man shall devote unto the LORD of all that he has, both man and beast, or field of his possession, shall be sold or redeemed, every devoted thing is most holy unto the LORD, this relating to vows of the most solemn kind, accompanied by a solemn imprecation not to fail. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

29 COMMENTARY: None devoted, which shall be devoted of men, shall be redeemed, but shall surely be put to death, this importing not sacrifice or violent death, but remaining till death unalterably in the devoted condition. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

30 COMMENTARY: All the tithe of the land, whether of the seed of the land or of the fruit of the tree, is the LORD's, it is holy unto the LORD, the law sanctioning an ancient usage, the whole produce being subjected to a yearly rent paid by tenants to God, the land's owner, as thank offering for providence. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

31 COMMENTARY: If a man will at all redeem aught of his tithes, he shall add thereto the fifth part thereof, tithe redemption costing value plus a fifth. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

32 COMMENTARY: Concerning the tithe of herd or flock, even of whatsoever passes under the rod, the tenth shall be holy unto the LORD, cattle being made to pass singly through a narrow gateway where a man with a rod dipped in ochre marked every tenth beast, male or female, sound or unsound. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

33 COMMENTARY: He shall not search whether it is good or bad, neither shall he change it, and if he changes it at all, then both it and the change thereof shall be holy, it shall not be redeemed, the tenth being taken without selection or exchange. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

34 COMMENTARY: These are the commandments which the LORD commanded Moses for Israel in Mount Sinai, the book's laws, for the most part ceremonial, having important spiritual bearing, a burdensome yoke yet in the Church's infancy the necessary discipline of a schoolmaster to Christ. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
