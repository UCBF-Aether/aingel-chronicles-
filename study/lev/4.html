Study Notes — Leviticus

Chapter 4

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: The sin offering law is addressed to Israel as a whole, introducing a rite for sins committed not wilfully but in ignorance or inadvertence. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: Sins through ignorance here are unintentional breaches of ceremonial law, made through haste or inadvertency of negative precepts, which if done knowingly and wilfully would have carried a capital penalty. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: The anointed priest means chiefly the high priest, whose office as typical mediator makes even an unconscious transgression grave, both for himself and by the influence of his example, and whose guilt is said to bring guilt on the people. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: The priest brings the young bullock to the tabernacle door, lays his hand on its head, and kills it before the LORD, following the form prescribed for burnt offerings. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: The anointed priest himself takes the bullock's blood into the tabernacle, a solemn ceremonial reserved for very grave offences, signalling that his sin has vitiated his services until atoned for by blood. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: Sprinkling the blood seven times before the veil shows that the priest's sin, though done in ignorance, has tainted the sanctuary service itself and requires expiation within the holy place. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: Blood is put on the horns of the golden altar of incense and the rest poured at the base of the burnt-offering altar, carrying the atonement from the inner sanctuary out to the court of the priests. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: All the fat of the bullock for the sin offering is taken off, as in the peace offering, the choice inward portions being reserved for the altar. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: The fat covering the inwards and lying upon them is removed with the rest of the altar portion, following the same pattern as the peace offering's fat. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: The kidneys with their fat and the caul above the liver are taken away and burnt on the altar of burnt offering, as was done with the bullock of the peace offerings. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: In ordinary circumstances the skin and flesh would be priests' perquisites, but for a high priest's sin the whole carcass is dealt with outside the camp, to mark the enormity of the transgression and the horror with which it is regarded. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: The whole bullock is carried to a clean place without the camp where the ashes are poured out and burnt on wood with fire, its total combustion outside the camp underscoring the gravity of the priest's sin. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: When the whole congregation sins through ignorance, through culpable neglect or misapprehension of the law, national guilt is contracted and national expiation becomes necessary. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: Once the congregation's sin is known, it offers a young bullock for the sin, the same sacrifice as for the priest, since the guilt taints the whole assembly. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: The elders, as heads of the tribes representing the people and as the principal aggressors in misleading the congregation, lay their hands on the bullock's head before the LORD. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: The anointed priest brings the bullock's blood into the tabernacle, as in the priest's own offering, because the congregation's sin likewise taints the sanctuary service. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: Dipping his finger in the blood, the priest sprinkles it seven times before the veil, the same inner-sanctuary expiation required when the ritual itself has been vitiated. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

18 COMMENTARY: Blood is put on the horns of the incense altar in the tabernacle and the rest poured at the base of the burnt-offering altar, completing the movement of atonement from sanctuary to court. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

19 COMMENTARY: All the fat is taken from the bullock and burnt on the altar, the only part of the animal offered on the altar in this rite. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

20 COMMENTARY: The bullock is dealt with exactly as the priest's sin-offering bullock, and by this atonement the congregation is forgiven. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

21 COMMENTARY: The bullock is carried forth without the camp and burnt as the first bullock was, since it is a sin offering for the congregation. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

22 COMMENTARY: A ruler, whether king, judge, or subordinate, is covered by this law, but his trespass is less serious in character and consequence than that of the high priest or the whole congregation. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

23 COMMENTARY: When his sin comes to his knowledge, the ruler brings a kid of the goats, a male without blemish, a sin offering of inferior value to the priest's or congregation's bullock. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

24 COMMENTARY: The ruler lays his hand on the goat's head and kills it where the burnt offering is killed, the same laying-on and slaughter as in the other sin offerings. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

25 COMMENTARY: For a ruler the blood is not carried into the sanctuary but is put by the priest's finger on the horns of the burnt-offering altar and poured at its base. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

26 COMMENTARY: All the fat is burnt on the altar as the fat of the peace offerings, and by this atonement the ruler's sin is forgiven, while the carcass, unlike the priest's bullock, is eaten by the priests-in-waiting. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

27 COMMENTARY: A common person who sins through ignorance is covered by the same law as the ruler, with an offering of a female kid or a ewe-lamb without blemish. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

28 COMMENTARY: When his sin comes to his knowledge, the common offerer brings a kid of the goats, a female without blemish, for the sin he has sinned. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

29 COMMENTARY: He lays his hand on the sin offering's head and slays it in the place of the burnt offering, following the ruler's ceremony exactly. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

30 COMMENTARY: The priest puts blood with his finger on the horns of the burnt-offering altar and pours out all the blood at its base, since this sin, unlike the priest's or congregation's, does not taint the sanctuary ritual. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

31 COMMENTARY: All the fat is taken away as from the peace offering and burnt for a sweet savour, and by this atonement the common person is forgiven. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

32 COMMENTARY: If a lamb is brought for a sin offering, it is to be a female without blemish, the same value and character as the female kid. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

33 COMMENTARY: The offerer lays his hand on the lamb's head and slays it for a sin offering where the burnt offering is killed, under the same ceremony as the ruler's and the common person's kid. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

34 COMMENTARY: The priest puts blood on the horns of the burnt-offering altar and pours out all the blood at its base, applying the atonement at the place where bloody sacrifices are appointed to be immolated. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

35 COMMENTARY: The fat is burnt as an offering made by fire, and the sinner is forgiven, not because these sacrifices had intrinsic power to cleanse conscience, but as figurative representations of the full and perfect sin offering to be made by Christ. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
