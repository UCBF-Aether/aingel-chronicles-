Study Notes — Leviticus

Chapter 18

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: The LORD speaks to Moses, giving the law of unlawful marriages after the blood law, as a law widely different from Egyptian and Canaanite custom. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: Israel is addressed as the LORD's people, I am the LORD your God, a renewed mention of divine sovereignty bearing particularly on these laws. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: They shall not do according to Egypt, where they dwelt, nor according to Canaan, where they are being brought, nor walk in their ordinances, since the enormities prohibited here were freely practised or publicly sanctioned in both countries. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: They shall do the LORD's judgments and keep His ordinances to walk in them, for He is the LORD their God, in place of Egyptian and Canaanite ordinances. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: Keeping His statutes and judgments brings life in them, a blessing verified in eras of pure religion by public prosperity and domestic happiness, and with a higher reference to spiritual life as applied by Christ and Paul. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: None shall approach any near of kin to uncover nakedness, a general summary of all particular prohibitions, against Egyptian laxity that sanctioned brother-sister and even parent-child marriages, forming the basis of later Christian marriage law. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: The nakedness of father or mother shall not be uncovered, she being his mother, the first particular case under the general summary, a consanguinity prohibition. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: The nakedness of his father's wife shall not be uncovered, for it is his father's nakedness, an affinity prohibition included in the general summary. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: The nakedness of his sister, father's daughter or mother's daughter, born at home or elsewhere, shall not be uncovered, a consanguinity case. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: The nakedness of son's daughter or daughter's daughter shall not be uncovered, for theirs is his own nakedness, a consanguinity case in the descending line. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: The nakedness of his father's wife's daughter, begotten by his father, she being his sister, shall not be uncovered, a half-sister case under the same summary. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: The nakedness of his father's sister shall not be uncovered, for she is near of kin to his father, an aunt case. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: The nakedness of his mother's sister shall not be uncovered, for she is near of kin to his mother. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: The nakedness of his father's brother shall not be uncovered, nor shall he approach his wife, for she is his aunt, an uncle's-wife affinity case. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: The nakedness of his daughter-in-law, his son's wife, shall not be uncovered, an affinity case. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: The nakedness of his brother's wife shall not be uncovered, for it is his brother's nakedness, an affinity case completing the eleven affinity cases. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: A woman and her daughter, or her son's daughter or daughter's daughter, shall not be taken to uncover nakedness, for they are near kinswomen, a wicked consanguinity case. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

18 COMMENTARY: Neither shall he take a wife to her sister to vex her, beside the other in her lifetime, a practice common among Egyptians and Chaldeans, the marginal reading also prohibiting polygamy as contrary to the original marriage law, though Moses tolerated it for hardness of heart. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

19 COMMENTARY: He shall not approach a woman to uncover her nakedness as long as she is put apart for her uncleanness, the separation law of chapter 15 applying within the marriage prohibitions. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

20 COMMENTARY: He shall not lie carnally with his neighbour's wife to defile himself with her, an adultery case among the enumerated enormities. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

21 COMMENTARY: He shall not let any seed pass through the fire to Molech, the Ammonite idol, king, whose brass calf-headed statue was heated and infants passed through its arms for lustration, nor profane God's name by giving foreigners occasion to call Him cruel for demanding human victims. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

22 COMMENTARY: He shall not lie with mankind as with womankind, for it is abomination, among the unnatural crimes enumerated with incest. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

23 COMMENTARY: Neither shall he lie with any beast to defile himself, nor shall a woman stand before a beast to lie down thereto, for it is confusion, an unnatural enormity. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

24 COMMENTARY: Israel shall not defile themselves in any of these things, for in all these the nations are defiled which God casts out before them, seventeen express incest cases, eleven of affinity and six of consanguinity, with aggravated unnatural crimes, being the cause of Canaan's expulsion. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

25 COMMENTARY: The land is defiled, therefore God visits its iniquity upon it, and the land itself vomits out her inhabitants, figurative language showing the hopeless depth of Canaanite moral corruption and the judicial character of their extermination. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

26 COMMENTARY: Israel shall keep His statutes and judgments and commit none of these abominations, neither native nor sojourning stranger, since the law binds the stranger dwelling among them as well. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

27 COMMENTARY: For all these abominations have the men of the land done, who were before them, and the land is defiled, the prior inhabitants having already polluted it by the same practices. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

28 COMMENTARY: That the land does not spew Israel out also, as it spewed out the nations before them, is the condition of their keeping, since the same defilement would bring the same expulsion. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

29 COMMENTARY: For whosoever commits any of these abominations, even the souls that commit them shall be cut off from among their people, death being sternly denounced without distinction on incest, affinity, consanguinity, and bestiality alike. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

30 COMMENTARY: Therefore they shall keep His ordinance, committing none of these abominable customs practised before them, and not defiling themselves, for God is only re-delivering the law imprinted on the natural heart, forbidden to all men from the beginning or at least from the flood. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
