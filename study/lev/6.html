Study Notes — Leviticus

Chapter 6

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: This law belongs with the previous chapter and covers witting trespass against the LORD expressed against a neighbour, such as lying over a deposit or fellowship. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: Things stolen, fraudulently gotten, or wrongfully kept are all covered, and the offender must restore the article to its rightful owner together with a fifth part from his own possessions. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: Finding a lost thing and lying about it with a false oath is likewise a trespass, since the sin lies in the deceitful keeping of what belongs to another. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: Because he has sinned and is guilty, he must restore what was taken violently, gotten deceitfully, delivered to keep, or found and kept. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: Whatever was sworn about falsely is restored in full principal with a fifth part more, given to its owner on the day of his trespass offering, so restitution precedes sacrifice. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: Repair to neighbour and society is not enough; he must also bring a ram without blemish at the priest's estimation as a trespass offering, as a token of sorrow for having hurt the cause of religion and of God. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: The priest makes atonement before the LORD, and he is forgiven for anything he has done in trespassing, the penalty acting as a mitigated fine joined to a sacred duty that revives responsibility to God. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: A new section of priestly instruction begins, addressed to Aaron and his sons concerning their official duties in the daily service. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: The burnt offering, a sacrifice that goes up in smoke, consists of two daily lambs, morning and evening, consumed by a slow fire so placed that the pieces feed it all night, a daily expression of national repentance and faith. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: The priest, in linen garment and linen breeches, takes up the ashes the fire has consumed and puts them beside the altar, tending the heaven-kindled fire so it is not extinguished or replaced with common fire. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: Changing out of his holy garments into others, he carries the ashes without the camp to a clean place, keeping the approach to the fireplace itself in officially holy garments. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: Wood is burnt on the altar every morning and the burnt offering laid in order upon it, with the fat of the peace offerings, so the fire is continually fed. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: The fire shall ever be burning and never go out, preserving the fire kindled from heaven at the tabernacle's consecration. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: The law of the meat offering follows, though it is a provision for the priests and their families it is to be regarded as most holy. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: The priest carries the meat offering to the altar, takes a handful of flour and oil with all the frankincense as an oblation, salts it, and burns it for a sweet savour as the memorial. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: The remainder is eaten by Aaron and his sons with unleavened bread in the holy place, in the court of the tabernacle, as the food of those who attend the service. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: It shall not be baked with leaven, for it is their portion of the offerings made by fire and is most holy, as are the sin offering and the trespass offering. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

18 COMMENTARY: All the males among Aaron's children eat of it by a perpetual statute, and everyone who touches these offerings shall be holy, while no one under ceremonial defilement may partake. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

19 COMMENTARY: The high priest's own meat offering is now prescribed for the day of his anointing, a perpetual offering distinct from the ordinary priests' portions. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

20 COMMENTARY: This daily meat offering of the high priest, a tenth of an ephah of fine flour, half in the morning and half at night, was probably meant for his successors in the high office, and was wholly consumed on the altar. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

21 COMMENTARY: Made in a pan with oil and brought in baked in pieces, it is offered for a sweet savour, designed to remind the priests that their own persons and services are accepted only through faith daily nourished from above. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

22 COMMENTARY: The priest anointed in Aaron's stead offers this same offering by a perpetual statute, wholly burnt to the LORD. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

23 COMMENTARY: Every meat offering for a priest shall be wholly burnt and not eaten, unlike the ordinary meat offerings whose remainder feeds the priests. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

24 COMMENTARY: A further priestly law is spoken to Aaron and his sons, now concerning the sin offering. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

25 COMMENTARY: The sin offering is killed where the burnt offering is killed before the LORD and is most holy, its fat and inwards being washed, salted, and burnt on the altar. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

26 COMMENTARY: The officiating priest eats the sin offering in the holy place, in the court of the tabernacle, the rest of the carcass belonging to him and his family alone. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

27 COMMENTARY: Whatever touches its flesh shall be holy, and blood sprinkled on a garment must be washed in a holy place, these minute ceremonies impressing the evil nature of sin and the care to prevent any taint clinging to them. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

28 COMMENTARY: An earthen vessel in which the flesh is boiled must be broken, being porous and likely to imbibe particles, while a bronze pot must be scoured and rinsed, not because the vessels are defiled but because they are now too sacred for ordinary use. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

29 COMMENTARY: All the males among the priests may eat of it, since it is most holy, but only within the precincts of the tabernacle and only if free from ceremonial defilement. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

30 COMMENTARY: No sin offering whose blood is brought into the tabernacle to reconcile in the holy place, as for the high priest or the whole congregation, shall be eaten; it shall be burnt in the fire outside the camp. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
