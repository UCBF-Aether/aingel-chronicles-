Study Notes — Leviticus

Chapter 19

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: The LORD speaks to Moses, rehearsing sundry laws of general application to the whole congregation, not to particular classes alone. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: All the congregation shall be holy, for the LORD their God is holy, separated from the world, since His character, laws, and service are holy. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: Every man shall fear his mother and father and keep the LORD's sabbaths, obedience to parents and Sabbath observance lying together at the foundation of practical religion. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: They shall not turn to idols nor make molten gods, for the LORD is their God, the first religious duty after holiness, parents, and Sabbath. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: A peace offering shall be offered at his own will, thank and vow offerings being always freewill offerings, eaten by offerer and friends except the wave and heave portions for priests. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: It shall be eaten the same day and on the next day, a freewill offering being allowed a second day, unlike a thank offering confined to its day. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: If it is eaten at all on the third day, it is abomination and shall not be accepted, the strict prohibition preventing superstitious virtue being attached to altar meat. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: Everyone who eats it bears iniquity, because he has profaned the LORD's hallowed thing, and that soul shall be cut off, deep criminality attaching to third-day eating. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: When reaping, they shall not wholly reap the field corners nor gather gleanings, the poor's right to glean and to unreaped corners being secured by positive statute, the earliest poor law in any people's code. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: They shall not glean the vineyard nor gather every grape, but leave them for poor and stranger, combining public duty with private benevolence at a time when rich hearts would be inclined to liberality. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: They shall not steal, deal falsely, or lie to one another, social duties inculcated against petty frauds and violations of truth in business. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: They shall not swear falsely by the LORD's name nor profane God's name, truth in speech being part of the same social honesty. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: They shall not defraud or rob a neighbour, nor let a hired man's wages remain all night until morning, exploitation of a neighbour being forbidden alongside theft. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: They shall not curse the deaf nor put a stumbling block before the blind, but fear God, humanity forbidding ridicule of bodily infirmities. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: They shall do no unrighteousness in judgment, not respecting the poor nor honouring the mighty, but judging a neighbour in righteousness, impartiality being required in law as in dealings. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: They shall not go up and down as a talebearer among the people, nor stand against the blood of a neighbour, circulating stories to others' prejudice being a little-thought-of vice opposed by brotherly kindness. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: They shall not hate a brother in heart, but in any wise rebuke a neighbour and not suffer sin upon him, remonstrating calmly and kindly instead of cherishing malice or revenge, lest they participate in his sin. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

18 COMMENTARY: They shall not avenge or bear grudge against their people, but love a neighbour as themselves, neighbour meaning fellow creature, a narrow national interpretation refuted by the parable of the Good Samaritan. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

19 COMMENTARY: They shall not let cattle gender with a diverse kind, sow mingled seed, or wear linen-woollen mingled garments, prohibitions aimed at unnatural lusts and Zabian magical sowing, and at mixtures injurious to crops and, in hot climates, to the body through electricity passing off. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

23 COMMENTARY: When they plant fruit trees, they shall count the fruit uncircumcised for three years and not eat it, gardeners teaching that trees thrive better and bear more abundantly afterwards if blossoms are pinched off in early years. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

24 COMMENTARY: In the fourth year all the fruit shall be holy to praise the LORD, the first allowed crop being devoted as a holy thing. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

25 COMMENTARY: In the fifth year they shall eat its fruit, that it may yield its increase, the delayed enjoyment following the years of restraint and dedication. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

26 COMMENTARY: They shall not eat anything with the blood, as in chapter 17, nor use enchantment nor observe times, divination by serpents and cloud-observation being forbidden as implying want of faith in God's providence. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

27 COMMENTARY: They shall not round the corners of their heads nor mar the corners of their beard, an Egyptian idolatrous coiffure, cropped in a circle with a square beard, being forbidden to separate Israel from neighbouring idolaters. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

28 COMMENTARY: They shall not make cuttings in their flesh for the dead nor print marks upon themselves, heathen bereavement gashes and tattooed idol marks being signs of apostasy and obstacles to return. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

29 COMMENTARY: A father shall not profane his daughter to cause her to be a whore, lest the land fall to whoredom and be full of wickedness, the same separation from heathen practice that forbids pagan hair, cuttings, and necromancy. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

30 COMMENTARY: They shall keep the LORD's sabbaths and reverence His sanctuary, a precept frequently repeated with prohibitions of idolatrous practice. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

31 COMMENTARY: They shall not regard familiar spirits nor seek wizards to be defiled by them, ventriloquists pretending communication with the invisible world and palmistry fortune-tellers being derogatory to God's honour and subversive of covenant relations. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

32 COMMENTARY: They shall rise up before the hoary head and honour the face of the old man and fear God, the humanity and brotherly kindness enforced in the neighbour duties extending to the aged. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

33 COMMENTARY: If a stranger sojourns with them, they shall not vex him, but encourage strangers to settle that they may come to know and worship the true God, treating them as friends, not aliens. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

34 COMMENTARY: The stranger dwelling with them shall be as one born among them, and they shall love him as themselves, for they were strangers in Egypt, their own kindly reception there grounding the duty. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

35 COMMENTARY: They shall do no unrighteousness in judgment, in meteyard, in weight, or in measure, the honesty required in judgment and dealings extending to measures kept to tabernacle standards. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

36 COMMENTARY: Just balances, just weights, a just ephah, and a just hin shall they have, for the LORD is their God who brought them out of Egypt, false balances being worse than open robbery, robbing under colour of equity. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

37 COMMENTARY: Therefore they shall observe all His statutes and judgments and do them, for He is the LORD, Creator, Deliverer, and Sovereign, with wisdom to establish laws and power to punish their violation. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

No note for verse(s) 20, 21, 22 — the public-domain sources consulted give no distinct explanation for these verses beyond the list itself, so nothing was invented for them.
