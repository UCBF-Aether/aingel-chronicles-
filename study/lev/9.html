Study Notes — Leviticus

Chapter 9

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: On the eighth day, after the seven days of consecration, Moses calls Aaron, his sons, and Israel's elders, so the newly made priests begin their official acts under Moses as mediator of the divine will. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: Aaron takes a young calf for a sin offering and a ram for a burnt offering, both without blemish and of the prescribed age, the calf perhaps recalling his sin in the golden calf. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: Aaron goes to the altar and slays the calf of the sin offering for himself, assisted by his sons, since the divisions of priestly labour are not yet completely arranged. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: Aaron brings the people's offering, takes the goat for the people's sin offering, slays it, and offers it for sin as the first, the same kind of goat as in Leviticus 4. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: He brings the meat offering, takes a handful, and burns it on the altar beside the morning burnt sacrifice, the meat offering accompanying the people's offerings though none was noted for the priests' own. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

18 COMMENTARY: He kills the bull and ram as peace offerings for the people, and his sons present the blood, which he sprinkles round about the altar, ending the ceremonial with a sacred feast. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

19 COMMENTARY: The fat from bull and ram, the fat tail, the inward fat, the kidneys, and the caul, are taken as the altar portion of the peace offerings. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

20 COMMENTARY: The fat is put on the breasts and burnt on the altar, while the breasts themselves are reserved for waving. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

21 COMMENTARY: The breasts and right shoulder are waved before the LORD as commanded, the priests' portion of the people's peace offerings. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

22 COMMENTARY: Aaron lifts his hand toward the people and blesses them, then comes down from the altar, which stood elevated above the floor with ascent by a gentle slope, the benediction being a necessary part of the high priest's duty in the form of Numbers 6. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

23 COMMENTARY: Moses and Aaron go into the tabernacle to bear the blood within the sanctuary and initiate the priests, then come out and bless the people, and the glory of the LORD appears to all, a fresh token of divine acceptance of the newly established worship. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

24 COMMENTARY: Fire comes out from before the LORD, a flame from the resplendent light in the holy place, and consumes the burnt offering and fat, a sign of acceptance, of Aaron's authority, and of God's actual residence there, and the people shout and fall on their faces in reverent worship of a present Deity. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

No note for verse(s) 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 9, 10, 11, 12, 13, 14, 16 — the public-domain sources consulted give no distinct explanation for these verses beyond the list itself, so nothing was invented for them.
