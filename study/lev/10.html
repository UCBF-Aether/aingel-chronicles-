Study Notes — Leviticus

Chapter 10

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Nadab and Abihu, Aaron's sons, take censers, put fire and incense in them, and offer strange fire, venturing unauthorized into the highest priestly office, together in a work for one man, and intruding where only the high priest might go, with common fire instead of the altar fire they had just seen descend. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: Fire goes out from the LORD and devours them, rather kills them, for neither bodies nor robes are consumed, the fire issuing from the most holy place as a first-instance judgment repressing contempt for sacred things. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: The LORD will be sanctified in those who come near Him, a warning given repeatedly to priests about cautious and reverent approach, and Aaron holds his peace, submitting in silent resignation to the righteous judgment of God. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: Moses calls Mishael and Elzaphan, Uzziel's sons, to carry their brethren from before the sanctuary out of the camp, so the painful intelligence spreads through the congregation and strikes salutary fear into priests and people. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: They carry the dead in their coats out of the camp as Moses commanded, their priestly vestments being buried with them as a sign of pollution by their wearers' sin. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: Aaron, Eleazar, and Ithamar are forbidden to uncover their heads or rend their garments in mourning, lest they die and wrath come on the congregation, the prohibition applying to their official costume while engaged in sacred duties. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: They shall not go out from the tabernacle door, lest they die, for the anointing oil is upon them, and they do as Moses commanded, remaining at their sacred duty despite bereavement. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: The LORD speaks to Aaron directly, introducing a prohibition that follows immediately on the catastrophe and has led many to conjecture, though only as conjecture, that the disobedient priests were intoxicated. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: Aaron and his sons shall not drink wine or strong drink when they go into the tabernacle, lest they die, a perpetual statute in their generations, guarding the clarity required for sacred service. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: This sobriety is required so they may distinguish between holy and unholy and between unclean and clean, the very distinction Nadab and Abihu had violated. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: They are also to teach Israel all the statutes the LORD has spoken by Moses, so that priestly instruction, not only priestly rite, is preserved. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: Moses rehearses to Aaron and his surviving sons the laws regulating priestly conduct, a timely and considerate reminder amid the distractions of bereavement, lest they forget a duty. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: The remainder of the meat offering is to be eaten without leaven beside the altar in the holy place, for it is most holy, as part of that rehearsed priestly law. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: The wave breast and heave shoulder are to be eaten in a clean place by Aaron, his sons, and his daughters, as their due from Israel's peace offerings. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: The heave shoulder and wave breast are brought with the fat offerings made by fire to be waved before the LORD, and they belong to Aaron and his sons by a perpetual statute. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: Moses diligently seeks the goat of the sin offering and finds it burnt, discovering a departure from ritual, and challenges the surviving sons, not Aaron whose heart is too lacerated, for the irregularity. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: Moses asks why they have not eaten the sin offering in the holy place, since it is most holy and given to bear the congregation's iniquity and make atonement for them, the priests typically representing the people and bearing their sins. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

18 COMMENTARY: Because its blood was not brought into the holy place, they should indeed have eaten it in the holy place as commanded, instead of burning it without the camp. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

19 COMMENTARY: Aaron answers that such things have befallen him, and if he had eaten the sin offering that day, would it have been accepted, explaining that all presentation duties were done except the festive part, omitted because his heart was too dejected or because he supposed the day's services were vitiated by judgment. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

20 COMMENTARY: Moses hears Aaron's explanation and is content, sympathising with his afflicted brother after pointing out the error, though Aaron was decidedly in the wrong, since no view of expediency ought to dispense with a positive statute. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
