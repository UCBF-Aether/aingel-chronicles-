Study Notes — Leviticus

Chapter 25

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: The LORD speaks to Moses in Mount Sinai, giving the sabbath-year and jubilee laws as Sinai legislation. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: When they come into the land God gives them, the land shall keep a sabbath unto the LORD, the observance beginning, some think, in the seventh year after entrance, others in the fourteenth after six years of agriculture following conquest and division. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: Six years they shall sow the field and prune the vineyard and gather its fruit, the six working years preceding the sabbath year. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: In the seventh year shall be a sabbath of rest unto the land, a sabbath for the LORD, neither sowing field nor pruning vineyard, a peculiar arrangement teaching God's providential presence. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: That which grows of its own accord of the harvest shall not be reaped, nor grapes of the undressed vine gathered, for it is a year of rest unto the land, its spontaneous produce being common property. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: The sabbath of the land shall be meat for them, for servant, maid, hired servant, and stranger sojourning with them, all sharing the spontaneous produce. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: For cattle and for beasts in the land shall all its increase be meat, the sabbath rest invigorating the land as the weekly Sabbath refreshes men and cattle. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: They shall number seven sabbaths of years, seven times seven years, forty-nine years, the space of seven sabbaths, the most extraordinary civil institution, named Jubilee from a horn or trumpet. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: Then shall the trumpet of jubilee sound on the tenth day of the seventh month, the day of atonement, throughout all the land, by public authority proclaiming universal redemption. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: They shall hallow the fiftieth year and proclaim liberty throughout the land to all inhabitants, it shall be a jubilee, every man returning to his possession and to his family, the prevailing opinion favouring the fiftieth year over the forty-ninth. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: A jubilee shall that fiftieth year be, neither sowing nor reaping what grows of itself nor gathering undressed vines, the land enjoying a sabbath of repose as in the sabbatic year. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: For it is the jubilee, holy unto them, and they shall eat the increase out of the field, spontaneous produce being eaten for subsistence but not hoarded as private stock. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: In the year of jubilee every man shall return unto his possession, inheritances however often alienated coming back to original proprietors, preserving families and tribes distinct and genealogies recorded. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: If they sell to a neighbour or buy from a neighbour's hand, they shall not oppress one another, the sale being limited by the jubilee period and requiring honest regard on both sides. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: According to the number of years after the jubilee shall he buy, and according to years of fruits shall he sell, the price following the remaining harvests until redemption. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: According to the multitude of years the price shall be increased, and according to fewness decreased, for according to the number of fruits he sells to his neighbour. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: They shall not oppress one another, but fear God, for He is the LORD their God, the same rule as verse 14, aimed at maintaining the original order of families and equality of condition. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

18 COMMENTARY: Therefore they shall do His statutes and keep His judgments and do them, and dwell in the land in safety, obedience securing safe dwelling. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

19 COMMENTARY: The land shall yield her fruit, and they shall eat their fill and dwell safely therein, the sabbath and jubilee obedience being rewarded with plenty. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

20 COMMENTARY: If they say, What shall we eat in the seventh year, behold, we shall not sow nor gather increase, the question anticipating the food deficiency from suspended labour. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

21 COMMENTARY: God will command His blessing in the sixth year, and it shall bring forth fruit for three years, a miraculous supply for the sabbatic year and for jubilee alike, staked by no legislator but one conscious of divine authority. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

22 COMMENTARY: They shall sow in the eighth year and eat yet of old fruit until the ninth year, until her fruits come in, they shall eat of the old store, the three-year supply covering sabbath, jubilee, and sowing years. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

23 COMMENTARY: The land shall not be sold for ever, for the land is God's, and they are strangers and sojourners with Him, holding it merely as tenants-at-will, with no power to dispose of it to strangers, though temporary sale with redemption at any time is allowed. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

24 COMMENTARY: In all the land of their possession they shall grant redemption of the land, the inalienable land being redeemable by design. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

25 COMMENTARY: If a brother becomes poor and sells some possession, and his redeemer, his near kinsman, comes to redeem it, then he may redeem what his brother sold, the kinsman-redeemer restoring the family inheritance. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

26 COMMENTARY: If the man has no redeemer but himself becomes able to redeem it, he may redeem it himself, self-redemption being equally allowed. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

27 COMMENTARY: Then let him count the years of the sale and restore the overplus to the man to whom he sold it, that he may return to his possession, the price being reckoned by remaining years to jubilee. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

28 COMMENTARY: But if he is not able to restore it, what was sold shall remain in the buyer's hand until the jubilee, and in the jubilee it shall go out and he shall return to his possession, recovering it free. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

29 COMMENTARY: If a man sells a dwelling house in a walled city, he may redeem it within a whole year after it is sold, within a full year he may redeem it, all house sales being subject to this condition. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

30 COMMENTARY: If it is not redeemed within a full year, the walled-city house shall be established forever to the buyer throughout generations and shall not go out in the jubilee, trading and foreign houses in walled towns differing from village houses connected with agriculture. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

31 COMMENTARY: But houses of villages without walls round about shall be counted as the fields of the country, they may be redeemed, and they shall go out in the jubilee, village houses being treated as parts of the land. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

32 COMMENTARY: Notwithstanding, the cities of the Levites and houses of their cities may they redeem at any time, Levites having no possessions but towns and houses, with the same privileges as other Israelites' lands. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

33 COMMENTARY: If a man purchases of the Levites, the house sold and the city of his possession shall go out in the jubilee, for Levite city houses are their possession among Israel, a permanent endowment for ministry support that cannot be alienated, though Levites may exchange among themselves. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

34 COMMENTARY: But the field of the suburbs of their cities may not be sold, for it is their perpetual possession, pasture land for cattle and flocks appropriated to Levitical cities. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

35 COMMENTARY: If a brother becomes poor and falls in decay, then they shall relieve him, whether stranger or sojourner, that he may live with them, a most benevolent provision for poor and unfortunate, requiring food, lodging, and money without usury. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

36 COMMENTARY: They shall take no usury nor increase from him, but fear God, that their brother may live with them, usury being severely condemned, though the prohibition is not applicable to modern business lending at legal rates. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

37 COMMENTARY: They shall not give him money upon usury, nor lend him victuals for increase, the same no-usury rule applying to food as to money. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

38 COMMENTARY: God is the LORD their God, who brought them out of Egypt to give them Canaan and to be their God, the exodus grounding the poor-relief law. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

39 COMMENTARY: If a brother becomes poor and is sold to his neighbour, he shall not be compelled to serve as a bondservant, an Israelite in misfortune being treated not as a slave but as a hired servant with temporary engagement. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

40 COMMENTARY: But as a hired servant and as a sojourner shall he be with his master, and serve until the year of jubilee, the engagement ending at jubilee even without redemption. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

41 COMMENTARY: Then shall he depart from his master, he and his children with him, and return to his own family and to his fathers' possession, regaining liberty and inheritance together. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

42 COMMENTARY: For they are God's servants, whom He brought out of Egypt, they shall not be sold as bondmen, Israel's servitude to God precluding perpetual servitude to man. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

43 COMMENTARY: His master shall not rule over him with rigour, but fear God, the hired-servant treatment forbidding harsh rule. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

44 COMMENTARY: Bondmen and bondmaids may be had of the heathen round about, of them shall they buy bondmen and bondmaids, perpetual servitude being confined to foreigners, unlike Israelite hired service. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

45 COMMENTARY: Moreover of strangers sojourning among them, and of their families born in the land, may they buy, and they shall be their possession, sojourner families being purchasable as perpetual servants. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

46 COMMENTARY: They may take them as inheritance for children after them, to inherit as possession, serving forever, but over Israelite brethren they shall not rule with rigour, the distinction between foreign bondage and brotherly hired service being absolute. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

47 COMMENTARY: If a sojourner or stranger waxes rich, and a brother beside him waxes poor and sells himself to the stranger or to his stock, the poor Israelite may be sold even to a foreigner, and the jubilee redemption still applies. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

48 COMMENTARY: After he is sold, he may be redeemed again, one of his brethren may redeem him, a kinsman redeeming him at any time before jubilee. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

49 COMMENTARY: Either his uncle, his uncle's son, or any near kin of his family may redeem him, or, if he is able, he may redeem himself, the redemption circle extending through the family. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

50 COMMENTARY: He shall reckon with him who bought him from the year he was sold until the jubilee, and the price of his sale shall be according to the number of years, as the wages of a hired servant, the ransom being years remaining multiplied by current wages. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

51 COMMENTARY: If there are yet many years, according to them he shall give again the price of his redemption out of the money he was bought for, the equitable principle governing a long remaining term. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

52 COMMENTARY: If there remain but few years unto the jubilee, then he shall count with him, and according to his years shall he give again the price of his redemption, a short term costing proportionally less. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

53 COMMENTARY: And as a yearly hired servant shall he be with him, and the other shall not rule with rigour over him in their sight, even a foreign master being restrained from harsh rule over an Israelite. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

54 COMMENTARY: If he is not redeemed in these years, then he shall go out in the year of jubilee, he and his children with him, liberty coming by jubilee as a matter of course. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

55 COMMENTARY: For unto God the children of Israel are servants, they are His servants whom He brought out of Egypt, for He is the LORD their God, the jubilee's liberty resting on Israel's prior servitude to God alone. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
