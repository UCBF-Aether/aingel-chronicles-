Study Notes — Nehemiah

Chapter 3

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Eliashib the high priest, grandson of Jeshua and first high priest after the return, rose up with his fellow priests and built the sheep gate near the temple, where sheep were washed and taken for sacrifice. JFB notes they sanctified it as the first part repaired, so that in its sanctification the whole building was sanctified, and that the "tower of Meah" means a hundred cubits of wall reaching to the tower of Hananeel. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: Next to Eliashib built the men of Jericho, and next to them Zaccur son of Imri. JFB explains the chapter's method: the wall was divided into portions assigned to each great family that had returned, so building went on in all parts at once, each group working on the side nearest its own place of abode. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: Uzziel the goldsmith and Hananiah the apothecary repaired next, fortifying Jerusalem as far as the broad wall. JFB identifies this as the double wall from the gate of Ephraim to the corner gate, four hundred cubits long, broken down by Joash but rebuilt by Uzziah so strongly that the Chaldeans left it standing. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: Hanun and the inhabitants of Zanoah repaired the valley gate and a thousand cubits of wall to the dung gate. JFB notes there were two towns called Zanoah in the territory of Judah. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: Malchiah son of Rechab, ruler of part of Bethhaccerem, repaired the dung gate with its doors, locks, and bars. JFB places Beth-haccerem in Judah, supposed to be the hill town between Jerusalem and Tekoa. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

19 COMMENTARY: Ezer son of Jeshua, ruler of Mizpah, repaired another piece over against the going up to the armoury at the turning of the wall. JFB explains the turning as the wall across the Tyropoeon valley, continuing the first wall that connected Mount Zion with the temple wall. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

25 COMMENTARY: Palal son of Uzai worked over against the turning of the wall and the tower lying out from the king's high house, by the court of the prison. JFB identifies that tower as a watch-tower by the royal palace, standing in the palace fortification wall. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

26 COMMENTARY: The Nethinims dwelt in Ophel, by the water gate toward the east and the tower that lies out. JFB explains that Nethinims means those "given to the sanctuary," and that even the meanest persons belonging to God's house contributed to the work, their names commemorated because repairing the holy city was an act of piety and patriotism. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

No note for verse(s) 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 9, 10, 11, 12, 15, 16, 17, 18, 20, 21, 22, 23, 24, 27, 28, 29, 30, 31, 32 — the public-domain sources consulted give no distinct explanation for these verses beyond the list itself, so nothing was invented for them.
