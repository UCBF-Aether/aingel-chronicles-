Study Notes — Nehemiah

Chapter 1

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Nehemiah, son of Hachaliah, writes from Shushan the palace in the month Chisleu, which JFB places at the close of November and most of December. Henry notes his modesty: he first says only that he was in Shushan, holding back his high court preferment until the story requires it. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: Hanani, called his brother though JFB notes the term likely means only a kinsman, came with certain men of Judah, and Nehemiah asked after the escaped Jews and Jerusalem. Henry stresses that neither distance nor his own dignity cooled his concern for brethren in distress. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: The report was bleak: the remnant in the province lived in great affliction and reproach, the wall of Jerusalem was broken down, and the gates were burned with fire. Henry adds that the temple was built and reform begun, yet this one good work remained undone, leaving the people exposed and despised. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: On hearing this, Nehemiah sat down and wept, mourned for days, fasted, and prayed before the God of heaven. Henry reads this as consecrated sorrow: he poured out his complaint to God, easing his spirit and seeking direction before forming any plan to help his people. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: His prayer opens with holy awe and confidence: the LORD God of heaven is the great and terrible God, yet he keeps covenant and mercy for those who love him and observe his commandments. Henry notes the address matches Daniel's prayer, joining fear of God's majesty with trust in his promised grace. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: He asks God to let his ear be attentive and his eyes open to the prayer offered day and night, and confesses plainly, "I and my father's house have sinned." Henry observes that owning his own share, not merely Israel's, is what humbles the confession. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: The confession sharpens: they have dealt very corruptly against God and have not kept the commandments, statutes, and judgments commanded through Moses. Henry calls this the malignity of sin: a corruption of ourselves and an affront to God. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: He pleads the word God commanded Moses: if they were unfaithful, he would scatter them among the nations. Henry notes the scattering had indeed been fulfilled, with Israel widely dispersed, and Nehemiah now puts God in remembrance of his own settled rule. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: The other half of Moses' word is the hope: if they return and keep God's commandments, he will gather them even from the farthest part of heaven to the place he chose for his name. Henry calls promises like this our best pleas in prayer, the word on which God has caused us to hope. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: He pleads their relation to God: these are his servants and his people, redeemed by his great power and strong hand. Henry argues that the God whose power redeemed them of old has lost none of that power, and should perfect the redemption he began. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: The prayer closes with a particular petition: prosper Nehemiah this day and grant him mercy in the sight of "this man," the king. JFB notes the cupbearer's office was one of rank and great influence through frequent access to the royal presence, and Henry adds that favour with men is comfortable only when seen as springing from God's mercy. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
