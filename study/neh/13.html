Study Notes — Nehemiah

Chapter 13

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: On the day of the dedication reading, they found written in the book of Moses that the Ammonite and Moabite should not come into the congregation of God for ever. Henry notes the public reading of the word discovers sin and duty, and profits us when it moves us to separate from the evil we had practised. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: The reason was that they had not met Israel with bread and water, but hired Balaam against them to curse them, though God turned the curse into a blessing. Henry notes their injury was the greater because Israel had done them no harm and was expressly forbidden to harm them. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: When they heard the law, they separated from Israel all the mixed multitude. Henry observes the mixed multitude had of old been a snare, falling a lusting, and these inmates were expelled as usurpers and dangerous. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: Eliashib the priest, who had oversight of the chamber of God's house, was allied to Tobiah. Henry traces the base alliance: the high priest's grandson had married Sanballat's daughter, and Eliashib counted the alliance an honour, though it was his greatest disgrace and a contempt of his consecration. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: Eliashib prepared a great chamber for Tobiah where formerly lay the meat offerings, frankincense, vessels, and tithes of corn, wine, and oil for Levites, singers, and porters, with the priests' offerings. Henry calls it wretched: the temple stores were thrown out to make a state-room for an Ammonite in the courts of God's house, next to setting up an idol there. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: All this happened while Nehemiah was not at Jerusalem, for in the thirty-second year of Artaxerxes he had gone to the king, and after certain days obtained leave to return. Henry notes the enemy sows tares when the servants sleep or are absent, as the calf was made while Moses was on the mount. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: When Nehemiah came to Jerusalem and understood the evil Eliashib had done for Tobiah in preparing him a chamber in the courts of God's house, it grieved him sorely. Henry notes nothing grieves a good magistrate more than seeing the ministers of God's house do wicked things. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: He cast forth all Tobiah's household stuff out of the chamber. Henry compares this to Christ cleansing the temple: those who would expel sin from their hearts must throw out its household stuff and all provision made for it, starving it of food and fuel. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: He commanded the chambers to be cleansed, then brought back the vessels of God's house with the meat offering and frankincense. Henry notes the chambers, having been profaned, were first sprinkled with the water of purification, then furnished again for God's service. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: Nehemiah perceived the Levites' portions had not been given them, so the Levites and singers had fled each to his field. Henry suggests Tobiah, seizing the store-chambers with Eliashib's connivance, converted the stores, and notes a scandalous maintenance makes a scandalous ministry, since the work is neglected because the workmen are. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: He contended with the rulers, asking why the house of God was forsaken, then gathered the Levites and set them in their place. Henry notes both sides failed: the people forsook the Levites, forbidden by the law, and then the Levites forsook their post, while magistrates had not kept either to duty. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: All Judah brought the tithe of corn, new wine, and oil to the treasuries. Henry observes his zeal provoked theirs: when they saw the Levites at their work, they could not for shame withhold their wages, for the better church-work is done the better church-dues are paid. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: He appointed treasurers over the treasuries: Shelemiah the priest, Zadok the scribe, Pedaiah the Levite, and Hanan son of Zaccur son of Mattaniah, counted faithful, with the office of distributing to their brethren. Henry's rule: let men be tried first and then trusted, tried in the less and then trusted with more. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: He prayed, "Remember me, O my God, concerning this, and wipe not out my good deeds that I have done for the house of my God, and for the offices thereof." Henry notes he asked only to be remembered, not rewarded or published, yet God did more than he asked, recording his deeds. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: In Judah Nehemiah saw some treading winepresses on the sabbath, bringing in sheaves, loading asses with wine, grapes, figs, and all burdens into Jerusalem, and he testified against them in the day they sold victuals. Henry notes the law expressly required rest even in harvest time, when temptation to take liberty was greatest. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: Men of Tyre dwelling there brought fish and all manner of ware and sold them on the sabbath to the children of Judah in Jerusalem. Henry laments that Judah's children had so little grace as to buy, encouraging traders to make the Father's day a day of merchandise against the fourth commandment. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: Nehemiah contended with the nobles of Judah, asking what evil thing they did in profaning the sabbath day. Henry notes the nobles were guilty by connivance, bearing the sword in vain, and by bad example, since their own sabbath sports and idle visits led businessmen to profane it by worldly employment. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: He reasoned that their fathers had done likewise, and God had brought all this evil upon them and the city, yet they brought more wrath on Israel by profaning the sabbath. Henry notes sabbath-breaking was among the sins for which judgments had come, and returning to it invited further judgment. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: He commanded the gates to be shut when it began to be dark before the sabbath, not to be opened till after the sabbath, setting his own servants at the gates so no burden would be brought in on the sabbath day. Henry praises the method: aim at reformation first, using the bit and bridle so the lash is not needed. — Matthew Henry

20 COMMENTARY: The merchants and sellers of all kinds of ware lodged outside Jerusalem once or twice. Henry notes they were forced to lodge without the city, no doubt wishing the sabbath were gone so they might sell corn. — Matthew Henry

21 COMMENTARY: Nehemiah testified against them, asking why they lodged about the wall, and warning that if they did so again he would lay hands on them, after which they came no more on the sabbath. Henry's lesson: if reformers put on resolution, more may be done to break bad customs than they imagine, for vice connived at grows daring but cowers before magistrates who make themselves a terror to it. — Matthew Henry

22 COMMENTARY: He commanded the Levites to cleanse themselves, come and keep the gates, and sanctify the sabbath, praying God to remember him and spare him according to the greatness of his mercy. Henry notes magistrates and ministers joining forces is what makes reformation likely to last, and that the best saints still stand in need of sparing mercy. — Matthew Henry

23 COMMENTARY: Nehemiah saw Jews who had married wives of Ashdod, Ammon, and Moab. Henry notes the corruption had returned after Ezra's reform, since an unclean spirit re-enters when no watchful eye is kept, and men still lean toward what is forbidden, whether from fondness for the far-fetched or hope of strengthening alliances. — Matthew Henry

24 COMMENTARY: Their children spoke half in the speech of Ashdod and could not speak the Jews' language, but spoke according to the language of each people's mothers. Henry observes children learn much from their mothers and imitate them, which is why the corrupt side inclines them and why Christians should not be unequally yoked. — Matthew Henry

25 COMMENTARY: Nehemiah contended with them, cursed them, smote certain of them, plucked off their hair, and made them swear not to give their daughters to their sons nor take their daughters for their sons or themselves. Henry contrasts Ezra, who plucked his own hair in sorrow, with Nehemiah, who plucked theirs in holy indignation, showing the divers graces of the same Spirit. — Matthew Henry

26 COMMENTARY: He argued from Solomon: did not Solomon king of Israel sin by these things, though no king was like him for wisdom, though he was beloved of God and king over all Israel, yet outlandish women caused him to sin? Henry's warning: the falls of great and good men are recorded so we take heed lest we fall on the same precipice. — Matthew Henry

27 COMMENTARY: He asked whether they should hearken to doing all this great evil, transgressing against God by marrying strange wives. Henry notes he showed the sin in the glass of the commandment, quoting Deuteronomy's precept, and would not connive at an iniquity to be punished by the judge. — Matthew Henry

28 COMMENTARY: One of the sons of Joiada son of Eliashib the high priest was son-in-law to Sanballat the Horonite, so Nehemiah chased him away. Henry notes the young priest would not put away his wife, so he was deprived and degraded, and Josephus says he went to Sanballat, who built him a temple on Mount Gerizim. — Matthew Henry

29 COMMENTARY: Nehemiah prayed, "Remember them, O my God, because they have defiled the priesthood, and the covenant of the priesthood, and of the Levites." Henry reads the prayer two ways: that God would convince and convert them, or that he would reckon with them, since those who defile the priesthood despise God. — Matthew Henry

30 COMMENTARY: He cleansed them from all strangers and appointed the wards of the priests and Levites, each in his business. Henry notes it was no loss to part with the scandal of the cloth, and the work would be done better without him. — Matthew Henry

31 COMMENTARY: He also appointed the wood offering at appointed times and the firstfruits, closing with prayer: "Remember me, O my God, for good." Henry notes the best public services are sometimes forgotten by men, so Nehemiah took God for his paymaster, and this summary of petitions suffices for happiness. — Matthew Henry
