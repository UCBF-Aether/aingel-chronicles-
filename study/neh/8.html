Study Notes — Nehemiah

Chapter 8

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: All the people gathered as one man to the street before the water gate and asked Ezra the scribe to bring the book of the law of Moses. JFB notes the occasion was the feast of trumpets at the start of the seventh month, the civil new year, and that the people's strong desire to hear the word read showed a greatly improved religious feeling after the neglect of captivity. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: Ezra the priest brought the law before the congregation of men and women and all who could hear with understanding, on the first day of the seventh month. JFB notes Ezra had returned with Nehemiah in a subordinate capacity and had devoted himself to producing a complete edition of the Scriptures, reviving the public reading the law required. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: He read before the water gate from morning until midday, and the ears of all the people were attentive to the book. JFB stresses the labour of the reading, lightened by the priests who shared it, and the people's sustained attention as the mark of the occasion. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: Ezra stood on a pulpit of wood made for the purpose, with six men at his right hand and seven at his left. JFB explains the pulpit was a raised scaffold broad enough for fourteen to stand on, and that the priests' presence showed their agreement with the truth declared and their share in reading and expounding. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: When Ezra opened the book in sight of all the people, they all stood up. JFB notes they stood either from respect for God's word or because the reading was prefaced by solemn prayer, concluded by a general "Amen, Amen." — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: Ezra blessed the LORD, the great God, and all the people answered "Amen, Amen" with lifted hands, bowed their heads, and worshipped with their faces to the ground. JFB ties the standing posture and the Amen to the solemn prayer that opened the reading. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: Jeshua, Bani, Sherebiah, and the other Levites caused the people to understand the law, while the people stood in their place. JFB notes commentators differ on whether the Levites translated sentence by sentence into Chaldee, the exiles' vernacular, or explained what Ezra had read to a people grown ignorant. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: They read in the book of the law of God distinctly, gave the sense, and caused the people to understand the reading. JFB's discussion of this work of interpretation covers exactly this verse: the Levites either rendered the Hebrew into the spoken dialect or expounded its meaning. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: Nehemiah the Tirshatha, Ezra, and the teaching Levites told the weeping people that the day was holy to the LORD, so they should not mourn or weep. JFB explains their tears: the law's denunciations brought their national sins to remembrance, yet a sacred festival called for joy and thankfulness, not only sorrow. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: They were told to eat the fat, drink the sweet, and send portions to those for whom nothing was prepared, for the day was holy and the joy of the LORD was their strength. JFB notes that sharing portions let poorer brethren join the public rejoicing, as the law directed for festivals. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: The Levites stilled the people, saying to hold their peace, for the day was holy, and not to be grieved. JFB places this calming word inside the same comfort: penitential sorrow was not to swallow the joy proper to a sacred festival. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: The people went their way to eat, drink, send portions, and make great mirth, because they had understood the words declared to them. JFB ties the mirth to understanding: the joy followed the sense of the law being opened to them, not mere feasting. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: On the second day the chiefs of the fathers, priests, and Levites gathered to Ezra to understand the words of the law. JFB's account of the feast that follows shows this second-day study as the source of the discovery: they returned to the text to learn what it required. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: They found written in the law commanded by Moses that Israel should dwell in booths in the feast of the seventh month. JFB refers this observance to Leviticus 23 and Deuteronomy 16, the appointed feast of tabernacles now revived. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: They were to publish in all their cities and in Jerusalem that the people should go to the mount and fetch olive, pine, myrtle, palm, and thick-tree branches to make booths, as written. JFB treats this proclamation as the practical beginning of the feast-keeping described in the following verses. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: The people went out, brought branches, and made booths on rooftops, in courts, in the courts of God's house, and in the streets of the water gate and the gate of Ephraim. JFB points to Leviticus 23:34 and Deuteronomy 16:13 as the pattern they followed. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: The whole congregation of returned exiles made booths and sat under them, with very great gladness, as had not been done since the days of Jeshua son of Nun. JFB clarifies this does not mean the feast had been wholly neglected since then, but that popular joy had never risen so high since Israel's entrance into Canaan. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

18 COMMENTARY: Day by day, from the first to the last day, the book of the law of God was read, the feast was kept seven days, and on the eighth day there was a solemn assembly according to the manner. JFB notes this daily reading went beyond what was enjoined, arising from the exuberant zeal of the time, and that the eighth day was the last and great day of the feast. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
