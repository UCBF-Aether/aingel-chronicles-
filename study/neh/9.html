Study Notes — Nehemiah

Chapter 9

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: On the twenty-fourth day of the seventh month the children of Israel assembled fasting, in sackcloth, with earth on their heads. Henry notes this occasional fast, a fortnight after the feasts, was a day to afflict the soul and loose the bands of wickedness: outward humiliation that gave glory to God and stirred one another to repentance. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: The seed of Israel separated themselves from all strangers, then stood and confessed their sins and the iniquities of their fathers. Henry explains the separation: those who would join themselves to God by prayer and covenant must separate from sin and sinners, for light has no communion with darkness. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: A fourth part of the day they read in the book of the law, and another fourth part they confessed and worshipped the LORD. Henry observes the time was equally divided, three hours reading and three hours prayer, so the word furnished matter for prayer and prayer made the word profitable. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: Jeshua, Bani, Kadmiel, Shebaniah, Bunni, Sherebiah, Bani, and Chenani stood on the stairs of the Levites and cried with a loud voice to the LORD. Henry notes they prayed successively, crying aloud not so God might hear better but that the people might, to excite their fervency. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: The Levites called the people to stand up and bless the LORD their God for ever, for his glorious name is exalted above all blessing and praise. Henry notes even the angels' praises fall infinitely short of what God's name deserves, and our goodness extends not to him. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: The prayer adores God as Jehovah alone, maker of heaven, the heaven of heavens with all their host, the earth, the seas, and all in them, who preserves them all while the host of heaven worships him. Henry makes this the first article of praise: the only living and true God, Creator and great Protector of all. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: God chose Abram, brought him out of Ur of the Chaldees, and gave him the name Abraham. Henry reads this as distinguishing free grace: God chose him, brought him out, and honoured him as father of many nations. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: God found Abraham's heart faithful, made covenant to give his seed the land of the Canaanites and other nations, and performed his words, for he is righteous. Henry notes God found the heart faithful because he made it so, and wherever he finds a faithful heart he proves a faithful God. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: God saw Israel's affliction in Egypt and heard their cry by the Red Sea. Henry notes the deliverance was seasonable now that they sought the perfecting of their deliverance from Babylon, and came in compassion to their affliction and in answer to their cry. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: God showed signs and wonders upon Pharaoh, his servants, and his people, for he knew they dealt proudly, and so got himself a name. Henry notes that wherein the proud dealt proudly, God showed himself above them, and to this day his name is glorified for that work. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: God divided the sea before them so they passed through on dry land, and cast their persecutors into the deeps as a stone into mighty waters. Henry stresses the miracle: deliverance for Israel was destruction for their enemies, irrecoverable as a stone sunk in water. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: By day God led them with a pillar of cloud and by night with a pillar of fire, to give them light in the way they should go. Henry notes the pillar both directed every stage and step and stood as a visible token of God's guiding, guarding presence. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: God came down on Mount Sinai, spoke with them from heaven, and gave right judgments, true laws, and good statutes and commandments. Henry calls this the greatest favour of all: no nation had such laws, and even the ceremonial institutions were tokens of God's goodness and types of gospel grace. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: God made known his holy sabbath and commanded precepts, statutes, and laws by Moses his servant. Henry notes the sabbath was a token of particular favour, distinguishing Israel from nations that had lost that ancient part of revealed religion, and a means of keeping communion with God. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: God gave bread from heaven for their hunger and water out of the rock for their thirst, and promised they should go in and possess the land he had sworn to give. Henry notes they had food convenient on the way and the good land at journey's end. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: But their fathers dealt proudly, hardened their necks, and did not hearken to God's commandments. Henry traces the obstinacy to pride: they thought it below them to bow their necks to God's yoke and set up their own will against God's. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: They refused to obey and were not mindful of God's wonders, hardening their necks and appointing a captain to return to bondage, yet God, ready to pardon, gracious, merciful, slow to anger, and of great kindness, did not forsake them. Henry notes they heard God's word but did not hearken, and saw his works but did not consider them as miracles or mercies. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: They made a molten calf and said, "This is thy God, that brought thee up out of Egypt," working great provocations. Henry names the two wilderness returns: to Egyptian slavery for garlic and onions, and to Egyptian idolatry, sottishly calling a calf their deliverer. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: Yet in his manifold mercies God did not forsake them in the wilderness: the pillar of cloud did not depart by day nor the pillar of fire by night to show them the way. Henry stresses the doubling of thanks owed when mercies, though forfeited, are continued. — Matthew Henry

20 COMMENTARY: God gave his good Spirit to instruct them, did not withhold manna from their mouth, and gave them water for their thirst. Henry notes that besides Sinai, the books of Moses were constant instruction, and men like Bezaleel, Joshua, and Caleb were filled with God's Spirit. — Matthew Henry

21 COMMENTARY: Forty years God sustained them in the wilderness, so they lacked nothing, their clothes did not wear out, and their feet did not swell. Henry marvels that never was a people so long and so tenderly nursed, carried as on eagles' wings over a rough and tedious way. — Matthew Henry

22 COMMENTARY: God gave them kingdoms and nations, dividing them, so they possessed the land of Sihon king of Heshbon and Og king of Bashan. Henry counts this among the favours: they were made numerous enough to replenish the land and victorious enough to master it. — Matthew Henry

23 COMMENTARY: Their children were multiplied as the stars of heaven, and God brought them into the land he had promised their fathers they should possess. Henry notes the settlement was happy: strong cities, furnished houses, and a fat land all made ready to their hands. — Matthew Henry

24 COMMENTARY: The children went in and possessed the land, subduing the Canaanites and their kings and nations, given into their hands to do with them as they would. Henry observes they set their feet, if they pleased, on the necks of kings, gaining a settlement they could not have wished easier or happier. — Matthew Henry

25 COMMENTARY: They took strong cities and a fat land, houses full of goods, wells dug, vineyards, oliveyards, and fruit trees in abundance, ate, were filled, became fat, and delighted in God's great goodness. Henry notes they found everything prepared, and could not wish to be more easy or happy had it not been their own fault. — Matthew Henry

26 COMMENTARY: Nevertheless they were disobedient and rebelled, cast God's law behind their backs, slew his prophets who testified to turn them to him, and wrought great provocations. Henry calls this returning the greatest injury for the greatest kindness, abusing both God's prophets and his favours. — Matthew Henry

27 COMMENTARY: Therefore God gave them into the hand of enemies who vexed them, but in their trouble they cried to him, and he heard from heaven and, according to his manifold mercies, gave them saviours who saved them. Henry notes these deliverances came not for any merit but purely according to God's mercies. — Matthew Henry

28 COMMENTARY: After they had rest they did evil again, so God left them in the hand of their enemies, but when they returned and cried, he heard from heaven and delivered them many times according to his mercies. Henry observes neither troubles nor deliverances held them to duty; neither fear nor love sufficed. — Matthew Henry

29 COMMENTARY: God testified against them to bring them again to his law, but they dealt proudly, did not hearken, sinned against his judgments, in which a man shall live if he does them, withdrew the shoulder, hardened their necks, and would not hear. Henry notes the design of all prophetic testimony was to lay their necks under the law's yoke and walk by its rule. — Matthew Henry

30 COMMENTARY: Many years God forbore them and testified against them by his Spirit in his prophets, yet they would not give ear, so he gave them into the hand of the peoples of the lands. Henry notes God's patience lengthened out as he waited to see if they would repent, and that refusing the Spirit's teaching leaves his word to accuse and judge. — Matthew Henry

31 COMMENTARY: Yet for his great mercies' sake God did not utterly consume them nor forsake them, for he is a gracious and merciful God. Henry explains: had he forsaken them they would have been consumed, but he stirred not up all his wrath, designing their reformation, not their destruction. — Matthew Henry

32 COMMENTARY: The prayer turns to petition, its only request: let not all the trouble seem little before God, great, mighty, and terrible, who keeps covenant and mercy, trouble that has lain on kings, princes, priests, prophets, fathers, and all the people since the kings of Assyria until this day. Henry notes they prescribe nothing to God, only asking him to take cognizance of it, as he did in Egypt. — Matthew Henry

33 COMMENTARY: They own God is just in all that is brought upon them, for he has done right, but they have done wickedly. Henry's rule for sharp and long rebukes: justify God and judge ourselves, for he will be clear when he judges. — Matthew Henry

34 COMMENTARY: Their kings, princes, priests, and fathers did not keep God's law nor hearken to his commandments and testimonies. Henry charges two contempts as the cause of their troubles: contempt of the good law and contempt of the good land God gave them. — Matthew Henry

35 COMMENTARY: Their kings did not serve God in their kingdom, nor did the people serve him in the use of his great goodness and the large, fat land set before them, nor turn from their wicked works. Henry notes the irony: those who would not serve God in their own land were made to serve their enemies in a strange land. — Matthew Henry

36 COMMENTARY: Now they are servants in the very land given to their fathers to eat its fruit and good, behold, servants in it. Henry calls it a sad change: free-born Israelites holding by as base a tenure as villenage under the kings of Persia, a work of sin. — Matthew Henry

37 COMMENTARY: The land yields much increase to the kings set over them because of their sins, who have dominion over their bodies and cattle at their pleasure, leaving them in great distress. Henry notes the land-tax amounted to a rack-rent, so that rents and profits accrued to the king and bare subsistence remained. — Matthew Henry

38 COMMENTARY: Because of all this, they make a sure covenant and write it, and their princes, Levites, and priests seal it. Henry notes the covenant was made with serious consideration, great solemnity, joint consent, and fixed resolution, without reserving a power of revocation. — Matthew Henry
