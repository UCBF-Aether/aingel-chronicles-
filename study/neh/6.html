Study Notes — Nehemiah

Chapter 6

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: When Sanballat, Tobiah, Geshem, and the other enemies heard the wall was built with no breach left, though the doors were not yet hung on the gates, they knew they must act now or never. Henry notes they could not attack the well-guarded Nehemiah directly, so they turned to wheedling. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: They sent word to meet together in one of the villages in the plain of Ono, pretending to consult about common interests, but intending mischief. Henry observes the subtlety: a village, not a city, lest suspicion arise that they meant to seize him. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: Nehemiah answered by messengers: "I am doing a great work, so that I cannot come down; why should the work cease, whilst I leave it, and come down to you?" Henry's rule: compliment must always give way to business, and idle meetings must not pull us from work we must not neglect. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: They sent the same solicitation four times, and he answered after the same manner each time. Henry notes we must never be overcome by importunity to do anything sinful or imprudent, but resist the same temptation with the same reason and resolution. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: Then Sanballat sent his servant a fifth time with an open letter in his hand. Henry notes the open letter was meant to suggest the charge was generally known and talked of, not a private whisper. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: The letter claimed it was reported among the nations, and Gashmu would aver it, that Nehemiah and the Jews meant to rebel, that he built the wall to be their king, and had appointed prophets to preach a king in Judah. Henry calls this the old trick: representing the malice of a few as the sense of the many. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: The letter closed by urging Nehemiah to come and take counsel together to quell the report. Henry exposes the lure: words softer than oil with war in the heart, hoping either to draw him off his work or to frighten him into staying his proceedings. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: Nehemiah sent back that no such things were done as claimed; Sanballat feigned them out of his own heart. Henry notes he denied not only that the charges were true but that they were even reported, and trusted God with his good name rather than omitting known duty for fear of misconstruction. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: He saw their aim: to make them afraid and weaken their hands so the work would not be done. His answer was prayer: "Now therefore, O God, strengthen my hands." Henry calls this the great relief of good people in every strait: to fetch grace by faith and prayer when enemies fill them with fears. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: He then visited Shemaiah son of Delaiah, who was shut up in his house and proposed they meet in the temple, shut its doors, for enemies would come by night to slay Nehemiah. Henry notes the plausible pretence: a strong, sacred refuge, with concern for his safety as its cover. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: Nehemiah refused: "Should such a man as I flee? and who is there, that, being as I am, would go into the temple to save his life? I will not go in." Henry's lesson: when tempted, remember who and what you are, and let holy courage refuse both to decline good work and to do a bad thing. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: He perceived God had not sent Shemaiah, who pronounced this prophecy against him because Tobiah and Sanballat had hired him. Henry notes the wickedness of mercenary prophets who betray God's cause under a pretence of communion with God. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: The hireling's purpose was that Nehemiah should be afraid, do so, and sin, giving his enemies matter for an evil report to reproach him. Henry observes the devil's own pattern: tempt men to sin so as to accuse them and glory in their shame. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: Nehemiah committed the cause to God: think upon Tobiah and Sanballat according to their works, and upon the prophetess Noadiah and the other prophets who would put him in fear. Henry notes he prescribed no revenge, but referred the matter to the Judge who knows hearts. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: The wall was finished on the twenty-fifth day of Elul, in fifty-two days. Henry marvels at how much work earnest, cheerful hands can do in a little time, noting the threats meant to weaken them only quickened them to finish before the enemy came. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: When the enemies and surrounding heathen heard, they were much cast down in their own eyes, for they perceived the work was wrought of God. Henry notes even heathens could see a special providence in the church's prosperity, and conclude that what is of God will prevail. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: Even then, nobles of Judah kept up a treacherous correspondence, sending many letters to Tobiah and receiving his. Henry laments that great men are not always wise or honest, and that nobles of Judah could do so mean a thing. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: Many in Judah were sworn to Tobiah because he was son-in-law to Shechaniah son of Arah, and his son Jehohanan had married the daughter of Meshullam son of Berechiah. Henry warns of the mischief of marrying strangers: a sinful love leads to a sinful league. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: They reported Tobiah's good deeds before Nehemiah and carried Nehemiah's words to him, while Tobiah sent letters to put Nehemiah in fear. Henry notes the double treachery: praising the wicked to his face and betraying his counsels behind his back. — Matthew Henry
