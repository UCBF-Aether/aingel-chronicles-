Study Notes — Nehemiah

Chapter 2

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: In Nisan, nearly four months after Chisleu, Nehemiah took up the wine and gave it to the king, having waited for a fitting moment to press his cause. Henry stresses that his prayers were seconded by serious endeavour: he did not sit still and expect God to do the work alone. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: The king noticed his sad countenance, though he was not sick, and asked why. Henry draws a lesson in sympathy: masters should concern themselves with their servants' sorrows, and sorrow for the church's calamities may well sadden a face even without sickness. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: Nehemiah answered with meekness and fear, "Let the king live for ever," then asked why he should not be sad when the city of his fathers' sepulchres lay waste and its gates were consumed with fire. Henry notes he could give a good reason for his grief, appealing even to the king himself. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: Asked what he requested, Nehemiah first prayed silently to the God of heaven for wisdom and favour. Henry calls this a secret, sudden ejaculation: those who would find favour with kings must first secure the favour of the King of kings. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: He then petitioned modestly but explicitly: if he had found favour, let the king send him to Judah, to the city of his fathers' sepulchres, to build it. Henry reads this as a request for a governor's commission to rebuild Jerusalem's wall, for a set time. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: The king, with the queen sitting by, asked how long the journey would be and when he would return, then granted leave and set a time. Henry sees God's hand here: Nehemiah gained his point not by merit or management but according to the good hand of his God upon him. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: Nehemiah further asked for letters to the governors beyond the river, that they would convey him safely and supply what he needed. Henry notes he asked not merely for passage but for provision from each province he crossed. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: He also asked for a letter to Asaph, keeper of the king's forest, for timber for the gates of the palace by the temple, for the city wall, and for his own house. Henry observes the king let him have whatever clauses he pleased inserted in his commission. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: Coming to the governors beyond the river, Nehemiah delivered the king's letters, escorted by captains of the army and horsemen. Henry reads the escort as both guard and honour: those whom the King of kings sends, he protects and dignifies. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: When Sanballat the Horonite and Tobiah the Ammonite heard of his coming, they were exceedingly grieved that a man had come to seek the welfare of Israel. Henry notes their vexation: their arts to weaken Israel were baffled by a generous royal project to strengthen it. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: Nehemiah came to Jerusalem and stayed three days, apparently unnoticed by the city's great men. Henry admires the humility: he came with great blessings yet without show or noise, lying hid until his work began. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: He arose in the night with a few men, telling no one what God had put in his heart to do, with no beast except the one he rode. Henry notes he would first see the ruins with his own eyes, quietly, before proposing anything. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: By night he went out by the valley gate, past the dragon well to the dung port, viewing the broken walls of Jerusalem. Henry's rule: good work is likely well done when first well considered, and those who would build the church's walls must first notice their ruins. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: He went on to the fountain gate and the king's pool, but there was no place for his beast to pass. Henry treats this night survey as careful preparation: he examined what remained of the old foundations and materials before choosing his method. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: He then went up by the brook in the night, viewed the wall, turned back, and entered again by the valley gate. Henry notes the wisdom of proceeding on his own inspection rather than on reports alone, and of doing it unobserved. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: The rulers did not know where he had gone or what he did, for he had told neither Jews, priests, nobles, nor rulers. Henry explains the silence: he would not trumpet his purpose before he knew it was practicable, and upright men do not sound a trumpet before their good offices. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: At last he told them plainly: they saw the distress, Jerusalem lying waste with gates burned by fire, so let them rise and build the wall to end their reproach. Henry notes he did not command imperiously despite his commission, but exhorted them as a brother. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: He told them of the good hand of his God upon him and of the king's words, and they answered, "Let us rise up and build," strengthening their hands for the good work. Henry observes that many a good work only waits for one good head to lead in it. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: Sanballat, Tobiah, and Geshem the Arabian laughed them to scorn, despised them, and charged rebellion against the king. Henry notes the double slander: the work was mocked as foolish and condemned as treason, though it stood under the king's own commission. — Matthew Henry

20 COMMENTARY: Nehemiah answered that the God of heaven would prosper them, so his servants would arise and build, but their enemies had no portion, right, or memorial in Jerusalem. Henry's ground: they served the only true God, and their maligners envied a right they did not share. — Matthew Henry
