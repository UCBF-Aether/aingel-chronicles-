Study Notes — Mark

Chapter 11

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: When they came near to Jerusalem, to Bethphage and Bethany, at the Mount of Olives, he sent forth two of his disciples. The entry begins from the Mount of Olives, the place of prophecy, and two disciples are sent as his agents. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: He said, Go into the village over against you, and as soon as you enter, you shall find a colt tied, whereon never man sat; loose him and bring him. The colt, never ridden, is fitted for a king's use, and Christ's knowledge of it proves his foresight in small things. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: If any man says to you, Why do you this? Say, The Lord has need of him, and straightway he will send him here. 'The Lord has need' is both reason and warrant: the owner's right yields to the Lord's need, and the promise secures consent. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: They went their way and found the colt tied by the door without in a place where two ways met, and they loosed him. The finding matched the word exactly, down to the door and the meeting of ways, confirming the disciples' trust. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: Certain of those standing there said, What do you, loosing the colt? The bystanders' challenge was reasonable, for loosing another's colt without leave needed explanation. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: They said to them even as Jesus had commanded, and they let them go. The Lord's word, repeated exactly, satisfied the owners, and obedience to a command is the best answer to a challenge. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: They brought the colt to Jesus, cast their garments on him, and he sat upon him. Garments for a saddle show the disciples' homage, and a king riding a colt fulfils Zechariah's prophecy of a lowly king. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: Many spread their garments in the way, and others cut down branches off the trees and strewed them in the way. Garments and branches were the crowd's carpet, the poor man's tribute to a king without a chariot. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: They that went before and they that followed cried, Hosanna; Blessed is he that comes in the name of the Lord. Before and behind, the procession cried Hosanna, save now, and blessed the comer in the Lord's name. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: Blessed be the kingdom of our father David, that comes in the name of the Lord: Hosanna in the highest. The kingdom of David was their hope, and Hosanna in the highest asked heaven itself to join the praise. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: Jesus entered into Jerusalem and into the temple, and when he had looked round about upon all things, and now the evening was come, he went out to Bethany with the twelve. The temple, not a palace, was his first visit, and his survey of all things preceded the cleansing, as a judge first views the case. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: On the morrow, when they came from Bethany, he was hungry. Hunger on the morning road sets up the fig tree, for Christ's true humanity felt need as men do. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: Seeing a fig tree afar off having leaves, he came, if perhaps he might find anything thereon, and when he came, he found nothing but leaves, for the time of figs was not yet. Leaves promised fruit and gave none, and the tree's season is noted lest the curse seem unjust: it is judged for profession without fruit. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: Jesus answered and said to it, No man eat fruit of you hereafter for ever, and his disciples heard it. The curse answers the tree's profession, and the disciples heard it, that the lesson might be theirs as well as Israel's. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: They came to Jerusalem, and Jesus went into the temple, and began to cast out those that sold and bought in the temple, and overthrew the tables of the moneychangers and the seats of those who sold doves. Buying, selling, changing, and dove-selling filled God's house with trade, and Christ cast them out with authority, overthrowing the furniture of the market. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: He would not suffer that any man should carry any vessel through the temple. Even carrying a vessel through the courts as a shortcut profaned the house, and Christ barred the temple from becoming a thoroughfare. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: He taught, saying, Is it not written, My house shall be called of all nations the house of prayer? But you have made it a den of thieves. The house for all nations' prayer had become a den of thieves, for traders' gain and priests' collusion robbed worshippers and God together. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

18 COMMENTARY: The scribes and chief priests heard it and sought how they might destroy him, for they feared him, because all the people were astonished at his doctrine. Fear of the astonished people restrained them, but their counsel was already murder, and doctrine that moves crowds moves rulers to destroy. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

19 COMMENTARY: When evening came, he went out of the city. Evening withdrew him to Bethany again, for Jerusalem at night was no safe lodging for its judge. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

20 COMMENTARY: In the morning, as they passed by, they saw the fig tree dried up from the roots. Dried from the roots, not merely withered in leaf, the tree shows judgment that reaches the source, and morning light revealed what the curse had done overnight. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

21 COMMENTARY: Peter calling to remembrance said, Master, behold, the fig tree which you cursed is withered away. Peter's remembrance links the curse to the sight, and his report invites the lesson Christ now gives. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

22 COMMENTARY: Jesus answering said to them, Have faith in God. The lesson of the fig tree is not cursing but faith: trust in God is the power that withers obstacles and bears fruit. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

23 COMMENTARY: For verily, whoever shall say to this mountain, Be removed and cast into the sea, and shall not doubt in his heart, but shall believe that those things which he says shall come to pass, he shall have whatever he says. Mountain-moving faith is faith without doubt, trusting God's promise rather than the mountain's size, and it receives what it asks in God's will. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

24 COMMENTARY: Therefore, whatever things you desire, when you pray, believe that you receive them, and you shall have them. Prayer joined to believing reception is the rule: believe that you receive, and you shall have, for faith is the hand that takes the gift. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

25 COMMENTARY: When you stand praying, forgive, if you have anything against any, that your Father in heaven may forgive you your trespasses. Forgiveness is the condition of prayer: an unforgiving heart cannot expect a forgiving Father, and standing to pray is the time to release debts. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

26 COMMENTARY: But if you do not forgive, neither will your Father in heaven forgive your trespasses. The warning is plain and mutual: forgiveness withheld from men withholds forgiveness from God, for grace received must be grace given. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

27 COMMENTARY: They came again to Jerusalem, and as he was walking in the temple, there came to him the chief priests, scribes, and elders. The temple walk was interrupted by the whole Sanhedrin in deputation, come to challenge the cleansing. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

28 COMMENTARY: They said to him, By what authority do you these things? And who gave you this authority to do these things? Authority was the question, for they denied any authority but their own, and demanded his warrant for judging the temple. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

29 COMMENTARY: Jesus answered, I will also ask of you one question, and answer me, and I will tell you by what authority I do these things. One question answers theirs, for their answer about John will decide their judgment about Christ. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

30 COMMENTARY: The baptism of John, was it from heaven, or of men? Answer me. John's baptism is the test case: if from heaven, Christ's authority follows, for John witnessed to him; if of men, they must face the people who counted John a prophet. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

31 COMMENTARY: They reasoned with themselves, saying, If we shall say, From heaven, he will say, Why then did you not believe him? Their reasoning was policy, not truth: they weighed consequences, not evidence, and feared the question's logic. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

32 COMMENTARY: But if we shall say, Of men, they feared the people, for all men counted John that he was a prophet indeed. Fear of the people barred the false answer, for the crowd's faith in John was too strong to contradict. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

33 COMMENTARY: They answered Jesus, We cannot tell, and Jesus answered, Neither do I tell you by what authority I do these things. 'We cannot tell' was a lie of policy, and Christ refuses his answer to men who refuse the truth they know, for wilful ignorance forfeits further light. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
