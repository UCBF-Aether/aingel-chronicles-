Study Notes — Mark

Chapter 5

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: They came over to the other side of the sea, into the country of the Gadarenes. Christ crossed a storm to reach one miserable man on a Gentile shore, showing his errand was not bounded by Israel. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: When he came out of the ship, immediately there met him out of the tombs a man with an unclean spirit. The possessed man met Christ at once, as if his misery sought its only physician. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: He had his dwelling among the tombs, and no man could bind him, not even with chains. Tombs were his home because death and uncleanness suited his tormentor, and human bonds were useless against him. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: He had often been bound with fetters and chains, and the chains had been plucked asunder and the fetters broken in pieces, and no man could tame him. Repeated restraint only proved his strength, and man's taming power had wholly failed. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: Always, night and day, he was in the mountains and in the tombs, crying and cutting himself with stones. His misery never rested, and the devil that possessed him made him his own tormentor. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: But when he saw Jesus afar off, he ran and worshipped him. Even at a distance the unclean spirit knew its master, and ran to him it could not flee from. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: He cried with a loud voice, What have I to do with you, Jesus, Son of the most high God? I adjure you by God, that you torment me not. The devil confesses Christ's sonship yet dreads his judgment, desiring only to be left in his rebellion. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: For Jesus had said to him, Come out of the man, you unclean spirit. The command came before the cry, and the devil's bargaining was already answered by authority. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: Jesus asked him, What is your name? He answered, My name is Legion, for we are many. The question exposes the multitude of the enemy, that the greatness of the deliverance may be known. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: He besought him much that he would not send them away out of the country. Devils cling to their haunts and beg for any leave rather than face their doom. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: Now there was there near the mountains a great herd of swine feeding. Swine, unclean by law, were fit company for unclean spirits, and their presence sets up the next act. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: All the devils besought him, saying, Send us into the swine, that we may enter into them. They preferred any body to none, and their request shows they cannot move without Christ's leave. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: Forthwith Jesus gave them leave, and the unclean spirits went out and entered the swine, and the herd ran violently down a steep place into the sea, about two thousand, and were choked. Christ permitted the loss to prove the reality of the possession and the devils' destructiveness, which would have destroyed the man as they destroyed the swine. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: The swineherds fled and told it in the city and in the country, and they went out to see what was done. Bad news travels fast, and the whole country came to judge a miracle by its cost. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: They came to Jesus and saw him that was possessed and had the legion, sitting, clothed, and in his right mind, and they were afraid. Sitting, clothed, and sane are the three marks of deliverance, yet the sight bred fear rather than faith. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: Those who saw it told them how it befell the possessed man, and also concerning the swine. The witnesses reported both the mercy and the loss, and the hearers weighed them together. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: They began to pray him to depart out of their coasts. Men who prized their swine above a soul asked Christ to leave, choosing their goods over their Saviour. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

18 COMMENTARY: When he came into the ship, he that had been possessed prayed him that he might be with him. Gratitude desired constant company with Christ, the natural wish of a delivered soul. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

19 COMMENTARY: Jesus did not suffer him, but said, Go home to your friends, and tell them how great things the Lord has done for you, and has had compassion on you. Home is the first pulpit of a convert, and his own story, told to those who knew his misery, is the fittest testimony. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

20 COMMENTARY: He departed and began to publish in Decapolis how great things Jesus had done for him, and all men marvelled. Obedience turned one man's deliverance into a ten-city proclamation, and wonder prepared the ground for later preaching. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

21 COMMENTARY: When Jesus passed over again by ship to the other side, much people gathered to him, and he was near the sea. His return found a waiting crowd, for his fame had gone before him. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

22 COMMENTARY: Behold, there came one of the rulers of the synagogue, Jairus by name, and when he saw him, he fell at his feet. A ruler's public falling at Christ's feet shows affliction breaking through rank and pride. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

23 COMMENTARY: He besought him greatly, saying, My little daughter lies at the point of death: come and lay your hands on her, that she may be healed, and she shall live. The father's prayer joins urgency with faith, asking Christ's presence and touch for a dying child. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

24 COMMENTARY: Jesus went with him, and much people followed him and thronged him. Christ went at once to the house of grief, and the crowd thronged a journey meant for one family. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

25 COMMENTARY: A certain woman, who had an issue of blood twelve years, pressed into that throng. Twelve years of uncleanness and isolation frame a suffering that human help had only lengthened. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

26 COMMENTARY: She had suffered many things of many physicians, spent all she had, and was nothing bettered, but rather grew worse. Earthly physicians had drained her purse and left her disease, a picture of all cures that fail before Christ. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

27 COMMENTARY: When she heard of Jesus, she came in the press behind and touched his garment. Hearing bred faith, and faith, though timid, pressed through a crowd to reach him from behind. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

28 COMMENTARY: For she said, If I may touch but his clothes, I shall be whole. Her faith fixed on even the hem of his garment, trusting that virtue went with Christ beyond his hands. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

29 COMMENTARY: Straightway the fountain of her blood was dried up, and she felt in her body that she was healed of that plague. The cure was immediate and sensible: she felt what faith had received. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

30 COMMENTARY: Jesus, immediately knowing in himself that virtue had gone out of him, turned about in the press and said, Who touched my clothes? He knew a healing touch from a thronging touch, and would not let a secret cure stay secret. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

31 COMMENTARY: His disciples said, You see the multitude thronging you, and say, Who touched me? They mistook the crowd's pressure for the woman's touch, and could not distinguish curiosity from faith. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

32 COMMENTARY: He looked round about to see her that had done this thing. His searching look called the timid believer out, that fear might turn to confession. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

33 COMMENTARY: The woman fearing and trembling, knowing what was done in her, came and fell down before him and told him all the truth. Fear and trembling gave way to full confession, and Christ drew out the whole story that her faith might be owned. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

34 COMMENTARY: He said to her, Daughter, your faith has made you whole; go in peace, and be whole of your plague. He names her daughter, credits her faith, and sends her away with peace added to health. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

35 COMMENTARY: While he yet spoke, there came from the ruler's house certain who said, Your daughter is dead; why trouble the Master any further? Bad news arrived mid-miracle, and human reason pronounced the case past help. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

36 COMMENTARY: As soon as Jesus heard the word spoken, he said to the ruler, Be not afraid, only believe. Christ answers a message of despair with two commands: fear must go, and faith must stay. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

37 COMMENTARY: He suffered no man to follow him, save Peter, James, and John the brother of James. Three chosen witnesses were enough for a private resurrection, and the crowd was left outside. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

38 COMMENTARY: He came to the ruler's house and saw the tumult, and those who wept and wailed greatly. Professional mourning had already filled the house, turning grief into noise before Christ entered. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

39 COMMENTARY: When he came in, he said, Why make this ado and weep? The damsel is not dead, but sleeps. Death in Christ's presence is but sleep, for he who will wake her speaks of her as sleeping. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

40 COMMENTARY: They laughed him to scorn, but when he had put them all out, he took the father and mother of the damsel and those with him, and entered where the damsel lay. Scorners were shut out, and only faith's witnesses were admitted to see the work. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

41 COMMENTARY: He took the damsel by the hand and said, Talitha cumi, which is, Damsel, I say to you, arise. His word in her own tongue, joined to his hand, commanded life back into death. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

42 COMMENTARY: Straightway the damsel arose and walked, for she was twelve years old, and they were astonished with a great astonishment. Rising and walking proved a complete return to life, not a mere sign, and astonishment was the fitting response. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

43 COMMENTARY: He charged them straitly that no man should know it, and commanded that something should be given her to eat. Secrecy guarded against a stir at the wrong time, and food proved she lived indeed and needed ordinary care. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
