Study Notes — Mark

Chapter 2

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Again Jesus entered Capernaum, and it was noised that he was in the house. His return to a known house drew crowds as surely as his miracles did. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: Straightway many gathered, so there was no room, not even about the door, and he preached the word to them. Christ used the crowd's attention for teaching, not only for healing. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: They brought to him one sick of the palsy, borne of four. Four bearers sharing one burden show how faith works by the help of friends. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: Unable to come near for the press, they uncovered the roof and let down the bed where the palsied man lay. Faith finds a way through obstacles and will break through roofs rather than miss Christ. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: When Jesus saw their faith, he said to the sick man, Son, thy sins be forgiven thee. He first heals the soul's disease, which is the root of misery, before healing the body. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: Certain scribes sat there reasoning in their hearts against him. Silent inward reasoning does not escape Christ, and unbelief often hides behind inward objection. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: They reasoned, why does this man speak blasphemies, for who can forgive sins but God only? Their premise was true, but they refused the conclusion it pointed to: that this man was God. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: Jesus immediately perceived in his spirit what they reasoned within themselves and asked why they reasoned so in their hearts. He proves his divine knowledge by reading thoughts, and answers objections before they are spoken. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: He asked whether it is easier to say, thy sins be forgiven, or, arise, take up thy bed, and walk. Forgiveness is unseen, so he will prove it by a visible work that only God can do. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: That they might know the Son of man has power on earth to forgive sins, he turned to the palsied man. The miracle is given as evidence, not as a display: it seals the pardon. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: He said, arise, take up thy bed, and go into thine house. Strength returns with the command, and the bed that carried him becomes the proof he carries home. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: Immediately he arose, took up his bed, and went forth before them all, so that all were amazed and glorified God, saying, we never saw it on this fashion. A cure so public and complete left even onlookers glorifying God. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: He went forth again by the sea side, and all the multitude resorted to him, and he taught them. Christ sought the open places where crowds could hear, and teaching remained his work. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: Passing by, he saw Levi son of Alphaeus sitting at the receipt of custom, and said, Follow me, and he arose and followed him. Grace calls men in the very place of their worldly business, and Levi's rise was as ready as the fishermen's. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: As Jesus sat at meat in his house, many publicans and sinners sat with him and his disciples, for there were many who followed him. Christ's table fellowship with outcasts was evangelism, not approval of their sins. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: The scribes and Pharisees asked his disciples how he could eat and drink with publicans and sinners. Their complaint was aimed at the disciples because they dared not yet attack Christ directly. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: Jesus answered, the whole have no need of the physician, but the sick: I came not to call the righteous, but sinners to repentance. He came as a physician to the diseased, and self-righteous men who feel no sickness refuse the cure. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

18 COMMENTARY: The disciples of John and of the Pharisees fasted, and people asked why Jesus' disciples did not fast. The question confuses the discipline of one season with the duty of another. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

19 COMMENTARY: Jesus answered, can the children of the bridechamber fast while the bridegroom is with them? Fasting suits mourning, not a wedding; Christ's presence was a feast to his disciples. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

20 COMMENTARY: But days will come when the bridegroom is taken away, and then they shall fast in those days. Mourning will have its season after his departure, and fasting then will be fitting. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

21 COMMENTARY: No man sews new cloth on an old garment, else the new piece takes away from the old and the rent is made worse. New gospel duties cannot be patched onto old ceremonial frames without tearing both. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

22 COMMENTARY: No man puts new wine into old bottles, else the wine bursts the bottles and is spilled; new wine must go into new bottles. The gospel's living power needs vessels fitted to it, not rigid old forms that cannot hold it. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

23 COMMENTARY: He went through the corn fields on the sabbath, and his disciples began to pluck ears of corn as they went. Hunger on a journey, not harvest work, explains their act. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

24 COMMENTARY: The Pharisees said, behold, why do they on the sabbath what is not lawful? They turned a work of necessity into a charge of sabbath-breaking by their traditions. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

25 COMMENTARY: Jesus asked, have you never read what David did when he had need and was hungry, he and those with him? Scripture itself supplies the precedent that necessity overrides ceremony. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

26 COMMENTARY: David went into the house of God in the days of Abiathar the high priest, ate the shewbread, lawful only for priests, and gave it to those with him. God himself accepted David's necessity, and the Pharisees' rule condemned what God had allowed. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

27 COMMENTARY: He said, the sabbath was made for man, and not man for the sabbath. The sabbath is a gift for man's rest and worship, not a yoke to starve him. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

28 COMMENTARY: Therefore the Son of man is Lord also of the sabbath. As Lord of the institution, Christ interprets its true use and frees it from the elders' additions. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
