Study Notes — Mark

Chapter 15

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Straightway in the morning the chief priests held a consultation with the elders, scribes, and the whole council, bound Jesus, carried him away, and delivered him to Pilate. Morning consultation bound him for Pilate, for the council, having condemned him, needed Roman power to kill him. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: Pilate asked him, Are you the King of the Jews? And he answering said, You say it. Pilate's question names the political charge, and 'You say it' affirms it while leaving Pilate to weigh what kind of king he is. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: The chief priests accused him of many things, but he answered nothing. Many accusations met silence, for Christ would not answer charges that answered themselves by their multitude and malice. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: Pilate asked him again, Answer you nothing? Behold how many things they witness against you. Pilate, astonished at silence, pressed him to answer the many witnesses, as if number made a case. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: But Jesus yet answered nothing, so that Pilate marvelled. Continued silence marvelled Pilate, for innocence that will not defend itself is rarer than guilt that pleads. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: Now at that feast he released to them one prisoner, whomever they desired. The feast custom of releasing a prisoner gave the crowd a choice, and Pilate hoped it would free Jesus. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: There was one named Barabbas, who lay bound with those who had made insurrection with him, who had committed murder in the insurrection. Barabbas, bound for insurrection and murder, was the alternative to Christ, a true criminal set against an innocent king. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: The multitude crying aloud began to desire him to do as he had ever done to them. The crowd, crying aloud, demanded the custom, and their demand became the priests' opportunity to steer the choice. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: Pilate answered, Will you that I release to you the King of the Jews? Pilate named Jesus King of the Jews, hoping the title would win the crowd, but the title itself became the priests' weapon. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: For he knew that the chief priests had delivered him for envy. Pilate knew envy, not justice, delivered Jesus, for the priests envied his following, and a judge who knows the motive yet yields is himself condemned. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: But the chief priests moved the people, that he should rather release Barabbas to them. The priests moved the people to choose Barabbas, for a stirred crowd will choose a murderer over a reprover. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: Pilate answered again, What will you then that I shall do to him whom you call the King of the Jews? Pilate's question, asking the crowd to sentence its king, abdicated judgment to the mob he should have restrained. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: They cried out again, Crucify him. The crowd's cry, repeated, demanded crucifixion, the Roman death for slaves and rebels, for the king they would not have. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: Then Pilate said, Why, what evil has he done? And they cried out the more exceedingly, Crucify him. Pilate's question about evil met a louder cry, for a mob answered with volume what it could not answer with reason. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: So Pilate, willing to content the people, released Barabbas to them, and delivered Jesus, when he had scourged him, to be crucified. Willing to content the people, Pilate released the murderer and scourged the innocent for crucifixion, and contenting a crowd cost a judge his conscience. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: The soldiers led him away into the hall, called Praetorium, and called together the whole band. The whole band gathered in the Praetorium, for mockery, like the trial, was done in company. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: They clothed him with purple, platted a crown of thorns, and put it about his head. Purple and thorns mocked his kingship: royal colour with a crown of pain, a parody that unknowingly crowned the true king. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

18 COMMENTARY: They began to salute him, Hail, King of the Jews! The salute, Hail, King, mocked Caesar's own greeting, and soldiers' jest became unwilling homage to the king they derided. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

19 COMMENTARY: They smote him on the head with a reed, and spit upon him, and bowing their knees worshipped him. Reed, spit, and kneeling worship together show mockery's cruelty, for they bowed to the king they struck. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

20 COMMENTARY: When they had mocked him, they took off the purple, put his own clothes on him, and led him out to crucify him. Mockery ended, his own clothes returned, and leading him out began the journey to Golgotha, where mockery would become execution. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

21 COMMENTARY: They compelled one Simon a Cyrenian, who passed by, coming out of the country, the father of Alexander and Rufus, to bear his cross. Simon, passing by from the country, was compelled to bear the cross, and his sons' names preserve a family that later knew the faith his burden introduced. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

22 COMMENTARY: They brought him to the place Golgotha, which is, being interpreted, The place of a skull. Golgotha, the skull's place, names the hill of death, and the cross stood where skulls marked execution's ground. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

23 COMMENTARY: They gave him to drink wine mingled with myrrh, but he received it not. Myrrhed wine, offered to deaden pain, he refused, for he would bear the cross with senses clear, and drink the cup his Father gave, not man's stupefaction. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

24 COMMENTARY: When they had crucified him, they parted his garments, casting lots upon them, what every man should take. Crucifixion done, soldiers parted his garments by lot, fulfilling Scripture in the very act of dividing his clothes. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

25 COMMENTARY: It was the third hour, and they crucified him. The third hour, nine in the morning, marks the crucifixion's time, that the day's hours may be counted against the darkness and death to follow. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

26 COMMENTARY: The superscription of his accusation was written over, THE KING OF THE JEWS. The superscription, written over him, accused him of kingship, and Pilate's title, meant as charge, proclaimed the truth to every passer-by. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

27 COMMENTARY: With him they crucified two thieves, one on his right hand and the other on his left. Two thieves, right and left, flanked him, fulfilling the places James and John had sought, now filled by criminals in shame. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

28 COMMENTARY: The scripture was fulfilled, which says, And he was numbered with the transgressors. Numbered with transgressors, he bore the transgressors' place, and Scripture's word was fulfilled in his company on the cross. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

29 COMMENTARY: They that passed by railed on him, wagging their heads, and saying, Ah, you that destroy the temple and build it in three days. Passers-by railed and wagged their heads, repeating the false temple charge as a taunt, for the cross seemed to refute the builder's claim. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

30 COMMENTARY: Save yourself, and come down from the cross. 'Save yourself' was the taunt's logic: a Saviour who cannot save himself is no saviour, yet saving himself would have unsaved them all. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

31 COMMENTARY: Likewise also the chief priests mocking said among themselves with the scribes, He saved others; himself he cannot save. Priests and scribes mocked among themselves, confessing he saved others while denying he could save himself, and their confession is the gospel in a taunt. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

32 COMMENTARY: Let Christ the King of Israel descend now from the cross, that we may see and believe. And they that were crucified with him reviled him. Descending would have won their sight, they claimed, but faith that waits for a sign from the cross mistakes the cross itself, and even fellow-sufferers reviled him. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

33 COMMENTARY: When the sixth hour was come, there was darkness over the whole land until the ninth hour. Darkness from noon to three covered the land, for creation mourned its maker, and midday night marked the atonement's horror. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

34 COMMENTARY: At the ninth hour Jesus cried with a loud voice, Eloi, Eloi, lama sabachthani? which is, My God, my God, why have you forsaken me? The ninth-hour cry, in his own tongue, quotes the psalm of forsakenness, for he bore the Father's hiding that sinners might never be forsaken. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

35 COMMENTARY: Some standing by, when they heard it, said, Behold, he calls Elijah. Hearers mistook Eloi for Elijah, for they listened for a name, not for the psalm, and their error shows how little the cross was understood even as it happened. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

36 COMMENTARY: One ran and filled a sponge full of vinegar, put it on a reed, and gave him to drink, saying, Let alone; let us see whether Elijah will come to take him down. Vinegar on a reed was offered while watchers waited for Elijah, and thirst's relief became another delay in the spectacle of his dying. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

37 COMMENTARY: Jesus cried with a loud voice, and gave up the ghost. A loud cry, not a whisper, ended his life, for he gave up his spirit willingly, not as one whose strength merely failed. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

38 COMMENTARY: The veil of the temple was rent in twain from the top to the bottom. The veil rent from top to bottom, by heaven's hand, not man's, opened the holy place at his death, for the way to God was now open through the torn veil of his flesh. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

39 COMMENTARY: When the centurion, who stood over against him, saw that he so cried out and gave up the ghost, he said, Truly this man was the Son of God. The centurion, facing the cross, confessed him Son of God, for his manner of dying convinced a Roman soldier whom priests had not convinced. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

40 COMMENTARY: There were also women looking on afar off, among whom was Mary Magdalene, Mary the mother of James the less and Joses, and Salome. Women looked on afar off, for love kept them near while fear kept them distant, and their names preserve the witnesses of his death. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

41 COMMENTARY: Who also, when he was in Galilee, followed him and ministered to him, and many other women who came up with him to Jerusalem. Galilean followers who had ministered to him followed to Jerusalem, and women's ministry, begun in Galilee, ended in faithful watching at the cross. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

42 COMMENTARY: When the evening was come, because it was the preparation, the day before the sabbath. Evening and preparation pressed burial, for the sabbath would forbid work, and the body must be laid before the holy day. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

43 COMMENTARY: Joseph of Arimathea, an honourable counsellor, who also waited for the kingdom of God, came, went in boldly to Pilate, and craved the body of Jesus. Joseph, honourable and waiting for the kingdom, went boldly to Pilate, for a secret disciple became bold when boldness was most needed. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

44 COMMENTARY: Pilate marvelled if he were already dead, and calling the centurion, asked whether he had been any while dead. Pilate marvelled at so speedy a death, and the centurion's testimony confirmed it, for the judge required proof before releasing the body. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

45 COMMENTARY: When he knew it of the centurion, he gave the body to Joseph. Pilate gave the body on the centurion's word, and the gift to Joseph honoured the dead as the trial had dishonoured the living. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

46 COMMENTARY: He bought fine linen, took him down, wrapped him in the linen, laid him in a sepulchre hewn out of a rock, and rolled a stone to the door of the sepulchre. Fine linen, a rock tomb, and a rolled stone gave him honourable burial, and the stone's rolling seemed to seal the story, though it sealed only the tomb. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

47 COMMENTARY: Mary Magdalene and Mary the mother of Joses beheld where he was laid. Two Marys beheld where he was laid, for witnesses of burial would become witnesses of resurrection, and they noted the place to return to it. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
