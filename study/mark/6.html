Study Notes — Mark

Chapter 6

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: He went out from there and came into his own country, and his disciples followed him. Nazareth, his home town, was the hardest place for his ministry, and his disciples went with him to see it. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: When the sabbath came, he began to teach in the synagogue, and many hearing him were astonished, asking, from where has this man these things, and what wisdom is given him, and what mighty works are wrought by his hands? Their astonishment owned his wisdom and works, but stopped short of faith. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: They asked, Is not this the carpenter, the son of Mary, the brother of James, Joses, Juda, and Simon? Are not his sisters here with us? And they were offended at him. Familiarity bred contempt: they knew his trade and his kin, and used that knowledge to reject his doctrine. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: Jesus said, A prophet is not without honour, but in his own country, and among his own kin, and in his own house. Those nearest a prophet know his meanness and forget his mission, so honour follows him everywhere but home. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: He could there do no mighty work, save that he laid his hands on a few sick folk and healed them. Unbelief tied his hands, not for lack of power, but because mighty works require a people willing to receive them. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: He marvelled because of their unbelief, and went round about the villages teaching. Christ marvels at unbelief that persists against evidence, and turns from one town to the villages that will hear. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: He called to him the twelve and began to send them forth by two and two, and gave them power over unclean spirits. Two by two they could witness, comfort, and confirm one another, and their power matched their commission. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: He commanded them to take nothing for their journey, save a staff only: no scrip, no bread, no money in their purse. Bare provision taught them to trust God's providence and their hearers' hospitality. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: But they were to be shod with sandals, and not put on two coats. Needful dress was allowed, but superfluity was forbidden: a minister's wants should be few and plain. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: He said, in whatever place you enter a house, there abide till you depart from that place. Staying put avoids the appearance of seeking better quarters and honours the first welcome. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: Whoever shall not receive you nor hear you, when you depart, shake off the dust under your feet for a testimony against them; it shall be more tolerable for Sodom and Gomorrah in judgment than for that city. Dust shaken off is a witness, not revenge, and rejecting the gospel is worse than Sodom's sin because the light was greater. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: They went out and preached that men should repent. The apostles' first sermon was the same as John's and Christ's: repentance, the gate of the kingdom. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: They cast out many devils, anointed with oil many sick, and healed them. Oil was the common medicine of the time, used as a sign while the healing itself was God's, confirming their word as it had confirmed Christ's. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: King Herod heard of him, for his name was spread abroad, and he said, John the Baptist is risen from the dead, and therefore mighty works show forth themselves in him. A guilty conscience turned fame into fear, and Herod heard John in every miracle. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: Others said, It is Elijah, and others, That it is a prophet, or as one of the prophets. Popular guesses reached for past prophets because they would not confess the present Christ. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: But when Herod heard, he said, It is John, whom I beheaded: he is risen from the dead. Herod's own verdict convicted him: the murdered prophet haunted the murderer's conscience. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: For Herod himself had laid hold on John, bound him in prison for Herodias' sake, his brother Philip's wife, for he had married her. The imprisonment began in unlawful marriage, and John's prison was the price of his faithfulness. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

18 COMMENTARY: For John had said to Herod, It is not lawful for you to have your brother's wife. John's reproof was plain and personal, naming the sin and the sinner without flattery. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

19 COMMENTARY: Therefore Herodias had a quarrel against him and would have killed him, but she could not. Herodias hated the reprover, but Herod's protection stood between her malice and John's life for a time. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

20 COMMENTARY: For Herod feared John, knowing he was a just and holy man, and observed him, and when he heard him, he did many things and heard him gladly. Herod's conscience honoured John even while his lust defied him, and glad hearing without obedience is the pattern of a half-reformed hearer. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

21 COMMENTARY: When a convenient day came, Herod on his birthday made a supper for his lords, high captains, and chief estates of Galilee. A birthday feast of the great gave malice its convenient opportunity, and revelry became the stage for murder. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

22 COMMENTARY: When the daughter of Herodias came in and danced, and pleased Herod and those who sat with him, the king said to the damsel, Ask of me whatever you will, and I will give it you. Dancing won a rash promise, and pleasure loosened a king's tongue before his court. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

23 COMMENTARY: He swore to her, Whatever you ask of me, I will give it you, unto the half of my kingdom. An oath made in mirth bound him before witnesses, and he valued his word to a dancer above justice to a prophet. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

24 COMMENTARY: She went forth and said to her mother, What shall I ask? And she said, The head of John the Baptist. The daughter asked her mother, and malice that had waited found its mouthpiece in a child's question. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

25 COMMENTARY: She came in straightway with haste to the king and asked, I will that you give me at once in a charger the head of John the Baptist. Haste betrayed eagerness, and the charger turned a feast dish into a platter for murder. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

26 COMMENTARY: The king was exceeding sorry, yet for his oath's sake and for those who sat with him, he would not reject her. Sorrow without repentance yielded to reputation: he feared his guests' opinion more than God. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

27 COMMENTARY: Immediately the king sent an executioner and commanded his head to be brought, and he went and beheaded him in the prison. A prophet's head was the price of a dance, and the prison that could not silence John became his place of martyrdom. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

28 COMMENTARY: He brought his head in a charger and gave it to the damsel, and the damsel gave it to her mother. The ghastly gift passed from hand to hand, showing how sin shared spreads its guilt. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

29 COMMENTARY: When his disciples heard of it, they came and took up his corpse and laid it in a tomb. John's disciples gave their master burial, a last loyalty that foreshadowed the burial of Christ himself. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

30 COMMENTARY: The apostles gathered to Jesus and told him all things, both what they had done and what they had taught. Returning ministers report to Christ, whose commission they carried, and his hearing is their account. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

31 COMMENTARY: He said to them, Come apart into a desert place and rest a while, for there were many coming and going, and they had no leisure so much as to eat. Christ grants rest after labour, and even holy work must pause for the body's need. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

32 COMMENTARY: They departed into a desert place by ship privately. Privacy was sought for rest, but the people's love would not let them rest long. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

33 COMMENTARY: The people saw them departing, and many knew him, and ran on foot out of all cities, and outwent them, and came together to him. Foot travellers outran the ship, so eager were they for teaching that distance and desert were nothing. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

34 COMMENTARY: When Jesus came out, he saw much people and was moved with compassion toward them, because they were as sheep not having a shepherd, and he began to teach them many things. Compassion turned rest into teaching, for shepherdless sheep are his first care, and teaching is his first mercy. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

35 COMMENTARY: When the day was far spent, his disciples came to him and said, This is a desert place, and now the time is far past. The disciples saw the place and the hour, but not yet the provision. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

36 COMMENTARY: Send them away, that they may go into the country and villages and buy themselves bread, for they have nothing to eat. Their counsel was reasonable and kind, but it sent a hungry multitude from the only table that could feed them. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

37 COMMENTARY: He answered, Give you them to eat, and they said, Shall we go and buy two hundred pennyworth of bread and give them to eat? His command exposed their resources: two hundred pennyworth was beyond them, and their arithmetic proved their need. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

38 COMMENTARY: He said, How many loaves have you? Go and see, and when they knew, they said, Five, and two fishes. Five loaves and two fishes were all the disciples' stock, and Christ begins his miracles with what his people actually have. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

39 COMMENTARY: He commanded them to make all sit down by companies upon the green grass. Orderly companies turned a desert crowd into a banquet, and the green grass was their tablecloth. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

40 COMMENTARY: They sat down in ranks, by hundreds and by fifties. Ranked seating made distribution possible and counting certain, and no one was overlooked. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

41 COMMENTARY: When he had taken the five loaves and the two fishes, he looked up to heaven, blessed, broke the loaves, and gave them to his disciples to set before them, and the two fishes he divided among them all. Blessing, breaking, and giving through the disciples' hands is the pattern of his provision: the gift comes from heaven through human hands. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

42 COMMENTARY: They all ate and were filled. Five loaves fed five thousand and filled them, for Christ's blessing multiplies what is given to him. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

43 COMMENTARY: They took up twelve baskets full of fragments, and of the fishes. Twelve baskets of remainder showed abundance beyond need, one basket for each apostle to carry as proof. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

44 COMMENTARY: They that ate of the loaves were about five thousand men. The number counts men only, so women and children besides make the miracle greater still. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

45 COMMENTARY: Straightway he constrained his disciples to get into the ship and go to the other side before to Bethsaida, while he sent away the people. He parted the disciples from the crowd's excitement, and dismissed the multitude himself. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

46 COMMENTARY: When he had sent them away, he departed into a mountain to pray. After feeding thousands, Christ sought the mountain for prayer, and public work ended in private communion. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

47 COMMENTARY: When evening came, the ship was in the midst of the sea, and he alone on the land. The disciples were alone on the water, and Christ alone on the land, yet neither was out of his sight. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

48 COMMENTARY: He saw them toiling in rowing, for the wind was contrary, and about the fourth watch of the night he came to them, walking upon the sea, and would have passed by them. He watched their toiling from the shore and came at the darkest watch, walking where they rowed, as if to pass by and be recognised. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

49 COMMENTARY: But when they saw him walking upon the sea, they supposed it had been a spirit, and cried out. Fear mistook their Saviour for a spirit, for trouble blinds eyes even to a friend's approach. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

50 COMMENTARY: For they all saw him and were troubled, and immediately he talked with them and said, Be of good cheer: it is I; be not afraid. His voice identified him before his form could, and 'it is I' is enough to quiet any storm of fear. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

51 COMMENTARY: He went up to them into the ship, and the wind ceased, and they were sore amazed in themselves beyond measure, and wondered. His presence, not their rowing, ended the storm, and amazement filled the place fear had held. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

52 COMMENTARY: For they considered not the miracle of the loaves, for their heart was hardened. The loaves should have taught them his power over nature, but a hardened heart forgets one miracle before the next arrives. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

53 COMMENTARY: When they had passed over, they came into the land of Gennesaret and drew to the shore. Gennesaret's plain received them, and a new field of labour opened at once. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

54 COMMENTARY: When they came out of the ship, straightway they knew him. The people of Gennesaret recognised him at once, for his fame had arrived before his boat. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

55 COMMENTARY: They ran through the whole region and began to carry about in beds those that were sick, where they heard he was. News of his landing moved the whole region to bring its sick to him, bed and all. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

56 COMMENTARY: Wherever he entered, into villages, cities, or country, they laid the sick in the streets and besought him that they might touch if it were but the border of his garment, and as many as touched him were made whole. The border of his garment, touched in faith, healed as surely as his hand, and no place was too small for his mercy. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
