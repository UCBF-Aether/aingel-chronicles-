Study Notes — Mark

Chapter 10

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: He arose and came into the coasts of Judea by the farther side of Jordan, and the people resorted to him again, and as he was wont, he taught them again. His custom was teaching, wherever the people resorted, and Judea heard the same gospel Galilee had heard. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: The Pharisees came and asked, Is it lawful for a man to put away his wife? tempting him. The question was a trap, for any answer would offend either Herod's party or the strict school, and they sought his words, not his wisdom. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: He answered, What did Moses command you? Christ sends them back to Moses, for the law itself, rightly read, answers the tempter. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: They said, Moses suffered to write a bill of divorcement and to put her away. They turned a permission, given to restrain worse evil, into a licence, as if suffering meant approving. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: Jesus answered, For the hardness of your heart he wrote you this precept. Divorce was permitted because of hard hearts, not because it was good; a concession to sin is no rule for godliness. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: But from the beginning of the creation God made them male and female. Creation, not custom, is the standard: the first institution defines marriage before any later permission qualified it. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: For this cause shall a man leave his father and mother, and cleave to his wife. Marriage creates a new bond stronger than the old, for leaving and cleaving belong to its very nature. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: They two shall be one flesh, so then they are no more two but one flesh. One flesh means one life, one interest, and one indissoluble union, not a partnership at will. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: What therefore God has joined together, let not man put asunder. Man must not sunder what God has joined, for the bond is God's making, not man's convenience. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: In the house his disciples asked him again of the same matter. Private inquiry followed public controversy, and the disciples sought the plain rule for their own conduct. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: He said, Whoever shall put away his wife and marry another commits adultery against her. Putting away and remarrying, except where God allows, is adultery, for the first bond still stands before God. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: If a woman shall put away her husband and be married to another, she commits adultery. The rule binds both sexes equally, a rare equality in that age, for the marriage bond is mutual. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: They brought young children to him, that he should touch them, and his disciples rebuked those that brought them. Parents brought children for a touch of blessing, and the disciples, thinking children too small for Christ, turned them away. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: But when Jesus saw it, he was much displeased, and said, Suffer the little children to come to me, and forbid them not, for of such is the kingdom of God. His displeasure at hindering children is the strongest rebuke in the passage: the kingdom belongs to such as these. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: Verily, whoever shall not receive the kingdom of God as a little child shall not enter therein. Childlike reception means humble, trusting, and unpretending acceptance, without the bargains adults make with God. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: He took them up in his arms, put his hands upon them, and blessed them. Arms, hands, and blessing together show Christ's tenderness to children, and his blessing is more than a touch. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: When he had gone forth into the way, there came one running and kneeled to him, and asked, Good Master, what shall I do that I may inherit eternal life? Running and kneeling showed earnestness, and his question sought the way to life, though it still trusted in doing. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

18 COMMENTARY: Jesus said, Why do you call me good? There is none good but one, that is, God. The answer tests his title: if Christ is truly good, he is God, and the young man must reckon with who stands before him. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

19 COMMENTARY: You know the commandments: Do not commit adultery, do not kill, do not steal, do not bear false witness, defraud not, honour your father and mother. Christ names the second table, the duties to neighbours, as the test the man claimed to have kept. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

20 COMMENTARY: He answered, Master, all these have I observed from my youth. His claim was sincere as far as outward keeping went, yet it showed he did not know his own heart's failure. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

21 COMMENTARY: Then Jesus beholding him loved him, and said, One thing you lack: go, sell whatever you have, and give to the poor, and you shall have treasure in heaven, and come, take up the cross, and follow me. Love, not anger, gave the hard command: one thing lacked, his wealth, and selling it would prove whether he loved God or goods. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

22 COMMENTARY: He was sad at that saying, and went away grieved, for he had great possessions. Sadness and grief, not anger, mark his departure, for he loved his possessions more than the treasure in heaven, and sorrow without surrender is the rich man's tragedy. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

23 COMMENTARY: Jesus looked round and said to his disciples, How hardly shall they that have riches enter into the kingdom of God! Riches are a hard road to heaven, for they promise security, breed self-sufficiency, and hold the heart fast. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

24 COMMENTARY: The disciples were astonished at his words, but Jesus answered again, Children, how hard is it for them that trust in riches to enter into the kingdom of God! The hardness lies not in having riches but in trusting them, and trust in wealth shuts the kingdom's door. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

25 COMMENTARY: It is easier for a camel to go through the eye of a needle than for a rich man to enter into the kingdom of God. The proverb's impossibility is deliberate: humanly, the rich man's entrance is as impossible as a camel through a needle's eye. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

26 COMMENTARY: They were astonished out of measure, saying among themselves, Who then can be saved? If the rich, who seem most blessed, cannot enter, the disciples fear no one can, and their astonishment is honest. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

27 COMMENTARY: Jesus looking upon them said, With men it is impossible, but not with God, for with God all things are possible. What is impossible to men is possible to God, and grace, not wealth or poverty, opens the kingdom. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

28 COMMENTARY: Then Peter began to say, Lo, we have left all and have followed you. Peter's claim, though true in measure, seeks a reckoning, and Christ answers it with promise, not reproof. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

29 COMMENTARY: Jesus answered, Verily, there is no man that has left house, brethren, sisters, father, mother, wife, children, or lands, for my sake and the gospel's. Every loss for Christ and the gospel is named, that no sacrifice may think itself forgotten. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

30 COMMENTARY: But he shall receive a hundredfold now in this time, houses, brethren, sisters, mothers, children, and lands, with persecutions, and in the world to come eternal life. A hundredfold in this life comes in the church's fellowship, with persecutions mixed in, and eternal life crowns it in the world to come. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

31 COMMENTARY: But many that are first shall be last, and the last first. The reversal warns Peter's reckoning: first in sacrifice may be last in reward, and God orders the kingdom by grace, not by seniority. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

32 COMMENTARY: They were in the way going up to Jerusalem, and Jesus went before them, and they were amazed, and as they followed, they were afraid, and he took again the twelve and began to tell them what things should happen to him. Going before them to danger amazed them, and fear followed, yet he again foretold what awaited him, that they might not be surprised. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

33 COMMENTARY: Saying, Behold, we go up to Jerusalem, and the Son of man shall be delivered to the chief priests and scribes, and they shall condemn him to death, and shall deliver him to the Gentiles. The third prediction names the betrayers and the judges: priests and scribes condemn, Gentiles execute, and Jerusalem is the place of the cross. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

34 COMMENTARY: They shall mock him, scourge him, spit upon him, kill him, and the third day he shall rise again. Mocking, scourging, and spitting precede killing, and the third day's rising closes the prediction as its only comfort. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

35 COMMENTARY: James and John, the sons of Zebedee, came to him, saying, Master, we would that you should do for us whatever we shall desire. Their request, made in the shadow of the cross, asks a blank favour, and ambition blinds them to the hour. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

36 COMMENTARY: He said to them, What would you that I should do for you? Christ asks them to name their desire, that its unfitness may be plain to themselves and to the rest. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

37 COMMENTARY: They said, Grant to us that we may sit, one on your right hand and the other on your left hand, in your glory. Right and left hand in glory are the places of highest honour, and they ask them before the cup, mistaking glory for privilege. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

38 COMMENTARY: But Jesus said, You know not what you ask: can you drink of the cup that I drink of, and be baptized with the baptism that I am baptized with? The cup and baptism are his suffering and death, and they do not know what they ask, for glory's road runs through both. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

39 COMMENTARY: They said, We can, and Jesus said, You shall indeed drink of the cup that I drink of, and with the baptism that I am baptized with shall you be baptized. Their confident 'we can' is accepted as prophecy: both would share his suffering, James by the sword and John by long persecution. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

40 COMMENTARY: But to sit on my right hand and on my left hand is not mine to give, but it shall be given to them for whom it is prepared. Places in glory are the Father's preparation, not the Son's favouritism, and ambition cannot claim what grace prepares. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

41 COMMENTARY: When the ten heard it, they began to be much displeased with James and John. The ten's displeasure was not holy zeal but rival ambition, for each wanted the places the two had asked. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

42 COMMENTARY: But Jesus called them to him and said, You know that they who are accounted to rule over the Gentiles exercise lordship over them, and their great ones exercise authority upon them. Gentile rule is lordship and authority exercised downward, and Christ names it that his disciples may reject it. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

43 COMMENTARY: But so shall it not be among you: but whoever will be great among you shall be your minister. Greatness in his kingdom is ministry, and the measure of honour is the measure of service. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

44 COMMENTARY: Whoever of you will be the chiefest shall be servant of all. Chief place means lowest service, for the servant of all is the chiefest in Christ's reckoning. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

45 COMMENTARY: For even the Son of man came not to be ministered unto, but to minister, and to give his life a ransom for many. The Son of man is the pattern: he came to serve, and his service ended in a ransom, his life given for many. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

46 COMMENTARY: They came to Jericho, and as he went out of Jericho with his disciples and a great number of people, blind Bartimaeus, son of Timaeus, sat by the highway side begging. A named beggar by the highway is the last healing before Jerusalem, and his name preserves the witness. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

47 COMMENTARY: When he heard that it was Jesus of Nazareth, he began to cry out, Jesus, son of David, have mercy on me. Hearing became faith: he named Jesus son of David, the Messiah's title, and cried for mercy, not for wages. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

48 COMMENTARY: Many charged him that he should hold his peace, but he cried the more a great deal, Son of David, have mercy on me. Rebuke increased his cry, for earnest prayer grows louder under opposition, and he would not be silenced from mercy. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

49 COMMENTARY: Jesus stood still and commanded him to be called, and they called the blind man, saying, Be of good comfort, rise; he calls you. Christ's standing still is his attention to one cry in a crowd, and the bystanders' comfort repeats his call. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

50 COMMENTARY: He, casting away his garment, rose and came to Jesus. The garment cast away shows haste and expectation: he left his beggar's cloak to come to his healer, as if he would not need it again. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

51 COMMENTARY: Jesus answered and said, What will you that I should do to you? The blind man said, Lord, that I might receive my sight. Christ asks, though he knows, that faith may speak its desire plainly, and the man's answer is simply sight. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

52 COMMENTARY: Jesus said, Go your way; your faith has made you whole, and immediately he received his sight, and followed Jesus in the way. Faith is credited with the cure, sight came immediately, and the healed beggar's first use of sight was to follow Jesus on the road to Jerusalem. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
