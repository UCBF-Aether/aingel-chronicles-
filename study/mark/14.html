Study Notes — Mark

Chapter 14

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: After two days was the feast of the Passover and unleavened bread, and the chief priests and scribes sought how they might take him by craft and put him to death. The Passover's nearness sharpened the rulers' plot, for they sought craft, not open arrest, to kill him. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: But they said, Not on the feast day, lest there be an uproar of the people. They feared the people's uproar more than God's judgment, and their timing was governed by politics, not by the feast's meaning. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: Being in Bethany in the house of Simon the leper, as he sat at meat, there came a woman having an alabaster box of ointment of spikenard very precious, and she broke the box and poured it on his head. In a leper's house, a woman broke precious spikenard over his head, anointing him for burial before his enemies could bury him. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: There were some that had indignation within themselves and said, Why was this waste of the ointment made? Indignation called devotion waste, for they measured love by price and saw only the cost, not the honour. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: For it might have been sold for more than three hundred pence and have been given to the poor, and they murmured against her. Three hundred pence, a year's wages, might have fed the poor, and their murmuring used the poor as a cover for grudging Christ his honour. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: Jesus said, Let her alone; why trouble her? She has wrought a good work on me. Christ defends her against trouble, and names her act a good work, for love's extravagance to him is never waste. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: For you have the poor with you always, and whenever you will you may do them good, but me you have not always. The poor remain for daily charity, but Christ's bodily presence was brief, and the hour for honouring him would not return. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: She has done what she could: she is come beforehand to anoint my body to the burying. She did what she could, and her anointing anticipated burial, for she understood his death better than his disciples. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: Verily, wherever this gospel shall be preached throughout the whole world, this also that she has done shall be spoken of for a memorial of her. Her deed travels with the gospel worldwide, a memorial that outlasts the ointment's fragrance, for God remembers what men called waste. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: Judas Iscariot, one of the twelve, went to the chief priests to betray him to them. From the anointing table Judas went to the priests, and one of the twelve became the betrayer, a warning that office does not secure grace. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: When they heard it, they were glad and promised to give him money, and he sought how he might conveniently betray him. Gladness and money sealed the bargain, and Judas sought convenience, waiting for a quiet hour to deliver his master. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: The first day of unleavened bread, when they killed the Passover, his disciples said, Where will you that we go and prepare that you may eat the Passover? The disciples' question about preparation shows they expected to keep the feast, and Christ's answer would direct them. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: He sent forth two disciples and said, Go into the city, and there shall meet you a man bearing a pitcher of water: follow him. A man bearing a pitcher, unusual work for a man, was the sign, and following him led them without asking, to the appointed house. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: Wherever he shall go in, say to the goodman of the house, The Master says, Where is the guestchamber, where I shall eat the Passover with my disciples? The Master's question claims the guestchamber by right, and the goodman's obedience shows Christ's authority over men's houses. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: He will show you a large upper room furnished and prepared: there make ready for us. A large, furnished, prepared upper room awaited them, for Christ's foresight had provided the place before they asked. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: His disciples went forth, came into the city, found as he had said, and made ready the Passover. Finding exactly as he said confirmed their faith, and they made ready the feast that would become the Lord's Supper. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: In the evening he came with the twelve. Evening brought him with the twelve, and the last supper gathered the whole apostolic company, betrayer included. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

18 COMMENTARY: As they sat and ate, Jesus said, Verily, one of you which eats with me shall betray me. The announcement at table, that a fellow-eater would betray him, turned the feast into a searching of hearts. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

19 COMMENTARY: They began to be sorrowful and to say to him one by one, Is it I? And another said, Is it I? Sorrow and self-suspicion, not accusation of others, mark the disciples, for each feared himself rather than naming another. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

20 COMMENTARY: He answered, It is one of the twelve, that dips with me in the dish. One of the twelve, dipping in the same dish, is the betrayer, and shared bread makes the treachery fouler, not lesser. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

21 COMMENTARY: The Son of man indeed goes, as it is written of him, but woe to that man by whom the Son of man is betrayed! Good were it for that man if he had never been born. Scripture's fulfilment does not excuse the betrayer, and woe beyond birth itself falls on him who sells his master. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

22 COMMENTARY: As they ate, Jesus took bread, blessed, broke it, gave to them, and said, Take, eat: this is my body. Blessing, breaking, and giving bread makes the supper: the bread is his body given, and eating is receiving him by faith. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

23 COMMENTARY: He took the cup, and when he had given thanks, he gave it to them, and they all drank of it. Thanks over the cup, shared by all, makes the cup common, for all drink the same new covenant. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

24 COMMENTARY: He said to them, This is my blood of the new testament, which is shed for many. The cup is the new testament's blood, shed for many, replacing the old covenant's sacrifices with his own offering. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

25 COMMENTARY: Verily, I will drink no more of the fruit of the vine until that day that I drink it new in the kingdom of God. His abstinence looks to the kingdom, where the vine's fruit will be drunk new, and the supper is a pledge of that banquet. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

26 COMMENTARY: When they had sung a hymn, they went out into the Mount of Olives. A hymn closed the supper, and they went out singing to the Mount of Olives, where prayer and betrayal awaited. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

27 COMMENTARY: Jesus said to them, All you shall be offended because of me this night, for it is written, I will smite the shepherd, and the sheep shall be scattered. All would be offended, for the shepherd smitten scatters the sheep, and Scripture foretold the disciples' flight. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

28 COMMENTARY: But after I am risen, I will go before you into Galilee. After rising, he will go before them into Galilee, and the promise of reunion follows the prediction of scattering, that hope may outlast shame. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

29 COMMENTARY: But Peter said, Although all shall be offended, yet will not I. Peter excepts himself from all, and his confidence, though loving, is the pride that precedes a fall. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

30 COMMENTARY: Jesus said to him, Verily, this day, even in this night, before the cock crow twice, you shall deny me thrice. This very night, before the cock's second crowing, Peter would deny him thrice, a precise prediction that leaves no room for self-trust. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

31 COMMENTARY: But he spoke the more vehemently, If I should die with you, I will not deny you in any wise. Likewise also said they all. Vehemence increased with warning, and all joined Peter's vow, so that all shared the confidence that would fail. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

32 COMMENTARY: They came to a place named Gethsemane, and he said to his disciples, Sit here, while I shall pray. Gethsemane, the oil-press, is where he would be pressed, and sitting disciples were left while he prayed alone. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

33 COMMENTARY: He took with him Peter, James, and John, and began to be sore amazed and very heavy. The three who saw his glory now saw his heaviness, and amazement and heaviness together show a sorrow beyond words. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

34 COMMENTARY: He said to them, My soul is exceeding sorrowful unto death: tarry here, and watch. Sorrow unto death is his confession, and tarrying to watch is the disciples' part, for his agony needed witnesses, not sleepers. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

35 COMMENTARY: He went forward a little, fell on the ground, and prayed that, if it were possible, the hour might pass from him. Falling on the ground, he prayed for the hour to pass, if possible, and his prostration shows the weight of the cup he faced. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

36 COMMENTARY: He said, Abba, Father, all things are possible to you; take away this cup from me: nevertheless not what I will, but what you will. Abba, Father, joins childlike trust with submission: the cup may be removed if the Father wills, but the Son's will yields to the Father's. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

37 COMMENTARY: He came and found them sleeping, and said to Peter, Simon, do you sleep? Could you not watch one hour? One hour's watch was too much for Peter, who had vowed to die with him, and sleep exposed the vow's weakness. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

38 COMMENTARY: Watch and pray, lest you enter into temptation: the spirit truly is ready, but the flesh is weak. Watching and praying guard against temptation, for a ready spirit in weak flesh needs both, and this is the rule for all disciples. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

39 COMMENTARY: Again he went away and prayed, and spoke the same words. A second prayer repeats the same words, for true submission is not one utterance but a persevering surrender. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

40 COMMENTARY: When he returned, he found them asleep again, for their eyes were heavy, neither knew they what to answer him. Heavy eyes and no answer mark a second failure, for sorrow itself had made them drowsy, and shame left them speechless. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

41 COMMENTARY: He came the third time and said, Sleep on now, and take your rest: it is enough, the hour is come; behold, the Son of man is betrayed into the hands of sinners. The third coming ends watching: sleep now, for the hour is come, and the Son of man is betrayed to sinners, not to saints. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

42 COMMENTARY: Rise up, let us go; lo, he that betrays me is at hand. Rising to meet the betrayer, Christ goes willingly to his arrest, and his rising contrasts with the disciples' sleeping. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

43 COMMENTARY: Immediately, while he yet spoke, came Judas, one of the twelve, and with him a great multitude with swords and staves, from the chief priests, scribes, and elders. While he spoke, Judas arrived with an armed multitude from the rulers, and the arrest came as a military expedition against an unarmed teacher. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

44 COMMENTARY: He that betrayed him had given them a token, saying, Whomever I shall kiss, that same is he; take him, and lead him away safely. The kiss was the agreed token, turning affection's sign into betrayal's mark, and 'lead him safely' shows they feared rescue. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

45 COMMENTARY: As soon as he came, he went straightway to him and said, Master, master, and kissed him. Straightway, with doubled 'Master', Judas kissed him, and haste and flattery crowned the treachery. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

46 COMMENTARY: They laid their hands on him and took him. Hands laid on Christ fulfilled the rulers' craft, and taking him began the passion his predictions had foretold. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

47 COMMENTARY: One of those standing by drew a sword, smote a servant of the high priest, and cut off his ear. A disciple's sword cut off an ear, for zeal without Christ's command wounds rather than defends, and the ear's loss shows the danger of carnal weapons. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

48 COMMENTARY: Jesus answered and said, Are you come out, as against a thief, with swords and staves to take me? Swords and staves against a teacher are the rulers' confession of fear, for they came as against a thief, not as against a criminal tried by law. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

49 COMMENTARY: I was daily with you in the temple teaching, and you took me not, but the scriptures must be fulfilled. Daily temple teaching had offered them open arrest, and their night arrest fulfilled Scripture, which ordained both his openness and their secrecy. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

50 COMMENTARY: They all forsook him and fled. All forsook and fled, fulfilling the smitten shepherd's scattering, and the disciples' vows dissolved at the first swords. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

51 COMMENTARY: There followed him a certain young man, having a linen cloth cast about his naked body, and the young men laid hold on him. A young man in a linen cloth followed, perhaps roused from sleep, and his following, though brief, shows one soul drawn after the fleeing twelve. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

52 COMMENTARY: He left the linen cloth and fled from them naked. Leaving the cloth and fleeing naked, he escaped their hands at the cost of shame, a small picture of flight's disgrace. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

53 COMMENTARY: They led Jesus away to the high priest, and with him were assembled all the chief priests, elders, and scribes. The whole council assembled at night for a trial, for the rulers' haste brooked no delay in condemning him. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

54 COMMENTARY: Peter followed him afar off, even into the palace of the high priest, and sat with the servants and warmed himself at the fire. Following afar off, Peter sat with servants at a fire, and warming himself among enemies prepared his denial. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

55 COMMENTARY: The chief priests and all the council sought for witness against Jesus to put him to death, and found none. A council seeking witnesses to kill, and finding none, shows the trial's injustice from its start, for justice seeks truth, not a verdict. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

56 COMMENTARY: For many bore false witness against him, but their witness agreed not together. Many false witnesses contradicted one another, and their disagreement itself witnessed to his innocence, for lies cannot agree. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

57 COMMENTARY: There arose certain and bore false witness against him, saying. Certain witnesses rose with a new charge, twisted from his own words, to supply what the council lacked. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

58 COMMENTARY: We heard him say, I will destroy this temple made with hands, and within three days I will build another made without hands. His saying about his body's temple was turned into a threat against the stone temple, for false witnesses pervert true words. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

59 COMMENTARY: But neither so did their witness agree together. Even on this charge they disagreed, and the council's case collapsed a second time, yet they pressed on to condemnation. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

60 COMMENTARY: The high priest stood up in the midst and asked Jesus, Answer you nothing? What is it which these witness against you? The high priest, standing in the midst, demanded an answer to charges already disproved, turning trial into browbeating. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

61 COMMENTARY: But he held his peace and answered nothing. Again the high priest asked, Are you the Christ, the Son of the Blessed? Silence answered false witnesses, but the direct question about his sonship demanded a direct answer, and he would not evade it. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

62 COMMENTARY: Jesus said, I am, and you shall see the Son of man sitting on the right hand of power, and coming in the clouds of heaven. 'I am' confesses his sonship, and sitting at power's right hand and coming in clouds promises his judges a future sight of the judge they condemn. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

63 COMMENTARY: Then the high priest rent his clothes and said, What need we any further witnesses? Rent clothes feigned horror at blasphemy, and the cry for no more witnesses ended the search for evidence with a verdict already chosen. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

64 COMMENTARY: You have heard the blasphemy: what think you? And they all condemned him to be guilty of death. The council, asked for its opinion, condemned him all together, for a confession of truth was counted blasphemy by men who had rejected it. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

65 COMMENTARY: Some began to spit on him, cover his face, buffet him, and say, Prophesy, and the servants struck him with the palms of their hands. Spitting, blindfolding, and buffeting, with a mock demand to prophesy, turned condemnation into sport, and servants joined their masters' cruelty. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

66 COMMENTARY: As Peter was beneath in the palace, there came one of the maids of the high priest. Beneath in the palace, a maid approached Peter, and the first denial began with a servant girl, not a soldier. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

67 COMMENTARY: When she saw Peter warming himself, she looked upon him and said, You also were with Jesus of Nazareth. Warming himself, Peter was recognised and charged with being with Jesus of Nazareth, the very association he would soon deny. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

68 COMMENTARY: But he denied, saying, I know not, neither understand I what you say, and he went out into the porch, and the cock crew. The first denial pleaded ignorance, and going out to the porch did not escape the cock's first crow, which marked the prophecy's beginning. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

69 COMMENTARY: A maid saw him again and began to say to those standing by, This is one of them. A second maid renewed the charge to bystanders, widening the audience of the denial to come. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

70 COMMENTARY: He denied it again, and a little after, those standing by said again to Peter, Surely you are one of them, for you are a Galilean, and your speech agrees thereto. A third charge, from speech and Galilean accent, proved what Peter denied, for his very words betrayed his origin. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

71 COMMENTARY: But he began to curse and swear, saying, I know not this man of whom you speak. Cursing and swearing crowned the third denial, for Peter, to prove he did not know Christ, used the language of those who do not know God. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

72 COMMENTARY: The second time the cock crew, and Peter called to mind the word Jesus said, Before the cock crow twice, you shall deny me thrice, and when he thought thereon, he wept. The second crow recalled the word, and thought brought tears, for remembrance of Christ's prediction turned denial into repentance. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
