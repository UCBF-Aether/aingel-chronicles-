Study Notes — Mark

Chapter 8

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: In those days the multitude being very great and having nothing to eat, Jesus called his disciples to him and said to them. A second great crowd, like the first, found Christ caring for bodies as well as souls. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: I have compassion on the multitude, because they have now been with me three days and have nothing to eat. Three days of hearing had emptied their provisions, and compassion noticed what hunger would soon enforce. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: If I send them away fasting to their own houses, they will faint by the way, for divers of them came from far. Dismissal without food would endanger the far-travelled, and compassion counts the journey home as well as the sermon. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: His disciples answered, From where can a man satisfy these men with bread here in the wilderness? Their question forgot the former feeding, as if a past miracle gave no promise for the present need. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: He asked them, How many loaves have you? And they said, Seven. Seven loaves, more than the former five, were still nothing to four thousand, and Christ again begins with what they have. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: He commanded the people to sit down on the ground, and took the seven loaves, gave thanks, broke, and gave to his disciples to set before them, and they set them before the people. Thanksgiving, breaking, and distribution through the disciples repeat the former pattern, and order precedes provision. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: They had a few small fishes, and he blessed, and commanded to set them also before them. Small fishes, blessed, joined the loaves, and no gift is too small for his blessing to multiply. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: So they ate and were filled, and they took up of the broken meat that was left seven baskets. All were filled, and seven baskets of remainder matched the seven loaves, a sign of abundance without waste. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: They that had eaten were about four thousand, and he sent them away. Four thousand fed in a wilderness, then sent home in peace, is the second witness to the same power. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: Straightway he entered into a ship with his disciples and came into the parts of Dalmanutha. From the wilderness table he crossed to Dalmanutha, and the Pharisees met him there with a new demand. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: The Pharisees came forth and began to question with him, seeking of him a sign from heaven, tempting him. They sought a sign from heaven after signs on earth, not to believe, but to tempt, as if earthly miracles were beneath their notice. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: He sighed deeply in his spirit and said, Why does this generation seek after a sign? Verily, there shall no sign be given to this generation. His sigh grieved at their wilful blindness, and refusal answered a demand made in unbelief. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: He left them, and entering into the ship again departed to the other side. When a generation demands signs on its own terms, Christ leaves it, and departure is itself a judgment. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: Now the disciples had forgotten to take bread, neither had they in the ship more than one loaf. One loaf among thirteen in a boat sets up the lesson on leaven, for their anxiety about bread was about to expose their misunderstanding. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: He charged them, saying, Take heed, beware of the leaven of the Pharisees, and of the leaven of Herod. Leaven is doctrine that spreads secretly: the Pharisees' hypocrisy and Herod's worldliness both corrupt by small degrees. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: They reasoned among themselves, saying, It is because we have no bread. They heard a warning about doctrine and turned it into a reproach about provisions, mistaking a spiritual caution for a complaint. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: When Jesus knew it, he said, Why reason you because you have no bread? Perceive you not yet, neither understand? Have you your heart yet hardened? His questions expose their hardness: after two feedings, one loaf should cause no anxiety. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

18 COMMENTARY: Having eyes, see you not? Having ears, hear you not? And do you not remember? Eyes that saw the feedings and ears that heard the teaching ought to have remembered, and forgetting is here charged as unbelief. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

19 COMMENTARY: When I broke the five loaves among five thousand, how many baskets full of fragments took you up? They said, Twelve. The first feeding's twelve baskets are recalled as arithmetic against anxiety, for the disciples themselves carried the proof. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

20 COMMENTARY: And when the seven among four thousand, how many baskets full of fragments took you up? And they said, Seven. The second feeding's seven baskets repeat the lesson, and two witnesses should have settled the matter. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

21 COMMENTARY: He said to them, How is it that you do not understand? After both feedings, to fear for bread in his presence is to fail to understand who is in the boat. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

22 COMMENTARY: He came to Bethsaida, and they brought a blind man to him and besought him to touch him. Bethsaida's friends brought the blind man as others had brought the deaf, trusting a touch to heal. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

23 COMMENTARY: He took the blind man by the hand, led him out of the town, and when he had spit on his eyes and put his hands upon him, he asked him if he saw anything. Leading him out of town separated the cure from the town's unbelief, and the question invited the man's own report. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

24 COMMENTARY: He looked up and said, I see men as trees, walking. Partial sight saw shapes without distinction, a stage of healing that mirrors the disciples' own half-understanding. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

25 COMMENTARY: After that he put his hands again upon his eyes, and made him look up, and he was restored and saw every man clearly. A second touch completed what the first began, and clear sight followed, as gradual light grows into full day. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

26 COMMENTARY: He sent him away to his house, saying, Neither go into the town, nor tell it to any in the town. Bethsaida, which had seen his works and not believed, was not to hear this cure proclaimed in its streets. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

27 COMMENTARY: Jesus went out with his disciples into the towns of Caesarea Philippi, and by the way he asked them, Whom do men say that I am? On the road, away from crowds, Christ examined what the world thought of him before asking his disciples. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

28 COMMENTARY: They answered, John the Baptist, but some say Elijah, and others, One of the prophets. Popular opinion made him a revived prophet, honourable but far short of the truth. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

29 COMMENTARY: He said to them, But whom say you that I am? And Peter answered, You are the Christ. The question turns from the crowd to the disciples, and Peter's confession names the Messiah the prophets promised. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

30 COMMENTARY: He charged them that they should tell no man of him. The confession was true but untimely for proclamation, until suffering and resurrection should define what Messiah meant. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

31 COMMENTARY: He began to teach them that the Son of man must suffer many things, and be rejected of the elders, chief priests, and scribes, and be killed, and after three days rise again. The first lesson after the confession is the cross: rejection by the rulers, death, and resurrection on the third day. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

32 COMMENTARY: He spoke that saying openly, and Peter took him and began to rebuke him. Open teaching met open rebuke, for Peter could confess a Messiah but not a suffering one. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

33 COMMENTARY: But when he had turned about and looked on his disciples, he rebuked Peter, saying, Get behind me, Satan, for you savour not the things of God, but the things of men. Before the disciples, Christ named Peter's counsel Satan's, because it savoured human glory and opposed God's way of the cross. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

34 COMMENTARY: When he had called the people to him with his disciples, he said, Whoever will come after me, let him deny himself, and take up his cross, and follow me. Discipleship is public and costly: self-denial, cross-bearing, and following are its three conditions. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

35 COMMENTARY: For whoever will save his life shall lose it, but whoever shall lose his life for my sake and the gospel's shall save it. Self-saving loses the soul, and self-losing for Christ saves it, for life kept from him is already lost. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

36 COMMENTARY: For what shall it profit a man, if he gain the whole world and lose his own soul? The whole world is a poor bargain for a soul, and the question exposes every worldly exchange. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

37 COMMENTARY: Or what shall a man give in exchange for his soul? Once the soul is lost, no price can buy it back, for nothing in the world equals its worth. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

38 COMMENTARY: Whoever therefore shall be ashamed of me and of my words in this adulterous and sinful generation, of him also shall the Son of man be ashamed when he comes in the glory of his Father with the holy angels. Shame of Christ now will be met with shame before the Father then, and confession now is answered by confession at his coming. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
