Study Notes — Mark

Chapter 13

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: As he went out of the temple, one of his disciples said, Master, see what manner of stones and what buildings are here! The disciple's admiration for the temple's stones and buildings invites a prophecy he did not expect. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: Jesus answering said, Do you see these great buildings? There shall not be left one stone upon another that shall not be thrown down. Great buildings are no security, for the temple itself, stone by stone, would be levelled, and admiration must not mistake masonry for permanence. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: As he sat upon the Mount of Olives over against the temple, Peter, James, John, and Andrew asked him privately. Four disciples, apart and facing the temple, ask privately what the crowd was not told, for prophecy is given to those who ask. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: Tell us, when shall these things be, and what shall be the sign when all these things shall be fulfilled? Their two questions, when and what sign, seek a timetable, but Christ's answer gives watchfulness rather than dates. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: Jesus answering them began to say, Take heed lest any man deceive you. The first word is caution, not curiosity: deception is the first danger of the last days, and heed is the first duty. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: For many shall come in my name, saying, I am Christ, and shall deceive many. Many false Christs, using his name, will deceive many, for a borrowed name is the deceiver's best credential. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: When you shall hear of wars and rumours of wars, be not troubled, for such things must needs be, but the end shall not be yet. Wars and rumours are necessary, not final, and trouble at their sound mistakes beginnings for the end. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: For nation shall rise against nation, and kingdom against kingdom, and there shall be earthquakes in divers places, famines and troubles: these are the beginnings of sorrows. Nations, earthquakes, and famines together are only beginnings, like birth-pangs before the greater travail. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: But take heed to yourselves, for they shall deliver you up to councils, and in the synagogues you shall be beaten, and you shall be brought before rulers and kings for my sake, for a testimony against them. Persecution before councils, synagogues, rulers, and kings is promised as testimony, not as accident, and suffering before great men preaches to them. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: The gospel must first be published among all nations. Before the end, the gospel must be published to all nations, and persecution itself becomes the publisher, carrying witnesses before kings. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: But when they shall lead you and deliver you up, take no thought beforehand what you shall speak, neither premeditate, but whatever shall be given you in that hour, that speak, for it is not you that speak, but the Holy Ghost. No premeditation is needed, for the hour supplies the words, and the Holy Ghost speaks through the accused. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: Now the brother shall betray the brother to death, and the father the son, and children shall rise up against their parents and cause them to be put to death. Persecution will break the nearest bonds, for faith will divide brother, father, and child, and family love will turn to betrayal. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: You shall be hated of all men for my name's sake, but he that shall endure to the end shall be saved. Hatred for his name is the disciple's lot, and endurance to the end, not a good beginning, is what saves. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: But when you shall see the abomination of desolation, spoken of by Daniel, standing where it ought not, let him that reads understand, then let them that are in Judea flee to the mountains. Daniel's abomination in the holy place is the signal to flee, and the reader is warned to understand, for flight must be immediate when the sign appears. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: Let him that is on the housetop not go down into the house, neither enter to take anything out of his house. The housetop fugitive must not descend for goods, for delay in such a flight is death. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: Let him that is in the field not turn back again to take up his garment. The field worker must not turn back for a garment, for life is worth more than clothing, and looking back loses the race. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: But woe to them that are with child, and to them that give suck in those days! Mothers with infants face flight's hardest burden, and woe is pronounced on the tenderness that cannot flee fast. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

18 COMMENTARY: Pray that your flight be not in the winter. Winter flight adds cold, mud, and closed roads to danger, and prayer may obtain a milder season for escape, for God orders even the weather of judgment. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

19 COMMENTARY: For in those days shall be affliction such as was not from the beginning of the creation which God created to this time, neither shall be. The affliction will exceed all former suffering from creation's beginning, a superlative that marks Jerusalem's fall as unique. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

20 COMMENTARY: Except the Lord had shortened those days, no flesh should be saved, but for the elect's sake, whom he has chosen, he has shortened the days. Shortened days are mercy to the elect: without shortening, no flesh would survive, and election limits judgment's length. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

21 COMMENTARY: Then if any man shall say to you, Lo, here is Christ, or, lo, he is there, believe him not. Claims of a hidden or local Christ in troubled times are to be disbelieved, for the true Christ comes openly and in glory. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

22 COMMENTARY: For false Christs and false prophets shall rise, and shall show signs and wonders, to seduce, if it were possible, even the elect. False signs will be so convincing that even the elect would be seduced if it were possible, and the elect's security lies in that impossibility, not in their own discernment. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

23 COMMENTARY: But take heed: behold, I have foretold you all things. Forewarning is the defence: all things foretold leave no excuse for surprise, and heed is the answer to every seduction. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

24 COMMENTARY: But in those days, after that tribulation, the sun shall be darkened, and the moon shall not give her light. After tribulation, sun and moon darken, for the powers of heaven themselves are shaken before the Son's coming. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

25 COMMENTARY: The stars of heaven shall fall, and the powers that are in heaven shall be shaken. Falling stars and shaken powers mark the dissolution of the old order, that the new may appear. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

26 COMMENTARY: Then shall they see the Son of man coming in the clouds with great power and glory. The Son of man comes in clouds, with power and glory, the open answer to every false Christ's secret claim. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

27 COMMENTARY: Then shall he send his angels, and shall gather together his elect from the four winds, from the uttermost part of earth to the uttermost part of heaven. Angels gather the elect from every wind and every extremity, for no distance or dispersion can lose God's chosen. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

28 COMMENTARY: Now learn a parable of the fig tree: when her branch is yet tender and puts forth leaves, you know summer is near. Tender branches and leaves are summer's sure sign, and nature teaches how to read signs. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

29 COMMENTARY: So in like manner, when you shall see these things come to pass, know that it is near, even at the doors. As leaves mean summer, so these signs mean the end is at the doors, not far off but pressing close. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

30 COMMENTARY: Verily, this generation shall not pass till all these things be done. The generation then living would see the temple's fall, for the prophecy's first fulfilment belonged to their own days. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

31 COMMENTARY: Heaven and earth shall pass away, but my words shall not pass away. Heaven and earth are less durable than his words, and the prophecy's certainty outlasts the world it describes. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

32 COMMENTARY: But of that day and that hour knows no man, no, not the angels in heaven, neither the Son, but the Father. Day and hour are the Father's secret, unknown to men, angels, and, in his humiliation, the Son, and curiosity about them is forbidden by the very ignorance of heaven. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

33 COMMENTARY: Take heed, watch and pray, for you know not when the time is. Ignorance of the time is the reason for watchfulness and prayer, for what cannot be dated must be watched for always. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

34 COMMENTARY: For the Son of man is as a man taking a far journey, who left his house, gave authority to his servants, and to every man his work, and commanded the porter to watch. The absent master leaves authority, work for every servant, and a special command to the porter, for all are stewards in his absence. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

35 COMMENTARY: Watch therefore, for you know not when the master of the house comes, at evening, midnight, cockcrowing, or morning. Evening, midnight, cockcrowing, and morning cover the whole night, for the master may come at any watch, and no watch is safe for sleep. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

36 COMMENTARY: Lest coming suddenly he find you sleeping. Sudden coming finds sleepers unready, and sleep is the picture of a soul unprepared for its Lord. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

37 COMMENTARY: What I say to you I say to all, Watch. The command given to four disciples is given to all, for watchfulness is not the apostles' duty only but every believer's. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
