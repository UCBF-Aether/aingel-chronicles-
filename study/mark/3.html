Study Notes — Mark

Chapter 3

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: He entered again into the synagogue, and there was a man with a withered hand. The synagogue, a place of worship, becomes the stage for a sabbath controversy. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: They watched him, whether he would heal on the sabbath, that they might accuse him. Their watching was not curiosity but a trap, seeking evidence against a healer. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: He said to the man with the withered hand, Stand forth. Christ brings the case into the open, refusing to heal in secret where malice could deny it. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: He asked them, is it lawful to do good on the sabbath, or evil, to save life, or to kill? They held their peace. To forbid a good deed on the sabbath is to choose the evil of neglect, and silence betrayed their conscience. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: Looking round on them with anger, grieved for the hardness of their hearts, he said, Stretch forth thine hand, and it was restored whole as the other. Christ's anger is holy grief at hardness, and his command gives the power it requires. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: The Pharisees went forth and straightway took counsel with the Herodians against him, how they might destroy him. Enemies who agreed on nothing else united against Christ, and a miracle of mercy provoked a plot of murder. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: Jesus withdrew with his disciples to the sea, and a great multitude from Galilee followed him, and from Judea. He avoided the plot without abandoning his work, and the crowds followed him out of the towns. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: People also came from Jerusalem, Idumaea, beyond Jordan, and about Tyre and Sidon, a great multitude, when they heard what great things he did. Fame drew Gentile borders as well as Jewish towns to the healer. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: He told his disciples a small ship should wait on him because of the multitude, lest they throng him. The boat was both a pulpit and a protection against a crushing crowd. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: For he had healed many, so that they pressed upon him to touch him, as many as had plagues. Suffering crowds sought even a touch, and their pressing showed the scale of his healings. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: Unclean spirits, when they saw him, fell down before him and cried, Thou art the Son of God. Even devils confessed his sonship, though their confession was forced and unwelcome. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: He straitly charged them that they should not make him known. Christ would not have his name proclaimed by devils, whose testimony would dishonour the truth. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: He went up into a mountain and called to him whom he would, and they came to him. The call to apostleship was by his sovereign choice, not by their application. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: He ordained twelve, that they should be with him, and that he might send them forth to preach. Companionship came before commission: they must first learn Christ before they preach him. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: They were also given power to heal sicknesses and to cast out devils. Miracles were to confirm their preaching, as they had confirmed his. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: Simon he surnamed Peter, marking the rock-like steadfastness he would need and, by grace, show. A new name marked a new calling. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: James son of Zebedee and John his brother he surnamed Boanerges, the sons of thunder. Their vehement, earnest spirit suited the thunder of gospel preaching, once grace had tamed it. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

18 COMMENTARY: Then followed Andrew, Philip, Bartholomew, Matthew, Thomas, James son of Alphaeus, Thaddaeus, and Simon the Canaanite. The twelve were ordinary men of varied trades and tempers, chosen to show that the work was God's. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

19 COMMENTARY: And Judas Iscariot, who also betrayed him; and they went into a house. The traitor stands in the list without disguise, a warning that nearness to Christ without grace saves no one. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

20 COMMENTARY: The multitude came together again, so that they could not so much as eat bread. The press of ministry left no leisure even for meals, a pattern of self-denying service. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

21 COMMENTARY: When his friends heard of it, they went out to lay hold on him, for they said, He is beside himself. Even his own kin mistook zeal for madness, and sought to restrain what they could not understand. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

22 COMMENTARY: The scribes from Jerusalem said, He has Beelzebub, and by the prince of devils casts out devils. Unable to deny the miracles, his enemies ascribed them to Satan, the worst charge malice could invent. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

23 COMMENTARY: He called them to him and said in parables, How can Satan cast out Satan? Christ answers slander calmly, by reason, inviting them to see the absurdity of their charge. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

24 COMMENTARY: If a kingdom be divided against itself, that kingdom cannot stand. A power at war with itself destroys itself, and Satan would not destroy his own kingdom. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

25 COMMENTARY: If a house be divided against itself, that house cannot stand. The same reason holds in smaller societies: internal war is ruin, not strength. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

26 COMMENTARY: If Satan rise up against himself and be divided, he cannot stand, but has an end. To cast out devils by Satan would be Satan ending his own reign, which he will not do. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

27 COMMENTARY: No man can enter a strong man's house and spoil his goods, except he first bind the strong man, and then he will spoil his house. Christ's casting out devils proves he has bound Satan, the strong man, and is plundering his house. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

28 COMMENTARY: Verily, all sins shall be forgiven to the sons of men, and blasphemies with which they blaspheme. No sin, however great, is beyond pardon for the penitent, and this promise is meant to encourage return. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

29 COMMENTARY: But he that blasphemes against the Holy Ghost has never forgiveness, but is in danger of eternal damnation. Wilful, settled ascribing of the Spirit's work to Satan hardens the heart past repentance, and so past pardon. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

30 COMMENTARY: He said this because they said, He has an unclean spirit. Their charge was itself the blasphemy in view: calling the Spirit's manifest work the devil's. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

31 COMMENTARY: Then his brethren and his mother came, standing without, and sent to him, calling him. Family ties sought him in the midst of his work, and the crowd became their messenger. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

32 COMMENTARY: The multitude sat about him and said, behold, thy mother and brethren without seek for thee. Those sitting at his feet reported the interruption, not knowing how he would answer. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

33 COMMENTARY: He answered, Who is my mother, or my brethren? The question does not dishonour his mother; it lifts the hearers to a higher kinship. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

34 COMMENTARY: Looking round on those who sat about him, he said, Behold my mother and my brethren! Disciples seated to learn are counted his nearest family. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

35 COMMENTARY: For whosoever shall do the will of God, the same is my brother, and my sister, and mother. Obedience, not blood, makes kinship with Christ, and that kinship is open to all who do God's will. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
