Study Notes — Mark

Chapter 1

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: The gospel is good news about Jesus Christ, and its foundation is that he is the Son of God. Henry stresses that Christ is both the author and the subject of this gospel. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: Malachi's messenger is John the Baptist, sent to prepare Christ's way before him. The gospel begins exactly as the prophets said it would, showing one divine original for both testaments. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: Isaiah's voice crying in the wilderness calls for straight paths: crooked judgments rectified and crooked affections set right, so Christ may enter the heart. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: John baptized in the wilderness and preached repentance as the way to remission of sins. Repentance and reformation were the duties required; forgiveness and sanctification were the blessings promised. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: People from all Judea and Jerusalem went out to John, confessed their sins, and were baptized in the Jordan. In this willing crowd Henry sees the first beginnings of a gospel church. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: John's camel's-hair clothing and food of locusts and wild honey bespoke self-denial and contempt of the world. The more we sit loose to the body and the world, the better we are prepared for Christ. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: John preached one mightier than himself, whose shoe-latchet he was unworthy to loose. The greatest saints are the most humble, and John labours to give all honour to Christ. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: John baptized with water, but Christ would baptize with the Holy Ghost. That baptism purifies by grace and refreshes by comfort, which water alone could only signify. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: Jesus came from Nazareth and was baptized by John in the Jordan. Henry reads this humility as Christ fulfilling all righteousness and sanctifying himself for our sakes. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: Coming up out of the water, Jesus saw the heavens opened and the Spirit descending like a dove upon him. Heaven's opening and the Spirit's descent together owned him as the Lord from heaven. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: The voice from heaven named Jesus the beloved Son, in whom God is well pleased. God loved him no less for his low estate, and delights in him as the referee between God and man. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: The Spirit immediately drove Jesus into the wilderness. Retirement for converse with God often follows great ordinations, and temptation follows baptism. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: For forty days he was tempted by Satan, was with the wild beasts, and was ministered to by angels. His preservation among beasts was an earnest of seasonable supply, and angel ministry comforts us against evil designs. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: After John was put in prison, Jesus came into Galilee preaching the gospel of the kingdom of God. The silencing of one minister does not suppress the gospel; God raises up others to carry it on. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: Jesus announced that the time was fulfilled and the kingdom of God at hand, and called hearers to repent and believe. Repentance and faith must go together: reform without trust in Christ cannot save, nor trust without reformation. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: Walking by the Sea of Galilee, Jesus saw Simon and Andrew casting a net, for they were fishers. Christ chose weak and lowly instruments, that the power might appear to be wholly God's. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: Jesus called them to follow him and promised to make them fishers of men. Ministers cast the gospel net into the sea of the world to win lost souls to Christ. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

18 COMMENTARY: Straightway they forsook their nets and followed him. Those Christ calls must sit loose to the world and forsake whatever is inconsistent with duty to him. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

19 COMMENTARY: Going a little farther, Jesus saw James and John in the ship mending their nets. Christ puts honour on those who are diligent in their business and loving to one another. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

20 COMMENTARY: He called them at once, and they left their father Zebedee in the ship with the hired servants and went after him. Even relations and companions must be left for Christ, though they left their father not without help. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

21 COMMENTARY: They went into Capernaum, and straightway on the sabbath Jesus entered the synagogue and taught. He lost no time in his work and honoured the sabbath by sabbath-work rather than by niceties of rest. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

22 COMMENTARY: The hearers were astonished at his doctrine, for he taught with authority and not as the scribes. The scribes repeated the law by rote, but Christ knew the mind of God and was commissioned to declare it. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

23 COMMENTARY: In the synagogue there was a man with an unclean spirit, who cried out. The devil is called unclean because he has lost purity, opposes the Holy Spirit, and pollutes the spirits of men. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

24 COMMENTARY: The spirit cried, let us alone, and confessed Jesus as the Holy One of God, come to destroy them. Devils believe and tremble, but have no hope in him; a bare notion of Christ goes no further than the devil does. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

25 COMMENTARY: Jesus rebuked him, saying, hold thy peace, and come out of him. Christ disdains testimony from an unclean spirit and commands with authority; he will not let a confession cloak malicious designs. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

26 COMMENTARY: The unclean spirit tore the man and cried loudly, then came out of him. When Christ delivers souls from Satan, it is often not without a grievous toss and tumult in the soul. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

27 COMMENTARY: All were amazed and questioned what new doctrine this was, since with authority he commanded even unclean spirits and they obeyed. Miracles confirmed doctrine and showed the design of his teaching: to conquer Satan and cure sick souls. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

28 COMMENTARY: Immediately his fame spread throughout all the region round about Galilee. The report prepared his way, now that John his forerunner was imprisoned. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

29 COMMENTARY: Forthwith they left the synagogue and entered the house of Simon and Andrew, with James and John. Christ's work moved from public teaching to private household mercy without pause. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

30 COMMENTARY: Simon's wife's mother lay sick of a fever, and at once they told Jesus of her. Bringing the sick to Christ by prayer and intercession is the proper use of acquaintance with him. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

31 COMMENTARY: He came, took her by the hand, lifted her up, and immediately the fever left her and she ministered to them. A complete cure restores to service, and gratitude shows itself in ministry. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

32 COMMENTARY: At evening, when the sun had set, they brought to him all the diseased and those possessed with devils. The sabbath's end freed them to carry their sick, and Christ's day of teaching closed in healing. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

33 COMMENTARY: All the city was gathered together at the door. The whole town's crowding shows both the misery that sought him and the fame his miracles had won. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

34 COMMENTARY: He healed many sick of divers diseases, cast out many devils, and suffered not the devils to speak because they knew him. Christ would not be witnessed to by devils, whose praise would dishonour him. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

35 COMMENTARY: In the morning, rising a great while before day, he went into a solitary place and prayed. Before the press of work, Christ sought free converse with God in retirement. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

36 COMMENTARY: Simon and those with him followed after him, pursuing him to his retreat. The disciples' eagerness shows how the crowds already pressed upon him. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

37 COMMENTARY: When they found him they said, all men seek for thee. Popular demand urged him to stay, but it did not govern his movements. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

38 COMMENTARY: He answered, let us go into the next towns, that I may preach there also, for therefore came I forth. Preaching was his chief business; miracles confirmed it but did not replace it. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

39 COMMENTARY: He preached in their synagogues throughout all Galilee and cast out devils. His doctrine and his war against Satan went together wherever he went. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

40 COMMENTARY: A leper came beseeching him, kneeling down, saying, if thou wilt, thou canst make me clean. His prayer joins full faith in Christ's power with full submission to his will. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

41 COMMENTARY: Jesus, moved with compassion, put forth his hand, touched him, and said, I will; be thou clean. Compassion moved him to touch the untouchable, and his word effected what his touch signified. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

42 COMMENTARY: As soon as he had spoken, immediately the leprosy departed from him and he was cleansed. The cure was instant and complete, proving the authority of the word that commanded it. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

43 COMMENTARY: Jesus straitly charged him and forthwith sent him away, forbidding delay or display. Christ sought no applause for his works and ordered the man's conduct as well as his cure. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

44 COMMENTARY: He was to say nothing to any man, but show himself to the priest and offer what Moses commanded, for a testimony to them. Obedience to the law gave the priests legal proof of the cleansing and of Christ's respect for Moses. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

45 COMMENTARY: But the man went out and began to publish it much, so that Jesus could no more openly enter the city, but stayed in desert places, and people came to him from every quarter. Zeal without obedience hindered Christ's public work, yet it could not stop the crowds from seeking him. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
