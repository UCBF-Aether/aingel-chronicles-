Study Notes — Mark

Chapter 9

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: He said to them, Verily, there are some standing here who shall not taste death till they have seen the kingdom of God come with power. The transfiguration, within days, would let some disciples see the kingdom's power in a glimpse before death. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: After six days Jesus took Peter, James, and John, and led them up into a high mountain apart by themselves, and he was transfigured before them. Three witnesses were chosen for a private glory, and the mountain apart became a sanctuary for what the crowd could not yet bear. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: His raiment became shining, exceeding white as snow, so as no fuller on earth can whiten them. His clothes shone with a whiteness no earthly fuller could produce, for the glory within broke through his garments. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: There appeared to them Elijah with Moses, and they were talking with Jesus. The law and the prophets, in Moses and Elijah, stood with Christ and talked with him, owning him as the end of both. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: Peter answered and said, Master, it is good for us to be here, and let us make three tabernacles, one for you, one for Moses, and one for Elijah. Peter would prolong the vision with tabernacles, putting Moses and Elijah on a level with Christ. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: For he knew not what to say, for they were sore afraid. Fear excused his words but did not justify them, for terror often speaks before it understands. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: There was a cloud that overshadowed them, and a voice came out of the cloud, saying, This is my beloved Son: hear him. The cloud and the voice repeat the baptism, adding the command to hear him above Moses and Elijah. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: Suddenly, when they looked round, they saw no man any more, save Jesus only with themselves. Moses and Elijah vanished, leaving Jesus only, for the law and prophets having witnessed, the Son alone remains. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: As they came down from the mountain, he charged them to tell no man what they had seen, till the Son of man were risen from the dead. The vision was sealed until the resurrection should give it its meaning, lest glory without a cross mislead the hearers. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: They kept that saying with themselves, questioning what the rising from the dead should mean. Obedience kept the secret, but their question shows they had not yet understood resurrection, though they had heard it foretold. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: They asked him, Why do the scribes say that Elijah must first come? The scribes' doctrine of Elijah's coming seemed to clash with a Messiah already come, and the disciples sought the reconciliation. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: He answered, Elijah truly comes first and restores all things, and how it is written of the Son of man that he must suffer many things and be set at nothing. Elijah's restoring work stands beside the Son's suffering, and both are written: glory for the forerunner does not cancel shame for the Messiah. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: But I say to you, Elijah is indeed come, and they have done to him whatever they listed, as it is written of him. John the Baptist was the Elijah who came, and men's treatment of him, ending in Herod's prison, fulfilled what was written. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: When he came to his disciples, he saw a great multitude about them, and the scribes questioning with them. From the mountain's glory he descended to a disputing crowd, and the scribes were pressing the disciples' failure. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: Straightway all the people, when they beheld him, were greatly amazed, and running to him saluted him. His coming, perhaps with some remaining brightness, amazed them, and the crowd ran to the master the scribes could not answer. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: He asked the scribes, What question you with them? Christ takes up his disciples' cause and turns the question back on the questioners, requiring them to state their charge. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: One of the multitude answered, Master, I have brought to you my son, who has a dumb spirit. A father's case replaces the scribes' dispute: a dumb spirit had silenced his son, and he brought the boy himself when the disciples could not help. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

18 COMMENTARY: Wherever it takes him, it tears him, and he foams, gnashes with his teeth, and pines away, and I spoke to your disciples that they should cast him out, and they could not. The spirit's violence and the disciples' failure are both stated plainly, for the father's hope now rests on Christ alone. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

19 COMMENTARY: He answered, O faithless generation, how long shall I be with you? How long shall I suffer you? Bring him to me. The rebuke falls on the whole generation, scribes, crowd, and disciples alike, yet it ends in the gracious command to bring the boy. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

20 COMMENTARY: They brought him to him, and when the spirit saw him, straightway it tore him, and he fell on the ground and wallowed foaming. The devil's last rage at Christ's presence made the boy's misery worse before it ended, as if to prove its power by its fury. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

21 COMMENTARY: He asked his father, How long is it since this came to him? And he said, Of a child. The question draws out a history of lifelong affliction, that the cure may be known as no sudden fancy. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

22 COMMENTARY: Ofttimes it has cast him into the fire and into the waters to destroy him, but if you can do anything, have compassion on us and help us. The father's 'if you can' doubts Christ's power even while begging his compassion, and his prayer joins himself with his son in one 'us'. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

23 COMMENTARY: Jesus said to him, If you can believe, all things are possible to him that believes. Christ turns the 'if' back on the father: the question is not his power but the father's faith, and faith opens all things to possibility. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

24 COMMENTARY: Straightway the father cried out and said with tears, Lord, I believe; help my unbelief. Tears join belief and unbelief in one cry, and this is the truest prayer of weak faith: it confesses its lack and begs Christ to supply it. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

25 COMMENTARY: When Jesus saw the people come running together, he rebuked the foul spirit, saying, You dumb and deaf spirit, I charge you, come out of him, and enter no more into him. Before the gathering crowd, Christ commands by name, and adds 'enter no more' to secure a lasting deliverance. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

26 COMMENTARY: The spirit cried and rent him sore and came out of him, and he was as one dead, so that many said, He is dead. The departure looked like death, and the bystanders' verdict shows how like death deliverance can appear. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

27 COMMENTARY: But Jesus took him by the hand and lifted him up, and he arose. The same hand that commanded the spirit lifted the boy, and rising proved the cure complete. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

28 COMMENTARY: When he came into the house, his disciples asked him privately, Why could not we cast him out? Private failure again sought private explanation, and the disciples did not hide their defeat from their master. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

29 COMMENTARY: He said, This kind can come forth by nothing but by prayer and fasting. Some victories require extraordinary means: prayer and fasting are the appointed weapons against stubborn powers, and the disciples' failure was a failure of dependence. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

30 COMMENTARY: They departed from there and passed through Galilee, and he would not that any man should know it. He travelled privately through Galilee, avoiding crowds to teach his disciples alone. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

31 COMMENTARY: For he taught his disciples and said, The Son of man is delivered into the hands of men, and they shall kill him, and after he is killed, he shall rise the third day. The second prediction is plainer than the first: delivered, killed, and risen the third day, a lesson repeated because it was not yet learned. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

32 COMMENTARY: But they understood not that saying, and were afraid to ask him. They neither understood nor dared to ask, and fear of reproof kept them from the explanation they needed. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

33 COMMENTARY: He came to Capernaum, and being in the house he asked them, What was it that you disputed among yourselves by the way? In the house, Christ examined the road's dispute, for private questioning follows public teaching. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

34 COMMENTARY: But they held their peace, for by the way they had disputed who should be the greatest. Silence confessed the shame of the dispute: ambition among disciples is exposed by the very question that names it. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

35 COMMENTARY: He sat down, called the twelve, and said, If any man desires to be first, the same shall be last of all and servant of all. Sitting as a teacher, Christ reverses ambition: the way to first place is last place, and greatness is service. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

36 COMMENTARY: He took a child, set him in the midst of them, and when he had taken him in his arms, he said to them. A child in the midst, embraced by Christ, becomes the living lesson that ambition must unlearn. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

37 COMMENTARY: Whoever shall receive one of such children in my name receives me, and whoever receives me receives not me but him that sent me. Receiving a lowly child in Christ's name is receiving Christ himself, and receiving Christ is receiving the Father who sent him. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

38 COMMENTARY: John answered, Master, we saw one casting out devils in your name, and he follows not us, and we forbade him because he follows not us. John's report shows party spirit among the disciples: they forbade a man for not following them, though he followed Christ's name. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

39 COMMENTARY: But Jesus said, Forbid him not, for there is no man who shall do a miracle in my name that can lightly speak evil of me. A miracle in Christ's name is a bond to Christ, and such a man will not quickly turn to revile him. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

40 COMMENTARY: For he that is not against us is on our part. Neutrality toward Christ's cause is counted friendship, and the disciples must not make enemies of those who are not. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

41 COMMENTARY: For whoever shall give you a cup of water to drink in my name, because you belong to Christ, verily, he shall not lose his reward. Even a cup of water, given for Christ's sake, is remembered and rewarded, for God measures the motive, not the gift. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

42 COMMENTARY: Whoever shall offend one of these little ones that believe in me, it is better for him that a millstone were hanged about his neck and he were cast into the sea. Offending a believing child is so great an evil that drowning with a millstone is the lesser misery, a warning against causing the weak to stumble. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

43 COMMENTARY: If your hand offends you, cut it off: it is better to enter into life maimed than having two hands to go into hell, into the fire that never shall be quenched. Hand, foot, and eye are to be sacrificed rather than kept to sin, for maimed life is better than whole damnation. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

44 COMMENTARY: Where their worm dies not and the fire is not quenched. The worm and fire are undying, for hell's punishment has no end, and its repetition in each verse stresses what men most wish to forget. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

45 COMMENTARY: If your foot offends you, cut it off: it is better to enter halt into life than having two feet to be cast into hell, into the fire that never shall be quenched. The foot that walks into sin must be stopped at any cost, for halting into life is better than running into hell. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

46 COMMENTARY: Where their worm dies not and the fire is not quenched. The repeated sentence is itself the sermon: judgment without end answers every excuse for keeping a sin. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

47 COMMENTARY: If your eye offends you, pluck it out: it is better to enter into the kingdom of God with one eye than having two eyes to be cast into hell fire. The eye that lusts must be plucked in resolve, for one-eyed entrance into the kingdom is better than two-eyed damnation. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

48 COMMENTARY: Where their worm dies not and the fire is not quenched. A third repetition seals the warning, that no reader may pass it as a figure of speech only. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

49 COMMENTARY: For everyone shall be salted with fire, and every sacrifice shall be salted with salt. As sacrifices were salted, so disciples are seasoned by trial, and fire purifies what it does not consume in the godly. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

50 COMMENTARY: Salt is good, but if the salt has lost its saltness, with what will you season it? Have salt in yourselves, and have peace one with another. Salt that has lost its savour is useless, and disciples must keep grace's seasoning within, which shows itself in peace among themselves. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
