Study Notes — Hosea

Chapter 9

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Rejoice not, O Israel, for joy, as other people, for thou hast gone a whoring from thy God, thou hast loved a reward upon every cornfloor, because Israel's joy is forbidden, since its harvest joy was paid as a harlot's wage upon every threshing floor. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: The floor and the winepress shall not feed them, and the new wine shall fail in her, because the harvest that was loved as a reward will itself fail, and floor and press will feed no one. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: They shall not dwell in the Lord's land, but Ephraim shall return to Egypt, and they shall eat unclean things in Assyria, because exile removes them from the holy land to lands where even their food is unclean. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: They shall not offer wine offerings to the Lord, neither shall they be pleasing unto him, their sacrifices shall be unto them as the bread of mourners, all that eat thereof shall be polluted, for their bread for their soul shall not come into the house of the Lord, because in exile there is no acceptable worship, and their bread is mourners' bread, unfit for the house of God. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: What will ye do in the solemn day, and in the day of the feast of the Lord, because the question exposes their helplessness, since the feasts cannot be kept in a land of exile. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: For, lo, they are gone because of destruction, Egypt shall gather them up, Memphis shall bury them, the pleasant places for their silver, nettles shall possess them, thorns shall be in their tabernacles, because flight from destruction ends in Egyptian graves, and their pleasant silver houses will be overrun with nettles and thorns. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: The days of visitation are come, the days of recompense are come, Israel shall know it, the prophet is a fool, the spiritual man is mad, for the multitude of thine iniquity, and the great hatred, because when judgment arrives, the people mock the prophet as a fool, and the hatred that caused their iniquity turns upon the messenger. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: The watchman of Ephraim was with my God, but the prophet is a snare of a fowler in all his ways, and hatred in the house of his God, because the prophet, meant as a watchman with God, is treated as a fowler's snare, and hatred meets him even in God's house. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: They have deeply corrupted themselves, as in the days of Gibeah, therefore he will remember their iniquity, he will visit their sins, because the corruption of Gibeah, the outrage of the Benjamite days, is repeated, and God will remember and visit as he did then. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: I found Israel like grapes in the wilderness, I saw your fathers as the firstripe in the fig tree at her first time, but they went to Baalpeor, and separated themselves unto that shame, and their abominations were according as they loved, because Israel was once as delightful as grapes in a desert, but at Baalpeor they joined themselves to shame and became abominable like the thing they loved. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: As for Ephraim, their glory shall fly away like a bird, from the birth, and from the womb, and from the conception, because the glory of children will depart at every stage, so that there is no birth, no womb, no conception left to boast in. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: Though they bring up their children, yet will I bereave them, that there shall not be a man left, yea, woe also to them when I depart from them, because even children reared to maturity will be bereaved, and the crowning woe is God's departure itself. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: Ephraim, as I saw Tyrus, is planted in a pleasant place, but Ephraim shall bring forth his children to the murderer, because Ephraim, planted pleasantly like Tyre, will itself hand its children over to the murderer. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: Give them, O Lord, what wilt thou give, give them a miscarrying womb and dry breasts, because the prophet, shrinking from a heavier curse, prays for barrenness itself as a mercy compared with children given to the murderer. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: All their wickedness is in Gilgal, for there I hated them, for the wickedness of their doings I will drive them out of mine house, I will love them no more, all their princes are revolters, because Gilgal, the shrine of their wickedness, is where hatred was fixed, and expulsion from God's house follows, since every prince has revolted. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: Ephraim is smitten, their root is dried up, they shall bear no fruit, yea, though they bring forth, yet will I slay even the beloved fruit of their womb, because the root itself is dried, so no fruit can follow, and even fruit already brought forth will be slain. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: My God will cast them away, because they did not hearken unto him, and they shall be wanderers among the nations, because rejection for not hearkening ends in wandering, a people without a home among the nations. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
