Study Notes — Hosea

Chapter 7

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: When I would have healed Israel, then the iniquity of Ephraim was discovered, and the wickedness of Samaria, for they commit falsehood, and the thief comes in, and the troop of robbers spoils without, because the very attempt to heal exposes the disease, with fraud within the city and robbery without. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: And they consider not in their hearts that I remember all their wickedness, now their own doings have beset them about, they are before my face, because they forget that God remembers, while their own deeds surround them and stand before his face. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: They make the king glad with their wickedness, and the princes with their lies, because court favour is won by flattering wickedness, and the king is pleased with the very sins that ruin him. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: They are all adulterers, as an oven heated by the baker, who ceases from raising after he has kneaded the dough, until it be leavened, because their lust, like a baker's oven, is heated and kept ready, only pausing until the dough has risen. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: In the day of our king the princes have made him sick with bottles of wine, he stretched out his hand with scorners, because the court keeps festival with wine until the king himself is sickened, and joins hands with mockers. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: For they have made ready their heart like an oven, while they lie in wait, their baker sleeps all the night, in the morning it burns as a flaming fire, because conspiracy smoulders overnight like a banked oven and bursts into flame by morning. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: They are all hot as an oven, and have devoured their judges, all their kings are fallen, there is none among them that calls unto me, because the heat of faction consumes judge after judge and king after king, and in all the revolutions no one turns to God. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: Ephraim, he has mixed himself among the people, Ephraim is a cake not turned, because meddling with foreign alliances has half-baked the nation, burnt on one side and raw on the other. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: Strangers have devoured his strength, and he knows it not, yea, grey hairs are here and there upon him, yet he knows it not, because foreign powers drain his strength and age creeps upon him, while he remains insensible to both. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: And the pride of Israel testifies to his face, and they do not return to the Lord their God, nor seek him for all this, because pride, which witnesses against them, still will not turn them back, even after all that has befallen them. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: Ephraim also is like a silly dove without heart, they call to Egypt, they go to Assyria, because the nation, witless as a dove, flutters between Egypt and Assyria, seeking help from both and finding it from neither. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: When they shall go, I will spread my net upon them, I will bring them down as the fowls of the heaven, I will chastise them, as their congregation has heard, because the dove that flies to foreign help will be caught in God's net, and chastised as the congregation was warned. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: Woe unto them, for they have fled from me, destruction unto them, because they have transgressed against me, though I have redeemed them, yet they have spoken lies against me, because flight from God, after redemption by God, deserves both woe and destruction. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: And they have not cried unto me with their heart, when they howled upon their beds, they assemble themselves for corn and wine, and they rebel against me, because their crying is howling for provisions, not prayer from the heart, and they gather for corn and wine while rebelling against the Giver. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: Though I have bound and strengthened their arms, yet do they imagine mischief against me, because God strengthened the very arms that are now lifted to plot against him. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: They return, but not to the most High, they are like a deceitful bow, their princes shall fall by the sword for the rage of their tongue, this shall be their derision in the land of Egypt, because a return that is not to God is no return, and a bow that fails at the shot is the image of their princes, derided even in Egypt. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
