Study Notes — Hosea

Chapter 13

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: When Ephraim spoke trembling, he exalted himself in Israel, but when he offended in Baal, he died, because Ephraim, once honoured while humble, died spiritually when Baal worship became his offence. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: And now they sin more and more, and have made them molten images of their silver, and idols according to their own understanding, all of it the work of the craftsmen, they say of them, let the men that sacrifice kiss the calves, because idolatry grows by its own progress, and men are told to kiss the calves their own craftsmen made. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: Therefore they shall be as the morning cloud, and as the early dew that passes away, as the chaff that is driven with the whirlwind out of the floor, and as the smoke out of the chimney, because all four images agree, that idolaters vanish swiftly and leave nothing behind. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: Yet I am the Lord thy God from the land of Egypt, and thou shalt know no god but me, for there is no saviour beside me, because the exodus God remains the only God and the only Saviour, whatever idols have been added since. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: I did know thee in the wilderness, in the land of great drought, because God's knowledge of Israel was proved in the wilderness, where drought made every provision a direct gift. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: According to their pasture, so were they filled, they were filled, and their heart was exalted, therefore have they forgotten me, because full pasture bred a proud heart, and the proud heart forgot the God who gave the pasture. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: Therefore I will be unto them as a lion, as a leopard by the way will I observe them, because God, who fed them, will now meet them as a lion on the road and as a leopard watching the way. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: I will meet them as a bear that is bereaved of her whelps, and will rend the caul of their heart, and there will I devour them like a lion, the wild beast shall tear them, because the bereaved bear is the fiercest image of all, and the rending reaches the very caul of the heart. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: O Israel, thou hast destroyed thyself, but in me is thine help, because destruction is Israel's own work, while help remains in God alone, which is the whole gospel of the chapter in one sentence. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: I will be thy king, where is any other that may save thee in all thy cities, and thy judges of whom thou saidst, give me a king and princes, because the king they demanded cannot save, and God himself must be king if any saving is to be done. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: I gave thee a king in mine anger, and took him away in my wrath, because the monarchy itself, given in anger for asking, will be taken away in wrath for abusing. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: The iniquity of Ephraim is bound up, his sin is hid, because the sin is not forgotten but stored up, bound like a sealed bundle, waiting for the day of reckoning. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: The sorrows of a travailing woman shall come upon him, he is an unwise son, for he should not stay long in the place of the breaking forth of children, because Ephraim is in birth pangs, yet like an unwise child he stays at the birth passage and will not come forth. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: I will ransom them from the power of the grave, I will redeem them from death, O death, I will be thy plagues, O grave, I will be thy destruction, repentance shall be hid from mine eyes, because God promises a ransom from death itself, and speaks to death and the grave as enemies he will plague and destroy, a promise Henry reads as reaching beyond Israel's restoration to resurrection. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: Though he be fruitful among his brethren, an east wind shall come, the wind of the Lord shall come up from the wilderness, and his spring shall become dry, and his fountain shall be dried up, he shall spoil the treasure of all pleasant vessels, because fruitfulness will wither before the east wind from the wilderness, and the Assyrian will spoil every pleasant vessel. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: Samaria shall become desolate, for she has rebelled against her God, they shall fall by the sword, their infants shall be dashed in pieces, and their women with child shall be ripped up, because rebellion against God ends in the desolation of Samaria, with the horrors of a sacked city named in full. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
