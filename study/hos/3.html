Study Notes — Hosea

Chapter 3

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Then said the Lord unto me, go yet, love a woman beloved of her friend, yet an adulteress, according to the love of the Lord toward the children of Israel, who look to other gods, and love flagons of wine, because the prophet's renewed love pictures God's love persisting toward a people still turned to idols. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: So I bought her to me for fifteen pieces of silver, and for a homer of barley, and a half homer of barley, because the price is that of a slave, showing how low the adulteress had fallen and how real the purchase of her back would be. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: And I said unto her, thou shalt abide for me many days, thou shalt not play the harlot, and thou shalt not be for another man, so will I also be for thee, because the discipline is a season of chaste separation, waiting without lovers and without husband, until restoration. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: For the children of Israel shall abide many days without a king, and without a prince, and without a sacrifice, and without an image, and without an ephod, and without teraphim, because the long exile strips away both lawful and unlawful worship, leaving the people with neither throne nor altar. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: Afterward shall the children of Israel return, and seek the Lord their God, and David their king, and shall fear the Lord and his goodness in the latter days, because the end of the discipline is return, seeking God and the Davidic king together, in the last days. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
