Study Notes — Hosea

Chapter 12

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Ephraim feeds on wind, and follows after the east wind, he daily increases lies and desolation, and they do make a covenant with the Assyrians, and oil is carried into Egypt, because the nation feeds on empty alliances, chasing the scorching east wind, and carrying oil as tribute to Egypt while covenanting with Assyria. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: The Lord has also a controversy with Judah, and will punish Jacob according to his ways, according to his doings will he recompense him, because the lawsuit reaches Judah too, and Jacob will be repaid according to his ways, not according to his name. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: He took his brother by the heel in the womb, and by his strength he had power with God, because Jacob's life began with grasping and rose to wrestling, and both acts are held up as the pattern of his people. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: Yea, he had power over the angel, and prevailed, he wept, and made supplication unto him, he found him in Bethel, and there he spoke with us, because Jacob prevailed not by strength but by weeping and supplication, and Bethel, where God spoke with him, is where God speaks with his people still. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: Even the Lord God of hosts, the Lord is his memorial, because the God who met Jacob is the Lord of hosts, and his name itself is the memorial by which he is to be remembered. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: Therefore turn thou to thy God, keep mercy and judgment, and wait on thy God continually, because the answer to Jacob's story is return, with mercy and judgment kept, and continual waiting on God. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: He is a merchant, the balances of deceit are in his hand, he loves to oppress, because Ephraim has become a Canaanite merchant, weighing with false balances and loving the oppression that fills them. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: And Ephraim said, yet I am become rich, I have found me out substance, in all my labours they shall find none iniquity in me that were sin, because wealth persuades itself that no iniquity can be found in it, and riches are taken as proof of innocence. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: And I that am the Lord thy God from the land of Egypt will yet make thee to dwell in tabernacles, as in the days of the solemn feast, because God, who brought them from Egypt, will bring them back to tabernacle life, as in the feast that remembers the wilderness. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: I have also spoken by the prophets, and I have multiplied visions, and used similitudes, by the ministry of the prophets, because God did not leave the people without witness, but multiplied visions and parables through prophet after prophet. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: Is there iniquity in Gilead, surely they are vanity, they sacrifice bullocks in Gilgal, yea, their altars are as heaps in the furrows of the fields, because Gilead is vanity itself, and Gilgal's altars stand like heaps of stones in the furrows, multiplying wherever the plough goes. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: And Jacob fled into the country of Syria, and Israel served for a wife, and for a wife he kept sheep, because Jacob's exile in Syria, serving for a wife and keeping sheep, is recalled to show how low the patriarch's beginnings were, and how much God raised him from. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: And by a prophet the Lord brought Israel out of Egypt, and by a prophet was he preserved, because the exodus and the preservation in the wilderness were both works done through a prophet, Moses, and the people owe both to God's prophetic word. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: Ephraim provoked him to anger most bitterly, therefore shall he leave his blood upon him, and his reproach shall his Lord return unto him, because bitter provocation leaves bloodguilt upon the provoker, and the reproach cast at God will be returned upon Ephraim. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
