Study Notes — Hosea

Chapter 11

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: When Israel was a child, then I loved him, and called my son out of Egypt, because God's love chose Israel in its childhood, and the exodus was the calling of a son, not merely the freeing of a people. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: As they called them, so they went from them, they sacrificed unto Baalim, and burned incense to graven images, because the more the prophets called, the farther the people went, turning the call itself into an occasion for Baal worship. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: I taught Ephraim also to go, taking them by their arms, but they knew not that I healed them, because God led Israel as a child is led by the arms, healing every fall, while Israel never knew the hand that healed. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: I drew them with cords of a man, with bands of love, and I was to them as they that take off the yoke on their jaws, and I laid meat unto them, because God drew with human cords of love, lifting the yoke to feed them, as a man lifts the yoke from an ox's jaws to let it eat. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: He shall not return into the land of Egypt, but the Assyrian shall be his king, because they refused to return, because Egypt is not the sentence, but Assyria will be king over a people who refused to return to God. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: And the sword shall abide on his cities, and shall consume his branches, and devour them, because of their own counsels, because the sword will settle upon the cities and eat up their defences, and the cause is their own counsels. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: And my people are bent to backsliding from me, though they called them to the most High, none at all would exalt him, because the people are bent, like a bow warped toward backsliding, and even when called upward, none will exalt God. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: How shall I give thee up, Ephraim, how shall I deliver thee, Israel, how shall I make thee as Admah, how shall I set thee as Zeboim, mine heart is turned within me, my repentings are kindled together, because God pauses over the sentence, naming the cities of the plain that were overthrown, and his heart turns within him at the thought of doing the same to Ephraim. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: I will not execute the fierceness of mine anger, I will not return to destroy Ephraim, for I am God, and not man, the Holy One in the midst of thee, and I will not enter into the city, because God's holiness, unlike man's anger, restrains the fierceness, and he will not come as a destroyer into the city. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: They shall walk after the Lord, he shall roar like a lion, when he shall roar, then the children shall tremble from the west, because the people will follow the roaring lion, and his children will come trembling from the west at the sound. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: They shall tremble as a bird out of Egypt, and as a dove out of the land of Assyria, and I will place them in their houses, says the Lord, because the exiles will flutter home like birds from Egypt and doves from Assyria, and God himself will place them in their houses. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: Ephraim compasses me about with lies, and the house of Israel with deceit, but Judah yet rules with God, and is faithful with the saints, because Ephraim surrounds God with lies while Judah, though not innocent, still holds to God and to the faithful. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
