Study Notes — Hosea

Chapter 5

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Hear ye this, O priests, and hearken, ye house of Israel, and give ye ear, O house of the king, for judgment is toward you, because ye have been a snare on Mizpah, and a net spread upon Tabor, because the leaders who should have guarded the people have become the very traps that caught them. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: And the revolters are profound to make slaughter, though I have been a rebuker of them all, because the apostates dig deep in their plots of blood, while God has rebuked them all along and they would not hear. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: I know Ephraim, and Israel is not hid from me, for now, O Ephraim, thou committest whoredom, and Israel is defiled, because nothing in Israel's idolatry is hidden from God, who knows Ephraim by name and by deed. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: They will not frame their doings to turn unto their God, for the spirit of whoredoms is in the midst of them, and they have not known the Lord, because the bent of the heart, set on idolatry, keeps the deeds from turning, and ignorance of God keeps the heart from bending. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: And the pride of Israel does testify to his face, therefore shall Israel and Ephraim fall in their iniquity, and Judah also shall fall with them, because pride itself witnesses against the nation, and Judah, which shares the pride, will share the fall. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: They shall go with their flocks and with their herds to seek the Lord, but they shall not find him, he has withdrawn himself from them, because sacrifices offered without repentance find a God who has withdrawn, and the flocks they bring cannot buy his presence. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: They have dealt treacherously against the Lord, for they have begotten strange children, now shall a month devour them with their portions, because the children of mixed and idolatrous marriages testify to the treachery, and the judgment will come within a month, swiftly devouring them with their estates. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: Blow ye the cornet in Gibeah, and the trumpet in Ramah, cry aloud at Bethaven, after thee, O Benjamin, because the alarm of invasion is sounded from the border towns, and Benjamin, on Judah's frontier, is called to hear it first. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: Ephraim shall be desolate in the day of rebuke, among the tribes of Israel have I made known that which shall surely be, because the desolation is not a threat but a certainty already declared among the tribes. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: The princes of Judah were like them that remove the bound, therefore I will pour out my wrath upon them like water, because Judah's princes, encroaching like men who move a neighbour's landmark, will be flooded with wrath in proportion. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: Ephraim is oppressed and broken in judgment, because he willingly walked after the commandment, because Ephraim chose to follow the commandment of Jeroboam's calf worship, and willing obedience to a wicked command is itself the oppression. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: Therefore will I be unto Ephraim as a moth, and to the house of Judah as rottenness, because judgment comes silently and inwardly, like a moth in cloth or rot in timber, before it shows outwardly. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: When Ephraim saw his sickness, and Judah saw his wound, then went Ephraim to the Assyrian, and sent to king Jareb, yet could he not heal you, nor cure you of your wound, because the nations run to Assyria for a cure, but the king whose name means the contentious one cannot heal what God has wounded. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: For I will be unto Ephraim as a lion, and as a young lion to the house of Judah, I, even I, will tear and go away, I will take away, and none shall rescue him, because God himself, who came as a moth, now comes as a lion, and no deliverer can rescue from his tearing. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: I will go and return to my place, till they acknowledge their offence, and seek my face, in their affliction they will seek me early, because God withdraws until affliction teaches the nation to confess and seek him earnestly, which is the purpose of the withdrawal. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
