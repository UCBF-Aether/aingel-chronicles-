Study Notes — Hebrews

Chapter 4

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Let us therefore fear, lest, a promise being left us of entering into his rest, any of you should seem to come short of it — partial neglect and a loose, wavering profession often cause men to seem to come short, so we must give diligence for a clear entrance into God's kingdom. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

2 COMMENTARY: For unto us was the gospel preached, as well as unto them: but the word preached did not profit them, not being mixed with faith in them that heard it — unbelief is at the root of all unfruitfulness under the word, for faith in the hearer is the life of the word. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

3 COMMENTARY: For we which have believed do enter into rest, as he said, As I have sworn in my wrath, if they shall enter into my rest: although the works were finished from the foundation of the world — believers enter the rest God prepared from creation, while unbelievers are shut out by his oath. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

4 COMMENTARY: For he spake in a certain place of the seventh day on this wise, And God did rest the seventh day from all his works — as God finished his work and then rested, so he will cause those who believe to finish their work and then enjoy their rest. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

5 COMMENTARY: And in this place again, If they shall enter into my rest — the warning is repeated, because the rest still stands open and still may be forfeited. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

6 COMMENTARY: Seeing therefore it remaineth that some must enter therein, and they to whom it was first preached entered not in because of unbelief — the first hearers failed through unbelief, but the promise remains for others who will believe. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

7 COMMENTARY: Again, he limiteth a certain day, saying in David, To day, after so long a time; as it is said, To day if ye will hear his voice, harden not your hearts — long after the wilderness generation, God still says "To day," so the offer of rest is present, not past. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

8 COMMENTARY: For if Jesus [Joshua] had given them rest, then would he not afterward have spoken of another day — the rest Joshua led Israel into was not the final rest, or David would not still speak of another day. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

9 COMMENTARY: There remaineth therefore a rest to the people of God — a more spiritual and excellent sabbath than the seventh day or Joshua's Canaan: a rest of grace, comfort, and holiness now, and a rest in glory hereafter. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

10 COMMENTARY: For he that is entered into his rest, he also hath ceased from his own works, as God did from his — entering God's rest means ceasing from one's own works, as God ceased from his at creation. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

11 COMMENTARY: Let us labour therefore to enter into that rest, lest any man fall after the same example of unbelief — the end proposed is rest spiritual and eternal, and after due and diligent labour, sweet and satisfying rest shall follow. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

12 COMMENTARY: For the word of God is quick, and powerful, and sharper than any twoedged sword, piercing even to the dividing asunder of soul and spirit, and of the joints and marrow, and is a discerner of the thoughts and intents of the heart — when God sets his word home by his Spirit, it convinces, converts, and comforts powerfully, discovering men's thoughts, purposes, and sinful ends. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

13 COMMENTARY: Neither is there any creature that is not manifest in his sight: but all things are naked and opened unto the eyes of him with whom we have to do — the word shows the sinner all that is in his heart, and nothing is hidden from God. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

14 COMMENTARY: Seeing then that we have a great high priest, that is passed into the heavens, Jesus the Son of God, let us hold fast our profession — Christ executed one part of his priesthood on earth in dying for us, and the other in heaven, pleading our cause; so hold fast the doctrines of faith in head, heart, lips, and life. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

15 COMMENTARY: For we have not an high priest which cannot be touched with the feeling of our infirmities; but was in all points tempted like as we are, yet without sin — it was needful that the Saviour have the fellow-feeling only a fellow-creature could have, with actual experience of sin's effects apart from its guilt. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

16 COMMENTARY: Let us therefore come boldly unto the throne of grace, that we may obtain mercy, and find grace to help in time of need — we come not dragged to a seat of justice but kindly invited to the mercy-seat, where grace reigns, to obtain mercy to pardon our sins and grace to purify our souls. — Matthew Henry (Concise)
