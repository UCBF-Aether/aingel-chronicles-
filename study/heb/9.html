Study Notes — Hebrews

Chapter 9

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Then verily the first covenant had also ordinances of divine service, and a worldly sanctuary — the first covenant's ceremonies had a typical reference to Christ, and its ordinances and articles point him out as our Light and the Bread of life to our souls. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

2 COMMENTARY: For there was a tabernacle made; the first, wherein was the candlestick, and the table, and the shewbread; which is called the sanctuary — the tabernacle, a movable temple, shadowed forth the unsettled state of the church on earth and the human nature of Christ, in whom the fulness of the Godhead dwelt bodily. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

3 COMMENTARY: And after the second veil, the tabernacle which is called the Holiest of all — behind the second veil lay the Holiest of all, the innermost sanctuary. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

4 COMMENTARY: Which had the golden censer, and the ark of the covenant overlaid round about with gold, wherein was the golden pot that had manna, and Aaron's rod that budded, and the tables of the covenant — these articles remind us of Christ's Divine Person, his holy priesthood, perfect righteousness, and all-prevailing intercession; thus was the Lord Jesus Christ all and in all, from the beginning. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

5 COMMENTARY: And over it the cherubims of glory shadowing the mercyseat; of which we cannot now speak particularly — the cherubim of glory shadowing the mercy-seat crowned the sanctuary, a glorious representation, interpreted by the gospel, of God's wisdom, confirming faith in him who was prefigured by them. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

6 COMMENTARY: Now when these things were thus ordained, the priests went always into the first tabernacle, accomplishing the service of God — the priests went continually into the first tabernacle to accomplish its service. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

7 COMMENTARY: But into the second went the high priest alone once every year, not without blood, which he offered for himself, and for the errors of the people — only the high priest entered the second, once a year, not without blood for himself and for the people's errors; sins are great errors in judgment and practice, leaving guilt on the conscience that only Christ's blood can wash away. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

8 COMMENTARY: The Holy Ghost this signifying, that the way into the holiest of all was not yet made manifest, while as the first tabernacle was yet standing — the restricted access signified that the way into the holiest was not yet made manifest while the first tabernacle stood; a few believers saw something of access through the promised Redeemer, but Israel in general looked no further than outward forms. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

9 COMMENTARY: Which was a figure for the time then present, in which were offered both gifts and sacrifices, that could not make him that did the service perfect, as pertaining to the conscience — those gifts and sacrifices were a figure for that time, unable to perfect the worshipper's conscience, neither discharging his debts nor resolving his doubts. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

10 COMMENTARY: Which stood only in meats and drinks, and divers washings, and carnal ordinances, imposed on them until the time of reformation — these stood only in meats, drinks, washings, and carnal ordinances, imposed until the time of reformation; gospel times are times of reformation, of clearer light, greater love, and greater freedom and obligation to holy living. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

11 COMMENTARY: But Christ being come an high priest of good things to come, by a greater and more perfect tabernacle, not made with hands, that is to say, not of this building — Christ came as High Priest of good things to come, through a greater and more perfect tabernacle not made with hands; all good things past, present, and to come are founded on his priestly office. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

12 COMMENTARY: Neither by the blood of goats and calves, but by his own blood he entered in once into the holy place, having obtained eternal redemption for us — our High Priest entered heaven once for all, not by goats' and calves' blood but by his own, and has obtained eternal redemption; none of us can enter God's gracious presence here or his glorious presence hereafter except by the blood of Jesus. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

13 COMMENTARY: For if the blood of bulls and of goats, and the ashes of an heifer sprinkling the unclean, sanctifieth to the purifying of the flesh — the old sacrifices only freed the outward man from ceremonial uncleanness and fitted him for outward privileges. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

14 COMMENTARY: How much more shall the blood of Christ, who through the eternal Spirit offered himself without spot to God, purge your conscience from dead works to serve the living God? — what gave such power to Christ's blood? His offering himself without any sinful stain in nature or life; it cleanses the most guilty conscience from dead, deadly works, to serve the living God, and the grace that seals pardon new-creates the polluted soul. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

15 COMMENTARY: And for this cause he is the mediator of the new testament, that by means of death, for the redemption of the transgressions that were under the first testament, they which are called might receive the promise of eternal inheritance — Christ is Mediator of the new testament because by his death he redeemed transgressions under the first, so the called might receive the promise of eternal inheritance. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

16 COMMENTARY: For where a testament is, there must also of necessity be the death of the testator — a testament is a willing deed bestowing legacies, and it takes effect only upon the testator's death. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

17 COMMENTARY: For a testament is of force after men are dead: otherwise it is of no strength at all while the testator liveth — until the testator dies, the testament has no force at all. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

18 COMMENTARY: Whereupon neither the first testament was dedicated without blood — so even the first testament was dedicated with blood, not without it. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

19 COMMENTARY: For when Moses had spoken every precept to all the people according to the law, he took the blood of calves and of goats, with water, and scarlet wool, and hyssop, and sprinkled both the book, and all the people — Moses sprinkled book and people with sacrificial blood, sealing the covenant in blood. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

20 COMMENTARY: Saying, This is the blood of the testament which God hath enjoined unto you — the blood marked the testament God had enjoined upon them. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

21 COMMENTARY: Moreover he sprinkled with blood both the tabernacle, and all the vessels of the ministry — tabernacle and vessels alike were sprinkled, because nothing could be clean to a sinner, not even his religious duties, except as his guilt was done away by a sacrifice of sufficient value. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

22 COMMENTARY: And almost all things are by the law purged with blood; and without shedding of blood is no remission — almost all things were purged with blood under the law, and without shedding of blood there is no remission; may we ascribe all real good works to this all-procuring cause, and offer our spiritual sacrifices as sprinkled with Christ's blood. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

23 COMMENTARY: It was therefore necessary that the patterns of things in the heavens should be purified with these; but the heavenly things themselves with better sacrifices than these — the patterns of heavenly things were purified with animal sacrifices, but the heavenly things themselves required better sacrifices: Christ's sacrifice is infinitely better than those of the law, which could neither procure pardon nor impart power against sin. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

24 COMMENTARY: For Christ is not entered into the holy places made with hands, which are the figures of the true; but into heaven itself, now to appear in the presence of God for us — Christ entered not a man-made copy but heaven itself, to appear in God's presence for us, pleading his blood there while we plead it here on earth. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

25 COMMENTARY: Nor yet that he should offer himself often, as the high priest entereth into the holy place every year with blood of others — unlike the high priest who entered yearly with others' blood, Christ does not offer himself often. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

26 COMMENTARY: For then must he often have suffered since the foundation of the world: but now once in the end of the world hath he appeared to put away sin by the sacrifice of himself — if repeated offering were needed, he must have suffered often since the foundation of the world; but now, once in the end of the world, he has appeared to put away sin by his own sacrifice. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

27 COMMENTARY: And as it is appointed unto men once to die, but after this the judgment — as men die once and then face judgment, and as no wisdom, learning, wealth, or power can keep anyone from death, so nothing but Christ's atoning sacrifice can deliver a sinner from condemnation at judgment. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

28 COMMENTARY: So Christ was once offered to bear the sins of many; and unto them that look for him shall he appear the second time without sin unto salvation — Christ was once offered to bear the sins of many, and to those who look for him he shall appear a second time, without sin, unto salvation; nor will anyone be saved from eternal punishment who despises or neglects this great salvation, and the believer's continual prayer is, "Even so come, Lord Jesus." — Matthew Henry (Concise)
