Study Notes — Hebrews

Chapter 6

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Therefore leaving the principles of the doctrine of Christ, let us go on unto perfection; not laying again the foundation of repentance from dead works, and of faith toward God — every part of God's truth should be set before gospel professors, and we should not always be speaking about outward things, which often take up too much attention and time. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

2 COMMENTARY: Of the doctrine of baptisms, and of laying on of hands, and of resurrection of the dead, and of eternal judgment — these foundational doctrines have their place and use, but the Hebrews are urged to go forward beyond first principles. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

3 COMMENTARY: And this will we do, if God permit — progress in doctrine depends on God's permission and grace, not on our resolve alone. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

4 COMMENTARY: For it is impossible for those who were once enlightened, and have tasted of the heavenly gift, and were made partakers of the Holy Ghost — the apostle is not speaking of mere professors, who have nothing to fall away from but an empty name, nor of partial backslidings, but of a deliberate falling away after receiving the knowledge of the truth and tasting its comforts. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

5 COMMENTARY: And have tasted the good word of God, and the powers of the world to come — believers not only taste of the word of God but drink it in; the merely nominal Christian, continuing unfruitful under the means of grace, stands near the awful state described here. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

6 COMMENTARY: If they shall fall away, to renew them again unto repentance; seeing they crucify to themselves the Son of God afresh, and put him to an open shame — this falling away is an open and avowed renouncing of Christ from enmity of heart, approving in mind the deeds of his murderers; it is impossible to renew them, not because Christ's blood is insufficient, but because this sin in its very nature is opposite to repentance and everything that leads to it. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

7 COMMENTARY: For the earth which drinketh in the rain that cometh oft upon it, and bringeth forth herbs meet for them by whom it is dressed, receiveth blessing from God — this fruitful field or garden receives the blessing, like the believer who drinks in the word. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

8 COMMENTARY: But that which beareth thorns and briers is rejected, and is nigh unto cursing; whose end is to be burned — the unfruitful professor, producing nothing but deceit and selfishness, is near that awful state, and everlasting misery is the end reserved for him; let us watch with humble caution and prayer. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

9 COMMENTARY: But, beloved, we are persuaded better things of you, and things that accompany salvation, though we thus speak — there are things never separated from salvation, which show a person to be in a state of salvation and will end in eternal salvation, better things than ever any dissembler or apostate enjoyed. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

10 COMMENTARY: For God is not unrighteous to forget your work and labour of love, which ye have shewed toward his name, in that ye have ministered to the saints, and do minister — works of love done for Christ's glory, or to his saints for Christ's sake, are evident marks of salvation, and more sure tokens of saving grace than enlightenings and tastings; no love counts as love but working love, and no works are right works which flow not from love to Christ. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

11 COMMENTARY: And we desire that every one of you do shew the same diligence to the full assurance of hope unto the end — hope here meant is a sure looking for good things promised, with love, desire, and valuing of them, and it has its degrees, as faith also. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

12 COMMENTARY: That ye be not slothful, but followers of them who through faith and patience inherit the promises — diligence and imitation of faithful, patient inheritors guard against sloth. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

13 COMMENTARY: For when God made promise to Abraham, because he could swear by no greater, he sware by himself — God's promise of blessedness to believers comes from his eternal purpose, settled between the eternal Father, Son, and Spirit. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

14 COMMENTARY: Saying, Surely blessing I will bless thee, and multiplying I will multiply thee — the promise to Abraham is the pattern of God's certain blessing to his people. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

15 COMMENTARY: And so, after he had patiently endured, he obtained the promise — patient endurance obtained what was promised, modelling the perseverance urged on the Hebrews. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

16 COMMENTARY: For men verily swear by the greater: and an oath for confirmation is to them an end of all strife — human oaths settle strife by appeal to a greater; how much more God's oath. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

17 COMMENTARY: Wherein God, willing more abundantly to shew unto the heirs of promise the immutability of his counsel, confirmed it by an oath — here we have two things which cannot change, the counsel and the oath of God, in which it is impossible for God to lie. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

18 COMMENTARY: That by two immutable things, in which it was impossible for God to lie, we might have a strong consolation, who have fled for refuge to lay hold upon the hope set before us — the consolations of God are strong enough to support his people under their heaviest trials, and here is a refuge for all sinners who flee to God's mercy through Christ's redemption, laying aside all other confidences. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

19 COMMENTARY: Which hope we have as an anchor of the soul, both sure and stedfast, and which entereth into that within the veil — we are in this world as a ship at sea, tossed up and down and in danger of being cast away; gospel hope is our anchor in the storms, sure and stedfast, grounded on God's free grace, Christ's merits and mediation, and the Spirit's influences. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

20 COMMENTARY: Whither the forerunner is for us entered, even Jesus, made an high priest for ever after the order of Melchisedec — Christ, the object and ground of the believer's hope, has entered within the veil as forerunner, so let us set our affections on things above and wait patiently for his appearance, when we shall certainly appear with him in glory. — Matthew Henry (Concise)
