Study Notes — Hebrews

Chapter 13

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Let brotherly love continue — the design of Christ in giving himself for us is to purchase a peculiar people, zealous of good works, and true religion is the strongest bond of friendship, so brotherly love must continue. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

2 COMMENTARY: Be not forgetful to entertain strangers: for thereby some have entertained angels unawares — hospitality is urged as a Christian duty, and some, entertaining strangers, have entertained angels unawares. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

3 COMMENTARY: Remember them that are in bonds, as bound with them; and them which suffer adversity, as being yourselves also in the body — remember prisoners as if bound with them, and sufferers as being yourselves also in the body, for true religion binds us to share their lot. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

4 COMMENTARY: Marriage is honourable in all, and the bed undefiled: but whoremongers and adulterers God will judge — marriage is honourable in all and the bed undefiled, but whoremongers and adulterers God will judge. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

5 COMMENTARY: Let your conversation be without covetousness; and be content with such things as ye have: for he hath said, I will never leave thee, nor forsake thee — the sin opposed to contentment is covetousness, an over-eager desire for this world's wealth with envy of those who have more; having treasures in heaven, we may be content with mean things here, and this promise contains the sum of all promises, confirmed in the original by no less than five negatives: the true believer shall have God's gracious presence in life, at death, and for ever. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

6 COMMENTARY: So that we may boldly say, The Lord is my helper, and I will not fear what man shall do unto me — so we may boldly say the Lord is our helper and not fear what man shall do, for men can do nothing against God, and God can make all men do against his people turn to their good. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

7 COMMENTARY: Remember them which have the rule over you, who have spoken unto you the word of God: whose faith follow, considering the end of their conversation — the instructions and examples of ministers who honourably and comfortably closed their testimony should be particularly remembered by survivors, and their faith followed, considering the end of their conduct. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

8 COMMENTARY: Jesus Christ the same yesterday, and to day, and for ever — though ministers die, the great Head and High Priest of the church ever lives and is ever the same: Christ is the same in the Old Testament day as in the gospel day, and will be so to his people for ever, equally merciful, powerful, and all-sufficient. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

9 COMMENTARY: Be not carried about with divers and strange doctrines. For it is a good thing that the heart be established with grace; not with meats, which have not profited them that have been occupied therein — believers should seek hearts established in simple dependence on free grace by the Holy Spirit, which comforts hearts and renders them proof against delusion, rather than being carried about by strange doctrines or meats which never profited their occupiers. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

10 COMMENTARY: We have an altar, whereof they have no right to eat which serve the tabernacle — we have an altar of which those serving the tabernacle have no right to eat; Christ is both our Altar and our Sacrifice, and he sanctifies the gift. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

11 COMMENTARY: For the bodies of those beasts, whose blood is brought into the sanctuary by the high priest for sin, are burned without the camp — the bodies of beasts whose blood was brought into the sanctuary for sin were burned without the camp, under the Levitical law. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

12 COMMENTARY: Wherefore Jesus also, that he might sanctify the people with his own blood, suffered without the gate — wherefore Jesus also, that he might sanctify the people with his own blood, suffered without the gate, outside the camp. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

13 COMMENTARY: Let us go forth therefore unto him without the camp, bearing his reproach — let us go forth therefore to him without the camp, bearing his reproach: go forth from the ceremonial law, from sin, from the world, and from ourselves, and living by faith in Christ, set apart to God through his blood, willingly separate from this evil world. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

14 COMMENTARY: For here have we no continuing city, but we seek one to come — for here we have no continuing city, but seek one to come; sin, sinners, nor death will suffer us to continue long here, so let us go forth now by faith and seek in Christ the rest and peace this world cannot afford. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

15 COMMENTARY: By him therefore let us offer the sacrifice of praise to God continually, that is, the fruit of our lips giving thanks to his name — by him let us offer the sacrifice of praise to God continually, the fruit of lips giving thanks to his name; the sacrifice of praise, including worship, prayer, and thanksgiving, we should offer always, bringing our sacrifices to this altar and High Priest. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

16 COMMENTARY: But to do good and to communicate forget not: for with such sacrifices God is well pleased — but to do good and to communicate forget not, for with such sacrifices God is well pleased; we must, according to our power, give to the necessities of men's souls and bodies, and God will accept these offerings with pleasure and bless the offerers through Christ. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

17 COMMENTARY: Obey them that have the rule over you, and submit yourselves: for they watch for your souls, as they that must give account, that they may do it with joy, and not with grief: for that is unprofitable for you — obey and submit to living ministers, so far as agreeable to God's mind and will made known in his word; Christians must not think themselves too wise, too good, or too great to learn, but search the Scriptures and receive teaching according to that rule as God's word; it is the hearers' interest that their ministers' account of them be with joy and not grief, for faithful ministers deliver their own souls, but a fruitless people's ruin will be on their own heads. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

18 COMMENTARY: Pray for us: for we trust we have a good conscience, in all things willing to live honestly — pray for us, for we trust we have a good conscience, willing in all things to live honestly; a good conscience has respect to all God's commands and all our duty, yet those who have it still need others' prayers, and the more earnestly people pray for their ministers, the more benefit they may expect from their ministry. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

19 COMMENTARY: But I beseech you the rather to do this, that I may be restored to you the sooner — I beseech you the rather to do this, that I may be restored to you the sooner; when ministers come to a praying people, they come with greater satisfaction and success. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

20 COMMENTARY: Now the God of peace, that brought again from the dead our Lord Jesus, that great shepherd of the sheep, through the blood of the everlasting covenant — now the God of peace, fully reconciled to believers, who made a way for peace between himself and sinners and loves peace on earth, especially in his churches, brought again from the dead our Lord Jesus, the great Shepherd of the sheep, through the blood of the everlasting covenant; how firm a covenant whose foundation is the Son of God's blood! — Matthew Henry (Concise)

21 COMMENTARY: Make you perfect in every good work to do his will, working in you that which is wellpleasing in his sight, through Jesus Christ; to whom be glory for ever and ever. Amen — may he make you perfect in every good work to do his will, working in you what is well-pleasing in his sight through Jesus Christ; the perfecting of saints in every good work is the great thing desired for them, that they may at length be fitted for heaven's employment and happiness, and there is no good thing wrought in us but it is God's work, and no good thing wrought in us by God but through Christ, for his sake and by his Spirit. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

22 COMMENTARY: And I beseech you, brethren, suffer the word of exhortation: for I have written a letter unto you in few words — I beseech you, brethren, suffer the word of exhortation, for I have written in few words; so bad are men, even believers, through remainders of corruption, that when the most important, comfortable doctrine is delivered for their own good with convincing evidence, there is need of earnest entreaty that they would bear it and not fall out with it, neglect it, or reject it. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

23 COMMENTARY: Know ye that our brother Timothy is set at liberty; with whom, if he come shortly, I will see you — know that our brother Timothy is set at liberty, with whom, if he comes shortly, the writer will see them. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

24 COMMENTARY: Salute all them that have the rule over you, and all the saints. They of Italy salute you — salute all who have rule over you and all the saints; they of Italy salute you; it is good to have the law of holy love and kindness written in Christians' hearts toward one another, for religion teaches true civility and is not ill-tempered or discourteous. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

25 COMMENTARY: Grace be with you all. Amen — grace be with you all; let God's favour be toward you, and his grace continually working in and with you, bringing forth fruits of holiness as first-fruits of glory. — Matthew Henry (Concise)
