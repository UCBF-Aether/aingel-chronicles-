Study Notes — Hebrews

Chapter 7

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: For this Melchisedec, king of Salem, priest of the most high God, who met Abraham returning from the slaughter of the kings, and blessed him — Melchizedek met Abraham returning from the rescue of Lot, and as king of Salem, "Peace," he typified Christ, the Prince of Peace, the great Reconciler of God and man. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

2 COMMENTARY: To whom also Abraham gave a tenth part of all; first being by interpretation King of righteousness, and after that also King of Salem, which is, King of peace — his name, "King of Righteousness," marked him as a type of the Messiah and his kingdom. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

3 COMMENTARY: Without father, without mother, without descent, having neither beginning of days, nor end of life; but made like unto the Son of God; abideth a priest continually — nothing is recorded of the beginning or end of his life, so he typically resembled the Son of God, whose existence is from everlasting to everlasting, with no successor in his priesthood. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

4 COMMENTARY: Now consider how great this man was, unto whom even the patriarch Abraham gave the tenth of the spoils — Abraham's gift shows Melchizedek's greatness, and the High Priest of whom he was a type must be much superior to the Levitical priests. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

5 COMMENTARY: And verily they that are of the sons of Levi, who receive the office of the priesthood, have a commandment to take tithes of the people according to the law, that is, of their brethren, though they come out of the loins of Abraham — the Levites took tithes by law from their own brethren. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

6 COMMENTARY: But he whose descent is not counted from them received tithes of Abraham, and blessed him that had the promises — Melchizedek, outside the Levitical descent, received tithes from Abraham and blessed him who had the promises; that man is rich and happy indeed who has the promises, of this life and of that to come. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

7 COMMENTARY: And without all contradiction the less is blessed of the better — the blessing proves the superiority: the less is blessed by the better. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

8 COMMENTARY: And here men that die receive tithes; but there he receiveth them, of whom it is witnessed that he liveth — Levitical tithe-takers are dying men, but Melchizedek is witnessed as living, a type of the ever-living Priest. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

9 COMMENTARY: And as I may so say, Levi also, who receiveth tithes, payed tithes in Abraham — Levi himself, in Abraham, paid tithes to Melchizedek. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

10 COMMENTARY: For he was yet in the loins of his father, when Melchisedec met him — Levi was still in Abraham's loins when Melchizedek met him, so the Levitical priesthood itself acknowledged Melchizedek's superiority. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

11 COMMENTARY: If therefore perfection were by the Levitical priesthood, (for under it the people received the law,) what further need was there that another priest should rise after the order of Melchisedec, and not be called after the order of Aaron? — the priesthood and law by which perfection could not come are done away; a Priest is risen, and a dispensation now set up, by which true believers may be made perfect. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

12 COMMENTARY: For the priesthood being changed, there is made of necessity a change also of the law — a changed priesthood necessarily brings a changed law. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

13 COMMENTARY: For he of whom these things are spoken pertaineth to another tribe, of which no man gave attendance at the altar — Christ pertains to a tribe, Judah, from which no man served at the altar, proving his priesthood is of a different order. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

14 COMMENTARY: For it is evident that our Lord sprang out of Juda; of which tribe Moses spake nothing concerning priesthood — our Lord's descent from Judah, a tribe Moses never joined to priesthood, confirms the change. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

15 COMMENTARY: And it is yet far more evident: for that after the similitude of Melchisedec there ariseth another priest — it is still more evident, since another Priest arises after Melchizedek's similitude, not Aaron's. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

16 COMMENTARY: Who is made, not after the law of a carnal commandment, but after the power of an endless life — the law that made Levitical priests showed them frail, dying creatures, unable to save their own lives, much less others' souls; but our High Priest holds his office by the power of endless life in himself. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

17 COMMENTARY: For he testifieth, Thou art a priest for ever after the order of Melchisedec — the testimony confirms an everlasting priesthood. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

18 COMMENTARY: For there is verily a disannulling of the commandment going before for the weakness and unprofitableness thereof — the former commandment is disannulled for its weakness and unprofitableness; it could not perfect anyone. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

19 COMMENTARY: For the law made nothing perfect, but the bringing in of a better hope did; by the which we draw nigh unto God — by the better hope we now draw nigh to God, into clearer light, more perfect liberty, and more abundant privileges than the Sinai covenant gave. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

20 COMMENTARY: And inasmuch as not without an oath he was made priest — Christ was made priest with an oath, unlike the Levitical priests. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

21 COMMENTARY: (For those priests were made without an oath; but this with an oath by him that said unto him, The Lord sware and will not repent, Thou art a priest for ever after the order of Melchisedec:) — the oath, "The Lord sware and will not repent," secures Christ's priesthood for ever. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

22 COMMENTARY: By so much was Jesus made a surety of a better testament — Jesus is Surety of a better covenant, distinguished from the Sinai covenant and the legal dispensation under which the church so long remained. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

23 COMMENTARY: And they truly were many priests, because they were not suffered to continue by reason of death — in Aaron's order there was a multitude of priests, high priests one after another, because death ended each one's service. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

24 COMMENTARY: But this man, because he continueth ever, hath an unchangeable priesthood — in Christ's priesthood there is only one and the same Priest, ever continuing, so his priesthood is unchangeable. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

25 COMMENTARY: Wherefore he is able also to save them to the uttermost that come unto God by him, seeing he ever liveth to make intercession for them — this is the believer's safety and happiness: the everlasting High Priest is able to save to the uttermost, in all times and in all cases, because he ever lives to intercede. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

26 COMMENTARY: For such an high priest became us, who is holy, harmless, undefiled, separate from sinners, and made higher than the heavens — observe Christ's personal holiness: free from all habits or principles of sin, with not the least disposition to it in his nature, harmless, undefiled, separate from sinners, and exalted above the heavens. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

27 COMMENTARY: Who needeth not daily, as those high priests, to offer up sacrifice, first for his own sins, and then for the people's: for this he did once, when he offered up himself — unlike priests who offered daily, first for themselves and then for the people, Christ offered himself once, and that once suffices. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

28 COMMENTARY: For the law maketh men high priests which have infirmity; but the word of the oath, which was since the law, maketh the Son, who is consecrated for evermore — the law made infirm men priests, but the word of the oath makes the Son, consecrated for evermore; so none who come to God in his beloved Son's name need be dismayed, for he will deliver them in trial, prosperity, death, and judgment. — Matthew Henry (Concise)
