Study Notes — Hebrews

Chapter 1

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: God spoke to his ancient people at sundry times and in divers manners, through successive generations — sometimes by personal direction, sometimes by dreams, visions, or Divine influence on the prophets' minds. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

2 COMMENTARY: The gospel revelation is excellent above the former, because God has now spoken by his Son, whom he appointed heir of all things and by whom he made the worlds. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

3 COMMENTARY: In beholding the power, wisdom, and goodness of Christ we behold the Father's, for the fulness of the Godhead dwells really, not merely typically, in him; having by himself purged our sins, he sat down at the right hand of the Majesty on high. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

4 COMMENTARY: The Son is made so much better than the angels, having by inheritance obtained a more excellent name than they — the name of Son and Messiah, anointed above all. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

5 COMMENTARY: No angel was ever addressed as God addresses the Son: "Thou art my Son, this day have I begotten thee," and "I will be to him a Father, and he shall be to me a Son." — Matthew Henry (Concise)

6 COMMENTARY: When God brings his firstbegotten into the world, he commands all the angels of God to worship him — proof that the Son is no mere angel, but the object of their worship. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

7 COMMENTARY: Of the angels God says only that he makes them spirits and ministers, winds and flames of fire — servants sent to do his pleasure, not sons on a throne. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

8 COMMENTARY: But to the Son he says, "Thy throne, O God, is for ever and ever," with a sceptre of righteousness — the Son is owned and honoured as God himself. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

9 COMMENTARY: The Son loved righteousness and hated iniquity, and therefore God anointed him with the oil of gladness above his fellows — above all prophets, priests, and kings ever employed in God's service. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

10 COMMENTARY: The Son is addressed as Lord who laid the foundation of the earth, and the heavens are the work of his hands — Creator, not creature. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

11 COMMENTARY: Heaven and earth shall perish, but the Son remains; they shall wax old as a garment, while he endures unchanged. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

12 COMMENTARY: As a sovereign folds up garments of state and remains a sovereign still, so Christ shall fold up earth and heaven like a vesture and be still the same. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

13 COMMENTARY: To no angel did God ever say, "Sit on my right hand, until I make thine enemies thy footstool" — that seat of honour belongs to the Son alone, whose enemies shall submit or be destroyed. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

14 COMMENTARY: The most exalted angels are but ministering spirits, sent forth to minister for them who shall be heirs of salvation — protecting, instructing, and comforting the saints under Christ. — Matthew Henry (Concise)
