Study Notes — Hebrews

Chapter 5

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: For every high priest taken from among men is ordained for men in things pertaining to God, that he may offer both gifts and sacrifices for sins — the High Priest must be a man, a partaker of our nature, because sinful man cannot come to God alone. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

2 COMMENTARY: Who can have compassion on the ignorant, and on them that are out of the way; for that he himself also is compassed with infirmity — a priest who knows infirmity can intercede with tenderness for those out of the way of truth, duty, and happiness, and lead them back from by-paths of error, sin, and misery. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

3 COMMENTARY: And by reason hereof he ought, as for the people, so also for himself, to offer for sins — the Aaronic priest's own infirmity required offerings for himself as well as for the people. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

4 COMMENTARY: And no man taketh this honour unto himself, but he that is called of God, as was Aaron — only those called of God can expect his assistance, acceptance, and blessing on their services. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

5 COMMENTARY: So also Christ glorified not himself to be made an high priest; but he that said unto him, Thou art my Son, to day have I begotten thee — Christ's priesthood, like Aaron's, rests on God's call, not on self-appointment. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

6 COMMENTARY: As he saith also in another place, Thou art a priest for ever after the order of Melchisedec — Christ's priesthood is not Aaronic but Melchizedekian, and it is for ever. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

7 COMMENTARY: Who in the days of his flesh, when he had offered up prayers and supplications with strong crying and tears unto him that was able to save him from death, and was heard in that he feared — Christ set an example not only to pray, but to be fervent in prayer; how many dry prayers, how few wetted with tears, do we offer up to God! — Matthew Henry (Concise)

8 COMMENTARY: Though he were a Son, yet learned he obedience by the things which he suffered — Christ, in the days of his flesh a tempted, suffering, dying Jesus, learned humble obedience to God's will by affliction, and we need affliction to teach us submission. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

9 COMMENTARY: And being made perfect, he became the author of eternal salvation unto all them that obey him — raised and exalted, he was given power to save to the uttermost all sinners who come unto God through him; but are we of that number? — Matthew Henry (Concise)

10 COMMENTARY: Called of God an high priest after the order of Melchisedec — God himself confirms Christ in this everlasting priesthood. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

11 COMMENTARY: Of whom we have many things to say, and hard to be uttered, seeing ye are dull of hearing — dull hearers make the preaching of the gospel difficult, and even those who have some faith may be dull hearers and slow to believe. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

12 COMMENTARY: For when for the time ye ought to be teachers, ye have need that one teach you again which be the first principles of the oracles of God; and are become such as have need of milk, and not of strong meat — much is looked for from those to whom much is given, and long-standing Christians still needing first principles are reproved. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

13 COMMENTARY: For every one that useth milk is unskilful in the word of righteousness: for he is a babe — to be unskilful denotes want of experience in the things of the gospel. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

14 COMMENTARY: But strong meat belongeth to them that are of full age, even those who by reason of use have their senses exercised to discern both good and evil — Christian experience is a spiritual sense, taste, or relish of the goodness and excellence of gospel truths, and no tongue can express the soul's satisfaction in a sense of Divine goodness in Christ. — Matthew Henry (Concise)
