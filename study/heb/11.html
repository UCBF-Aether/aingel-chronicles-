Study Notes — Hebrews

Chapter 11

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Now faith is the substance of things hoped for, the evidence of things not seen — faith is a firm persuasion and expectation that God will perform all he has promised in Christ, giving the soul present enjoyment and subsistence of hoped-for things, and proving to the mind the reality of things unseen by the bodily eye. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

2 COMMENTARY: For by it the elders obtained a good report — by faith the elders obtained a good report, an honourable character in God's word; faith was the principle of their holy obedience, remarkable services, and patient sufferings. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

3 COMMENTARY: Through faith we understand that the worlds were framed by the word of God, so that things which are seen were not made of things which do appear — the Bible gives the most true and exact account of the origin of all things, and all we see of creation was brought into being by God's command; we are to believe it, not wrest it to suit men's fancies. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

4 COMMENTARY: By faith Abel offered unto God a more excellent sacrifice than Cain, by which he obtained witness that he was righteous, God testifying of his gifts: and by it he being dead yet speaketh — Abel brought a sacrifice of atonement from the firstlings, acknowledging himself a sinner deserving death, hoping for mercy only through the great Sacrifice; Cain's proud rage against the accepted worshipper led to persecution and murder of believers in every age, yet Abel, being dead, yet speaks by his instructive example. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

5 COMMENTARY: By faith Enoch was translated that he should not see death; and was not found, because God had translated him: for before his translation he had this testimony, that he pleased God — Enoch was translated, removed that he should not see death, as God took him into heaven, as Christ will do the saints alive at his second coming. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

6 COMMENTARY: But without faith it is impossible to please him: for he that cometh to God must believe that he is, and that he is a rewarder of them that diligently seek him — we cannot come to God unless we believe he is what Scripture reveals him to be, and those who would find God must seek him with all their heart. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

7 COMMENTARY: By faith Noah, being warned of God of things not seen as yet, moved with fear, prepared an ark to the saving of his house; by the which he condemned the world, and became heir of the righteousness which is by faith — Noah's faith influenced his practice and moved him to prepare an ark; his faith condemned others' unbelief and his obedience their contempt and rebellion, showing how believers, warned to flee wrath to come, take refuge in Christ and become heirs of the righteousness of faith. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

8 COMMENTARY: By faith Abraham, when he was called to go out into a place which he should after receive for an inheritance, obeyed; and he went out, not knowing whither he went — we are often called to leave worldly connections, interests, and comforts; if heirs of Abraham's faith, we shall obey and go forth not knowing what may befall us, found in the way of duty, looking for God's promises. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

9 COMMENTARY: By faith he sojourned in the land of promise, as in a strange country, dwelling in tabernacles with Isaac and Jacob, the heirs with him of the same promise — Abraham sojourned as in a strange country, dwelling in tents with Isaac and Jacob, heirs of the same promise, as pilgrims travelling toward their heavenly home. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

10 COMMENTARY: For he looked for a city which hath foundations, whose builder and maker is God — he looked for a city with foundations, whose builder and maker is God, laying hold of blessings at a great distance and rejoicing in them though a stranger. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

11 COMMENTARY: Through faith also Sara herself received strength to conceive seed, and was delivered of a child when she was past age, because she judged him faithful who had promised — Sarah received the promise as God's promise, convinced he both could and would perform it. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

12 COMMENTARY: Therefore sprang there even of one, and him as good as dead, so many as the stars of the sky in multitude, and as the sand which is by the sea shore innumerable — from one as good as dead sprang a multitude like stars and sand, showing God's faithfulness to his promise. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

13 COMMENTARY: These all died in faith, not having received the promises, but having seen them afar off, and were persuaded of them, and embraced them, and confessed that they were strangers and pilgrims on the earth — many who have a part in the promises do not soon receive the things promised, yet faith makes them present, loves and rejoices in them, and by faith they overcome death's terrors and bid a cheerful farewell to this world. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

14 COMMENTARY: For they that say such things declare plainly that they seek a country — by confessing themselves strangers and pilgrims, they declared plainly that they seek a country. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

15 COMMENTARY: And truly, if they had been mindful of that country from whence they came out, they might have had opportunity to have returned — those once truly and savingly called out of a sinful state have no mind to return into it. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

16 COMMENTARY: But now they desire a better country, that is, an heavenly: wherefore God is not ashamed to be called their God: for he hath prepared for them a city — all true believers desire the heavenly inheritance, and the stronger faith is, the more fervent those desires; notwithstanding their meanness by nature and vileness by sin, God is not ashamed to be called their God, such is his mercy and love to them. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

17 COMMENTARY: By faith Abraham, when he was tried, offered up Isaac: and he that had received the promises offered up his only begotten son — the greatest trial and act of faith on record is Abraham's offering of Isaac, where every word shows a trial. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

18 COMMENTARY: Of whom it was said, That in Isaac shall thy seed be called — the promise itself, that in Isaac his seed would be called, sharpened the trial, for obedience seemed to destroy the promise. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

19 COMMENTARY: Accounting that God was able to raise him up, even from the dead; from whence also he received him in a figure — Abraham reasoned down doubt and fear by looking to God's almighty power, accounting him able to raise Isaac even from the dead, and received him back in a figure; the best way to enjoy our comforts is to give them up to God, who will then give them again as shall be best for us. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

20 COMMENTARY: By faith Isaac blessed Jacob and Esau concerning things to come — Isaac blessed Jacob and Esau concerning things to come, for things present are not the best things, and no man knows love or hatred by having or wanting them. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

21 COMMENTARY: By faith Jacob, when he was a dying, blessed both the sons of Joseph; and worshipped, leaning upon the top of his staff — Jacob lived by faith and died by faith; faith has a great work at last, to help the believer die to the Lord, honouring him by patience, hope, and joy. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

22 COMMENTARY: By faith Joseph, when he died, made mention of the departing of the children of Israel; and gave commandment concerning his bones — Joseph, tried by temptation, persecution, and by honours and power in Pharaoh's court, yet carried through by faith, looked beyond Egypt to Israel's departure and gave commandment concerning his bones. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

23 COMMENTARY: By faith Moses, when he was born, was hid three months of his parents, because they saw he was a proper child; and they were not afraid of the king's commandment — in Moses' parents' faith there was a mixture of unbelief, but God was pleased to overlook it; it is a great mercy to be free from wicked laws, but when we are not, we must use all lawful means for our security. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

24 COMMENTARY: By faith Moses, when he was come to years, refused to be called the son of Pharaoh's daughter — faith gives strength against the sinful, slavish fear of men, setting God before the soul and showing the vanity of the creature. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

25 COMMENTARY: Choosing rather to suffer affliction with the people of God, than to enjoy the pleasures of sin for a season — the pleasures of sin are but short, ending either in speedy repentance or speedy ruin, and suffering is to be chosen rather than sin, for there is more evil in the least sin than in the greatest suffering. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

26 COMMENTARY: Esteeming the reproach of Christ greater riches than the treasures in Egypt: for he had respect unto the recompence of the reward — God's people are and always have been a reproached people, and Christ accounts himself reproached in their reproaches, so that reproach becomes greater riches than the treasures of the richest empire; believers may and ought to have respect to the recompense of reward. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

27 COMMENTARY: By faith he forsook Egypt, not fearing the wrath of the king: for he endured, as seeing him who is invisible — by faith we may be fully sure of God's providence and gracious, powerful presence, and such a sight of God enables believers to keep on to the end whatever they meet. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

28 COMMENTARY: Through faith he kept the passover, and the sprinkling of blood, lest he that destroyed the firstborn should touch them — it is not owing to our own righteousness or best performances that we are saved from God's wrath, but to Christ's blood and imputed righteousness; true faith makes sin bitter even while receiving pardon and atonement. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

29 COMMENTARY: By faith they passed through the Red sea as by dry land: which the Egyptians assaying to do were drowned — by faith Israel passed through the Red Sea as by dry land, while the Egyptians attempting it were drowned; all our spiritual privileges on earth should quicken us on our way to heaven. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

30 COMMENTARY: By faith the walls of Jericho fell down, after they were compassed about seven days — the Lord will make even Babylon fall before his people's faith, and when he has some great thing to do for them, he raises up great and strong faith in them. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

31 COMMENTARY: By faith the harlot Rahab perished not with them that believed not, when she had received the spies with peace — a true believer desires not only covenant with God but communion with his people, and is willing to fare as they fare; by her works Rahab declared herself just, though she was not justified by her works, since her work was faulty in manner and not perfectly good, and could not answer God's perfect justice. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

32 COMMENTARY: And what shall I more say? for the time would fail me to tell of Gedeon, and of Barak, and of Samson, and of Jephthae; of David also, and Samuel, and of the prophets — after all our searches into Scripture there is more to be learned; we should be pleased at how great the number and how strong the faith of Old Testament believers, though the objects of faith were not then so fully made known as now. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

33 COMMENTARY: Who through faith subdued kingdoms, wrought righteousness, obtained promises, stopped the mouths of lions — it is the excellence of faith that, while it helps men do great things like Gideon, it keeps them from high thoughts of themselves; by faith God's servants shall overcome even the roaring lion seeking whom he may devour. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

34 COMMENTARY: Quenched the violence of fire, escaped the edge of the sword, out of weakness were made strong, waxed valiant in fight, turned to flight the armies of the aliens — faith like Barak's has recourse to God in all dangers and then makes grateful returns for all mercies; the believer's faith endures to the end and, in dying, gives victory over death like Samson. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

35 COMMENTARY: Women received their dead raised to life again: and others were tortured, not accepting deliverance; that they might obtain a better resurrection — by faith we have comfort of the promises and are prepared to wait for them; though we do not hope for our dead restored in this world, faith supports us under loss and directs us to hope of a better resurrection. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

36 COMMENTARY: And others had trial of cruel mockings and scourgings, yea, moreover of bonds and imprisonment — few ever met greater trials or showed more lively faith than David, whose Psalms testify to the trials and acts of faith and have ever been of great value to God's people. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

37 COMMENTARY: They were stoned, they were sawn asunder, were tempted, were slain with the sword: they wandered about in sheepskins and goatskins; being destitute, afflicted, tormented — the grace of God often fixes upon very undeserving persons to do great things for them and by them, and faith puts men upon acknowledging God in all their ways, as Jephthah, making them bold and courageous in a good cause. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

38 COMMENTARY: (Of whom the world was not worthy:) they wandered in deserts, and in mountains, and in dens and caves of the earth — what a difference between God's judgment of a saint and man's! The world is not worthy of those scorned, persecuted saints whom persecutors reckon unworthy to live; they are not worthy of their company, example, or counsel, for they know not what a saint is nor how to use him, and hate and drive away such as they do Christ's offer of grace. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

39 COMMENTARY: And these all, having obtained a good report through faith, received not the promise — these all, having obtained a good report through faith, received not the promise, and the world considers the righteous unworthy to live, while God declares the world unworthy of them. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

40 COMMENTARY: God having provided some better thing for us, that they without us should not be made perfect — God provided some better thing for us, so that they without us should not be made perfect; as our advantages are so much beyond theirs, so should our obedience of faith, patience of hope, and labour of love be greater, and unless we get true faith as they had, they will rise up to condemn us at the last day. — Matthew Henry (Concise)
