Study Notes — Hebrews

Chapter 3

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Holy brethren, partakers of the heavenly calling, are to consider Christ Jesus — the Apostle of our profession, the Messenger sent by God, the great Revealer of the faith and hope we profess. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

2 COMMENTARY: Christ was faithful to him that appointed him, as Moses was faithful in all his house — yet Moses' faithfulness was but a type of Christ's. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

3 COMMENTARY: Christ is counted worthy of more glory than Moses, inasmuch as he who built the house has more honour than the house itself: Christ is Master and Maker of his church, not merely a servant in it. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

4 COMMENTARY: Every house is built by some man, but he that built all things is God — so the church, as God's house, belongs to Christ its builder and Owner. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

5 COMMENTARY: Moses verily was faithful in all his house, as a servant, for a testimony of those things which were to be spoken after — a faithful servant, pointing forward to Christ. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

6 COMMENTARY: But Christ as a Son over his own house — whose house are we, if we hold fast the confidence and the rejoicing of the hope firm unto the end; setting out well is not enough without stedfast perseverance. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

7 COMMENTARY: Wherefore, as the Holy Ghost says, "To day if ye will hear his voice" — the Spirit's warning comes with present urgency, for to-morrow is not ours. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

8 COMMENTARY: Harden not your hearts, as in the provocation, in the day of temptation in the wilderness — hardening of heart is the spring of all other sins, and days of temptation become days of provocation when we depend wholly on God yet rebel. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

9 COMMENTARY: Your fathers tempted me, proved me, and saw my works forty years — long privilege did not cure unbelief; the sins of others, especially of our relations, should be warnings to us. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

10 COMMENTARY: Wherefore I was grieved with that generation, and said, They do alway err in their heart, and they have not known my ways — sin, especially by God's professing people, not only provokes God but grieves him, though he waits long to be gracious. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

11 COMMENTARY: So I sware in my wrath, They shall not enter into my rest — sin long persisted in will at last make God's wrath discover itself in destroying the impenitent, and there is no resting under the wrath of God. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

12 COMMENTARY: Take heed, brethren, lest there be in any of you an evil heart of unbelief, in departing from the living God — all who would get safe to heaven must look about them, for distrust soon leads to desertion. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

13 COMMENTARY: But exhort one another daily, while it is called To day, lest any of you be hardened through the deceitfulness of sin — none, even the strongest, needs no help from other Christians, and sin appears fair but is vile, promising much and performing nothing. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

14 COMMENTARY: For we are made partakers of Christ, if we hold the beginning of our confidence stedfast unto the end — perseverance in faith is the best evidence of the sincerity of our faith. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

15 COMMENTARY: While it is said, To day if ye will hear his voice, harden not your hearts, as in the provocation — hearing the word often is a means of salvation, yet if not hearkened to it exposes us more to Divine wrath. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

16 COMMENTARY: For some, when they had heard, did provoke: howbeit not all that came out of Egypt by Moses — outward privilege did not save the provokers; we must pray to be numbered with true believers. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

17 COMMENTARY: But with whom was he grieved forty years? Was it not with them that had sinned, whose carcases fell in the wilderness? — unbelief left a whole generation short of the promised land. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

18 COMMENTARY: And to whom sware he that they should not enter into his rest, but to them that believed not? — their exclusion was the fruit of unbelief, not of any failure in God's promise. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

19 COMMENTARY: So we see that they could not enter in because of unbelief — as our obedience follows according to the power of our faith, so our sins and want of care follow according to the prevailing of unbelief in us. — Matthew Henry (Concise)
