Study Notes — Hebrews

Chapter 10

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: For the law having a shadow of good things to come, and not the very image of the things, can never with those sacrifices which they offered year by year continually make the comers thereunto perfect — the tabernacle and Sinai ordinances were only emblems and types of the gospel, and sacrifices offered continually could not make worshippers perfect in pardon and purified conscience. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

2 COMMENTARY: For then would they not have ceased to be offered? because that the worshippers once purged should have had no more conscience of sins — if the sacrifices had perfected, they would have ceased, for purged worshippers would have no more conscience of sins. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

3 COMMENTARY: But in those sacrifices there is a remembrance again made of sins every year — instead, those sacrifices made a remembrance of sins every year, proving they did not take them away. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

4 COMMENTARY: For it is not possible that the blood of bulls and of goats should take away sins — it is not possible that bulls' and goats' blood should take away sins. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

5 COMMENTARY: Wherefore when he cometh into the world, he saith, Sacrifice and offering thou wouldest not, but a body hast thou prepared me — when Christ came into the world, he declared that God would not have animal sacrifice and offering, but had prepared him a body for the true sacrifice. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

6 COMMENTARY: In burnt offerings and sacrifices for sin thou hast had no pleasure — burnt offerings and sin sacrifices gave God no pleasure as ends in themselves. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

7 COMMENTARY: Then said I, Lo, I come (in the volume of the book it is written of me,) to do thy will, O God — Christ came, as written of him in the volume of the book, to do God's will; the fountain of all he has done for his people is God's sovereign will and grace. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

8 COMMENTARY: Above when he said, Sacrifice and offering and burnt offerings and offering for sin thou wouldest not, neither hadst pleasure therein; which are offered by the law — he first sets aside the legal sacrifices, in which God had no pleasure. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

9 COMMENTARY: Then said he, Lo, I come to do thy will, O God. He taketh away the first, that he may establish the second — then he establishes the second: he takes away the first covenant's sacrifices to establish God's will as the ground of salvation. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

10 COMMENTARY: By the which will we are sanctified through the offering of the body of Jesus Christ once for all — by that will we are sanctified through the offering of Christ's body once for all; the atoning sacrifice had to be one capable of consenting, placing himself of his own will in the sinner's stead, and Christ did so, his free-will sufferings being of infinite value because the Sufferer is of infinite worth. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

11 COMMENTARY: And every priest standeth daily ministering and offering oftentimes the same sacrifices, which can never take away sins — every priest stood daily offering the same sacrifices, which can never take away sins. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

12 COMMENTARY: But this man, after he had offered one sacrifice for sins for ever, sat down on the right hand of God — but this Man, after offering one sacrifice for sins for ever, sat down at God's right hand, his work finished. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

13 COMMENTARY: From henceforth expecting till his enemies be made his footstool — from henceforth he expects till his enemies are made his footstool. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

14 COMMENTARY: For by one offering he hath perfected for ever them that are sanctified — by one offering he has perfected for ever those who are sanctified; the righteousness brought in and the sacrifice once offered are of eternal power, and his salvation shall never be done away. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

15 COMMENTARY: Whereof the Holy Ghost also is a witness to us: for after that he had said before — the Holy Ghost also witnesses to this new covenant. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

16 COMMENTARY: This is the covenant that I will make with them after those days, saith the Lord, I will put my laws into their hearts, and in their minds will I write them — the covenant promise is the law written in hearts and minds, so that by it we may know we are justified. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

17 COMMENTARY: And their sins and iniquities will I remember no more — and their sins and iniquities God will remember no more; under the new covenant full and final pardon is to be had, a vast difference from the old, where sacrifices were repeated and only pardon as to this world was obtained. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

18 COMMENTARY: Now where remission of these is, there is no more offering for sin — where remission is, there is no more offering for sin; one Sacrifice suffices for all nations and ages, for spiritual pardon and freedom from punishment in the world to come. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

19 COMMENTARY: Having therefore, brethren, boldness to enter into the holiest by the blood of Jesus — believers therefore have boldness to enter the holiest by Jesus' blood, and having an open way to God's presence, it becomes them to use this privilege. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

20 COMMENTARY: By a new and living way, which he hath consecrated for us, through the veil, that is to say, his flesh — the way is new and living, consecrated through the veil, his flesh; our way to heaven is by a crucified Saviour, his death is to us the way of life. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

21 COMMENTARY: And having an high priest over the house of God — and having a High Priest over God's house, we have both access and an advocate. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

22 COMMENTARY: Let us draw near with a true heart in full assurance of faith, having our hearts sprinkled from an evil conscience, and our bodies washed with pure water — let us draw near with a true heart in full assurance of faith, hearts sprinkled from an evil conscience and bodies washed with pure water, baptism reminding us our conduct should be pure and holy; it would be contempt of Christ still to keep at a distance. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

23 COMMENTARY: Let us hold fast the profession of our faith without wavering; (for he is faithful that promised;) — let us hold fast our profession without wavering, for he who promised is faithful. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

24 COMMENTARY: And let us consider one another to provoke unto love and to good works — believers are to consider how they can serve each other, especially stirring one another to more vigorous love and good works. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

25 COMMENTARY: Not forsaking the assembling of ourselves together, as the manner of some is; but exhorting one another: and so much the more, as ye see the day approaching — not forsaking assembling together, but exhorting one another, so much the more as we see the day approaching; the communion of saints is a great help and means of stedfastness, and we should observe coming times of trial and be quickened to greater diligence, for a trying day is coming on all men, the day of death. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

26 COMMENTARY: For if we sin wilfully after that we have received the knowledge of the truth, there remaineth no more sacrifice for sins — the sin here is a total and final falling away, when men with full and fixed will despise and reject Christ, the only Saviour, resist the Spirit, the only Sanctifier, and renounce the gospel, the only way of salvation; for such wilful apostasy no other sacrifice remains. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

27 COMMENTARY: But a certain fearful looking for of judgment and fiery indignation, which shall devour the adversaries — but only a fearful looking for judgment and fiery indignation to devour the adversaries; God gives some notorious sinners on earth a fearful foreboding of this in their consciences. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

28 COMMENTARY: He that despised Moses' law died without mercy under two or three witnesses — under Moses' law, the despiser died without mercy on two or three witnesses. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

29 COMMENTARY: Of how much sorer punishment, suppose ye, shall he be thought worthy, who hath trodden under foot the Son of God, and hath counted the blood of the covenant, wherewith he was sanctified, an unholy thing, and hath done despite unto the Spirit of grace? — how much sorer punishment for one who tramples the Son of God, counts the covenant blood unholy, and does despite to the Spirit of grace? What punishment can be sorer than to die without mercy? To die by mercy — by the mercy and grace despised; how dreadful when not only God's justice but his abused grace and mercy call for vengeance! — Matthew Henry (Concise)

30 COMMENTARY: For we know him that hath said, Vengeance belongeth unto me, I will recompense, saith the Lord. And again, The Lord shall judge his people — vengeance belongs to the Lord, and the Lord shall judge his people. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

31 COMMENTARY: It is a fearful thing to fall into the hands of the living God — it is a fearful thing to fall into the hands of the living God; yet this does not mean any soul sorrowing for sin will be shut out from mercy, or anyone willing to accept Christ's sacrifice refused — him that comes to Christ, he will in no wise cast out. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

32 COMMENTARY: But call to remembrance the former days, in which, after ye were illuminated, ye endured a great fight of afflictions — call to remembrance former days, when after illumination you endured a great fight of afflictions; many and various afflictions united against the early Christians, and they had a great conflict. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

33 COMMENTARY: Partly, whilst ye were made a gazingstock both by reproaches and afflictions; and partly, whilst ye became companions of them that were so used — partly made a gazingstock by reproaches and afflictions, partly companions of those so used; the Christian spirit is not selfish, but puts us upon pitying, visiting, helping, and pleading for others. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

34 COMMENTARY: For ye had compassion of me in my bonds, and took joyfully the spoiling of your goods, knowing in yourselves that ye have in heaven a better and an enduring substance — you had compassion in bonds and took joyfully the spoiling of your goods, knowing you have in heaven a better and enduring substance; all things here are but shadows, but the saints' happiness in heaven lasts for ever, and enemies can never take it away as earthly goods. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

35 COMMENTARY: Cast not away therefore your confidence, which hath great recompence of reward — cast not away therefore your confidence, which has great recompense of reward; this will make rich amends for all we may lose and suffer here. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

36 COMMENTARY: For ye have need of patience, that, after ye have done the will of God, ye might receive the promise — you have need of patience, that after doing God's will you might receive the promise; the greatest part of the saints' happiness as yet is in promise, and it is a trial of patience to be content to live after work is done and stay for reward till God's time. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

37 COMMENTARY: For yet a little while, and he that shall come will come, and will not tarry — for yet a little while, and he who shall come will come and will not tarry; he will soon come at death to end all sufferings and give a crown of life. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

38 COMMENTARY: Now the just shall live by faith: but if any man draw back, my soul shall have no pleasure in him — now the just shall live by faith, but if any draw back, God's soul has no pleasure in him; God is never pleased with formal profession and outward duties from those who do not persevere, but beholds them with great displeasure. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

39 COMMENTARY: But we are not of them who draw back unto perdition; but of them that believe to the saving of the soul — but we are not of those who draw back to perdition, but of those who believe to the saving of the soul; those kept faithful in great trials in the past have reason to hope for the same grace to live by faith still, till they receive the end of faith and patience, the salvation of their souls; living by faith and dying in faith, our souls are safe for ever. — Matthew Henry (Concise)
