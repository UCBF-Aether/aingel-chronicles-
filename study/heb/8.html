Study Notes — Hebrews

Chapter 8

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Now of the things which we have spoken this is the sum: We have such an high priest, who is set on the right hand of the throne of the Majesty in the heavens — the sum of all declared is that Christians have such a High Priest as they needed, seated at the right hand of the Majesty in heaven. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

2 COMMENTARY: A minister of the sanctuary, and of the true tabernacle, which the Lord pitched, and not man — Christ ministers in the true tabernacle the Lord pitched, not the man-made copy. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

3 COMMENTARY: For every high priest is ordained to offer gifts and sacrifices: wherefore it is of necessity that this man have somewhat also to offer — as every high priest must have something to offer, Christ too had his offering: himself, given as a sacrifice to God for his people's sins. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

4 COMMENTARY: For if he were on earth, he should not be a priest, seeing that there are priests that offer gifts according to the law — on earth, where Levitical priests offer according to the law, Christ's priesthood would have no place; his ministry is heavenly. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

5 COMMENTARY: Who serve unto the example and shadow of heavenly things, as Moses was admonished of God when he was about to make the tabernacle: for, See, saith he, that thou make all things according to the pattern shewed to thee in the mount — the earthly priests serve only a copy and shadow of heavenly things, and in all obedience and worship we should keep close to God's word, the only and perfect standard. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

6 COMMENTARY: But now hath he obtained a more excellent ministry, by how much also he is the mediator of a better covenant, which was established upon better promises — Christ's ministry is more excellent because he mediates a better covenant, established on better promises: the promises of all spiritual blessings and eternal life, made sure through Christ, are of infinitely greater value than the temporal benefits secured to Israel as a nation. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

7 COMMENTARY: For if that first covenant had been faultless, then should no place have been sought for the second — the very seeking of a second covenant shows the first was faulty: the law made all liable to condemnation for sin, yet could not remove that guilt or clear the conscience from its sense and terror. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

8 COMMENTARY: For finding fault with them, he saith, Behold, the days come, saith the Lord, when I will make a new covenant with the house of Israel and with the house of Judah — the fault lay with the people, and God promises a new covenant with both houses. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

9 COMMENTARY: Not according to the covenant that I made with their fathers in the day when I took them by the hand to lead them out of the land of Egypt; because they continued not in my covenant, and I regarded them not, saith the Lord — unlike the Sinai covenant, which they broke and which therefore could not save them, the new covenant rests on God's own work. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

10 COMMENTARY: For this is the covenant that I will make with the house of Israel after those days, saith the Lord; I will put my laws into their mind, and write them in their hearts: and I will be to them a God, and they shall be to me a people — God once wrote his laws to his people; now he will write his laws in them, giving understanding to know and believe them, memories to retain them, hearts to love them, courage to profess them, and power to practise them. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

11 COMMENTARY: And they shall not teach every man his neighbour, and every man his brother, saying, Know the Lord: for all shall know me, from the least to the greatest — a plentiful outpouring of God's Spirit will make the gospel ministry so effectual that Christian knowledge shall spread mightily among persons of all sorts, and all shall know the Lord. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

12 COMMENTARY: For I will be merciful to their unrighteousness, and their sins and their iniquities will I remember no more — by Christ's blood a full remission of sins is provided, so God remembers them no more; notice the freeness of this pardon, its fulness, and its fixedness, and how pardon opens a wide door to all spiritual blessings. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

13 COMMENTARY: In that he saith, A new covenant, he hath made the first old. Now that which decayeth and waxeth old is ready to vanish away — calling the covenant new makes the first old, and what decays and waxes old is ready to vanish; all worldly vanities, outward privileges, or mere notions of religion will likewise soon vanish and leave those who trust in them miserable for ever. — Matthew Henry (Concise)
