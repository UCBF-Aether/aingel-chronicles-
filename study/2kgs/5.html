Study Notes — 2 Kings

Chapter 5

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Naaman, captain of Syria's host, was great and honourable with his master because the LORD had given deliverance to Syria by him, yet he was a leper. Henry notes no man's greatness can place him out of reach of life's sorest calamities: every man has some blemish, some damp to his joy. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

2 COMMENTARY: The Syrians had brought away captive from Israel a little maid, who waited on Naaman's wife. Henry observes that though only a girl, she could give an account of the famous prophet among the Israelites, and children should early be told of God's wondrous works. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

3 COMMENTARY: The maid said to her mistress, "Would God my lord were with the prophet that is in Samaria! for he would recover him of his leprosy." Henry praises her as a good servant who desired her master's welfare though a captive, and notes servants may bless families by telling what they know of God's glory. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

4 COMMENTARY: One went in and told Naaman what the maid from Israel had said. Henry notes Naaman did not despise her words because of her meanness. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

5 COMMENTARY: The king of Syria sent Naaman with a letter to the king of Israel, bearing ten talents of silver, six thousand pieces of gold, and ten changes of raiment. Henry's lesson is that those who seek the blessings the LORD sends must come as beggars for a free gift, not as lords to demand or purchase. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

6 COMMENTARY: The letter said the king had sent Naaman so the king of Israel might recover him of his leprosy. Henry frames this as a demand made in worldly terms, expecting a cure to be commanded like a court favour. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

7 COMMENTARY: When the king of Israel read the letter he rent his clothes, asking, "Am I God, to kill and to make alive, that this man doth send unto me to recover a man of his leprosy?" Henry notes the king saw only the impossible demand, not the prophet in his land. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

8 COMMENTARY: When Elisha heard the king had rent his clothes, he sent to ask why, and bade the king let Naaman come to him so he would know there was a prophet in Israel. Henry presents Elisha as the true channel of the healing the king despaired of. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

9 COMMENTARY: Naaman came with horses and chariot and stood at Elisha's door. Henry notes Elisha knew Naaman to be a proud man, and would let him know that before the great God all men stand on the same level. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

10 COMMENTARY: Elisha sent a messenger telling Naaman to wash in Jordan seven times, and his flesh would come again and he would be clean. Henry observes God's commands make trial of men's spirits, especially those directing a sinner how to apply for salvation's blessings. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

11 COMMENTARY: Naaman was wroth and went away, saying he had thought Elisha would come out, stand, call on his God, and strike his hand over the place. Henry sees in him the folly of pride: a cure will not content him unless he is cured with pomp and parade, and he scorns to be healed unless humoured. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

12 COMMENTARY: Naaman asked whether Abana and Pharpar, rivers of Damascus, were not better than all Israel's waters, and whether he might not wash in them and be clean. Henry notes human wisdom thinks it can supply wiser methods of cleansing than God's plain way. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

13 COMMENTARY: His servants reasoned that if the prophet had bid him do some great thing he would have done it, so how much rather should he do it when told simply to wash and be clean. Henry advises masters to hear reason, and to have ears open to good advice even from those below them. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

14 COMMENTARY: Naaman went down and dipped himself seven times in Jordan according to the man of God's word, and his flesh came again like a little child's, and he was clean. Henry's summary is plain: "Believe, and be saved; Repent, and be pardoned; Wash, and be clean" — the believer applies for salvation without neglecting, altering, or adding to the Saviour's directions. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

15 COMMENTARY: Naaman returned to Elisha with all his company, confessing that now he knew there was no God in all the earth but in Israel, and begging Elisha to take a blessing. Henry notes the mercy of the cure affected Naaman more than the miracle, and those who experience divine grace are best able to speak of its power. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

16 COMMENTARY: Elisha swore by the LORD that he would receive none, and though Naaman urged him, he refused. Henry explains Elisha refused not because receiving was unlawful, but to show the new convert that God's servants looked on worldly wealth with holy contempt, and the whole work was from God. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

17 COMMENTARY: Naaman asked for two mules' burden of earth, for he would henceforth offer sacrifice to no god but the LORD. Henry notes it is not well violently to oppose the lesser mistakes that unite with men's first convictions, for we cannot bring men forward faster than the LORD prepares them. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

18 COMMENTARY: Naaman asked the LORD's pardon for bowing in the house of Rimmon when his master leaned on his hand there. Henry warns that if, in covenanting with God, we desire to reserve any known sin and continue indulging it, that is a breach of his covenant, and those who truly hate evil will abstain from all appearances of it. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

19 COMMENTARY: Elisha said, "Go in peace," and Naaman departed a little way. Henry treats this dismissal as patient allowance for a new convert's weakness, not approval of reserved sin. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

20 COMMENTARY: Gehazi, Elisha's servant, said his master had spared Naaman the Syrian, taking nothing, and swore he would run after him and take somewhat of him. Henry contrasts him with Naaman's wise and good servants: Elisha, a holy prophet, has but one servant, and he proves a base liar. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

21 COMMENTARY: Gehazi followed Naaman, who lighted down from his chariot to meet him and asked if all was well. Henry notes the love of money, the root of all evil, was at the bottom of Gehazi's sin. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

22 COMMENTARY: Gehazi lied that his master had sent him for a talent of silver and two changes of garments for two young prophets newly come from Mount Ephraim. Henry observes he thought to impose on the prophet, but soon found the Spirit of prophecy could not be deceived. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

23 COMMENTARY: Naaman urged Gehazi to take two talents, binding them in two bags with two changes of garments, and sending two servants to bear them. Henry warns it is folly to presume upon sin in hopes of secrecy, for conscience and the eye of God go with us into every by-path. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

24 COMMENTARY: When Gehazi came to the tower he took the gifts from the servants' hands, hid them in the house, and sent the men away. Henry notes he that covers his sin shall not prosper, and a lying tongue is but for a moment. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

25 COMMENTARY: Gehazi went in and stood before his master, and when Elisha asked whence he came, he said he had gone nowhere. Henry stresses it was in vain to lie to the Holy Ghost, and all carnal contrivances are open before God. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

26 COMMENTARY: Elisha asked whether his heart had not gone with Gehazi when Naaman turned from his chariot to meet him, and whether it was a time to receive money, garments, oliveyards, vineyards, sheep, oxen, and servants. Henry warns it is not a time to increase wealth by ways dishonourable to God and religion or injurious to others. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

27 COMMENTARY: Elisha declared Naaman's leprosy would cleave to Gehazi and his seed for ever, and Gehazi went out from his presence a leper as white as snow. Henry asks what Gehazi was profited, gaining two talents but losing his health, honour, peace, service, and, without repentance, his soul for ever. — Matthew Henry (Concise)
