Study Notes — 2 Kings

Chapter 10

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Ahab had seventy sons in Samaria, and Jehu wrote letters to Samaria's rulers, elders, and the bringers-up of Ahab's children. JFB notes the seventy probably comprehended Ahab's whole posterity, assembled in the capital, and Jehu's challenge was a stroke of policy to discover whether they were pacific or hostile. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: Jehu's letter pointed out that his master's sons were with them, along with chariots, horses, a fenced city, and armour. JFB notes the tenor of the letters suggests their first intention was to select the fittest royal and set him up as king. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: Jehu challenged them to choose the best and meetest of their master's sons, set him on his father's throne, and fight for their master's house. JFB presents this challenge as designed to elicit their views and test their disposition. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: They were exceedingly afraid, saying two kings had not stood before Jehu, so how could they stand? JFB notes Jehu's bold character and rapid success terrified Samaria's and Jezreel's civic authorities into submission. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: The governor of the house, the governor of the city, the elders, and the bringers-up of the children sent to Jehu saying they were his servants and would do as he bid. JFB explains the bringers-up were principal grandees charged with the support and education of royal princes. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: Jehu's second letter demanded that if they were his and would hearken, they should take the heads of their master's sons and come to him at Jezreel by tomorrow. JFB notes the barbarous practice of a successful usurper slaughtering all who might claim the throne, frequently exemplified in Eastern history. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: When the letter came, they took the king's sons, slew seventy persons, and put their heads in baskets to send to Jezreel. JFB's section on the beheading of Ahab's seventy children frames this wholesale slaughter. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: A messenger told Jehu the heads of the king's sons had been brought, and Jehu ordered them laid in two heaps at the gate's entrance until morning. JFB notes the exhibition of enemies' heads at a gate was always considered a glorious trophy. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: In the morning Jehu told the people they were righteous, for he had conspired against his master and slain him, but who had slain all these? JFB observes Jehu's speech was artfully framed to impress the crowd that the massacre was the result of divine judgments on Ahab's house. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: Jehu declared nothing of the LORD's word spoken against Ahab's house would fall to the earth, for the LORD had done what he spoke by Elijah. JFB notes the speech prepared the public mind to hear, without horror, of the further extinction of Ahab's friends and supporters. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: Jehu slew all that remained of Ahab's house in Jezreel, with his great men, kinsfolks, and priests, leaving him none remaining. JFB presents this as the further revolting tragedy prepared for by Jehu's speech: the extinction of all influential supporters of Ahab's dynasty. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: Jehu departed to Samaria, and at the shearing house in the way he met company. JFB's section carries the purge from Jezreel toward Samaria. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: Jehu met the brethren of Ahaziah king of Judah, who said they were going down to salute the king's children and the queen's children. JFB explains they were not full but step-brothers, sons of Jehoram by concubines, ignorant of the revolution and travelling to visit royal relatives. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: Jehu ordered them taken alive, and they were slain at the pit of the shearing house, forty-two men, none left. JFB notes they were seized and put to death for fear they might stimulate the party still faithful to Ahab's dynasty. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: Jehu met Jehonadab son of Rechab, saluted him, and asked whether his heart was right as Jehu's heart was with his, and Jehonadab said it was. JFB describes Jehonadab as a man of piety and simple primitive life, highly esteemed and influential, whose friendship Jehu saw would advantage his cause. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: Jehu said, "Come with me, and see my zeal for the LORD," and made Jehonadab ride in his chariot. JFB notes Jehu paid him the distinguished attention of a seat in his chariot, and the joining of hands was the recognized mode of striking a league and testifying fealty to a new sovereign. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: When Jehu came to Samaria he slew all that remained unto Ahab there, till he had destroyed him according to the LORD's word to Elijah. JFB's section closes the purge of Ahab's house with this completion in Samaria. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

18 COMMENTARY: Jehu gathered the people and said Ahab had served Baal a little, but Jehu would serve him much. JFB presents this as the opening of Jehu's deep-laid plot to destroy Baal's worshippers, done from political motives to secure the party that worshipped God in Israel. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

19 COMMENTARY: Jehu called for all Baal's prophets, servants, and priests, letting none be wanting, on pain of death. JFB notes Baal's votaries were classified as prophets, priests, and servants generally, and could easily be convened in one spacious temple, their number already diminished by Elijah's and Elisha's ministries. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

20 COMMENTARY: Jehu proclaimed a solemn assembly for Baal, and it was proclaimed. JFB explains the solemn sacrifice in Baal's honour was a deep-laid plot for the worshippers' extinction, in harmony with the Mosaic law and worthy of a constitutional king of Israel. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

21 COMMENTARY: Jehu sent through all Israel, and all Baal's worshippers came, so the house of Baal was full from one end to another. JFB notes the summons gathered every worshipper into the temple. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

22 COMMENTARY: Jehu told the keeper of the vestry to bring forth vestments for all Baal's worshippers, and he brought them out. JFB explains Baal's priests were clad in robes of white byssus kept in a particular wardrobe under an officer's care. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

23 COMMENTARY: Jehu and Jehonadab went into Baal's house and told the worshippers to look that none of the LORD's servants were among them, but only Baal's worshippers. JFB presents this search as part of the plot's execution, ensuring only Baalites would be trapped. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

24 COMMENTARY: When they went in to offer sacrifices, Jehu appointed fourscore men outside, charging that if any escaped, the life of him who let him go would answer for it. JFB parallels this treacherous massacre's means with terrible tragedies in later Eastern history. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

25 COMMENTARY: As soon as the burnt offering ended, Jehu ordered his guard and captains to go in, slay them, and let none come forth, casting them out. JFB's section "He destroys the worshippers of Baal" records this slaughter as the plot's fulfilment. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

26 COMMENTARY: They brought out the images from Baal's house and burned them. JFB notes the temple's destruction completed the extinction of Baal worship in Israel. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

27 COMMENTARY: They broke down Baal's image and house, making it a draught house to this day. JFB presents this desecration as the final end of Baal's house in Israel. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

28 COMMENTARY: Thus Jehu destroyed Baal out of Israel. JFB's section closes with this summary of the purge. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

29 COMMENTARY: Howbeit Jehu departed not from the sins of Jeroboam son of Nebat, who made Israel to sin, keeping the golden calves in Bethel and Dan. JFB explains Jehu had no intention of carrying his zeal beyond a certain point, and, considering it impolitic to let subjects travel to Jerusalem, re-established the symbolic calf worship. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

31 COMMENTARY: Jehu took no heed to walk in the law of the LORD God of Israel with all his heart, for he departed not from Jeroboam's sins. JFB's note on verse 29 applies here: Jehu's zeal stopped short, and he kept the calves for political reasons rather than following God's law fully. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

No note for verse(s) 30, 32, 33, 34, 35, 36 — the public-domain sources consulted give no distinct explanation for these verses beyond the list itself, so nothing was invented for them.
