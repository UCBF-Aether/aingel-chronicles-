Study Notes — 2 Kings

Chapter 24

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: In Jehoiakim's days Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon came up, and Jehoiakim became his servant three years, then turned and rebelled. JFB identifies Nebuchadnezzar as the son of Nabopolassar, founder of the Chaldee monarchy, whose invasion fell in Jehoiakim's fourth year and Nebuchadnezzar's first, the young king, detained at home by his father's death, dispatching tributary nations' armies to chastise the revolt. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: The LORD sent against Jehoiakim bands of Chaldees, Syrians, Moabites, and Ammonites, sending them against Judah to destroy it according to the word spoken by his servants the prophets. JFB notes this hostile band was only an instrument executing the divine judgment denounced against Judah, and though marching by Assyria's monarch's orders, they are described as sent by the LORD. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: Surely at the LORD's commandment this came upon Judah, to remove them out of his sight for Manasseh's sins, according to all he did. JFB presents this as the divine judgment for the people's sins, executed through the tributary armies. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: This was also for the innocent blood Manasseh shed, filling Jerusalem with innocent blood, which the LORD would not pardon. JFB cross-refers this unpardonable guilt to the earlier declaration of God's unturned wrath. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: Jehoiakim slept with his fathers, and Jehoiachin his son reigned in his stead. JFB explains "slept with his fathers" means only that he died, for he was not buried with his royal ancestors, and whether he fell in battle or suffered posthumous insults, he was not honoured with sepulture rites as predicted. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: Egypt's king came no more out of his land, for Babylon's king had taken from Egypt's river to the Euphrates all that had pertained to Egypt's king. JFB identifies Egypt's king as Pharaohnechoh, now confined by Babylon's conquests. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: Jehoiachin was eighteen when he began to reign, reigning three months in Jerusalem, his mother Nehushta daughter of Elnathan of Jerusalem. JFB explains Jehoiachin means "God-appointed," contracted into Jeconiah and Coniah, and at eight his father had taken him into partnership, so he began to reign alone at eighteen. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: Jehoiachin did evil in the LORD's sight, according to all his father had done. JFB notes that, untaught by experience and deaf to prophetic warnings, he pursued the evil courses that had brought disasters on Judah's royal family and people. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: At that time Nebuchadnezzar's servants came up against Jerusalem, and the city was besieged. JFB notes this was within three months of Jehoiachin's accession, in the spring, when he showed hostility to his Assyrian liege lord by forming a league with Egypt, so Nebuchadnezzar sent generals to besiege Jerusalem as Jeremiah had foretold. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: Nebuchadnezzar himself came against the city while his servants besieged it. JFB notes Nebuchadnezzar soon followed his generals in person. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: Jehoiachin went out to Babylon's king with his mother, servants, princes, and officers, and Babylon's king took him in his eighth year. JFB explains that, convinced resistance was hopeless, Jehoiachin surrendered expecting to retain his throne as vassal, but Nebuchadnezzar's clemency toward Judah's kings was exhausted, so Jehoiachin was sent captive to Babylon with the queen mother, generals, and officers. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: Nebuchadnezzar carried out all the treasures of the LORD's house and the king's house, cutting in pieces all the gold vessels Solomon had made, as the LORD had said. JFB notes the palace and temple were ransacked, smaller golden vessels having been taken at the first capture and placed in his god's temple, while now the gold plating was torn off all the larger temple furniture. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: He carried away all Jerusalem, all the princes, all mighty men of valour, ten thousand captives, and all craftsmen and smiths, leaving none but the poorest sort of the land's people. JFB explains the elite for rank, usefulness, and moral worth, all who might be useful in Babylon or dangerous in Palestine, were carried off, ten thousand in all. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: He carried away Jehoiachin to Babylon, with the king's mother, wives, officers, and Judah's mighty men. JFB notes those left consisted chiefly of the poorer sort and unskilled workmen. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: All the men of might, seven thousand, and craftsmen and smiths, a thousand, all strong and apt for war, did Babylon's king bring captive to Babylon. JFB breaks down the ten thousand captives as seven thousand warriors, one thousand craftsmen and smiths, and two thousand kings' wives, officers, princes, priests, and prophets. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: Babylon's king made Mattaniah, Jehoiachin's father's brother, king in his stead, changing his name to Zedekiah. JFB explains Nebuchadnezzar, maintaining a show of monarchy, appointed Josiah's third and youngest son, full brother of Jehoahaz and uncle of captive Jehoiachin, and, as conquerors changed captives' names in token of supremacy, gave him the new name Zedekiah, "The righteous of God," apparently allowing the puppet king to choose his own Hebrew name. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

18 COMMENTARY: Zedekiah was twenty-one when he began to reign and reigned eleven years in Jerusalem, his mother Hamutal daughter of Jeremiah of Libnah. JFB's section on Zedekiah's evil reign opens with this accession. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

19 COMMENTARY: Zedekiah did evil in the LORD's sight, according to all Jehoiakim had done. JFB notes his heart toward God was the same as Jehoiakim's: impenitent and heedless of God's word. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

20 COMMENTARY: For through the LORD's anger it came to pass in Jerusalem and Judah, until he had cast them out from his presence, that Zedekiah rebelled against Babylon's king. JFB explains that in God's righteous providence Zedekiah's policy as king would prove ruinous, instigated by neighbouring ambassadors urging a common league to throw off the yoke, though Jeremiah warned against it, and the infatuated, perjured Zedekiah persisted. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

No note for verse(s) 5 — the public-domain sources consulted give no distinct explanation for these verses beyond the list itself, so nothing was invented for them.
