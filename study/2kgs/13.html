Study Notes — 2 Kings

Chapter 13

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: In the twenty-third year of Joash son of Ahaziah king of Judah, Jehoahaz son of Jehu began to reign over Israel in Samaria for seventeen years. JFB notes under him Israel's apostasy grew greater and more confirmed than under Jehu, as he pursued his predecessors' policy of supporting calf worship. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: Jehoahaz did evil in the LORD's sight and followed the sins of Jeroboam son of Nebat, departing not from them. JFB presents this continued calf worship as the cause of the severer national chastisement that followed. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: The LORD's anger was kindled against Israel, and he delivered them into the hand of Hazael king of Syria and Benhadad his son all their days. JFB identifies Hazael and Benhadad as the instruments the LORD employed in scourging the revolted nation. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: Jehoahaz besought the LORD, and the LORD hearkened to him, for he saw Israel's oppression under Syria. JFB explains he was heard not on his own account but for the ancient covenant with the patriarchs, and because divine honour required deliverance to check the idolatrous enemy's blasphemous taunts that God had forsaken Israel. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: The LORD gave Israel a saviour, so they went out from under Syria's hand and dwelt in their tents as before. JFB explains this saviour was not one patriotic defender but the deliverance obtained through Jehoahaz's two successors, Joash and Jeroboam. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: Nevertheless they departed not from the sins of Jeroboam's house, but walked therein, and the grove remained in Samaria. JFB identifies the grove as Asherah, the idol set up by Ahab, which ought to have been demolished. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: Jehoahaz was left only fifty horsemen, ten chariots, and ten thousand footmen, for Syria's king had destroyed them and made them like dust in threshing. JFB explains threshing imagery: grain beaten in the open air with straw shattered to pieces, a figure of national suffering little short of extermination, originating in a barbarous war custom Hazael literally followed. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: The rest of Jehoahaz's acts and his might are written in the chronicles of Israel's kings. JFB notes his might is particularly mentioned to show the grievous oppression was not due to the king's cowardice but solely to God's righteous judgment for foul apostasy. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: Jehoahaz slept with his fathers, was buried in Samaria, and Joash his son reigned in his stead. JFB's section "Joash succeeds him" opens the second half of the chapter with this succession. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: In the thirty-seventh year of Joash king of Judah, Jehoash son of Jehoahaz began to reign over Israel in Samaria for sixteen years. JFB's section on Joash's succession frames his reign as the beginning of Israel's deliverance. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: Joash did evil in the LORD's sight, departing not from all the sins of Jeroboam son of Nebat, but walking therein. JFB's section notes even the delivering king continued in Jeroboam's sins. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: The rest of Joash's acts, his might, and how he fought against Amaziah king of Judah are written in the chronicles of Israel's kings. JFB notes this summary occurs early and is repeated in the account of Judah's king, cross-referring to the war with Amaziah. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: Joash slept with his fathers, Jeroboam sat upon his throne, and Joash was buried in Samaria with Israel's kings. JFB's section closes Joash's reign with this succession. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: Elisha fell sick of the sickness whereof he died, and Joash king of Israel came down to him, weeping over his face, "My father, my father, the chariot of Israel, and the horsemen thereof." JFB contrasts Elisha's death by disease with Elijah, who did not die, and explains the king's sorrow as grief for the incalculable loss to the kingdom, with "my father" as a complimentary phrase for an eminent guardian and deliverer. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: Elisha told Joash to take bow and arrows, and he took them. JFB explains hostilities were proclaimed by a formal discharge of an arrow into enemy country, and Elisha directed this symbolic act to intimate the victories promised over the Syrians. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: Elisha told the king to put his hand upon the bow, and Elisha put his hands upon the king's hands. JFB explains this represented the power imparted to the bow shot as coming from the LORD through the prophet. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: Elisha told him to open the eastward window, and when he opened it, Elisha said to shoot, and he shot, and Elisha declared it the arrow of the LORD's deliverance from Syria, to smite the Syrians in Aphek till consumed. JFB notes shooting eastward declared war for the territory east of Samaria the Syrians had taken. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

18 COMMENTARY: Elisha told Joash to take the arrows, and he took them, and Elisha said to smite upon the ground, and Joash smote thrice and stayed. JFB explains shooting arrows into the ground tokened the number of victories he would gain, so stopping at the third betrayed weakness of faith. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

19 COMMENTARY: The man of God was wroth, saying Joash should have smitten five or six times, and then he would have smitten Syria till consumed, but now he would smite Syria only thrice. JFB notes that as each discharged arrow signified a victory, the more arrows shot, the more victories gained, and stopping so soon made his conquests incomplete. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

20 COMMENTARY: Elisha died and was buried, and Moabite bands invaded the land at the coming in of the year. JFB notes Elisha enjoyed a happier life than Elijah, with a milder character, and the spring was the usual season for campaigns, when Moabite bands made incursions. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

21 COMMENTARY: As men were burying a man, they spied a band, cast the man into Elisha's sepulchre, and when he touched Elisha's bones he revived and stood on his feet. JFB explains both bodies were swathed, not coffined, so they could contact, and the miracle's object was to stimulate Israel's faith in Elisha's still unaccomplished predictions about the Syrian war. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

22 COMMENTARY: Hazael king of Syria oppressed Israel all the days of Jehoahaz. JFB's account of the Syrian scourge frames this oppression as the judgment under which Israel groaned. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

23 COMMENTARY: But the LORD was gracious to Israel, had compassion, and had respect to them because of his covenant with Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob, and would not destroy or cast them from his presence. JFB notes Jehoahaz was heard for the ancient covenant's sake, not his own. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

24 COMMENTARY: Hazael king of Syria died, and Benhadad his son reigned in his stead. JFB's section records this succession as the turn toward Israel's deliverance. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

25 COMMENTARY: Jehoash son of Jehoahaz took again from Benhadad the cities Hazael had taken from his father in war, smiting him three times and recovering Israel's cities. JFB notes this fulfils the prediction: Joash regained all the cities the Syrians had taken from his father. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
