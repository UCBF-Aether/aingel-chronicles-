Study Notes — 2 Kings

Chapter 9

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Elisha called one of the children of the prophets, telling him to gird up his loins, take a box of oil, and go to Ramothgilead. JFB notes this errand fulfilled the last commission given to Elijah at Horeb, and Ramothgilead was a fortress of defence against the Syrians east of Jordan. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: The messenger was to look out Jehu son of Jehoshaphat son of Nimshi, take him from among his brethren into an inner chamber. JFB explains the inner chamber ensured the messenger's safety and prevented obstruction of the business. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: He was to pour the oil on Jehu's head and say the LORD had anointed him king over Israel, then open the door and flee without tarrying. JFB notes this was only part of the message, the full announcement following in verses 7-10, and the flight was for fear of being surprised by court spies. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: The young prophet went to Ramothgilead. JFB calls his ready undertaking of this delicate and hazardous mission an eminent proof of piety and obedience, and the anointing through a commissioned prophet a divine intimation of investiture with sovereign power. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: When he came, the captains of the host were sitting, and he said he had an errand to Jehu. JFB's section "Jehu is anointed" covers this approach to the army's commanders at Ramothgilead. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: Jehu arose and went into the house, where the prophet poured oil on his head and declared the LORD had anointed him king over Israel. JFB notes anointing was sometimes done long before actual possession of the throne, and Elijah had left this commission in Elisha's charge until God's time. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: Jehu was to smite the house of Ahab his master, that God might avenge the blood of his servants the prophets and all the LORD's servants at Jezebel's hand. JFB notes the full announcement of the message is given here in verses 7-10. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: The whole house of Ahab was to perish, cut off to the last male. JFB's exposition of the anointing commission presents this as the divine sentence on Ahab's house. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: The house of Ahab would be made like the house of Jeroboam son of Nebat and the house of Baasha son of Ahijah. JFB treats this as the pattern of dynastic judgment already seen in Israel. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: Dogs would eat Jezebel in the portion of Jezreel, with none to bury her, and then the prophet opened the door and fled. JFB notes the portion of Jezreel was what had formerly been Naboth's vineyard. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: When Jehu came out to his lord's servants, one asked why this mad fellow had come to him. JFB explains the attendants knew the stranger was a prophet by his garb, gestures, and address, and soldiers readily concluded such persons crackbrained from their contempt of the world and grotesque actions. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: They hasted, took every man his garment, put it under Jehu on the top of the stairs, and blew trumpets saying, "Jehu is king." JFB explains the upper cloaks spread on the ground were a token of homage, and the top of the stairs, the most conspicuous place of an Oriental structure, served as a substitute throne, while Jehu's popularity with the army favoured Providence's design. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: Jehu son of Jehoshaphat son of Nimshi conspired against Joram, who had kept Ramothgilead with the army. JFB notes Joram was guarding or besieging Ramothgilead with most of Israel's force, but his wounds had compelled his retirement, leaving the troops under Jehu. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: King Joram had returned to Jezreel to be healed of the wounds the Syrians gave him at Ramah, and Jehu said none should escape the city to tell it in Jezreel. JFB notes the king's wounds had compelled his retirement from the scene of action. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: Jehu rode in a chariot to Jezreel, where Joram lay, and Ahaziah king of Judah had come down to see him. JFB describes Jehu, full of ambitious designs, crossing the Jordan to execute his commission on the house of Ahab. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: A watchman on Jezreel's tower spied Jehu's company coming and told Joram. JFB describes the tower commanding the whole region eastward toward the Jordan, so that Jehu's retinue could be descried from Gilboa and Beth-shan, and a horseman was dispatched to question their approach. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

18 COMMENTARY: A horseman went to meet Jehu asking, "Is it peace?" and Jehu answered, "What hast thou to do with peace? turn thee behind me," and the watchman reported the messenger had been detained. JFB's account follows this first messenger meeting Jehu miles out on the plain and being turned into the rear of the advancing troops. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

19 COMMENTARY: A second horseman was sent with the same question, received the same answer, and likewise came not back. JFB notes the second messenger met Jehu closer to the city and was likewise turned into the rear. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

20 COMMENTARY: The watchman reported that the messenger had come to them but returned not, and the driving was like Jehu son of Nimshi's, for he drove furiously. JFB quotes this very observation as the moment the alarmed monarch sensed his impending danger. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

21 COMMENTARY: Joram ordered his chariot made ready, and he and Ahaziah king of Judah went out against Jehu, meeting him in the portion of Naboth the Jezreelite. JFB describes the two sovereigns ascending their chariots for a feeble resistance, meeting Jehu where the portion of Naboth lay. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

24 COMMENTARY: Jehu drew a bow with his full strength and smote Jehoram between his arms, and the arrow went out at his heart, so he sank down in his chariot. JFB notes Joram was quickly dispatched by an arrow from Jehu's strong arm in the portion of Naboth. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

25 COMMENTARY: Jehu told Bidkar his captain to take up Jehoram and cast him in the portion of Naboth's field, recalling the LORD's word. JFB explains this fulfilled the doom pronounced on Ahab, deferred on his repentance to be executed on his son. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

26 COMMENTARY: Jehu recalled that the LORD had said he had seen the blood of Naboth and his sons and would requite him in this plat. JFB notes that although the sons' deaths are not expressly mentioned, they are plainly implied in the confiscation of Naboth's property. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

27 COMMENTARY: When Ahaziah king of Judah saw this, he fled by the way of the garden house, and Jehu followed after him. JFB heads this section "Ahaziah is slain," noting Ahaziah was grandnephew to Joram and great-grandson to Ahab, and locating Ibleam near Megiddo and Gur as an adjoining hill. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

30 COMMENTARY: When Jehu came to Jezreel, Jezebel heard of it, painted her face, tired her head, and looked out at a window. JFB explains she painted her eyes after the universal Eastern custom of staining eyelids with black powder, aiming by royal attire not to captivate but to overawe Jehu. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

35 COMMENTARY: They went to bury Jezebel but found no more of her than the skull, the feet, and the palms of her hands. JFB notes the dog has a rooted aversion to prey on human hands and feet. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

36 COMMENTARY: They reported back to Jehu, and he said this was the LORD's word spoken by Elijah the Tishbite, that dogs would eat Jezebel's flesh in Jezreel's portion. JFB notes Jehu's statement was not a literal but a paraphrased quotation of Elijah's prophecy. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

37 COMMENTARY: Jezebel's carcase would be as dung upon the field in Jezreel's portion, so that none could say, "This is Jezebel." JFB heads this closing section "Jezebel eaten by dogs," fulfilling Elijah's word. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

No note for verse(s) 12, 22, 23, 28, 29, 31, 32, 33, 34 — the public-domain sources consulted give no distinct explanation for these verses beyond the list itself, so nothing was invented for them.
