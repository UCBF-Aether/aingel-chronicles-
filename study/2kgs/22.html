Study Notes — 2 Kings

Chapter 22

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Josiah was eight when he began to reign and reigned thirty-one years in Jerusalem, his mother Jedidah daughter of Adaiah of Boscath. JFB notes that, happier than his grandfather Manasseh, he fell in his minority under better guardians who trained him in piety, and his young affections were so enlisted for true religion that he adhered all his life with undeviating perseverance to God and righteousness. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: Josiah did right in the LORD's sight, walking in all David his father's way, turning not aside to the right or left. JFB presents this lifelong perseverance as the fruit of his godly upbringing. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: In Josiah's eighteenth year he sent Shaphan the scribe to the LORD's house. JFB notes that before this period he had already begun national reformation, with builders employed, money brought by the people and received by Levites at the door, and the whole land purified from idolatry's traces, and now he addressed the temple's repair. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: Shaphan was to go to Hilkiah the high priest, that he might sum the silver brought into the LORD's house, which the door-keepers had gathered from the people. JFB explains the king directed Hilkiah to take a general survey to ascertain what repair was necessary. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: The silver was to be delivered to the doers of the work overseeing the LORD's house, to repair the breaches. JFB notes the preliminary steps were already taken, with builders employed and preparations made. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: The money went to carpenters, builders, and masons, and to buy timber and hewn stone to repair the house. JFB's account of the repair preparations covers these workmen and materials. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: No reckoning was made with the workmen for the money delivered into their hand, because they dealt faithfully. JFB's section on the temple repair presents this faithful administration. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: Hilkiah the high priest told Shaphan, "I have found the book of the law in the house of the LORD," and gave the book to Shaphan. JFB identifies the book as the law of Moses, the Pentateuch, the temple copy laid beside the ark in the most holy place, lost during Manasseh's and Amon's ungodly reigns, or under Ahaz when the temple was profaned, and now found during the repair. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: Shaphan came to the king, reported his servants had gathered the money brought into the house and delivered it to the work's overseers. JFB notes the book was delivered by Hilkiah the discoverer to Shaphan the scribe, who then brought the king word. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: Shaphan showed the king that Hilkiah had delivered him a book, and read it before the king. JFB notes the book was shown and read to the king, and thinks the passage read was probably Deuteronomy 28-30, with its renewal of the national covenant and terrible curses against lawbreakers. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: When the king heard the words of the book of the law, he rent his clothes. JFB explains the grief and terror this reading produced: though other copies circulated and Josiah must have known the contents in some degree, this reading found his heart as never before, with strong faith in God's word and painful consciousness that long apostasies had exposed the nation to the denounced judgments. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: The king commanded Hilkiah, Ahikam son of Shaphan, Achbor son of Michaiah, Shaphan, and Asahiah to go and inquire of the LORD. JFB notes the king's agitated feelings prompted immediate counsel on averting the curses under which his kingdom lay, sending a deputation of principal officers to one endowed with the prophetic spirit. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: They were to inquire of the LORD for the king, the people, and all Judah, concerning the book's words, for great was the LORD's wrath kindled against them because their fathers had not hearkened to the book's words. JFB notes Ahikam was a friend of Jeremiah, and the deputation sought counsel on the curses. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: Hilkiah, Ahikam, Achbor, Shaphan, and Asahiah went to Huldah the prophetess, wife of Shallum son of Tikvah, keeper of the wardrobe, who dwelt in Jerusalem in the college. JFB notes the occasion was urgent, so they were sent not to the young Zephaniah nor to Jeremiah, probably absent at Anathoth, but to Huldah at hand and known for prophetic gifts, dwelling in the Misnah, a suburb of Jerusalem, and held in such veneration that she and Jehoiada were the only non-Davidic persons buried in Jerusalem. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: Huldah said, "Thus saith the LORD God of Israel, Tell the man that sent you to me." JFB notes her oracular response blended judgment with mercy: it announced impending calamities for the city, yet consoled the king that punishment would not come in his lifetime, because of his faith, penitence, and pious zeal for God's glory and worship. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: The LORD declared he would bring evil upon this place and its inhabitants, even all the words of the book Judah's king had read. JFB presents this as the announced impending calamity, at no distant period, for the city and its inhabitants. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: This was because they had forsaken the LORD, burned incense to other gods, and provoked him with all the works of their hands, so his wrath would be kindled and not quenched. JFB's account of Huldah's response covers this ground of judgment. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

18 COMMENTARY: But to Judah's king who sent them to inquire, the LORD would speak a different word. JFB notes the response's mercy side: the king was consoled with assurance tied to his own faith and piety. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

19 COMMENTARY: Because Josiah's heart was tender and he had humbled himself before the LORD, hearing the words against this place, rending his clothes and weeping, the LORD had heard him. JFB attributes the mercy to Josiah's faith, penitence, and pious zeal displayed in his public capacity and royal influence. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

20 COMMENTARY: Therefore the LORD would gather Josiah to his fathers, and he would be gathered to his grave in peace, his eyes not seeing all the evil to be brought on this place, and they brought the king word again. JFB notes the season of punishment and sorrow would not come during Josiah's lifetime. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
