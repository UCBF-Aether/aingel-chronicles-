Study Notes — 2 Kings

Chapter 11

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: When Athaliah mother of Ahaziah saw her son was dead, she arose and destroyed all the seed royal. JFB explains she was incited partly to keep David's family from outliving hers, partly in self-defence against Jehu, who was bent on destroying Ahab's posterity to which she belonged, but chiefly from personal ambition to rule and establish Baal worship. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: Jehosheba, daughter of King Joram and sister of Ahaziah, took Joash son of Ahaziah and stole him from among the king's sons being slain, hiding him and his nurse in the bedchamber from Athaliah. JFB notes Jehosheba was not Athaliah's daughter but a secondary wife's, and the bedchamber was a priests' closet in the temple of which Jehoiada and his wife had sole charge. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: Joash was hidden with Jehosheba in the house of the LORD six years while Athaliah reigned over the land. JFB's section "Jehoash saved from Athaliah's massacre" frames these six hidden years. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: In the seventh year Jehoiada sent and fetched the rulers over hundreds, the captains, and the guard, brought them into the LORD's house, made a covenant with them, took an oath, and showed them the king's son. JFB notes such a general convocation could scarcely have been obtained except at a public solemn festival, and Jehoiada's conspiracy admits full justification, since Athaliah was a usurper of a race destined for destruction, while the young prince held an inherent right to David's throne. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: Jehoiada commanded that a third part entering on the sabbath should keep the watch of the king's house. JFB's account of Jehoiada's plan covers this division of the guard for the plot's execution. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: A third part was to be at the gate of Sur, and a third part at the gate behind the guard, for the watch of the house, that it be not broken down. JFB notes Jehoiada arranged with the rulers the plan and time of carrying the plot into execution. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: Two parts of those going forth on the sabbath were to keep the watch of the LORD's house about the king. JFB presents this as part of Jehoiada's arranged plan, supported by the chief civil and ecclesiastical authorities. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: They were to compass the king round about, every man with weapons in hand, and whoever came within the ranges was to be slain, and they were to be with the king as he went out and came in. JFB notes Jehoiada, most probably high priest, acted under an impulse of the Divine Spirit through the prophets' counsels. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: The captains over the hundreds did according to all Jehoiada the priest commanded, each taking his men, those entering and those going out on the sabbath, and coming to Jehoiada. JFB's section "He is made king" covers this obedient muster. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: The priest gave the captains King David's spears and shields that were in the temple of the LORD. JFB's account of the coronation preparations includes arming the guard from the temple's stores. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: The guard stood, every man with weapons in hand, round about the king, from the right corner of the temple to the left, by the altar and the temple. JFB's section describes this armed ring around the young king. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: Jehoiada brought forth the king's son, put the crown on him, gave him the testimony, and they made him king and anointed him, clapping hands and saying, "God save the king." JFB's section "He is made king" records this coronation as the restoration of David's heir. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: When Athaliah heard the noise of the guard and people, she came to the people into the temple of the LORD. JFB notes the profound secrecy of the conspiracy made the vast crowd's unusual acclamations startling, rousing the tyrant's suspicions, and she was permitted into the courts by Jehoiada's directions so she might be secured. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: Athaliah looked and saw the king standing by a pillar, as the manner was, with princes and trumpeters, and all the land rejoicing and blowing trumpets, and she rent her clothes crying, "Treason, Treason." JFB notes the king stood by a pillar, or on a platform erected for that purpose. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: Jehoiada commanded the captains to bring Athaliah out within the ranges, and whoever followed her was to be slain with the sword, for the priest said not to slay her in the LORD's house. JFB explains "without the ranges" meant beyond the fences, so the sacred place might not be stained with human blood. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: They laid hands on her, and she went by the way horses came into the king's house, and there she was slain. JFB's section "Athaliah slain" closes the usurper's reign with her execution outside the temple. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: Jehoiada made a covenant between the LORD and the king and the people, that they should be the LORD's people, and likewise between the king and the people. JFB explains the covenant with the LORD was a renewal of the national covenant, and the covenant between king and people bound the king to rule by divine law while the people gave allegiance to the LORD's anointed. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

18 COMMENTARY: All the people went into Baal's house, broke it down, broke his altars and images, and slew Mattan Baal's priest before the altars, while the priest appointed officers over the LORD's house. JFB notes the immediate fruit of the covenant's renewal was the destruction of Baal's temple and the slaughter of his priests, restoring pure worship. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

19 COMMENTARY: Jehoiada took the rulers, captains, guard, and all the people, and brought the king down from the LORD's house through the guard's gate to the king's house, where he sat on the throne of the kings. JFB notes the covenant's fruit included the establishment of the young king on Judah's hereditary throne. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

20 COMMENTARY: All the people of the land rejoiced, the city was quiet, and they had slain Athaliah with the sword beside the king's house. JFB's section closes with the land's rejoicing at the restoration of God's worship and David's heir. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

No note for verse(s) 21 — the public-domain sources consulted give no distinct explanation for these verses beyond the list itself, so nothing was invented for them.
