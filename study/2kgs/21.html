Study Notes — 2 Kings

Chapter 21

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Manasseh was twelve when he began to reign and reigned fifty-five years in Jerusalem, his mother Hephzibah. JFB notes he was born three years after his father's recovery, and his minority under guardians hostile to his father's reforming policy helps explain his anti-theocratic principles, as degenerate leaders who had him in their power trained him in their views and seduced him into open patronage of idolatry. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: Manasseh did evil in the LORD's sight, after the abominations of the heathen the LORD cast out before Israel. JFB notes Hezekiah's reformation was only partially accomplished, the true fear of God had vanished from the masses, and corruption openly practised by degenerate leaders shaped the young prince. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: Manasseh built up again the high places Hezekiah had destroyed, reared altars for Baal, made a grove as Ahab had, and worshipped all the host of heaven. JFB explains this as the restoration of high places and Baal pillars, with the Asherah representing all the host of heaven in pure star-worship of Chaldaic and Assyrian origin. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: Manasseh built altars in the LORD's house, of which the LORD had said, "In Jerusalem will I put my name." JFB's account of his idolatry places these altars within the temple dedicated to the true God's worship. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: He built altars for all the host of heaven in the two courts of the LORD's house. JFB identifies the two courts as the priests' court and the large court of the people. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: Manasseh made his son pass through the fire, observed times, used enchantments, dealt with familiar spirits and wizards, and wrought much wickedness in provoking the LORD. JFB explains observing times came from watching clouds, enchantments were jugglery and spells, familiar spirits were ventriloquists pretending to consult a spirit, and wizards were knowing ones pretending to reveal secrets and interpret dreams, a Chaldaean influx Manasseh liberally patronized and even practised himself. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: He set a graven image of the grove he had made in the house of which the LORD had spoken to David and Solomon, saying there he would put his name for ever. JFB dwells on placing the Asherah within the temple precincts as the most aggravated outrage of the royal idolater. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: The LORD had said he would not make Israel's feet move any more from the land given their fathers, only if they would observe all he commanded and the law Moses commanded. JFB alludes this promise to the covenant with David, noting the condition was expressed from Israel's first plantation in Canaan. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: But they hearkened not, and Manasseh seduced them to do more evil than the nations the LORD destroyed before Israel. JFB notes that through Manasseh's pernicious influence they were seduced into greater excesses of idolatrous corruption than even the original Canaanites. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: The LORD spoke by his servants the prophets. JFB identifies these prophets as Hosea, Joel, Nahum, Habakkuk, and Isaiah, whose counsels and warnings were recorded in the national chronicles and now form part of the sacred canon. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: Because Manasseh had done these abominations, acting more wickedly than the Amorites before him and making Judah sin with his idols, the LORD would bring evil on Jerusalem and Judah. JFB's section on the prophetic sentence frames this indictment. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: The LORD declared he was bringing such evil upon Jerusalem and Judah that whoever heard of it, both his ears would tingle. JFB calls this a strong metaphorical form announcing an extraordinary and appalling event. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: The LORD would stretch over Jerusalem the line of Samaria and the plummet of Ahab's house, wiping Jerusalem as a man wipes a dish, turning it upside down. JFB explains captives doomed to destruction were marked off by measuring-line and plummet, so the line meant Samaria's destruction and the plummet Ahab's family's extermination, threatening Judah with utter destruction like both. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: The LORD would forsake the remnant of his inheritance and deliver them into their enemies' hands, to become a prey and spoil to all their enemies. JFB notes the remnant was Judah, alone remaining of the chosen people, and forsaking them meant falling into enemy power. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: This was because they had done evil in God's sight and provoked him to anger since their fathers came out of Egypt, even unto this day. JFB's section on the prophetic judgment covers this long provocation. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: Moreover Manasseh shed innocent blood very much, till he filled Jerusalem from one end to another, besides his sin in making Judah sin. JFB notes that not content with idolatrous abomination, he cruelly persecuted all nonconformists, deluging the land with good men's blood, among whom tradition says Isaiah was sawn asunder. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

18 COMMENTARY: Manasseh slept with his fathers, was buried in the garden of his own house, and Amon his son reigned in his stead. JFB's section on Amon's reign follows this burial and succession. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

19 COMMENTARY: Amon was twenty-two when he began to reign and reigned two years in Jerusalem, his mother Meshullemeth daughter of Haruz of Jotbah. JFB notes Amon continued his father's idolatrous policy, and after an inglorious two-year reign was massacred by his own domestics. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

20 COMMENTARY: Amon did evil in the LORD's sight, as his father Manasseh did. JFB presents Amon as continuing the idolatrous policy of his father. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

21 COMMENTARY: Amon walked in all the way his father walked in, served the idols his father served, and worshipped them. JFB's section on Amon frames this as the continuation of Manasseh's idolatry. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

22 COMMENTARY: Amon forsook the LORD God of his fathers and walked not in the LORD's way. JFB notes Amon's reign simply continued his father's apostasy. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

23 COMMENTARY: Amon's servants conspired against him and slew the king in his own house. JFB notes he was massacred by some of his own domestics. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

24 COMMENTARY: The people of the land slew all who had conspired against King Amon and made Josiah his son king in his stead. JFB notes the people slew the regicide conspirators and placed Josiah on the throne. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

26 COMMENTARY: Amon was buried in his sepulchre in the garden of Uzza, and Josiah his son reigned in his stead. JFB's section closes Amon's reign with this burial and Josiah's accession. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

No note for verse(s) 17, 25 — the public-domain sources consulted give no distinct explanation for these verses beyond the list itself, so nothing was invented for them.
