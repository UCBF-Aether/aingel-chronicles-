Study Notes — 2 Kings

Chapter 12

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: In the seventh year of Jehu, Jehoash began to reign, reigning forty years in Jerusalem, his mother Zibiah of Beersheba. JFB's section "Jehoash reigns well while Jehoiada lived" opens with this reign. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: Jehoash did right in the LORD's sight all his days wherein Jehoiada the priest instructed him. JFB qualifies this: his outward rectitude owed more to the salutary influence of his preserver and tutor Jehoiada than to his own mind's sincere dictates. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: But the high places were not taken away, and the people still sacrificed and burnt incense there. JFB explains the popular fondness for private rites in groves and hills was so inveterate that even powerful monarchs could not suppress it, and the difficulty grew after Athaliah's maladministration. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: Jehoash told the priests all the money of dedicated things brought into the LORD's house should be used for repairs. JFB lists the three levies: the half-shekel of everyone passing the account, redemption prices of devoted persons or things, and freewill offerings, the first two paid annually. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: The priests were to take the money, every man from his acquaintance, and repair the breaches of the house wherever found. JFB presents this as the young king's measure for repairing the temple by levying taxes. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: But in Jehoash's twenty-third year the priests had not repaired the breaches. JFB notes this mode of collection proved less productive than expected, chiefly through the priests' dilatoriness. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: Jehoash called Jehoiada and the priests and asked why they had not repaired the breaches. JFB explains a new arrangement was then proposed: a chest placed by the high priest at the temple entrance, into which door-keeping Levites put the people's repair money, separating building funds from priests' general money. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: The priests consented to receive no more money from the people, nor to repair the breaches themselves. JFB notes the duty of attending to the work no longer devolved on the priests but was undertaken by the king. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: Jehoiada took a chest, bored a hole in its lid, and set it beside the altar on the right as one entered the LORD's house, and the door-keeping priests put in all money brought to the house. JFB describes this chest as the means of separating repair money so people would give more liberally, knowing offerings were devoted to repairs. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: When they saw much money in the chest, the king's scribe and the high priest came, put it up in bags, and told it. JFB notes the king sent his secretary with the high priest's agent to count the chest money from time to time. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: They gave the counted money to those who did the work and had oversight of the LORD's house. JFB explains the overseers paid the workmen and purchased materials, and sums put in labelled, sealed bags were a common Eastern currency custom. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: The money went to masons, hewers of stone, and to buy timber and hewed stone to repair the breaches, and for all else needed to repair the house. JFB notes the overseers paid workmen and purchased all necessary materials. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: No silver bowls, snuffers, basons, trumpets, or vessels of gold or silver were made for the LORD's house from the money brought in. JFB explains that when repairs were completed, the surplus was appropriated to temple furniture, but repair money itself was not diverted to vessels. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: The money was given to the workmen, and they repaired the LORD's house with it. JFB notes the overseers' integrity was undoubted, so no account was exacted of how they applied the money. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: No reckoning was made with the men into whose hands the money was delivered to bestow on workmen, for they dealt faithfully. JFB stresses this undoubted integrity as the reason no account was exacted. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: The trespass money and sin money was not brought into the LORD's house; it was the priests'. JFB notes other moneys levied at the temple were left to the priests' disposal as the law directed. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: Hazael king of Syria went up, fought against Gath, took it, and set his face to go up to Jerusalem. JFB cross-refers this campaign to the fuller account in Chronicles. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

18 COMMENTARY: Jehoash king of Judah took all the hallowed things of Jehoshaphat, Jehoram, and Ahaziah, with his own hallowed things and all the gold in the treasuries, and sent them to Hazael, who went away from Jerusalem. JFB's section places this tribute after Hazael's advance on Jerusalem. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

20 COMMENTARY: Joash's servants arose, conspired, and slew him in the house of Millo going down to Silla. JFB heads this section "He is slain" and cross-refers the conspiracy to Chronicles. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

21 COMMENTARY: Jozachar son of Shimeath and Jehozabad son of Shomer, his servants, smote him, and he died, and they buried him with his fathers in the city of David, and Amaziah his son reigned in his stead. JFB's closing section records the assassination and succession. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

No note for verse(s) 19 — the public-domain sources consulted give no distinct explanation for these verses beyond the list itself, so nothing was invented for them.
