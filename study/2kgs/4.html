Study Notes — 2 Kings

Chapter 4

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: A widow of a prophet's wife cried to Elisha that her husband, who feared the LORD, was dead, and the creditor had come to take her two sons as bondmen. Henry notes those who leave families under debt know not what trouble they cause, and Elisha readily received the poor widow's complaint. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

2 COMMENTARY: Elisha asked what she had in the house, and she answered nothing save a pot of oil. Henry presents this as the small beginning from which God would supply her, showing the best way to help the distressed is to help them improve by industry what little they have. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

3 COMMENTARY: Elisha told her to borrow empty vessels from all her neighbours, and borrow not a few. Henry notes the oil would flow as long as she had vessels to receive it: we are never straitened in God, all our straitness is in ourselves. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

4 COMMENTARY: She was to shut the door on herself and her sons and pour out into all the vessels, setting aside those that were full. Henry observes the miracle was worked so as to show industry joined to God's supply. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

5 COMMENTARY: She did so, her sons bringing vessels to her while she poured out. Henry's lesson is that faith does not fail God's promise; he gives more than we ask, and were there more vessels, there is enough in God to fill them. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

6 COMMENTARY: When the vessels were full and her son said there was no more vessel, the oil stayed. Henry notes the Redeemer's all-sufficiency is only stayed from supplying sinners when no more apply to him for salvation. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

7 COMMENTARY: Elisha told her to sell the oil, pay her debt, and live with her sons on the rest. Henry stresses that though her creditors were hard, they must be paid even before provision for her children, for paying every just debt is a main law of the Christian religion. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

8 COMMENTARY: One day Elisha passed to Shunem, where a great woman constrained him to eat bread. Henry notes Elisha was well thought of for his late services, and a good man takes as much pleasure in serving others as in raising himself. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

9 COMMENTARY: The Shunammite told her husband she perceived Elisha was a holy man of God passing by continually. Henry presents her hospitality as the fruit of recognizing God's servant. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

10 COMMENTARY: She proposed making a little chamber on the wall with a bed, table, stool, and candlestick for Elisha whenever he came. Henry notes it is a happiness to dwell among one's own people who love and respect us, and to do them good. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

11 COMMENTARY: One day Elisha came, turned into the chamber, and lay there. Henry's outline treats this settled hospitality as the ground of the unasked mercy that would follow. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

12 COMMENTARY: Elisha told Gehazi to call the Shunammite, and she stood before him. Henry notes the LORD sees the secret wish suppressed in obedience to his will, and hears his servants' prayers for their benefactors. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

13 COMMENTARY: Elisha offered to speak for her to the king or the captain of the host, but she answered she dwelt among her own people. Henry remarks it would be well with many if they knew when they are really well off, and needed no great offices. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

14 COMMENTARY: When Elisha asked what could be done for her, Gehazi answered she had no child and her husband was old. Henry notes the LORD sees secret wishes and sends unasked-for, unexpected mercies. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

15 COMMENTARY: Elisha called her again, and she stood in the door. Henry stresses that the professions of men of God are not delusive like those of men of the world. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

16 COMMENTARY: Elisha promised that about this season, according to the time of life, she would embrace a son, though she begged him not to deceive her handmaid. Henry presents this as the unexpected mercy sent in answer to unspoken desire. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

17 COMMENTARY: The woman conceived and bore a son at the season Elisha had spoken. Henry's passage shows God's promise fulfilled exactly as spoken. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

18 COMMENTARY: When the child was grown, he went out one day to his father among the reapers. Henry introduces the sudden death that follows: all a mother's tenderness cannot keep alive a child of promise. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

19 COMMENTARY: The child cried, "My head, my head," and his father had a lad carry him to his mother. Henry notes the suddenness of the affliction that struck a child of prayer, given in love. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

20 COMMENTARY: The child sat on his mother's knees till noon, and then died. Henry praises the prudent, pious mother who guarded her lips under this sudden affliction, with not one peevish word escaping her. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

21 COMMENTARY: She laid the child on the bed of the man of God, shut the door, and went out. Henry observes her confidence in God's goodness, ready to believe he would restore what he had taken away. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

22 COMMENTARY: She asked her husband for a young man and an ass to run to the man of God and return. Henry notes she had not thought it enough to have Elisha's help sometimes in her family, but attended on public worship and sought him at once. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

23 COMMENTARY: Her husband asked why she would go that day, neither new moon nor sabbath, and she answered, "It shall be well." Henry echoes her answer: all is well that God does, and all is well with us that stay behind if affliction furthers us on our way thither. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

24 COMMENTARY: She saddled an ass and told her servant to drive and go forward, slackening not for her unless she bade him. Henry presents her urgent, resolute journey to the prophet. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

25 COMMENTARY: She came to the man of God at Mount Carmel, and when Elisha saw her afar off he sent Gehazi to meet her. Henry notes it well becomes men of God to inquire about the welfare of their friends and families. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

26 COMMENTARY: Gehazi was to ask whether it was well with her, her husband, and the child, and she answered, "It is well." Henry explains: all well, and yet the child dead in the house — yes, all is well that God does. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

27 COMMENTARY: When she came to the man of God on the hill she caught him by the feet, and Gehazi would have thrust her away, but Elisha said to let her alone, for her soul was vexed and the LORD had hid it from him. Henry praises her guarded lips and great faith under affliction. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

28 COMMENTARY: She asked, "Did I desire a son of my lord? did I not say, Do not deceive me?" Henry notes her confidence: he who wrought such faith would not disappoint it. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

29 COMMENTARY: Elisha told Gehazi to gird up his loins, take his staff, and go, saluting no man by the way, and lay the staff on the child's face. Henry presents this as the prophet's first means toward restoring the child. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

30 COMMENTARY: The mother swore by the LORD and by Elisha's soul that she would not leave him, so Elisha arose and followed her. Henry's theme is her steadfast faith that would not be put off with a servant's errand. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

31 COMMENTARY: Gehazi went before them and laid the staff on the child's face, but there was neither voice nor hearing, so he returned saying the child was not awaked. Henry notes the minister cannot give divine life, yet must use every means with earnestness as if he could. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

32 COMMENTARY: When Elisha came into the house, the child was dead and laid upon his bed. Henry's passage moves to the prophet's own fervent prayer for the dead child. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

33 COMMENTARY: Elisha went in, shut the door upon them two, and prayed unto the LORD. Henry stresses that those who would convey spiritual life to dead souls must feel deeply for their case and labour fervently in prayer. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

34 COMMENTARY: Elisha lay upon the child, mouth to mouth, eyes to eyes, hands to hands, and the child's flesh waxed warm. Henry presents this earnest, bodily intercession as the means God honoured. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

35 COMMENTARY: Elisha walked in the house to and fro, went up again and stretched himself upon the child, and the child sneezed seven times and opened his eyes. Henry concludes that Elisha cried unto God in faith, and the beloved son was restored alive to his mother. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

36 COMMENTARY: Elisha called Gehazi to call the Shunammite, and when she came in he said, "Take up thy son." Henry's lesson is that faith which trusted God through death received its child back alive. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

37 COMMENTARY: She went in, fell at Elisha's feet, bowed to the ground, took up her son, and went out. Henry notes, "O woman, great is thy faith!" — he that wrought it would not disappoint it. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

38 COMMENTARY: Elisha came again to Gilgal during a dearth, with the sons of the prophets sitting before him, and he told his servant to set on the great pot and seethe pottage. Henry notes there was a famine of bread but not of hearing God's word, for Elisha had the prophets sitting before him to hear his wisdom. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

39 COMMENTARY: One went out to gather herbs, found a wild vine, and gathered wild gourds, shredding them into the pottage without knowing them. Henry observes that if a mess of pottage is all our dinner, this great prophet had no better for himself and his guests. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

40 COMMENTARY: As they ate, they cried out, "There is death in the pot," and could not eat. Henry warns the table often becomes a snare, and when receiving life's supports we must keep up an expectation of death and a fear of sin. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

41 COMMENTARY: Elisha said to bring meal, cast it into the pot, and pour out for the people, and there was no harm in the pot. Henry notes Elisha made hurtful food safe and wholesome, and we must acknowledge God's goodness in making our food nourishing: "I am the LORD that healeth thee." — Matthew Henry (Concise)

42 COMMENTARY: A man from Baalshalisha brought the man of God twenty loaves of barley from the firstfruits, with full ears of corn. Henry presents this gift as the small provision God would multiply. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

43 COMMENTARY: Elisha's servitor objected, asking what setting this before a hundred men would do, but Elisha said again to give the people that they might eat, for the LORD said they would eat and leave thereof. Henry notes Elisha made a little food go a great way, having freely received and freely giving. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

44 COMMENTARY: He set it before them, and they ate and left thereof according to the word of the LORD. Henry concludes God has promised to abundantly bless his church's provision and satisfy her poor with bread: whom he feeds, he fills, and what he blesses comes to much. — Matthew Henry (Concise)
