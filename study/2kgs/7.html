Study Notes — 2 Kings

Chapter 7

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Elisha said that by tomorrow about this time a measure of fine flour would sell for a shekel, and two measures of barley for a shekel, in Samaria's gate. Henry notes man's extremity is God's opportunity to make his power glorious: his time to appear for his people is when their strength is gone. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

2 COMMENTARY: The lord on whose hand the king leaned answered that even if the LORD made windows in heaven, this could not be, and Elisha replied he would see it with his eyes but not eat of it. Henry warns unbelief greatly dishonours God and deprives men of favours he designed for them; such shall see the promise at a distance but never taste it. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

3 COMMENTARY: Four leprous men at the gate's entrance asked one another why they sat there until they died. Henry's passage shows God using the outcasts at the gate as his messengers of deliverance. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

4 COMMENTARY: The lepers reasoned that entering the famine-struck city meant death, sitting still meant death, and going to the Syrians might at least bring life if the Syrians saved them alive. Henry notes providence ordered their coming just as the Syrians fled. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

5 COMMENTARY: They rose in the twilight to go to the Syrian camp, and when they came to its uttermost part, no man was there. Henry observes God can make the stoutest heart tremble, and those who will not fear God he can make fear at the shaking of a leaf. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

6 COMMENTARY: The LORD had made the Syrian host hear a noise of chariots and horses, as of a great host, so they said the king of Israel had hired the Hittite and Egyptian kings against them. Henry presents this unseen terror as God's own doing. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

7 COMMENTARY: The Syrians arose and fled in the twilight, leaving their tents, horses, asses, and camp as it was, fleeing for their lives. Henry notes providence ordered that the lepers came as soon as the Syrians were fled. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

8 COMMENTARY: The lepers went into one tent, ate and drank, and carried away silver, gold, and raiment, hiding it, then went into another tent and did likewise. Henry observes their consciences would soon tell them mischief would befall them if they cared only for themselves. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

9 COMMENTARY: They said to one another they did not well, for this was a day of good tidings and they held their peace, and if they tarried till morning, mischief would come upon them, so they resolved to tell the king's household. Henry draws the evangelistic lesson: those who have found Christ's unsearchable riches will not long delay to report the good tidings to others. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

10 COMMENTARY: They came and called to the city's porter, telling him they had come to the Syrian camp and found no man there, only horses and asses tied and tents as they were. Henry notes natural humanity and fear of punishment check selfishness, but love to Christ gladly shares good things with brethren. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

11 COMMENTARY: The porter called the other porters, and they told it to the king's house within. Henry's outline shows the good tidings spreading from the gate to the palace. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

12 COMMENTARY: The king arose in the night and told his servants the Syrians had surely hidden themselves in the field, planning to catch the Israelites alive when they came out of the city. Henry presents the king's suspicion as the foil to God's plain deliverance. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

13 COMMENTARY: A servant proposed taking five remaining horses — like all the multitude of Israel left, as if already consumed — and sending to see. Henry notes Israel's wants were supplied in a way they little thought of, encouraging dependence on God's power in the greatest straits. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

14 COMMENTARY: They took two chariot horses, and the king sent after the Syrian host to go and see. Henry's theme is God's promise safely relied on, for no word of his shall fall to the ground. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

15 COMMENTARY: The messengers followed the Syrians to Jordan, finding the way full of garments and vessels the Syrians had cast away in their haste. Henry notes this as the evidence of a flight ordered by God. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

16 COMMENTARY: The people went out and spoiled the Syrian tents, so a measure of fine flour sold for a shekel and two measures of barley for a shekel, according to the LORD's word. Henry observes God's promise was fulfilled exactly, supplying Israel's wants unexpectedly. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

17 COMMENTARY: The king appointed the lord on whose hand he leaned to have charge of the gate, and the people trode upon him in the gate, and he died, as the man of God had spoken. Henry notes the unbelieving nobleman saw the plenty, to silence and shame his unbelief, but did not eat of it. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

18 COMMENTARY: All came to pass as Elisha had spoken to the king: two measures of barley for a shekel and a measure of fine flour for a shekel in the gate of Samaria. Henry stresses God's promise may be safely relied on, for no word of his shall fall to the ground. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

19 COMMENTARY: The lord had answered the man of God that even if the LORD made windows in heaven, this could not be, and Elisha had said he would see it with his eyes but not eat thereof. Henry draws the lesson of how deeply God resents distrust of his power, providence, and promise. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

20 COMMENTARY: So it fell out to him: the people trode upon him in the gate, and he died. Henry closes with the certainty of God's threatenings upon the guilty and the uncertainty of life and its enjoyments. — Matthew Henry (Concise)
