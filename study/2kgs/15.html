Study Notes — 2 Kings

Chapter 15

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: In the twenty-seventh year of Jeroboam king of Israel, Azariah son of Amaziah king of Judah began to reign. JFB notes Judah's throne may have stood vacant eleven or twelve years after Amaziah's murder, since Azariah was only four when his father died and a regency governed his minority. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: Azariah was sixteen when he began to reign and reigned fifty-two years in Jerusalem, his mother Jecholiah of Jerusalem. JFB describes his reign as very active and eventful, fully related in Chronicles. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: Azariah did right in the LORD's sight, according to all his father Amaziah had done. JFB notes the brief formula records his religious policy, though his reign's full character appears in Chronicles. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: Save that the high places were not removed, and the people still sacrificed and burnt incense there. JFB's formula discussion covers this standing exception to Azariah's good reign. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: The LORD smote the king so he was a leper to his death, dwelling in a separate house, while Jotham his son judged the people. JFB explains Azariah, elated by great power, presumptuously arrogated priestly functions, and was punished with leprosy, equivalent to death since it excluded him from society, while his son Jotham administered the kingdom as viceroy. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: Azariah slept with his fathers, was buried with them in the city of David, and Jotham his son reigned in his stead. JFB notes after his long reign he died and was buried in the royal burying-field, though not in the royal cemetery of the city of David. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: In the thirty-eighth year of Azariah, Zachariah son of Jeroboam reigned over Israel in Samaria for six months. JFB notes an interregnum of ten, twelve, or even twenty-two years may have preceded his accession, and he pursued the calf-worship policy. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: Zachariah did evil in the LORD's sight as his fathers had done, departing not from Jeroboam's sins. JFB notes his short reign pursued the religious policy of calf worship. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: Shallum son of Jabesh conspired against Zachariah, smote him before the people, slew him, and reigned in his stead. JFB notes his reign was abruptly terminated by violence, fulfilling the prophecy to Jehu that his family would hold Israel's throne for four generations. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: This was the LORD's word spoken to Jehu, that his sons would sit on Israel's throne to the fourth generation, and so it came to pass. JFB lists the four generations — Jehoahaz, Joash, Jeroboam, and Zechariah — and notes public knowledge of this prediction may have prompted Shallum's murderous design. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: Shallum son of Jabesh began to reign in the thirty-ninth year of Uzziah king of Judah, reigning a full month in Samaria. JFB notes he was opposed and slain by Menahem, who, according to Josephus, commanded forces besieging Tirzah and marched directly against the usurper. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: Menahem son of Gadi went up from Tirzah to Samaria, smote Shallum son of Jabesh, slew him, and reigned in his stead. JFB explains Menahem raised the siege of Tirzah, twelve miles east of Samaria, and marched against Shallum. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: Menahem smote Tiphsah and all within it and its coasts from Tirzah, because they opened not to him, ripping up all its women with child. JFB identifies Tiphsah as Thapsacus on the Euphrates, Solomon's border city, which, refusing to open its gates, was taken by storm and spoiled with barbarous excesses regardless of age or sex. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: In the thirty-ninth year of Azariah, Menahem son of Gadi began to reign over Israel in Samaria for ten years. JFB notes his government followed his predecessors' religious policy. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

18 COMMENTARY: Menahem did evil in the LORD's sight, departing not all his days from Jeroboam's sins. JFB notes his reign was conducted on the religious policy of his predecessors. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

19 COMMENTARY: Pul king of Assyria came against the land, and Menahem gave him a thousand talents of silver, that his hand might be with him to confirm the kingdom in his hand. JFB identifies Pul as the first Assyrian king after Nimrod mentioned in biblical history, and explains Menahem turned the marching enemy into a confederate for his usurped dominion by this tribute. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

20 COMMENTARY: Menahem exacted the money from Israel, from all mighty men of wealth, fifty shekels of silver each, to give to Assyria's king, and Assyria's king turned back and stayed not in the land. JFB notes the tribute, equal to £262,200, was raised by a tax on Israel's grandees and bribed Pul to return home. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

22 COMMENTARY: Menahem slept with his fathers, and Pekahiah his son reigned in his stead. JFB's section on Pekahiah notes several months intervened between Menahem's death and Pekahiah's accession, probably owing to a contest about the throne. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

23 COMMENTARY: In the fiftieth year of Azariah, Pekahiah son of Menahem began to reign over Israel in Samaria for two years. JFB notes the date suggests months intervened after Menahem's death, probably from a contest about the throne. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

24 COMMENTARY: Pekahiah did evil in the LORD's sight, departing not from Jeroboam's sins. JFB's section on his reign frames it within the continuing calf-worship policy. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

25 COMMENTARY: Pekah son of Remaliah, a captain of Pekahiah, conspired against him, smote him in Samaria in the palace of the king's house, with Argob and Arieh and fifty Gileadites, and reigned in his stead. JFB notes Argob and Arieh were more probably influential friends of the king murdered along with him than the captain's accomplices. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

27 COMMENTARY: In the fifty-second year of Azariah, Pekah son of Remaliah began to reign over Israel in Samaria for twenty years. JFB's section on Pekah's reign opens with this accession. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

28 COMMENTARY: Pekah did evil in the LORD's sight, departing not from Jeroboam's sins. JFB's section frames his reign within the same apostasy. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

29 COMMENTARY: In Pekah's days Tiglathpileser king of Assyria came and took Ijon, Abel-beth-maachah, Janoah, Kedesh, Hazor, Gilead, and Galilee, all Naphtali, carrying them captive to Assyria. JFB notes Tiglathpileser, who succeeded Pul and is supposed an usurper since he gives no genealogy, invaded Syria, and the places are listed as conquered in the invasion's progress, his annals having been found in the Nimroud mound. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

30 COMMENTARY: Hoshea son of Elah conspired against Pekah son of Remaliah, smote him, slew him, and reigned in his stead in the twentieth year of Jotham son of Uzziah. JFB notes Hoshea did not obtain the kingdom until about nine or ten years after the crime, and the date means the twentieth year after Jotham's reign began, since the historian had not yet introduced Ahaz. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

32 COMMENTARY: In the second year of Pekah son of Remaliah, Jotham son of Uzziah king of Judah began to reign. JFB's section on Jotham's reign opens with this accession. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

33 COMMENTARY: Jotham was twenty-five when he began to reign and reigned sixteen years in Jerusalem, his mother Jerusha daughter of Zadok. JFB notes he began to reign alone at twenty-five, having already ruled as his father's viceroy. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

34 COMMENTARY: Jotham did right in the LORD's sight, according to all his father Uzziah had done. JFB's section presents his reign as following his father's good pattern. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

35 COMMENTARY: Howbeit the high places were not removed, and the people still sacrificed and burned incense there, though Jotham built the higher gate of the LORD's house. JFB notes the higher gate was not the temple itself but one of its courts, probably leading into the palace. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

37 COMMENTARY: In those days the LORD began to send against Judah Rezin king of Syria and Pekah son of Remaliah. JFB notes this is the first intimation of the hostile feelings of Israel and Syria toward Judah, leading to their alliance and joint war preparations, though war was not actually waged until Ahaz's reign. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

38 COMMENTARY: Jotham slept with his fathers, was buried with them in the city of David his father, and Ahaz his son reigned in his stead. JFB's section closes Jotham's reign with this burial and succession. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

No note for verse(s) 6, 11, 15, 21, 26, 31, 36 — the public-domain sources consulted give no distinct explanation for these verses beyond the list itself, so nothing was invented for them.
