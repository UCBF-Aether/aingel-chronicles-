Study Notes — 2 Kings

Chapter 20

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: In those days Hezekiah was sick unto death, and Isaiah son of Amoz came saying, "Thus saith the LORD, Set thine house in order; for thou shalt die, and not live." JFB notes this sudden severe illness occurred in the very year of the Assyrian invasion, and setting his house in order referred chiefly to the kingdom, since he had as yet no son, while the disease was malignant and mortal unless God miraculously interposed. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: Hezekiah turned his face to the wall and prayed unto the LORD. JFB notes this was not like Ahab's fretful discontent, but to secure a better opportunity for prayer. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: Hezekiah besought the LORD to remember how he had walked before him in truth and with a perfect heart, doing what was good in his sight, and he wept sore. JFB explains his thoughts turned to David's promise, which he had kept as faithfully as human infirmity admitted, free from great crimes that cut life short, grieving partly from love of life, partly from the Mosaic dispensation's obscurity about immortality, and partly from his reformation plans being frustrated. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: Before Isaiah had gone out into the middle court, the LORD's word came to him. JFB identifies the middle court as that of the royal castle. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: Isaiah was told to turn again and tell Hezekiah, captain of God's people, that the LORD, the God of David his father, had heard his prayer and seen his tears, and would heal him, so that on the third day he would go up to the LORD's house. JFB calls this an immediate answer assuring the LORD was mindful of his promise to David, to be accomplished both by prolonging Hezekiah's life and delivering him from Assyria, with perfect recovery in three days showing the cure's miraculous character. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: The LORD would add fifteen years to Hezekiah's days and deliver him and Jerusalem from Assyria's king, defending the city for his own sake and David's sake. JFB notes the promise joined prolongation of life with deliverance from the Assyrians. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: Isaiah said to take a lump of figs, and they laid it on the boil, and Hezekiah recovered. JFB notes figs are used by Arab physicians to ripen and soothe inflammatory ulcers, and the text gives no hint plague was raging in Jerusalem. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: Hezekiah asked Isaiah what sign the LORD would heal him and he would go up to the LORD's house on the third day. JFB explains his recovery in nature's course was so unlooked for that he asked for a token to justify reliance on the prophet's word. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: Isaiah said the sign would be that the LORD would do the thing he had spoken: the shadow would go forward ten degrees or go back ten degrees. JFB explains the dial's original word meant degrees or steps, and many supposed it a stair whose shadows marked the sun's course, though more probably it was a proper instrument imported from Babylon by Ahaz. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: Hezekiah answered it was a light thing for the shadow to go down ten degrees, but let it return backward ten degrees. JFB notes the king specified this sign, and it was granted to him. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: Isaiah cried unto the LORD, and he brought the shadow ten degrees backward, by which it had gone down in Ahaz's dial. JFB notes the retrogression was miraculously accomplished by God's omnipotent power, temporary, local, and intended only for Hezekiah and his court's satisfaction. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: At that time Berodachbaladan son of Baladan, king of Babylon, sent letters and a present to Hezekiah, having heard he had been sick. JFB identifies Berodachbaladan as the first king of Babylon mentioned in sacred history, formerly Assyria's viceroys having ruled Babylon, who threw off the yoke and resisted Assyria, and whose congratulations likely accompanied proposals for a defensive alliance against their common enemy. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: Hezekiah hearkened to them and showed them all his precious things, silver, gold, spices, precious ointment, armour, and all found in his treasures, hiding nothing. JFB notes that though he had paid Sennacherib so much tribute as to exhaust his treasury, presents had since come from various quarters, and possibly the Assyrian camp's spoils restored what he had paid; yet the vain display offended his divine liege lord, who sent Isaiah to reprove him, and it baited the rapacious foreigners' cupidity. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: Isaiah came to Hezekiah asking what these men had said and whence they came, and Hezekiah answered they had come from far, from Babylon. JFB presents Isaiah's visit as the reproof sent for the vain display. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: Isaiah asked what they had seen in Hezekiah's house, and Hezekiah answered there was nothing among his treasures he had not showed them. JFB notes the king's answer showed how elated he was by the compliment of their visit. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: Isaiah said to Hezekiah, "Hear the word of the LORD." JFB's section on the Babylonian embassy turns here to the prophetic sentence on the display. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: Isaiah declared days would come when all in Hezekiah's house and all his fathers had laid up would be carried to Babylon, with nothing left. JFB notes these rapacious foreigners would at no distant period return, pillage the country, and transfer all the displayed possessions to Babylon. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

18 COMMENTARY: Of Hezekiah's sons issuing from him, some would be taken away to be eunuchs in Babylon's king's palace. JFB notes his posterity would be carried as court attendants in Babylon. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

19 COMMENTARY: Hezekiah said to Isaiah, "Good is the word of the LORD which thou hast spoken," adding there would be peace and truth in his days. JFB notes this indicated humble, pious resignation, and the concluding part, uttered after a pause as an ejaculation to himself, expressed thankfulness that judgment would be suspended during his own lifetime. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

21 COMMENTARY: Hezekiah slept with his fathers, and Manasseh his son reigned in his stead. JFB's chapter closes with this succession after Hezekiah's lengthened life and deliverance. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

No note for verse(s) 20 — the public-domain sources consulted give no distinct explanation for these verses beyond the list itself, so nothing was invented for them.
