Study Notes — 2 Kings

Chapter 2

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: When the LORD would take up Elijah into heaven by a whirlwind, Elijah went with Elisha from Gilgal. Henry notes the LORD had let Elijah know his time was at hand, so he went to the schools of the prophets to give his last exhortations and blessing. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

2 COMMENTARY: Elijah told Elisha to tarry at Gilgal, but Elisha swore by the LORD and by Elijah's life that he would not leave him. Henry urges followers of Christ not to come short by tiring at last, as Elisha refused to leave when the parting blessing was near. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

3 COMMENTARY: The sons of the prophets at Bethel told Elisha the LORD would take away his master that day, and he answered that he knew it and they should hold their peace. Henry frames this journey as Elijah's farewell to the prophetic schools. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

4 COMMENTARY: Elijah again told Elisha to tarry, this time at Bethel, for the LORD had sent him to Jericho, but Elisha again refused to leave him. Henry presents Elisha's steadfast attendance as the proper means of obtaining much of his master's spirit. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

5 COMMENTARY: The sons of the prophets at Jericho likewise told Elisha his master would be taken away that day, and he again answered that he knew it and they should hold their peace. Henry notes the removal of Elijah was a type of Christ's ascension and the opening of heaven to believers. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

6 COMMENTARY: Elijah told Elisha to tarry at Jericho, for the LORD had sent him to Jordan, but Elisha swore once more he would not leave him, so they both went on. Henry's lesson is perseverance to the end of the journey. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

7 COMMENTARY: Fifty men of the sons of the prophets stood to view afar off while Elijah and Elisha stood by Jordan. Henry notes these witnesses watched the parting that would confirm Elisha's succession. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

8 COMMENTARY: Elijah wrapped his mantle together, smote the waters, and they divided so that the two went over on dry ground. Henry observes the waters of Jordan, which once yielded to the ark, now yielded to the prophet's mantle as a token of God's presence, and death itself is the Jordan the faithful must pass through. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

9 COMMENTARY: Once over, Elijah asked Elisha what he should do for him before being taken away, and Elisha asked for a double portion of his spirit. Henry notes that fulness from which prophets and apostles had all their supply still exists, and we are told to ask large supplies from it. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

10 COMMENTARY: Elijah said Elisha had asked a hard thing, but if he saw him when taken, it would be so, and if not, not. Henry treats diligent attendance on Elijah, particularly in his last hours, as the proper means for Elisha to obtain much of his spirit. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

11 COMMENTARY: As they went on talking, a chariot of fire and horses of fire appeared and parted them, and Elijah went up by a whirlwind into heaven. Henry notes the chariot appeared like fire — glorious, not for burning but brightness — and Elijah was found engaged in serious discourse about God's kingdom when his Lord came for him. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

12 COMMENTARY: Elisha cried, "My father, my father, the chariot of Israel, and the horsemen thereof," then rent his clothes in two pieces. Henry observes Elijah was to Israel, by his counsels, reproofs, and prayers, better than the strongest force of chariot and horse, and the world could ill spare him. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

13 COMMENTARY: Elisha took up Elijah's mantle that had fallen from him and went back to stand by the bank of Jordan. Henry says Elijah left his mantle as a token of the Spirit's descent, more than thousands of gold and silver, and Christ likewise bequeathed his gospel to his disciples. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

14 COMMENTARY: Elisha smote the waters with the mantle, asking, "Where is the LORD God of Elijah?" and the waters divided, so he went over. Henry notes Elisha inquired after God when creature-comforts were removed, and asks what it avails us to have the mantles of the departed if we have not their spirit and their God. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

15 COMMENTARY: The sons of the prophets at Jericho saw him and said the spirit of Elijah rested on Elisha, then bowed to the ground before him. Henry presents the divided river as proof God's people need not fear passing through the Jordan of death as on dry ground. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

16 COMMENTARY: They offered fifty strong men to go seek Elijah, lest the Spirit had cast him on some mountain or valley. Henry calls their proposed search needless: traversing hills and valleys will never bring us to Elijah, but following his holy faith and zeal will in due time. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

17 COMMENTARY: When they urged Elisha till he was ashamed, he said to send, and fifty men sought three days but found Elijah not. Henry notes wise men may yield for peace's sake to what their judgment counts needless and fruitless. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

18 COMMENTARY: When they returned to Elisha at Jericho, he said, "Did I not say unto you, Go not?" Henry's point stands: the search confirmed what Elisha already knew, that Elijah had been taken to heaven. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

19 COMMENTARY: The men of Jericho told Elisha the city's situation was pleasant but its water was naught and the ground barren. Henry observes prophets should make every place they come to better for them, sweetening bitter spirits and making barren souls fruitful. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

20 COMMENTARY: Elisha asked for a new cruse with salt in it, and they brought it to him. Henry treats the salt cast into the water as an apt emblem of grace's effect on the sinful heart. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

21 COMMENTARY: Elisha cast the salt into the spring, saying the LORD had healed the waters so there would be no more death or barren land from there. Henry notes the word of God, like that salt, sweetens bitter spirits and makes barren souls fruitful. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

22 COMMENTARY: The waters were healed to this day according to Elisha's word. Henry adds that whole families, towns, and cities sometimes take a new appearance through the preaching of the gospel, wickedness changed into fruitfulness in works of righteousness. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

23 COMMENTARY: Elisha went up to Bethel, and little children came out of the city mocking him, saying, "Go up, thou bald head." Henry notes they bade him "go up" in reflection on Elijah's taking up into heaven, abusing Elisha's character as God's prophet. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

24 COMMENTARY: Elisha turned, looked on them, and cursed them in the name of the LORD, and two she bears came out of the wood and tare forty-two of them. Henry stresses the curse was not causeless and Elisha acted by divine impulse, for the LORD must be glorified as a righteous God who hates sin and will reckon for it. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

25 COMMENTARY: Elisha went from there to Mount Carmel, and from there returned to Samaria. Henry closes with the warning to young persons not to mock any for defects, especially not to scoff at any for well-doing, and to parents to train children betimes. — Matthew Henry (Concise)
