Study Notes — 2 Kings

Chapter 1

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: After Ahab's death, Moab rebelled against Israel. Henry notes that when Ahaziah rebelled against the LORD, Moab revolted from him: sin weakens and impoverishes, and man's revolt from God is often punished by the rebellion of those who owe him subjection. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

2 COMMENTARY: Ahaziah fell through a lattice in his upper chamber in Samaria and was sick, sending messengers to inquire of Baalzebub, god of Ekron, whether he would recover. Henry observes that wherever we go there is but a step between us and death, and a man's house, though his castle, cannot secure him against God's judgments. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

3 COMMENTARY: The angel of the LORD told Elijah to meet the king's messengers and ask whether there was no God in Israel that they went to inquire of Baalzebub. Henry's lesson is that those who will not inquire of God's word for comfort shall hear it to their terror, whether they will or no. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

4 COMMENTARY: Elijah was to tell Ahaziah that because he had sent to Baalzebub, he would not come down from his bed but would surely die. Henry notes the whole creation, groaning under man's sin, will at length sink and break like this lattice, and he is never safe who has God for his enemy. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

5 COMMENTARY: The messengers returned to Ahaziah sooner than expected, prompting the king to ask why they had come back. Henry frames their return as the first sign that God's word, not Baalzebub's oracle, would decide the king's fate. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

6 COMMENTARY: The messengers reported that a man had met them and sent them back with the word that Ahaziah would surely die for inquiring of Baalzebub. Henry stresses that the prophet's message confronted the king's idolatry directly. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

7 COMMENTARY: The king asked what manner of man had come up to meet them and spoken these words. Henry's passage shows Ahaziah, thunderstruck by the prophet's words, seeking to identify the man who dared pronounce his doom. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

8 COMMENTARY: They described a hairy man with a girdle of leather about his loins, and the king said, "It is Elijah the Tishbite." Henry treats Elijah's recognizable figure as the voice of God confronting a king who had turned from him. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

9 COMMENTARY: The king sent a captain with fifty men to Elijah, who sat on a hill; the captain ordered the man of God to come down by the king's command. Henry calls these haughty, daring sinners, whose challenge Elijah would answer not to secure himself but to prove his mission. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

10 COMMENTARY: Elijah answered that if he were a man of God, fire would come down from heaven and consume the captain and his fifty, and fire did so. Henry notes Elijah called fire to reveal God's wrath from heaven against ungodliness, acting by a divine impulse. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

11 COMMENTARY: The king sent another captain with fifty, who repeated the command to come down quickly. Henry's theme is obstinacy: the fatal end of the first captain did not teach the second submission. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

12 COMMENTARY: Elijah answered again, and fire from heaven consumed the second captain and his fifty. Henry observes that the LORD judges men's practices by their principles, and his judgment is according to truth. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

13 COMMENTARY: The third captain went up, fell on his knees before Elijah, and begged that his life and the lives of his fifty servants might be precious in Elijah's sight. Henry notes this captain humbled himself and cast himself on the mercy of God and Elijah, learning submission from the fatal end of obstinacy in others. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

14 COMMENTARY: The third captain pleaded that fire had consumed the two former captains and their fifties, and begged that his life be precious. Henry draws the general lesson that there is nothing to be got by contending with God. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

15 COMMENTARY: The angel of the LORD told Elijah to go down with the captain and not be afraid of him. Henry notes the courage of faith has often struck terror into the proudest sinner, and asks who can harm those whom God shelters. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

16 COMMENTARY: Elijah went down to the king and repeated the sentence: because Ahaziah had sent messengers to inquire of Baalzebub, as if there were no God in Israel, he would not come down from his bed but would surely die. Henry observes Ahaziah was so thunderstruck that neither he nor any about him offered the prophet violence. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

17 COMMENTARY: Ahaziah died according to the word of the LORD spoken by Elijah, and Jehoram reigned in his stead because Ahaziah had no son. Henry's closing warning is that many who think to prosper in sin are called hence, like Ahaziah, when they do not expect it. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

18 COMMENTARY: The rest of Ahaziah's acts are written in the book of the chronicles of the kings of Israel. Henry ends the chapter urging all to seek the LORD while he may be found, before a sudden end like Ahaziah's. — Matthew Henry (Concise)
