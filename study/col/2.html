Study Notes — Colossians

Chapter 2

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Paul would have them know what great conflict he had for them, for Laodicea, and for all who had not seen his face, since a soul prospers when it has clear knowledge of the truth as it is in Jesus. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: His aim was that their hearts might be comforted, knit together in love, and brought to the full assurance of understanding in acknowledging the mystery of God, of the Father, and of Christ. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: In Christ are hid all the treasures of wisdom and knowledge, hid not from believers but for them, displayed in his person and redemption though hidden from proud unbelievers. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: Paul warned them lest any man beguile them with enticing words, since many are ruined by the false disguises and fair appearances of evil principles. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: Though absent in the flesh, Paul was with them in spirit, joying and beholding their order and the stedfastness of their faith in Christ. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: As they had received Christ Jesus the Lord, consenting to him and taking him for theirs, so they were to walk in him. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: They were to be rooted and built up in him and stablished in the faith as taught, abounding therein with thanksgiving, since no one can be built up in Christ without first being founded upon him. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: They were to beware lest any spoil them through philosophy and vain deceit after the tradition of men and the rudiments of the world, not after Christ, since vain philosophy pleases men's fancies while hindering their faith. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: In Christ dwells all the fulness of the Godhead bodily, so believers have in him the substance of all the shadows of the ceremonial law. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: Believers are complete in him, who is the head of all principality and power, and Henry notes that to be complete is to be furnished with all things necessary for salvation. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: In him they are circumcised with the circumcision made without hands, putting off the body of the sins of the flesh, which Henry reads as the circumcision of the heart and crucifixion of the flesh. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: Buried with him in baptism, they are risen with him through faith in the operation of God who raised him from the dead, setting forth death and burial to sin and resurrection to newness of life. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: Though dead in sins and the uncircumcision of their flesh, they have been quickened together with him, having all trespasses forgiven, since Christ's death was the death of their sins and his resurrection the quickening of their souls. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: The handwriting of ordinances against them, a yoke to the Jews and a partition-wall to the Gentiles, was blotted out and taken out of the way, nailed to his cross, now that the substance had come and the shadows fled. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: Having spoiled principalities and powers, he made a show of them openly, triumphing over them in the cross, so that the devil and all the powers of hell were conquered and disarmed by the dying Redeemer. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: Let no man therefore judge them in meat or drink, or in respect of a holyday, new moon, or sabbath days, since bigoted judgments about such things troubled believers needlessly. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: Those rites were a shadow of things to come, but the body is of Christ, and all the defects of the ceremonial law are made up in the gospel by his complete sacrifice. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: Let no man beguile them of their reward in voluntary humility and worshipping of angels, intruding into unseen things and vainly puffed up by a fleshly mind, since such seeming humility really hides pride. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: Those who worship angels do not hold the Head, from whom the whole body, knit together by joints and bands, grows with the increase of God, and it is an insult to Christ, the only Mediator, to use any intercessors but him. — Matthew Henry

20 COMMENTARY: If they are dead with Christ from the rudiments of the world, why should they, as though living in the world, be subject to ordinances, since true believers cannot live in the fashions of the world. — Matthew Henry

21 COMMENTARY: The ordinances of touch not, taste not, handle not are the commandments and doctrines of men, and Henry notes that what the Lord has left indifferent should be left to like freedom. — Matthew Henry

22 COMMENTARY: Those things are all to perish with the using, and the commandments and doctrines of men cannot stand against the appointments of the gospel. — Matthew Henry

23 COMMENTARY: Such things have indeed a show of wisdom in will-worship and humility and neglecting of the body, but they are not in any honour to God, tending only to gratify self-will, self-wisdom, and self-righteousness. — Matthew Henry
