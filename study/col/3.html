Study Notes — Colossians

Chapter 3

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: If believers have risen with Christ, they are to seek the things above, where Christ sits at the right hand of God, making heaven their scope and aim rather than living as they list. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: They are to set their affection on things above, not on things on the earth, since heaven and earth are contrary to one another and both cannot be followed together. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: Believers are dead to sin, its dominion broken and its power gradually subdued by grace, and their life is hid with Christ in God, safe with one whom they have not yet seen. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: When Christ, who is their life, shall appear, they also shall appear with him in glory, at the general assembling of all the redeemed. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: They are therefore to mortify their members upon the earth, killing and suppressing corrupt workings as weeds or vermin that spread and destroy, making no provision for carnal indulgences. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: For the sake of such sins the wrath of God comes on the children of disobedience, which is why mortification is necessary, since sins not killed will kill us. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: The Colossians had themselves once walked in these sins when they lived in them, but the gospel changes the higher as well as the lower powers of the soul. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: Now they are to put off all anger, wrath, malice, blasphemy, and filthy communication, keeping continual opposition to all corrupt workings and avoiding occasions of sin. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: They are not to lie to one another, seeing they have put off the old man with his deeds, and Henry notes the duty of holiness rests on everyone because Christ is the Christian's All. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: They have put on the new man, renewed in knowledge after the image of him that created him, as the gospel supports right reason and conscience over appetite and passion. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: In this new man there is neither Greek nor Jew, circumcision nor uncircumcision, Barbarian, Scythian, bond nor free, since no difference of country or condition exempts anyone from the duty of holiness. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: As the elect of God, holy and beloved, they are to put on bowels of mercies, kindness, humbleness of mind, and meekness, being lowly and compassionate towards all. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: They are to forbear and forgive one another, even where quarrels arise, imitating the forgiveness through which they themselves are saved. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: Above all these things they are to put on charity, which is the bond of perfectness, binding the other graces together. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: The peace of God is to rule in their hearts, to which they are called in one body, and thanksgiving helps make them agreeable to all men. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: The word of Christ is to dwell in them richly in all wisdom, teaching and admonishing one another in psalms and hymns and spiritual songs, since the soul prospers when full of the Scriptures and the grace of Christ. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: Whatever they do in word or deed, they are to do all in the name of the Lord Jesus, giving thanks to God the Father, in believing dependence on him. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: Wives are to submit to their own husbands, as is fit in the Lord, a submission not to a severe lord or stern tyrant but to a husband engaged to affectionate duty. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: Husbands must love their wives with tender and faithful affection, and not be bitter against them. — Matthew Henry

20 COMMENTARY: Children are to obey their parents in all things, for this is well pleasing unto the Lord, and dutiful children are the most likely to prosper. — Matthew Henry

21 COMMENTARY: Fathers are not to provoke their children to anger, lest they be discouraged, since parents must be tender as well as children obedient. — Matthew Henry

22 COMMENTARY: Servants are to obey their masters in all things consistent with duty to God, not with eyeservice as menpleasers, but in singleness of heart, fearing God, just and faithful even when out of their master's eye. — Matthew Henry

23 COMMENTARY: Whatever they do, they are to do it heartily, as to the Lord and not unto men, with diligence and cheerfulness rather than idleness or discontent at God's providence. — Matthew Henry

24 COMMENTARY: They shall receive from the Lord the reward of the inheritance, for in serving their masters according to Christ's command they serve the Lord Christ himself. — Matthew Henry

25 COMMENTARY: He that does wrong shall receive for the wrong he has done, with no respect of persons, since the righteous Judge will deal justly between master and servant alike. — Matthew Henry
