Study Notes — Colossians

Chapter 1

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Paul writes as an apostle by God's will alone, with Timothy as a brother in the work, and Henry notes that all true Christians are brethren to one another. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: The Colossians are addressed as saints and faithful brethren in Christ, and grace and peace are wished to them from God the Father and the Lord Jesus Christ. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: Paul gives thanks to God and prays always for the Colossians, since faith, hope, and love are the principal graces of the Christian life and proper matter for thanksgiving. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: Paul had heard of their faith in Christ and of their love to all the saints, and Henry notes that Christian love arises from the Holy Spirit and is full of holiness. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: Their hope is laid up in heaven, treasured where no enemy can take it, and they had heard of it before in the word of the truth of the gospel. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: The gospel had come to them as it had come into all the world, bringing forth fruit in them since the day they heard it and knew the grace of God in truth. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: They had learned the gospel from Epaphras, a dear fellowservant and faithful minister of Christ, whose teaching formed their principles and lives according to it. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: Epaphras had declared to Paul their love in the Spirit, the kind of love that Henry distinguishes from worldly or carnal love because it is holy and unselfish. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: Paul was constant in prayer that they might be filled with the knowledge of God's will in all wisdom and spiritual understanding. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: That knowledge was meant to show itself in a worthy walk, fruitful in every good work and increasing in the knowledge of God, since good words will not do without good works. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: They were to be strengthened with all might according to God's glorious power, and Henry notes the special use of this strength was for sufferings, borne with patience and joy. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: Amidst their trials they gave thanks to the Father, whose special grace fitted them to partake of the inheritance provided for the saints in light. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: God had delivered them from the power of darkness and translated them into the kingdom of his dear Son, turning willing subjects of Satan into subjects of Christ. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: In Christ they have redemption through his blood, even the forgiveness of sins, purchased by his atoning death, with all other spiritual blessings bestowed. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: Christ in his human nature is the visible discovery of the invisible God, the firstborn of every creature, born or begotten before all creation as the Scripture way of representing eternity. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: All things were created by him, visible and invisible, thrones and dominions and powers, made by his power, according to his pleasure, and for his praise and glory. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: He is before all things, and by him all things consist, since he not only created them at first but upholds them by the word of his power. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: As Mediator he is the Head of the body, the church, the beginning and firstborn from the dead, so that in all things he might have the preeminence, with all grace and strength coming from him. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: It pleased the Father that in him should all fulness dwell, a fulness of merit and righteousness, of strength and grace for believers. — Matthew Henry

20 COMMENTARY: Having made peace through the blood of his cross, God reconciled all things to himself by Christ, a mode of redemption Henry calls most suitable, since God showed his justice in requiring full satisfaction. — Matthew Henry

21 COMMENTARY: The Colossians had once been alienated and enemies in their minds by wicked works, yet now they were reconciled, notwithstanding God's hatred of sin. — Matthew Henry

22 COMMENTARY: They were reconciled in the body of his flesh through death, to be presented holy and unblameable and unreproveable in God's sight. — Matthew Henry

23 COMMENTARY: This standing depends on continuing in the faith, grounded and settled and not moved away from the hope of the gospel preached to every creature, of which Paul was made a minister. — Matthew Henry

24 COMMENTARY: Paul rejoiced in his sufferings for them, filling up what remains of the afflictions of Christ for the church's sake, since the sufferings of the Head and of the members make up, as it were, one body of sufferings. — Matthew Henry

25 COMMENTARY: He was made a minister according to the dispensation of God given to him for them, to fulfil the word of God among them. — Matthew Henry

26 COMMENTARY: The mystery hidden from ages and generations is now made manifest to God's saints, and believers are to be thankful that mysteries so long hidden have now been made known. — Matthew Henry

27 COMMENTARY: God would make known among the Gentiles the riches of the glory of this mystery, which is Christ in them, the hope of glory, and the test of that hope is whether Christ dwells and reigns in us. — Matthew Henry

28 COMMENTARY: Paul preached Christ, warning and teaching every man in all wisdom, that he might present every man perfect in Christ Jesus. — Matthew Henry

29 COMMENTARY: For this he laboured, striving according to Christ's working in him mightily, and Henry urges believers likewise to be faithful to death through all trials. — Matthew Henry
