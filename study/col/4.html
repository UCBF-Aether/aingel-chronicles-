Study Notes — Colossians

Chapter 4

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Masters are to give their servants what is just and equal, knowing they also have a Master in heaven, and Henry notes not only strict justice but equity and kindness is required, dealing with servants as they expect God to deal with themselves. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: Believers are to continue in prayer and watch in the same with thanksgiving, since no duties can be done aright unless they persevere in fervent prayer. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: They are to pray also for Paul and his companions, that God would open a door of utterance to speak the mystery of Christ, for which Paul was in bonds, since the people are to pray particularly for their ministers. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: Paul desired to make the mystery manifest as he ought to speak, and Henry notes believers must be careful in all converse to do good and recommend religion by all fit means. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: They are to walk in wisdom toward those without, redeeming the time, since diligence in redeeming time commends religion to others, while even carelessness may cause a lasting prejudice against the truth. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: Their speech is to be always with grace, seasoned with salt, that they may know how to answer every man, since discourse need not always be of grace but must always be with grace, discreet and seasonable. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: Tychicus, a beloved brother and faithful minister and fellowservant in the Lord, would declare all Paul's state to them, and Henry notes ministers are servants to Christ and fellow-servants to one another under one Lord. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: Tychicus was sent for this purpose, that he might know their estate and comfort their hearts, and it is a great comfort under life's troubles to have fellow Christians caring for us. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: With him came Onesimus, a faithful and beloved brother, one of themselves, who would make known all things at Rome, showing what amazing changes divine grace makes, since faithless servants become faithful and beloved brethren. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: Aristarchus the fellowprisoner and Marcus, sister's son to Barnabas, salute them, and Henry notes Paul had once differed with Barnabas over Mark, yet was now reconciled and recommended him, an example of a truly Christian and forgiving spirit. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: Jesus called Justus, of the circumcision, was among Paul's fellowworkers unto the kingdom of God, and such fellowship was a comfort to him in his imprisonment. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: Epaphras, one of themselves and a servant of Christ, saluted them, always labouring fervently for them in prayers that they might stand perfect and complete in all the will of God, and Henry notes the effectual, fervent prayer is the prevailing prayer. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: Paul bore record that Epaphras had a great zeal for them and for Laodicea and Hierapolis, showing the communion of saints and ministers that comforted the apostle. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: Luke the beloved physician and Demas greeted them, fellow-workers in the same work of promoting the salvation of others. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: Paul asked them to salute the brethren in Laodicea and Nymphas and the church in his house, extending the same fellowship beyond Colosse itself. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: When the epistle was read among them, it was also to be read in the church of the Laodiceans, and they were likewise to read the epistle from Laodicea, sharing the apostolic teaching between the churches. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: Archippus was to take heed to the ministry he had received in the Lord, that he fulfil it, since Henry warns that the smiles or frowns of the world and self-love lead many to fall far short of fulfilling their ministry. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: The salutation was by Paul's own hand, with the charge to remember his bonds and the closing blessing of grace, and those who preach Paul's doctrine and follow his example may expect the divine favour and blessing. — Matthew Henry
