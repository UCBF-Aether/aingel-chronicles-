Study Notes — 1 Kings

Chapter 14

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: At that time Abijah, Jeroboam's son, fell sick, and the king who had made gods of gold now faced a trouble no idol could heal. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: Jeroboam told his wife to disguise herself so she would not be known as his wife, and go to Shiloh to Ahijah, who had told him he would be king. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: She was to take ten loaves, cracknels, and a cruse of honey, a peasant's present for a prophet, and ask what would become of the child. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: Jeroboam's wife reached Ahijah's house at Shiloh, but the prophet could not see for age — Henry notes the paradox: blind through age, he was still blessed with visions of the Almighty, which need no bodily eyes and are rather favoured by the want of them. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: The Lord told Ahijah that Jeroboam's wife was coming, feigning to be another woman, to ask about her son, so disguise was useless before God. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: At the sound of her feet Ahijah named her at the door, wife of Jeroboam, why feign to be another — Henry's point is plain dealing: the prophet was no courtier, and those who hide from God in disguise will be confounded on the day of discovery. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: Go tell Jeroboam, said the Lord, that though God exalted him from among the people and made him prince over Israel, he had not kept covenant. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: God had rent the kingdom from David's house and given it to Jeroboam, yet he had not been as David, who followed God with all his heart. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: Jeroboam's evil stood above all before him, Henry argues, because Saul never worshipped idols and Solomon did so only in his dotage without making Israel sin — while Jeroboam's gilded calves changed the truth of God into a lie and provoked him to anger. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: Therefore God would bring evil on Jeroboam's house and cut off every male, him that is shut up and left in Israel, leaving none to continue his name. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: Him that died in the city the dogs would eat, and him that died in the field the fowls would eat, for the Lord had spoken it, a burial of dishonour. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: She was to arise and go home, and when her feet entered the city the child would die, the prophecy giving her a sign she could not miss. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: All Israel would mourn this child because in him alone was found some good thing toward God — Henry calls a good child in Jeroboam's house a miracle of grace, and notes God often takes soonest those he loves best. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: Moreover the Lord would raise up a king over Israel who would cut off Jeroboam's house that day, and what day it would be, judgment was already on its way. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: The Lord would smite Israel as a reed shaken in water and root them out of the good land he gave their fathers, scattering them beyond the river. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: He would give Israel up because of Jeroboam's sins, who sinned and made Israel to sin, the king's sin becoming the nation's ruin. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: Jeroboam's wife arose and came to Tirzah, and when she came to the threshold of the door, the child died, exactly as Ahijah had said. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: They buried him and all Israel mourned for him, according to the Lord's word spoken by Ahijah, the one good son lamented by the whole nation. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: The rest of Jeroboam's acts, how he warred and reigned, are written in the chronicles of Israel's kings, a fuller record beyond this account. — Matthew Henry

20 COMMENTARY: Jeroboam reigned twenty-two years, slept with his fathers, and Nadab his son reigned in his stead, the house continuing only to be cut off. — Matthew Henry

21 COMMENTARY: Rehoboam son of Solomon reigned in Judah, forty-one years old at his accession, reigning seventeen years in Jerusalem, the city God had chosen. — Matthew Henry

22 COMMENTARY: Judah did evil in the Lord's sight and provoked him to jealousy with sins above all their fathers had done, the southern kingdom following the northern into sin. — Matthew Henry

23 COMMENTARY: Judah built high places, images and groves on every high hill and under every green tree — Henry notes the folly: they fancied they exalted God on the hills and pleased him in the pleasant shade, while profaning his name and ordinances by serving idols with them. — Matthew Henry

24 COMMENTARY: Sodomites were in the land, following the abominations of nations God had cast out — Henry's sequence is stark: they dishonoured God by one sin, and God left them to dishonour themselves by another, so that doing like the cast-out, they could expect to be cast out like them. — Matthew Henry

25 COMMENTARY: In Rehoboam's fifth year Shishak of Egypt came up against Jerusalem, the first foreign invasion of the divided kingdom. — Matthew Henry

26 COMMENTARY: He took away the treasures of the Lord's house and the king's house, even all, including Solomon's shields of gold, wealth gathered in peace now carried off in war. — Matthew Henry

27 COMMENTARY: Rehoboam made brass shields in their stead and committed them to the chief of the guard who kept the king's house door, gold replaced by brass. — Matthew Henry

28 COMMENTARY: When the king went into the Lord's house, the guard bore the shields and brought them back to the guard chamber, a diminished glory kept up by ceremony. — Matthew Henry

29 COMMENTARY: The rest of Rehoboam's acts are written in the chronicles of Judah's kings, the record of a reign that began with a lost kingdom. — Matthew Henry

30 COMMENTARY: There was war between Rehoboam and Jeroboam all their days, the division hardening into perpetual border war. — Matthew Henry

31 COMMENTARY: Rehoboam slept with his fathers and was buried in Jerusalem, his mother Naamah an Ammonitess — Henry's summary is bleak: no good is said of him, and where his father, grandfather and grandson reigned forty years apiece, sin shortened his reign to seventeen. — Matthew Henry
