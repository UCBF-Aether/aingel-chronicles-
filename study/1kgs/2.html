Study Notes — 1 Kings

Chapter 2

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: As David's days drew near death, he charged Solomon, leaving his son a dying father's counsel rather than mere state business. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: David said he went the way of all the earth, and urged Solomon to be strong and show himself a man, since courage is needed to rule well. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: The charge was to keep the Lord's charge, walking in his ways and keeping his statutes, commandments, judgments, and testimonies as written in Moses. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: Obedience was tied to God's word to David, that if his children took heed to walk before God in truth, there would never fail a man on Israel's throne. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: David reminded Solomon what Joab did to Abner and Amasa, two captains slain in peacetime, a murder whose blood still stained the kingdom. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: Solomon was to act according to his wisdom and not let Joab's grey head go down to the grave in peace, leaving justice to the new reign. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: By contrast, kindness was to be shown to Barzillai's sons, letting them eat at the king's table, repaying their father's loyalty in David's flight. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: Shimei, who had cursed David grievously at Bahurim, was still with Solomon's kingdom, and David named the old offence without excusing it. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: Solomon was not to hold him guiltless, but as a wise man to bring his grey head down with blood, judging a known enemy of the crown. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: David slept with his fathers and was buried in the city of David, closing a reign that had begun in obscurity and ended in a settled kingdom. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: His reign lasted forty years, seven in Hebron and thirty-three in Jerusalem, a measure Henry notes as a long and eventful reign. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: Solomon sat on his father's throne and his kingdom was established greatly, since God's promise, not mere succession, secured it. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: Adonijah came to Bathsheba, and she asked whether he came peaceably, rightly suspicious after his recent attempt on the throne. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: He said he had something to say, and she bade him speak, giving a hearing that would expose his design. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: He claimed the kingdom had been his and all Israel had set their faces on him, a half-truth that ignored David's oath and God's choice. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: He asked one petition and begged her not to deny him, using his mother's intercession as his way back toward power. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: Adonijah pressed his suit through Bathsheba, hoping marriage to Abishag would renew his claim to the crown he had lost. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: Bathsheba agreed to speak for him, apparently seeing a small petition, though Solomon would see the royal claim behind it. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: Bathsheba went to Solomon, who rose to meet her and set a seat at his right hand, honouring his mother even while hearing a dangerous request. — Matthew Henry

20 COMMENTARY: She called it one small petition and asked him not to refuse her, and Solomon promised to hear her, as a son to his mother. — Matthew Henry

21 COMMENTARY: She asked that Abishag be given to Adonijah, not seeing, as Henry notes, that a king's widow was a claim to the kingdom itself. — Matthew Henry

22 COMMENTARY: Solomon answered that asking Abishag for Adonijah was asking the kingdom for him, and for Joab and Abiathar with him, his old party. — Matthew Henry

23 COMMENTARY: Solomon swore by the Lord that Adonijah had spoken this word against his own life, since the petition renewed his treason. — Matthew Henry

24 COMMENTARY: He appealed to the Lord who had established him on David's throne and made him a house, as promised, so the judgment was God's cause, not revenge. — Matthew Henry

25 COMMENTARY: Benaiah was sent and fell upon Adonijah, and he died, ending the second rising of a man already spared once on condition of good behaviour. — Matthew Henry

26 COMMENTARY: Abiathar was told to go to his fields at Anathoth, for though worthy of death for his treason, he had borne the ark before David. — Matthew Henry

27 COMMENTARY: Solomon thrust Abiathar out of the priesthood, fulfilling God's word against Eli's house at Shiloh, so that even mercy served prophecy. — Matthew Henry

28 COMMENTARY: Joab fled to the altar, but former fidelity excuses no later treachery, and the altar shelters no wilful murderer. — Matthew Henry

29 COMMENTARY: Solomon was told Joab was by the altar, and Benaiah was sent to summon him out, since sanctuary did not protect a wilful murderer. — Matthew Henry

30 COMMENTARY: Joab refused to come forth and said he would die there, choosing the altar as his place of death rather than submit to judgment. — Matthew Henry

31 COMMENTARY: Solomon ordered Benaiah to do as Joab said and bury him, so that the innocent blood Joab shed might be taken away from David's house. — Matthew Henry

32 COMMENTARY: The Lord would return Joab's blood on his own head, for he slew Abner and Amasa, men more righteous and better than he, without David's knowledge. — Matthew Henry

33 COMMENTARY: Their blood would return on Joab and his seed forever, while on David and his seed and throne there would be peace forever from the Lord. — Matthew Henry

34 COMMENTARY: Benaiah slew Joab and buried him in his own house in the wilderness, and the kingdom was purged of its most dangerous soldier. — Matthew Henry

35 COMMENTARY: Benaiah was put over the host in Joab's room and Zadok over the priesthood in Abiathar's room, settling both army and sanctuary under loyal men. — Matthew Henry

36 COMMENTARY: Shimei was summoned and told to build a house in Jerusalem and dwell there, never leaving the city, a confinement meant to test his submission. — Matthew Henry

37 COMMENTARY: On the day he crossed the brook Kidron he would surely die, a clear condition with a clear boundary, so that disobedience would be his own act. — Matthew Henry

38 COMMENTARY: Shimei agreed the saying was good and dwelt in Jerusalem three years, accepting the king's terms in word. — Matthew Henry

39 COMMENTARY: After three years two of Shimei's servants ran away to Achish at Gath, and the loss of servants tempted him to break his oath. — Matthew Henry

40 COMMENTARY: Shimei saddled his ass and went to Gath to seek his servants, and brought them back, crossing the very boundary he had sworn not to cross. — Matthew Henry

41 COMMENTARY: Solomon was told Shimei had gone to Gath and returned, so the breach was known and proved before any sentence. — Matthew Henry

42 COMMENTARY: Solomon reminded him of the oath by the Lord and the protestation, asking why he had not kept the Lord's oath and the king's command. — Matthew Henry

43 COMMENTARY: He asked why Shimei had not kept the command, since the condition had been plain and the time for obedience had been long. — Matthew Henry

44 COMMENTARY: Solomon further charged Shimei with the wickedness his own heart knew, the cursing of David, which the breach of oath now brought to judgment. — Matthew Henry

45 COMMENTARY: Solomon would be blessed and David's throne established before the Lord forever, for purging the kingdom of treason secured it, not weakened it. — Matthew Henry

46 COMMENTARY: With Shimei executed, the throne stood established, for taking away its underminers is how a king of peace proves also a king of righteousness. — Matthew Henry
