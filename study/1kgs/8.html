Study Notes — 1 Kings

Chapter 8

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Solomon assembled the elders, tribal heads, and fathers of Israel to bring up the ark from the city of David, the whole nation joining the dedication. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: All Israel assembled at the feast in Ethanim, the seventh month, the feast of tabernacles, a season of ingathering fit for the ark's entry. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: All the elders came, and the priests took up the ark, the Levites' charge carried out by the appointed hands. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: They brought up the ark, the tabernacle, and all the holy vessels, uniting the old sanctuary with the new house. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: The people, rich and easy and cheerful, consecrated not only their cheerfulness but a part of their wealth to God's honour. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: The priests brought the ark into the oracle, under the wings of the cherubim, to its settled place in the most holy place. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: The cherubim spread their wings over the ark's place and covered the ark and its staves, the guardians overshadowing the mercy seat. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: The staves were drawn out so their ends were seen in the holy place but not outside, a sign the ark had reached its rest and would travel no more. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: Nothing was in the ark save the two tables Moses put there at Horeb, the covenant alone remaining as the heart of Israel's worship. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: When the priests came out, the cloud filled the Lord's house, God's visible token that he had taken possession of it. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: The priests could not stand to minister because of the cloud, for the glory of the Lord had filled the house, and man's service yielded to God's presence. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: Solomon said the Lord said he would dwell in thick darkness, explaining the cloud as God's chosen veil, not his absence. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: He said he had surely built a house for God to dwell in, a settled place to abide in forever, the moving tabernacle now at rest. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: The king turned and blessed all the congregation, all Israel standing, a king acting as the people's mouth in blessing. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: He blessed the Lord God of Israel, who spoke with his mouth to David and fulfilled it with his hand, promise and performance joined. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: Since Israel came out of Egypt, God chose no city to build his house in, but chose David to be over his people, election preceding the building. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: It was in David's heart to build a house for the Lord's name, a good desire even though another would execute it. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: The Lord told David that his desire was well, he did well that it was in his heart, commending the intention though refusing the work. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: David would not build the house, but the son from his loins would build it for God's name, a division of honour between father and son. — Matthew Henry

20 COMMENTARY: The Lord had performed his word, Solomon had risen in David's room and sat on Israel's throne, and had built the house for the Lord's name. — Matthew Henry

21 COMMENTARY: Solomon set a place for the ark containing the covenant made with the fathers when they came out of Egypt, the law at the centre of the house. — Matthew Henry

22 COMMENTARY: Solomon stood before the altar and spread forth his hands toward heaven, the posture of earnest and public prayer. — Matthew Henry

23 COMMENTARY: God keeps covenant, and better than his word, keeps mercy besides, doing for his servants what he never expressly promised. — Matthew Henry

24 COMMENTARY: Performance is the crown of promise, and fresh experiences of its truth call for enlarged praises. — Matthew Henry

25 COMMENTARY: Solomon asked God to keep the further promise, that David's house would never lack a man on Israel's throne if his children walked before God. — Matthew Henry

26 COMMENTARY: He prayed, let thy word be verified which thou spakest to David, resting the future on God's already spoken promise. — Matthew Henry

27 COMMENTARY: When we have done the most we can for God, we must own the infinite distance between our services and his perfections, for no place can include him who is present everywhere. — Matthew Henry

28 COMMENTARY: Yet he asked God to have respect to his servant's prayer and supplication, and to hearken to the cry made before him that day. — Matthew Henry

29 COMMENTARY: He asked that God's eyes be open toward this house night and day, toward the place of which God said, My name shall be there. — Matthew Henry

30 COMMENTARY: Heaven is God's true dwelling-place, of which the temple is but a figure, and the prayer is that when he hears, he forgive the sin that separates. — Matthew Henry

31 COMMENTARY: If a man trespass against his neighbour and an oath is laid on him to swear before the altar, the temple becomes the court of appeal. — Matthew Henry

32 COMMENTARY: In difficult matters the throne of grace would be a throne of judgment, righting the injured who appealed believingly and punishing the injurious who appealed presumptuously. — Matthew Henry

33 COMMENTARY: When Israel is smitten before the enemy for sinning, and turns and confesses God's name and prays toward this house, mercy is sought. — Matthew Henry

34 COMMENTARY: No prayer for removal could be answered unless they also turned from their sin and turned again to God, truly repenting and reforming. — Matthew Henry

35 COMMENTARY: When heaven is shut and there is no rain because of sin, and they pray toward this place and confess and turn, the drought becomes a call to repentance. — Matthew Henry

36 COMMENTARY: God is asked to hear, forgive, and teach his servants the good way wherein they should walk, and give rain on the land. — Matthew Henry

37 COMMENTARY: Famine, pestilence, blasting, mildew, locust, or siege, whatever plague falls, is brought within the same prayer toward this house. — Matthew Henry

38 COMMENTARY: Each petitioner knows the plague of his own heart, where the shoe pinches, and spreads his case before God as Hezekiah spread the letter. — Matthew Henry

39 COMMENTARY: God is asked to hear in heaven, forgive, and give to every man according to his ways, whose heart God knows, for God alone knows all hearts. — Matthew Henry

40 COMMENTARY: The end is that they may fear God all their days in the land given to their fathers, forgiveness leading to reverence, not licence. — Matthew Henry

41 COMMENTARY: Moreover the stranger, not of Israel, who comes from a far country for God's name, is included in the prayer of the house. — Matthew Henry

42 COMMENTARY: Strangers will hear of God's great name, strong hand, and stretched-out arm, and when they come and pray toward this house, they too are heard. — Matthew Henry

43 COMMENTARY: God is asked to hear in heaven and do according to all the stranger calls for, so that all peoples may know God's name and fear him as Israel does. — Matthew Henry

44 COMMENTARY: If Israel goes out to battle wheresoever God sends them, and prays toward the city and house God chose, their cause is laid before God. — Matthew Henry

45 COMMENTARY: God is asked to hear their prayer and maintain their cause, since a war undertaken at God's sending may still need God's hearing. — Matthew Henry

46 COMMENTARY: If they sin, for there is no man that sinneth not, and God delivers them captive to the enemy, the prayer looks beyond defeat to return. — Matthew Henry

47 COMMENTARY: Yet if in captivity they bethink themselves and repent and make supplication, saying, We have sinned and done perversely, God is entreated. — Matthew Henry

48 COMMENTARY: If they return to God with all heart and soul in the land of their enemies and pray toward their land, city, and house, their prayer crosses the distance. — Matthew Henry

49 COMMENTARY: God is asked to hear in heaven and maintain their cause, and to forgive their sins and transgressions against him. — Matthew Henry

50 COMMENTARY: He is asked to forgive his people and give them compassion before their captors, that they may have compassion on them. — Matthew Henry

51 COMMENTARY: For they are God's people and inheritance, brought out of Egypt from the midst of the iron furnace, a redemption that grounds the plea. — Matthew Henry

52 COMMENTARY: Solomon asked that God's eyes be open to his servant's supplication and his people's, to hearken whenever they call. — Matthew Henry

53 COMMENTARY: For God separated Israel from all peoples to be his inheritance, as he spoke by Moses, a separation that is the ground of their prayers. — Matthew Henry

54 COMMENTARY: When Solomon had ended praying, he arose from before the altar, from kneeling with hands spread toward heaven, the long prayer complete. — Matthew Henry

55 COMMENTARY: He stood and blessed all Israel with a loud voice, turning from prayer to God to blessing for the people. — Matthew Henry

56 COMMENTARY: Blessed be the Lord, he said, who has given rest to Israel according to all he promised, not one word of his good promise having failed. — Matthew Henry

57 COMMENTARY: The Lord's presence is all in all to a church and nation, comfort enough when the congregation scatters and meets no more together. — Matthew Henry

58 COMMENTARY: He prayed God would incline their hearts to walk in his ways and keep his commandments, since a heart inclined by God keeps what a command alone cannot. — Matthew Henry

59 COMMENTARY: He prayed these words of supplication be near to the Lord day and night, maintaining the cause of his servant and his people at all times. — Matthew Henry

60 COMMENTARY: The end is that all peoples may know the Lord is God and there is none else, the temple's witness reaching beyond Israel. — Matthew Henry

61 COMMENTARY: A perfect heart means obedience universal without dividing, upright without dissembling, constant without declining: this is evangelical perfection. — Matthew Henry

62 COMMENTARY: The king and all Israel offered sacrifice before the Lord, worship following prayer as its seal. — Matthew Henry

63 COMMENTARY: Solomon offered twenty-two thousand oxen and a hundred and twenty thousand sheep as peace offerings, a sacrifice of dedication beyond ordinary scale. — Matthew Henry

64 COMMENTARY: The king hallowed the middle of the court that day, because the brass altar was too little for the burnt and peace offerings and fat. — Matthew Henry

65 COMMENTARY: Solomon held a feast with all Israel, a great congregation from Hamath to Egypt's river, seven days and seven days, fourteen days of joy. — Matthew Henry

66 COMMENTARY: They carried their joy home with them, blessing the king and going to their tents joyful, for God's goodness was the matter of their joy, as it should be of ours. — Matthew Henry
