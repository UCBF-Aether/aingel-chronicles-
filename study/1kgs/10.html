Study Notes — 1 Kings

Chapter 10

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: The queen of Sheba heard of Solomon's fame concerning the Lord's name and came to prove him with hard questions, seeking wisdom, not mere splendour. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: She came with a very great train, camels bearing spices, much gold, and precious stones, a royal embassy bringing the wealth of Arabia to Jerusalem. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: Solomon told her all her questions, and nothing was hid from him, a wisdom that answered difficulty without evasion. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: When she had seen Solomon's wisdom and the house he built, the report she had heard began to be confirmed by her eyes. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: The meat of his table, the seating of his servants, the attendance of his ministers, their apparel, his cupbearers, and his ascent to the Lord's house left her with no more spirit, overwhelmed by the order of it all. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: She said the report she heard in her own land of his acts and wisdom was true, yet she had not believed until she saw. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: The half had not been told her, she said, for his wisdom and prosperity exceeded the fame she had heard, a confession that truth outran report. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: Happy are thy men and servants, she said, who stand continually before thee and hear thy wisdom, for daily access to wisdom is itself a blessing. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: He made thee king not to live in pomp and pleasure, but to do judgment and justice: duty before honours and profits. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: She gave the king a hundred and twenty talents of gold, very great store of spices, and precious stones, a gift whose like never came again. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: The almug trees are named as extraordinary, perhaps much admired by the queen of Sheba herself. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: Of the almug trees Solomon made pillars for the Lord's house and the king's house, and harps and psalteries for singers, wood of the south turned to worship. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: Solomon gave the queen all her desire, whatever she asked, besides his royal bounty, generosity answering generosity. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: The gold that came to Solomon in one year was six hundred and sixty-six talents, a sum that marks the height of his wealth. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: Besides Ophir gold, customs from merchants and land-taxes from conquered tributaries swelled the yearly sum. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: The targets and shields were not for service but for state, carried before him to signify power used for the defence and protection of the good. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: He made three hundred shields of beaten gold, three pounds to a shield, and put them in the house of the forest of Lebanon, gold as armour in a time of peace. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: On his throne he sat to give laws to his subjects, audience to ambassadors, and judgment upon appeals. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: The lions beside the stays were a memorandum of the courage and resolution with which he ought to execute judgment, not fearing the face of man. — Matthew Henry

20 COMMENTARY: Twelve lions on the steps carried the same memorandum: the righteous judge is bold as a lion. — Matthew Henry

21 COMMENTARY: Such is the nature of worldly wealth: plenty of it makes it the less valuable, and spiritual riches should lessen our esteem of earthly possessions far more. — Matthew Henry

22 COMMENTARY: Alongside substantial goods came apes to play with and peacocks to please the eye, a sign that prosperity overcharged had grown childish and wanton. — Matthew Henry

23 COMMENTARY: So Solomon exceeded all kings of the earth for riches and wisdom, the two gifts God had joined in him at Gibeon. — Matthew Henry

24 COMMENTARY: All the earth sought Solomon to hear his wisdom, which God had put in his heart, so that Jerusalem became a school for the nations. — Matthew Henry

25 COMMENTARY: Princes sent presents as recompense for his advice, consulting him as an oracle, out of love and admiration for his wisdom. — Matthew Henry

26 COMMENTARY: Solomon gathered fourteen hundred chariots and twelve thousand horsemen, bestowing them in chariot cities and with the king at Jerusalem. — Matthew Henry

27 COMMENTARY: Solomon made wealth circulate until the kingdom was as rich as the king, seeking his people's welfare rather than keeping subjects poor. — Matthew Henry

28 COMMENTARY: Solomon had horses brought out of Egypt, and linen yarn, his merchants receiving them at a price, a trade that broke the law against multiplying horses from Egypt. — Matthew Henry

29 COMMENTARY: A chariot came out of Egypt for six hundred shekels of silver, and a horse for a hundred and fifty, and so Solomon supplied the kings of the Hittites and of Syria. — Matthew Henry
