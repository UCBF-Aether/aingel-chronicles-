Study Notes — 1 Kings

Chapter 13

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: A man of God came out of Judah by the Lord's word to Bethel, where Jeroboam stood by the altar to burn incense, prophecy confronting idolatry at its own altar. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: He cried against the altar, O altar, altar, thus saith the Lord, a child named Josiah, born to David's house, would offer the high-place priests upon it and burn men's bones upon it. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: He gave a sign that day: the altar would be rent and its ashes poured out, a present miracle pledging a future judgment centuries away. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: When Jeroboam heard it, he put forth his hand from the altar, saying, Lay hold on him, and his hand dried up so he could not pull it back, power withering as it reached for the prophet. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: The altar was rent and the ashes poured out according to the sign, so that the king's withered hand and the broken altar witnessed together. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: The king begged the man of God to entreat the Lord and pray for his hand's restoration, and at the prophet's prayer his hand was restored whole. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: The king then invited the prophet home to refresh himself and receive a reward, a kindness that would have blunted the prophecy's edge. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: Though hungry, weary and perhaps poor, the prophet refused the king's entertainment and reward — Henry's lesson is self-denial: he would not think himself wiser than God, nor trade one forbidden meal for a chance to discourse further with the king. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: For so he was charged by the Lord's word: eat no bread, drink no water, nor turn again by the way he came, a command that kept the mission uncompromised. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: So he went another way and returned not by the way he came to Bethel, obedience shown in the very road he walked. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: An old prophet dwelt in Bethel, and his sons told him all the man of God had done that day and the words he spoke to the king. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: Their father asked what way the man of God had gone, for his sons had seen it, and he determined to follow him. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: When his sons reported all that had passed, the old prophet saddled his ass and rode after the man of God — Henry suspects a bad design, to draw him into a snare, since false prophets have ever been the worst enemies to the true. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: The old prophet found the man of God sitting under an oak and asked whether he was the man of God from Judah — Henry already distrusts the questioner, calling him a false prophet whose sons attended Jeroboam's altars instead of reproving the idolatry. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: The old prophet invited him home to eat bread, the same invitation the king had offered, now from a brother prophet's mouth. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: The man of God refused again, saying he might not return or eat or drink in that place, holding to his charge against a second tempter. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: He repeated the Lord's word: eat no bread nor drink water there, nor turn again by the way he came, a charge he knew by heart. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: The old prophet lied, saying, I am a prophet also, and an angel spoke to me by the Lord's word to bring thee back to eat and drink, a false revelation set against a true command. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: So the man of God went back with him and ate bread and drank water in his house, persuaded by a claimed vision to disobey a known word. — Matthew Henry

20 COMMENTARY: As they sat at table, the Lord's word came to the prophet who had brought him back, judgment arriving in the very house of the sin. — Matthew Henry

21 COMMENTARY: He cried to the man of God from Judah that because he disobeyed the Lord's mouth and kept not his commandment, his carcase would not come to his fathers' sepulchre. — Matthew Henry

22 COMMENTARY: Because he came back and ate and drank where God had forbidden it, his burial among his fathers was forfeited, a sentence proportioned to the disobedience. — Matthew Henry

23 COMMENTARY: After he had eaten and drunk, the old prophet saddled the ass for him, sending him on the road where judgment awaited. — Matthew Henry

24 COMMENTARY: A lion met him by the way and slew him, and his carcase was cast in the way, with the ass and lion standing by it, the beast killing but not devouring, a miracle of judgment. — Matthew Henry

25 COMMENTARY: Passers-by saw the carcase with the lion standing by it and carried the tale to the old prophet's city — Henry's wonder is the restraint: the lion's commission was to kill the prophet and no further, mercy remembered in the midst of wrath. — Matthew Henry

26 COMMENTARY: When the prophet who brought him back heard it, he said, It is the man of God who disobeyed the Lord's mouth, therefore the Lord delivered him to the lion. — Matthew Henry

27 COMMENTARY: He told his sons to saddle his ass, and they saddled it, and he went to recover the body he had led astray. — Matthew Henry

28 COMMENTARY: He found the carcase with the ass and lion standing by it, the lion having not eaten the carcase nor torn the ass, judgment restrained from further harm. — Matthew Henry

29 COMMENTARY: The prophet took up the carcase, laid it on the ass, and brought it back to Bethel to mourn and bury him, honouring in death the man he had deceived in life. — Matthew Henry

30 COMMENTARY: He laid the carcase in his own grave, and they mourned, Alas, my brother, a lament that acknowledged both the prophet's office and his fall. — Matthew Henry

31 COMMENTARY: After burying him, he told his sons that when he died they should bury him beside the man of God and lay his bones beside his bones, hoping to share his vindication. — Matthew Henry

32 COMMENTARY: For the saying cried against Bethel's altar and against all the high-place houses in Samaria would surely come to pass, the prophet's word outliving the prophet. — Matthew Henry

33 COMMENTARY: After this Jeroboam returned not from his evil way, but made again priests of the lowest people for the high places, whoever would, he consecrated. — Matthew Henry

34 COMMENTARY: This thing became sin to Jeroboam's house, even to cut it off and destroy it from the face of the earth, idolatry ending in extinction. — Matthew Henry
