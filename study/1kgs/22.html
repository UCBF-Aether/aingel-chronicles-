Study Notes — 1 Kings

Chapter 22

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: They continued three years without war between Syria and Israel, the covenant of chapter 20 holding for a season. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: In the third year Jehoshaphat king of Judah came down to the king of Israel, a friendly visit that would lead to a joint war. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: Ahab said to his servants, Know ye that Ramoth in Gilead is ours, and we be still, and take it not out of Syria's king's hand, a claim left unpressed for three years. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: Jehoshaphat answered, I am as thou art, my people as thy people, my horses as thy horses — Henry calls it too great a complaisance: Jehoshaphat and his people were not so wicked as Ahab's, and the compliment nearly cost him dear when in battle he was taken for Ahab. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: Jehoshaphat added, Enquire, I pray thee, at the word of the Lord today, a godly caution before a rash alliance. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: Ahab gathered his prophets, about four hundred men, and asked, Shall I go against Ramothgilead, or forbear, and they said, Go up, for the Lord shall deliver it into the king's hand. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: Jehoshaphat asked, Is there not here a prophet of the Lord besides, that we might enquire of him, unsatisfied with four hundred voices speaking as one. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: Ahab said there was yet one man, Micaiah son of Imlah, by whom they might enquire, but I hate him, for he doth not prophesy good concerning me, but evil. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: Ahab called an officer and said, Hasten hither Micaiah son of Imlah, the hated prophet summoned because the godly king insisted. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: The two kings sat each on his throne, in robes, in a void place at Samaria's gate, with all the prophets prophesying before them, a royal court for a false oracle. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: Zedekiah made horns of iron and declared that with these Ahab would push the Syrians till consumed — Henry notes the horns pictured the two kings' honour and power, and the sign was an imitation of the true prophets, dressing a false prophecy in their manner. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: All the prophets prophesied so, saying, Go up to Ramothgilead and prosper, for the Lord shall deliver it into the king's hand, unanimity without truth. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: The messenger sent to call Micaiah told him the prophets with one mouth declared good to the king, and begged him to speak accordingly. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: Micaiah answered, As the Lord liveth, what the Lord saith unto me, that will I speak, a prophet's oath against a court's pressure. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: When Micaiah came, Ahab asked, Shall we go against Ramothgilead, or forbear, and Micaiah answered at first in irony, Go, and prosper, for the Lord shall deliver it. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: Ahab adjured him, How many times shall I adjure thee that thou tell me nothing but that which is true in the Lord's name, demanding truth from a prophet he hated for telling it. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: Micaiah said, I saw all Israel scattered on the hills, as sheep that have not a shepherd, and the Lord said, These have no master, let them return every man to his house in peace, a vision of a kingless army. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: Ahab said to Jehoshaphat, Did I not tell thee he would prophesy no good concerning me, but evil, turning a true warning into a personal grievance. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: Micaiah opened his vision with the Lord on his throne and heaven's host at either hand — Henry's point is the rebuke to two throned kings: you think you may do what you will, but the Lord sits above all thrones, and every man's judgment proceeds from him, so the prophet must say as he says. — Matthew Henry

20 COMMENTARY: The Lord asked who would persuade Ahab to go up and fall at Ramoth, and the court answered this way and that — Henry stresses the vision is spoken after the manner of men: God is no author of sin, but he permitted the lying spirit, so that the favour Ahab had shown Benhadad would be punished by Benhadad's own Syrians. — Matthew Henry

21 COMMENTARY: There came forth a spirit, stood before the Lord, and said, I will persuade him, a lying spirit sent to deceive a king who loved lies. — Matthew Henry

22 COMMENTARY: The spirit answered that he would be a lying spirit in the mouth of all Ahab's prophets, and was told to go forth and prevail — Henry warns the lying spirit never deceives so fatally as in the mouth of prophets, and those given up to believe them are manifestly marked for ruin. — Matthew Henry

23 COMMENTARY: Now therefore, Micaiah concluded, the Lord hath put a lying spirit in the mouth of all thy prophets, and the Lord hath spoken evil concerning thee, judgment by the very prophets Ahab trusted. — Matthew Henry

24 COMMENTARY: Zedekiah went near, smote Micaiah on the cheek, and asked, Which way went the Spirit of the Lord from me to speak unto thee, violence answering a vision. — Matthew Henry

25 COMMENTARY: Micaiah answered, Thou shalt see in that day, when thou shalt go into an inner chamber to hide thyself, the false prophet's hiding place foretold. — Matthew Henry

26 COMMENTARY: Ahab ordered Micaiah carried back to Amon, governor of the city, and to Joash, the king's son, to prison. — Matthew Henry

27 COMMENTARY: He was to be fed with bread of affliction and water of affliction, until Ahab came in peace, a prophet imprisoned for telling the king he would not return in peace. — Matthew Henry

28 COMMENTARY: Micaiah staked all on the event: if Ahab returned in peace, the Lord had not spoken by him — Henry notes he ran no hazard by the appeal, for he knew whom he had believed, and God will rather let thousands fall than one jot of his servants' word fail. — Matthew Henry

29 COMMENTARY: Both kings went up to Ramoth all the same — Henry marvels chiefly at Jehoshaphat, who proceeded after so fair a warning: carried away by easiness of temper, he weighed 400 voices against one and hoped, since only Ahab's fall was foretold, that he might safely venture. — Matthew Henry

30 COMMENTARY: Ahab said to Jehoshaphat, I will disguise myself and enter the battle, but put thou on thy robes, a king hiding himself while leaving his ally conspicuous. — Matthew Henry

31 COMMENTARY: Syria's king commanded his thirty-two chariot captains, saying, Fight neither with small nor great, save only with the king of Israel, a single target for the whole battle. — Matthew Henry

32 COMMENTARY: The chariot captains mistook robed Jehoshaphat for Israel's king and turned to fight him until he cried out — Henry reads both sides of it: by the danger God showed displeasure at the confederacy, for having said 'I am as thou art' he was now indeed taken for Ahab, and by the deliverance, that God had not deserted him. — Matthew Henry

33 COMMENTARY: When the captains perceived it was not the king of Israel, they turned back from pursuing him, the disguise working for Ahab and against his ally. — Matthew Henry

34 COMMENTARY: A certain man drew a bow at a venture, and smote the king of Israel between the joints of the harness, a random arrow guided by God's sentence. — Matthew Henry

35 COMMENTARY: Held up in his chariot through the day, the king died at evening with his blood running into the chariot — Henry pictures the lingering death: time to feel himself die and to reflect, with horror, on Baal's altars, Naboth's vineyard and Micaiah's prison, flattered into his own ruin and brought to the king of terrors without hope. — Matthew Henry

36 COMMENTARY: There went a proclamation throughout the host about sunset, Every man to his city, and every man to his own country, the scattered sheep of Micaiah's vision. — Matthew Henry

37 COMMENTARY: Henry notes the royal corpse was carried back to Samaria and buried there, while the washing of his bloody chariot at the pool fulfilled Elijah's word: dogs licked his blood, avenging Naboth, making no distinction between royal blood and any other. — Matthew Henry

38 COMMENTARY: At Samaria's pool the dogs licked up Ahab's blood as his chariot was washed — Henry notes the dogs made no difference between royal blood and other blood: Naboth's blood was avenged there, and the licking dogs picture the terrors that prey on a guilty soul after death. — Matthew Henry

39 COMMENTARY: The rest of Ahab's acts, his ivory house and the cities he built, are recorded in Israel's chronicles — Henry notes the ivory house, many parts inlaid with ivory, was perhaps intended to vie with the stately palace Solomon built for Judah's kings. — Matthew Henry

40 COMMENTARY: So Ahab slept with his fathers, and Ahaziah his son reigned in his stead, the house continuing toward the judgment pronounced on it. — Matthew Henry

41 COMMENTARY: Jehoshaphat son of Asa began to reign over Judah in Ahab's fourth year, the southern history resuming after the northern king's death. — Matthew Henry

42 COMMENTARY: Jehoshaphat took the throne at thirty-five and held it twenty-five years — Henry balances the account: not so long as his father's forty-one, yet without the blemishes on his father's latter end, and better, he says, to die in the midst of reputation than to outlive it. — Matthew Henry

43 COMMENTARY: He walked in all the ways of Asa his father, turning not aside, doing right in the Lord's eyes, though the high places were not taken away, and the people still offered and burned incense there. — Matthew Henry

44 COMMENTARY: Jehoshaphat made peace with the king of Israel, ending the border war that had lasted since the division. — Matthew Henry

45 COMMENTARY: The rest of Jehoshaphat's acts, his might and how he warred, are written in Judah's chronicles — Henry's summary is brief: he pleased God, and God blessed him with strength and success. — Matthew Henry

46 COMMENTARY: The remnant of the sodomites, which remained in his father Asa's days, he took out of the land, completing Asa's purge. — Matthew Henry

47 COMMENTARY: There was then no king in Edom, a deputy was king, so Judah's southern neighbour remained under a deputy rather than a king. — Matthew Henry

48 COMMENTARY: Jehoshaphat made ships of Tarshish to go to Ophir for gold, but they went not, for the ships were broken at Eziongeber, a trading venture that failed before it sailed. — Matthew Henry

49 COMMENTARY: Ahaziah son of Ahab said, Let my servants go with thy servants in the ships, but Jehoshaphat would not, refusing a second alliance with Ahab's house. — Matthew Henry

50 COMMENTARY: Jehoshaphat slept with his fathers and was buried in Jerusalem, Jehoram his son succeeding — Henry closes his story as one of Judah's best reigns: not the longest, but one in which he did right, turned not aside, and was blessed with strength and success. — Matthew Henry

51 COMMENTARY: Ahaziah began to reign in Samaria in Jehoshaphat's seventeenth year, reigning two years — Henry's epitaph is sharp: some sinners God makes quick work with, and Ahaziah took no warning from his father's destruction by the false prophets he trusted, following instead his father and his still-living mother Jezebel. — Matthew Henry

52 COMMENTARY: He did evil in the Lord's sight, walking in his father's way, his mother's way, and Jeroboam's way, who made Israel sin, three lines of evil meeting in one king. — Matthew Henry

53 COMMENTARY: For he served Baal and worshipped him, and provoked the Lord God of Israel to anger, according to all his father had done, the book closing on a house still under sentence. — Matthew Henry
