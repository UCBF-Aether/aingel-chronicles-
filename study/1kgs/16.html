Study Notes — 1 Kings

Chapter 16

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: The Lord's word came to Jehu son of Hanani against Baasha, prophecy following the usurper as it had followed Jeroboam. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: Though God exalted Baasha out of the dust and made him prince over Israel, he walked in Jeroboam's way and made Israel sin, provoking God to anger. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: God would take away Baasha's posterity and his house, and make his house like Jeroboam's house, the same judgment on the same sin. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: Him that died of Baasha in the city the dogs would eat, and him that died in the fields the fowls would eat, the dishonourable burial pronounced on Jeroboam now pronounced on Baasha. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: Baasha slept with his fathers and was buried in Tirzah, and Elah his son reigned in his stead, the house continuing briefly before its fall. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: Also by Jehu came the Lord's word against Baasha and his house, both because of his evil and because he slew Jeroboam's house, murder answering murder. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: In Asa's twenty-sixth year Elah son of Baasha began to reign over Israel in Tirzah, two years, a reign cut short by his own servant. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: Zimri, captain of half his chariots, conspired against Elah while the king drank himself drunk in Arza's house — Henry's warning is that Elah loved ease and safety better than honour or duty, staying behind while his army renewed the siege, and death comes terribly upon men found in the act of sin. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: Zimri went in and smote and killed Elah in Asa's twenty-seventh year, and reigned in his stead, treason succeeding treason. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: As soon as Zimri sat on the throne, he slew all Baasha's house, leaving not one that pisseth against the wall, neither kinsfolk nor friends. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: Thus Zimri destroyed all Baasha's house according to the Lord's word spoken by Jehu, a usurper executing God's sentence without knowing it. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: For all Baasha's sins and Elah's sins, by which they sinned and made Israel sin, provoking God with their vanities, the idols that are nothing. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: The rest of Elah's acts are written in the chronicles of Israel's kings, a reign remembered chiefly for its violent end. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: Zimri reigned seven days in Tirzah while Israel encamped against Gibbethon — Henry notes Tirzah was beautiful but unfortified, so Omri soon made himself master of it, and Zimri's week ended in the burning palace. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: When the encamped army heard Zimri had slain the king, they made Omri, captain of the host, king that day in the camp — Henry notes they would not tamely submit to the murderer, and quit the siege of Gibbethon, so that the Philistines gained by Israel's quarrel. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: Omri went up from Gibbethon with all Israel and besieged Tirzah, the army choosing its own king against the palace usurper. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: When Zimri saw the city was taken, he went into the palace's castle and burnt the king's house over himself with fire, and died, a seven-day king ending in self-destruction. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: For his sins in doing evil, walking in Jeroboam's way and in his sin wherewith he made Israel sin, Zimri's reign and death are both charged to idolatry. — Matthew Henry

20 COMMENTARY: The rest of Zimri's acts and his treason are written in the chronicles of Israel's kings, a week of power and a paragraph of history. — Matthew Henry

21 COMMENTARY: Then Israel was divided into two parts, half following Tibni son of Ginath to make him king, and half following Omri, civil war within the northern kingdom. — Matthew Henry

22 COMMENTARY: The people who followed Omri prevailed against Tibni's people, so Tibni died and Omri reigned, the army's choice winning the contest. — Matthew Henry

23 COMMENTARY: In Asa's thirty-first year Omri began to reign over Israel, twelve years, six years in Tirzah, a reign that would found a dynasty and a capital. — Matthew Henry

24 COMMENTARY: Omri bought the hill of Samaria from Shemer for two talents of silver and built the city named for its former owner — Henry contrasts the wandering seats of Israel's kings, Shechem, Tirzah, now Samaria, with Judah's constancy to Jerusalem: those that cleave to the Lord fix, but those that leave him ever wander. — Matthew Henry

25 COMMENTARY: But Omri wrought evil in the Lord's eyes, and did worse than all before him, the new capital housing an old idolatry made worse. — Matthew Henry

26 COMMENTARY: He walked in all Jeroboam's way and in his sin wherewith he made Israel sin, provoking God with their vanities, so that even a founder king is measured by Jeroboam's sin. — Matthew Henry

27 COMMENTARY: The rest of Omri's acts and his might are written in the chronicles of Israel's kings, a record that leaves his buildings to stand for his power. — Matthew Henry

28 COMMENTARY: Omri slept with his fathers and was buried in Samaria, Ahab his son succeeding — Henry notes he died in his bed, as Jeroboam and Baasha had, leaving it to his posterity to fill up the measure of his iniquity and then pay off the scores. — Matthew Henry

29 COMMENTARY: In Asa's thirty-eighth year Ahab son of Omri began to reign over Israel, twenty-two years in Samaria, the worst reign yet to come. — Matthew Henry

30 COMMENTARY: Ahab did evil in the Lord's sight above all before him, a verdict that crowns a long catalogue of evil kings. — Matthew Henry

31 COMMENTARY: Ahab counted Jeroboam's sins a light thing and took Jezebel, Ethbaal's daughter, to wife, serving Baal — Henry's maxim is that making light of less sins makes way for greater, and he seems to have married her with the very design of bringing in Baal's worship. — Matthew Henry

32 COMMENTARY: He reared an altar for Baal in the house of Baal he built in Samaria, giving the foreign god a temple in Israel's capital. — Matthew Henry

33 COMMENTARY: Ahab made a grove and did more to provoke the Lord God of Israel than all Israel's kings before him, idolatry now married to the throne. — Matthew Henry

34 COMMENTARY: In his days Hiel the Bethelite built Jericho, laying its foundation in Abiram his firstborn and setting up its gates in Segub his youngest, according to Joshua's curse, so that the old curse outlived the old age. — Matthew Henry

No note for verse(s) 5 — the public-domain sources consulted give no distinct explanation for these verses beyond the list itself, so nothing was invented for them.
