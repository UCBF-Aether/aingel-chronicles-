Study Notes — 1 Kings

Chapter 3

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Solomon made affinity with Pharaoh and took his daughter, a political marriage Henry censures as a first step toward forbidden alliances, though the kingdom was at peace. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: The people still sacrificed in high places because no house had yet been built for the Lord's name, a fault of the times that Solomon's temple would remove. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: Solomon loved the Lord and walked in David's statutes, only sacrificing in high places, so his heart was right even where the worship was still irregular. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: He went to Gibeon, the great high place where the tabernacle stood, and offered a thousand burnt offerings, a princely sacrifice at the start of his reign. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: At Gibeon the Lord appeared to Solomon in a dream by night and said, Ask what I shall give thee, an offer that tests what a king truly wants. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: Solomon praised God's great mercy to David, who walked before him in truth, righteousness, and uprightness of heart, and to whom a son on the throne was given. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: He confessed himself but a little child, not knowing how to go out or come in, though king over a great people, a humility fitting a young ruler. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: His people were great and uncountable, chosen by God, so the burden of judging them rightly was greater than any one man could bear alone. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: He asked for an understanding heart to judge the people and discern good and bad, since without wisdom a king cannot govern God's people. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: The speech pleased the Lord, because Solomon asked for what serves God and his people rather than for himself. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: God answered that because he had not asked for long life, riches, or his enemies' lives, but for understanding to discern judgment, his prayer would be granted. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: God gave him a wise and understanding heart, such as none before or after him had, a wisdom that was God's gift, not Solomon's achievement. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: God also gave what he had not asked, riches and honour, so that no king in his days would be like him, showing that seeking God's kingdom first adds the rest. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: Long life was promised on condition that he walk in God's ways and keep his statutes as David did, so even a gift of grace carried a condition of obedience. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: Solomon awoke, and it was a dream, yet he acted on it at once, coming to Jerusalem and standing before the ark and offering peace offerings and a feast. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: Two harlots then came to the king and stood before him, bringing a dispute no witness could settle, a fit first case for his new wisdom. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: The first woman told how she and the other woman lived in one house and she bore a child, with no stranger present to witness what followed. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: Three days later the other woman also bore a child, and they were alone together in the house, so only the two mothers knew the truth. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: The other woman's child died in the night because she overlaid it, the sad accident at the root of the whole dispute. — Matthew Henry

20 COMMENTARY: One mother, having overlaid her own child, put the cheat upon the other by a night exchange, and the wronged mother appealed to public justice to be righted. — Matthew Henry

21 COMMENTARY: In the morning the first mother found the child dead, but considering it closely saw it was not the son she had borne. — Matthew Henry

22 COMMENTARY: The other woman denied it, claiming the living child as hers and the dead as her rival's, so the case stood as oath against oath. — Matthew Henry

23 COMMENTARY: The king restated both claims plainly, showing he had heard each side and would judge by something deeper than their words. — Matthew Henry

24 COMMENTARY: He commanded a sword to be brought, an order that looked like cruelty but was designed to draw out the truth from the mothers' hearts. — Matthew Henry

25 COMMENTARY: He ordered the living child divided in two, half to each woman, a sentence no true mother could accept and no false mother would fear. — Matthew Henry

26 COMMENTARY: The true mother's bowels yearned for her son, and she begged the king to give the child alive to the other woman rather than slay it, love surrendering its right to save the child's life. — Matthew Henry

27 COMMENTARY: The king gave the living child to the woman who would rather lose him than see him killed, for she was the mother, as her compassion proved. — Matthew Henry

28 COMMENTARY: The judgment made Solomon both feared and loved, for his people knew no unjust thing could pass undiscovered before him. — Matthew Henry
