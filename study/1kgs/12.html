Study Notes — 1 Kings

Chapter 12

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Rehoboam went to Shechem, for all Israel had come there to make him king, the northern tribes gathering at their own ancient centre. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: Jeroboam, still in Egypt where he had fled from Solomon, heard of it, and the news called him home to the crisis. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: They sent and called Jeroboam, and he and all Israel's congregation came and spoke to Rehoboam, the returned exile now their spokesman. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: They complained Solomon had made their yoke grievous, and asked Rehoboam to lighten the grievous service and heavy yoke, promising then to serve him. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: Rehoboam told them to depart for three days and come again, taking time to consult, a wise delay that he then wasted. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: He consulted the old men who had stood before Solomon, asking how to answer the people, the counsel of experience first sought. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: The way to rule is to serve: deny thyself for once, stoop to do good, and sit highest, easiest, and safest by winning hearts. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: He forsook the old men's counsel and consulted the young men who had grown up with him and stood before him, choosing flatterers over fathers. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: He asked the young men what counsel they gave for answering the people's demand to lighten the yoke. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: The young men told him to answer that his little finger was thicker than his father's loins, a boast of greater strength to come. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: His scorpions were whips with rowels in them, fetching blood at every lash, to use his people as brute beasts, loaded and beaten at his pleasure. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: By taking time to consider, he gave the disaffected time to ripen things for a revolt, and his delay itself showed how little concern he had for their ease. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: The king answered the people roughly and forsook the old men's counsel, a roughness that broke the kingdom in a sentence. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: He spoke after the young men's counsel, that his father made the yoke heavy and he would add to it, whips to scorpions, the folly now made policy. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: The king hearkened not to the people, for the cause was from the Lord, to perform his word to Jeroboam by Ahijah, so that even Rehoboam's folly served God's judgment. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: When all Israel saw the king would not hear them, they answered, What portion have we in David, and cried, to your tents, O Israel, now see to thine own house, David. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: But as for Israel dwelling in Judah's cities, Rehoboam reigned over them, the kingdom now split between north and south. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: Adoram was the very man they most complained of, so the sight of him exasperated them: Rehoboam as unhappy in his ambassador as in his counsellors. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: So Israel rebelled against the house of David unto this day, a revolt that began in a grievance and hardened into a permanent division. — Matthew Henry

20 COMMENTARY: When all Israel heard Jeroboam had returned, they sent and called him to the congregation and made him king over all Israel, none following David's house but Judah alone. — Matthew Henry

21 COMMENTARY: Rehoboam, come to Jerusalem, assembled Judah and Benjamin, a hundred and eighty thousand chosen warriors, to fight for the kingdom's recovery. — Matthew Henry

22 COMMENTARY: But the word of God came to Shemaiah the man of God, a prophet sent to stop a war before it began. — Matthew Henry

23 COMMENTARY: Shemaiah was told to speak to Rehoboam, to all Judah and Benjamin, and to the remnant of the people, a message for the whole divided nation. — Matthew Henry

24 COMMENTARY: To march would be to fight not only brethren, whom they ought to love, but God, to whom they ought to submit, and no cause prospers when fought in disobedience to him. — Matthew Henry

25 COMMENTARY: Jeroboam built Shechem in mount Ephraim and dwelt there, then went out and built Penuel, fortifying his new kingdom's centres. — Matthew Henry

26 COMMENTARY: Jeroboam said in his heart, Now shall the kingdom return to David's house, fearing that worship at Jerusalem would win his people's hearts back. — Matthew Henry

27 COMMENTARY: If the people went up to sacrifice in Jerusalem, he reasoned, their hearts would turn to Rehoboam, and they would kill him and return to Judah's king. — Matthew Henry

28 COMMENTARY: Jeroboam pretended to consult their ease, calling the journey a heavy yoke to shake off, though sensible glories wither by degrees in men's estimation. — Matthew Henry

29 COMMENTARY: He set one in Bethel and the other in Dan, a calf at each end of his kingdom, so that no one need travel far to sin. — Matthew Henry

30 COMMENTARY: This thing became a sin, for the people went to worship before the one, even unto Dan, the convenience of idolatry becoming its own snare. — Matthew Henry

31 COMMENTARY: He made a house of high places and priests of the lowest of the people, not of Levi, a priesthood made to order for a worship made to order. — Matthew Henry

32 COMMENTARY: He ordained a feast in the eighth month, on the fifteenth day, like the feast in Judah, and offered on the altar, counterfeiting the true calendar. — Matthew Henry

33 COMMENTARY: So he offered on the altar he made at Bethel, sacrificing to the calves he had made, and placed in Bethel the priests of the high places he had made, his own invention now fully instituted. — Matthew Henry
