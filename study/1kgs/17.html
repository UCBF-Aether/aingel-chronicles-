Study Notes — 1 Kings

Chapter 17

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Elijah the Tishbite, of Gilead, appeared suddenly before Ahab and swore by the living God of Israel, before whom he stood, that there would be neither dew nor rain but according to his word. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: The word of the Lord came to Elijah, directing a prophet who had just pronounced judgment to his next hiding place. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: He was told to turn eastward and hide by the brook Cherith, before Jordan, a refuge east of the king's reach. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: He would drink of the brook, and God had commanded the ravens to feed him there, unclean birds made carriers of a prophet's bread. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: He went and did according to the Lord's word, dwelling by Cherith, obedience following the command without delay. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: The ravens brought bread and flesh morning and evening, and he drank of the brook, a daily miracle that fed him twice a day. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: After a while the brook dried up, because there was no rain in the land, so that even the prophet felt the drought he had announced. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: The word of the Lord came to him again, sending him from a dried brook to a new provision. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: He was told to arise and go to Zarephath, which belonged to Zidon, Jezebel's own country, and dwell there, for God had commanded a widow to sustain him. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: He arose and went to Zarephath, and at the city gate he saw the widow gathering sticks, the very woman God had appointed, in the lowliest work. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: As she went to fetch water, he called to her for a morsel of bread, testing her charity with a small request first. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: She swore by the Lord his God that she had no cake, only a handful of meal in a barrel and a little oil, and she was gathering sticks to make a last meal for herself and her son, and then die. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: Elijah said, Fear not, do as thou hast said, but make me a little cake first, and afterward make for thee and thy son, faith putting God's prophet before one's own need. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: The promise was that meal and oil would hold out until rain returned — Henry's gloss is that they multiplied not in the hoarding but in the spending: there is that scattereth and yet increaseth, and when God blesses a little it goes a great way. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: She went and did according to Elijah's saying, and she, and he, and her house ate many days, her obedience opening the store she feared was empty. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: The barrel of meal wasted not, nor did the cruse of oil fail, according to the Lord's word spoken by Elijah, a miracle that lasted as long as the famine. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: After these things the widow's son fell sick, so sore that no breath was left in him — Henry warns we must not think it strange to meet sharp affliction even in the way of duty: miraculous feeding did not secure him from sickness, and our mountain never stands so strong but it may be moved. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: In grief the widow charged Elijah with coming to call her sin to remembrance and slay her son — Henry reads the outburst two ways: passion venting sorrow without hope of relief, and penitence, since God remembers our sins against us precisely to make us remember them against ourselves and repent. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: Elijah answered no expostulation but took the boy from his mother's bosom to the loft where he lodged — Henry notes he brought the case straight to God, having taken a particular kindness to the child, so that the affliction was his own more than by sympathy. — Matthew Henry

20 COMMENTARY: He cried to the Lord, O Lord my God, hast thou also brought evil upon the widow with whom I sojourn, by slaying her son, a prayer that argues with God from his own goodness. — Matthew Henry

21 COMMENTARY: He stretched himself upon the child three times and cried, O Lord my God, let this child's soul come into him again, earnest, repeated prayer for life. — Matthew Henry

22 COMMENTARY: The Lord heard Elijah's voice, and the child's soul came into him again, and he revived, the first resurrection in Israel's story. — Matthew Henry

23 COMMENTARY: Elijah took the child down from the chamber and delivered him to his mother, saying, See, thy son liveth, the dead restored to the living. — Matthew Henry

24 COMMENTARY: The woman said, Now by this I know thou art a man of God, and the Lord's word in thy mouth is truth, her faith confirmed by the life of her son. — Matthew Henry
