Study Notes — 1 Kings

Chapter 1

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: David, about seventy, was worn out by a life of war, care, and sin's chastening, so that no clothes could keep him warm. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: His servants proposed a young attendant to cherish him, a medical regimen of the East meant to prolong his life, not mere comfort. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: The search found Abishag of Shunem, a town in Issachar on the plain of Esdraelon, brought to attend the aged king. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: She cherished and ministered to him, yet David knew her not, and Henry notes how even the strongest frame must at last burn out. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: Adonijah, now the oldest surviving son, exalted himself and claimed the throne, preparing the equipage of a king before any appointment. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: David had never displeased him or asked why he acted so, and Henry warns that indulgent parents are often chastised with disobedient children. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: Adonijah secured Joab, the bold army commander with a grudge, and Abiathar, whose influence over the priests was equally great. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: Zadok, Benaiah, Nathan, Shimei, Rei, and the mighty men did not follow Adonijah, holding instead to David and to God's appointed order. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: At En-rogel, a deep well east of Jerusalem, Adonijah made a great open-air feast, a political entertainment to court popularity for his claim. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: He pointedly did not invite Nathan, Benaiah, the mighty men, or Solomon, showing he knew his claim could not bear their presence. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: Nathan, knowing God's will for Solomon, moved to defeat the revolt by counsel, first speaking to Bathsheba, the person most at risk. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: His counsel was to save her life and Solomon's, for rivals to a throne in the East were commonly slain once a usurper was settled. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: Bathsheba was to remind David of his sworn promise that Solomon would reign after him, appealing to his own solemn word. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: Nathan arranged to come in after her and confirm her words, so that the feeble king would not be overwhelmed by a sudden alarm. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: Bathsheba entered the chamber where the very old king lay attended by Abishag, a scene of a court already in its last days. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: She bowed in obeisance, and the king asked what she wanted, giving her leave to speak plainly. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: She rehearsed David's oath that Solomon her son would reign and sit on his throne, grounding her plea in his promise, not in ambition. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: She reported that Adonijah already reigned, and that the king did not know it, exposing how far the plot had advanced in secret. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: Adonijah's abundant sacrifices and his guest list of the king's sons and captains showed a coronation feast in all but name. — Matthew Henry

20 COMMENTARY: The eyes of all Israel were on David to name his successor, since kings commonly designated an heir before death rather than leaving it to birth order alone. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

21 COMMENTARY: Otherwise, she warned, she and Solomon would be counted offenders when David slept with his fathers, that is, put to death as rivals. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

22 COMMENTARY: While she still spoke, Nathan came in, exactly as arranged, so that two witnesses would press the matter on the king. — Matthew Henry

23 COMMENTARY: Nathan was announced and bowed before the king, approaching with the respect due to a sovereign even while bringing unwelcome news. — Matthew Henry

24 COMMENTARY: Nathan asked whether David himself had said Adonijah should reign, forcing the king to own or disown the report directly. — Matthew Henry

25 COMMENTARY: He reported Adonijah's feast that day and its guests, Joab and Abiathar and the king's sons, as evidence of a settled design. — Matthew Henry

26 COMMENTARY: Nathan, Zadok, Benaiah, and Solomon were all excluded, which proved the gathering was a party, not a kingdom assembly. — Matthew Henry

27 COMMENTARY: Nathan asked whether this was done by the king without showing his servant who should sit on the throne, appealing to David's duty to declare it. — Matthew Henry

28 COMMENTARY: David answered by calling Bathsheba back, rousing himself in his age to the duty the emergency required. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

29 COMMENTARY: He swore by the Lord who had redeemed his soul out of all distress, recalling past deliverances as ground for present resolve. — Matthew Henry

30 COMMENTARY: He renewed his oath that Solomon would reign after him and sit on his throne that very day, leaving no room for delay or doubt. — Matthew Henry

31 COMMENTARY: Bathsheba bowed with her face to the earth and wished David long life, receiving the promise with reverence rather than triumph. — Matthew Henry

32 COMMENTARY: David summoned Zadok, Nathan, and Benaiah, the loyal officers of church and state, to execute the succession at once. — Matthew Henry

33 COMMENTARY: Solomon was to ride on David's own mule, a public declaration of favour, since the king's state mule was forbidden to subjects on pain of death. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

34 COMMENTARY: Zadok and Nathan were to anoint him at Gihon and blow the trumpet, anointing being used for a disputed succession or a new dynasty. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

35 COMMENTARY: The people were then to bring him up to sit on David's throne, a public recognition of the successor during the old king's lifetime. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

36 COMMENTARY: Benaiah answered Amen and prayed that the Lord God of David would be with Solomon, a loyal officer blessing the appointment he would serve. — Matthew Henry

37 COMMENTARY: He further prayed Solomon's throne might be greater than David's, a wish Henry reads as loyalty desiring the kingdom's increase, not rivalry. — Matthew Henry

38 COMMENTARY: Zadok, Nathan, Benaiah, and the Cherethites and Pelethites, the bodyguard, brought Solomon down to Gihon in ordered procession. — Matthew Henry

39 COMMENTARY: Zadok took the horn of sacred oil from the tabernacle and anointed Solomon, and the trumpet sounded, God save king Solomon. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

40 COMMENTARY: All the people came up after him with pipes and great joy, so that the earth rang, showing the nation's glad consent to God's choice. — Matthew Henry

41 COMMENTARY: Adonijah's guests heard the noise as they finished eating, and Joab, hearing the trumpet, asked what the city's uproar meant. — Matthew Henry

42 COMMENTARY: Jonathan, son of Abiathar, arrived and was welcomed as a bearer of good tidings, though his tidings would break the party. — Matthew Henry

43 COMMENTARY: Jonathan declared plainly that David had made Solomon king, ending all pretence that Adonijah's feast had royal authority. — Matthew Henry

44 COMMENTARY: He detailed the procession, the priests and prophet, Benaiah and the bodyguard, sent by the king himself with Solomon. — Matthew Henry

45 COMMENTARY: Zadok and Nathan had anointed Solomon at Gihon, and the rejoicing city had come up after him, so the act was complete and public. — Matthew Henry

46 COMMENTARY: Solomon now sat on the throne of the kingdom, and the usurpation collapsed without a sword being drawn. — Matthew Henry

47 COMMENTARY: The king's servants blessed David, praying Solomon's name be made better than his, a court blessing on the succession. — Matthew Henry

48 COMMENTARY: David blessed the Lord God of Israel for giving him one to sit on his throne while his eyes yet saw it, a dying man's gratitude. — Matthew Henry

49 COMMENTARY: Adonijah's guests were afraid and rose and went every man his way, for combinations built on evil principles dissolve when interest turns. — Matthew Henry

50 COMMENTARY: Adonijah fled in fear to the horns of the altar, which were symbols of grace and sanctuary, though not a refuge for rebels and murderers. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

51 COMMENTARY: Solomon was told Adonijah clung to the altar's horns and would not leave until Solomon swore he would not be slain. — Matthew Henry

52 COMMENTARY: Solomon spared him on condition of worthy behaviour, promising no hair of his head would fall if he proved a worthy man, but death for wickedness. — Matthew Henry

53 COMMENTARY: Adonijah was brought down from the altar and bowed to Solomon, and Solomon sent him to his house in strict privacy and peace. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
