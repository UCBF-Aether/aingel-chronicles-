Study Notes — 1 Kings

Chapter 9

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: When Solomon had finished the Lord's house and the king's house and all his desire, God appeared to him again, blessing completed work with a second visit. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: The Lord appeared the second time, as at Gibeon, showing that God repeats his presence to those who finish the work he gave them. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: God said he had heard Solomon's prayer and hallowed the house, putting his name there forever, with his eyes and heart there perpetually. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: The throne's establishment depended on the constancy of his obedience, for integrity of heart is the main matter: no religion without sincerity. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: If we perform our part of the covenant, God will not fail to perform his; the entail of blessing follows those who tread in their ancestors' steps. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: But if Solomon or his children turned from following God and served other gods, the covenant's curses would follow as surely as its blessings. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: God would cut off Israel from the land he gave them and cast the hallowed house out of his sight, making Israel a proverb and byword among peoples. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: The house's height would make its fall the more amazing, and their sin would be read in their punishment, for they deserted the temple and therefore God deserted it. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: The answer would be, because they forsook the Lord who brought their fathers out of Egypt, and took hold of other gods, therefore God brought this evil on them. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: Solomon was a wise builder who sat down first and counted the cost, raising his levy not for war but for building. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: Hiram of Tyre had furnished cedar, fir, and gold according to Solomon's desire, a Gentile partner in the work of God's house. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: Hiram came from Tyre to see the cities Solomon gave him, and they pleased him not, a disappointment that strained a profitable friendship. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: He asked, What cities are these, my brother, and called them Cabul, a name of displeasure, whether meaning unpleasing or bound, as commentators differ. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: Hiram sent the king sixscore talents of gold, a great sum that shows the scale of their trade even amid the dispute over the cities. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: The levy Solomon raised was for building the Lord's house, his own house, Millo, Jerusalem's wall, Hazor, Megiddo, and Gezer, a programme of fortification and building. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: Solomon was no warlike prince, but the king of Egypt took cities for him to build: God making the earth help the woman. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: Solomon built Gezer and lower Bethhoron, rebuilding what war had ruined and strengthening the roads into his kingdom. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: He built Baalath and Tadmor in the wilderness, frontier and desert posts securing trade and territory. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: He built all the store cities, chariot cities, and horsemen's cities, and whatever he desired in Jerusalem, Lebanon, and his dominion. — Matthew Henry

20 COMMENTARY: Solomon served himself of the remaining nations' labour, herein observing God's law that bondmen shall be of the heathen. — Matthew Henry

21 COMMENTARY: Their children, whom Israel could not utterly destroy, Solomon put to tribute service, a bondage of labour that remained to that day. — Matthew Henry

22 COMMENTARY: Of Israel Solomon made no bondmen, but they were men of war, servants, princes, captains, and rulers of chariots and horsemen, free citizens in his service. — Matthew Henry

23 COMMENTARY: Five hundred and fifty chief officers ruled over the work and bore rule over the people, the administration of so vast a building programme. — Matthew Henry

24 COMMENTARY: Pharaoh's daughter came up from the city of David to her own house Solomon built for her, and then he built Millo, keeping the holy city distinct from the royal household. — Matthew Henry

25 COMMENTARY: Three times a year Solomon offered burnt and peace offerings on the altar he built, and burned incense before the Lord, so he finished the house in worship, not only in building. — Matthew Henry

26 COMMENTARY: Solomon made a navy of ships at Eziongeber, beside Eloth on the Red Sea, in Edom, opening Israel to the trade of the south. — Matthew Henry

27 COMMENTARY: Hiram sent his seamen, men with knowledge of the sea, with Solomon's servants in the navy, Tyrian skill guiding Israelite ships. — Matthew Henry

28 COMMENTARY: Canaan had no gold in it, teaching that hoarded trading wealth is not the best part of riches, and Solomon's merchandise of wisdom is better than silver and fine gold. — Matthew Henry
