Study Notes — 1 Kings

Chapter 18

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: After many days, in the third year, the Lord's word came to Elijah: go, show thyself to Ahab, and I will send rain upon the earth, the drought ending by the same word that began it. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: Elijah went to show himself to Ahab, and there was a sore famine in Samaria, the city suffering most where the king's idolatry was strongest. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: Ahab called Obadiah, governor of his house, who feared the Lord greatly, a godly steward in an ungodly court. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: When Jezebel cut off the Lord's prophets, Obadiah took a hundred prophets, hid them by fifty in a cave, and fed them with bread and water, a secret church preserved by one man's courage. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: Ahab and Obadiah searched the land for grass to save the beasts — Henry notes Ahab fenced against the effect of the drought while never seeking the favour of God that would remove its cause: he took pains to seek grass, but none to seek God. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: They divided the land between them, Ahab going one way and Obadiah another, the king himself reduced to searching for grass. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: As Obadiah went, Elijah met him, and Obadiah knew him, fell on his face, and asked, Art thou that my lord Elijah, reverence for the prophet he had long sought. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: Elijah answered, I am, and told him to tell his lord, Behold, Elijah is here, a message that put Obadiah's life at risk. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: Obadiah protested, What have I sinned, that thou wouldest deliver thy servant into Ahab's hand to slay me, fearing Ahab's wrath if Elijah vanished again. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: He reminded Elijah that Ahab had sent to every nation and kingdom to seek him, taking oaths that he was not there, so the king's search had been worldwide. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: And now, he said, thou sayest, Go tell thy lord, Behold, Elijah is here, the very message that would expose him if Elijah disappeared. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: As soon as I am gone, he feared, the Spirit of the Lord would carry Elijah where he knew not, and Ahab, not finding him, would slay the messenger. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: He pleaded his own record, that he had hid a hundred prophets when Jezebel slew the Lord's prophets, feeding them in caves, a service Elijah should remember. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: And now, he repeated, thou sayest, Go tell thy lord, Behold, Elijah is here, and Ahab will slay me, fear speaking twice because the danger was real. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: Elijah swore by the Lord of hosts, before whom he stood, that he would surely show himself to Ahab that day, an oath that steadied Obadiah. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: So Obadiah went to meet Ahab and told him, and Ahab went to meet Elijah, king and prophet meeting at last. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: When Ahab saw Elijah, he said, Art thou he that troubleth Israel, blaming the prophet for the judgment the king's own sins had brought. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: Elijah returned the charge: not the prophet who foretells judgment troubles Israel, but the king who follows Baalim — Henry calls Ahab the Achan, the true troubler of the land, since those who procure God's judgments do the mischief, not he who merely warns of them. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: Elijah ordered all Israel and Baal's prophets to Carmel, where an altar to God had formerly stood — Henry notes he summoned the court prophets, Jezebel's own household chaplains, to a public trial, and Ahab issued the writs for the great assembly. — Matthew Henry

20 COMMENTARY: So Ahab sent to all Israel and gathered the prophets together to Carmel, the whole nation summoned to a public trial of the gods. — Matthew Henry

21 COMMENTARY: Elijah charged the people with halting between two opinions — Henry reads it as the same Israelites worshipping God to please the prophets and Baal to please Jezebel's court, a divided heart where God will have all or none. — Matthew Henry

22 COMMENTARY: He said, I, even I only, remain a prophet of the Lord, but Baal's prophets are four hundred and fifty men, one against many, yet the odds favour truth. — Matthew Henry

23 COMMENTARY: He proposed two bullocks, one for Baal's prophets and one for himself, each cut in pieces and laid on wood with no fire under it, the test to be fire from heaven. — Matthew Henry

24 COMMENTARY: The people accepted the trial as fair — Henry notes their reasoning: God had often answered by fire, so if Baal could not, he stood exposed as a usurper, and the case was so plain it needed no fear of searching. — Matthew Henry

25 COMMENTARY: Elijah gave Baal's prophets the first turn, Henry says, for their greater confusion — with one guard against fraud, that no fire be put under the sacrifice, lest Satan's lying wonders counterfeit the trial. — Matthew Henry

26 COMMENTARY: For hours Baal's prophets cried as one man and leaped about the altar — Henry calls the performance senseless and brutish: clamour offered to a god who could neither hear nor answer, while they hoped by dancing to please their deity. — Matthew Henry

27 COMMENTARY: At noon Elijah bantered the silent prophets: their god must be talking, meditating, or lost in thought — Henry defends the scorn, saying idolatry is a most ridiculous thing and it is justice to expose it so. — Matthew Henry

28 COMMENTARY: They cried aloud and cut themselves with knives and lancets till blood gushed out, after their manner, a frenzy that proved their god's silence. — Matthew Henry

29 COMMENTARY: Till the evening sacrifice there was no answer and none that regarded — Henry's point is stark: idols can do neither good nor evil, and God would not suffer even a lying wonder to cloud a trial settled by consent of the parties. — Matthew Henry

30 COMMENTARY: Elijah told all the people to come near, and they came near, and he repaired the Lord's altar that was broken down, restoring worship before offering sacrifice. — Matthew Henry

31 COMMENTARY: He took twelve stones, according to the number of the tribes of Jacob, to whom God said, Israel shall be thy name, a divided nation remembered as one. — Matthew Henry

32 COMMENTARY: Elijah built the altar in the Lord's name, Henry stresses, by direction from God and with an eye to him, not for his own honour — and dug the trench to hold two measures of seed. — Matthew Henry

33 COMMENTARY: With bullock and wood in place Elijah asked, in effect, where is the fire — Henry's answer: God will provide himself fire, and no sacrifice was ever more acceptable, though offered far from ark and temple with neither priest nor Levite. — Matthew Henry

34 COMMENTARY: He said, Do it the second time, and they did it, and Do it the third time, and they did it, twelve barrels drenching the sacrifice. — Matthew Henry

35 COMMENTARY: The water ran round about the altar, and he filled the trench also with water, so that no natural fire could be suspected. — Matthew Henry

36 COMMENTARY: At the evening sacrifice Elijah prayed, Lord God of Abraham, Isaac, and Israel, let it be known this day that thou art God in Israel, and I am thy servant, and I have done all these things at thy word. — Matthew Henry

37 COMMENTARY: Elijah's short prayer pleaded two things, Henry says: God's glory, that Israel might know he is God and Elijah his servant acting at his word, and the people's turning, that their hearts be turned back again. — Matthew Henry

38 COMMENTARY: Then the Lord's fire fell and consumed the sacrifice, wood, stones, dust, and licked up the water in the trench, a fire that left nothing for doubt. — Matthew Henry

39 COMMENTARY: The people fell down as a jury giving its verdict, Jehovah he is God — yet Henry warns most were convinced only, not converted: they yielded to the truth that he is God but not to the covenant that he should be theirs. — Matthew Henry

40 COMMENTARY: Baal's prophets were seized and slain by the law's sentence, Henry notes, while Ahab, terrified by the fire, dared not oppose it; the absent grove prophets escaped only to become, later, instruments of Ahab's destruction. — Matthew Henry

41 COMMENTARY: Elijah told Ahab to go up, eat and drink, for there was a sound of abundance of rain, the prophet hearing the rain before any cloud appeared. — Matthew Henry

42 COMMENTARY: On Carmel's top Elijah cast himself down with his face between his knees — Henry reads the posture as humility, reverence and importunity, abasing himself in the sense of his own meanness now that God had honoured him. — Matthew Henry

43 COMMENTARY: Six times the servant saw nothing, yet Elijah kept his mind close in prayer and sent him again — Henry's lesson: though the answer of fervent prayer may not come quickly, we must continue instant in prayer and not faint, for at the end it shall speak and not lie. — Matthew Henry

44 COMMENTARY: At the seventh time the servant said, Behold, a little cloud like a man's hand arises out of the sea, a small sign of a great rain. — Matthew Henry

45 COMMENTARY: The heavens grew black with clouds and wind and great rain fell as Ahab rode to Jezreel — Henry notes the blessing arrived from a cloud no bigger than a man's hand, and bids us never despise the day of small things. — Matthew Henry

46 COMMENTARY: The hand of the Lord was on Elijah, and he girded his loins and ran before Ahab to the entrance of Jezreel, the prophet outrunning the king's chariot. — Matthew Henry
