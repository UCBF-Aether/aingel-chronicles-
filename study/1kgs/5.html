Study Notes — 1 Kings

Chapter 5

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Hiram of Tyre sent servants to Solomon on hearing of his anointing, for he had ever loved David, and friendship passed to the son. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: Solomon sent to Hiram, opening the temple business by message rather than by force, as befits a work of peace. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: Solomon explained that David could not build the house because wars were round about him until God put his enemies under his feet. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: Now God had given Solomon rest on every side, with neither adversary nor evil occurrence, the very season for building God's house. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: Solomon purposed to build a house for the Lord's name, as God had spoken to David, that his son would build it. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: He asked Hiram to command cedars hewn in Lebanon, with his servants beside Hiram's, and wages as Hiram should appoint. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: Hiram rejoiced greatly and blessed the Lord for giving David a wise son over a great people, a Gentile king honouring Israel's God. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: Hiram agreed to furnish cedar and fir according to Solomon's desire, joining Tyre's skill to Israel's work. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: His servants would bring timber down to the sea and float it to the place Solomon appointed, then Solomon's men would take it up. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: Hiram gave cedar and fir according to all Solomon's desire, a full supply for a house meant to be costly and lasting. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: Each side made good its engagement, corn for timber, justice kept on both sides of the bargain. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: Solomon's wisdom ripened mutual love into a lasting league, for wisdom teaches us to strengthen friendship with those proved honest and fair. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: Solomon raised a levy of thirty thousand men out of all Israel, a service that was honourable rotation, not slavery of Israelites. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: Ten thousand a month went to Lebanon in courses, a month there and two months at home, so the burden was shared and bearable. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: Seventy thousand bore burdens and eighty thousand hewed in the mountains, the great labour force behind the temple's stone and timber. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: Besides these, three thousand three hundred chief officers ruled over the work, a hierarchy that kept so vast a project in order. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: The king commanded great, costly, hewed stones for the foundation, since the house of God was to rest on stone fit for its dignity. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: Many hands and many eyes went to the preparation, for the undertaking was vast, and the temple was only the chief of Solomon's buildings. — Matthew Henry
