Study Notes — 1 Kings

Chapter 11

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Solomon loved many strange women besides Pharaoh's daughter, Moabites, Ammonites, Edomites, Zidonians, and Hittites, alliances that became snares. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: These were the nations God had forbidden Israel to marry, warning that they would surely turn away their hearts after their gods, and Solomon clave to them in love. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: He had seven hundred wives, princesses, and three hundred concubines, and his wives turned away his heart, multitude itself becoming the means of his fall. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: His heart was not perfect because it was not constant: he left his first love and did not persevere to the end as he had begun. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: He went after Ashtoreth of the Zidonians and Milcom of the Ammonites, the very abominations his own temple had been built to exclude. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: Solomon did evil in the sight of the Lord and went not fully after him as David did, a partial following that is itself a turning away. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: He built a high place for Chemosh of Moab and for Molech of Ammon on the hill before Jerusalem, idolatry in sight of the temple. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: Likewise he did for all his strange wives, who burned incense and sacrificed to their gods, the king becoming the patron of the worship he should have destroyed. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: God keeps account of the gracious visits he makes us, and Solomon turned from the Lord who had appeared to him twice. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: God had commanded him concerning this very thing, not to go after other gods, but he kept not the commandment, disobedience after direct warning. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: Because he had not kept God's covenant and statutes, God said he would rend the kingdom from him and give it to his servant. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: Yet in Solomon's days God would not do it, for David's sake, but would rend it out of his son's hand, mercy delaying judgment for the father's sake. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: God did not take the kingdom quickly or wholly, because Solomon had not turned quickly or wholly: one tribe remained for Jerusalem's sake and the temple's. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: The Lord stirred up Hadad the Edomite as adversary, of the king's seed in Edom, the first rod raised against Solomon's apostasy. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: When David was in Edom and Joab went up to bury the slain, having smitten every male in Edom, Hadad's story began in that slaughter. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: For six months Joab remained with all Israel until he had cut off every male in Edom, a severity that left a survivor with a cause for revenge. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: From the general slaughter a branch of the royal family, then a little child, was preserved by servants and conveyed to Egypt. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: They rose out of Midian, came to Paran, took men with them, and came to Egypt to Pharaoh, a fugitive gathering a following on the way. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: Pharaoh sheltered him kindly as a distressed prince, and in time he married the queen's sister. — Matthew Henry

20 COMMENTARY: The queen herself conceived such kindness for the child that she brought him up in Pharaoh's house among the king's children. — Matthew Henry

21 COMMENTARY: He waited until David and Joab were dead, and while Solomon stayed wise and watchful he remained quiet, striking only when Solomon sinned away his wisdom. — Matthew Henry

22 COMMENTARY: Pharaoh asked what Hadad had lacked with him that he sought to go home, and Hadad answered only, let me go, a determination that would not be argued away. — Matthew Henry

23 COMMENTARY: God stirred up another adversary, Rezon son of Eliadah, who had fled from Hadadezer king of Zobah, a second rod from a different quarter. — Matthew Henry

24 COMMENTARY: Rezon headed the Syrian remains and lived by spoil until Solomon grew careless, then seized Damascus and reigned there. — Matthew Henry

25 COMMENTARY: Other princes loved and admired Israel, but Rezon abhorred them, yet till Solomon's revolt, when his defence departed, he could do them no mischief. — Matthew Henry

26 COMMENTARY: His widowed mother found Providence had made up her husband's loss in an active, ingenious son, her support and comfort. — Matthew Henry

27 COMMENTARY: The cause was this: Solomon built Millo and repaired the breaches of the city of David, and Jeroboam's service on that work brought him to notice. — Matthew Henry

28 COMMENTARY: Industry is the way to preferment: the man diligent in his business shall stand before kings, not on the level with mean men. — Matthew Henry

29 COMMENTARY: As Jeroboam went out of Jerusalem, Ahijah the Shilonite, wearing a new garment, found him in the field, and they two were alone. — Matthew Henry

30 COMMENTARY: Ahijah caught the new garment on himself and rent it in twelve pieces, a prophetic sign of a kingdom about to be torn. — Matthew Henry

31 COMMENTARY: Jeroboam got ten tribes not for his deserts but for chastising Solomon's apostasy: Israel deserved so bad a prince. — Matthew Henry

32 COMMENTARY: Two tribes are called one tribe here, because little Benjamin was in a manner lost in the thousands of Judah. — Matthew Henry

33 COMMENTARY: The reason was that they had forsaken God and worshipped Ashtoreth, Chemosh, and Milcom, and had not walked in God's ways or kept his statutes. — Matthew Henry

34 COMMENTARY: Solomon kept the crown all his life not for his own sake, having forfeited it, but for David's sake, who kept God's commandments. — Matthew Henry

35 COMMENTARY: God would take the kingdom from Solomon's son's hand and give ten tribes to Jeroboam, judgment falling on the son for the father's sin. — Matthew Henry

36 COMMENTARY: To Solomon's son God would give one tribe, that David might have a light always before God in Jerusalem, the lamp of his house never going out. — Matthew Henry

37 COMMENTARY: God said he would take Jeroboam and he would reign according to all his soul desired, and be king over Israel, a throne offered on condition. — Matthew Henry

38 COMMENTARY: If Jeroboam hearkened to all God commanded and walked in his ways and did right, keeping his statutes as David did, God would build him a sure house as he built for David. — Matthew Henry

39 COMMENTARY: God would afflict David's seed for this, but not forever, a limit set even on the chastening of the chosen house. — Matthew Henry

40 COMMENTARY: Solomon foolishly sought to kill his successor, as if he could defeat the counsel of the Lord, while Jeroboam prudently withdrew, content in exile because sure of a kingdom at last. — Matthew Henry

41 COMMENTARY: The Book of the Acts of Solomon was likely written by a chronologer he employed, from whose annals the sacred writer extracted what God saw fit to transmit. — Matthew Henry

42 COMMENTARY: Solomon reigned in Jerusalem over all Israel forty years, a reign as long as David's, but ending in a divided promise. — Matthew Henry

43 COMMENTARY: His reign lasted forty years, as long as his father's, but not his life: sin shortened his days, while graves fill with one generation and houses with the next. — Matthew Henry
