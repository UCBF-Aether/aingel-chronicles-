Study Notes — 1 Kings

Chapter 6

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Building began in the 480th year after the exodus, in Solomon's fourth year, in Zif, linking the temple to the deliverance it commemorated. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: The house was sixty cubits long, twenty broad, and thirty high, a noble but not vast size, its glory lying in its workmanship and meaning, not bulk. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: The porch before the temple was twenty cubits long and ten broad, a fitting entrance to a house set apart for God's name. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: Windows of narrow lights gave light while guarding the interior, admitting brightness without opening the holy place to common view. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: Chambers built round the walls served the temple's use, showing the house was ordered for service, not for show alone. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: The lowest chamber was five cubits broad, the middle six, and the third seven, widening as the wall narrowed upward, a skilful building device. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: The stone was made ready before it was brought, so no hammer, axe, or iron tool was heard in the house, a quiet befitting God's dwelling. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: The chambers served the offices of the house, vestries where the priests dressed and the vessels were kept, so that all might be done decently and in order. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: The side chambers, five cubits high, rested on the house with cedar timber, tied to the main building yet distinct from it. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: Then the word of the Lord came to Solomon, interrupting the building account to remind the builder of the condition on which all rested. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: If Solomon walked in God's statutes and kept his judgments, God would perform his word to David concerning the house. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: God promised to dwell among Israel and not forsake his people, so the temple's value lay in God's presence, not in its gold. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: Solomon built the house and finished it, the work going on under God's warning that obedience, not architecture, secures blessing. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: The inner walls were lined with cedar boards from floor to ceiling, hiding the stone behind carved wood. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: Twenty cubits at the sides were built with cedar to form the oracle, the most holy place set apart within the house. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: The temple before the oracle was forty cubits long, the holy place where daily service was performed. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: The cedar wainscot was strong and durable and of a very sweet smell, curiously carved with knops and flowers. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: The oracle was prepared within to set there the ark of the covenant, the throne of God's presence among his people. — Matthew Henry

20 COMMENTARY: The oracle was twenty cubits square and twenty high, a perfect cube, fit to signify the perfection of the heavenly dwelling. — Matthew Henry

21 COMMENTARY: Solomon spared no expense to make the house sumptuous, gold even under foot, as it should be in every living temple. — Matthew Henry

22 COMMENTARY: Gold was under foot there, as it should be in all living temples, its very abundance lessening its worth. — Matthew Henry

23 COMMENTARY: Within the oracle he made two cherubim of olive tree, each ten cubits high, guardians of the mercy seat in larger form. — Matthew Henry

24 COMMENTARY: Each cherub's wings spanned five cubits each way, ten from tip to tip, covering the breadth of the oracle. — Matthew Henry

25 COMMENTARY: Both cherubim were of one measure and one size, alike in form, as the angels of God are alike in their service. — Matthew Henry

26 COMMENTARY: Each cherub stood ten cubits high, matching the height of the oracle they filled. — Matthew Henry

27 COMMENTARY: He set them within the inner house with wings stretched so that one wing touched one wall and the other touched the other, their wings meeting in the midst. — Matthew Henry

28 COMMENTARY: He overlaid the cherubim with gold, so that even the figures of the heavenly host shone with the house's glory. — Matthew Henry

29 COMMENTARY: All the walls round about were carved with cherubim, palm trees, and open flowers, the garden and heaven imagery of Eden renewed in the temple. — Matthew Henry

30 COMMENTARY: The floor was overlaid with gold within and without, so that the worshipper walked on gold even in the outer holy place. — Matthew Henry

31 COMMENTARY: The door into the oracle was narrower than the door into the temple, as many enter into profession who come short of salvation. — Matthew Henry

32 COMMENTARY: The two olive doors were carved with cherubim, palm trees, and open flowers and covered with gold, so the very doors preached the sanctuary's meaning. — Matthew Henry

33 COMMENTARY: The temple door had olive posts a fourth part of the wall, a lesser but still noble entrance to the holy place. — Matthew Henry

34 COMMENTARY: Its two fir doors had folding leaves, practical doors of a working sanctuary, yet also carved and gilded. — Matthew Henry

35 COMMENTARY: The carved cherubim were not to be worshipped themselves, but to show how great is he whom we are to worship. — Matthew Henry

36 COMMENTARY: The low wall let the people see what was done and hear what the priests said, for even then they were not kept wholly in the dark or at a distance. — Matthew Henry

37 COMMENTARY: The foundation was laid in the fourth year, in Zif, the second month, seven years before the finish, a work begun in spring. — Matthew Henry

38 COMMENTARY: Seven years and a half saw it finished, soon done for so vast and elegant a building, because Solomon was in earnest and many hands made quick work. — Matthew Henry

No note for verse(s) 9 — the public-domain sources consulted give no distinct explanation for these verses beyond the list itself, so nothing was invented for them.
