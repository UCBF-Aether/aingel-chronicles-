Study Notes — 1 Kings

Chapter 19

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Ahab told Jezebel all Elijah had done, and how he had slain all the prophets with the sword, the victory at Carmel reported to its fiercest enemy. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: Jezebel sent a messenger to Elijah, swearing by her gods that by tomorrow she would make his life as the life of one of them, a queen's oath of murder. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: When he saw that, he arose and went for his life, coming to Beersheba in Judah, and leaving his servant there, the prophet who faced Ahab now fleeing a woman's threat. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: Alone under the juniper he asked that he might die, saying it is enough, for he was no better than his fathers — Henry distinguishes the passionate wish of corruption from Paul's deliberate desire to depart and be with Christ: God left him to himself to show his boldness had been in the Lord's power, not his own. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: As he lay and slept under the juniper, an angel touched him and said, Arise and eat, God feeding the prophet who had asked to die. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: He looked and found cake baked on coals and water at his head, ate and drank and lay down again — Henry notes he was not only provided for but attended by an angel who guarded him sleeping and twice called him to his food, so that he fared better than the grove prophets at Jezebel's table. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: The angel came a second time and bade him eat, because the journey was too great for him — Henry's note is that God, who appoints the voyage, victuals the ship accordingly: he knows what service and trials he designs us for and furnishes grace sufficient for them. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: In the strength of that food he travelled forty days and nights to Horeb — Henry notes he neither needed food nor desired it, traversing the desert a day for a year of Israel's wanderings, to show that man lives not by bread alone. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: He came to a cave and lodged there, and the Lord's word came to him, What doest thou here, Elijah, a question that searches the fugitive's heart. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: Elijah pleaded he had been very jealous for the Lord, but Israel had forsaken the covenant, thrown down the altars and slain the prophets, and he alone was left — Henry reads it as despair of success, not want of zeal: he had laboured in vain, and despair hinders many a good enterprise, forgetting that those with God are not alone. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: God said, Go forth and stand on the mount before the Lord, and behold, the Lord passed by, with a great strong wind rending mountains and breaking rocks, but the Lord was not in the wind. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: After wind, earthquake and fire came a still small voice, and the Lord was not in the fire — Henry's point is that God chose to speak in whispers soft: wind and fire awaken awe, but gracious souls are more affected by tender mercies than terrors, and faith comes by hearing the word. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: When Elijah heard it, he wrapped his face in his mantle, went out, and stood in the cave's entrance, reverence before a God who speaks softly. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: The voice asked again, What doest thou here, Elijah, and he answered again with the same complaint, showing his heart had not yet been changed by the vision. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: The Lord said, Go, return on thy way to the wilderness of Damascus, and when thou comest, anoint Hazael to be king over Syria, work given to a prophet who thought his work was done. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: Jehu son of Nimshi he was to anoint king over Israel, and Elisha son of Shaphat of Abel-meholah to be prophet in his room, a succession of instruments against Baal. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: Him that escaped Hazael's sword Jehu would slay, and him that escaped Jehu's sword Elisha would slay, judgment by three hands, none escaping. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: Yet God said, I have left me seven thousand in Israel, all knees that have not bowed to Baal, and every mouth that has not kissed him, so Elijah was not alone. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: Elijah found Elisha ploughing with twelve yoke, himself with the twelfth, and cast his mantle upon him — Henry stresses the unexpected call: not in the schools of the prophets but in the field, for an honest calling in the world puts no man out of the way of his heavenly calling. — Matthew Henry

20 COMMENTARY: Elisha left the oxen and ran after Elijah, asking first to kiss his father and mother — Henry notes the call was powerful: an invisible hand touched his heart at the mere casting of the mantle, and he left the oxen to go as they would. — Matthew Henry

21 COMMENTARY: Elisha returned, took a yoke of oxen, slew them, boiled their flesh with the instruments of the oxen, gave to the people, and they ate, then he arose, went after Elijah, and ministered to him, burning his old livelihood behind him. — Matthew Henry
