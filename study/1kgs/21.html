Study Notes — 1 Kings

Chapter 21

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: After these things Naboth the Jezreelite had a vineyard in Jezreel, hard by Ahab's palace in Samaria, a small inheritance beside great power. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: Ahab asked Naboth to give him the vineyard for a garden of herbs, since it was near his house, offering a better vineyard or its worth in money, a fair offer for a forbidden thing. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: Naboth answered, The Lord forbid it me, that I should give the inheritance of my fathers unto thee, for the law forbade selling the family inheritance permanently. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: Ahab came home heavy and displeased, laid on his bed, turned his face away, and would eat no bread, a king sulking like a child over a refusal. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: Jezebel came to him and asked why his spirit was sad and he ate no bread, the queen probing a grief her husband could not bear alone. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: He told her he had asked Naboth for his vineyard for money or a better vineyard, and Naboth answered, I will not give thee my vineyard. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: Jezebel answered, Dost thou now govern Israel, arise, eat bread, and be merry, I will give thee Naboth's vineyard, a wife taking the government into her own hands. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: She wrote letters in Ahab's name, sealed them with his seal, and sent them to the elders and nobles of Naboth's city, forgery clothed in royal authority. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: She wrote, Proclaim a fast, and set Naboth on high among the people, a religious assembly made the cover for a murder. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: She ordered two sons of Belial set before him to witness, Thou didst blaspheme God and the king, and then carry him out and stone him, law itself turned into a weapon. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: The men of his city, the elders and nobles, did as Jezebel had sent in the letters, obedience to a forged order proving how far corruption had spread. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: They proclaimed a fast and set Naboth on high among the people, the solemn assembly staged exactly as commanded. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: Two men of Belial came in, sat before him, and witnessed against him, Thou didst blaspheme God and the king, and they carried him out of the city and stoned him to death. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: Then they sent to Jezebel, saying, Naboth is stoned, and is dead, the deed reported to its author as a completed errand. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: When Jezebel heard Naboth was stoned and dead, she told Ahab to arise and take possession of the vineyard, for Naboth was not alive but dead, murder now called inheritance. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: When Ahab heard Naboth was dead, he rose up to go down to the vineyard to take possession of it, the king accepting the fruit of his wife's crime. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: The word of the Lord came to Elijah the Tishbite, the prophet sent again to meet a king in the very place of his sin. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: Elijah was sent to meet Ahab in Naboth's vineyard at the very hour he took possession — Henry notes the timing is the charge: by taking possession he avowed all that was done and made himself guilty after the fact, as an accessory, for he took the garden and with it the guilt. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: He was to speak, Thus saith the Lord, Hast thou killed, and also taken possession, and in the place where dogs licked Naboth's blood, dogs shall lick thy blood, even thine. — Matthew Henry

20 COMMENTARY: Ahab greeted Elijah as his enemy, asking, Hast thou found me — Henry reads both hatred and fear in it: his conscience had made God his enemy, so he reckoned God's minister his enemy for telling the truth, and the prophet's sight struck him like the handwriting on Belshazzar's wall. — Matthew Henry

21 COMMENTARY: Elijah pronounced that evil would be brought on Ahab and his posterity cut off — Henry notes the sentence deliberately repeats Jeroboam's and Baasha's: the house made like theirs, city dead for dogs and field dead for birds, and Ahab's own royal blood licked where Naboth's had been. — Matthew Henry

22 COMMENTARY: His house would be made like Jeroboam's house and like Baasha's house, because of his provocation and because he made Israel sin. — Matthew Henry

23 COMMENTARY: Of Jezebel also the Lord spoke, saying, The dogs shall eat Jezebel by the wall of Jezreel, a sentence as particular as her crime. — Matthew Henry

24 COMMENTARY: Him that died of Ahab in the city the dogs would eat, and him that died in the field the fowls would eat, the same dishonour pronounced on Jeroboam and Baasha now pronounced on Ahab. — Matthew Henry

25 COMMENTARY: But there was none like Ahab, who sold himself to work wickedness in the Lord's sight, whom Jezebel his wife stirred up, a king whose wickedness was both chosen and encouraged. — Matthew Henry

26 COMMENTARY: He did very abominably in following idols, according to all the Amorites did, whom the Lord cast out before Israel, so that Israel's king copied the nations God had judged. — Matthew Henry

27 COMMENTARY: Hearing the sentence, Ahab rent his clothes, put on sackcloth, fasted and went softly — Henry calls him a kind of penitent only: the garments were rent but not the heart, an outward profession of repentance where the truth and substance were wanting. — Matthew Henry

28 COMMENTARY: The word of the Lord came to Elijah, asking, Seest thou how Ahab humbleth himself before me. — Matthew Henry

29 COMMENTARY: Because Ahab humbled himself, God said, I will not bring the evil in his days, but in his son's days will I bring it on his house, mercy delaying, not cancelling, the sentence. — Matthew Henry
