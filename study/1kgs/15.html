Study Notes — 1 Kings

Chapter 15

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: In Jeroboam's eighteenth year Abijam began to reign over Judah, the two kingdoms' histories now running side by side. — Matthew Henry

2 COMMENTARY: He reigned three years in Jerusalem, and his mother was Maachah, daughter of Abishalom, a short reign under a strong idolatrous influence. — Matthew Henry

3 COMMENTARY: He walked in all his father's sins and his heart was not perfect with the Lord, unlike David's heart, the standard by which Judah's kings are measured. — Matthew Henry

4 COMMENTARY: Nevertheless for David's sake God gave him a lamp in Jerusalem, to set up his son after him and establish Jerusalem, grace preserving the house for the father's sake. — Matthew Henry

5 COMMENTARY: Because David did right in the Lord's eyes and turned not aside from anything God commanded, save in the matter of Uriah, his house endured. — Matthew Henry

6 COMMENTARY: There was war between Rehoboam and Jeroboam all the days of his life, the old division outliving both kings who made it. — Matthew Henry

7 COMMENTARY: The rest of Abijam's acts are recorded in Judah's chronicles, and war continued between Abijam and Jeroboam — Henry clarifies it was not set battles, which were forbidden, but frequent border encounters and reprisals. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: Abijam slept with his fathers and was buried in Jerusalem, Asa his son succeeding — Henry notes the disgrace in the very name: where Chronicles calls him Abijah, 'my father is the Lord,' here Jah is taken away, and God cut short his reign to make way for a better man. — Matthew Henry

9 COMMENTARY: In Jeroboam's twentieth year Asa began to reign over Judah, a long reign beginning where his father's short one ended. — Matthew Henry

10 COMMENTARY: He reigned forty-one years in Jerusalem, and his mother's name was Maachah, daughter of Abishalom, the same queen mother now facing a reforming son. — Matthew Henry

11 COMMENTARY: Asa did right in the Lord's eyes, as David his father did, a reforming king after two evil reigns. — Matthew Henry

12 COMMENTARY: He took away the sodomites from the land and removed all the idols his fathers had made, purging both persons and things of idolatry. — Matthew Henry

13 COMMENTARY: Asa removed even his mother Maachah from being queen for her grove idol — Henry's maxim is that reformation must begin at home: he loved his mother well, but he loved God better, and would not connive at idolatry in the court. — Matthew Henry

14 COMMENTARY: But the high places were not removed, yet Asa's heart was perfect with the Lord all his days, a good king with an unfinished reformation. — Matthew Henry

15 COMMENTARY: He brought into the Lord's house the things his father had dedicated and the things he himself had dedicated, silver, gold, and vessels, restoring the temple's wealth. — Matthew Henry

16 COMMENTARY: There was war between Asa and Baasha king of Israel all their days, the border war continuing into a third generation. — Matthew Henry

17 COMMENTARY: Baasha went up against Judah and built Ramah, to let none go out or come in to Asa, a fortress meant to seal Judah's border. — Matthew Henry

18 COMMENTARY: Asa took all the silver and gold left in the treasures of the Lord's house and the king's house and sent them to Benhadad of Syria, buying an ally with temple treasure. — Matthew Henry

19 COMMENTARY: He pleaded a league between himself and Benhadad, and between their fathers, and sent the gift to break Benhadad's league with Baasha. — Matthew Henry

20 COMMENTARY: Benhadad hearkened to Asa and sent his captains against Israel's cities — Henry notes the diversion succeeded, yet displeased God: Asa had tempted Benhadad to break his league and trusted indirect policy over the God who had done so much for him. — Matthew Henry

21 COMMENTARY: When Baasha heard it, he left off building Ramah and dwelt in Tirzah, the Syrian diversion forcing him to abandon his fortress. — Matthew Henry

22 COMMENTARY: Asa proclaimed throughout Judah, none exempted, and they took away Ramah's stones and timber, building with them Geba of Benjamin and Mizpah. — Matthew Henry

23 COMMENTARY: The rest of Asa's acts, his might, all he did, and the cities he built, are written in the chronicles of Judah's kings, though in his old age he was diseased in his feet. — Matthew Henry

24 COMMENTARY: Asa slept with his fathers and was buried in Jerusalem, Jehoshaphat his son succeeding — Henry's epitaph is that he reigned long, finished at last with honour, and left his throne to a successor no way inferior to him. — Matthew Henry

25 COMMENTARY: Nadab son of Jeroboam began to reign over Israel in Asa's second year, reigning two years, a brief reign in a violent house. — Matthew Henry

26 COMMENTARY: He did evil in the Lord's sight, walking in his father's way and in his sin wherewith he made Israel to sin, the son repeating the father's idolatry. — Matthew Henry

27 COMMENTARY: Baasha conspired against Nadab at Gibbethon, where Nadab was besieging a Philistine-held city to retake it — Henry notes the king had so little interest in his people's affections that his army did not avenge him but chose his murderer as successor. — Matthew Henry

28 COMMENTARY: In Asa's third year Baasha slew Nadab and reigned in his stead, a usurper who would himself be judged for the same sins. — Matthew Henry

29 COMMENTARY: When he reigned, he smote all Jeroboam's house, leaving not one that breathed, according to the Lord's word by Ahijah. — Matthew Henry

30 COMMENTARY: All this came because of Jeroboam's sins and his making Israel sin — Henry's moral is that those who provoke God do it to their own confusion, and Ahijah's threatening proved no bugbear: no word of God falls to the ground. — Matthew Henry

31 COMMENTARY: The rest of Nadab's acts are written in the chronicles of Israel's kings, a short record for a short reign. — Matthew Henry

32 COMMENTARY: There was war between Asa and Baasha all their days, the two kingdoms still at war even as their kings changed. — Matthew Henry

33 COMMENTARY: In Asa's third year Baasha began to reign over all Israel in Tirzah, twenty-four years, a long reign for a man who took the throne by blood. — Matthew Henry

34 COMMENTARY: He did evil in the Lord's sight, walking in Jeroboam's way and in his sin wherewith he made Israel to sin, the destroyer of Jeroboam's house becoming his heir in sin. — Matthew Henry
