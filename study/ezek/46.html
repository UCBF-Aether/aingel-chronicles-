Study Notes — Ezekiel

Chapter 46

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: The inner court east gate shall be shut six working days, but opened on sabbath and new moon. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

2 COMMENTARY: The prince shall enter by that gate's porch without, stand by its post, priests preparing his burnt and peace offerings, worshipping at the threshold, then going forth, gate unshut till evening. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

3 COMMENTARY: Likewise the land's people shall worship at this gate's door before the LORD in sabbaths and new moons. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

4 COMMENTARY: The prince's sabbath burnt offering: six unblemished lambs and an unblemished ram. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

5 COMMENTARY: Meat offering an ephah for a ram, for lambs as he is able to give, and a hin of oil to an ephah. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

6 COMMENTARY: New moon day: an unblemished young bullock, six lambs, and a ram, all without blemish. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

7 COMMENTARY: Meat offering an ephah for bullock and ram, for lambs as his hand attains, and a hin of oil to an ephah. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

8 COMMENTARY: When the prince enters, he goes in by that gate's porch way, and goes forth by the same way. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

9 COMMENTARY: In solemn feasts, north-gate entrants go out by the south gate, and south-gate entrants by the north, not returning by the entry gate, but going forth over against it. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

10 COMMENTARY: The prince in their midst, going in when they go in, going forth when they go forth. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

11 COMMENTARY: In feasts and solemnities, meat offering an ephah to bullock and ram, to lambs as he is able, and a hin of oil to an ephah. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

12 COMMENTARY: When the prince prepares voluntary burnt or peace offerings, the east gate is opened, he prepares them as on sabbath, goes forth, and after going forth the gate is shut. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

13 COMMENTARY: Daily prepare a burnt offering, a first-year unblemished lamb, every morning, for daily worship must never be omitted. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

14 COMMENTARY: Prepare a meat offering every morning, a sixth of an ephah, a third of a hin of oil to temper fine flour, a continual meat offering by perpetual ordinance. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

15 COMMENTARY: Thus they shall prepare lamb, meat offering, and oil every morning for a continual burnt offering. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

16 COMMENTARY: If the prince gives a gift to any son, its inheritance shall be his sons', their possession by inheritance. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

17 COMMENTARY: If he gives inheritance to a servant, it is his to the liberty year, then returns to the prince, but his inheritance remains his sons'. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

18 COMMENTARY: Moreover the prince shall not take people's inheritance by oppression to thrust them from possession, but give sons inheritance from his own, that people be not scattered. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

19 COMMENTARY: After brought through the side entry into the north-looking holy priest chambers, behold a place on the two sides westward. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

20 COMMENTARY: This is where priests boil trespass and sin offerings and bake meat offering, not bearing them to the utter court to sanctify the people. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

21 COMMENTARY: Then brought into the utter court, passing its four corners, behold in every corner a court. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

22 COMMENTARY: In the four corners courts joined, forty cubits long, thirty broad, all four of one measure. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

23 COMMENTARY: A row of building round about in them, made with boiling places under the rows round about. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

24 COMMENTARY: These are the boilers' places, where house ministers boil the people's sacrifice. — Matthew Henry (Concise)
