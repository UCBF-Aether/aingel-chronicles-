Study Notes — Ezekiel

Chapter 3

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Ezekiel must eat the roll and then go speak to Israel, receiving God's truths as food for his soul before he preaches them. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

2 COMMENTARY: He opened his mouth and was caused to eat the roll, feeding upon God's word by faith so he would be strengthened for his work. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

3 COMMENTARY: The roll was honey for sweetness in his mouth, for gracious souls receive even God's terrors with delight, as his own truth. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

4 COMMENTARY: He must go to Israel and speak with God's words, speaking all that, and only that, which God spoke to him. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

5 COMMENTARY: He is not sent to a people of strange speech, but to his own house of Israel, so no language barrier excuses their refusal. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

6 COMMENTARY: Had he been sent to foreign peoples, they would have hearkened, a reproach to Israel, whom Jonah's preaching had once moved at Nineveh. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

7 COMMENTARY: Israel will not hearken to him because they will not hearken to God, being all impudent and hardhearted, resisting both word and rod. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

8 COMMENTARY: God made his face strong against their faces, promising to strengthen him for earnest preaching whatever the success. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

9 COMMENTARY: His forehead, harder than flint, is God's gift of courage, so he must fear them not nor be dismayed at their looks. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

10 COMMENTARY: All God's words must be received in his heart and heard with his ears, inwardly digested before they are spoken aloud. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

11 COMMENTARY: He must go to the captives, his own people, and say Thus saith the Lord GOD, whether they will hear or forbear. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

12 COMMENTARY: The Spirit took him up amid a great rushing and the blessing of God's glory from his place, a sign God could bear him out in his work. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

13 COMMENTARY: The noise of wings touching and wheels over against them marked the heavenly company rejoicing in and attending this mission. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

14 COMMENTARY: He went in bitterness and heat of spirit, overwhelmed with grief for his people's sins and the glory he had seen, yet God's hand was strong upon him. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

15 COMMENTARY: At Telabib by the Chebar he sat where the captives sat, astonished among them seven days, sharing their place before speaking. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

16 COMMENTARY: After seven days the word of the LORD came to him again, ending his silence with a fresh commission. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

17 COMMENTARY: He is made a watchman to Israel, to hear the word at God's mouth and give them warning from God. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

18 COMMENTARY: If he fails to warn the wicked, the wicked dies in his iniquity, but his blood is required at the watchman's hand. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

19 COMMENTARY: If he warns the wicked and the wicked turns not, the wicked dies in his sin, but the watchman has delivered his soul. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

20 COMMENTARY: A righteous man who turns to iniquity and is unwarned dies in his sin, his former righteousness not remembered, and his blood is required of the silent watchman. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

21 COMMENTARY: If the righteous man is warned and does not sin, he shall live because he was warned, and the watchman too has delivered his soul. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

22 COMMENTARY: God's hand was on him again, sending him into the plain to talk with him there, for the servant must still seek communion with God. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

23 COMMENTARY: In the plain the glory of the LORD stood as at the Chebar, and he fell on his face, humbled before the same glory. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

24 COMMENTARY: The Spirit entered him and set him on his feet, commanding him to shut himself within his house, a season of restraint. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

25 COMMENTARY: Bands would be put upon him, binding him from going out among the people, a sign of Israel's coming confinement and his own. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

26 COMMENTARY: His tongue would cleave to his mouth so he could not reprove them, because they are a rebellious house that has refused reproof. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

27 COMMENTARY: When God opens his mouth, he must boldly set life and death, blessing and curse, before them, leaving each to hear or forbear. — Matthew Henry (Concise)
