Study Notes — Ezekiel

Chapter 29

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: In the tenth year, tenth month, twelfth day, the word of the LORD came against Egypt. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

2 COMMENTARY: Set thy face against Pharaoh king of Egypt, and prophesy against him and all Egypt. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

3 COMMENTARY: God is against Pharaoh, the great dragon lying amid his rivers, who said, My river is mine own, and I have made it for myself, pride in property forgetting God gave it. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

4 COMMENTARY: God will put hooks in his jaws, cause river fish to stick to his scales, and bring him up out of his rivers, forcing him from his security. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

5 COMMENTARY: Left thrown in the wilderness with all his river fish, fallen on open fields, ungathered, given for meat to beasts and fowls. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

6 COMMENTARY: All Egypt shall know he is the LORD, because they have been a staff of reed to Israel. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

7 COMMENTARY: When Israel took hold by the hand, Egypt broke and rent their shoulder, and when they leaned, it broke and made their loins stand still. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

8 COMMENTARY: Therefore God will bring a sword upon Egypt and cut off man and beast from her. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

9 COMMENTARY: Egypt shall be desolate and waste, and they shall know he is the LORD, because Pharaoh said, The river is mine, and I have made it. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

10 COMMENTARY: God is against Pharaoh and his rivers, making Egypt utterly waste and desolate from Syene's tower to Ethiopia's border. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

11 COMMENTARY: No foot of man or beast shall pass through it, nor shall it be inhabited forty years. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

12 COMMENTARY: Egypt desolate among desolate countries, her cities among wasted cities for forty years, Egyptians scattered among nations and dispersed through countries. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

13 COMMENTARY: Yet at the end of forty years God will gather the Egyptians from the peoples whither scattered. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

14 COMMENTARY: He will bring again Egypt's captivity, returning them to Pathros, their habitation land, where they shall be a base kingdom. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

15 COMMENTARY: It shall be the basest of kingdoms, never exalting itself above nations, diminished so it shall no more rule over nations. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

16 COMMENTARY: It shall be no more Israel's confidence, bringing iniquity to remembrance when they look after Egypt, but they shall know he is the Lord GOD, for God breaks creature-stays we lean on. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

17 COMMENTARY: In the twenty-seventh year, first month, first day, the word of the LORD came again, concerning Tyre's wages. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

18 COMMENTARY: Nebuchadrezzar's army served a great service against Tyre, every head bald and shoulder peeled, yet had no wages for it. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

19 COMMENTARY: Therefore God will give Egypt to Nebuchadrezzar, taking multitude, spoil, and prey as wages for his army. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

20 COMMENTARY: God gave him Egypt for his labour against Tyre, because they wrought for him, for every man shall have his reward. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

21 COMMENTARY: In that day God will cause Israel's horn to bud forth and give Ezekiel opening of mouth among them, and they shall know he is the LORD. — Matthew Henry (Concise)
