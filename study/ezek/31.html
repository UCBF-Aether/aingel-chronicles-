Study Notes — Ezekiel

Chapter 31

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: In the eleventh year, third month, first day, the word of the LORD came, warning Pharaoh by Assyria's fall. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

2 COMMENTARY: Speak to Pharaoh and his multitude: Whom art thou like in thy greatness? — Matthew Henry (Concise)

3 COMMENTARY: Behold, the Assyrian was a cedar in Lebanon, fair branches, shadowing shroud, high stature, top among thick boughs. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

4 COMMENTARY: Waters made him great, the deep set him on high, rivers round his plants and little rivers to all field trees. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

5 COMMENTARY: His height exalted above all field trees, boughs multiplied and branches long because of multitude of waters. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

6 COMMENTARY: Fowls nested in his boughs, beasts brought forth young under branches, and all great nations dwelt under his shadow. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

7 COMMENTARY: Thus he was fair in greatness and branch length, for his root was by great waters. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

8 COMMENTARY: Garden of God's cedars could not hide him, firs not like his boughs, chestnuts not like his branches, no tree like him in beauty. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

9 COMMENTARY: God made him fair by multitude of branches, so all Eden's trees envied him. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

10 COMMENTARY: Therefore, because he lifted himself in height, shot up his top among thick boughs, and his heart was lifted up in his height, pride precedes fall. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

11 COMMENTARY: God delivered him to the mighty one of the heathen, who shall surely deal with him, driving him out for wickedness. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

12 COMMENTARY: Strangers, terrible of nations, cut him off and left him, branches fallen on mountains and valleys, boughs broken by rivers, peoples gone from his shadow. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

13 COMMENTARY: Upon his ruin fowls of heaven remain and beasts of the field upon his branches. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

14 COMMENTARY: To the end that no water-drinking trees exalt themselves for height nor stand up in height, for all are delivered to death, to earth's nether parts, with pit-goers. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

15 COMMENTARY: In his grave day God caused mourning, covered the deep, restrained floods, stayed great waters, caused Lebanon to mourn and field trees to faint. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

16 COMMENTARY: Nations shook at his fall's sound, cast down to hell with pit-descenders, and Eden's choice Lebanon trees shall be comforted in earth's nether parts. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

17 COMMENTARY: They also went down into hell with him to sword-slain, and his arm, dwelling under his shadow among heathen. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

18 COMMENTARY: To whom art thou thus like in glory and greatness among Eden's trees? Yet thou shalt be brought down with them to earth's nether parts, lying among uncircumcised sword-slain. This is Pharaoh and all his multitude. — Matthew Henry (Concise)
