Study Notes — Ezekiel

Chapter 19

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Moreover take up a lamentation for the princes of Israel, for their fall must be mourned, not merely announced. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

2 COMMENTARY: What is thy mother? A lioness, lying down among lions, nourishing her whelps among young lions, Judah compared to a lioness. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

3 COMMENTARY: She brought up one whelp, which became a young lion, learned to catch prey, and devoured men, cruel and oppressive to his own subjects. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

4 COMMENTARY: The nations heard of him, took him in their pit, and brought him with chains to Egypt, the terrifying enslaved in turn. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

5 COMMENTARY: When she saw her hope was lost after waiting, she took another whelp and made him a young lion. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

6 COMMENTARY: He went up and down among lions, became a young lion, learned to catch prey, and devoured men, as his brother had. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

7 COMMENTARY: He knew their desolate palaces and laid waste their cities, and the land was desolate by the noise of his roaring. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

8 COMMENTARY: The nations set against him from the provinces, spread their net over him, and took him in their pit. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

9 COMMENTARY: Put in ward in chains and brought to the king of Babylon into holds, his voice shall no more be heard on Israel's mountains. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

10 COMMENTARY: Thy mother is like a vine in thy blood, planted by waters, fruitful and full of branches by reason of many waters. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

11 COMMENTARY: She had strong rods for sceptres of rulers, her stature exalted among thick branches, appearing in her height with many branches. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

12 COMMENTARY: But she was plucked up in fury, cast to the ground, her fruit dried by the east wind, her strong rods broken, withered, and consumed by fire. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

13 COMMENTARY: And now she is planted in the wilderness, in a dry and thirsty ground, destroyed though not plucked up by the roots. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

14 COMMENTARY: Fire gone out of a rod of her branches has devoured her fruit, so she has no strong rod for a sceptre to rule, a lamentation that shall remain a lamentation. — Matthew Henry (Concise)
