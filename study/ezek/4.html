Study Notes — Ezekiel

Chapter 4

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: A tile portrayed with Jerusalem makes the coming siege visible, a sign to the house of Israel. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

2 COMMENTARY: Fort, mount, camp, and battering rams round about picture the siege in full, showing sin as the provoking cause of the city's ruin. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

3 COMMENTARY: An iron pan set as an iron wall between prophet and city shows God's fixed resolve against Jerusalem, a sign to Israel. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

4 COMMENTARY: Lying on his left side, he bears the iniquity of the house of Israel, the sign-act embodying their guilt. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

5 COMMENTARY: Three hundred and ninety days answer the years of Israel's iniquity, a day for a year of idolatrous rebellion. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

6 COMMENTARY: Forty days on his right side then bear Judah's iniquity, each day appointed for a year. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

7 COMMENTARY: With face set toward besieged Jerusalem and arm uncovered, he prophesies against it openly. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

8 COMMENTARY: Bands hold him from turning side to side until the days of his siege are ended, so the sign cannot be softened or cut short. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

9 COMMENTARY: Bread of wheat, barley, beans, lentils, millet, and fitches mixed in one vessel figures coarse scarcity fare for 390 days. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

10 COMMENTARY: Twenty shekels of food a day, eaten from time to time, pictures rationed eating in a besieged city. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

11 COMMENTARY: Water by measure, a sixth of a hin, pictures rationed drinking alongside rationed bread. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

12 COMMENTARY: Barley cakes baked with human dung in their sight picture the defilement awaiting Israel in exile. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

13 COMMENTARY: So shall Israel eat defiled bread among the Gentiles where God will drive them, their plenty abused to luxury punished by want. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

14 COMMENTARY: Ezekiel pleads not delicacy but conscience: from youth he has never eaten what dies of itself, is torn, or is abominable. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

15 COMMENTARY: God relents in the sign itself, giving cow's dung for man's dung, so the prophet's obedience need not break the law he kept. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

16 COMMENTARY: God will break the staff of bread in Jerusalem, so bread is eaten by weight with care and water by measure with astonishment. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

17 COMMENTARY: Wanting bread and water, astonied at one another, they consume away for their iniquity, a woeful work sin makes. — Matthew Henry (Concise)
