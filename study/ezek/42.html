Study Notes — Ezekiel

Chapter 42

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Then brought forth into the utter court northward, into the chamber over against the separate place, before the north building. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

2 COMMENTARY: Before the length of a hundred cubits was the north door, breadth fifty cubits. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

3 COMMENTARY: Over against the inner court's twenty cubits and the utter court's pavement, gallery against gallery in three stories. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

4 COMMENTARY: Before the chambers a walk ten cubits broad inward, a way of one cubit, doors toward the north. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

5 COMMENTARY: Upper chambers were shorter, for galleries were higher than these, lower, and middlemost. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

6 COMMENTARY: They were in three stories, but had not court pillars, so the building was straitened more than lowest and middlemost from the ground. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

7 COMMENTARY: The outer wall over against the chambers, toward the utter court on their forepart, length fifty cubits. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

8 COMMENTARY: Utter court chambers' length fifty cubits, and lo, before the temple a hundred cubits. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

9 COMMENTARY: From under these chambers was the entry on the east side, as one goes into them from the utter court. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

10 COMMENTARY: Chambers were in the court wall's thickness toward the east, over against the separate place and the building. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

11 COMMENTARY: The way before them like the north chambers' appearance, as long and broad, goings out according to fashions and doors. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

12 COMMENTARY: According to the south chambers' doors was a door in the way's head, directly before the east wall, as one enters them. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

13 COMMENTARY: North and south chambers before the separate place are holy chambers, where approaching priests eat most holy things, laying there meat, sin, and trespass offerings, for the place is holy. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

14 COMMENTARY: Priests entering shall not go out into the utter court, but lay ministering garments there, being holy, put on other garments, and approach the people's things. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

15 COMMENTARY: When measuring the inner house ended, he brought him forth toward the east gate and measured it round about. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

16 COMMENTARY: He measured the east side five hundred reeds round about. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

17 COMMENTARY: He measured the north side five hundred reeds round about. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

18 COMMENTARY: He measured the south side five hundred reeds. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

19 COMMENTARY: Turning to the west side, he measured five hundred reeds. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

20 COMMENTARY: Measured by four sides, it had a wall round about, five hundred reeds long and broad, separating sanctuary from profane place. — Matthew Henry (Concise)
