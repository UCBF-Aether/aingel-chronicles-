Study Notes — Ezekiel

Chapter 13

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: The word of the LORD comes against the prophets of Israel, a commission to expose those who speak from themselves. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

2 COMMENTARY: Prophesy against prophets who prophesy out of their own hearts, and bid them hear the word of the LORD, which they have not heard. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

3 COMMENTARY: Woe to foolish prophets who follow their own spirit and have seen nothing, for God gives wisdom where he gives a warrant, and these had none. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

4 COMMENTARY: Israel's prophets are like foxes in the deserts, crafty, destructive, and useless to the vineyard they should guard. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

5 COMMENTARY: They have not gone up into the gaps nor made up the hedge for Israel to stand in battle in the day of the LORD, standing not against sin. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

6 COMMENTARY: They have seen vanity and lying divination, saying The LORD saith when he has not sent them, yet making others hope their word will be confirmed. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

7 COMMENTARY: Have they not seen a vain vision and spoken lying divination, claiming The LORD saith it, though he has not spoken? — Matthew Henry (Concise)

8 COMMENTARY: Because they have spoken vanity and seen lies, God himself is against them. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

9 COMMENTARY: God's hand shall be upon the prophets of vanity and lies: excluded from the assembly, unwritten in Israel's writing, never entering the land. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

10 COMMENTARY: They seduced God's people saying Peace when there was no peace, one building a wall and others daubing it with untempered mortar. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

11 COMMENTARY: Say to the daubers that the wall shall fall, by overflowing shower, great hailstones, and stormy wind rending it. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

12 COMMENTARY: When the wall falls, men shall ask, Where is the daubing wherewith ye daubed it, the flattery exposed by the storm. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

13 COMMENTARY: God will rend it with stormy wind in fury, overflowing shower in anger, and hailstones in fury to consume it, the Chaldean army desolating the land. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

14 COMMENTARY: The wall shall be broken to the ground, its foundation discovered, and the daubers consumed in its midst, knowing he is the LORD. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

15 COMMENTARY: God's wrath accomplished upon wall and daubers alike, he will say, The wall is no more, neither they that daubed it. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

16 COMMENTARY: To wit, the prophets of Israel who see visions of peace for Jerusalem when there is no peace. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

17 COMMENTARY: Likewise set his face against the daughters of his people who prophesy out of their own heart, for pleasing lies are no respecter of sex. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

18 COMMENTARY: Woe to the women who sew pillows to all armholes and make kerchiefs for every stature to hunt souls, making people secure and proud. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

19 COMMENTARY: They pollute God among his people for handfuls of barley and pieces of bread, slaying souls that should not die and saving souls that should not live by lies. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

20 COMMENTARY: God is against their pillows for hunting souls to make them fly, and will tear them from their arms and let the hunted souls go. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

21 COMMENTARY: Their kerchiefs also God will tear, delivering his people from being hunted, and they shall know he is the LORD. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

22 COMMENTARY: With lies they made the righteous sad whom God had not made sad, and strengthened the wicked not to return, by promising him life. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

23 COMMENTARY: Therefore they shall see no more vanity nor divine divinations, for God will deliver his people out of their hand. — Matthew Henry (Concise)
