Study Notes — Ezekiel

Chapter 5

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: A sharp knife and barber's razor shaving head and beard signify God's utter rejecting and abandoning of his people, the hair then weighed and divided. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

2 COMMENTARY: A third burned in the city, a third smitten with the knife, a third scattered to the wind with a sword drawn after them, picture famine, sword, and exile. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

3 COMMENTARY: A few bound in his skirts are the small remnant God preserves with care, though few are reserved. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

4 COMMENTARY: Even some of the preserved few are cast into the fire, for to whatever refuge sinners flee, God's wrath can follow. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

5 COMMENTARY: This is Jerusalem, set in the midst of the nations, honoured with a central place and therefore judged in their sight. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

6 COMMENTARY: She changed God's judgments into wickedness more than the nations, refusing his judgments and statutes and not walking in them. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

7 COMMENTARY: Multiplying more than the surrounding nations in evil, and not even keeping the judgments of those nations, Jerusalem forfeits excuse. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

8 COMMENTARY: God himself is against her and will execute judgments in her midst in the sight of the nations. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

9 COMMENTARY: What God will do in her he has not done before and will not do again, because of all her abominations. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

10 COMMENTARY: Fathers eating sons and sons eating fathers in the siege, with the remnant scattered to all winds, is the dreadful sentence in its extremity. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

11 COMMENTARY: Because she defiled God's sanctuary with detestable things, God will diminish her without sparing and without pity. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

12 COMMENTARY: A third dies by pestilence and famine, a third falls by the sword, a third is scattered with a sword drawn after them. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

13 COMMENTARY: When God's anger is accomplished and his fury rests, they shall know the LORD has spoken in his zeal. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

14 COMMENTARY: Jerusalem will be made waste and a reproach among the surrounding nations, in the sight of all who pass by. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

15 COMMENTARY: She becomes a reproach, taunt, instruction, and astonishment to the nations when God executes judgments in anger and furious rebukes. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

16 COMMENTARY: Evil arrows of famine, sent for destruction, increase until the staff of bread is broken. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

17 COMMENTARY: Famine, evil beasts, pestilence, blood, and sword together complete the judgment, and the LORD has spoken it. — Matthew Henry (Concise)
