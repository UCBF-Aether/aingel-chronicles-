Study Notes — Ezekiel

Chapter 1

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Among the captives by the Chebar, in his thirtieth year, Ezekiel saw the heavens opened and visions of God, a mercy given even in affliction. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

2 COMMENTARY: The date is fixed to the fifth year of King Jehoiachin's captivity, grounding the vision in real exile history. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

3 COMMENTARY: The word of the LORD came expressly to Ezekiel the priest, son of Buzi, in Chaldea, and God's hand was upon him for the work. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

4 COMMENTARY: A whirlwind from the north, a great cloud and infolding fire with brightness and amber colour, opens a vision meant to strike awe and raise expectation of blessing. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

5 COMMENTARY: Four living creatures came out of the fire, in the likeness of a man, representing God attended by a vast company of angels, his messengers. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

6 COMMENTARY: Each creature had four faces and four wings, showing angelic readiness and service in every direction. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

7 COMMENTARY: Their straight feet, like a calf's foot, sparkling like burnished brass, picture firm, steady, upright service. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

8 COMMENTARY: Hands of a man under their wings show that the angels have not only wings for motion but hands for action, doing what duty requires. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

9 COMMENTARY: Their wings joined together, they went straight forward without turning, making no mistakes and needing no second doing of their work. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

10 COMMENTARY: The four faces — man, lion, ox, and eagle — picture understanding, strength and boldness, diligence and patience, and quick, piercing sight, all excelled by the angels. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

11 COMMENTARY: Two wings joined upward and two covering their bodies show both readiness for God's errands and reverent humility before him. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

12 COMMENTARY: Each went straight forward wherever the Spirit was to go, turning not aside to trifle with anything. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

13 COMMENTARY: Like burning coals and lamps, with lightning going out of the fire, the creatures are seraphim, or burners, ardent in love to God and zeal in his service. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

14 COMMENTARY: The creatures ran and returned like a flash of lightning, losing no time on whatever business God sent them upon. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

15 COMMENTARY: A wheel upon the earth beside each living creature introduces providence, the ordered government of God working alongside his angels. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

16 COMMENTARY: The wheels, coloured like beryl and as a wheel within a wheel, look dark and perplexed to us, yet are wisely ordered for the best. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

17 COMMENTARY: The wheels went on their four sides without turning, for the motion of providence on its axle is regular and steady. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

18 COMMENTARY: Rings so high they were dreadful, and full of eyes round about, show providence directed by infinite wisdom, beholding evil and good everywhere. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

19 COMMENTARY: When the creatures went the wheels went, and when they were lifted up the wheels were lifted up, for angels are ministers of God's providence. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

20 COMMENTARY: The spirit of the living creature was in the wheels, so the same wisdom, power, and holiness that guide the angels order all events below. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

21 COMMENTARY: Creatures and wheels moved, stood, and rose together, a steady, constant motion with no chance or fortune in it. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

22 COMMENTARY: A firmament like terrible crystal stretched over the creatures' heads, glorious but awe-inspiring, as God's counsel is high and deep. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

23 COMMENTARY: Under the firmament their wings stood straight toward one another, two covering each body, in ordered reverence. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

24 COMMENTARY: The noise of their wings, like great waters and the voice of the Almighty, roused a careless world to listen, then they let down their wings so God's voice might be heard. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

25 COMMENTARY: A voice came from the firmament over their heads once they stood with wings lowered, for the voice of providence opens ears to the voice of the word. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

26 COMMENTARY: Above the firmament was a sapphire throne, and upon it the likeness of a man, the eternal Son, who afterwards took human nature. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

27 COMMENTARY: Amber and fire from his loins upward and downward, with brightness round about, mark a throne of glory, grace, triumph, government, and judgment. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

28 COMMENTARY: The brightness like a rainbow in rain shows God's covenant mercy bounding even his wrath against Jerusalem, and Ezekiel fell on his face before the glory of the LORD. — Matthew Henry (Concise)
