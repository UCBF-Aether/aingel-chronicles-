Study Notes — Ezekiel

Chapter 34

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: The word of the LORD comes against Israel's shepherds, who neglected duty and abused trust. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

2 COMMENTARY: Prophesy against Israel's shepherds: Woe to shepherds feeding themselves! Should not shepherds feed the flocks? — Matthew Henry (Concise)

3 COMMENTARY: No rank or office exempts neglect: the shepherds abused the trust reposed in them, feeding themselves while the flock went unfed, duty neglected and trust abused together. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

4 COMMENTARY: Diseased not strengthened, sick not healed, broken not bound, driven not brought again, lost not sought, but ruled with force and cruelty. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

5 COMMENTARY: The people became as sheep without a shepherd and were given up as prey to their enemies: scattering was not the worst of it, exposure to enemies was. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

6 COMMENTARY: The charge is total neglect: none of the shepherds searched or sought the strays at all, so the flock wandered unlooked-for until God himself undertook the search. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

7 COMMENTARY: This summons to hear opens Henry's mercy section (vv 7-16): the Lord himself intends mercy toward the scattered flock, finding them in darkness and ignorance and bringing them to his fold, as the good Shepherd's tender care of his people's souls. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

8 COMMENTARY: As God lives, because his flock became prey and meat to beasts, with no shepherd searching, while shepherds fed themselves and not the flock. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

9 COMMENTARY: The summons is repeated after the charge is restated, shepherds who fed themselves and never searched for the flock; Henry's point is that the Lord will require the flock at their hand, cause them to cease feeding it, and deliver it from their mouth. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

10 COMMENTARY: God is against the shepherds, will require his flock at their hand, cause them to cease feeding, and deliver his flock from their mouth. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

11 COMMENTARY: For God himself, even he, will both search his sheep and seek them out, in tender care of the good Shepherd. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

12 COMMENTARY: As a shepherd seeks his scattered flock in the day he is among them, so God will seek his sheep and deliver them from all scattered places in the cloudy and dark day. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

13 COMMENTARY: The mercy is restoration first: a people dispersed among nations are brought home to their own land, and the good Shepherd's tender care is the larger picture behind the return. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

14 COMMENTARY: He finds them in darkness and ignorance and brings them to his fold: good pasture and a good fold together mean provision with safety, on the mountains where the Shepherd leads. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

15 COMMENTARY: He leads them in righteousness and causes them to rest on his love and faithfulness: lying down is rest for disquieted saints under the good Shepherd, not mere idleness. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

16 COMMENTARY: He will seek the lost, bring again the driven, bind the broken, strengthen the sick, but destroy the fat and strong, feeding them with judgment. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

17 COMMENTARY: The whole nation seemed one flock, yet they were very different characters, and God knew how to distinguish between them: judgment sorts character, not membership. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

18 COMMENTARY: Seemeth it small to eat up good pasture but tread down the residue, and drink deep waters but foul the residue with feet? — Matthew Henry (Concise)

19 COMMENTARY: And as for God's flock, they eat what the strong trod with feet and drink what they fouled with feet. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

20 COMMENTARY: Judgment between fat and lean is judgment between oppressor and oppressed: the strong who fed themselves are fed with judgment, while the lean are delivered from their mouth. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

21 COMMENTARY: With force and cruelty they ruled: side-thrusts, shoulder-pushes and horn-pushes against the diseased scattered the weak abroad, rule by force rather than shepherding. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

22 COMMENTARY: Deliverance and discrimination come together: the flock is delivered from the shepherds' mouth so it is meat for them no more, and cattle is judged against cattle within it. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

23 COMMENTARY: Verses 23-31 prophesy of Christ: one shepherd over the flock, David's greater Son, under whom as good Shepherd the church becomes a blessing to all around. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

24 COMMENTARY: The LORD is their God and David his prince among them: covenant order restored, with the shepherd-prince standing under God rather than in God's place. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

25 COMMENTARY: The covenant of peace ends the prey: evil beasts cease and the flock dwells safely and sleeps in woods and wilderness, safety in the very places that once exposed it. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

26 COMMENTARY: Under the good Shepherd the church would be a blessing to all around, with showers of blessings wherever Christ's truth is preached, blessing that falls in season on the hill and its surroundings. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

27 COMMENTARY: Field trees shall yield fruit, earth her increase, and they shall be safe, knowing he is the LORD when yoke bands are broken and they are delivered from self-servers. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

28 COMMENTARY: No more prey to heathen nor devoured by beasts, but dwelling safely, none making them afraid. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

29 COMMENTARY: God will raise up a plant of renown, and they shall be no more consumed with hunger nor bear heathen shame any more, the Tree of life bearing salvation's fruits. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

30 COMMENTARY: Knowledge comes by deliverance: they know the LORD is with them and they are his people because the bands of their yoke are broken and they are delivered from self-serving hands. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

31 COMMENTARY: The closing word seals the bond: the flock of his pasture are men, and he is their God, a people owned by a Shepherd who feeds rather than feeds on them. — Matthew Henry (Concise)
