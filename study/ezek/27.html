Study Notes — Ezekiel

Chapter 27

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: The word of the LORD comes again, with a lamentation for Tyre, whose trade shows God's eye upon men in markets as in church. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

2 COMMENTARY: Take up a lamentation for Tyrus, for those living at ease unprepared for trouble are to be lamented. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

3 COMMENTARY: Situate at the sea's entry, merchant of many isles, Tyre said, I am of perfect beauty, reckoning herself beautified though not sanctified. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

4 COMMENTARY: Her borders in the midst of the seas, her builders perfected her beauty. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

5 COMMENTARY: Ship boards of Senir fir and masts of Lebanon cedar built her fleet. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

6 COMMENTARY: Oars of Bashan oak and benches of ivory from Chittim's isles furnished her ships. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

7 COMMENTARY: Fine broidered Egyptian linen was her sail, and blue and purple from Elishah covered her. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

8 COMMENTARY: Zidon and Arvad supplied mariners, and Tyre's own wise men were her pilots. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

9 COMMENTARY: Gebal's ancients and wise men were her calkers, and all the sea's ships with mariners occupied her merchandise. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

10 COMMENTARY: Persia, Lud, and Phut were her men of war, hanging shield and helmet in her, setting forth her comeliness. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

11 COMMENTARY: Arvad's men on her walls and Gammadims in her towers hung shields round about, making her beauty perfect. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

12 COMMENTARY: Tarshish traded in her fairs silver, iron, tin, and lead, by reason of all kinds of riches. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

13 COMMENTARY: Javan, Tubal, and Meshech traded persons of men and vessels of brass in her market. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

14 COMMENTARY: Togarmah traded horses, horsemen, and mules in her fairs. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

15 COMMENTARY: Dedan's men were merchants, many isles her merchandise, bringing ivory horns and ebony as presents. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

16 COMMENTARY: Syria traded wares of her making, emeralds, purple, broidered work, fine linen, coral, and agate. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

17 COMMENTARY: Judah and Israel traded wheat of Minnith, Pannag, honey, oil, and balm in her market. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

18 COMMENTARY: Damascus traded wares for all riches, wine of Helbon and white wool. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

19 COMMENTARY: Dan and Javan going to and fro occupied her fairs with bright iron, cassia, and calamus. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

20 COMMENTARY: Dedan traded precious clothes for chariots. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

21 COMMENTARY: Arabia and Kedar's princes occupied with lambs, rams, and goats. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

22 COMMENTARY: Sheba and Raamah traded chief spices, precious stones, and gold in her fairs. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

23 COMMENTARY: Haran, Canneh, Eden, Sheba's merchants, Asshur, and Chilmad were her merchants. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

24 COMMENTARY: These traded in blue clothes, broidered work, and chests of rich apparel bound with cords and made of cedar. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

25 COMMENTARY: Tarshish ships sang of her in her market, and she was replenished and made very glorious in the midst of the seas. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

26 COMMENTARY: Her rowers brought her into great waters, and the east wind broke her in the midst of the seas. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

27 COMMENTARY: Riches, fairs, merchandise, mariners, pilots, calkers, occupiers, men of war, and all her company shall fall into the sea in her ruin day. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

28 COMMENTARY: The suburbs shall shake at the sound of her pilots' cry. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

29 COMMENTARY: All oarsmen, mariners, and pilots shall come down from ships and stand upon land. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

30 COMMENTARY: They shall cry bitterly, cast dust on heads, and wallow in ashes. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

31 COMMENTARY: Utterly bald and girded with sackcloth, they shall weep with bitterness of heart and bitter wailing. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

32 COMMENTARY: In wailing they shall lament, What city is like Tyrus, like the destroyed in the midst of the sea? — Matthew Henry (Concise)

33 COMMENTARY: When her wares went forth, she filled many people and enriched earth's kings with riches and merchandise. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

34 COMMENTARY: Broken by seas in the waters' depths, her merchandise and all her company shall fall, for those making creatures their confidence fall with them. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

35 COMMENTARY: All isles' inhabitants shall be astonished, kings sore afraid and troubled in countenance. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

36 COMMENTARY: Merchants shall hiss at her, and she shall be a terror and never be any more, for earthly prosperity is vanity. — Matthew Henry (Concise)
