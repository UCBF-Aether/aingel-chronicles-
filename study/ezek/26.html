Study Notes — Ezekiel

Chapter 26

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: In the eleventh year, first day of the month, the word of the LORD came against Tyre. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

2 COMMENTARY: Because Tyre said against Jerusalem, Aha, she is broken that was the gates of the people, she is turned unto me, I shall be replenished now she is laid waste, it thrived on ruin. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

3 COMMENTARY: God is against Tyre and will cause many nations to come up against her, as the sea causes waves to come up. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

4 COMMENTARY: They shall destroy Tyre's walls and break down towers, and God will scrape her dust and make her like a rock's top. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

5 COMMENTARY: She shall be a place for spreading nets in the sea, for God has spoken it, and a spoil to the nations. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

6 COMMENTARY: Her daughters in the field shall be slain by the sword, and they shall know he is the LORD. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

7 COMMENTARY: God will bring Nebuchadrezzar king of Babylon, a king of kings, from the north, with horses, chariots, horsemen, companies, and much people. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

8 COMMENTARY: He shall slay her daughters in the field, make a fort, cast a mount, and lift up the buckler against her. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

9 COMMENTARY: He shall set engines of war against her walls and with axes break down her towers. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

10 COMMENTARY: Abundance of horses' dust shall cover her, walls shaking at horsemen, wheels, and chariots entering her gates as into a breached city. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

11 COMMENTARY: Horses' hoofs shall tread down her streets, her people slain by sword, and strong garrisons brought to the ground. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

12 COMMENTARY: They shall spoil her riches and merchandise, break down walls, destroy pleasant houses, and lay stones, timber, and dust in the water. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

13 COMMENTARY: God will cause the noise of her songs to cease, and the sound of her harps shall be no more heard. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

14 COMMENTARY: Made like a rock's top, a place to spread nets, she shall be built no more, for the LORD has spoken it. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

15 COMMENTARY: Shall not the isles shake at the sound of her fall, when the wounded cry and slaughter is made in her midst? — Matthew Henry (Concise)

16 COMMENTARY: All princes of the sea shall come down from thrones, lay away robes and broidered garments, clothe themselves with trembling, sit on the ground, trembling and astonished. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

17 COMMENTARY: They shall lament, How art thou destroyed, inhabited of seafaring men, renowned city strong in the sea, causing terror on all that haunt it! — Matthew Henry (Concise)

18 COMMENTARY: Now shall the isles tremble in the day of her fall, troubled at her departure. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

19 COMMENTARY: When God makes her a desolate city like uninhabited cities, bringing up the deep and great waters to cover her. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

20 COMMENTARY: Brought down with those descending to the pit, set in earth's low desolate places, she shall not be inhabited, while God sets glory in the land of the living. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

21 COMMENTARY: God will make her a terror, and she shall be no more, sought for yet never found again. — Matthew Henry (Concise)
