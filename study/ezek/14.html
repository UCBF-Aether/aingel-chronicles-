Study Notes — Ezekiel

Chapter 14

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Certain elders of Israel came and sat before Ezekiel, seeking inquiry while keeping idols in their hearts. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

2 COMMENTARY: The word of the LORD came, exposing the seekers before answering them. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

3 COMMENTARY: These men have set up idols in their heart and put the stumblingblock of iniquity before their face, so should God be inquired of at all by them? — Matthew Henry (Concise)

4 COMMENTARY: Every man who sets up heart-idols and comes to the prophet will be answered by the LORD according to the multitude of his idols. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

5 COMMENTARY: That God may take Israel in their own heart, for they are all estranged from him through their idols. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

6 COMMENTARY: Repent, and turn yourselves from your idols, and turn away your faces from all abominations, for no outward form pleases God while an idol possesses the heart. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

7 COMMENTARY: Israelite or sojourning stranger who separates himself and sets up heart-idols, then inquires of a prophet, will be answered by the LORD himself. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

8 COMMENTARY: God will set his face against that man, make him a sign and a proverb, and cut him off from his people. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

9 COMMENTARY: If the prophet be deceived in speaking, the LORD has given that prophet over to deception, and will destroy him from Israel. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

10 COMMENTARY: Prophet and seeker shall bear the same punishment of iniquity, for corruptions are idols of their own setting up. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

11 COMMENTARY: That Israel may go no more astray nor be polluted with transgressions, but be his people and he their God. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

12 COMMENTARY: The word of the LORD came again, now on national sin and national judgment. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

13 COMMENTARY: When a land sins by grievous trespass, God breaks the staff of bread, sends famine, and cuts off man and beast. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

14 COMMENTARY: Though Noah, Daniel, and Job were in it, they should deliver only their own souls by their righteousness. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

15 COMMENTARY: If noisome beasts spoil the land until none may pass through, the judgment stands. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

16 COMMENTARY: Though the three men were in it, they shall deliver neither sons nor daughters, only themselves, and the land shall be desolate. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

17 COMMENTARY: Or if God brings a sword through the land cutting off man and beast, the same limit holds. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

18 COMMENTARY: Though the three men were in it, they shall deliver neither sons nor daughters, but only themselves. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

19 COMMENTARY: Or if pestilence pours out fury in blood, cutting off man and beast, the same limit holds. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

20 COMMENTARY: Though Noah, Daniel, and Job were in it, they shall deliver neither son nor daughter, but only their own souls. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

21 COMMENTARY: How much more when all four sore judgments, sword, famine, noisome beast, and pestilence, are sent upon Jerusalem together? — Matthew Henry (Concise)

22 COMMENTARY: Yet a remnant, sons and daughters, shall be brought forth to the exiles, whose ways and doings will comfort them concerning Jerusalem's evil. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

23 COMMENTARY: Seeing their ways, the exiles shall be comforted and know God has not done without cause all he has done in Jerusalem. — Matthew Henry (Concise)
