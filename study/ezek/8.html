Study Notes — Ezekiel

Chapter 8

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: In the sixth year, sixth month, fifth day, as Ezekiel sat with Judah's elders before him, the hand of the Lord GOD fell upon him. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

2 COMMENTARY: A likeness as fire below and brightness like amber above is the glorious personage who takes hold of the prophet in vision. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

3 COMMENTARY: A hand took him by a lock of his head, and the Spirit lifted him between earth and heaven to Jerusalem in visions of God. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

4 COMMENTARY: The glory of the God of Israel was there, as in the plain vision, even while idolatry stood in his own house. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

5 COMMENTARY: Looking northward at the gate of the altar, he sees the image of jealousy in the entry, a base idol prepared in the inner court. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

6 COMMENTARY: These great abominations would drive God far from his sanctuary, yet greater abominations remain to be seen. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

7 COMMENTARY: Brought to the court door, he finds a hole in the wall, the first crack in a secret, screened wickedness. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

8 COMMENTARY: Digging in the wall, he finds a door, for hypocrisy behind outward profession always leaves some hole betraying it. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

9 COMMENTARY: Going in, he must behold the wicked abominations done in secret, for a clear view of them admits the justice of severe punishment. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

10 COMMENTARY: Every form of creeping things, abominable beasts, and all Israel's idols portrayed round the wall show superstition sparing no toil. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

11 COMMENTARY: Seventy ancients, with Jaazaniah son of Shaphan among them, stand censer in hand before the images, a thick cloud of incense rising. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

12 COMMENTARY: In the dark, each man in his chamber of imagery, they say the LORD sees not and has forsaken the earth, laying blame for their sins on God. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

13 COMMENTARY: Turn yet again, for greater abominations follow, each discovery humbling the searcher further. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

14 COMMENTARY: At the north gate of the LORD's house sit women weeping for Tammuz, yearly lamentation attended with infamous practices. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

15 COMMENTARY: Greater abominations than these still remain, for the more human nature is examined, the more abominations appear. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

16 COMMENTARY: In the inner court, between porch and altar, about twenty-five men, backs to the temple and faces east, worship the sun, supposed priests themselves. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

17 COMMENTARY: Filling the land with violence and provoking God to anger, they put the branch to their nose, an idolatrous custom honouring their idols. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

18 COMMENTARY: Therefore God will deal in fury, without sparing or pity, and will not hear them though they cry loudly in his ears. — Matthew Henry (Concise)
