Study Notes — Ezekiel

Chapter 18

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: The word of the LORD comes again, to answer a proverb that blames fathers for children's ruin. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

2 COMMENTARY: What mean ye by this proverb, The fathers have eaten sour grapes, and the children's teeth are set on edge? — Matthew Henry (Concise)

3 COMMENTARY: As God lives, they shall have no more occasion to use this proverb in Israel. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

4 COMMENTARY: All souls are God's, father's and son's alike, and the soul that sinneth, it shall die, nor perish for another's sins alone. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

5 COMMENTARY: If a man be just, doing what is lawful and right, his case is stated by works showing faith. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

6 COMMENTARY: Not eating upon mountains, not lifting eyes to idols, not defiling his neighbour's wife, nor coming near a menstruous woman, marks the just man. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

7 COMMENTARY: Not oppressing any, restoring the pledge, spoiling none, giving bread to the hungry and covering the naked, marks the just man. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

8 COMMENTARY: Not giving on usury nor taking increase, withdrawing his hand from iniquity, and executing true judgment, marks the just man. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

9 COMMENTARY: Walking in God's statutes and keeping his judgments to deal truly, he is just and shall surely live. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

10 COMMENTARY: If that just man begets a robber son, a shedder of blood doing like evils, the father's piety cannot shield him. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

11 COMMENTARY: Doing none of those duties, but eating on mountains and defiling his neighbour's wife, the son reverses his father. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

12 COMMENTARY: Oppressing poor and needy, spoiling by violence, withholding the pledge, lifting eyes to idols, and committing abomination, the son is guilty himself. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

13 COMMENTARY: Giving on usury and taking increase, shall he then live? He shall not live, but surely die, his blood upon him. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

14 COMMENTARY: If that wicked man begets a son who sees his father's sins, considers, and does not such like, the chain is broken. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

15 COMMENTARY: Not eating on mountains, not lifting eyes to idols, not defiling his neighbour's wife, marks the considering son. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

16 COMMENTARY: Not oppressing any, not withholding the pledge, not spoiling by violence, but feeding the hungry and clothing the naked, marks him. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

17 COMMENTARY: Taking his hand from the poor, refusing usury and increase, executing God's judgments and walking in his statutes, he shall not die for his father's iniquity. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

18 COMMENTARY: As for his father, who cruelly oppressed and spoiled his brother and did what is not good, he shall die in his iniquity. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

19 COMMENTARY: Yet they ask, Why does not the son bear the father's iniquity? When the son does lawful and right and keeps all statutes, he shall surely live. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

20 COMMENTARY: The soul that sinneth shall die; son bears not father's iniquity, nor father son's; righteousness rests on the righteous and wickedness on the wicked. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

21 COMMENTARY: If the wicked turns from all his sins, keeps all statutes, and does lawful and right, he shall surely live and not die, a true penitent being a true believer. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

22 COMMENTARY: All his transgressions shall not be mentioned to him, for in his righteousness, fruit of faith and conversion, he shall live. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

23 COMMENTARY: God has no pleasure in the wicked's death, but that he should return from his ways and live. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

24 COMMENTARY: When the righteous turns from righteousness to iniquity and all abominations, his former righteousness shall not be mentioned, and in his sin he shall die. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

25 COMMENTARY: Yet they say, The way of the Lord is not equal. Hear: is not God's way equal, and are not their ways unequal? — Matthew Henry (Concise)

26 COMMENTARY: When a righteous man turns to iniquity and dies in it, he dies for the iniquity he has done. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

27 COMMENTARY: When a wicked man turns from wickedness to lawful and right, he shall save his soul alive. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

28 COMMENTARY: Because he considers and turns from all his transgressions, he shall surely live and not die, the beginning and progress of repentance. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

29 COMMENTARY: Yet Israel says, The way of the Lord is not equal, but God's ways are equal and theirs unequal, and in disputes with God he is in the right. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

30 COMMENTARY: Therefore God will judge each Israelite according to his ways, and bids them repent and turn from all transgressions, so iniquity shall not be their ruin. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

31 COMMENTARY: Cast away all transgressions and make a new heart and new spirit, for why will ye die, O house of Israel? — Matthew Henry (Concise)

32 COMMENTARY: God has no pleasure in the death of him that dies, wherefore turn yourselves and live, seeking from God the change he commands. — Matthew Henry (Concise)
