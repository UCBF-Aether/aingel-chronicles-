Study Notes — Ezekiel

Chapter 9

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: With a loud voice, those with charge over the city are called to draw near, every man with his destroying weapon. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

2 COMMENTARY: Six men with slaughter weapons come from the north gate, and among them a man clothed in linen with a writer's inkhorn, a Mediator figure, stands by the brazen altar. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

3 COMMENTARY: God's glory rises from the cherub to the threshold, denoting the LORD about to leave his mercy-seat and pronounce judgment. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

4 COMMENTARY: The man in linen must mark the foreheads of those who sigh and cry over Jerusalem's abominations, the remnant God keeps pure in wicked times. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

5 COMMENTARY: The others must follow through the city and smite without sparing or pity. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

6 COMMENTARY: Slay utterly old and young, maids, children, and women, but come not near the marked, and begin at the sanctuary, for God hates sin most in those nearest him. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

7 COMMENTARY: Defile the house and fill the courts with the slain, and they go forth and slay in the city. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

8 COMMENTARY: Left alone amid the slaying, Ezekiel falls on his face and pleads whether God will destroy all the residue of Israel. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

9 COMMENTARY: Israel and Judah's iniquity is exceeding great, land full of blood and city full of perverseness, while they claim the LORD has forsaken the earth and sees not. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

10 COMMENTARY: God's eye shall not spare nor pity, but recompense their way upon their head, for where he shows no mercy he does no injustice. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

11 COMMENTARY: The man in linen reports, I have done as thou hast commanded me, faithful to the trust, as Christ loses none given him. — Matthew Henry (Concise)
