Study Notes — Ezekiel

Chapter 40

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: In the twenty-fifth year of captivity, year's beginning, tenth day, fourteenth year after the city was smitten, the LORD's hand brought Ezekiel thither. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

2 COMMENTARY: In God's visions he was brought to Israel, set on a very high mountain, by which was as a city's frame on the south, opening the temple vision to chapter 48. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

3 COMMENTARY: There was a man like brass in appearance, with flax line and measuring reed, standing in the gate, whether Son of God or angel is not clear. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

4 COMMENTARY: Behold with eyes, hear with ears, set heart on all shown, for to show them he was brought hither, and he must declare all to Israel. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

5 COMMENTARY: A wall outside the house round about, and a measuring reed of six cubits by cubit and handbreadth, measuring the building one reed broad and one reed high. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

6 COMMENTARY: Coming to the east gate, going up its stairs, he measured its thresholds, each one reed broad. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

7 COMMENTARY: Every little chamber one reed long and broad, five cubits between chambers, and the inner gate porch threshold one reed. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

8 COMMENTARY: He measured also the inner gate porch, one reed. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

9 COMMENTARY: Then the gate porch, eight cubits, its posts two cubits, the porch inward. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

10 COMMENTARY: The east gate's little chambers, three on each side, of one measure, and posts of one measure on each side. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

11 COMMENTARY: He measured the gate entry's breadth, ten cubits, and its length, thirteen cubits. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

12 COMMENTARY: Space before little chambers one cubit each side, and chambers six cubits each side. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

13 COMMENTARY: He measured the gate from one chamber roof to another's, twenty-five cubits broad, door against door. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

14 COMMENTARY: He made posts of sixty cubits, even to the court post round about the gate. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

15 COMMENTARY: From the entrance gate face to the inner gate porch face were fifty cubits. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

16 COMMENTARY: Narrow windows to chambers and posts within the gate round about, likewise to arches, windows inward, and palm trees on each post. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

17 COMMENTARY: Then brought into the outward court, lo, chambers and a pavement round about, thirty chambers upon the pavement. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

18 COMMENTARY: The pavement by the gates over against their length was the lower pavement. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

19 COMMENTARY: He measured from the lower gate forefront to the inner court without, a hundred cubits eastward and northward. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

20 COMMENTARY: The outward court north gate he measured, length and breadth. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

21 COMMENTARY: Its little chambers three on each side, posts and arches after the first gate's measure, fifty cubits long, twenty-five broad. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

22 COMMENTARY: Its windows, arches, and palm trees after the east gate's measure, seven steps up, arches before them. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

23 COMMENTARY: The inner court gate stood over against the north and east gates, and he measured gate to gate a hundred cubits. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

24 COMMENTARY: Brought toward the south, behold a south gate, posts and arches measured according to these measures. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

25 COMMENTARY: Windows in it and its arches round about, like those windows, fifty cubits long, twenty-five broad. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

26 COMMENTARY: Seven steps up to it, arches before them, palm trees one on each side upon its posts. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

27 COMMENTARY: There was an inner court gate toward the south, and he measured gate to gate southward a hundred cubits. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

28 COMMENTARY: Brought to the inner court by the south gate, he measured the south gate according to these measures. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

29 COMMENTARY: Its chambers, posts, and arches according to these measures, windows round about, fifty cubits long, twenty-five broad. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

30 COMMENTARY: The arches round about twenty-five cubits long and five cubits broad. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

31 COMMENTARY: Its arches toward the utter court, palm trees on posts, going up by eight steps. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

32 COMMENTARY: Brought into the inner court toward the east, he measured the gate according to these measures. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

33 COMMENTARY: Its chambers, posts, and arches according to these measures, windows round about, fifty cubits long, twenty-five broad. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

34 COMMENTARY: Its arches toward the outward court, palm trees on posts on each side, going up by eight steps. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

35 COMMENTARY: Brought to the north gate, he measured it according to these measures. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

36 COMMENTARY: Its chambers, posts, arches, and windows round about, fifty cubits long, twenty-five broad. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

37 COMMENTARY: Its posts toward the utter court, palm trees on posts on each side, going up by eight steps. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

38 COMMENTARY: Chambers and entries by the gate posts, where they washed the burnt offering. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

39 COMMENTARY: In the gate porch two tables on each side, to slay thereon burnt, sin, and trespass offerings, for Christ is both Altar and Sacrifice. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

40 COMMENTARY: At the side without, going up to the north gate entry, two tables, and on the porch side two tables. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

41 COMMENTARY: Four tables on each side by the gate, eight tables whereupon they slew sacrifices. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

42 COMMENTARY: Four hewn stone tables for burnt offering, a cubit and a half long and broad, one cubit high, holding instruments for slaying burnt offering and sacrifice. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

43 COMMENTARY: Within were hooks, a hand broad, fastened round about, and upon the tables was the offering's flesh. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

44 COMMENTARY: Without the inner gate were singers' chambers in the inner court, at the north gate side, prospect south, and one at the east gate side, prospect north. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

45 COMMENTARY: The south-prospect chamber is for priests, keepers of the house's charge. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

46 COMMENTARY: The north-prospect chamber is for priests, keepers of the altar's charge, sons of Zadok among Levi's sons, coming near to minister to the LORD. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

47 COMMENTARY: So he measured the court, a hundred cubits long and broad, foursquare, and the altar before the house. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

48 COMMENTARY: Brought to the house porch, he measured each post, five cubits each side, and the gate breadth three cubits each side. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

49 COMMENTARY: The porch length twenty cubits, breadth eleven cubits, steps up to it, and pillars by the posts, one on each side. — Matthew Henry (Concise)
