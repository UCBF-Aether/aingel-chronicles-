Study Notes — Ezekiel

Chapter 21

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: The word of the LORD comes, explaining the south-forest parable as a sword against Jerusalem and the whole land. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

2 COMMENTARY: Set thy face toward Jerusalem, drop thy word toward the holy places, and prophesy against the land of Israel. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

3 COMMENTARY: God is against the land and will draw his sword from its sheath, cutting off righteous and wicked alike in national judgment. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

4 COMMENTARY: Seeing God cuts off righteous and wicked, his sword goes forth against all flesh from south to north. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

5 COMMENTARY: All flesh shall know the LORD has drawn his sword, and it shall not return any more. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

6 COMMENTARY: Sigh, son of man, with breaking of loins and bitterness before their eyes, for the prophet must show himself in earnest, lamenting ruin he declares. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

7 COMMENTARY: Asked why he sighs, he must answer, For the tidings, because it comes: every heart melts, hands feeble, spirits faint, knees weak as water. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

8 COMMENTARY: Again the word of the LORD comes, with the sword sharpened and furbished. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

9 COMMENTARY: Prophesy, A sword, a sword is sharpened and also furbished, glittering to the terror of those against whom it is drawn. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

10 COMMENTARY: Sharpened for sore slaughter and furbished to glitter, it contemns the rod of God's son as every tree, so should we then make mirth? — Matthew Henry (Concise)

11 COMMENTARY: Given to be furbished that it may be handled, the sword is sharpened and furbished to give into the slayer's hand, God strengthening instruments for their service. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

12 COMMENTARY: Cry and howl, son of man, for the sword shall be upon God's people and all Israel's princes, terrors by reason of the sword, so smite upon thy thigh. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

13 COMMENTARY: Because it is a trial, and even if the sword contemn the rod, it shall be no more, says the Lord GOD. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

14 COMMENTARY: Smite hands together and let the sword be doubled the third time, the sword of the slain, of great men slain entering their privy chambers. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

15 COMMENTARY: The sword's point is set against all gates, that hearts faint and ruins multiply, made bright and wrapped up for slaughter. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

16 COMMENTARY: Go one way or other, right hand or left, whithersoever thy face is set, for the sword spares no quarter. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

17 COMMENTARY: God also will smite his hands together and cause his fury to rest, for the LORD has said it. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

18 COMMENTARY: The word of the LORD comes again, foreseeing Nebuchadnezzar's march, determined by divination yet secretly led by God. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

19 COMMENTARY: Appoint two ways for Babylon's sword, both from one land, choosing a place at the head of the way to the city. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

20 COMMENTARY: Appoint a way to Rabbath of the Ammonites and to Judah in defenced Jerusalem. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

21 COMMENTARY: The king of Babylon stood at the parting of the way to use divination, making arrows bright, consulting images, looking in the liver. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

22 COMMENTARY: At his right hand was divination for Jerusalem, appointing captains, shouting slaughter, battering rams, mounts, and forts. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

23 COMMENTARY: It shall seem false divination to oath-sworn Jerusalem, but he will call iniquity to remembrance, that they may be taken. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

24 COMMENTARY: Because their iniquity is remembered, transgressions discovered, and sins appearing in all doings, they shall be taken with the hand. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

25 COMMENTARY: And thou, profane wicked prince of Israel, whose day is come when iniquity shall have an end, hear thy sentence. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

26 COMMENTARY: Remove the diadem and take off the crown, exalting the low and abasing the high, for this order shall not be the same. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

27 COMMENTARY: God will overturn, overturn, overturn, until he come whose right it is, and he will give it him, the Messiah's kingdom making way through overturnings. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

28 COMMENTARY: Prophesy concerning the Ammonites and their reproach: the sword is drawn, furbished for slaughter to consume because of glittering. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

29 COMMENTARY: While Ammon's diviners see vanity and divine lies, bringing her upon the necks of the slain whose day is come, her doom is sealed. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

30 COMMENTARY: Shall God cause the sword to return to its sheath? He will judge Ammon in her nativity land, where she was created. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

31 COMMENTARY: God will pour out indignation, blow against her in wrath's fire, and deliver her to brutish men skilful to destroy. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

32 COMMENTARY: She shall be fuel to the fire, her blood in the midst of the land, no more remembered, for the LORD has spoken it. — Matthew Henry (Concise)
