Study Notes — Ezekiel

Chapter 16

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Again the word of the LORD comes, to set Jerusalem's dealings with God under a parable of a rescued infant, espoused, then unfaithful. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

2 COMMENTARY: Son of man, cause Jerusalem to know her abominations, for the parable's design is to raise hatred of idolatry. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

3 COMMENTARY: Her birth and nativity are of Canaan, father an Amorite and mother a Hittite, no holy pedigree to boast of. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

4 COMMENTARY: At birth her navel was not cut, nor was she washed, salted, or swaddled, utterly unattended. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

5 COMMENTARY: No eye pitied her, but she was cast out in the open field, loathed, in the day she was born. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

6 COMMENTARY: Passing by and seeing her polluted in her blood, God said, Live, twice over, rescuing the exposed infant from death. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

7 COMMENTARY: She multiplied as the bud of the field, increased and waxed great, and came to excellent ornaments, though once naked and bare. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

8 COMMENTARY: In the time of love God spread his skirt over her, swore to her, and entered covenant with her, and she became his. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

9 COMMENTARY: He washed her thoroughly, washed away her blood, and anointed her with oil. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

10 COMMENTARY: He clothed her with broidered work, badgers' skin shoes, fine linen girdle, and silk covering. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

11 COMMENTARY: He decked her with ornaments, bracelets on her hands, and a chain on her neck. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

12 COMMENTARY: A jewel on her forehead, earrings, and a beautiful crown completed her adornment. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

13 COMMENTARY: Decked with gold and silver, fed fine flour, honey, and oil, she was exceeding beautiful and prospered into a kingdom. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

14 COMMENTARY: Her renown went forth among the heathen for beauty perfect through God's comeliness put upon her. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

15 COMMENTARY: But she trusted in her own beauty and played the harlot because of her renown, pouring out fornications on every passer-by. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

16 COMMENTARY: Taking her garments, she decked high places with divers colours and played the harlot there, an unprecedented defilement. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

17 COMMENTARY: Taking God's gold and silver jewels, she made images of men and committed whoredom with them. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

18 COMMENTARY: She covered the images with her broidered garments and set God's oil and incense before them. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

19 COMMENTARY: God's own meat, fine flour, oil, and honey, she set before idols for a sweet savour. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

20 COMMENTARY: Moreover she took sons and daughters born to God and sacrificed them to idols to be devoured, no small matter among her whoredoms. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

21 COMMENTARY: She slew God's children and made them pass through the fire for idols. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

22 COMMENTARY: In all her abominations she remembered not the days of her youth, when naked, bare, and polluted in blood. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

23 COMMENTARY: After all her wickedness comes a double woe, for judgment follows ingratitude. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

24 COMMENTARY: She built an eminent place and a high place in every street, idolatry made public and pervasive. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

25 COMMENTARY: At every head of the way her high place made her beauty abhorred, opening her feet to every passer-by and multiplying whoredoms. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

26 COMMENTARY: She committed fornication with the Egyptians, great of flesh, increasing whoredoms to provoke God to anger. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

27 COMMENTARY: Therefore God stretched out his hand, diminished her ordinary food, and delivered her to the Philistines, who were ashamed of her lewd way. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

28 COMMENTARY: She played the whore also with the Assyrians, unsatiable, yet could not be satisfied. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

29 COMMENTARY: She multiplied fornication from Canaan unto Chaldea, and yet was not satisfied therewith. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

30 COMMENTARY: How weak is her heart, doing all these things, the work of an imperious whorish woman. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

31 COMMENTARY: Building eminent places at every way's head, she was not even as a harlot, for she scorned hire. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

32 COMMENTARY: But as a wife committing adultery, taking strangers instead of her husband, her sin is covenant-breaking, not mere hire. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

33 COMMENTARY: Gifts are given to all whores, but she gives gifts to her lovers and hires them to come to her on every side. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

34 COMMENTARY: Contrary to other women, none follows her to commit whoredom, yet she gives reward and receives none, therefore she is contrary. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

35 COMMENTARY: Wherefore, O harlot, hear the word of the LORD, for sentence now follows the indictment. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

36 COMMENTARY: Because her filthiness was poured out through whoredoms with lovers and idols, and by the blood of her children given to them, judgment is set. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

37 COMMENTARY: God will gather all her lovers, loved and hated, round about against her, and discover her nakedness to them. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

38 COMMENTARY: She shall be judged as women breaking wedlock and shedding blood are judged, given blood in fury and jealousy. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

39 COMMENTARY: Given into their hand, her eminent place thrown down, high places broken, clothes stripped, jewels taken, left naked and bare. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

40 COMMENTARY: A company brought against her shall stone her and thrust her through with swords. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

41 COMMENTARY: Her houses burned and judgments executed in sight of many women shall make her cease playing the harlot and giving hire any more. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

42 COMMENTARY: So God's fury shall rest, his jealousy depart, and he will be quiet and no more angry. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

43 COMMENTARY: Because she remembered not her youth but fretted God in all things, her way is recompensed upon her head, without adding lewdness above abominations. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

44 COMMENTARY: Every proverb-user shall use this proverb against her: As is the mother, so is her daughter. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

45 COMMENTARY: She is her mother's daughter, loathing husband and children, and sister of sisters who loathed theirs, mother a Hittite and father an Amorite. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

46 COMMENTARY: Her elder sister is Samaria at her left hand, and her younger sister Sodom at her right, with their daughters. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

47 COMMENTARY: She walked not merely after their ways, but as if that were a very little thing, was corrupted more than they in all her ways. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

48 COMMENTARY: As God lives, Sodom and her daughters have not done as Jerusalem and her daughters have done. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

49 COMMENTARY: Sodom's iniquity was pride, fulness of bread, and abundance of idleness, neither strengthening the poor and needy. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

50 COMMENTARY: Haughty and committing abomination before God, Sodom was taken away as God saw good. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

51 COMMENTARY: Samaria has not committed half Jerusalem's sins, yet Jerusalem multiplied abominations more than they and justified her sisters. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

52 COMMENTARY: Having judged her sisters, she must bear her own shame for sins more abominable than theirs, confounded that she justified them. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

53 COMMENTARY: When God brings again the captivity of Sodom and Samaria, he will bring again Jerusalem's captives in their midst. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

54 COMMENTARY: That she may bear her own shame and be confounded in all she has done, in being a comfort to them. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

55 COMMENTARY: When Sodom and Samaria return to their former estate, Jerusalem and her daughters shall return to theirs. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

56 COMMENTARY: Her sister Sodom was not mentioned by her mouth in the day of her pride. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

57 COMMENTARY: Before her wickedness was discovered, she was reproached by Syria's daughters and the Philistines round about, who despise her. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

58 COMMENTARY: She has borne her lewdness and abominations, says the LORD. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

59 COMMENTARY: God will deal with her as she has done, despising the oath in breaking the covenant. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

60 COMMENTARY: Nevertheless God will remember his covenant with her in her youth and establish an everlasting covenant, mercy remembered after full warning. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

61 COMMENTARY: Then she shall remember her ways and be ashamed when she receives her sisters as daughters, but not by her covenant. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

62 COMMENTARY: God will establish his covenant with her, and she shall know he is the LORD. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

63 COMMENTARY: That she may remember, be confounded, and never open her mouth any more because of shame, when God is pacified toward her for all she has done. — Matthew Henry (Concise)
