Study Notes — Ezekiel

Chapter 7

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: The word of the LORD comes again, abrupt and repeated, because the prophet is deeply affected by the coming calamities. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

2 COMMENTARY: An end, the end, is come upon the four corners of the land, a universal judgment none can avoid. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

3 COMMENTARY: Now the end is upon her, and God will judge her according to her ways and recompense all her abominations. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

4 COMMENTARY: God's eye shall not spare nor pity, but recompense her ways, and she shall know he is the LORD. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

5 COMMENTARY: An evil, an only evil, behold, is come, a singular, unmatched calamity. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

6 COMMENTARY: The end watches for her and is come, no longer a distant rumour but a present reality. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

7 COMMENTARY: The morning is come, the day of trouble near, and not the sounding again of the mountains, no mere echo of trouble. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

8 COMMENTARY: Shortly God will pour out his fury and judge according to her ways, for sin is the root of these judgments. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

9 COMMENTARY: Without sparing or pity, God recompenses ways and abominations, and they shall know he is the LORD that smiteth. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

10 COMMENTARY: Behold the day, the morning gone forth, the rod blossomed and pride budded, punishment ripening out of pride itself. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

11 COMMENTARY: Violence risen into a rod of wickedness leaves none remaining, not even wailing for them, so complete is the ruin. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

12 COMMENTARY: Buyer must not rejoice nor seller mourn, for wrath is upon all the multitude, and trade itself is undone. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

13 COMMENTARY: The seller shall not return to what was sold, for the vision touches the whole multitude and none strengthens himself in iniquity. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

14 COMMENTARY: Though the trumpet is blown and all made ready, none goes to battle, for God's wrath is upon all the multitude. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

15 COMMENTARY: Sword without, pestilence and famine within: field dies by sword, city is devoured by famine and pestilence. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

16 COMMENTARY: The few who escape shall be on the mountains like doves of the valleys, mourning every one for his iniquity, sorrow at last sanctified. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

17 COMMENTARY: All hands feeble and all knees weak as water picture terror draining all strength. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

18 COMMENTARY: Sackcloth, horror, shame on all faces, and baldness on all heads are the outward marks of inward desolation. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

19 COMMENTARY: Silver cast in the streets and gold removed cannot deliver in the day of wrath, for wealth is their snare and the stumblingblock of their iniquity. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

20 COMMENTARY: The beauty of his ornament, set in majesty, was turned to images of abomination, so God set it far from them. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

21 COMMENTARY: God gives the ornament into strangers' hands for a prey and to the wicked for spoil, and they shall pollute it. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

22 COMMENTARY: God's face turned away, robbers enter and defile his secret place, for the temple stands them in no stead without godliness. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

23 COMMENTARY: Make a chain, for the land is full of bloody crimes and the city full of violence, and law-breakers are bound by judgment's chains. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

24 COMMENTARY: The worst of the heathen shall possess their houses, the pomp of the strong cease, and holy places be defiled. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

25 COMMENTARY: Destruction comes, and they shall seek peace and find none. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

26 COMMENTARY: Mischief upon mischief and rumour upon rumour leave prophet without vision, priest without law, and ancients without counsel. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

27 COMMENTARY: King mourns, prince is clothed with desolation, and the people's hands are troubled, judged after their way and deserts. — Matthew Henry (Concise)
