Study Notes — Ezekiel

Chapter 12

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: The word of the LORD comes to Ezekiel again, to a people who must be shown their fate in signs. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

2 COMMENTARY: He dwells amid a rebellious house with eyes that see not and ears that hear not. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

3 COMMENTARY: He must prepare stuff for removing and remove by day in their sight, that they may consider, though rebellious. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

4 COMMENTARY: Bringing forth his stuff by day and going forth at evening as captives do, he acts the removal before their eyes. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

5 COMMENTARY: Digging through the wall in their sight and carrying out thereby, he signifies an escape by stealth. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

6 COMMENTARY: Bearing it on his shoulder in twilight, face covered so he sees not the ground, he is set for a sign to Israel, signifying Zedekiah's conduct and fate. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

7 COMMENTARY: He did as commanded, bringing forth his stuff by day, digging through the wall by hand at evening, and bearing it on his shoulder in their sight. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

8 COMMENTARY: In the morning the word of the LORD came, to explain the sign he had acted. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

9 COMMENTARY: The rebellious house asks, What doest thou, the question the sign was designed to provoke. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

10 COMMENTARY: The burden concerns the prince in Jerusalem and all the house of Israel among them. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

11 COMMENTARY: I am your sign: as he has done, so shall it be done to them, and they shall remove and go into captivity. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

12 COMMENTARY: The prince shall bear on his shoulder in twilight and go forth through a dug wall, face covered so he sees not the ground. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

13 COMMENTARY: God's net spread upon him takes him in a snare to Babylon, which he shall not see, though he shall die there. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

14 COMMENTARY: All about him to help him, and all his bands, are scattered to every wind, with a sword drawn after them. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

15 COMMENTARY: Scattering them among nations and dispersing them in countries will teach them he is the LORD. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

16 COMMENTARY: A few left from sword, famine, and pestilence shall declare all their abominations among the heathen, and know he is the LORD. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

17 COMMENTARY: The word of the LORD comes again with a second sign, of eating in fear. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

18 COMMENTARY: Eat bread with quaking and drink water with trembling and carefulness, expressing Jerusalem's condition during the siege. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

19 COMMENTARY: Jerusalem's inhabitants shall eat bread with carefulness and drink water with astonishment, their land desolate because of the violence of its dwellers. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

20 COMMENTARY: Inhabited cities laid waste and land desolate will teach them he is the LORD. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

21 COMMENTARY: The word of the LORD comes again, now against a proverb of delay. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

22 COMMENTARY: The proverb, The days are prolonged, and every vision faileth, hardened them in sin from God's very forbearance. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

23 COMMENTARY: God will make this proverb cease, and they shall say instead, The days are at hand, and the effect of every vision. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

24 COMMENTARY: There shall be no more vain vision nor flattering divination within the house of Israel. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

25 COMMENTARY: God will speak and his word shall come to pass, no more prolonged, performed in their own days, O rebellious house. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

26 COMMENTARY: Again the word of the LORD comes, against the subtler delay that the vision is for many days to come. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

27 COMMENTARY: They say the vision is for times far off, putting the evil day at a distance, which no one can do except by seeking peace with the Lord. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

28 COMMENTARY: None of God's words shall be prolonged any more, but the word he has spoken shall be done. — Matthew Henry (Concise)
