Study Notes — Habakkuk

Chapter 1

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: The burden which Habakkuk the prophet did see. The subject of the prophecy, Henry notes, is the destruction of Judah and Jerusalem for the people's sins, and the consolation of the faithful under national calamity. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

2 COMMENTARY: O LORD, how long shall I cry, and thou wilt not hear, even cry out of violence, and thou wilt not save? The servants of the Lord are deeply afflicted, Henry observes, by seeing ungodliness and violence prevail, especially among those who profess the truth. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

3 COMMENTARY: Why dost thou show me iniquity and cause me to behold grievance, with spoiling and violence before me and strife and contention raised up? No man scrupled doing wrong to his neighbour, and the prophet longed for the world where holiness and love reign for ever. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

4 COMMENTARY: Therefore the law is slacked and judgment never goes forth, for the wicked compass the righteous and wrong judgment proceeds. When sentence against evil is not executed speedily, Henry warns, men's hearts become fully set in them to do evil. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

5 COMMENTARY: Behold among the heathen and regard and wonder marvellously, for God will work a work in their days which they will not believe though told them. They were to notice what was going forward among the heathen by the Chaldeans, and consider themselves a nation to be scourged by them. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

6 COMMENTARY: For lo, God raises up the Chaldeans, a bitter and hasty nation marching through the breadth of the land to possess dwellings not theirs. Most men, Henry notes, presume on continued prosperity or that calamities will not come in their days. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

7 COMMENTARY: They are terrible and dreadful; their judgment and dignity proceed of themselves. The Chaldeans answer to no law but their own will, fierce and cruel and bearing down all before them. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

8 COMMENTARY: Their horses are swifter than leopards and more fierce than evening wolves, and their horsemen spread themselves, coming from far. The invader's speed and ferocity leave no room for escape. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

9 COMMENTARY: They shall come all for violence, their faces supping up as the east wind, gathering captivity as the sand. Nothing before them stands; they sweep up peoples like wind-blown dust. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

10 COMMENTARY: They shall scoff at kings and princes shall be a scorn to them, deriding every stronghold, for they shall heap dust and take it. Fortress walls are no defence against an army that laughs at kings. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

11 COMMENTARY: Then shall his mind change, and he shall pass over and offend, imputing this his power unto his god. It is the common offence of proud people, Henry observes, to take glory to themselves; the closing words of this section still give a glimpse of comfort. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

12 COMMENTARY: Art thou not from everlasting, O LORD my God, mine Holy One? We shall not die. However matters may be, God is the Lord our God, our Holy One, and the prophet will not entertain hard thoughts of him or of his service. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

13 COMMENTARY: Thou art of purer eyes than to behold evil, and canst not look on iniquity; why then lookest thou on the treacherous and holdest thy tongue when the wicked devours the man more righteous than he? God is holy and does not approve wickedness, Henry insists, and by this principle we must abide even when his providence seems not to agree with it. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

14 COMMENTARY: And makest men as the fishes of the sea, as creeping things with no ruler over them? Left to the Chaldeans, peoples seem as defenceless as fish with no protector. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

15 COMMENTARY: They take up all of them with the angle, catch them in their net, and gather them in their drag, therefore they rejoice and are glad. Some victims are taken one by one, others caught in shoals, as in a net. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

16 COMMENTARY: Therefore they sacrifice unto their net and burn incense unto their drag, because by them their portion is fat and their meat plenteous. They admire their own cleverness and contrivance, idolising themselves and sacrificing to the net because it is their own. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

17 COMMENTARY: Shall they therefore empty their net and not spare continually to slay the nations? Whatever advantages we possess, Henry concludes, we must give all the glory to God, for God will soon end successful and splendid robberies, and death and judgment shall make predators cease to prey. — Matthew Henry (Concise)
