Study Notes — Habakkuk

Chapter 3

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: A prayer of Habakkuk the prophet upon Shigionoth. The word prayer here seems used for an act of devotion, Henry notes, and the chapter is the prophet's prayer set to music for the chief singer. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

2 COMMENTARY: O LORD, I have heard thy speech, and was afraid: O LORD, revive thy work in the midst of the years, in the midst of the years make known; in wrath remember mercy. The Lord would revive his work among the people in the midst of the years of adversity, Henry says, and this may be applied to every season when the church suffers under afflictions and trials. Mercy is what we must flee to for refuge and rely upon as our only plea: we must not say, Remember our merit, but, Lord, remember thy own mercy. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

3 COMMENTARY: God came from Teman, and the Holy One from mount Paran, and his glory covered the heavens while the earth was full of his praise. God's people, when in distress and ready to despair, seek help by considering the days of old and pleading them with God in prayer, Henry observes. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

4 COMMENTARY: His brightness was as the light; he had horns coming out of his hand, and there was the hiding of his power. God appeared in his glory, Henry notes, and the resemblance between the Babylonish and Egyptian captivities presents itself, with the possibility of a like deliverance through Jehovah's power. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

5 COMMENTARY: Before him went the pestilence, and burning coals went forth at his feet. All the powers of nature are shaken and the course of nature changed, Henry says, but all is for the salvation of God's own people. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

6 COMMENTARY: He stood, and measured the earth; he beheld, and drove asunder the nations; and the everlasting mountains were scattered, the perpetual hills did bow; his ways are everlasting. Even what seems least likely, Henry observes, shall be made to work for their salvation. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

7 COMMENTARY: I saw the tents of Cushan in affliction, and the curtains of the land of Midian did tremble. The neighbouring peoples trembled at God's march, as Israel's enemies had trembled of old. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

8 COMMENTARY: The prophet asked whether the LORD was displeased against the rivers or wroth against the sea, since he rode upon his horses and chariots of salvation. The waters were not the object of God's anger; they were the path of his salvation. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

9 COMMENTARY: God's bow was made quite naked according to the oaths of the tribes, even his word, and he clave the earth with rivers. His bow was bared according to his sworn word to the tribes. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

10 COMMENTARY: The mountains saw thee, and they trembled; the overflowing of the water passed by; the deep uttered his voice, and lifted up his hands on high. Nature itself responded to God's appearing, mountain, water, and deep together. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

11 COMMENTARY: The sun and moon stood still in their habitation; at the light of thine arrows they went, and at the shining of thy glittering spear. Even sun and moon halted before the brightness of God's weapons. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

12 COMMENTARY: Thou didst march through the land in indignation, thou didst thresh the heathen in anger. God's march through the land was judgment on the nations oppressing his people. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

13 COMMENTARY: God went forth for the salvation of his people, even for salvation with his anointed, wounding the head of the house of the wicked and discovering its foundation unto the neck. Hereby is given a type and figure of the redemption of the world by Jesus Christ, Henry says, for in all Israel's salvations God looked upon Christ the Anointed and brought deliverances to pass by him. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

14 COMMENTARY: Thou didst strike through with his staves the head of his villages; they came out as a whirlwind to scatter me; their rejoicing was as to devour the poor secretly. The enemy's own weapons were turned upon his leaders. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

15 COMMENTARY: Thou didst walk through the sea with thine horses, through the heap of great waters. The Red Sea march stands behind the prayer as the pattern of deliverance. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

16 COMMENTARY: When I heard, my belly trembled; my lips quivered at the voice; rottenness entered into my bones, and I trembled in myself, that I might rest in the day of trouble, when he cometh up unto the people, he will invade them with his troops. When we see a day of trouble approach, Henry says, it concerns us to prepare; a good hope through grace is founded in holy fear. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

17 COMMENTARY: Although the fig tree shall not blossom, neither shall fruit be in the vines; the labour of the olive shall fail, and the fields shall yield no meat; the flock shall be cut off from the fold, and there shall be no herd in the stalls — the prophet looked back upon the church's former experiences, Henry notes, observed what great things God had done, and so was not only recovered but filled with holy joy. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

18 COMMENTARY: Yet I will rejoice in the LORD, I will joy in the God of my salvation. He resolved to delight and triumph in the Lord, Henry says, for when all is gone, his God is not gone; destroy the vines and fig-trees and you make all the mirth of a carnal heart cease, but those who, when full, enjoyed God in all, when emptied and poor can enjoy all in God. — Matthew Henry (Concise)

19 COMMENTARY: The LORD God is my strength, and he will make my feet like hinds' feet and make me to walk upon mine high places, a song for the chief singer on stringed instruments. Joy in the Lord is especially seasonable when we meet with losses and crosses, Henry concludes, for even when provisions are cut off we may be supplied by the graces and comforts of God's Spirit. — Matthew Henry (Concise)
