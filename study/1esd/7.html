Study Notes — 1 Esdras

Chapter 7

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: JFB notes Tatnai and his companions did speedily what the decree commanded, a concurrence of favorable events accelerating the restoration and infusing new spirit and energy into the workmen. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: JFB notes the governor and his companions furthered the work speedily, and the workmen labored with unabating assiduity till the temple was brought to completion. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: JFB notes the prophets' appeals had given a new impulse to the building, and that the concurrence of favorable events under Darius accelerated the work to completion. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: JFB notes the temple's foundation was laid in April 536 B.C. and it was completed on February 21, 515 B.C., twenty-one years after it was begun, by the commandment of God and with the consent of the Persian kings. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: JFB notes the temple was completed on February 21, 515 B.C., being twenty-one years after its foundation was laid. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: JFB notes the dedication was kept with demonstrations of the liveliest joy, since the aged who had wept at the laying of the foundation were most, if not all, now dead, and all rejoiced at the completion of this national undertaking. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: JFB notes the twelve he-goats correspond to the dedication of the tabernacle, as in Numbers 7:87 and 8:17. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: JFB notes the priests were set in their divisions and the Levites in their courses as written in the book of Moses, and that although David arranged the courses by families, it was Moses who assigned to priests and Levites their rights, privileges, stations, and duties. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: JFB notes those who had separated themselves from the filthiness of the heathen had given satisfactory evidence of being true proselytes, not only renouncing impure idolatrous worship but undergoing circumcision, a condition indispensable to participation in the Passover. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: JFB notes the religious joy characterizing the feast was testified by lively gratitude to God, whose overruling power and converting grace had produced so marvelous a change in the hearts of mighty potentates, disposing them, pagans though they were, to aid the cause and provide for the worship of the true God. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: JFB notes the Lord turned the heart of the king of Assyria, that is, the king of the Persian empire which now included Assyria's possessions and surpassed its glory, and that his favorable disposition secured the Jews peace, prosperity, and the privileges of their own religion during the rest of his reign. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

No note for verse(s) 6, 10, 11, 12 — the public-domain sources consulted give no distinct explanation for these verses beyond the list itself, so nothing was invented for them.
