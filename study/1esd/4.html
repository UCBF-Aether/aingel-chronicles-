Study Notes — 1 Esdras

Chapter 4

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

3 COMMENTARY: Bissell notes the verb for obey here is a rare word meaning to hear so as to obey, found elsewhere in the Septuagint only at Nahum 1:12. — E. C. Bissell

4 COMMENTARY: Bissell notes the verb rendered demolish or break down carries the secondary meaning of making an end of, as in Ephesians 6:13 and in Josephus and Xenophon. — E. C. Bissell

9 COMMENTARY: Bissell compares the command to cut down with Deuteronomy 20:19, where besiegers are forbidden to destroy fruit trees, since the tree of the field is man's life. — E. C. Bissell

13 COMMENTARY: Bissell identifies the third speaker as Zorobabel, referring to the margin of the 1611 version at Ezra 3:2 and to his introduction's discussion of the book's arrangement of materials. — E. C. Bissell

15 COMMENTARY: Bissell notes the verb rendered have borne is used in the same sense at Isaiah 46:3, 4 Maccabees 10:2, Luke 1:57, and John 16:21. — E. C. Bissell

17 COMMENTARY: Bissell compares making garments for men with Proverbs 31:13 and 19, and notes bringing glory is better taken in the sense of ornamentation, as in the additions to Esther, 1 Maccabees 14:9, and Matthew 6:29. — E. C. Bissell

21 COMMENTARY: Bissell explains the phrase about spending life with his wife by the Greek idiom for dying by or with his wife, used in the same sense at Genesis 35:18, Herodotus 4:90, and Thucydides 2:49, meaning he remains by his wife till death. — E. C. Bissell

26 COMMENTARY: Bissell notes the case of Jacob, who served for Rachel, was probably in the speaker's mind when he says many have become servants for women's sakes, citing Genesis 29:20. — E. C. Bissell

29 COMMENTARY: Bissell compares the concubine standing at the king's right hand with Psalm 45:9, where the queen stands at the king's right hand in gold of Ophir. — E. C. Bissell

32 COMMENTARY: Bissell notes the phrase about being reconciled to him has the margin in the Authorized Version, or be friends with him, and the Greek word is the same one employed at Matthew 5:24. — E. C. Bissell

33 COMMENTARY: Bissell notes the princes are the megistanes, rendered great men in the Authorized Version at Revelation 6:15, and that the construction for looking one upon another has caused variation in the manuscripts. — E. C. Bissell

35 COMMENTARY: Bissell notes the question about who is great that does these things refers to the sun, not to God the Creator, since the present tense excludes the Creator, and that stronger than all things uses a comparative strengthened by a preposition. — E. C. Bissell

36 COMMENTARY: Bissell notes the verb rendered calleth means rather calleth in the sense of inviteth, though the margin of the Authorized Version has praiseth, following Athanasius. — E. C. Bissell

37 COMMENTARY: Bissell notes unrighteous is used here in contradistinction to truth, and compares the usage at verse 40 and Hebrews 6:10. — E. C. Bissell

39 COMMENTARY: Bissell notes the phrase about making no distinctions is literally distinction or difference, and that the sense is debated, whether truth practices right while requiring it from the unrighteous and wicked, or deals righteously with all the unrighteous and evil. — E. C. Bissell

40 COMMENTARY: Bissell compares the ascription of strength, kingdom, power, and majesty with 1 Chronicles 29:11, and notes Fritzsche's judgment that the closing doxology shows the author is not identifying truth with God, though Ewald thinks divine truth in Israel is meant. — E. C. Bissell

42 COMMENTARY: Bissell notes the title of kinsman bestowed here is the same honorary title explained at 3:7, a mark of royal favor rather than blood kinship. — E. C. Bissell

43 COMMENTARY: Bissell notes Josephus gives a different coloring to the king's conduct, reporting that while yet a subject Darius had vowed that if he came to the throne he would send all the vessels of God in Babylon back to Jerusalem. — E. C. Bissell

44 COMMENTARY: Bissell cross-refers the vessels Cyrus removed to 1:41, 2:10, and 6:18-19, tracing the same sacred vessels through the book's account of their captivity and restoration. — E. C. Bissell

45 COMMENTARY: Bissell explains the Edomites are the Idumaeans, descendants of Esau, who after Nebuchadnezzar's siege were allowed to occupy part of the depopulated country and retained it for several hundred years, and notes that their actually burning the temple is nowhere else stated, so no more may be meant than participation with others in the act. — E. C. Bissell

46 COMMENTARY: Bissell compares the title King of heaven with Daniel 4:37 and Tobit 13:7 and 11, where the same title is used for God. — E. C. Bissell

50 COMMENTARY: Bissell notes what is said of the Idumaeans giving over the villages only took place to a limited extent. — E. C. Bissell

52 COMMENTARY: Bissell explains the yearly provision for the daily burnt offerings more literally as offerings presented as fruits to be sacrificed daily, and notes it is not clear where the compiler gets his information that seventeen burnt offerings were to be sacrificed every day, comparing Exodus 29:38 and Numbers 28:3ff. — E. C. Bissell

56 COMMENTARY: Bissell notes the word rendered dwellings has the margin in the Authorized Version, portions of land, and that the Syriac and Vulgate render it by lots and the Old Latin by possessions, so that joined with wages it seems to mean dwellings including the land appertaining to them. — E. C. Bissell

62 COMMENTARY: Bissell notes freedom and liberty form a good example of paronomasia, and that the word for liberty is employed in the Septuagint mostly in connection with the year of Jubilee. — E. C. Bissell

63 COMMENTARY: Bissell notes with music possibly means musical instruments, as the Authorized Version supposes, comparing verse 59, Daniel 3:5, and 1 Maccabees 9:39 and 41, while the Old Latin has striking cymbals with music in great joy for seven days. — E. C. Bissell

No note for verse(s) 1, 2, 5, 6, 7, 8, 10, 11, 12, 14, 16, 18, 19, 20, 22, 23, 24, 25, 27, 28, 30, 31, 34, 38, 41, 47, 48, 49, 51, 53, 54, 55, 57, 58, 59, 60, 61 — the public-domain sources consulted give no distinct explanation for these verses beyond the list itself, so nothing was invented for them.
