Study Notes — 1 Esdras

Chapter 6

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: JFB notes the difficulties and obstacles had first cooled the Jews' zeal and then led to abandonment of the work under a pretended belief that the time for rebuilding had not yet come, until after fifteen years of suspension Haggai and Zechariah upbraided them for sloth, negligence, and worldly selfishness and promised blessing if they resumed with vigor. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: JFB notes the strong appeals and animating exhortations of the prophets gave a new impulse to the building, and that it was in the second year of Darius Hystaspes that the work, after long interruption, was resumed. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: JFB notes Tatnai was governor on this side the river, that Syria formed one satrapy including Palestine, Phoenicia, and Cyprus, and that though superior to the native Jewish governors he never interfered with internal government except when order was threatened, so he repaired to Jerusalem to ascertain the real state of matters by personal inspection, probably incited by Samaritan complaints. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: JFB notes the governor's inquiries reduced to two heads: by what authority the national temple was undertaken, and the names of the principal promoters and directors of the undertaking. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: JFB notes the historian throws in the parenthetic remark that the eye of God was upon the elders, so that the formidable presence and authoritative inquiries of the satrap did not dispirit the Jews or afford triumph to their opponents, and matters were to go on till contrary orders arrived from Babylon. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: JFB notes God averted the threatening cloud and procured favor for the elders, so that they were not interrupted in their proceedings till communications with the court should be made and received. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: JFB notes Tatnai transmitted all the information in an official report to his imperial master, with a recommendatory suggestion that search be made among the national archives at Babylon for the original decree of Cyrus, so the Jews' statement might be verified. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: JFB notes Tatnai prudently resolved to repair to Jerusalem to ascertain the real state of matters by personal inspection and enquiry, in company with another dignified officer and his provincial council. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: JFB notes the house is described as built with great stones, literally stones of rolling, that is, stones of such extraordinary size that they could not be carried but had to be rolled or dragged along the ground. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: JFB notes the elders were left uninterrupted in their proceedings while the report went to court, so that the work went on prosperously in their hands until contrary orders arrived. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: JFB notes the two heads of enquiry were authority for the undertaking and the names of the principal promoters, to which the Jews returned ready and distinct replies. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: JFB notes the governor demanded the names of the principal promoters and directors, the second head of his enquiry, and that the Jews returned ready and distinct replies. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: JFB notes the Jews were perfectly warranted to proceed with the building in virtue of Cyrus's edict, since a public decree remains in force until revoked, and the laws of the Medes and Persians changed not. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

18 COMMENTARY: JFB notes Tatnai's report recorded that Cyrus had not only released the exiles and permitted their return for the express purpose of rebuilding the house of God, but by royal grace had restored the sacred vessels Nebuchadnezzar had carried off as trophies. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

19 COMMENTARY: JFB notes the Jews' reply traced the work to Cyrus's decree, which released them for the express purpose of rebuilding and restored the sacred vessels, with commandment that the temple be built in its place. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

20 COMMENTARY: JFB notes this was not part of the Jews' answer, since they could not have said the building had continued, knowing it had long ceased, but Tatnai used these expressions in his report, either viewing the stoppage as a temporary interruption or supposing the Jews were always working a little as they had means and opportunities. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

21 COMMENTARY: JFB notes Tatnai accompanied his report with a recommendatory suggestion that search be made among the national archives at Babylon for the original decree of Cyrus, that the truth of the Jews' statement might be verified. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

22 COMMENTARY: JFB notes the whole conduct of Tatnai and the general tone of his dispatch were marked by sound discretion and prudent moderation, free from party bias, evincing a desire only to do his duty, in favorable contrast with his predecessor Rehum. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

23 COMMENTARY: JFB notes Darius was Darius Hystaspes, on whose reign the decipherment of the cuneiform inscriptions at Behistun has thrown great light, and that the house of the rolls was the Babylonian register house, comparable to the chambers of clay tablets discovered in the palace at Nineveh. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

28 COMMENTARY: JFB notes the decree granted the privilege of drawing from the provincial treasury of Syria whatever was required for furthering the work and providing sacrifice for the temple service, that the priests might daily pray for the health of the king and the prosperity of the empire. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

29 COMMENTARY: JFB notes the decree granted drawing from the tribute beyond the river for the sacrifices of the Lord, including bullocks, rams, and lambs, so the priests might daily pray for the king and the empire. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

30 COMMENTARY: JFB notes the expenses were to be given out of the king's goods, even the tribute beyond the river, for whatever was required for the work and the daily service of the temple. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

31 COMMENTARY: JFB notes the sacrifices were provided that the priests might daily pray for the health of the king and the prosperity of the empire. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

32 COMMENTARY: JFB notes the warning against altering the decree was specially directed against the turbulent and fanatical Samaritans, and that the edict's extremely favorable purport owed something to Darius's admiration for Cyrus and more to deep impressions of the being and providence of the God of Israel. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

33 COMMENTARY: JFB notes the warning was specially directed against the turbulent and fanatical Samaritans who would hinder the house, and that the edict's favorable purport proceeded from deep impressions made even on idolatrous people as to the being and providence of the God of Israel. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

34 COMMENTARY: JFB notes a concurrence of favorable events accelerated the restoration and infused new energy into the workmen, who labored with unabating assiduity till the temple was brought to completion. — Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

No note for verse(s) 13, 14, 15, 16, 24, 25, 26, 27 — the public-domain sources consulted give no distinct explanation for these verses beyond the list itself, so nothing was invented for them.
