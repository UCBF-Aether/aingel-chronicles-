Study Notes — Ezra

Chapter 9

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Now when these things were done, the princes came to me, saying, The people of Israel, priests and Levites, have not separated themselves from the peoples of the lands, doing according to their abominations, even of the Canaanites, Hittites, Perizzites, Jebusites, Ammonites, Moabites, Egyptians and Amorites. Henry notes the charge is comprehensive: the separation commanded at the conquest has been undone by the returned people themselves. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: For they have taken of their daughters for themselves and for their sons: so that the holy seed have mingled themselves with the peoples of those lands: yea, the hand of the princes and rulers has been chief in this trespass. JFB notes the leaders led the sin, which is why Ezra hears it from the princes with such gravity. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: And when I heard this thing, I rent my garment and my mantle, and plucked off the hair of my head and beard, and sat down astonied. Henry notes Ezra's grief is bodily and public: tearing and plucking are mourning's language, not mere disapproval. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: Then were assembled unto me every one that trembled at the words of the God of Israel, because of the transgression of those carried away; and I sat astonied until the evening sacrifice. JFB notes a second company gathers: those who tremble at God's word stand with Ezra, while the transgressors are still unnamed. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: And at the evening sacrifice I arose from my heaviness; and having rent my garment and mantle, I fell upon my knees, and spread out my hands unto the LORD my God. Henry notes the timing: private grief becomes public prayer at the hour of the evening sacrifice, when the people are gathered. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: And said, O my God, I am ashamed and blush to lift up my face to thee, my God: for our iniquities are increased over our head, and our trespass is grown up unto the heavens. JFB notes Ezra confesses as one of the people: 'our iniquities,' though he himself has not taken a strange wife. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: Since the days of our fathers have we been in a great trespass unto this day; and for our iniquities have we, our kings and priests, been delivered into the hand of the kings of the lands, to the sword, to captivity, to a spoil, and to confusion of face, as it is this day. Henry notes the history lesson: the present trespass repeats the old one, and exile was its wages. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: And now for a little space grace has been showed from the LORD our God, to leave us a remnant to escape, and to give us a nail in his holy place, that our God may lighten our eyes, and give us a little reviving in our bondage. JFB notes the 'nail' is a peg or stake fixing a tent: a small, firm hold in the holy place, not yet a rebuilt nation. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: For we were bondmen; yet our God has not forsaken us in our bondage, but has extended mercy unto us in the sight of the kings of Persia, to give us a reviving, to set up the house of our God, and to repair its desolations, and to give us a wall in Judah and Jerusalem. Henry notes the paradox: still bondmen, yet revived; still under Persia, yet with a house and a wall. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: And now, O our God, what shall we say after this? for we have forsaken thy commandments. After grace recounted, the prayer turns to the new offence, which makes the old mercy seem thrown away. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: Which thou hast commanded by thy servants the prophets, saying, The land unto which ye go to possess it is an unclean land with the filthiness of the peoples of the lands, with their abominations, which have filled it from one end to another with their uncleanness. JFB notes Ezra quotes the prophets' summary of the law: the land's uncleanness is moral, the peoples' abominations, not its soil. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: Now therefore give not your daughters unto their sons, neither take their daughters unto your sons, nor seek their peace or their wealth for ever: that ye may be strong, and eat the good of the land, and leave it for an inheritance to your children for ever. Henry notes the prohibition aims at the future: intermarriage would trade the children's inheritance for present alliances. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: And after all that is come upon us for our evil deeds and our great trespass, seeing that thou our God hast punished us less than our iniquities deserve, and hast given us such deliverance as this. JFB notes the mercy is measured: punishment less than deserved, deliverance greater than expected. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: Should we again break thy commandments, and join in affinity with the peoples of these abominations? wouldest not thou be angry with us till thou hadst consumed us, so that there should be no remnant nor escaping? Henry notes the question is its own warning: a second offence after such deliverance could leave no remnant at all. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: O LORD God of Israel, thou art righteous: for we remain yet escaped, as it is this day: behold, we are before thee in our trespasses: for we cannot stand before thee because of this. JFB notes the prayer ends without excuse: God's righteousness is confessed, and the people stand guilty before it, awaiting what he will do. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
