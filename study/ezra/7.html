Study Notes — Ezra

Chapter 7

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: After these things, in the reign of Artaxerxes, Ezra son of Seraiah, son of Azariah, son of Hilkiah, comes forward. JFB notes 'son' here means descendant: Seraiah the high priest died at Riblah long before, so Ezra is his grandson or later descendant. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: This Ezra went up from Babylon, a ready scribe in the law of Moses which the LORD God of Israel had given, and the king granted him all his request, according to the hand of the LORD his God upon him. Henry notes the title 'ready scribe' means not a mere copyist but a rabbi learned in law and polity, and his success is credited to God's hand, not court favour alone. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: There went up with him some of Israel, priests, Levites, singers, porters and Nethinim, to Jerusalem, in the seventh year of Artaxerxes. JFB notes this second caravan is smaller and more priestly: Ezra brings teachers and temple servants, not only settlers. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: He came to Jerusalem in the fifth month, in the seventh year of the king. The arrival date anchors Ezra's mission in a real reign, after the temple had already stood for decades. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: On the first day of the first month he began to go up from Babylon, and on the first day of the fifth month he came to Jerusalem, according to the good hand of his God upon him. Henry notes the four-month journey is summed up in one refrain: God's good hand, repeated as the cause of every success in this book. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: For Ezra had prepared his heart to seek the law of the LORD, and to do it, and to teach in Israel statutes and judgments. JFB notes the threefold order: seek, do, teach. Ezra's authority to teach rests on a heart prepared and a life obedient. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: This is the copy of the letter Artaxerxes gave to Ezra the priest, the scribe, even a scribe of the words of the LORD's commandments and statutes to Israel. The letter is quoted in full, so the reader can weigh Ezra's commission in the king's own words. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: 'Artaxerxes, king of kings, unto Ezra the priest, a scribe of the law of the God of heaven, perfect peace.' Henry notes the heathen king's titles acknowledge the God of heaven by name, a confession wrung from a Persian court. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: The king decrees that all of Israel, priests and Levites in his realm, who are minded of their own freewill to go up to Jerusalem, may go with Ezra. JFB notes the return remains voluntary: the decree opens the road, it does not drive the people down it. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: Forasmuch as thou art sent by the king and his seven counsellors, to enquire concerning Judah and Jerusalem according to the law of thy God in thy hand. Henry notes Ezra is sent as an inspector of the law's observance, with the law-book itself as his standard. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: And to carry the silver and gold which the king and his counsellors have freely offered to the God of Israel, whose habitation is in Jerusalem. The king's own free offering precedes any tax: the court gives before it commands giving. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: And all the silver and gold thou canst find in all the province of Babylon, with the freewill offering of the people and priests, willingly offered for the house of their God at Jerusalem. JFB notes two treasuries feed the temple: the king's gift and the people's willing gifts. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: That thou mayest buy speedily with this money bullocks, rams, lambs, with their meat and drink offerings, and offer them on the altar of the house of your God at Jerusalem. The money is earmarked for sacrifice, not for Ezra's enrichment. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

18 COMMENTARY: And whatever seems good to thee and thy brethren to do with the rest of the silver and gold, do after the will of your God. Henry notes the latitude is bounded: discretion is allowed, but only within God's will. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

19 COMMENTARY: The vessels given for the service of the house of thy God, deliver thou before the God of Jerusalem. Vessels are not Ezra's to keep; they are to be presented at the house itself. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

20 COMMENTARY: And whatever more is needful for the house of thy God, which thou shalt have occasion to bestow, bestow it out of the king's treasure house. The king underwrites even unforeseen needs, an open-ended grant for God's house. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

21 COMMENTARY: And I, even I Artaxerxes, decree to all treasurers beyond the river, that whatever Ezra the priest, scribe of the law of the God of heaven, requires of you, it be done speedily. JFB notes the decree to the treasurers makes Ezra's word the king's word across the whole province. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

22 COMMENTARY: Unto a hundred talents of silver, a hundred measures of wheat, a hundred baths of wine, a hundred baths of oil, and salt without prescribing how much. The fixed limits for most goods, with salt unlimited, show a practical court that knows temple needs. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

23 COMMENTARY: Whatever is commanded by the God of heaven, let it be diligently done for the house of the God of heaven: for why should there be wrath against the realm of the king and his sons? Henry notes the king's motive is self-protection: a neglected God of heaven might turn his wrath on the empire. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

24 COMMENTARY: Also we certify you, that touching any priests, Levites, singers, porters, Nethinim or ministers of this house of God, it shall not be lawful to impose toll, tribute or custom upon them. JFB notes the temple staff is made tax-exempt, a privilege that marks the house as under royal protection. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

25 COMMENTARY: And thou, Ezra, after the wisdom of thy God in thy hand, set magistrates and judges to judge all the people beyond the river, all such as know the laws of thy God; and teach them that know them not. Henry notes Ezra's commission joins government and teaching: judges enforce the law, and the ignorant are taught it, not merely punished by it. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

26 COMMENTARY: And whoever will not do the law of thy God and the law of the king, let judgment be executed speedily upon him, whether unto death, banishment, confiscation of goods, or imprisonment. JFB notes the two laws are joined: disobedience to God's law is now also an offence against the king. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

27 COMMENTARY: Blessed be the LORD God of our fathers, who has put such a thing as this in the king's heart, to beautify the house of the LORD at Jerusalem. Henry notes Ezra's first response is praise, crediting God, not the king, for the king's heart. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

28 COMMENTARY: And hath extended mercy unto me before the king, his counsellors and all his mighty princes. And I was strengthened as the hand of the LORD my God was upon me, and I gathered out of Israel chief men to go up with me. JFB notes the refrain returns: God's hand strengthens, and strengthened men then gather others. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

No note for verse(s) 2, 3, 4, 5 — the public-domain sources consulted give no distinct explanation for these verses beyond the list itself, so nothing was invented for them.
