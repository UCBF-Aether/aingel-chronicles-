Study Notes — Ezra

Chapter 3

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: In the seventh month, with the people settled in their cities, they gathered as one man to Jerusalem. Henry notes the unity: the feast month that once saw the tabernacle's dedication now sees a scattered people act together. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: Jeshua son of Jozadak with his fellow priests, and Zerubbabel son of Shealtiel with his brethren, built the altar of the God of Israel. Priest and governor act together, and the altar comes before the temple, as atonement comes before building. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: They set the altar on its bases, though fear was on them because of the peoples of the countries round about. JFB notes worship was resumed under threat, and morning and evening offerings began at once, as the law required. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: They kept the feast of tabernacles as written, with daily burnt offerings by number as each day required. Henry notes the return to the written calendar: exile had interrupted the feasts, but not the law that ordered them. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: Afterwards they offered the continual burnt offering, the new-moon offerings, all the set feasts of the LORD, and every freewill offering. The full round of worship is restored, not only the great feast. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: From the first day of the seventh month they began to offer burnt offerings, though the temple foundation was not yet laid. Henry stresses the point: sacrifice did not wait for a finished house, because the altar itself was God's appointed place. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: Money went to masons and carpenters, and meat, drink and oil to Tyre and Sidon for cedar from Lebanon to Joppa, under Cyrus's grant. JFB notes the parallel with Solomon's building, now on a humbler scale and by a foreign king's leave. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: In the second year, in the second month, Zerubbabel, Jeshua, the priests, Levites and all the returned exiles began, appointing Levites from twenty years old upward to set forward the work. Henry notes the careful organisation: the work is entrusted to a tested age of Levites. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: Jeshua with his sons and brethren, Kadmiel and his sons, the sons of Judah, and the sons of Henadad with their brethren the Levites, stood together to set forward the workmen. The overseers are named in families, showing the whole Levite body engaged. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: When the builders laid the foundation, priests in apparel stood with trumpets and Levites, sons of Asaph, with cymbals, to praise the LORD after David's ordinance. JFB notes worship and work are joined: the foundation is laid to music, as David ordered. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: They sang by course, praising and giving thanks because he is good, for his mercy endures for ever toward Israel, and all the people shouted greatly. Henry notes the ancient refrain is sung over a new beginning, linking restoration to the old covenant mercies. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: Many ancient priests, Levites and chiefs who had seen the first house wept loudly when this foundation was laid before their eyes, while many shouted for joy. JFB notes the tears were not mere grief: they remembered a glory this second house could not yet match. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: The people could not discern the shout of joy from the weeping, for the shout was loud and heard afar off. Henry notes joy and sorrow mingled at every true restoration, since what is regained still bears the marks of what was lost. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
