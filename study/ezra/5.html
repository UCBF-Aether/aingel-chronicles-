Study Notes — Ezra

Chapter 5

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Then Haggai and Zechariah son of Iddo prophesied to the Jews in Judah and Jerusalem in the name of the God of Israel. Henry notes the work restarted not by a new royal decree but by God's word through his prophets. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: Then Zerubbabel and Jeshua rose up and began to build the house of God, and the prophets of God were helping them. JFB notes the prophets helped not only by preaching but by presence, sharing the risk of an unauthorised resumption. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: Tatnai, governor beyond the river, with Shethar-boznai and their companions, asked who had commanded them to build this house and make up this wall. Henry notes the question is fair on its face, and the builders answer it without defiance. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: Then said we unto them after this manner, What are the names of the men that make this building? The question demands the builders' names, so that responsibility can be fixed if the king disallows the work. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: But the eye of their God was upon the elders of the Jews, so that they could not cause them to cease until the matter came to Darius. Henry's key note: God's watchful eye, not the builders' arguments, kept the work going through the inquiry. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: This is the copy of the letter Tatnai, Shethar-boznai and the Apharsachites beyond the river sent to Darius. Unlike the adversaries' letter, this report is preserved as a fair, factual account. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: They sent a letter written thus: 'Unto Darius the king, all peace.' The courteous opening matches the measured tone of the whole report, which states facts before asking for a ruling. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: They report they went into Judea to the house of the great God, built with great stones, timber laid in the walls, the work going fast on and prospering. JFB notes even opponents testify the building is solid and the work vigorous. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: They asked the elders who commanded them to build this house and make up these walls. The question is repeated in the report exactly as asked, showing the inquiry was orderly. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: They asked their names also, to certify the king and write the names of the chief men. Henry notes the builders were not anonymous: they stood to their work openly, ready to answer for it. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: The elders answered: 'We are the servants of the God of heaven and earth, and build the house that was built many years ago, which a great king of Israel built and set up.' JFB notes the answer claims a title older than any Persian decree: they rebuild what Solomon built for the God of heaven. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: But after their fathers provoked the God of heaven to wrath, he gave them into Nebuchadnezzar's hand, who destroyed this house and carried the people to Babylon. Henry notes the frank confession: the builders do not hide that exile was judgment for sin. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: But in the first year of Cyrus king of Babylon, Cyrus made a decree to build this house of God. The elders rest their case on a royal decree, which any king's archive can verify. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: The gold and silver vessels Nebuchadnezzar took from Jerusalem's temple to Babylon, Cyrus took out and delivered to Sheshbazzar, whom he had made governor. JFB notes the vessels' return is evidence: the decree was acted on, not merely spoken. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: Cyrus said to him: 'Take these vessels, go, carry them into the temple at Jerusalem, and let the house of God be built in his place.' The charge ties vessels and building together: the house must be rebuilt because its vessels have a home to return to. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: Then Sheshbazzar came and laid the foundation of the house of God at Jerusalem, and since that time even until now it has been in building, and yet is not finished. Henry notes the long, interrupted history is stated plainly, without blaming any king for the delay. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: Now therefore, if it seem good to the king, let search be made in the king's treasure house at Babylon, whether Cyrus made such a decree, and let the king send his pleasure concerning this matter. JFB notes the fair proposal: the builders invite the very search that will vindicate them. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
