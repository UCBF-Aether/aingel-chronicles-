Study Notes — Ezra

Chapter 6

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Then Darius made a decree, and search was made in the house of the rolls where treasures were laid up in Babylon. Henry notes the very inquiry meant to stop the work becomes the means of finding its warrant. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: There was found at Achmetha, in the palace in Media, a roll with a record written. JFB notes the decree was not in Babylon's archive but in the Median summer palace, showing God's providence guiding the search to the right place. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: In Cyrus's first year, Cyrus decreed concerning God's house at Jerusalem: let the house be built where sacrifices were offered, its foundations strongly laid, sixty cubits high and sixty broad. The dimensions recall a substantial temple, not a mere shrine. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: With three rows of great stones and a row of new timber, and expenses given out of the king's house. JFB notes the mixed stone and timber construction follows Solomon's pattern, and the king himself pays. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: The gold and silver vessels Nebuchadnezzar took from Jerusalem's temple to Babylon must be restored to the temple at Jerusalem, each to its place. Henry notes restoration is complete only when vessels and house are together again. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: Now therefore Tatnai, governor beyond the river, Shethar-boznai and the Apharsachites beyond the river, be ye far from thence. The accusers are told to keep their distance, a reversal of their attempt to stand over the work. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: Let the work of this house of God alone; let the governor of the Jews and the elders build this house of God in its place. JFB notes Darius does not merely permit the building, he forbids interference with it. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: Darius further decrees what shall be done for the elders: from the king's goods, even the tribute beyond the river, expenses are to be given forthwith, that they be not hindered. Henry notes the adversaries' own tribute now funds the temple. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: Whatever they need, young bullocks, rams, lambs for burnt offerings, wheat, salt, wine and oil, according to the priests' appointment at Jerusalem, is to be given day by day without fail. The daily supply matches the daily sacrifice. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: That they may offer sacrifices of sweet savours to the God of heaven, and pray for the life of the king and of his sons. JFB notes the heathen king asks the prayers of Israel's priests, and the temple's worship is to bless even its patron. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: Whoever alters this word shall have timber pulled from his house, and being set up, be hanged thereon, and his house made a dunghill. Henry notes the grim penalty guards the decree: the king's own house becomes the warning against tampering. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: And the God who caused his name to dwell there destroy all kings and people who put their hand to alter and destroy this house at Jerusalem. Darius invokes Israel's God against future destroyers, and adds, 'let it be done with speed.' — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: Then Tatnai, Shethar-boznai and their companions did speedily according to what Darius had sent. JFB notes the same officials who questioned the work now carry out its funding with the same diligence they once used to stop it. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: The elders built and prospered through the prophesying of Haggai and Zechariah, and finished according to the commandment of the God of Israel and of Cyrus, Darius and Artaxerxes. Henry notes three authorities converge: God's command, the prophets' word, and three kings' decrees. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: This house was finished on the third day of Adar, in the sixth year of Darius. JFB notes the date closes a work begun under Cyrus, halted for years, and finished by prophecy and providence together. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: The children of Israel, priests, Levites and the rest of the returned exiles kept the dedication of this house of God with joy. Henry notes joy is the proper dedication mood: the house is received as a gift, not merely completed as a task. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: At the dedication they offered a hundred bullocks, two hundred rams, four hundred lambs, and for a sin offering for all Israel twelve he-goats, according to the tribes. JFB notes the twelve goats confess the whole nation's sin, though only a remnant returned. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

18 COMMENTARY: They set the priests in their divisions and Levites in their courses for God's service at Jerusalem, as written in the book of Moses. The temple is not only built but staffed, in Moses' order, so worship can continue after the feast. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

19 COMMENTARY: The returned exiles kept the passover on the fourteenth day of the first month. Henry notes the first passover in the new temple rehearses the Exodus: a delivered people eat the feast of deliverance in their own house again. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

20 COMMENTARY: Priests and Levites were purified together, all pure, and killed the passover for all the exiles, for their brethren the priests, and for themselves. JFB notes the Levites serve the whole congregation, including priests, in one common purification. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

21 COMMENTARY: The Israelites who had come again out of captivity ate, and all such as had separated themselves from the filthiness of the heathen of the land to seek the LORD God of Israel. Henry notes the feast is open to converts who separate from idolatry, not only to blood descendants. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

22 COMMENTARY: They kept unleavened bread seven days with joy, for the LORD had made them joyful and turned the heart of the king of Assyria to them, to strengthen their hands. JFB notes 'king of Assyria' is Darius as lord of the old Assyrian lands, whose changed heart is itself God's work. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
