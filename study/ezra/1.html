Study Notes — Ezra

Chapter 1

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from public-domain commentators, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Cyrus acts in his very first year, so that Jeremiah's word about seventy years is fulfilled at once. Henry stresses that God stirred a heathen king's spirit, showing that even empires serve God's purposes for his people. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: Cyrus confesses that the God of heaven gave him all the kingdoms of the earth and charged him to build a house at Jerusalem. JFB notes this language shows God using a conqueror who at least acknowledged Israel's God as supreme. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: The proclamation is an open invitation, not a forced march: whoever is of God's people may go up. Henry reads the added 'his God be with him' as both permission and blessing on the return. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: Those who stayed behind were to help the returners with silver, gold, goods and beasts, besides freewill offerings. JFB treats this as neighbours bearing the cost of worship, as Egypt once enriched Israel at the Exodus. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: The heads of Judah and Benjamin, the priests and Levites rose up, with all whose spirit God had raised. Henry notes the double movement: a royal decree without, and God's stirring within, without which no one would have left Babylon. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: Neighbours strengthened their hands with silver, gold, goods, beasts and precious things. The gifts match the needs of a journey and a building, and Henry sees God's providence supplying both. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: Cyrus brought out the temple vessels Nebuchadnezzar had carried off and lodged in the house of his gods. Their return reverses the plunder of Jerusalem and restores what idolatry had seized. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: The vessels were handed over by Mithredath the treasurer and counted out to Sheshbazzar, prince of Judah. JFB identifies Sheshbazzar with Zerubbabel, the hereditary prince recognised among the exiles. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: The inventory begins with thirty gold chargers, a thousand silver chargers and twenty-nine knives. JFB notes these are the larger vessels only, the detailed count proving the restoration was careful, not careless. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: Thirty gold basins, four hundred and ten silver basins of a second sort, and a thousand other vessels complete the list. Each class is counted, so that nothing sacred is lost in the transfer. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: All the gold and silver vessels numbered five thousand and four hundred, and Sheshbazzar brought them all up from Babylon to Jerusalem. JFB explains the gross sum exceeds the itemised list because small vessels are folded into the total. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
