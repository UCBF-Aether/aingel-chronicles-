Study Notes — Genesis

Chapter 19

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown, John Gill and Adam Clarke, all public-domain works, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Two angels came to Sodom in the evening while Lot sat in the gate, and Lot rose, bowed, and welcomed them, showing the same hospitality his uncle had shown at Mamre. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: Lot urged them to turn into his house, wash their feet, and stay the night, insisting because he knew the danger of strangers spending a night in Sodom's streets. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: He pressed them until they accepted, then made them a feast with unleavened bread, a hurried but generous welcome in a hostile city. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: Before they lay down, the men of Sodom, young and old from every quarter, surrounded the house, showing that the city's wickedness was general, not confined to a few. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: The mob demanded Lot bring out his guests for shameful abuse, exposing the depth of Sodom's sin and the peril in which Lot's hospitality had placed his guests. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: Lot went out to them and shut the door behind him, trying to stand between the mob and his guests at great risk to himself. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: He begged them not to act wickedly, appealing to conscience in a city where conscience had long been silenced. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: In a desperate and wrongful bargain, Lot offered his two daughters to the mob to protect his guests, a sinful proposal Henry condemns even while noting Lot's loyalty to the laws of hospitality. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: The mob scorned Lot as an outsider who dared to judge them, and threatened to treat him worse than his guests, showing how a lone righteous voice is hated in a corrupt city. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: They pressed hard against Lot and nearly broke the door, but the angels pulled him inside and shut it, rescuing the man who had tried to rescue them. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: The angels struck the mob with blindness, so that they wearied themselves groping for the door, a just picture of sin blinding men to the very way they seek. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: The angels then asked Lot who else of his family was in the city, sons-in-law, sons, daughters, and kin, so that all who belonged to him could be brought out before judgment. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: They told Lot they would destroy the place because its outcry had grown great before God, and God had sent them to carry out the sentence. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: Lot warned his sons-in-law to leave, but they thought he was joking, a warning that the world's laughter can deafen men to the last call to escape. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: At dawn the angels urged Lot to hurry with his wife and two daughters, lest they be swept away in the city's punishment. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: Lot lingered, and the angels seized his hand and his family's hands and set them outside the city, because God was merciful to him even when his own feet were slow. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: Outside, they were told to escape for their lives, not to look back, and not to stay in the plain, but to flee to the mountains. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

18 COMMENTARY: Lot answered, not so, my lord, pleading humbly with the messengers rather than simply obeying the command to flee to the mountains. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

19 COMMENTARY: Lot said God's mercy had been great in saving his life, yet he feared he could not escape to the mountain before disaster overtook him, mixing thankfulness with fear. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

20 COMMENTARY: He pointed to a small city nearby where he could flee and his soul could live, asking that it be spared for his sake. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

21 COMMENTARY: The angel granted Lot's request and said he would not overthrow the little city Lot had named, but Lot must hurry there, since judgment waited on his escape. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

22 COMMENTARY: The angel said he could do nothing until Lot reached safety, and the city was therefore called Zoar, little, after Lot's own plea. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

23 COMMENTARY: The sun had risen when Lot entered Zoar, a race against dawn in which his life hung on haste. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

24 COMMENTARY: Then God rained brimstone and fire on Sodom and Gomorrah from heaven, a judgment from above that matched the cities' sins in suddenness and completeness. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

25 COMMENTARY: He overthrew those cities, the whole plain, all their inhabitants, and all that grew on the ground, leaving nothing of the fair land Lot had chosen. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

26 COMMENTARY: Lot's wife looked back, against the plain command, and became a pillar of salt, a lasting warning that a heart still turned toward a condemned place cannot escape with the body. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

27 COMMENTARY: Abraham rose early and went to the place where he had stood before God, to learn the outcome of his intercession. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

28 COMMENTARY: He looked toward Sodom and Gomorrah and saw smoke rising from the whole plain like the smoke of a furnace, where cities had stood. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

29 COMMENTARY: God remembered Abraham when he destroyed the cities of the plain, and sent Lot out of the overthrow, so Lot's rescue is credited to Abraham's pleading as well as to God's mercy. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

30 COMMENTARY: Lot, afraid to stay in Zoar, moved up to the mountains he had first refused, and lived in a cave with his two daughters, a poor end for a man who had once chosen the richest plain. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

31 COMMENTARY: The elder daughter said their father was old and there was no man left in the land to come to them, and proposed a plan to preserve offspring through him. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

32 COMMENTARY: She proposed making their father drink wine and lying with him, so that they might preserve seed of their father, a scheme born of isolation and despair. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

33 COMMENTARY: They made Lot drink wine that night, and the elder lay with him, while he did not know when she lay down or rose up, a shameful act the narrative records without approval. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

34 COMMENTARY: The next day the elder told the younger what had been done and urged her to do the same that night, so the sin was repeated deliberately, not as a single night's lapse. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

35 COMMENTARY: They made Lot drink wine again that night, and the younger lay with him, and again he did not know when she lay down or rose up. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

36 COMMENTARY: Thus both of Lot's daughters conceived by their father, and the record leaves his drunken unawareness as no excuse for the household's disgrace. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

37 COMMENTARY: The elder daughter bore a son and called him Moab, father of the Moabites, a people who would trouble Israel for generations. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

38 COMMENTARY: The younger daughter bore a son and called him Ben-ammi, father of the Ammonites, so both neighbouring nations are traced back to Lot's ruin, a sober end to his choice of Sodom. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
