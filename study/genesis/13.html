Study Notes — Genesis

Chapter 13

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown, John Gill and Adam Clarke, all public-domain works, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Abram returned from Egypt to the south of Canaan, rich in livestock, silver, and gold, but his wealth was now held by a man humbled by his Egyptian failure and restored to the land of promise. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: Abram's great wealth in cattle and precious metals is noted because it soon becomes the occasion of strife, showing that prosperity itself can test a household. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: Abram journeyed back stage by stage to Bethel, retracing his steps to the place where he had first worshipped in Canaan, as a man returning to his first faithfulness. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: At Bethel Abram returned to the altar he had built at first and called on God's name again, a picture of a believer going back to the worship he had left behind in Egypt. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: Lot also had flocks, herds, and tents, so the two households had both grown large, and their shared prosperity, not poverty, is what made living together difficult. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: The land could not support both households together with their herds, so what had been a blessing of increase became a practical problem that had to be faced honestly. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: Quarrels broke out between Abram's herdsmen and Lot's herdsmen, and the Canaanites and Perizzites living nearby could see it, which made the strife a poor witness for Abram's household. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: Abram took the lead in making peace, reminding Lot that they were kinsmen and that strife between brothers was shameful, especially before watching neighbours. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: Abram offered Lot the first choice of land, yielding his own right as elder and host. Henry praises this as the peacemaker's wisdom: giving way in lesser matters to keep love intact. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: Lot looked out over the well-watered plain of Jordan, green like a garden, and chose by sight, judging the land by its present richness rather than by its people or its future. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: Lot chose the whole Jordan plain for himself and moved east, so the two kinsmen parted, with Abram left in Canaan and Lot heading toward the cities of the plain. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: Abram stayed in Canaan while Lot pitched his tent toward Sodom, a first step of nearness to a wicked city that would later cost him dearly. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: The men of Sodom are described as exceedingly wicked, a warning note set beside Lot's choice: the fairest land can hold the worst neighbours. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: After Lot's departure, God spoke to Abram again, as if to show that losing Lot's company was no loss to the promise, which rested on God alone. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: Abram was told to look in every direction, because the whole land he could see was given to him and his offspring, a promise far wider than the portion Lot had chosen. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: His offspring would be as countless as dust, a promise that stretched Abram's faith, since he was still childless, and pointed to a multitude no man could number. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: Abram was told to walk through the land in its length and breadth, taking possession by faith and survey, since God had given it to him even before his feet owned it. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

18 COMMENTARY: Abram moved his tent to the oaks of Mamre near Hebron and built an altar there, settling into the promised land in worship and in peace after the quarrel was ended. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
