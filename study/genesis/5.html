Study Notes — Genesis

Chapter 5

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown, John Gill and Adam Clarke, all public-domain works, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: This is the book of the generations of Adam, tracing the line through Seth down to Noah in ten generations. Gill stresses only the direct line is given, not every branch, so the promised line stays traceable. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown; John Gill

2 COMMENTARY: God created humanity male and female, blessed them and called their name Adam on the day they were created. Gill notes the shared name marks their union: one man and one woman joined as man and wife. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown; John Gill

3 COMMENTARY: Adam lived 130 years and fathered a son in his own likeness, after his image, named Seth. Gill stresses the contrast: Seth bears Adam's fallen likeness, not the unfallen image of God in which Adam was first created. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown; John Gill

4 COMMENTARY: After Seth, Adam lived 800 years and fathered other sons and daughters. The long life shows the early world filling through large families, not through Seth alone. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown; John Gill

5 COMMENTARY: All Adam's days were 930 years, and he died. Gill notes these are solar years, and the verse closes the first life with the sentence from Eden fulfilled: death came to Adam too. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown; John Gill

6 COMMENTARY: Seth lived 105 years and fathered Enos. The next link in the godly line is named at the age when fatherhood begins the next generation. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown; John Gill

9 COMMENTARY: Enos lived 90 years and fathered Cainan. The chain continues without break, each patriarch named as the father of the next in the line to Noah. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown; John Gill

12 COMMENTARY: Cainan lived 70 years and fathered Mahalaleel. The genealogy moves steadily forward, preserving the line through which worship and promise would continue. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown; John Gill

15 COMMENTARY: Mahalaleel lived 65 years and fathered Jared. Again the verse marks the handover from one generation to the next in the Seth line. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown; John Gill

18 COMMENTARY: Jared lived 162 years and fathered Enoch. Jared's son is the Enoch whose walk with God will break the bare pattern of this chapter. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown; John Gill

21 COMMENTARY: Enoch lived 65 years and fathered Methuselah. Enoch's fatherhood begins the generation that will live longest in this list. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown; John Gill

22 COMMENTARY: Enoch walked with God after fathering Methuselah for 300 years, and fathered other sons and daughters. Henry and JFB stress walking with God means a life of steady communion and obedience, not a single act. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown; John Gill

23 COMMENTARY: All Enoch's days were 365 years. His life is notably shorter than his neighbours', which makes his end, not his age, the point of the story. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown; John Gill

24 COMMENTARY: Enoch walked with God, and he was not, for God took him. JFB and Henry read this as translation: Enoch did not die as the others did, but was taken by God as a reward for his walk and a sign of life beyond death. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown; John Gill

25 COMMENTARY: Methuselah lived 187 years and fathered Lamech. Methuselah, the oldest man in the list, becomes the father of the next link toward Noah. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown; John Gill

28 COMMENTARY: Lamech lived 182 years and fathered a son. Lamech's son will be Noah, so this verse opens the last generation before the flood. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown; John Gill

29 COMMENTARY: Lamech named him Noah, saying this one shall comfort us concerning our work and the toil of our hands because of the ground God cursed. The name is a prayer for relief from the curse of Genesis 3, not yet a prediction of the flood. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown; John Gill

32 COMMENTARY: Noah was 500 years old and fathered Shem, Ham and Japheth. Gill notes they were not triplets: Shem is named first for dignity and for the line of promise, though Japheth appears to have been eldest. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown; John Gill

No note for verse(s) 7, 8, 10, 11, 13, 14, 16, 17, 19, 20, 26, 27, 30, 31 — the public-domain sources consulted give no distinct explanation for these verses beyond the list itself, so nothing was invented for them.
