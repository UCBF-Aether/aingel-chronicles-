Study Notes — Genesis

Chapter 7

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown, John Gill and Adam Clarke, all public-domain works, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: God told Noah to come into the ark, with all his house, for Noah alone had God seen righteous in this generation. Righteousness here is the faithfulness already described in chapter 6, not a claim of sinlessness. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: Of every clean beast Noah was to take seven pairs, male and female, and of unclean beasts one pair. The larger number of clean animals anticipates sacrifice after the flood, and provision for worship as well as survival. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: Of birds also seven pairs, to keep seed alive on the earth. The point is continuity: life is preserved in kinds, so the world after the flood can be repopulated. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: In seven days God would send rain for forty days and forty nights and destroy every living substance he had made from the earth. The seven days are a final warning period, and the forty days mark a complete, deliberate judgment. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: Noah did according to all God commanded him. As in chapter 6, obedience is total and unhurried: he does what he is told, not a selection of it. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: Noah was 600 years old when the flood of waters came on the earth. The age anchors the flood in Noah's lifetime and in the genealogy, not in vague mythic time. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: Noah, his sons, his wife and his sons' wives entered the ark because of the flood waters. The household enters together: salvation here is a family preserved by one man's faithfulness. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: Of clean and unclean beasts, birds and creeping things, pairs came to Noah. The animals arrive as God directed, reinforcing that this rescue is God's ordering, not Noah's roundup alone. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: They entered two by two, male and female, as God commanded Noah. The pairing preserves reproduction after the flood, and the repetition stresses obedience to the pattern given. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: After seven days, the waters of the flood came on the earth. The delay ends exactly as announced, showing the warning period was real and the judgment was not hasty. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: In Noah's 600th year, on the seventeenth day of the second month, the fountains of the great deep broke up and the windows of heaven opened. JFB and Henry read this as water from below and above at once: a total undoing of the ordered waters of creation. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: Rain fell on the earth forty days and forty nights. The steady duration matters: this is not a brief storm but a sustained judgment. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: On that same day Noah, Shem, Ham and Japheth, Noah's wife and his sons' wives entered the ark. The verse repeats the entry to fix the date: the household was inside before the waters rose. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: With them entered every beast, cattle, creeping thing and bird after its kind. The ark's company is complete by kinds, matching the command of chapter 6. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: They came to Noah into the ark, two by two, of all flesh with the breath of life. Life itself, in all its kinds, is carried through judgment inside the one vessel God appointed. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: Those that entered, male and female of all flesh, went in as God commanded, and the LORD shut him in. Henry stresses the shutting: God himself seals the ark, so safety depends on God, not on Noah's latch. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: The flood lasted forty days, the waters increased and bore up the ark, and it was lifted above the earth. The vessel built to float, not sail, now does exactly its work. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

18 COMMENTARY: The waters prevailed and increased greatly, and the ark moved on the face of the waters. The ark rides above a world being unmade, a picture of preservation through judgment. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

19 COMMENTARY: The waters prevailed exceedingly, and all the high hills under heaven were covered. The language stresses totality: no high ground offered escape, so deliverance could only be in the ark. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

20 COMMENTARY: The waters rose fifteen cubits upward, and the mountains were covered. The measurement makes the point concrete: even the highest landmarks disappeared under water. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

21 COMMENTARY: All flesh died that moved on the earth: birds, cattle, beasts, creeping things and every man. The judgment announced in chapter 6 falls exactly on the order it named. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

22 COMMENTARY: All in whose nostrils was the breath of life, of all that was on dry land, died. Land life perishes; only what was in the ark, and water life not named here, remains. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

23 COMMENTARY: Every living substance on the earth was destroyed, man and beast and creeping thing and fowl, and only Noah and those with him in the ark remained alive. The verse sums the flood as total judgment with a single exception: the household God preserved. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

24 COMMENTARY: The waters prevailed on the earth 150 days. The long prevailing shows the flood was not a momentary event but a sustained state, before any abating begins. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
