Study Notes — Genesis

Chapter 26

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown, John Gill and Adam Clarke, all public-domain works, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Another famine came, besides the one in Abraham's days, and Isaac went to Abimelech king of the Philistines at Gerar, following his father's pattern of seeking refuge. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: The Lord appeared to Isaac and told him not to go down to Egypt, but to live in the land God would show him. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: God told Isaac to sojourn in that land, promising to be with him and bless him, and to give all these countries to him and his seed, performing the oath sworn to Abraham. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: God promised to multiply Isaac's seed as the stars of heaven, give them all these lands, and bless all nations in his seed. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: This was because Abraham had obeyed God's voice and kept his charge, commandments, statutes and laws, so Isaac inherited a covenant already proved by his father's obedience. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: Isaac therefore dwelt in Gerar, obeying the command to stay rather than fleeing to Egypt. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: When the men of Gerar asked about his wife, Isaac said she was his sister, fearing they would kill him for Rebekah because she was beautiful, repeating his father's old failure. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: After a long time there, Abimelech looked out of a window and saw Isaac showing affection to Rebekah, which exposed the pretence. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: Abimelech called Isaac and charged him plainly that Rebekah was certainly his wife, asking how he could have called her his sister, and Isaac admitted he had feared for his life. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: Abimelech warned that one of the people might easily have taken Rebekah, and Isaac would have brought guilt on the whole people, showing how a private lie endangered others. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: Abimelech therefore charged all his people that whoever touched Isaac or Rebekah would surely be put to death, giving them royal protection. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: Isaac sowed in that land and reaped a hundredfold in the same year, and the Lord blessed him, prosperity coming even in a famine land. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: Isaac grew steadily greater until he became very great, his wealth increasing step by step under God's blessing. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: He owned flocks, herds and many servants, and the Philistines envied him, as prosperity often breeds envy in neighbours. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: The Philistines stopped up all the wells Abraham's servants had dug, a spiteful stratagem, still practised in the East, to annoy a rival by choking his water supply. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: Abimelech told Isaac to leave, because he had become much mightier than they were, envy turning into expulsion. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: Isaac departed and encamped in the valley of Gerar and dwelt there, yielding ground rather than fighting for it. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

18 COMMENTARY: Isaac reopened the wells dug in Abraham's days that the Philistines had stopped, and called them by the same names his father had given them, restoring what envy had destroyed. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

19 COMMENTARY: Isaac's servants dug in the valley and found a well of flowing water, a fresh spring in a land where water meant life. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

20 COMMENTARY: The herdsmen of Gerar quarrelled over it, claiming the water was theirs, so Isaac called the well Esek, contention, because they contended with him. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

21 COMMENTARY: They dug another well and quarrelled over that too, so he called it Sitnah, enmity, a second dispute over the same necessity. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

22 COMMENTARY: He moved on and dug another well, over which they did not quarrel, so he called it Rehoboth, broad places, saying now the Lord had made room for them and they would be fruitful in the land. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

23 COMMENTARY: From there Isaac went up to Beersheba, returning to the place associated with Abraham's covenant and worship. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

24 COMMENTARY: The Lord appeared to him that night and said he was the God of Abraham, telling Isaac not to fear, for God was with him and would bless and multiply his seed for Abraham's sake. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

25 COMMENTARY: Isaac built an altar there, called on the name of the Lord, pitched his tent, and his servants dug a well, settling worship, home and water together. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

26 COMMENTARY: Abimelech came to him from Gerar with Ahuzzath his friend and Phichol his army commander, seeking Isaac after having driven him away. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

27 COMMENTARY: Isaac asked why they had come, since they hated him and had sent him away, showing honest surprise at their approach. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

28 COMMENTARY: They answered that they had plainly seen the Lord was with Isaac, and proposed a covenant between them, with an oath on both sides. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

29 COMMENTARY: They asked that Isaac do them no harm, as they had not touched him and had sent him away in peace, and they called him now the blessed of the Lord. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

30 COMMENTARY: Isaac made them a feast, and they ate and drank, sealing reconciliation with a shared meal. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

31 COMMENTARY: In the morning they rose and swore to one another, and Isaac sent them away in peace, the covenant confirmed and the parting friendly. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

32 COMMENTARY: That same day Isaac's servants came and told him about the well they had dug, saying they had found water, providence crowning the day of peace. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

33 COMMENTARY: He called the well Shibah, oath, so the city was named Beersheba, well of the oath, to this day. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

34 COMMENTARY: Esau at forty took two Hittite wives, Judith daughter of Beeri and Basemath daughter of Elon, marriages outside the covenant family. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

35 COMMENTARY: These wives were a grief of mind to Isaac and Rebekah, because, as Henry and JFB note, they were idolatrous Canaanites taken without parental counsel, wounding the household's faith and peace. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
