Study Notes — Genesis

Chapter 17

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown, John Gill and Adam Clarke, all public-domain works, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: At ninety-nine, God appeared to Abram as God Almighty, the all-sufficient God who pours out blessings, and commanded him to walk before him and be blameless, living always under God's eye. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown; Adam Clarke

2 COMMENTARY: God promised to make his covenant with Abram and multiply him exceedingly, renewing an old promise rather than making a new one, now that Abram was old and childless by Sarai. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: Abram fell on his face in reverence, the posture of a man overwhelmed by God's nearness, and God continued to speak to him in that humble position. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: The covenant was stated plainly: Abram would become the father of many nations, so the promise was never meant for one family alone. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: His name was changed from Abram to Abraham, father of a multitude, so that every time his name was spoken it would preach the promise back to him. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: God promised to make him exceedingly fruitful, with nations and kings descending from him, a greatness measured in peoples, not in wealth alone. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: The covenant was everlasting, for Abraham and his offspring after him, with God himself as their God, which is the greatest part of the promise. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: God gave the whole land of Canaan, where Abraham now lived as a stranger, as an everlasting possession, holding the present pilgrimage and the future inheritance together. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: Abraham and his offspring were to keep the covenant in their turn, so the promise carried a duty with it and was not a bare gift without response. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: Every male child was to be circumcised, a sign cut into the flesh that marked the whole household as belonging to God's covenant. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown; Adam Clarke

11 COMMENTARY: Circumcision was the token of the covenant between God and Abraham, a sign and seal of spiritual blessings promised, not the blessings themselves, as Clarke stresses. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown; Adam Clarke

12 COMMENTARY: The rite was to be done at eight days old, because before that a child was counted unclean, and Clarke notes circumcision was understood as consecrating the person to God. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown; Adam Clarke

13 COMMENTARY: The sign applied to those born in the house and those bought with money alike, so the whole household, free and servant, stood inside the covenant. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: An uncircumcised male would be cut off from his people for breaking the covenant, a severe warning that the sign was not optional for those claiming the promise. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: Sarai's name was changed to Sarah, princess, matching Abraham's new name and showing that the promise belonged to her as well, not to Abraham alone. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: God blessed Sarah and promised a son from her, with nations and kings of peoples coming from her, so the long-barren wife became the mother of the covenant line. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: Abraham fell on his face and laughed, asking whether a child could be born to a man of a hundred and a woman of ninety. Clarke and Henry read this as joyful wonder at an impossible thing, not scornful unbelief. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown; Adam Clarke

18 COMMENTARY: Abraham pleaded that Ishmael might live before God, a father's natural wish that the son he already loved would not be set aside. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

19 COMMENTARY: God answered that Sarah would bear Isaac, and the covenant would be established with him and his offspring, naming the child before he was conceived. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

20 COMMENTARY: Ishmael was not forgotten: God heard Abraham for him, promising blessing, fruitfulness, twelve princes, and a great nation, even though the covenant would run through Isaac. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

21 COMMENTARY: The distinction was kept clear: the covenant itself would be established with Isaac, born to Sarah at the set time in the next year. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

22 COMMENTARY: When God finished speaking, he went up from Abraham, leaving the old man with a promise that now had a name, a time, and a sign. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

23 COMMENTARY: That very same day Abraham circumcised Ishmael and every male in his household, and Clarke notes that such a painful rite could only have been borne by men fully convinced it was God's command. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown; Adam Clarke

24 COMMENTARY: Abraham himself was circumcised at ninety-nine, showing that the head of the household did not exempt himself from the sign he imposed on others. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

25 COMMENTARY: Ishmael was thirteen when circumcised, old enough to understand and consent, which underlines that his household's obedience was willing, not forced. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

26 COMMENTARY: Abraham and Ishmael were circumcised on the same day, father and son together entering the covenant sign without delay. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

27 COMMENTARY: Every man of the house, born at home or bought from a foreigner, was circumcised with him, so the whole household was marked as God's people in one day's obedience. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
