Study Notes — Genesis

Chapter 24

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown, John Gill and Adam Clarke, all public-domain works, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Abraham was old and well advanced in years, and the Lord had blessed him in all things, so he could now turn with a settled mind to his son's marriage. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: Abraham charged his eldest servant, who managed all he had, to put his hand under Abraham's thigh and swear, using the solemn oath-form of the day. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: The servant was to swear he would not take a wife for Isaac from the Canaanites among whom they lived, guarding the covenant family from a faithless match. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: Instead he must go to Abraham's own country and kindred and take a wife for Isaac from there, keeping the marriage within the family that knew God's call. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: The servant asked what to do if the woman would not follow him to Canaan, and whether he should then bring Isaac back, a practical difficulty Abraham had to settle in advance. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: Abraham warned him on no account to bring Isaac back there, since God had called Abraham out of that land and the promise pointed forward, not backward. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: Abraham trusted that the God who had called him and sworn to give Canaan to his seed would send his angel ahead of the servant to prosper the errand. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: If the woman would not come, the servant would be free from the oath, but Isaac must still not be taken back, so the condition protected both the mission and the promise. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: The servant put his hand under Abraham's thigh and swore concerning the matter, accepting the charge as a sacred trust. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: The servant took ten camels laden with his master's goods and set out for Mesopotamia, to the city of Nahor, equipped to show that Isaac was a worthy match. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: He made the camels kneel by a well outside the city in the evening, when women came out to draw water, the natural meeting place for such an errand. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: The servant prayed that God would show kindness to Abraham and give him success, asking for a clear sign by the well. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: He proposed that the young woman who, when asked for a drink, offered water for his camels too, would be the one God had appointed for Isaac. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: That sign was meant to reveal a woman who was simple, industrious, humble, cheerful and hospitable, qualities fitting a wife for the promised heir. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: Before he had finished praying, Rebekah came out with her jar on her shoulder, the daughter of Bethuel son of Milcah, Nahor's wife, and therefore of Abraham's kindred. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: Rebekah was very beautiful and unmarried, and she went down to the well, filled her jar and came up, exactly the sort of person the prayer had described. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: The servant ran to meet her and asked for a little water from her jar, testing the sign he had prayed for. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

18 COMMENTARY: She readily gave him a drink, answering promptly and kindly, the first half of the sign fulfilled. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

19 COMMENTARY: She then offered to draw water for his camels as well, until they had finished drinking, a labour of real generosity that completed the sign. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

20 COMMENTARY: She hurried, emptied her jar into the trough and ran again to the well to draw for all his camels, showing industry without complaint. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

21 COMMENTARY: The servant watched her in silence, wondering whether the Lord had indeed made his journey successful. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

22 COMMENTARY: When the camels had finished drinking, he gave her a gold nose ring and two gold bracelets, gifts that honoured her and signalled a serious proposal to follow. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

23 COMMENTARY: He asked whose daughter she was and whether there was room in her father's house for his party to lodge, moving from gratitude to practical inquiry. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

24 COMMENTARY: She answered that she was the daughter of Bethuel son of Milcah, whom Milcah bore to Nahor, confirming she was of Abraham's kindred. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

25 COMMENTARY: She added that there was straw and fodder in plenty and room to lodge, a hospitable welcome that matched her kindness at the well. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

26 COMMENTARY: The servant bowed his head and worshipped the Lord, recognising God's hand in an answer that came even before his prayer had ended. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

27 COMMENTARY: He blessed the Lord for leading him by the right way to his master's relatives, giving God the credit for guidance he had only asked for moments before. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

28 COMMENTARY: Rebekah ran home and told her mother's household what had happened, bringing the news to her family without delay. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

29 COMMENTARY: Her brother Laban, hearing of the gifts and the stranger, ran out to the man at the well, eager to meet him. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

30 COMMENTARY: Laban had seen the ring and bracelets and heard Rebekah's account, so he went to invite the servant in, showing the family's welcome. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

31 COMMENTARY: Laban welcomed him as blessed of the Lord and urged him to come in, asking why he stood outside when house and camel room were ready. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

32 COMMENTARY: The servant came in, his camels were unloaded and given straw and fodder, and water was provided for washing the feet of him and his men, the full courtesy of a host. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

33 COMMENTARY: When food was set before him, the servant refused to eat until he had told his errand, putting his master's business before his own comfort. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

34 COMMENTARY: He introduced himself plainly as Abraham's servant, so his hearers would know whose interests he represented. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

35 COMMENTARY: He described Abraham's great wealth in flocks, herds, silver, gold, servants and camels, showing that Isaac stood to inherit a substantial household. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

36 COMMENTARY: He told how Sarah, in her old age, had borne Isaac, and how Abraham had given all he had to this son, making Isaac the clear heir. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

37 COMMENTARY: He repeated Abraham's charge and oath, that Isaac's wife must not come from the Canaanites but from Abraham's kindred. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

38 COMMENTARY: He repeated the instruction to go to his father's house and kindred and take a wife for Isaac from there. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

39 COMMENTARY: He repeated his own question about what to do if the woman would not follow, showing his hearers that he had raised the difficulty honestly. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

40 COMMENTARY: He repeated Abraham's answer, that God's angel would go before him and prosper his way, so he could take a wife for Isaac from Abraham's kindred. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

41 COMMENTARY: He repeated the condition that he would be free from the oath if her kindred refused her, but not if he failed to make the journey and ask. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

42 COMMENTARY: He told how he had come to the well that day and prayed for success, asking God to show him the appointed woman by her kindness. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

43 COMMENTARY: He repeated the sign he had prayed for, that the young woman who gave him drink and watered his camels would be the one. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

44 COMMENTARY: He told how Rebekah had done exactly that, saying she would draw for his camels also, and how he knew then that she was the Lord's choice. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

45 COMMENTARY: He added that before he had finished praying in his heart, Rebekah came with her jar, went down to the well and drew water. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

46 COMMENTARY: He repeated her words offering drink and water for the camels, and how he had drunk while she drew for them. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

47 COMMENTARY: He told how he had asked whose daughter she was, and how she had answered that she was Bethuel's daughter, confirming the kindred. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

48 COMMENTARY: He told how he had bowed and blessed the Lord for leading him to his master's brother's house, a retelling that let the family see God's hand as he had seen it. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

49 COMMENTARY: He then asked plainly whether they would show kindness and truth to his master, and if not, to tell him so he could turn elsewhere, pressing for a decision. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

50 COMMENTARY: Laban and Bethuel answered together that the matter had come from the Lord, and they could not speak against it either way. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

51 COMMENTARY: They offered Rebekah herself, saying she should go and become Isaac's wife, as the Lord had spoken. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

52 COMMENTARY: Hearing their consent, the servant bowed to the ground and worshipped the Lord, again giving thanks before taking any further step. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

53 COMMENTARY: He brought out jewels of silver and gold and clothing for Rebekah, and gave precious things to her brother and mother, sealing the agreement with honourable gifts. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

54 COMMENTARY: Then he and his men ate and drank and stayed the night, and in the morning he asked to be sent on his way back to his master without delay. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

55 COMMENTARY: Rebekah's brother and mother asked that she stay at least ten days before departing, a natural wish to delay a sudden parting. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

56 COMMENTARY: The servant asked them not to delay him, since the Lord had prospered his way, and he wanted to return to his master at once. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

57 COMMENTARY: They proposed to call Rebekah herself and ask her decision, giving the young woman a voice in a marriage that would take her far from home. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

58 COMMENTARY: Rebekah answered simply that she would go, consenting freely to leave her family for a husband she had not yet met, trusting the providence she had seen. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

59 COMMENTARY: They sent Rebekah away with her nurse and Abraham's servant and his men, entrusting her to the journey. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

60 COMMENTARY: Her family blessed her, wishing her to become the mother of thousands of ten thousands and that her seed would possess the gate of their enemies. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

61 COMMENTARY: Rebekah and her maids mounted camels and followed the servant, setting out on the long journey to Canaan. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

62 COMMENTARY: Isaac, who lived in the south country, came from the way of the well Lahai-roi, returning toward home as the party approached. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

63 COMMENTARY: Isaac went out to meditate in the field at evening, and lifting his eyes, he saw the camels coming, a quiet meeting prepared by providence. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

64 COMMENTARY: Rebekah also lifted her eyes and saw Isaac, and she got down from her camel in respectful greeting. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

65 COMMENTARY: She asked the servant who the man walking in the field to meet them was, and when told it was Isaac, she covered herself with a veil, as a bride meeting her husband. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

66 COMMENTARY: The servant told Isaac all the things he had done, giving his master a full account of the journey and its success. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

67 COMMENTARY: Isaac brought Rebekah into his mother Sarah's tent and took her as his wife, and he loved her, and he was comforted after his mother's death, as one generation's loss was met by the next generation's gift. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
