Study Notes — Genesis

Chapter 8

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown, John Gill and Adam Clarke, all public-domain works, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: God remembered Noah and all the living things with him in the ark, and sent a wind over the earth, and the waters subsided. Remembered does not mean God had forgotten, but that he now acted on his covenant promise to preserve them. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: The fountains of the deep and the windows of heaven were stopped, and the rain was restrained. The sources of judgment are closed in reverse order: what was opened in chapter 7 is now shut. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: The waters returned from off the earth continually, and after 150 days they were abated. The slow receding matches the slow prevailing: restoration, like judgment, takes time. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: In the seventh month, on the seventeenth day, the ark rested on the mountains of Ararat. Resting on high ground is the first sign that the world can be inhabited again, though the household does not yet leave. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: The waters decreased continually until the tenth month, when the tops of the mountains were seen. The appearing of mountain tops marks steady progress, but the land is still far from dry. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: After forty days Noah opened the window of the ark he had made. Noah tests conditions before acting, rather than rushing out at the first sign of land. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: He sent out a raven, which went forth to and fro until the waters dried from the earth. The raven, able to feed on carrion and rest elsewhere, does not return with news, so it proves little about dry land. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: He sent out a dove to see if the waters had abated from the ground. The dove, a cleaner bird that needs dry resting ground, is a better test of habitability. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: The dove found no rest for her foot and returned to the ark, for waters were still on the whole earth, so Noah took her in. The return is itself information: the earth is not yet ready. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: He waited another seven days and sent the dove out again. Noah's patience is deliberate: he tests again after a full week rather than forcing an exit. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: In the evening the dove returned with a freshly plucked olive leaf in her mouth, so Noah knew the waters had abated. The leaf is the first sign of new plant life and of land emerging enough for trees to show. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: He waited another seven days, sent the dove out, and she did not return. Her staying away signals the earth can now support her, and Noah reads the absence as permission to prepare to leave. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: In Noah's 601st year, in the first month, on the first day, the waters were dried from the earth, and Noah removed the ark's covering and saw the ground was dry. Seeing dry ground is not yet leaving: he still waits for God's command. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: In the second month, on the twenty-seventh day, the earth was dried. The exact date closes the flood year: from entry to full drying, more than a year has passed inside the ark. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: God spoke to Noah, telling him to go out of the ark. After all the signs, the exit itself comes by command, not by Noah's calculation alone. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: The command names Noah, his wife, his sons and his sons' wives. The same household that entered together leaves together, preserved as a family. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: God told him to bring out every living thing, that they might breed abundantly and multiply on the earth. The purpose of preservation is now stated: life is to fill the renewed earth again. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

18 COMMENTARY: Noah went out, with his sons, his wife and his sons' wives. Obedience completes the pattern: entry by command, waiting by patience, exit by command. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

19 COMMENTARY: Every beast, creeping thing, bird and moving thing went out of the ark after their kinds. The whole company leaves in order, kind by kind, as it entered. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

20 COMMENTARY: Noah built an altar to the LORD and offered burnt offerings of every clean beast and clean bird. Henry notes this is the first altar named, and Noah worships before he builds a house or plants a field, using the clean animals set apart for this purpose. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

21 COMMENTARY: The LORD smelled a sweet savour and said he would not again curse the ground for man's sake, though man's heart is evil from youth, nor again smite every living thing. Henry stresses God does not deny human evil; he promises mercy despite it, on the ground of acceptable sacrifice. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

22 COMMENTARY: While the earth remains, seedtime and harvest, cold and heat, summer and winter, day and night shall not cease. God's promise restores the regular order of seasons, so human life can plan and plant in trust. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
