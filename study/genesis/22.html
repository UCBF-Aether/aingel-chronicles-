Study Notes — Genesis

Chapter 22

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown, John Gill and Adam Clarke, all public-domain works, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: God tested Abraham, not by tempting him to sin but by proving his faith, and Abraham answered at once, "ready at a moment's warning" for God's service. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: The command named Isaac in ever closer terms, his son, his only son, whom he loved, so that the cost of obedience was felt in full before it was obeyed. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: Abraham rose early and prepared everything beforehand, wood, knife and servants, showing no delay or reluctance, and set out on the two-day journey to Moriah. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: On the third day Abraham saw the place from afar, leaving the long journey behind and approaching the appointed mountain. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: Abraham told his servants to stay with the donkey while he and Isaac went to worship, adding that they would come back, a word of trust in the outcome. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: Isaac carried the wood and Abraham the fire and knife, and father and son walked together, Isaac bearing the load as a willing participant. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: Isaac asked where the lamb for the offering was, seeing fire and wood but no victim, a natural question from a son who knew the pattern of sacrifice. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: Abraham answered that God himself would provide a lamb, words spoken in confidence that God would find a way, even if he could not yet see how Isaac would be restored. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: At the place Abraham built an altar, arranged the wood, bound Isaac and laid him on it, an act Henry and JFB say required sustaining faith in both father and son. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: Abraham stretched out his hand and took the knife, carrying obedience to the very point of action. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: The angel of the Lord called Abraham by name from heaven, stopping him at the decisive moment. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: God forbade any harm to Isaac, declaring that Abraham's willingness proved he feared God, since he had not withheld his only son. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: Abraham saw a ram caught by its horns in a thicket and offered it instead of his son, a substitution provided at exactly the needed moment. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: Abraham named the place Jehovah-jireh, the Lord will provide, and the saying arose that in the mount of the Lord provision shall be seen. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: The angel called to Abraham a second time from heaven, now to confirm blessing after the test had been passed. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: God swore by himself, since he could swear by no greater, that because Abraham had not withheld his son, blessing would surely follow. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: God promised descendants as numerous as stars and sand, who would possess the gate of their enemies, a picture of fruitful and victorious posterity. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

18 COMMENTARY: In Abraham's seed all nations would be blessed, because he had obeyed; JFB notes faith and obedience are presented together here, faith proved by works. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

19 COMMENTARY: Abraham returned to his young men, and they went together to Beersheba, where Abraham settled after the trial. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

20 COMMENTARY: After these things Abraham was told that Milcah had also borne children to his brother Nahor, news that quietly prepares the way for Rebekah. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

23 COMMENTARY: Bethuel, one of Nahor's sons, became the father of Rebekah, the woman who would later become Isaac's wife and carry the promise forward. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

No note for verse(s) 21, 22, 24 — the public-domain sources consulted give no distinct explanation for these verses beyond the list itself, so nothing was invented for them.
