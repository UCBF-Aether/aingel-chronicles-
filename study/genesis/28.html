Study Notes — Genesis

Chapter 28

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown, John Gill and Adam Clarke, all public-domain works, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Isaac called Jacob, blessed him and charged him not to take a wife from the daughters of Canaan, joining blessing to a solemn charge. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: Isaac told Jacob to go to Paddan-aram, to Bethuel his mother's father, and take a wife from Laban's daughters, keeping marriage within the kindred. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: Isaac prayed that God Almighty would bless Jacob, make him fruitful and multiply him into a multitude of peoples. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: He prayed that Jacob would receive the blessing of Abraham, for himself and his seed, so he would inherit the land where he was a stranger, which God gave to Abraham. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: Isaac sent Jacob away, and Jacob went to Paddan-aram to Laban son of Bethuel the Aramean, brother of Rebekah, travelling alone and on foot, unlike his father's grand escort. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: Esau saw that Isaac had blessed Jacob and sent him to Paddan-aram for a wife, with the charge not to marry a Canaanite, and he took note of the example. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: Esau also saw that Jacob had obeyed his father and mother and gone to Paddan-aram, obedience that shamed his own disregard. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: Esau saw that the daughters of Canaan displeased Isaac his father, a judgment he might have heeded long before. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: Esau then went to Ishmael and took Mahalath, Ishmael's daughter and Nebaioth's sister, as a wife in addition to his other wives, a partial reform that made matters worse by joining a rejected family. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: Jacob went out from Beersheba toward Haran, an exile even though he carried the blessing, blessed with plenty yet going away poor, and blessed with rule yet going out to service. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: Jacob came to a place and stayed the night because the sun had set, taking stones for a pillow and lying down, exposed and cold, yet trusting God's protection. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: He dreamed of a ladder set on earth reaching to heaven, with angels ascending and descending on it, a vision of providence at work and, as Henry reads it, of Christ as the way between heaven and earth. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: The Lord stood above the ladder and said he was the God of Abraham and Isaac, promising the land on which Jacob lay to him and his seed. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: God promised that Jacob's seed would be as the dust of the earth, spreading west, east, north and south, and that in him and his seed all families of the earth would be blessed. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: God promised to be with Jacob, keep him wherever he went, bring him back to this land, and not leave him until all that had been promised was done. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: Jacob awoke and said, surely the Lord is in this place, and I did not know it, recognising God's presence where he had expected only a lonely lodging. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: Jacob was afraid and said, how awesome is this place, this is none other than the house of God, and this is the gate of heaven. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

18 COMMENTARY: Jacob rose early, took the stone he had used as a pillow, set it up as a pillar and poured oil on top of it, marking the place with a memorial. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

19 COMMENTARY: He called the place Bethel, house of God, though the city had formerly been called Luz, renaming it for the vision he had received. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

20 COMMENTARY: Jacob vowed that if God would be with him, keep him on his journey, give him bread to eat and clothing to wear, he would trust him as his God. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

21 COMMENTARY: He added that if he returned in peace to his father's house, then the Lord would be his God, making safe return the seal of the promise. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

22 COMMENTARY: He promised that the stone he had set up would be God's house, and that of all God gave him he would surely give a tenth, a vow of worship and thanksgiving in return for protection. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
