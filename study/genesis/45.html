Study Notes — Genesis

Chapter 45

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown, John Gill and Adam Clarke, all public-domain works, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Joseph can no longer restrain himself before all who stand by, and he sends every man out, so his brothers' past will not become known to his household or to Egypt. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: He weeps aloud, in the vehement Eastern manner of expressing overwhelming feeling, so loudly that Pharaoh's house hears. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: His announcement, I am Joseph, leaves his brothers troubled and unable to answer, astonished, confounded, and shrinking from the brother they had wronged. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: He bids them come near and repeats that he is Joseph their brother whom they sold, speaking not to wound them but to convince them of his identity. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: He tells them not to be grieved or angry with themselves, since God sent him before them to preserve life, tracing an overruling providence without excusing their crime. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: Two years of famine have passed and five remain with neither ploughing nor harvest, a drought that stopped the Nile's overflow and left the land unfit for seed. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: God sent Joseph ahead to preserve a posterity and save lives by a great deliverance, so his exile is reframed as a rescue mission for the whole family. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: It was not they who sent him, Joseph insists, but God, who made him a father to Pharaoh, that is, vizier, provider, and benefactor, as well as lord of Pharaoh's house. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: They are to hurry to Jacob with the news that God has made Joseph lord of all Egypt, and to bring their father down without delay. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: Jacob is to dwell in Goshen, near Joseph, with his sons, flocks, and all he has, so the family will be kept together under Joseph's care. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: Joseph promises to nourish his father there through the five remaining famine years, lest he and his household come to poverty. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: Joseph points out that their own eyes and Benjamin's eyes see that it is his mouth speaking, so the message rests on sight and voice, not report alone. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: They are to tell Jacob of all Joseph's glory in Egypt and all they have seen, and to hasten their father down, making the brothers themselves witnesses to Jacob. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: Joseph falls on Benjamin's neck and weeps, and Benjamin weeps on his, a sudden change from condemned criminal to fondled brother that tears alone can relieve. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: He kisses all his brothers and weeps over them, and afterwards they talk with him, since forgiveness shown by embrace does more than words alone. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: The news reaches Pharaoh's house that Joseph's brothers have come, and it pleases Pharaoh and his servants, who welcome the family of their benefactor. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: Pharaoh himself invites the family to migrate, telling the brothers to load their beasts and go to Canaan, a liberality that honours Joseph's service. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

18 COMMENTARY: Pharaoh offers them the good of Egypt, that they may eat its fat, so their settlement is to be generous, not merely tolerated. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

19 COMMENTARY: Wagons are commanded for their little ones and wives and for bringing their father, a novelty in Palestine, where wheeled carriages were almost unknown. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

20 COMMENTARY: They are told not to regard their household stuff, since the good of all Egypt is theirs, so nothing need hold them back from the move. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

21 COMMENTARY: The sons of Israel do as commanded, and Joseph gives them wagons by Pharaoh's order and provision for the way, so the journey home is fully supplied. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

22 COMMENTARY: Each man receives changes of raiment, a customary gift of honour, while Benjamin receives five changes and three hundred pieces of silver, a fond partiality Joseph now feels safe to show. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

23 COMMENTARY: To his father Joseph sends ten asses laden with Egypt's good things and ten she-asses with corn, bread, and meat for the way, a large and liberal token of filial love. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

24 COMMENTARY: As he sends them away he warns them not to fall out by the way, a caution greatly needed, since the journey would revive their part in selling Joseph and soon bring it to Jacob's knowledge. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

25 COMMENTARY: They go up out of Egypt to Canaan, up because the Hebrews counted northward travel as going up, and the return journey is now a homecoming with news. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

26 COMMENTARY: They tell Jacob that Joseph is yet alive and is governor over all Egypt, and Jacob's heart faints, because he cannot believe them. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

27 COMMENTARY: They repeat all Joseph's words, and when Jacob sees the wagons Joseph sent to carry him, his spirit revives, since the wagons are visible proof. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

28 COMMENTARY: Jacob answers that it is enough, Joseph is alive, and he will go and see him before he dies, so the father's long grief turns to a single purpose. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
