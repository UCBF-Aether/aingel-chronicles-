Study Notes — Genesis

Chapter 44

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown, John Gill and Adam Clarke, all public-domain works, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Joseph orders his steward to fill the men's sacks with food as much as they can carry, heaping kindness on them even as he prepares a further trial. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: Every man's money is again put in his sack's mouth, and Joseph's silver cup in Benjamin's, so the youngest, lately most favoured, will bear the sharpest test. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: As soon as morning light comes they are sent away with their asses, departing in gladness, unaware that the trial is already planted in their baggage. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: Before they are far out of the city Joseph sends his steward after them, so the pursuit comes swiftly, while the cup is still freshly missed. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: The steward charges them with rewarding evil for good, stealing a cup of daily use that his lord drinks from, which would soon be missed and thoroughly searched for. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: Overtaking them, the steward speaks these words to them, and JFB notes the accusation must have struck them like a thunderbolt, as men so often made objects of suspicion. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: They protest their innocence and detest so base a thing, urging as proof of honesty that they brought their money back from Canaan. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: They argue that men who returned money would hardly steal silver or gold from their lord's house, so the charge contradicts their known conduct. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: They offer the severest terms, that whoever has the cup shall die and the rest become bondmen, a rash pledge that shows how sure they feel of innocence. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: The steward softens their terms, so that only the man with the cup shall be bondman and the rest go free, keeping the test focused on Benjamin alone. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: Each man hastily takes his sack down and opens it, inviting the search they had offered, confident that nothing will be found. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: Beginning with the eldest and ending with the youngest, the cup is found in Benjamin's sack, the very order that makes the blow fall last and hardest. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: They rend their clothes, load their asses again, and return to the city together, refusing to leave Benjamin even though the steward's terms allowed them to go. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: Judah and his brothers come to Joseph's house and fall before him to the ground, owning themselves his servants in a bowing deeper than mere courtesy. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: Joseph asks what deed this is, and whether they do not know a man like him can divine, pressing the apparent evidence against them. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: Judah answers that they cannot clear themselves, saying God has found out their iniquity, likely referring to their old injury to Joseph, and they surrender as servants. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: Joseph, with an air of justice, sentences only Benjamin to bondage and dismisses the rest in peace, since only the guilty, by the evidence, should suffer. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

18 COMMENTARY: Judah comes near and begs leave to speak, addressing Joseph with respect and deference, calling him lord and ascribing to him authority even as Pharaoh. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

19 COMMENTARY: Judah recalls that Joseph first asked whether they had a father or a brother, showing the whole trial began with Joseph's own questions, not their fault. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

20 COMMENTARY: Judah describes Benjamin as a young child of his father's old age, the only one left of his mother, loved by his father, and therefore worthy of compassion. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

21 COMMENTARY: Judah recalls Joseph's command to bring Benjamin down that he might set his eyes on him, which had seemed to promise kindness, not slavery. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

22 COMMENTARY: Judah recalls their plea that the lad cannot leave his father, for if he did his father would die, a plea Joseph had heard and yet overruled. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

23 COMMENTARY: Judah recalls the condition that unless the youngest came they should see Joseph's face no more, the hard rule that forced Benjamin's journey. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

24 COMMENTARY: Judah reports that on returning home they told their father Joseph's words, faithfully carrying the governor's terms to the one who had to decide. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

25 COMMENTARY: Judah recalls Jacob's later order to go back and buy a little food, which reopened the whole question of Benjamin's going. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

26 COMMENTARY: Judah recalls their answer that they could not go without Benjamin, since they could not see the man's face unless their youngest was with them. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

27 COMMENTARY: Judah recalls Jacob's words that his wife bore him two sons, Joseph and Benjamin, the frame in which the father counts his loss. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

28 COMMENTARY: Judah recalls Jacob's belief that the one son went out and was torn in pieces and never seen since, a lie long told that the brothers had begun to believe themselves. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

29 COMMENTARY: Judah recalls Jacob's warning that if this one also is taken, his grey hairs will be brought down with sorrow to the grave. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

30 COMMENTARY: Judah argues that his father's life is bound up in the lad's life, so returning without Benjamin would itself be the father's death blow. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

31 COMMENTARY: Judah warns that when Jacob sees the lad is not with them he will die, and they will have brought his grey hairs down with sorrow. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

32 COMMENTARY: Judah pleads his own suretyship, that he promised his father to bear the blame for ever if he did not bring Benjamin back. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

33 COMMENTARY: Judah offers himself as bondman instead of Benjamin, so the sentence is satisfied, Joseph loses nothing, and Jacob loses the son he can better spare. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

34 COMMENTARY: Judah closes by saying he cannot bear to see the evil that would come on his father, a plea of tenderness that, as Henry notes, could melt a heart of stone, and it breaks Joseph's restraint. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
