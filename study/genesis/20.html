Study Notes — Genesis

Chapter 20

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown, John Gill and Adam Clarke, all public-domain works, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Abraham journeyed south into the Negeb, living between Kadesh and Shur and sojourning in Gerar, still a pilgrim moving from place to place in the promised region. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: In Gerar, Abraham again called Sarah his sister, repeating his old Egyptian device, and Abimelech, king of Gerar, sent and took her, so the old fault endangered the promise a second time. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: God came to Abimelech in a dream by night and told him he was a dead man for taking another man's wife, warning the king before any wrong was done. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: Abimelech had not come near Sarah, and he pleaded that he had acted in the integrity of his heart, asking whether God would slay even a righteous nation for a wrong done unknowingly. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: He explained that Abraham himself had said she was his sister, and Sarah had said he was her brother, so his deed rested on their own words, and his conscience was clear in the matter. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: God answered that he knew Abimelech's integrity, and had himself kept the king from sinning by not allowing him to touch Sarah, showing God's hand guarding the promise even through a pagan king. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: God told Abimelech to restore Sarah, because Abraham was a prophet who would pray for him, and warned that refusal would mean death for him and all his household. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: Abimelech rose early in the morning, told all his servants what God had said, and they were greatly afraid, since the dream left no doubt about the danger. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: Abimelech called Abraham and demanded what he had done to bring such great sin on his kingdom, for Abraham's deception had nearly made an innocent king guilty. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: Abimelech pressed Abraham to explain what he had seen or feared that made him do this, giving Abraham a full hearing before judging him. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: Abraham answered that he thought there was no fear of God in that place and that men would kill him for his wife, an excuse Henry notes as both a slander on Gerar and a confession of distrust in God's protection. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: Abraham added that Sarah truly was his sister, his father's daughter though not his mother's, and had become his wife, so his statement was a half-truth used to hide the whole truth. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: He explained that when God caused him to wander from his father's house, he had asked Sarah to say he was her brother wherever they went, a standing agreement of fear made long before Gerar. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: Abimelech restored Sarah to Abraham with sheep, oxen, and servants, making generous amends and honouring the prophet he had nearly wronged. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: Abimelech told Abraham the whole land was before him to dwell wherever he pleased, turning a dangerous sojourn into a free and welcome stay. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: Abimelech gave Sarah a thousand pieces of silver as a covering of the eyes, a public vindication before her brother and all who were with her, so that her honour was cleared. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: Abraham prayed to God, and God healed Abimelech, his wife, and his maidservants, so that they could bear children again, showing Abraham's restored role as intercessor. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

18 COMMENTARY: The barrenness of Abimelech's household had come because God had closed every womb on Sarah's account, and its lifting proved that God had guarded Sarah throughout the affair. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
