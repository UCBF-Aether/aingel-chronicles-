Study Notes — Genesis

Chapter 47

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown, John Gill and Adam Clarke, all public-domain works, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Joseph first tells Pharaoh that his father and brothers have come, a man who bears prosperity and adversity equally well and will not settle his family without his master's sanction. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: He takes some of his brothers, probably the five eldest, since seniority is the least invidious principle of selection for presentation at court. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: Pharaoh asks the brothers what their occupation is, the question Joseph had prepared them for, so their plain answer can now be given. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: They answer as instructed, that they have come to sojourn, since famine is sore in Canaan and there is no pasture for their flocks, and they ask to dwell in Goshen, showing a faith that still looks back to Canaan as home. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: Pharaoh speaks to Joseph, acknowledging that his father and brothers have come to him, so the royal welcome is given through Joseph as their patron. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: Pharaoh answers Joseph, granting the land of Goshen and even offering positions over his own cattle to able men among them, a generous royal welcome. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: Joseph brings Jacob in, and the old patriarch blesses Pharaoh on entering, a simple and dignified act that must have struck the court, since the lesser is blessed by the greater. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: Pharaoh asks Jacob how old he is, a question drawn by the deep interest his aged appearance creates in a land where life was shorter than among the mountaineers of Canaan. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: Jacob calls his one hundred and thirty years few and evil, reckoning by days, since in retrospect they seem short and his life has been an almost unbroken series of trouble. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: Jacob blesses Pharaoh again as he goes out, closing the audience with the same quiet dignity with which he entered it. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: Joseph settles his father and brothers in the best of the land, the land of Rameses as Pharaoh commanded, the rich pasture country of Goshen along the eastern Nile branch. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: Joseph nourishes his father, his brothers, and all his father's household with bread according to their families, so the whole clan is sustained through the famine. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: There is no bread in all the land, JFB noting this is the second famine year, when private stores are exhausted, Egypt and Canaan faint, and the people become universally dependent on the government. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: Joseph gathers all the money in Egypt and Canaan for the corn bought, and brings it into Pharaoh's house, so the state's reserve is honestly accounted for. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: When money fails, the people come to Joseph saying they will die before him, since their money is spent, and Joseph must find another way to keep them alive. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: Joseph says to give their cattle, the wisest course for preserving both people and cattle, since the cattle, bought by Joseph, would be fed at royal expense and likely returned after the famine. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: They bring their cattle and Joseph gives them bread in exchange for horses, flocks, herds, and asses, feeding them for that year with their livestock as payment. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

18 COMMENTARY: The next year they come again, saying they cannot hide that their money and cattle are spent, so nothing is left but their bodies and their land. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

19 COMMENTARY: They ask Joseph to buy them and their land for bread, so they and the land will not die and lie desolate, offering themselves rather than starving. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

20 COMMENTARY: Joseph buys all the land of Egypt for Pharaoh, since the Egyptians sell their fields under the famine's pressure, and the land becomes Pharaoh's. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

21 COMMENTARY: As for the people, Joseph removes them to cities, obviously for convenience, bringing country people near the cities where the corn stores are. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

22 COMMENTARY: Only the priests' land is not bought, since their lands are inalienable temple endowments and they already receive an annual allowance from the state. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

23 COMMENTARY: Joseph tells the people he has bought them and their land, and gives them seed to sow, so the next crop can begin even though the land is now Pharaoh's. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

24 COMMENTARY: At harvest they are to give a fifth to Pharaoh and keep four-fifths for seed, food, and their households, a produce rent that makes them tenants on fair terms. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

25 COMMENTARY: The people answer that Joseph has saved their lives and they will gladly be Pharaoh's servants, hailing him as benefactor, not oppressor. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

26 COMMENTARY: Joseph makes the fifth-part law over all Egypt, except the priests' land, a lasting settlement that stands to the day of the writing. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

27 COMMENTARY: Israel dwells in Goshen, gains possessions there, and grows and multiplies exceedingly, so the family that came as seventy begins to become a nation. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

28 COMMENTARY: Jacob lives seventeen years in Egypt, making his whole life one hundred and forty-seven years, seventeen years of quiet after a life of wandering. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

29 COMMENTARY: As his death draws near, Jacob calls Joseph and asks him to deal kindly and truly with him, not to bury him in Egypt, a request that reveals his faith in the promises. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

30 COMMENTARY: Jacob asks to lie with his fathers, carried out of Egypt to their burial place, and Joseph promises to do as he has said. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

31 COMMENTARY: Jacob demands an oath, Joseph swears, and Israel bows himself on the bed's head, or as Hebrews renders it, on the top of his staff, in worshipful acceptance. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
