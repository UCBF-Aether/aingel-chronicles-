Study Notes — Genesis

Chapter 50

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown, John Gill and Adam Clarke, all public-domain works, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Joseph falls on his father's face, weeps over him, and kisses him, performing as principal member of the family the duty of closing his eyes and giving the farewell kiss. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: Joseph commands his servants the physicians to embalm his father, since in Egypt embalmers were a distinct class who infused resinous substances, dried the body, and wrapped it in linen for a chest. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: Forty days are fulfilled for embalming, and the Egyptians mourn seventy days, a period of public mourning as for a royal personage, including the whole embalming time. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: When mourning is past, Joseph speaks to Pharaoh's house through others, since as a mourner he is unfit to appear in the royal presence, and he asks them to speak in Pharaoh's ears. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: He reports his father's oath, that he must be buried in the grave dug in Canaan, and asks leave to go up and bury him and return, so the request is framed as duty, not flight. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: Pharaoh grants leave, saying go up and bury your father as he made you swear, honouring both the oath and Joseph's service. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: Joseph goes up with all Pharaoh's servants, the elders of his house, and the elders of Egypt, a funeral cavalcade of court attachés and state officers over a journey of some three hundred miles. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: All Joseph's house, his brothers, and his father's house go too, leaving only their little ones and flocks in Goshen, so the family is present while its base remains in Egypt. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: Chariots and horsemen go up as well, making a very great company, an imposing escort that honours both Jacob and Joseph. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: At the threshing floor of Atad beyond Jordan they mourn with a great and sore lamentation for seven days, the last and most violent halt for grief before the burial. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: The Canaanites, seeing the mourning, call the place Abel-mizraim, the mourning of the Egyptians, so the spot keeps the name of that grief. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: Jacob's sons do as he commanded them, carrying out the burial charge faithfully, so the father's last orders are obeyed by the whole family. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: His sons carry him into Canaan and bury him in Machpelah's cave, bought by Abraham from Ephron before Mamre, though Egyptian attendants could not join that ceremony. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: After burying his father, Joseph returns to Egypt with his brothers and all who went up with him, keeping the promise made to Pharaoh. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: With their father dead, the brothers fear Joseph will hate them and requite the evil they did, a fear that shows guilt still unquiet even after years of kindness. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: They send a message to Joseph, saying their father commanded before his death that Joseph should forgive their trespass and sin, appealing to Jacob's authority for pardon. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: They ask Joseph to forgive the servants of his father's God, and Joseph weeps when they speak to him, deeply affected by their fear and their plea. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

18 COMMENTARY: The brothers themselves come, fall down before him, and say they are his servants, renewing the bowing of the dreams in humble submission. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

19 COMMENTARY: Joseph tells them not to fear, asking whether he is in God's place, so vengeance is refused as a prerogative that belongs to God alone. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

20 COMMENTARY: Joseph explains that though they meant evil against him, God meant it for good, to bring about the saving of many people alive, a summary of providence over the whole story. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

21 COMMENTARY: He promises not to fear, for he will nourish them and their little ones, and he comforts them and speaks kindly to them, so assurance follows theology. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

22 COMMENTARY: Joseph dwells in Egypt with his father's household and lives one hundred and ten years, eighty years after his elevation, witnessing the growth of his family and kingdom. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

23 COMMENTARY: Joseph sees Ephraim's children to the third generation, and Manasseh's son Machir's children are brought up on his knees, a patriarch's long view of his house. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

24 COMMENTARY: Dying, Joseph says God will surely visit them and bring them out of Egypt to the land sworn to Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob, holding the promise firm at the end of his life. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

25 COMMENTARY: Joseph takes an oath from Israel that when God visits them they shall carry up his bones from Egypt, the strongest proof of his faith, since he asks for no burial in Canaan now but for a share in the future return. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

26 COMMENTARY: Joseph dies at one hundred and ten and is embalmed and put in a coffin in Egypt, closing Genesis with a coffin in Egypt and a promise pointing back to Canaan. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
