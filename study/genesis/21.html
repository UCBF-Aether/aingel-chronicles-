Study Notes — Genesis

Chapter 21

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown, John Gill and Adam Clarke, all public-domain works, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: God kept his word to Sarah exactly as promised, visiting her in mercy after long waiting; Henry stresses that no word of God falls to the ground. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: Isaac was born at the set time God had spoken, showing that God's timing, not ours, is the best time for promised mercy. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: Abraham named the child Isaac, meaning laughter, as God had directed, rather than choosing a grander name of his own invention. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: Abraham circumcised Isaac on the eighth day, keeping God's time in obedience just as God had kept time in performing the promise. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: Abraham was a hundred years old when Isaac was born, underscoring that the birth came by special promise, not ordinary human strength. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: Sarah's laughter now turned from doubt to joy, as she said God had given her both cause to rejoice and a heart to rejoice. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: Sarah marvelled that, old as she was, she should not only bear a child but nurse him, a mercy beyond what anyone would have predicted. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: The child grew and was weaned, and Abraham made a great feast in thankfulness for God's care through the perils of infancy. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: Sarah saw Ishmael mocking Isaac, an older boy abusing a younger child; Henry calls mocking a great sin and a foretaste of persecution of the promised seed. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: Sarah demanded that Hagar and Ishmael be cast out, so that the son of the bondwoman would not share the inheritance with Isaac. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: The demand grieved Abraham deeply, both because Ishmael had misbehaved and because expulsion seemed a harsh punishment from a tender father. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: God told Abraham to listen to Sarah in this, because the covenant line must run through Isaac, not through Ishmael. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: God also assured Abraham that Ishmael would still become a nation, because he was Abraham's seed, so expulsion from the household was not ruin in this world. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: Abraham obeyed promptly, early in the morning, sending Hagar and Ishmael away on foot with bread and water, submitting his own inclination to God's command. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: In the wilderness of Beersheba their water ran out, and the once well-fed household found itself in desperate want. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: Hagar put Ishmael under a shrub and sat apart, unable to watch him die, forgetting in present distress the earlier promise that he would live and grow great. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: God heard the boy's voice and sent his angel to comfort Hagar, calling her by name and telling her not to fear. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

18 COMMENTARY: The angel told Hagar to lift up the boy and hold him, renewing the promise that Ishmael would be made a great nation. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

19 COMMENTARY: God opened Hagar's eyes to see a well of water, and she filled the bottle and gave the boy a drink, a timely provision she had overlooked in despair. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

20 COMMENTARY: God was with Ishmael as he grew up in the wilderness, where he became an archer, living by the bow as a free desert dweller. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

21 COMMENTARY: Ishmael settled in the wilderness of Paran, and his mother took him a wife from Egypt, her own homeland, keeping him apart from Isaac's household. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

22 COMMENTARY: Abimelech, with Phichol his army commander, came to Abraham, acknowledging that God was with him in all he did. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

23 COMMENTARY: Abimelech asked Abraham to swear kindness to him and his descendants, as he himself had shown kindness to Abraham. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

24 COMMENTARY: Abraham agreed to swear, entering the proposed covenant in good faith. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

25 COMMENTARY: Abraham first raised a complaint about a well of water that Abimelech's servants had seized, insisting that even a covenant of friendship must deal honestly with wrongs. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

26 COMMENTARY: Abimelech answered that he had not known of the seizure, and had only now heard of it, showing the wrong was his servants', not his own policy. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

27 COMMENTARY: Abraham gave sheep and oxen to Abimelech, and the two men made a covenant together, sealing their agreement with gifts. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

28 COMMENTARY: Abraham set seven ewe lambs apart by themselves, as a separate token within the covenant-making. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

29 COMMENTARY: Abimelech asked what the seven lambs meant, since a gift set apart in this way needed explanation. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

30 COMMENTARY: Abraham explained that Abimelech was to accept the seven lambs as witness that Abraham had dug the well, settling ownership publicly. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

31 COMMENTARY: The place was therefore called Beersheba, the well of the oath, because the two men swore there. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

32 COMMENTARY: After the covenant at Beersheba, Abimelech and Phichol returned to the land of the Philistines, leaving Abraham in possession. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

33 COMMENTARY: Abraham planted a tamarisk tree at Beersheba and there called on the name of the everlasting God, marking the place with lasting worship. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

34 COMMENTARY: Abraham stayed many days in the land of the Philistines, dwelling as a sojourner even where he had made peace. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
