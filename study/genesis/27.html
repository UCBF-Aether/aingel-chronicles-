Study Notes — Genesis

Chapter 27

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown, John Gill and Adam Clarke, all public-domain works, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: When Isaac was old and his eyes were dim, he called Esau his elder son and said, my son, and Esau answered, here I am, ready as a son should be. — John Gill

2 COMMENTARY: Isaac said he was old and did not know the day of his death, a sober reminder that even a long life ends without warning. — John Gill

3 COMMENTARY: Isaac told Esau to take his weapons, quiver and bow, go to the field and hunt venison for him, a father's request for a last meal from his hunter son. — John Gill

4 COMMENTARY: Isaac asked Esau to make savoury food such as he loved, so he could eat and bless him before he died, joining affection, appetite and blessing in one act. — John Gill

5 COMMENTARY: Rebekah overheard Isaac speaking to Esau, and when Esau went to hunt, she began her own plan, listening to a blessing meant for another son. — John Gill

6 COMMENTARY: Rebekah told Jacob that she had heard his father charge Esau to bring venison and prepare food so he could bless him before the Lord before he died. — John Gill

7 COMMENTARY: She repeated Isaac's words to Jacob, that Esau should bring venison and make savoury meat for a blessing before death, so Jacob would understand the stakes. — John Gill

8 COMMENTARY: Rebekah urged Jacob to obey her voice and do what she commanded, pressing a son's duty of obedience into service of her scheme. — John Gill

9 COMMENTARY: She told Jacob to fetch two good kids from the flock so she could make savoury meat such as his father loved, substituting goat for venison. — John Gill

10 COMMENTARY: Jacob was to bring the food to his father to eat, so that Isaac would bless him before he died, the blessing itself being the prize. — John Gill

11 COMMENTARY: Jacob objected that Esau was hairy and he was smooth, so his father might feel him and think he was deceiving him, bringing a curse instead of a blessing. — John Gill

12 COMMENTARY: Jacob feared that if Isaac touched him he would be found a deceiver and bring a curse on himself, a fear that showed he knew the act was wrong. — John Gill

13 COMMENTARY: Rebekah took the curse on herself, saying, let your curse be on me, my son, only obey my voice and fetch the kids, a rash promise no mother could truly keep. — John Gill

14 COMMENTARY: Jacob went, fetched the kids and brought them to his mother, and she made savoury meat such as his father loved, the plan moving from words to deeds. — John Gill

15 COMMENTARY: Rebekah took Esau's best garments, which were with her in the house, and put them on Jacob her younger son, dressing deception in a brother's clothes. — John Gill

16 COMMENTARY: She put goat skins on Jacob's hands and on the smooth of his neck, to imitate Esau's hairiness for a blind father's touch. — John Gill

17 COMMENTARY: She gave the savoury meat and bread she had prepared into Jacob's hand, sending him in to play the part of his brother. — John Gill

18 COMMENTARY: Jacob came to his father and said, my father, and Isaac answered, here I am, asking who he was, a question that should have stopped the deception. — John Gill

19 COMMENTARY: Jacob said he was Esau his firstborn, that he had done as Isaac asked, and urged his father to rise, eat his venison and bless him, a direct lie told to gain the blessing. — John Gill

20 COMMENTARY: Isaac marvelled that Jacob had found game so quickly, and Jacob answered that the Lord his God had brought it to him, adding God's name to a falsehood. — John Gill

21 COMMENTARY: Isaac asked Jacob to come near so he could feel whether he was truly Esau, still doubting despite the voice and the food. — John Gill

22 COMMENTARY: Jacob came near, and Isaac felt him and said the voice was Jacob's voice but the hands were Esau's hands, suspicion held in check by touch. — John Gill

23 COMMENTARY: Isaac did not recognise him, because his hands were hairy like Esau's, so he blessed him, deceived by the skins and the garments. — John Gill

24 COMMENTARY: Isaac asked once more, are you really my son Esau, and Jacob answered, I am, repeating the lie at the very threshold of blessing. — John Gill

25 COMMENTARY: Isaac told him to bring the food near so he could eat of his son's venison and bless him, and Jacob brought it, and he ate, and brought wine, and he drank. — John Gill

26 COMMENTARY: Isaac then said, come near now and kiss me, my son, seeking by smell and affection one more assurance before blessing. — John Gill

27 COMMENTARY: Jacob came near and kissed him, and Isaac smelled his garments, like the smell of a field the Lord had blessed, and blessed him with the fragrance of the open country in mind. — John Gill

28 COMMENTARY: Isaac blessed him with the dew of heaven, the fatness of the earth, and plenty of corn and wine, a blessing of fruitful land and rich provision. — John Gill

29 COMMENTARY: He blessed him with peoples serving him and nations bowing down, lordship over his brothers, and his mother's sons bowing to him, with a curse on those who cursed him and blessing on those who blessed him. — John Gill

30 COMMENTARY: As soon as Isaac had finished blessing Jacob, and Jacob had scarcely left his presence, Esau came in from hunting, the timing exposing how narrowly the deception had succeeded. — John Gill

31 COMMENTARY: Esau also made savoury food and brought it to his father, asking him to rise, eat his son's venison and bless him, unaware the blessing had already been given. — John Gill

32 COMMENTARY: Isaac asked who he was, and Esau answered, I am your son, your firstborn Esau, and the truth of the two claims collided. — John Gill

33 COMMENTARY: Isaac trembled exceedingly, asking who had hunted and brought food that he had already eaten and blessed, and he declared that the one blessed must remain blessed, the blessing being irrevocable. — John Gill

34 COMMENTARY: Esau cried with a great and exceedingly bitter cry, begging his father to bless him also, a cry of loss that Henry reads as the fruit of his earlier contempt for the birthright. — John Gill

35 COMMENTARY: Isaac answered that his brother had come with deceit and taken his blessing, naming plainly what Jacob had done, even while he could not undo it. — John Gill

36 COMMENTARY: Esau said Jacob was rightly named supplanter, for he had supplanted him twice, first taking the birthright and now the blessing, and he asked whether Isaac had reserved any blessing for him. — John Gill

37 COMMENTARY: Isaac answered that he had made Jacob lord over Esau and given him corn and wine and all his brothers as servants, asking what was left for Esau. — John Gill

38 COMMENTARY: Esau wept aloud and begged for even one blessing, asking if his father had only one blessing, a plea that shows how much he now valued what he had once despised. — John Gill

39 COMMENTARY: Isaac gave Esau a lesser blessing, that his dwelling would be away from the fatness of the earth and the dew of heaven, a life of hardship rather than plenty. — John Gill

40 COMMENTARY: Esau would live by his sword and serve his brother, but in time he would break the yoke from his neck, a prophecy of Edom's later struggle for freedom. — John Gill

41 COMMENTARY: Esau hated Jacob because of the blessing and said in his heart that the days of mourning for his father were near, and then he would kill his brother, hatred turning to murderous intent. — John Gill

42 COMMENTARY: Rebekah was told of Esau's words, and she sent for Jacob and warned him that Esau planned to kill him in revenge, so her scheme now threatened the son it meant to help. — John Gill

43 COMMENTARY: Rebekah urged Jacob to flee at once to her brother Laban at Haran, the only safety from his brother's anger. — John Gill

44 COMMENTARY: Jacob was to stay with Laban a few days until his brother's fury turned away, a hope that the time apart would be short, though it would in fact last many years. — John Gill

45 COMMENTARY: Rebekah said that when Esau's anger turned and he forgot what Jacob had done, she would send for him, asking why she should lose both sons in one day, whether by murder or by exile. — John Gill

46 COMMENTARY: Rebekah then told Isaac, without mentioning Esau's threat, that she was weary of life because of the Hittite wives, and asked what good her life would be if Jacob married likewise, a pretext to gain Isaac's consent for Jacob's departure. — John Gill
