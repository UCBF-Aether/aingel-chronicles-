Study Notes — Genesis

Chapter 35

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown, John Gill and Adam Clarke, all public-domain works, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: God told Jacob to arise, go up to Bethel, dwell there, and build an altar to the God who appeared to him when he fled from Esau. JFB notes the command came seasonably, when Jacob longed to leave Shechem, and tenderly, as a reminder of a neglected vow. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: Jacob told his household to put away strange gods, be clean, and change garments. Henry reads the outward cleansing as a sign of inward purifying: clean clothes without a clean heart mean nothing. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: They were to arise and go to Bethel, where Jacob would build an altar to the God who answered him in distress and was with him on the way. The journey itself was to be an act of worship, not mere relocation. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: They gave Jacob all the strange gods and earrings, and he hid them under the oak by Shechem. JFB explains the earrings were linked to incantation and idolatry, and burial under a consecrated tree put them where no bold hand would disturb them. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: As they journeyed, the terror of God fell on the surrounding cities, and no one pursued Jacob's sons. Henry's lesson is that the way of duty is the way of safety: when we are about God's work, we are under special protection. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: So Jacob came to Luz, which is Bethel, in Canaan, with all his people. JFB notes Luz was the Canaanite town name, from its almond trees, while Bethel was Jacob's family name for the place. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: There Jacob built an altar and called the place El-bethel, because God had appeared to him there when he fled from his brother. Henry notes the comfort lies not in Bethel the house of God, but in El-bethel, the God of the house. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: Deborah, Rebekah's nurse, died and was buried beneath Bethel under an oak, called Allon-bachuth. JFB notes old nurses were honoured and loved as mothers, and her death was greatly lamented. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: God appeared to Jacob again after his return from Padan-aram and blessed him. The appearance after the solemn rites showed God's acceptance and renewed the covenant promises. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: God said Jacob's name would no longer be Jacob only, but Israel. As at Peniel, the new name marked a confirmed standing, not a fresh bargain. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: God declared himself God Almighty and commanded fruitfulness: a nation and a company of nations, and kings, would come from Jacob. Henry notes God all-sufficient is able to make good the promise in due time and to support Jacob meanwhile. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: The land given to Abraham and Isaac was promised to Jacob and to his seed after him. The promise tied Jacob's personal blessing to the same land and lineage as his fathers. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: God went up from Jacob in the place where he had talked with him. JFB notes God's presence had been shown in visible form, with acceptance shown by fire consuming the sacrifice. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: Jacob set up a stone pillar where God had spoken, poured a drink offering and oil on it. The rite repeated his earlier consecration of the place, now with fuller worship. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: Jacob called the place where God spoke with him Bethel. The name, house of God, fixed the memory of the meeting in the landscape. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: They journeyed from Bethel, and a little way from Ephrath Rachel went into hard labour. JFB notes Jacob left Bethel a better and more devoted servant of God, only to be plunged into mourning on the road. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: In hard labour the midwife told Rachel not to fear, for she would have this son also. The reassurance came in the midst of danger, not after it. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

18 COMMENTARY: As her soul departed, Rachel called the child Ben-oni, son of my sorrow, but his father called him Benjamin. JFB explains Benjamin as son of the right hand, particularly dear and precious, while Henry adds Jacob would not renew the sorrow of his wife's death every time he named his son. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

19 COMMENTARY: Rachel died and was buried on the way to Ephrath, which is Bethlehem. JFB notes Ephrath was the old name and Bethlehem the later name, meaning house of bread. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

20 COMMENTARY: Jacob set a pillar on Rachel's grave, which remained the pillar of Rachel's grave. JFB notes the spot still marked in his day agreed with the record, about a mile from Bethlehem. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

21 COMMENTARY: Israel journeyed on and spread his tent beyond the tower of Edar. The journey continued through grief, without a pause in the narrative for comfort. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

22 COMMENTARY: While Israel dwelt in that land, Reuben lay with Bilhah his father's concubine, and Israel heard of it; the verse then opens the reckoning that Jacob's sons were twelve. Henry notes only that Israel heard it, and that is enough: secret sin is generally disappointed in its secrecy. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

23 COMMENTARY: Leah's sons are listed: Reuben the firstborn, Simeon, Levi, Judah, Issachar, and Zebulun. The grouping by mother preserves the family structure behind later tribal history. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

24 COMMENTARY: Rachel's sons are listed: Joseph and Benjamin. The short list carries the weight of the chapters that follow, since Joseph's story dominates the rest of Genesis. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

25 COMMENTARY: Bilhah's sons are listed: Dan and Naphtali. They are named as Rachel's handmaid's sons, keeping the household's divisions visible. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

26 COMMENTARY: Zilpah's sons are listed: Gad and Asher, said to have been born to Jacob in Padan-aram. JFB's caution applies here too: the statement is true of the company in general, with Benjamin as the known exception. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

27 COMMENTARY: Jacob came to Isaac his father at Mamre, the city of Arbah, which is Hebron, where Abraham and Isaac had sojourned. The return to his father's home closed the long journey from Padan-aram. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

28 COMMENTARY: Isaac's days were a hundred and fourscore years. The long life is recorded simply, as the measure of a patriarch's span. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

29 COMMENTARY: Isaac died, old and full of days, and was gathered to his people, and Esau and Jacob buried him. Henry notes the brothers' agreement at their father's funeral shows how wonderfully God had changed Esau's mind, and JFB adds the death is recorded here by anticipation, since Isaac lived years longer in the story's chronology. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
