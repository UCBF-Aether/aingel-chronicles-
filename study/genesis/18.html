Study Notes — Genesis

Chapter 18

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown, John Gill and Adam Clarke, all public-domain works, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: God appeared to Abraham by the oaks of Mamre as he sat at his tent door in the heat of the day, a homely setting for a heavenly visit. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: Abraham looked up and saw three men standing nearby, and he ran to meet them and bowed, showing the eager hospitality of the ancient East. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: Abraham begged them not to pass by his servant, but to accept his hospitality, treating honoured guests as a blessing rather than a burden. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: He offered water for their feet and rest under the tree, the simple courtesies owed to travellers in a hot and dusty land. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: He promised a morsel of bread to refresh their hearts before they went on, modest words for what became a generous meal. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: Abraham hurried into the tent and urged Sarah to knead three measures of fine meal into cakes, sharing the work of welcome between husband and wife. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: He ran to the herd, chose a tender young calf, and had a servant prepare it at once, so the meal was both quick and of the best he had. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: He set curds, milk, and the calf before them and stood by under the tree while they ate, serving his guests himself rather than leaving them to servants. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: The visitors asked after Sarah, drawing her into the conversation, and Abraham answered that she was in the tent, nearby and listening. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: One of them promised that at the set time next year he would return, and Sarah would have a son, repeating the covenant promise now as a dated appointment. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: Abraham and Sarah were both old and well advanced in years, and Sarah had long passed childbearing age, so the promise faced every ordinary impossibility. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: Sarah laughed to herself at the thought of pleasure and a child in her old age, with her lord also old, a laugh Henry reads as mingled wonder and doubt. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: God asked why Sarah laughed and whether anything was too hard for him, turning her private laugh into a public lesson about God's power. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: The question, whether anything is too hard for God, stands as the chapter's key: the promise did not depend on Sarah's age but on God's ability. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: Sarah denied laughing, because she was afraid, but was told plainly that she had laughed, showing that nothing spoken or thought in the tent was hidden from God. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: The men rose and looked toward Sodom, and Abraham went with them to send them on their way, as a host escorting honoured guests. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: God asked whether he should hide from Abraham what he was about to do, treating his friend as a confidant rather than keeping judgment secret. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

18 COMMENTARY: The reason given was Abraham's future: he would become a great nation and all nations would be blessed in him, so he was fit to know God's dealings with the world. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

19 COMMENTARY: God said he knew Abraham would command his household after him to keep God's way in justice and righteousness, so that the promises could be fulfilled through a taught and obedient family. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

20 COMMENTARY: The outcry against Sodom and Gomorrah was great and their sin very grave, a cry to heaven for justice that demanded God's personal inquiry. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

21 COMMENTARY: God said he would go down and see whether the outcry matched the facts, a human way of speaking that pictures God's judgment as careful and first-hand, never hasty. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

22 COMMENTARY: The two men turned toward Sodom while Abraham remained standing before God, left alone with the judge of all the earth to plead for the cities. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

23 COMMENTARY: Abraham drew near and asked whether God would sweep away the righteous with the wicked, the bold question on which his whole intercession rests. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

24 COMMENTARY: He asked whether fifty righteous people in the city would save it, arguing that a just judge would spare a whole city for the sake of the good within it. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

25 COMMENTARY: He pressed that it would be far from God to slay the righteous with the wicked, treating them alike, since the judge of all the earth must do right. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

26 COMMENTARY: God agreed to spare the city for fifty righteous, showing that Abraham's reasoning about justice was right and that God welcomes bold, humble pleading. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

27 COMMENTARY: Abraham answered that he was only dust and ashes, yet ventured to speak, a model of intercession that joins lowliness with persistence. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

28 COMMENTARY: He asked whether five fewer, forty-five righteous, would still save the city, and God agreed, so the pleading moved downward step by step. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

29 COMMENTARY: He went on to forty, and God agreed again, and Henry notes that each concession encouraged Abraham to ask further rather than presuming too far at once. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

30 COMMENTARY: He asked for thirty, and God agreed, the number falling while God's patience in hearing held steady. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

31 COMMENTARY: He asked for twenty, and God agreed, so the intercession kept narrowing toward the small remnant that might yet be found. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

32 COMMENTARY: He asked one last time, for ten, and God agreed to spare the city for ten righteous, the lowest number Abraham dared to name. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

33 COMMENTARY: God left off speaking and Abraham returned home, and JFB notes he stopped either because he thought the cities now safe, or because God restrained further pleading. In fact, as the next chapter shows, there were not ten righteous there. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
