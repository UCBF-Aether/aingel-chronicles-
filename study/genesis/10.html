Study Notes — Genesis

Chapter 10

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown, John Gill and Adam Clarke, all public-domain works, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: These are the generations of Shem, Ham and Japheth after the flood, to whom sons were born. The chapter is a table of peoples, showing how the one household of chapter 9 spread into nations. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown; John Gill

2 COMMENTARY: The sons of Japheth are Gomer, Magog, Madai, Javan, Tubal, Meshech and Tiras. JFB and Clarke identify these names with peoples spreading north and west, toward Asia Minor and Europe. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown; John Gill

5 COMMENTARY: From these the isles of the Gentiles were divided in their lands, each after tongue, family and nation. The verse summarises Japheth's spread: peoples divided by language and kin, not yet by separate kingdoms alone. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown; John Gill

6 COMMENTARY: The sons of Ham are Cush, Mizraim, Phut and Canaan. Ham's line spreads south and west into Africa and Canaan, the regions that will dominate much of the story ahead. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown; John Gill

8 COMMENTARY: Cush fathered Nimrod, who began to be a mighty one on the earth. Nimrod is the first figure in the table given a personal character, not just a name in a list. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown; John Gill

9 COMMENTARY: He was a mighty hunter before the LORD, so that it became a proverb. JFB notes mighty hunter can mean a hunter of men as well as beasts: a founder of power through conquest, whose fame passed into saying. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown; John Gill

10 COMMENTARY: The beginning of his kingdom was Babel, Erech, Accad and Calneh, in the land of Shinar. Nimrod's kingdom starts in Mesopotamia, and Babel will become the centre of the next chapter's story. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown; John Gill

11 COMMENTARY: Out of that land went Asshur and built Nineveh, Rehoboth, Calah and Resen. The Assyrian cities show Nimrod's power spreading north, founding the great cities later known as Assyria's capitals. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown; John Gill

12 COMMENTARY: Resen is named as a great city lying between Nineveh and Calah. JFB notes it completes the Assyrian group founded from Nimrod's kingdom, showing that power's cities, not just its founder, spread north. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown; John Gill

15 COMMENTARY: Canaan fathered Sidon his firstborn, and Heth. The Canaanite list begins with the coastal city Sidon and the Hittites, peoples Israel will later meet in the promised land. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown; John Gill

20 COMMENTARY: These are the sons of Ham, after families, tongues, countries and nations. The summary closes Ham's section as it closed Japheth's: a people divided by kin, language and land. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown; John Gill

21 COMMENTARY: To Shem also, the elder brother of Japheth, children were born, and Shem is the father of all the children of Eber. Shem's line is introduced last and with honour, because the Hebrews, Eber's children, come through him. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown; John Gill

22 COMMENTARY: The children of Shem are Elam, Asshur, Arphaxad, Lud and Aram. These names point to peoples east and north of Mesopotamia, including Elam and Aram, known in later history. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown; John Gill

24 COMMENTARY: Arphaxad fathered Salah, and Salah fathered Eber. This short chain is the direct line toward Abraham: through Eber, the name Hebrew will later be traced. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown; John Gill

25 COMMENTARY: To Eber were born Peleg, for in his days the earth was divided, and his brother Joktan. JFB and Gill read the division as the scattering at Babel, so Peleg's name itself memorialises that event. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown; John Gill

31 COMMENTARY: These are the sons of Shem, after families, tongues, lands and nations. Shem's section closes with the same formula, completing the threefold table. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown; John Gill

32 COMMENTARY: These are the families of Noah's sons after their generations, in their nations, and from these the nations were divided on the earth after the flood. The chapter ends by stating its purpose: all nations after the flood descend from Noah's three sons. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown; John Gill

No note for verse(s) 3, 4, 7, 13, 14, 16, 17, 18, 19, 23, 26, 27, 28, 29, 30 — the public-domain sources consulted give no distinct explanation for these verses beyond the list itself, so nothing was invented for them.
