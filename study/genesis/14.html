Study Notes — Genesis

Chapter 14

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown, John Gill and Adam Clarke, all public-domain works, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Four eastern kings under Chedorlaomer made war on five kings of the plain, a clash of small princes of the kind common in that age, in which Lot's home would soon be caught. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: The war set the five kings of Sodom, Gomorrah, Admah, Zeboiim, and Bela against the four eastern kings, a clash of small princes of the kind common in that age. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: All these kings joined forces in the valley of Siddim, the low plain later covered by the salt sea, choosing it as the ground for their battle. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: The cities of the plain had served Chedorlaomer for twelve years and then rebelled in the thirteenth, so this war was a overlord coming to reclaim rebellious subjects. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: In the fourteenth year Chedorlaomer and his allies marched through the region, defeating the Rephaim, Zuzim, Emim, and Horites, clearing all opposition on their way to the plain. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: The campaign reached to the edge of the desert, showing the wide sweep of the eastern kings' power before they turned on Sodom and its allies. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: Turning back, the invaders struck En-mishpat and the Amalekites and Amorites, so that by the time they reached the plain they had already beaten every people in their path. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: The five kings went out to meet them in the valley of Siddim, choosing battle in the open rather than waiting behind their city walls. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: Four kings fought against five, and the smaller force won, a reminder that numbers alone do not decide battles when discipline and surprise are on the other side. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: The kings of Sodom and Gomorrah fled and fell into the tar pits, while the rest escaped to the mountains, leaving their cities open to plunder. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: The victors took all the goods and food of Sodom and Gomorrah and left, so the cities were stripped even before their later destruction. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: They also carried off Lot, Abram's nephew, with his goods, which turned a distant war into Abram's own business and called him to act. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: An escapee brought the news to Abram, who lived by Mamre's oaks among Amorite allies, and the report named him Abram the Hebrew, marking him out as a man apart from the peoples around him. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: On hearing that his kinsman was captive, Abram armed 318 trained men born in his own house and pursued as far as Dan, showing a household strong enough to field its own force. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: Abram divided his force and attacked by night, a stratagem JFB notes as typical of such warfare: a sudden rush from several sides in the dark throws an unsuspecting camp into panic. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: Abram recovered all the goods and brought back Lot with his goods, the women, and the people, so his rescue was complete and no one was left behind. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: On his return the king of Sodom came out to meet Abram in the King's Valley, to honour the deliverer and, as the next verses show, to bargain over the spoils. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

18 COMMENTARY: Melchizedek, king of Salem and priest of God Most High, brought out bread and wine, refreshing the weary victors and marking the meeting as sacred, not merely political. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

19 COMMENTARY: Melchizedek blessed Abram in the name of God Most High, possessor of heaven and earth, ascribing the victory to God rather than to Abram's sword. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

20 COMMENTARY: He blessed God for delivering Abram's enemies into his hand, and Abram gave him a tenth of all, honouring the priest as God's representative and refusing to keep the whole spoil for himself. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

21 COMMENTARY: The king of Sodom then proposed a bargain: the people to him, the goods to Abram, an offer that looked generous but would have left Abram enriched by Sodom. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

22 COMMENTARY: Abram answered with a lifted hand, a solemn oath, to God Most High, showing that his refusal was a matter of conscience before God, not mere bargaining. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

23 COMMENTARY: Abram would take nothing, not even a thread or a sandal strap, lest Sodom's king ever claim to have made him rich. His reward, Henry notes, must come from God alone. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

24 COMMENTARY: Abram made only two exceptions: what his men had already eaten, and the share due to his allies Aner, Eshcol, and Mamre, whose rights he would not sign away on their behalf. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
