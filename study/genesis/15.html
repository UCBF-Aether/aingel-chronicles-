Study Notes — Genesis

Chapter 15

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown, John Gill and Adam Clarke, all public-domain works, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: After the battle, God spoke to Abram in a vision, telling him not to fear and promising to be his shield and great reward, since Abram had refused Sodom's riches and might now fear revenge. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: Abram answered honestly that he was still childless, so what use were God's gifts while his household heir was Eliezer of Damascus, a servant rather than a son. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: Abram pressed the point: God had given him no offspring, so by the custom of the time a household servant stood to inherit, which seemed to close the promise rather than fulfil it. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: God answered plainly that Eliezer would not be the heir, but a son from Abram's own body would, narrowing the promise to a true son and not merely an adopted heir. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: God brought Abram outside to count the stars, promising offspring as countless as they were, and giving his faith something vast to look at when his own house looked empty. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: Abram believed God, and God counted that belief as righteousness. Henry and JFB stress this as the heart of the chapter: Abram was accepted by trusting God's word, not by any work of his own. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: God reminded Abram that he had brought him out of Ur to give him this land, grounding the future promise in a past deliverance Abram had already lived through. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: Abram asked how he would know he would inherit the land, not in unbelief, JFB notes, but seeking a confirming sign, and God graciously gave him one rather than rebuking the question. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: God directed Abram to prepare a heifer, a goat, a ram, a turtledove, and a pigeon, the animals used in solemn covenants, so that the promise would be sealed by a recognized rite. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: Abram cut the larger animals in half and laid the pieces opposite each other, as parties to a covenant passed between them, but left the birds whole, following the custom of the rite. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: Birds of prey swooped on the carcasses and Abram drove them away, a small act Henry reads as watchfulness: the sacrifice, and the promise it sealed, had to be guarded from spoiling. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: As the sun set, a deep sleep and a great horror of darkness fell on Abram, a solemn dread befitting a man about to hear hard truths about his descendants' future. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: God told Abram his offspring would be strangers and servants in a foreign land, afflicted for four hundred years, so the promise of the land came with a long warning of suffering first. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: God added that he would judge the oppressing nation and that Abram's people would leave with great wealth, so their bondage would end in deliverance, not in ruin. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: Abram himself, by contrast, would go to his fathers in peace and be buried in a good old age, spared the sight of the bondage that lay ahead for his children. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: His descendants would return in the fourth generation, because the iniquity of the Amorites was not yet full, showing God's patience even toward peoples marked for judgment. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: When darkness fell, a smoking furnace and a burning lamp passed between the pieces, and JFB notes that God alone passed through, binding himself to the promise while Abram, who was bound to nothing, only watched. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

18 COMMENTARY: That same day God made a covenant with Abram, giving his offspring the land from the river of Egypt to the Euphrates, a boundary far wider than Abram then possessed. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

No note for verse(s) 19, 20, 21 — the public-domain sources consulted give no distinct explanation for these verses beyond the list itself, so nothing was invented for them.
