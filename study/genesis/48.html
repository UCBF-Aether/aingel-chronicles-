Study Notes — Genesis

Chapter 48

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown, John Gill and Adam Clarke, all public-domain works, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Joseph is told his father is sick and takes his two sons Manasseh and Ephraim with him, so they may receive their dying grandfather's blessing. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: Jacob is told Joseph is coming, and Israel strengthens himself and sits up on the bed, gathering himself for a solemn act of blessing. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: Jacob recalls that God Almighty appeared to him at Luz in Canaan and blessed him, returning to a landmark of his history to engage Joseph's interest in the people of God rather than Egypt. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: He repeats the covenant promise, to be made fruitful and multiplied into a multitude, with the land given to his seed as an everlasting possession. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: Joseph's two sons, born in Egypt before Jacob came, are now claimed as Jacob's own, as Reuben and Simeon, so Joseph receives a double portion by adoption. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: Later children of Joseph will be his own and called by their brothers' names in their inheritance, a rule that settles future genealogies and explains why only certain names appear in the lists. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: Jacob recalls Rachel's death and roadside burial at Ephrath as he adopts Joseph's sons. Henry notes the dying patriarch calls his losses to mind to make death and the grave familiar, and that strong affection in enjoyment brings long affliction in loss. — Matthew Henry

8 COMMENTARY: Israel sees Joseph's sons and asks who they are, a question from dim eyes and from a father formally presenting the boys for blessing. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: Joseph answers that they are his sons, whom God has given him in Egypt, and Jacob asks that they be brought near so he may bless them. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: Israel's eyes are dim with age so he cannot see clearly, yet Joseph brings the boys near, and Jacob kisses and embraces them. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: Israel says he had not thought to see Joseph's face again, and God has shown him also Joseph's offspring, a double restoration beyond his hope. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: Joseph brings the boys from beside his knees and bows with his face to the earth, in reverence before the blessing his father is about to give. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: Joseph places Ephraim toward Israel's left hand and Manasseh toward his right, arranging them so the firstborn would receive the right-hand blessing by custom. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: Israel stretches out his right hand to Ephraim the younger and his left to Manasseh, guiding his hands wittingly, literally making his hands wise, and crossing them on purpose. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: He blesses Joseph, invoking the God before whom Abraham and Isaac walked, the God who has fed Jacob all his life long, so the blessing rests on a lifetime of provision. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: He calls on the Angel who redeemed him from all evil to bless the lads, an Angel Jacob himself identifies with God Almighty, whose name is repeated three times in the blessing. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: Joseph is displeased when he sees the right hand on Ephraim, and tries to lift his father's hand to Manasseh's head, thinking dim eyes have caused a mistake. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

18 COMMENTARY: Joseph explains to his father that Manasseh is the firstborn and asks him to put his right hand on his head, appealing to birth order. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

19 COMMENTARY: Jacob refuses, saying he knows it, Manasseh also shall become a people and be great, but his younger brother shall be greater, and his seed a multitude of nations, a prediction later verified in Ephraim's might. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

20 COMMENTARY: He blesses them that day with the formula, God make thee as Ephraim and as Manasseh, so their prosperity becomes the proverbial wish for others, and he sets Ephraim before Manasseh. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

21 COMMENTARY: Israel tells Joseph he is dying, but God will be with them and bring them back to the land of their fathers, speaking of death with composure and of restoration with certainty. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

22 COMMENTARY: He gives Joseph one portion above his brothers, a ridge of land near Shechem, which JFB thinks Jacob recovered from the Amorites by sword and bow and later became Joseph's burial place. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
