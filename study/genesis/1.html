Study Notes — Genesis

Chapter 1

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown, John Gill and Adam Clarke, all public-domain works, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: The whole universe had a beginning, and God alone made it from nothing. Henry stresses that this first truth answers every claim that the world made itself or always existed. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: At first the earth was a dark, formless, watery waste. JFB pictures the Spirit brooding over it, like a bird over eggs, ordering what was dead and disordered into a world fit for life. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: God's command alone brought light, clearing the gloom that covered the waters. Henry notes that light came by God's word before the sun is named, showing creation obeys his voice, not nature alone. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: God separated light from darkness, setting up their regular alternation. JFB explains this as day following night in order, not as a battle between two powers. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: The first day is counted from evening to morning, the ancient way of reckoning a day. This frames each day as a complete, ordered period under God's direction. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: The firmament is the expanse of sky and air set in the midst of the waters. JFB explains it as the atmosphere that lifts clouds above and leaves waters below. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: God divided the waters above from those below by that expanse. The point is order: waters are given their place, not left as a threatening chaos. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: God named the expanse Heaven, the visible sky above the earth. Naming marks ownership and purpose: the sky is part of God's ordered creation, not a god itself. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: The waters below were gathered into one place so dry land could appear. JFB describes seas, lakes and rivers forming as the waters rushed into hollows left for them. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: God named the dry land Earth and the gathered waters Seas, and saw that it was good. Henry notes that what God orders and names is fit for the life he will place on it. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: The earth was made to sprout plants, grasses and trees, each yielding seed after its kind. JFB stresses they grew at once by divine power, not by slow farming, and reproduce true to their kind. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: The land produced vegetation as commanded, each kind true to itself. The orderly fruitfulness of plants is presented as God's provision, built into creation from the start. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: The third day closes with land, sea and plants all in place. The sequence matters: a home is prepared before its inhabitants arrive. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: Lights were set in the sky to divide day from night and mark seasons, days and years. Their job is to measure time and give light, not to rule human fate. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: Those lights also serve as lamps for the earth. Henry notes they are instruments under God, useful servants rather than objects of worship. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: The sun is the greater light for the day and the moon the great light for the night, with the stars also made. JFB explains the moon counts as great because of its visible rule of the night, even though its light is borrowed. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: God placed these lights in the sky to shine on the earth. Their position is deliberate: they are set where they can serve the world below. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

18 COMMENTARY: They rule day and night and divide light from darkness. Henry calls them deputy rulers under God, whose best rule is simply giving light and doing good. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

19 COMMENTARY: The fourth day closes the ordering of the heavens. Sun, moon and stars now keep the rhythm of time that human life will live by. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

20 COMMENTARY: The waters and sky were filled with living creatures, from small swarming life to great sea creatures and birds. JFB notes their rapid increase shows life made to thrive in its own element. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

21 COMMENTARY: God created the great sea creatures and every kind of bird and sea life. No creature, however large or strange, lies outside God's making. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

22 COMMENTARY: God blessed sea and sky creatures to be fruitful and multiply and fill their realms. Blessing here is God's gift of life and increase, not mere permission. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

23 COMMENTARY: The fifth day closes with waters and air teeming with life. The world is no longer empty: it is filling exactly as God commanded. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

24 COMMENTARY: The land was commanded to bring forth cattle, creeping things and wild beasts, each after its kind. JFB groups land animals into tame, wild and creeping kinds, all made in one day's work. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

25 COMMENTARY: God made each kind of land animal and saw that it was good. Each creature is suited to its own way of life, which Henry takes as evidence of God's wisdom in fitting means to ends. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

26 COMMENTARY: Man was made last, by a special counsel, in God's image and likeness, to rule the other creatures. JFB stresses the image lies not in body or mere mind, but in moral uprightness: knowledge, righteousness and true holiness. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

27 COMMENTARY: God created humanity male and female in his own image. The image is shared by both, and the verse repeats the point to show its weight: people bear a dignity no other creature carries. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

28 COMMENTARY: God blessed humanity to multiply, fill the earth, subdue it and rule over sea, sky and land creatures. Henry and JFB read this as stewardship: rule under God, not ownership apart from him. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

29 COMMENTARY: God gave seed-bearing plants and fruit trees as food for people. Provision comes with creation itself: humanity is fed from what God has already made fruitful. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

30 COMMENTARY: Green plants were likewise given as food to beasts, birds and creeping things. The original order is one of provision for all living things, without predation named here. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

31 COMMENTARY: God saw all he had made and called it very good, closing the sixth day. Henry contrasts this with our own works: God can look back on his work without shame, because it is exactly as he intended. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
