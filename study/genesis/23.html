Study Notes — Genesis

Chapter 23

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown, John Gill and Adam Clarke, all public-domain works, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Sarah lived 127 years; JFB notes she is the only woman in Scripture whose age, death and burial are all recorded, an honour to the mother of the Hebrew people. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: Sarah died at Kiriath-arba, that is Hebron, and Abraham came to mourn and weep for her, first sitting in customary mourning and then giving way to natural grief. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: Abraham rose from before his dead and spoke to the sons of Heth, seeking their help to obtain a burial place, since his pilgrim life had left him owning no grave plot. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: Abraham described himself as a stranger and sojourner among them, asking for a burial possession so he could bury his dead out of his sight. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: The children of Heth answered Abraham courteously, ready to hear his request. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: They called Abraham a mighty prince among them and offered him the choice of their tombs, saying none would withhold his sepulchre, a generous public welcome. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: Abraham stood and bowed to the people of the land, showing respectful courtesy in return before stating his particular request. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: Abraham asked them to intercede with Ephron son of Zohar on his behalf, so that the negotiation would come through a respected intermediary. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: Abraham asked specifically for the cave of Machpelah at the end of Ephron's field, offering to pay its full worth for a permanent burial possession. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: Ephron was sitting among the Hittites at the city gate, where all business was transacted, and answered Abraham in the hearing of all who entered the gate. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: Ephron offered Abraham both field and cave as a gift before witnesses, a show of Eastern generosity that, JFB warns, still expected a costly return gift. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: Abraham bowed again before the people of the land, acknowledging the offer with the same public courtesy. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: Abraham insisted on paying money for the field, asking Ephron to accept payment so that his burial of Sarah would rest on clear ownership, not on favour. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: Ephron answered Abraham, beginning to name terms after the offer of a gift had been politely set aside. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: Ephron named the land at four hundred shekels of silver, adding that such a sum was a trifle between them, a polite form that still stated a real price. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: Abraham agreed to Ephron's price and weighed out four hundred shekels of silver, current merchant money, in the hearing of the sons of Heth. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: The field at Machpelah before Mamre, with its cave and all its trees within its borders, was thus made over to Abraham in a formal, witnessed transfer. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

18 COMMENTARY: The purchase was confirmed to Abraham as a possession before all who went in at the gate of Ephron's city, so the title was public and beyond dispute. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

19 COMMENTARY: Abraham then buried Sarah in the cave of Machpelah before Mamre, that is Hebron, the only spot of ground he owned in the promised land. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

20 COMMENTARY: Field and cave were made sure to Abraham by the sons of Heth as a burial possession, securing a family grave for generations to come. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
