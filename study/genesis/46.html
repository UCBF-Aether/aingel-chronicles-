Study Notes — Genesis

Chapter 46

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown, John Gill and Adam Clarke, all public-domain works, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: Israel sets out with all he has and comes to Beer-sheba, the covenant spot of Abraham and Isaac on the direct route to Egypt, where he pauses to seek God's guidance before leaving Canaan. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: God speaks to Israel in visions of the night, a virtual renewal of the covenant that answers Jacob's prayer and removes his doubts about going down. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: God tells Jacob not to fear going to Egypt, for there he will be made a great nation, a promise fulfilled when seventy souls grew into an immense multitude. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: God promises to go down with Jacob and surely bring him up again, a promise realised for Jacob himself in his burial in Canaan and for his posterity in their return, while Joseph will close his eyes. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: Jacob rises from Beer-sheba and his sons carry him, with wives and little ones, in Pharaoh's wagons, the aged patriarch conveyed by slow stages while his sons manage the journey. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: They take their cattle and their goods, meaning their substance and precious things, not mere furniture, and come into Egypt, Jacob and all his seed with him. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: Sons, grandsons, daughters, and granddaughters are all brought with him, the daughters-in-law likely included in the general account of the emigrants. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: The list that follows is introduced as the names of Israel's children who came into Egypt, and JFB explains it as heads of families at the time of immigration, not only those born in Canaan. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: Reuben's sons are listed, and JFB notes Reuben seems to have had only two sons before the second grain journey, so the later sons must have been born in Egypt. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: Simeon's sons are listed, including Shaul, son of a Canaanite woman, and JFB notes variant names for Jemuel and Zohar and that Ohad's line later disappears from the tribal lists. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: Judah's sons are listed, with Er and Onan noted as dead in Canaan and Pharez's sons Hezron and Hamul added, whom JFB reads as a parenthetical note supplying heads of families in place of the dead. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: Issachar's sons are listed, and JFB notes the variant forms Pua for Phuvah and Jashub for Job in the later tribal reckoning. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: Leah's sons, with Dinah, are summed as thirty-three souls, and JFB notes Dinah and Serah are the only women named, perhaps because Dinah became housekeeper after Jacob's wives died. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: Gad's sons are listed, and JFB notes the variant forms Zephon for Ziphion and Ozni for Ezbon in the later lists. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: Asher's sons are listed, with Serah their sister, and JFB notes the rhyming names and that Beriah's sons Heber and Malchiel appear as heads of families. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

20 COMMENTARY: Joseph's sons Manasseh and Ephraim, born in Egypt to Asenath, are classed in this catalogue as heads of families, though born outside Canaan. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

21 COMMENTARY: Benjamin's ten names are listed, and JFB argues they include grandsons and a great-grandson, since Benjamin, at most about twenty-two, cannot have had ten sons born in Canaan. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

23 COMMENTARY: Dan's son Hushim is listed, with the variant form Shuham in the later tribal list. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

26 COMMENTARY: All the souls that came with Jacob, besides his sons' wives, are counted as sixty-six, a broad reckoning that includes grandsons under the name of sons, since the migration is a new epoch in Israel's history. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

27 COMMENTARY: With Joseph's two sons the whole house of Jacob is seventy souls, including Jacob, Joseph, and his sons, and JFB notes the preponderance of sons helped guard the family against Canaanite intermarriage. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

28 COMMENTARY: Judah is sent ahead to Joseph to direct the way to Goshen, a prudent step both to inform the king of so large a company and to receive Joseph's guidance to their settlement. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

29 COMMENTARY: Joseph makes ready his horse-drawn chariot, fitting his public dignity, and goes up to Goshen to meet his father, falling on his neck and weeping a good while. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

30 COMMENTARY: Israel says he can now die in peace, since he has seen Joseph's face and knows he is alive, the happiness of a father reaching its height. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

31 COMMENTARY: Joseph tells his brothers he will go up and inform Pharaoh of their arrival, a tribute of respect due to the king, with instructions worthy of a brother and a religious man. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

32 COMMENTARY: He will describe them as shepherds who have brought their flocks, herds, and all they have, stating their occupation plainly rather than concealing it. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

33 COMMENTARY: Joseph anticipates that Pharaoh will call them and ask their occupation, and he coaches them in advance so their answer will be ready and consistent. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

34 COMMENTARY: They are to answer that their occupation has been with livestock from youth, both they and their fathers, so they may dwell in Goshen apart from Egyptian society, since every shepherd is an abomination to the Egyptians. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

No note for verse(s) 11, 14, 18, 19, 22, 24, 25 — the public-domain sources consulted give no distinct explanation for these verses beyond the list itself, so nothing was invented for them.
