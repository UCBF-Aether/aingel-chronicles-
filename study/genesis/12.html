Study Notes — Genesis

Chapter 12

Everything below is COMMENTARY on the chapter — it explains the verses; it is not Scripture, and it never changes the Scripture text. Notes are condensed from Matthew Henry, Jamieson-Fausset-Brown, John Gill and Adam Clarke, all public-domain works, and each note names its source.

Read the chapter itself: Standard · Modern

1 COMMENTARY: God's call tested whether Abram loved God more than his native soil, his kindred, and his father's house, and whether he would trust God to show him a land he had not yet seen. Henry notes the call came first in Ur, before Abram lived in Haran. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

2 COMMENTARY: God promised to make Abram a great nation, to bless him, and to make his name great, which was a particular comfort to a childless man, since his greatness would come through God's gift, not his own family strength. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

3 COMMENTARY: God bound himself to Abram's cause, blessing those who blessed him and opposing those who cursed him, and crowned the promise with blessing for all families of the earth through him, which Henry reads as pointing to Christ. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

4 COMMENTARY: Abram obeyed at once, without dispute, leaving Haran at seventy-five, an age when most would seek rest. Lot went with him, drawn by his uncle's example. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

5 COMMENTARY: Abram took Sarai, Lot, all their goods, and the people they had gathered in Haran, and pressed on to Canaan itself, not stopping short as the earlier journey from Ur had done. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

6 COMMENTARY: Abram passed through the land to Shechem, but found Canaanites already in possession, so even in the promised land he lived as a guest by permission, learning to live by faith rather than by present possession. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

7 COMMENTARY: God appeared to Abram in Canaan and renewed the promise of the land to his offspring, and Abram answered with worship, building an altar where God had met him. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

8 COMMENTARY: Abram moved on to the hill country between Bethel and Ai, pitched his tent there, and built another altar and called on God's name, marking each stage of his journey with open worship. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

9 COMMENTARY: Abram kept journeying southward, still unsettled, which Henry reads as the lot of God's people in this world: strangers and pilgrims, moving on from strength to strength. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

10 COMMENTARY: A severe famine in Canaan drove Abram to Egypt, where food was plentiful. The commentators note the trial: the land of promise itself could not yet feed him, and faith had to survive want. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

11 COMMENTARY: As they neared Egypt, Abram spoke to Sarai about her beauty, showing that his fear of danger there was already working on his mind before any threat had appeared. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

12 COMMENTARY: Abram feared the Egyptians would kill him to take Sarai, a fear that reveals how his trust, so firm in leaving home, wavered when his own life seemed at stake. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

13 COMMENTARY: Abram asked Sarai to say she was his sister, which was partly true of their kinship but was meant to hide their marriage. Henry calls this his fault: a device of fear rather than honest trust in God's protection. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

14 COMMENTARY: On entering Egypt, Sarai's beauty was noticed at once, just as Abram had feared, and the danger he had tried to manage by his own plan now fell on her instead. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

15 COMMENTARY: Pharaoh's princes praised Sarai to the king, and she was taken into his house, which put her in real peril and showed how Abram's scheme had endangered the very promise of an heir. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

16 COMMENTARY: Pharaoh treated Abram well for Sarai's sake, giving him livestock and servants, so Abram grew rich by the very deceit that had put his wife at risk, an uneasy prosperity Henry notes without excusing. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

17 COMMENTARY: God struck Pharaoh's house with plagues, protecting Sarai when Abram's plan could not, and thereby exposing the truth and guarding the promised line. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

18 COMMENTARY: Pharaoh confronted Abram about the deception, showing that even a pagan king could see the wrong of it, and Abram had no defence to offer for what he had done. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

19 COMMENTARY: Pharaoh's question pressed the point home: by calling Sarai his sister, Abram had led the king to take another man's wife, and only God's intervention had kept the wrong from going further. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown

20 COMMENTARY: Pharaoh sent Abram away under guard with his wife and all he had, a merciful dismissal rather than punishment, and a humbling end to Abram's first venture outside the promised land. — Matthew Henry; Jamieson-Fausset-Brown
