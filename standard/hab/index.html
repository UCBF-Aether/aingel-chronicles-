HABAKKUK

Habakkuk

Habakkuk is a conversation, not just a sermon, and it lasts only 3 chapters. The prophet looks at violence and wrongdoing in his own land and asks God plainly: how long will you let this stand? God's answer — that a proud empire will be used, and then itself judged — troubles him even more, and he asks again. The book closes not with explanations but with a prayer and a song: the prophet chooses to rejoice even if the fields fail and the stalls stand empty.

In this edition Habakkuk is printed from the King James Version of 1611, the public-domain text as received. It belongs in this canon among the Twelve Prophets, where the Ethiopian Church keeps the full chorus of shorter prophetic voices beside Isaiah, Jeremiah, and Ezekiel. Few books give readers more honest permission to bring hard questions to God — and to stay in the conversation.

Watch for the watchtower scene in chapter 2, where Habakkuk stands still and waits to hear what God will say.
