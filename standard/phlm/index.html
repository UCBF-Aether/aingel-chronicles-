THE EPISTLE TO PHILEMON

Philemon

Philemon is the shortest of Paul's letters here, a single chapter naming Paul, a prisoner, as sender. He writes to Philemon, a believer in Colossae, about Onesimus, Philemon's runaway slave who had become a Christian through Paul's ministry. Instead of commanding, Paul appeals in love: receive Onesimus back no longer as a slave but as a beloved brother, and charge any debt to Paul's own account. It is a masterclass in gentle persuasion and reconciliation. In this edition the text is the King James Version of 1611, printed as received from the public domain. This tiny letter stands in this canon among Paul's writings, kept by the Ethiopian Church, because it shows the gospel quietly transforming a household relationship from the inside. Read it slowly; almost every sentence carries tact, humor, and affection. Watch for Paul's promise to repay and his hope of being released and visiting soon.
