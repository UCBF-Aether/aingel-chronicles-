THE EPISTLE TO THE ROMANS

Romans

Romans is a letter in 16 chapters, presented as the apostle Paul's writing to the church in Rome, a community he had not yet visited. It is the most carefully argued book in the New Testament: all people, Jew and Gentile alike, stand in need of mercy; God makes people right through faith in Jesus; a new life follows in the Spirit; and God's promises to Israel have not failed. Its final chapters turn practical, calling believers to love, humility, patience with one another, and honest life in the wider world.

In this edition Romans is printed from the King James Version of 1611, the public-domain text as received. It stands in this canon at the head of Paul's letters, where the Ethiopian Church, like the wider church, places it first — not because it was written first, but because it sums up the message so fully.

Watch for the turning point in chapter 12 — after eleven chapters of mercy, one small word, 'therefore,' changes everything into daily life.
