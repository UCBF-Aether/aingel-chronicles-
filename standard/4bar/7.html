4 Baruch

Chapter 7

1 And Baruch arose and went forth from the tomb, and found the eagle sitting without.

2 And the eagle said unto him: Hail, Baruch, steward of the faith. And Baruch said unto him: For thou art chosen above all the fowls of heaven, that thou speakest; by the brightness of thine eyes it is manifest; and now shew me what thou doest here.

3 And the eagle said unto him: I am sent hither, that thou mayest send by me every word which thou wilt.

4 And Baruch said unto him: Canst thou indeed bring this word unto Jeremiah in Babylon? And the eagle said unto him: For this was I sent.

5 And Baruch took the book, and fifteen figs from the basket of figs which Abimelech brought, and bound them upon the neck of the eagle.

6 And he said: Unto thee I speak, O eagle, king of all birds: go in peace and in safety; carry the message for me; and be not like the raven which Noah sent forth, and it returned not again unto him; but be like the dove which the third time brought word unto Noah. So do thou also take these good words unto Jeremiah, and unto them that are with him, the Israelites, that it may be well with thee; and take this gladness unto the people, the chosen of God.

7 And if all the birds surround thee, and all the enemies of righteousness, desiring to kill thee, be strong; and the Lord shall give thee strength. And turn not aside to the right nor to the left, but as a flying arrow go straight; go in the strength of God.

8 And when Baruch had said this, the eagle flew with the book, and went into Babylon; and rested upon a post without the city, in a desert place; and abode there until Jeremiah passed by, and some of the people.

9 And they passed by there to bury a man that was dead; for Jeremiah had asked Nebuchadnezzar, saying: Give me a place where I may bury [my dead] of my people; and he gave it him.

10 And as they went weeping for him that was dead, they came before that eagle; and the eagle cried with a loud voice, saying: Unto thee I speak, Jeremiah, chosen of God: go and gather all the people, and bring them hither, that they may hear the good message which I have brought.

11 And when he heard this, he glorified God; and then he gathered all the people, and their wives, and their children, and they came where the eagle was.

12 And that eagle came down upon the corpse, and it revived and lived; and this he did that they might believe. And all the people marvelled because of that which was done.

13 And they said: Of a truth this is God, who appeared unto our fathers in the wilderness by Moses; and in the likeness of an eagle he appeared unto us, as it were a great eagle.

14 And the eagle said unto Jeremiah, saying: Come, loose this book, and read it unto the people; and he read it unto them.

15 And when the people heard it, they wept all together, and cast ashes upon their heads; and they said unto Jeremiah: Save us; what shall we do, that we may enter into our city?

16 And Jeremiah arose and said unto them: All that ye have heard in the book, so do; and he shall bring you into your city.

17 And Jeremiah wrote a book unto Baruch, saying thus: My beloved son, be not weary in prayer, beseeching God for us; that he may lighten our way, until we come out from under the command of this sinful king.

18 And thou art found righteous before God, who suffered thee not to come with us, that thou shouldest not see the evil that is done unto them, unto the people in Babylon.

19 As a father who hath an only son, and he is delivered up to be punished; and they that are with his father, even they that comfort him, cover their faces, that they may not see the father, who is consumed with grief.

20 Even so God had pity on thee, and suffered thee not to come to Babylon, that thou shouldest not see the affliction of the people.

21 For since we came into this land, we have had no rest from grief until this day, sixty and six years this day; when we go forth we find of the people some hanged by Nebuchadnezzar the king, weeping and saying: Have mercy upon us, O god of Sor.

22 And when I heard this word, I mourned and wept, because the hanged called upon a strange god, saying: Have mercy upon us. And again I remembered the feasts which we kept in Jerusalem, before we were taken captive; and when I remembered, I entered into my house, crying and weeping.

23 And now make supplication unto our God, where ye are, thou and Abimelech, for the people, that they may hearken unto my voice and unto the word of my mouth, that they may go out from Persia.

24 And now I tell thee: all the time that we have abode here, they have held us, saying: Sing us new songs from the hymns of Zion, the song of your God. And we say unto them: How shall we sing unto you, while we are in a strange land?

25 And when Jeremiah had thus written, he bound his book upon the neck of the eagle, and said unto him: Go in peace, and may the Lord keep thee.

26 And the eagle went and flew, and brought the book unto Baruch; and Baruch took it and read the book, and wept when he heard of the afflictions of the people and their tribulations.

27 And Jeremiah took those figs, and gave them unto the sick that were among the people; and abode teaching them, that they should not do the deeds of the people of Babylon.
