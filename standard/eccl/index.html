Ecclesiastes

Ecclesiastes is the Bible's most honest book about disappointment. Its speaker, the Preacher — tradition credits Solomon, writing as a king who had tried everything — looks at wealth, work, pleasure, and power and calls them vanity: a breath, a chasing after wind. Over 12 chapters he circles the same hard questions. Is anything lasting? Does wisdom actually help? Why do the wicked prosper? He never lands on easy answers, but he does land somewhere: enjoy the simple gifts in front of you, and remember God, because God will judge. The text here is the King James Version, printed as received. Ecclesiastes stands in every major canon, including the Ethiopian canon this edition follows, and its place was debated even in ancient times — some rabbis questioned it. It stayed because its honesty is itself a kind of wisdom. Watch for the famous poem in chapter 3 — a time to be born, and a time to die — and for how the bleakest book in the Bible still ends in reverence.
