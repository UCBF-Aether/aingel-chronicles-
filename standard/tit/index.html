THE EPISTLE TO TITUS

Titus

Titus is a short personal letter naming Paul as sender, written to Titus, his coworker organizing the young churches on the island of Crete. Paul tells him to appoint elders of blameless character, silence deceitful teachers, and teach each group in the household how sound doctrine shows itself in daily conduct. The engine of it all is grace: the kindness of God has appeared, teaching believers to live soberly, righteously, and godly while awaiting Christ's appearing. In this edition the text is the King James Version of 1611, printed as received from the public domain. The Ethiopian Church keeps it in this canon with the other pastoral letters to Timothy, and its three chapters make a compact manual of church life and good works. Watch for the two great appearing passages that frame Christian hope and holy living.
