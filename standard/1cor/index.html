THE FIRST EPISTLE TO THE CORINTHIANS

1 Corinthians

First Corinthians is a letter from the apostle Paul to the young church in Corinth, a busy port city full of talent, pride, and problems. The letter itself names Paul as its sender, and he writes like a pastor who has heard troubling reports: the believers are splitting into factions, tolerating serious wrongdoing, arguing over food, worship, and gifts of the Spirit, and even doubting the resurrection. Paul answers question by question, correcting gently at times and sharply at others, always calling them back to love and to the cross. In this edition the text is the King James Version of 1611, printed as received from the public domain. The letter belongs in this canon because the Ethiopian Church, like churches everywhere, kept Paul's letters as Scripture for teaching the whole people of God. Read it as practical wisdom for a real congregation, not abstract theory. Watch for the great chapter on love and the long, careful defense of the resurrection near the end.
