OBADIAH

Obadiah

Obadiah is the shortest book in the Old Testament — a single chapter, twenty-one verses — and it is aimed at one nation: Edom, Israel's own brother. The Edomites were descendants of Esau, Jacob's twin, and when Babylon destroyed Jerusalem, they did not help. They stood aside, gloated, looted, and handed over refugees. Obadiah's whole message is God's answer to that betrayal: pride will be brought down, violence done to a brother will return on the doer's own head, and Mount Zion will be restored. Small as it is, the book carries a permanent warning about standing by while family suffers. The text here is the King James Version, printed as received. Obadiah stands among the twelve minor prophets in the Ethiopian canon this edition follows, as in other canons — proof that the canon keeps even a one-chapter voice when the voice is true. Watch for how much weight those twenty-one verses carry: no book this short should be skipped, because its charge — you should not have looked on your brother's day — still lands.
