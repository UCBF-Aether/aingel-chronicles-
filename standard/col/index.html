THE EPISTLE TO THE COLOSSIANS

Colossians

Colossians is a letter naming Paul as sender, written to believers in Colossae, a church Paul had not visited himself. Word had reached him of teaching that diminished Christ by adding rituals, visions, and strict rules about food and days. Paul answers by exalting Christ above everything: the image of the invisible God, creator and sustainer of all things, and head of the church. Then he shows what the new life looks like at home and at work. In this edition the text is the King James Version of 1611, printed as received from the public domain. The Ethiopian Church preserves it in this canon alongside Paul's other letters, a compact guard against anything that would shrink Christ. Its four chapters pair naturally with Ephesians, its close companion in theme. Watch for the great hymn to Christ's supremacy and the clothing imagery of the new self.
