THE SINODOS

Sinodos

The Sinodos is Ethiopia's great church-order collection in thirty-seven chapters — practical teaching for how the church should live, worship, ordain, discipline, and care for its people. In its Ethiopian form it gathers four parts: Sirate Tsion, the Order of Zion; Tizaz, the Commandments; Gitsew; and Abtilis. Together they carry the apostles' ordering of the church into Ethiopian life, covering bishops, priests, deacons, widows, prayer, fasting, and the settling of disputes. This is not theory; it is the working rulebook of a living church.

For this edition it was translated anew, directly from the Ge'ez, using the Ge'ez text printed by G. Horner in The Statutes of the Apostles in 1904. Horner's own English served only as a check on difficult passages. Our route was Horner, not the later edition you may see cited elsewhere. The Sinodos belongs in this canon because the Ethiopian Church counts its church-order books as Scripture, shaping daily faithfulness, not only belief.

Watch for how concrete the instructions are — holiness here has names, times, duties, and care for the poor.
