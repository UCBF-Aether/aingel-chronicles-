ZENA AYHUD

Josippon

Josippon is a history of the Jewish people in 104 chapters, from Adam through the Second Temple and its fall. It began as a medieval Hebrew work tradition credits to Joseph ben Gorion, retelling biblical history alongside accounts drawn from older historians, and the Ethiopian Church took it into its own canon in Ge'ez dress, as the Zena Ayhud, with a Christian redaction and a discourse appendix in chapters 78 to 104.

In this edition Josippon was translated anew from the Ge'ez, from the codex BL EAP704/2/3, copied in 1595–1605, with the appendix rendered anew here from that Ge'ez text. The chronicle itself has existed in English since 1558, so no new claim is made for it. Three short passages the base Ge'ez witness has physically lost — chapter 7, verses 12–14; chapter 84, verses 21–35; and chapter 104, verses 14–19 — are honestly absent here, never reconstructed.

Watch for how history itself becomes teaching here — kings rise and fall, but the story keeps returning to faithfulness.
