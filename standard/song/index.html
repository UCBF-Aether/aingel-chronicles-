THE SONG OF SOLOMON

Song of Songs

The Song of Songs is a love poem — eight chapters of lovers calling to each other across gardens, vineyards, and city streets, praising each other's beauty in rich, earthy images of spices, doves, and spring rain. There is almost no law, history, or doctrine in it; it simply celebrates desire, longing, and delight as good gifts. Tradition credits Solomon, whose name appears in its opening line, and readers across the centuries have also heard in it a picture of God's love for his people, and of Christ's love for the church. The text here is the King James Version, printed as received. The Song stands in the Ethiopian canon this edition follows, as it does in Jewish and other Christian canons. It belongs because the canon makes room for joy as well as instruction — love poetry is scripture too. Watch for the refrain that runs through it — do not stir up love until it pleases — and for the fierce claim near the end that love is strong as death.
