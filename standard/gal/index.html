THE EPISTLE TO THE GALATIANS

Galatians

Galatians is Paul's urgent letter to churches in Galatia, where teachers were insisting that Gentile believers must follow the law of Moses to be fully accepted by God. The letter names Paul as its sender, and he writes with unusual heat, telling his own conversion story, recounting his meeting with the Jerusalem apostles, and arguing from Abraham that people are made right with God by faith in Christ, not by works of the law. He closes with the fruit of the Spirit and a call to carry one another's burdens. In this edition the text is the King James Version of 1611, printed as received from the public domain. It belongs in this canon as one of Paul's foundational letters, kept by the Ethiopian Church with the whole apostolic witness. First-time readers will find its argument direct and its passion impossible to miss. Watch for the sharp moment when Paul confronts Peter face to face at Antioch.
