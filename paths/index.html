Reading Paths

The whole canon is a country. These trails are three ways to walk it — each stop is a door, with one line about why it is worth opening.

The Hidden Books in 14 Days · Meeting Jesus · For the Skeptic

The Hidden Books in 14 Days

Fourteen days, one hidden book a day — the Scriptures Ethiopia kept when many others let them go.

  1. Day 1 — Tobit (whole book)Start gently: a faithful family in exile and a journey with a companion who is more than he seems — a warm doorway into the books beyond the familiar 66.
  2. Day 2 · 1 Enoch, highlights: 1 Enoch 1 — blessing and woesEnoch opens by blessing the righteous and warning the wicked — hear the voice Jude and the early church knew well.
  3. Day 2 · 1 Enoch, highlights: 1 Enoch 6 — the fall of the WatchersHere begins the famous story of the Watchers who came down — the chapter behind so much later tradition about fallen angels.
  4. Day 2 · 1 Enoch, highlights: 1 Enoch 12 — Enoch sent to the WatchersEnoch is sent as a scribe and messenger to the fallen Watchers — intercession, even for angels who fell.
  5. Day 2 · 1 Enoch, highlights: 1 Enoch 72 — the Book of LuminariesThe Book of Luminaries opens here, mapping the sun and moon — ancient Israel's calendar written in the sky.
  6. Day 3 · Jubilees, highlights: Jubilees 1 — Moses on the mountainAn angel retells all of history to Moses, divided into jubilees — Genesis, but with the calendar and the covenants in full view.
  7. Day 3 · Jubilees, highlights: Jubilees 2 — creation retoldCreation again, slower and more ordered — angels, Sabbath, and a world made with intention.
  8. Day 3 · Jubilees, highlights: Jubilees 6 — Noah and the calendarAfter the flood, covenant and calendar come together — why the feasts fall when they fall, according to this tradition.
  9. Day 3 · Jubilees, highlights: Jubilees 23 — toward the latter daysA sobering, hopeful look down the generations toward the latter days — Jubilees at its most prophetic.
  10. Day 4 — Judith (whole book)A widow, a besieged town, and courage nobody expected — one of the great adventure stories of the wider canon.
  11. Day 5 — 2 Esdras (whole book)Ezra argues with an angel about suffering, evil, and the end of the age — some of the most honest questioning in all Scripture.
  12. Day 6 — Esther, the fuller book (whole book)The Esther you may know, plus the Greek additions this canon carries as chapters 11–17 — prayers and dreams the shorter form leaves out.
  13. Day 7 · Daniel 13 — SusannaA courtroom story of integrity and a young Daniel who cross-examines the liars — the Daniel chapter many Bibles do not carry.
  14. Day 7 · Daniel 14 — Bel and the DragonDaniel exposes a hollow idol and faces a dragon — wit, courage, and a very practical kind of faith.
  15. Day 8 — Baruch (whole book)Jeremiah's scribe speaks to a people in exile: confession, comfort, and wisdom — chapter 6 is the Letter of Jeremiah.
  16. Day 8 — 4 Baruch (whole book)Baruch again, after Jerusalem falls — translated anew from the Ge'ez for this edition, grieving and hoping at once.
  17. Day 9 — Wisdom of Solomon (whole book)Kings are told to love wisdom, and Wisdom herself is praised as breath of God's power — poetry for rulers and ordinary readers alike.
  18. Day 10 · Sirach, highlights: Sirach 1 — the beginning of wisdomSirach opens where Proverbs does: all wisdom is from the Lord, and reverence is where it starts.
  19. Day 10 · Sirach, highlights: Sirach 24 — Wisdom praises herselfWisdom speaks in her own voice, seeking a home among the peoples — one of the most beautiful chapters in the wider canon.
  20. Day 10 · Sirach, highlights: Sirach 44 — praise of the faithful who came before'Let us now praise famous men' — a roll of honour from Enoch onward, written to steady a new generation.
  21. Day 10 · Sirach, highlights: Sirach 51 — a closing prayerThe book closes in thanksgiving from a man who sought wisdom young and found her — a fitting last word.
  22. Day 11 · 1 Meqabyan, highlights: 1 Meqabyan 1 — Ethiopia's Maccabees beginNot the Greek Maccabees — Ethiopia's own book of kings, idolatry, and refusal to bow, translated anew from the Ge'ez.
  23. Day 11 · 1 Meqabyan, highlights: 1 Meqabyan 18 — the cost comes into focusHalfway in, faithfulness gets expensive — read this as the turning heart of the book.
  24. Day 11 · 1 Meqabyan, highlights: 1 Meqabyan 36 — the closing chapterFinish the arc and feel how this book lands its call to stand firm — the other Meqabyan books wait for you in the full canon.
  25. Day 12 · Josippon, highlights: Josippon 1 — a history opensEthiopia's great history of the Jewish people opens — a whole people's story, carried in Ge'ez and translated anew for this edition.
  26. Day 12 · Josippon, highlights: Josippon 37 — deep in the storySample the middle of the chronicle and get the feel of its voice — part history, part sermon, wholly earnest.
  27. Day 12 · Josippon, highlights: Josippon 84 — history turns to teachingLate in the book the chronicle gives way to discourse — a good place to see Josippon's second register.
  28. Day 12 · Josippon, highlights: Josippon 104 — the final chapterRead the book's ending as it survives in the manuscript — Josippon closes the long story it began.
  29. Day 13 · Sinodos, highlights: Sinodos 1 — the church's order beginsHow did the early church order its common life? Sinodos, translated anew from the Ge'ez, answers as tradition received it from the apostles.
  30. Day 13 · Sinodos, highlights: Sinodos 20 — order in the middleA midway sample of the canons — practical, pastoral, and surprisingly concrete about everyday church life.
  31. Day 13 · Sinodos, highlights: Sinodos 37 — the closing canonsThe Sinodos closes — and its sister church-order books, the Didascalia, the Covenants, and the Qalementos, wait nearby in the full canon.
  32. Day 14 — Psalm 151One psalm past the 150 you may know — David, in tradition, remembering the shepherd years and the giant.
  33. Day 14 — The Prayer of ManassehA wicked king's prayer of repentance, kept as an appendix to the Psalter — fifteen verses, and not one of them wasted.
  34. Day 14 — Teaching of the Mysteries (whole book)Finish with the smallest treasure: an Ethiopian book of the risen Christ's teaching, translated anew from the Ge'ez — five short chapters to sit with slowly.

Meeting Jesus

From promise to arrival, cross to empty tomb — meet him in the order the story itself unfolds.

  1. Isaiah 9 — a child is bornThe church has long read this promise at Christmas: light in darkness, and a child whose names carry the whole hope.
  2. Isaiah 53 — the suffering servantRead the servant song slowly, as the church has read it for centuries — wounded, silent, and bearing others' griefs.
  3. Micah 5 — from BethlehemA small town named centuries ahead, in this tradition — the ruler to come would come from Bethlehem.
  4. Psalm 22 — the psalm of the crossThe psalm Jesus himself quoted from the cross — begin here, and notice how it ends in trust, not despair.
  5. Daniel 7 — the Son of ManDaniel sees one like a son of man coming with the clouds — the title Jesus would take most often for himself.
  6. Matthew 1 — the arrival, in a genealogyMatthew starts with a family tree full of unlikely names — grace has been running in this family a long time.
  7. Luke 2 — the birth at BethlehemShepherds, angels, and a baby in a manger — Luke tells the arrival the way a careful storyteller would.
  8. John 1 — the Word made fleshJohn begins before the beginning: the Word was with God, and the Word became flesh and lived among us.
  9. Mark 1 — the ministry eruptsMark wastes no time — baptism, wilderness, calling, healing, all in one breathless first chapter.
  10. Matthew 5 — the Sermon on the Mount beginsBlessed are the poor in spirit — sit on the hillside for the most famous sermon ever preached.
  11. Matthew 27 — the crossRead it plainly and without rushing past — this is the chapter the whole story has been walking toward.
  12. Matthew 28 — the empty tombBefore dawn, an earthquake, an angel, and women running with fear and great joy — he is not here; he is risen.
  13. John 20 — Mary in the gardenJohn lingers where Matthew hurries: a gardener who is not a gardener, and a name spoken — 'Mary.'
  14. Acts 2 — the church's answer beginsPentecost: wind, fire, and Peter preaching the risen Jesus to the very city that crucified him — and thousands believe.
  15. 1 Corinthians 15 — the resurrection chapterPaul hands on what he received and lists the witnesses by name — the church's earliest creed, and its great argument for Easter.
  16. Teaching of the Mysteries 1 — the risen TeacherOne step beyond the familiar canon: as Sources & Method documents, this is an Ethiopian book of the risen Christ's teaching, here in English as a whole book for the first time — read it as the tradition that kept it presents it.

For the Skeptic

Don't take our word for it — check the receipts.

  1. Sources & Method — how this edition was madeStart where a skeptic should: every book's source, edition, and translation route, named — judge the method before you judge the text.
  2. 1 Enoch 1 — read the actual textNo summaries, no spin — the opening chapter of the book quoted in Jude, in R. H. Charles's classic translation as received.
  3. Jubilees 1 — read the actual textJubilees in its own words — and yes, Sources & Method tells you plainly that its scan was damaged and how 598 repairs were logged against Charles (1902).
  4. Josippon, highlights: Josippon 1 — a rebuilt book opensThis Josippon was rebuilt from its base Ge'ez codex photographed folio by folio — read its opening and check the claim against the manuscript story in Sources & Method.
  5. Josippon, highlights: Josippon 84 — where honesty costs somethingHonestly, then: the manuscript itself has lost three passages — chapter 7 verses 12–14, chapter 84 verses 21–35, and chapter 104 verses 14–19 — hunted through every reachable witness and left as blanks rather than invented. This chapter carries one of them.
  6. The Honest Page — what we could not doThe limits, losses, and judgment calls, stated plainly in one place — a project that hides nothing here is easier to trust elsewhere.
  7. Daniel 13 — Susanna, the chapter you may not haveCheck your own Bible for Daniel 13. If it is not there, ask why Ethiopia kept it — then read it and judge the story on its merits.
  8. Psalm 151 — one more psalmPrinted here from Charles Thomson's 1808 English Septuagint, as Sources & Method records — a psalm the Hebrew Psalter does not carry, verifiable in any Septuagint.
  9. The full contents — choose your own testYour turn: pick any book, any chapter, and compare it against the sources named for it. The whole canon is open — that is the point.
