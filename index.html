The Museum

The library has grown a museum — a churchseum, in the word coined for this place: the art the canon traveled with, the sounds it was sung and rung in, and the very ground it happened on, and the world as believers pictured it. Five rooms, and everything in them old, real, and freely given.

  • The Gallery Historic sacred art on eight walls — Ethiopian Gospel illuminations, the oldest church paintings, illuminated Psalters, the great engravers, Enoch’s heavens, Byzantium’s icons and relics, sacred architecture, and panel paintings.
  • The Music Room Old recordings kept whole — chant, the oldest song in the world, and the bells of sixteen towers — each with its history and its credits.
  • Places of the Book The ancient places of the canon that still stand — rock-hewn Lalibela, the scroll cave at Qumran, the cave where John heard the Revelation — each photographed, with its story.
  • The Hidden Shelf Extra books related to the faith but not part of it — old, lost, and strange writings, kept for history. Not canon, not Scripture.
  • The Ancient Maps Room How Christians pictured the world before modern maps — Ptolemy’s measured world, the Peutinger road map, the Madaba link, Beatus, Hereford, and Ortelius.
